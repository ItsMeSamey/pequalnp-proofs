# A global physical-reader expansion and a cross-shore matching

Choose a gate minimum minimizing Σ_e c_e (alternation-switch-charge.md), and fix one minimizing proof DAG per native flag. For a nonempty set S of marked OR gates let R(S) be the union of their distinct direct gate readers, and U(S) the union of flags whose fixed proofs visit S. View U as a bipartite graph of actual flags, with minimum vertex-cover size τ(U). Then

|U(S)|+τ(U(S)) ≥ |S∪R(S)|+1.

Pin every g∈S to zero. Each gate in S and every direct reader disappears by zero propagation or forwarding; count their union once, including nested and shared slots. For every flag outside U its chosen proof survives (forwarded ORs may be omitted). No OR gains a mark by this restriction. The set U is nonempty, since otherwise pinning gives a smaller correct circuit. At the very end OR in all U flags, assigning each to an endpoint in a minimum vertex cover and patching as a union of stars. A k-edge star costs at most k+1 including its output OR; total cost ≤|U|+τ(U). These terms reject all independent negatives, and on each patched flag they give a proof with zero marked switches because their ANDs follow raw-only ORs and only ORs lie above. Older proofs on the other flags retain their cost; each flag in U previously had c_e≥1. Thus if the displayed inequality failed we would either reduce size or improve the secondary objective. This conclusion concerns the whole original scalar promise.

Since τ(U)≤|U|, Hall matching gives a global assignment of every marked OR to a visited flag, with at most two switches per flag. The actual reader union term can be much larger than |S|. For arbitrary nonnegative switch weights α_g, applying the inequality to S_t={g:α_g≥t} and integrating over 0<t≤max α gives a physical weighted version. Its right side is the sum over OLD physical gates v of max{α_g:v=g or v is a reader of g}, plus max α. Its left side is Σ_e max{α_g:e's chosen proof visits g} plus ∫τ(U(S_t))dt, with empty maxima treated as zero. One may set α_g to the probability of a witness in a single common distribution; no gate pays more than its maximum weight in this ledger.

For private switches g_i with reader h_i=g_i∧y_i and raw guards from either shore, use a different intervention: pin the h_i rather than the g_i. For any family S of k such switches, gate privacy and the raw second operand give 2k distinct eliminated g_i,h_i. Define U by flags visiting a selected h_i. Every such proof visited a marked g_i, so the same final patch strictly improves its cost. This yields |U|+τ(U)≥2k+1. If d is the number of distinct guards appearing in S, they form a vertex cover of U, so |U|+d≥2k+1.

For any fixed nonempty full family of such switches, give each switch two left copies. Its real right neighbors are its visited flags, and both copies may use one extra dummy token for its own raw guard. Omit the dummy of any one chosen guard entirely. For any subset of left copies, its underlying switches S have at least |U|+d−1≥2|S| right neighbors, enough for Hall. A single matching therefore uses at least 2k−d+1 distinct REAL flags in the full family, across both shores with no duplicate real flag. Without dummies the separate-shore expansion in minimum-raw-guard-switch-hall.md is stronger on that one shore. This expansion gives a lower bound on the flag footprint of switch families; it does not bound the total number of switch visits over all flag proofs.
