# Injective receiver-to-flag and endpoint accounting

Fix one residual circuit and its positive incident flags and independent negative tests. Assume the residual lower bound is M and there are at most M+K physical gates after previously realized removals, with K a nonnegative integer. For each member of any collection of distinct physical OR gates g choose an operand to forward. Let B_g be the set of residual flags where g was true and that operand was false, evaluated before any changes. Fix a map π assigning each positive flag to one of its endpoints.

For every subset S of these gates, project all members simultaneously onto the specified operands. This removes at least |S| distinct physical gates. Negative outputs can only drop. For every flag outside B=union of B_g for g in S, induction shows the old and new flag evaluations agree everywhere, including at comparable or reconvergent marked gates. Repair B by grouping its flags by the chosen endpoint π. For a group of d flags at v add the OR of its d other endpoints, AND with v, then OR into the rebuilt output. This costs at most d+1 gates and accepts only inputs containing actual flags. Total repair ≤|B|+|π(B)|.

Construct a bipartite graph with the physical receivers on the left; on the right use disjoint resources for individual flags, individual endpoints, and K slack units. Each g sees B_g, π(B_g), and all slack units. The inequality |S|≤|B|+|π(B)|+K for every S is necessary under the size bound, or else projection plus repair gives a circuit smaller than M. Hall's theorem therefore gives an injective assignment saturating the receivers. This holds for every choice of fixed receiver directions and π, on this one residual circuit. Reversing the argument, any deficient Hall set is a concrete circuit-size certificate. A physical receiver can contribute at most one left unit regardless of its fanout or witness multiplicity. The same proof allows forwarding any earlier wire bounded above by g on every required negative; outside B only positive domination, not equality, is then needed.

The theorem detects repeated supporting flags even when separate gates cannot be bypassed at zero cost. It does not guarantee a Hall deficiency in every order-401 circuit.
