# Polynomial size versus sharing for scalar incidence

Let a binary monotone circuit of size s compute incidence in a plane of order q>=4 and N=q^2+q+1 on at most q-1 selected vertices per shore. Inputs have arbitrary fanout. Let t count distinct shared internal gates (fanout >=2) and k=q+1. This applies both to the original sparse circuit and independently to its bilateral hoisted form.

Maintain independent pinned positive inputs, all their neighbors zero, and a set of good remaining incidence edges, initially all Nk flags. Every good flag is accepted on its two endpoints; every negative mask which, together with the pins, fits the caps remains rejected. Simplify constants, remove dead gates and bypass unary gates. These operations do not increase the number of shared gates: the one surviving parent of a bypassed shared unary gate can inherit its fanout, in place of that shared unary gate.

Pick an earliest remaining shared gate g. Its current source cone is a formula with ell raw leaf occurrences; no strictly internal ancestor has another live use. When g becomes constant the entire cone disappears. Thus the successive cones are disjoint in original gate IDs; across at most t such steps the sum of ell is at most s+t. Each step destroys one shared gate outright before the remaining simplifications.

Put j=floor(sqrt(ell/(2k))). If j>=1 and some independent set of at most 2j surviving raw inputs makes g true, pin it to one and pin all its neighbors zero. It is independent of all earlier pins because surviving vertices have no previously pinned neighbor. Per new pin, lost flags are incident to at most k newly zeroed neighbors, hence at most k^2 flags. Surviving good flags remain accepted by monotonicity: their two endpoints survive, and all other raw bits were previously zero at their minimal test. The source gate becomes one. In units of k flags the cost is at most 2kj<=sqrt(2k ell).

Otherwise, if j>=1, g rejects every independent set of size at most 2j. Let M count good flags accepted by g. Select d=j+1 of the k perfect matching classes of the plane to cover the most accepted flags. The resulting subgraph has at least dM/k edges and maximum degree d. Every proper-neighborhood KW negative in this graph consists of at most 2j independent vertices, hence g rejects it. It also rejects both singleton endpoints of an accepted flag; thus every positive-degree vertex is essential in this tested function. Star ranks from formula-leaves.md, using essentiality for a degree-one vertex whose edge is oriented away, yield at least |E|(1+1/d) leaves. Therefore M/k<=ell/(j+2)<=sqrt(2k ell). Set g to zero and discard precisely the good flags it accepted. No negative becomes positive. If j=0, always use the zero step: g is zero at the all-zero remaining input after constant folding, so every accepted flag touches a raw occurrence. This gives M/k<=ell<=sqrt(2k ell). The selection requires d<=k, automatic in the nontrivial regimes below.

Write C=sqrt(2kt(s+t)). Cauchy-Schwarz bounds all the step costs in the chosen units by C. At least k(N-C) good flags survive and there are H<=floor(C/k) pinned ones, since every pin was budgeted k units. The remaining circuit is a formula. Select the d=q-H most populated matching classes of its good flags. Each proper-neighborhood negative uses at most d-1 vertices per shore in this graph, legal together with all H pins. The singleton masks remain negative as well, so every nonisolated endpoint of these edges is essential. The final formula therefore has at least (d+1)(N-C) leaves. It has at most s+1 leaves. This comparison uses d>=2.

If C>=N/2 and t>=1, t<=s gives s>=N^2/(16kt). If C<N/2, H<=floor(q/2), the local choices obey 2kj<=C, and the final formula has at least Nq/4 leaves. Consequently (s+1)(t+1)=Omega(Nq), including t=0 by the original formula bound. No fanout bound is imposed on the shared internal gates.

At q401, s<646425 and t<=40 give C<144189 and H<=358. The final lower bound is at least 44*(161203-144189)=748616 leaves, exceeding s+1. Thus every hypothetical below-target circuit has at least 41 shared internal gates. This bounded-sharing statement does not prove unrestricted order 401.

At q401 use j=floor(B/804), B=ell/8+1206. This j is between 1 and 102 and both 804j and ell/(j+2) are at most B. Indeed ell=8B-9648, and when 1<=j<=5 we have B(6-j)<804(j+1)(6-j)<=9648. For j>=6 the desired inequality is immediate. Search for an independent certificate with at most j points AND at most j lines. In the absence of such a certificate the previous KW negative test still applies. Each pin spends at most j on either shore, hence once C=sum B the final matching degree can be 401-floor(C/804).

Write A for total eliminated cone leaves, T for actual eliminations. The final formula has at most s+T+1-A leaves by disjointness of eliminated gate slots. For t<=66 we have C=A/8+1206T<=160407.25 and 402-floor(C/804)>=203. The leaf lower bound is at least 203(N-A/8-1206T). Subtract the available s+T+1-A: the difference decreases in A, hence is smallest at A=s+T. There the available bound is 1 while the lower bound exceeds 161000. Thus a below-target circuit has at least 67 internally shared gates.
