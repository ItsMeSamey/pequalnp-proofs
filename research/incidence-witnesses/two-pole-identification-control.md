# Sharp small threshold identifications in an expanded core

Replace each physical raw by one target variable or zero. Let t count the retained physical sites. To realize threshold two on m target bits, each pair of different labels must be joined by a physical flag; no flag has equal labels at both ends. At most one target label occurs just on points and at most one just on lines, so t≥2m−2.

Equality requires both pure labels to be singletons p,d, which must be incident. Each other label has one point on d and one line through p. No cross incidence between two such labels is possible because each of those lines meets d only at p. Hence equality is possible only through m=3, realized by a four-vertex path when m=3.

At t=2m−1, if there is one pure label, it is singleton and the other m−1 labels have one raw per shore. Take the pure label to be a point; all mixed lines form a pencil and each mixed point reaches at most one other mixed label. Thus binom(m−1,2)≤m−1. With two pure labels and one extra occurrence at a mixed label, two ordinary mixed labels again could not see each other, hence m≤4. With the extra occurrence on one pure label, suppose the opposite pure label is a singleton line d. Every mixed point is on d, and every mixed line meets d at most once, so binom(m−2,2)≤m−2 and m≤5. In particular t≥2m for m≥6.

For m=4 use a pure pole p and three lines through it; pair the three line labels cyclically with points lying on the next line. This uses seven slots. For m=5 take two points a,p of one pure label, a on d and p off d. The singleton d is the other pure label. Choose three points P_i on d distinct from a, and lines l_i=pP_i. Pair P_i and l_(i+1) into the three remaining threshold labels cyclically. This gives exactly the required off-diagonal flags with nine physical slots. A row d and pole p of an expanded two-pole core suffice, taking a=O and q≥3.

The threshold circuit itself has at most 3m−5 gates for m≥2: start with OR and AND of two bits, then update threshold by ORing it with the product of the new bit and the preceding prefix OR, omitting the final OR-prefix computation. For m=2 use one gate. Consequently its possible lower bound never exceeds 2t−4 for m≥3. At m=3,t=4 equality holds, since threshold two of three is not read-once and needs four gates. The only positive exception is m=2,t=2, with one gate versus zero. This is a comparison against an elimination budget, not a standalone graph lower bound on arbitrary induced restrictions.

For the direct shore-respecting n-crown, let a,b count singleton labels on the two shores. If a≥4 and b≥2 then the two singleton columns have at least two common singleton-row neighbors, a forbidden C4. Thus t≥min(3n−1,4n−6). For n≥5 one has t≥3n−1, whereas a circuit using prefix and suffix ORs to obtain all-except-i sets costs at most 5n−7. At n=4 the sharper 11-slot minimum from crown-identifications.md suffices. At n=3 the six-site crown is the two-arm theta, exact size 8. At n=2 its two disjoint edges cost 3 on four sites. None beats the comparison 2t−4.

## Exact optimum on the nine physical inputs

Before identification the m=5 construction is a three-arm theta plus a single leaf a adjacent only to d. Its unrestricted binary monotone circuit size is exactly 13. The upper circuit ORs the three expressions l_i AND (p OR P_i) and the expression d AND (a OR P_1 OR P_2 OR P_3). For the lower bound, specialize a to zero: at least its first reader is eliminated, and the three-arm theta remains, with unrestricted lower bound 12 as proved in theta-residual.md. Hence even a coordinated full-cube refactoring cannot save a gate. A threshold identification gives at most ten gates' worth of lower bound and any directly embedded crown on nine slots at most eight. The accompanying check-nine-site-control.py checks the PG(2,3) embedding, exact full truth table, every individual bypass, and every threshold quotient row.
