# Exact conservation of zero wire occurrences

Fix a deletion set D and let E be a set of rich ORs certifiably replaceable by one as in rich-raw-addition.md. Count the edited slots once. After the edits fix all deleted inputs to zero and evaluate all surviving inputs at one. Write z_g for the indicator of a zero internal wire, f_g for its original outgoing wire count (including f=0 at the root), t_g for the count of zero operands, and U_D for the total fanout of deleted raw inputs. Let R count all edited gates plus the unedited gates with at least one zero parent. Then

    R = U_D + sum_g (f_g-1) z_g + #ANDs with t_g=1 + sum_{g in E}(1-t_g).

Indeed sum t_g=U_D+sum f_g z_g. At an unedited OR the contribution is t_g-z_g, at an AND it is t_g-z_g+1[t_g=1], and an edited OR contributes one with z_g=0. All the counted slots can be removed. When the residual output is nonzero the second term is nonnegative on a live original circuit.

Under balanced flag deletion E consists of the genuine branching ORs with at least one deleted witnessing line. For each of its t labels L, an OR has a pure point singleton on L at each parent. If the parent is zero after edits that singleton had to be deleted; all semantic edits increase signals. Hence the expectation of t_g times the edit indicator is at most 2tJ. The probability of an edit is at least tp-(t-1)Js. Summing gives expected edit correction at least W(p-2J-Js/2) when its coefficient is positive. Independently, each AND with two distinct raw inputs contributes at least 2(p-J) to the exactly-one term. These bounds add to the exact baseline pU. One can omit the edit class entirely when its coefficient is negative.

With U>=4N, q=401 and h=80, W>=32638 suffices, hence 164 full stars suffice. With h=203 and no rich edits, 6443 distinct raw-pair ANDs suffice on their own. The unrestricted order remains open.

Another term works even in the absence of raw ANDs: an AND with one parent equal to OR of raw a,b, and whose other parent has a positive certificate C of size at most two disjoint from {a,b}, contributes whenever a,b are deleted and C survives. This event has probability at least p^2(1-p)^2 under independent p-deletion. Do not edit the raw OR parent when using this certificate (a raw pair cannot be a rich OR). Under balanced flag deletion the same event can be counted exactly by inclusion-exclusion on the at most four specified raw vertices. The AND is one slot even if both orientations yield witnesses.

The zero-flow identity and the rich and raw-AND extensions were independently audited by rank. The method measures architectural parameters, and makes no all-circuit q401 or complexity-class assertion.

For a numerical low-raw-AND branch at q=401, suppose U>=4N and C distinct ANDs admit the raw-OR-parent certificate above. Delete each raw variable independently with probability 1/2, and choose no semantic edits. Even when the residual output vanishes the fanout term is at least -1. Therefore E[R-2|D|] >= C/16-1. Set k=138. A union bound gives probability at most epsilon = 2N 2^-402 sum_{i=0}^{137} binom(402,i) that some vertex has fewer than k surviving neighbours. On the good event the residual flow lower bound applied with n=2N-|D| implies, if the original size is at most L-1 for L=646425, that X=R-2|D|+2|D|/(k+1) is at most L-4N+4N/(k+1). On a bad event X is at most L-1. Thus C/16 > L-4N+1+2N/(k+1)+(L-1)epsilon suffices, as E|D|=N. The right hand side is below 3951.039, so C>=63217 proves the target. The input side of the raw OR and the points or lines used in the certificate may be arbitrary.

At p=1/2 an AND of two distinct raw-pair ORs contributes at least 1/4, even when the raw pairs overlap. For pairs {a,b},{a,c} the common variable must be zero and the other two bits different, probability 1/4. Disjoint pairs yield 3/8. Thus 15805 such ANDs suffice with U>=4N and no assumption on full stars or raw ANDs. A single disjoint singleton certificate yields 1/8, so 31609 such ANDs suffice. Distinct ANDs of different types may be combined by summing their strongest individual lower bounds.
