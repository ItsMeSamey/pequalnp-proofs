# Pure cofilters on actual bilateral planted negatives

Let c=1/16, N=q²+q+1, and take named query-only heads C_p in one physical monotone circuit. Each accepts all d_p≥q−O(1) singleton query lines incident with its own point p. It suffices that these properties hold in the single common zero-payload restriction of possibly mixed actual cofilters. Select each point independently for R with probability c/q. Given R with M≥q lines missing R, draw a uniform q-subset W of them. Otherwise draw no test. Put ρ_p=Pr[M≥q and C_p(W)=0 | p∈R]. Then the number of physical OR gates is Ω(log q·Σ_pρ_p). In particular a bank of O(N) gates accepts the query sets of all but an o(1) fraction of actually selected planted points with high probability. These false activations are allowed for an approximate filter; on an exact gated row its other operand must then reject.

Take D=ceil(q^(2/3)). On a rejection for p trace the d_p true singleton labels through C_p. At an AND fix one operand rejecting W; it accepts every singleton traversing this gate. At an OR take one accepting child per label, both children rejecting W. Stop at a row with ≤D singleton labels through p. The physical (p,l,OR) triples on these paths are distinct, since each l has a single path for p and a DAG path cannot revisit a gate. The OR-choice strings form a prefix code up to D colliding labels: a bit string determines its execution and termination time. Thus there are at least d_p log₂(d_p/D)=Ω(q log q) triples per successful head. Shared heads and arbitrary overlapping cones are permitted.

Fix an OR with pure-query singleton set S of size m and fix p. For this OR to be charged, S meets the pencil of p in >D lines and W misses S. Conditional on p∈R remove its pencil; let k be the number of remaining members of S. For every u≠p give u weight equal to the number of these k lines containing u, at most q+1. Their total weight is k(q+1). Let X sum the weights of selected u≠p. It upper bounds the number of those k lines blocked by R. Independence and convexity of exp on [0,1] give
```
E exp(X/(q+1)) ≤ exp((e−1)ck/q).
```
Hence Pr[X≥k/2]≤exp(−k/(5q)) for q≥3. Otherwise on a valid draw at least k/2 candidate lines survive into the M-line pool, so their avoidance by W costs at most exp(−qk/(2N)). For all large q the event of a valid draw and S missed has probability at most 2 exp(−k/(5q)). This does not require an independent choice of R or W for each p. M<q is itself negligible: except with exp(−Ω(q)) probability |R|≤q/4, ensuring M≥N−(q/4)(q+1)>q. The same holds conditional on any prescribed p∈R.

When m≤D²/2 the total incidences of S with its >D-rich pencils are ≤2m: selecting D lines per rich point and using codegree one bounds their number by 2m/D, and duplicates in their full pencil intersections cost at most the number of point pairs. Thus the expected contribution of this OR, summed over p with the conditioning p∈R separately for each term, is O(m exp(−(m−q−1)_+/(5q)))=O(q). When m>D²/2 use N(q+1)exp(−Ω(q^(1/3))) instead. Comparing total expected charges with the entropy lower bound proves the theorem. Equivalently one may sum unconditionally over selected p, multiplying both sides by c/q. The fraction claim uses |R|=(c+o(1))N/q with high probability and Markov's inequality.

If a completed p-row must accept each own flag and its noncofilter factor can be forced on an independent extension of the actual R,W whenever p∈R, the cofilter on the original negative must reject by monotonicity. A constant mean frequency of this condition forces Ω(N log q) physical ORs for these common-payload heads. This implication is conditional on warm extensions and on common-payload flag acceptance. A mixed receiver that becomes selective only upon adding its own point does not automatically supply such a head.
