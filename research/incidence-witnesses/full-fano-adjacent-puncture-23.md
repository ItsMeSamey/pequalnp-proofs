# A 23-gate lower bound on the full Fano native promise

Take an exact binary monotone gate minimum on arbitrary point masks and at most two queries. Constants and dead wires may be removed. A raw is good if it has at least two outgoing slots or its only reader is an AND. First obtain an equal-size minimum in which every query is good.

For a sole-use query x initially feeding OR, follow its unique OR spine until the exit branches or its sole reader is an AND. The exit cannot be the output because the singleton x rejects. The exit represents x OR the off-spine siblings; every sibling is independent of x because no earlier spine gate has more than one reader. Every sibling is zero on every legal independent mask n: first remove x, preserving that sibling's value. Pick a point y of x off the at most two remaining active lines; the three points on x suffice. Add y, preserving independence and legality. If a sibling was true then the whole exit is already true, hence turning on x cannot alter the scalar. But the resulting input dominates native flag (y,x), so monotonicity forces acceptance even if this last input has three lines and exceeds the promise. Contradiction.

Replace the entire spine by x at its exit and OR each original off-spine sibling onto the output. This costs at most the number of removed spine ORs. Every changed surviving gate only decreases before the appended collector, so all negatives still reject. On a flag with x active the exit stays true; otherwise losing a positive requires a previously true sibling, which has no dependence on the spine and remains true. The resulting circuit is again a minimum. Trimming any dead logic or tied slots that saves a gate would contradict minimum. Other query fanouts/initial reader types are undisturbed: no off-spine sibling can be a raw since it rejects every singleton, and a sole spine gate has no external use. The new x either has multiple slots or a sole AND. Repeating for all bad query raws terminates.

At least one point p is good too. Otherwise take the earliest gate reading any point; its sole-use point raw p enters an OR. Its sibling cannot be another point raw r: the two would be interchangeable everywhere, distinguished by a query incident to one and not the other. Otherwise the sibling B depends on queries alone. B cannot accept a singleton query y. With y active add an incident query of p if necessary, totalling at most two queries. The pure-query mask rejects, but adding p must fire and cannot change the p OR B exit. Since B rejects query singletons (and zero), it is zero on every native flag. Bypass this OR to p. The output only decreases and all flags persist, contradicting minimum.

Delete that good point p and any incident query l, setting both to zero. Each raw contributes its outgoing slots; each sole AND also contributes one slot in the zero-flow accounting. Here are details. Evaluate all other raw inputs at one. The residual output is nonzero. For every gate with a zero parent count one removable gate. If t is its number of zero operands, z its output's zero indicator, and f its original number of outgoing slots, then summing t−z plus an additional one for each AND with exactly one zero parent counts these removable gates exactly. Summing over all gates gives the two raw fanouts plus Σ(f−1)z plus the number of such ANDs. All terms in the sum are nonnegative since the original circuit was trimmed and its output stays nonzero. A sole-use raw x entering an AND needs its other operand true on every native flag through x: otherwise activating x cannot change the output on that flag. This other operand is independent of x, and hence true on every opposite-endpoint singleton through x. At least one of these survives the two deletions. The sole AND therefore has exactly one zero parent. The two compensating sole ANDs, if both are needed, are distinct; one sole AND containing both raws would make a flag using only one of them impossible. Consequently at least 2+2=4 physical gates are removed.

The residual must accept the 16 edges of the 12-site adjacent puncture and reject all its maximal two-point/two-query negatives. This follows directly by zero restriction and by Fano flag transitivity. The established nineteen-gate unrestricted lower bound for that twelve-site promise (punctured-fano-any-twenty-maxima-nineteen.md) gives s≥19+4=23. The same bound follows for full-cube circuits by restriction of the correctness requirement. This argument does not certify the 27-gate formula optimum for circuits with arbitrary fanout.
