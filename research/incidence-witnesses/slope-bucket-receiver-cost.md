# Hostile configurations and shared OR cost in the slope-bucket construction

For the owner-isolating circuit of few-slopes-physical-cofilter.md with any number of hash banks, full-promise correctness forces each owner slope to have a globally singleton bin in at least one bank. Otherwise choose a slope a failing this condition, an affine point p=(1,y), and an off-p seed query (a,d) with d≠y−a. For each bank i choose another slope c_i in the bin of a there and add query (c_i,y−a), which misses p. There are only q−1 choices for c_i regardless of the number of banks. Every affected bin now routes p via the digit choices of a and finds intercept y−a. The enabled owner a accepts though these at most q distinct queries all miss p. Repeated queries only improve the cardinality bound.

A physical price applies if the singleton bins expose the native shear's full pre-intercept payload OR interface with shared OR-only computation after fixing all query labels to one. On this one common assignment each of the q² affine nonvertical lines must have its payload disjunction simultaneously available. Every producing OR subgraph for one line contains at least q−1 binary OR merges of nonempty supports covering q distinct leaves (suppress empty and redundant branches, or use union-find). At least q−1 gates with support size at least two are needed: retain inclusion-only OR pathways and view each support≥2 gate as joining at most two existing components of the q leaves; singleton and empty-support gates contribute no joins. Such a gate cannot be used by two distinct output cones, as its support would lie in their intersection of size at most one. Therefore even arbitrary cross-slope OR sharing costs at least q²(q−1) gates under this exposed-interface condition. The original unsimplified singleton-bin shear also reduces to OR-only after the same common assignment; positive input digit rails are constants. The assignment need not be a promised query because the specified physical construction has explicit semantics there. A general scalar implementation preserving only final promised answers does not expose these intermediate outputs and is outside this lower bound.
