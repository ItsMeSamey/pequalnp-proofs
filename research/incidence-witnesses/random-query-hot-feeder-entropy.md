# An entropy bound for singleton-query witnesses in point-owned receivers

Draw W uniformly among (q−1)-subsets of the N lines and use P* equal to its unhit points. In the final stage of near-unit-feeder-intervals.md consider flags served by an oriented physical AND g,h with A_g(W)={p}, h(W+p)=0 and with h also TRUE ON THE ORIGINAL SINGLETON query l. This last condition is stronger than acceptance on W+p+l. Allow every satisfying candidate, whether or not chosen by the output trace. Write H(W) for the number of distinct qualifying flags, assigning each once to any qualifying orientation. At any fixed W an orientation belongs to at most one p. Let a and o be the physical AND and OR counts.

Put d=ceil(q^(2/3)). For any group of r≥1 distinct assigned lines at one receiver and its p, trace h backwards for each of these query singleton labels. All reached signals reject W+p: at AND choose uniformly for the whole group one rejecting child (it accepts every assigned label currently traversing it); at OR choose a child accepting the label, with both children rejecting W+p. Stop on reaching a signal whose ORIGINAL pure line singleton support contains ≤d lines through p. Raw signals meet this stopping rule. Record only OR-branch bits, since AND choices are fixed per signal for this group, and fix all tie breaking. These strings form a prefix code after merging identical strings: the unique execution given a bit prefix determines its own stopping time. At most d distinct assigned labels have any one code. The mean bit length is therefore at least log₂(r/d) by the binary entropy bound (or Kraft and convexity). Each bit is one physical (p,l,OR) occurrence. Distinct paths for the same flag are never counted since assignment and traversal are both single-valued, and a path never visits the same gate twice.

A physical OR with ORIGINAL line-singleton support V of size m can be charged only if W avoids V. On each charged point p its pencil contains more than d of these lines. For m≤d²/2 there are at most 2m/d such points: taking d lines at each point gives j≤m(d−1)/(d²−m) by the pairwise-intersection Cauchy count. Their total incidences with V are at most m+binom(j,2)≤2m using m≤d²/2 and geometric codegree one. This bounds all its distinct (p,l) charges when W avoids V. For larger m use N(q+1) as a trivial charge bound. Uniform sampling gives P[W avoids V]≤exp(−m(q−1)/N). The expected per-OR charge is thus O(q), uniformly in the gate: m exp(−m(q−1)/N)=O(q), and the larger support case contributes at most N(q+1)exp(−d²(q−1)/(2N))=o(1).

There are at most 2a nonempty receiver groups at each W. Summing the entropy lower bound and applying convexity first over groups and then over W yields

    E[H] log₂(E[H]/(2ad)) ≤ O(q o).

The zero-group case has H=0. In particular when the original circuit has O(N) gates, E[H]=O(Nq/log q). To see this, separate E[H]≤Nq^(5/6) and the larger case, in which the logarithm is Ω(log q). On average (1/e+o(1))N(q+1) flags survive the W pin. Thus for a linear-size original-promise circuit, almost all these surviving flags, up to an o(Nq) error in expectation, must at the final stage use a point-owned complementary AND whose line-side sibling fires on W+p+l but DOES NOT fire on the unshifted singleton query l. This conclusion also holds for the probabilistically normalized setting of common-near-unit-extension.md when its bad-extension event has o(1) probability; discard those draws at cost o(Nq). It does not rule out activation by W+l, by p+l or by their combination.
