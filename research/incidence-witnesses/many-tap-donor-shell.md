# Many donor rows below few collector exits

Consider an exact scalar circuit on the arbitrary-point, at-most-q-line incidence promise in a projective plane with N=q²+q+1. Its physical output is H AND B. The collector B is a binary OR tree on distinct physical row gates d_p=p AND C_p; the tree designates one upward parent edge per nonroot vertex and permits additional uses in the surrounding DAG. Assume at least αN eligible distinct row owners, with fixed α>0. For each eligible p fix one certificate K_p of at most r queries missing p on which C_p accepts with no points. This is a hypothesis on the original physical circuit.

Mark B, and mark H if H is itself a vertex of the collector. Also mark each collector vertex v (including row gates) from which a path to H starts with an edge other than its designated upward tree edge. Only this first edge is constrained: its suffix may traverse arbitrary gates, re-enter the collector, or traverse other tree edges. In particular any extra use through intermediate private consumers that eventually reaches H counts. Group the eligible rows by their first marked vertex on their upward paths, starting at the row gate itself. Let b be the number of nonempty groups. If b=o(q/(r+1)), then the whole original DAG has at least (4+α−o(1))N gates.

Write m for the number of eligible rows. Choose an integer t with b=o(t) and t=o(q/(r+1)), and sample t owners independently and uniformly from the m. For example, round the geometric mean of b and q/(r+1). If π denotes the distribution on groups and π̂ the empirical one, coordinatewise variance and Cauchy–Schwarz give E TV(π,π̂)≤(1/2)√(b/t). A pair of distinct draw indices conflicts if a drawn point belongs to the other draw's certificate line union in either direction. Each event has probability ≤2r(q+1)/m, by conditioning on the other draw; repeated copies of an owner do not conflict since its own certificate avoids it. Remove both endpoints of every conflict. The expected removed fraction is at most 2(t−1)r(q+1)/m. Choose a realization with the sum of TV and removed fraction o(1). For the set of groups with no retained draw, all their original empirical mass was removed. Their true mass is at most that removed fraction plus TV. Thus the groups hit by the retained draws contain at least (1−o(1))m distinct eligible row gates.

Pin the distinct retained points P and the union Z of their certificates high. They are mutually nonincident, |P|≤t and |Z|≤tr. Pin all neighbors of either pinned set on the other shore low, leaving all other sites free. Each donor row selected is now constantly one. Therefore every hit group's first marked vertex is constantly one, even though many other rows in that group were not selected. So is B. Every free mask with at most q−|Z| queries is a legal extension; its incidences are exactly the free incidences. In particular the restricted H computes the residual predicate on all such masks. The free graph has n≥2N−(|P|+|Z|)(q+2)=2N−o(N), minimum degree at least q+1−max(|P|,|Z|)=q−o(q), and query allowance at least two for all sufficiently large q. If H itself were forced constant, this would contradict the surviving flag and empty tests.

Replace all forced hit taps and B by one wherever they feed H, propagate pins, and keep only gates needed by the resulting guard. Every path from any eligible row in a hit group to H passes through its first marked vertex: before reaching that vertex a path must follow the designated collector edges, by definition of the marks (and H would itself be marked if reached). Thus all the distinct physical row gates in hit groups are absent from the remaining guard cone, including any row that is itself its group's tap. Gates that supply shared cofilters may remain. The remaining physical gate count is at most s−(1−o(1))m and at least 2n−1−floor(2n/(d+1))=4N−o(N) by the codegree-one two-by-two bound for minimum degree d (residual-peel-census.md). Adding these disjoint counts gives the claim. This conclusion depends on the physical collector and its exit groups; a semantic row expansion alone supplies no removable row gates.
