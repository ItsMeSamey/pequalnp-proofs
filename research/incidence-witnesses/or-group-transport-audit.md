# Physical OR groups and the five-sixths demand

The source audited was protected-dense-signature-401.md §“A round always pays extra” together with protected-or-group-403-404.md (SHA-256 57041cb9e9120137a3063f1df4fb65456fc2a21e81a297dcf37dbe9f4a44ca96). The graph argument survives shared outputs, nested groups, repeated read ports and a pair of raw reads with the same terminal group.

Fix the actual residual circuit and the ordinary surviving raw labels R. Use its physical terminal OR rows as vertices. For each x in R, take a pair a(x), b(x) from IncidencePhysicalOrGroups.route_all at wireId x. A row g is a Member for x exactly when it is one of these endpoints. This equivalence follows from both membership witnesses and the last universal clause of route_all. In particular, no leaves from below a shared OR are implicitly assigned to every consumer of that shared result. A top is identified by its circuit row, even when two rows compute the same function.

**Cross-incidence.** For x in R, suppose y and z are labels other than x, with y a member of a(x) and z a member of b(x). They may be equal. If y and z are nonincident, select t among x's surviving neighbors outside N(y) and N(z). At most two choices are blocked by codegree one, since x differs from each of y,z. The labels y,z,t form an independent set of size at most three. Labels and neighborhoods are in the same residual relation; the original raw-ID map is injective on both shores. The tested negative assignment on this set must reject. After inserting x it must accept because t is an incident surviving neighbor. Each top for x is already high on the negative by member_hot, and remains high after inserting x. IncidencePhysicalOrGroups.shield says the output cannot change. This contradiction proves that y and z are incident. It also applies if a(x) and b(x) are nested or equal. No condition asserting that an entire group has only ordinary raw inputs is used.

**Single-endpoint labels.** If a(x)=b(x)=v, any different ordinary label y at v would contradict cross-incidence with y=z. Thus v supports exactly one ordinary label and is unused by all other edges. Distinct such labels determine distinct vertices. Remove these isolated one-edge components before constructing the simple graph, then restore one vertex and one label each at the end. This avoids needing a separate gate-decreasing duplicate-edit theorem. Occupation by a protected raw, a sole raw or an internal signal does not affect isolation in the graph on R.

**Two-endpoint labels.** If x and y have the same two-element endpoint set, cross-incidence for x would require y incident with itself. Distinct labels therefore give distinct unordered pairs of physical top rows. Use these pairs as edges, leaving all unused top rows as isolated vertices. The number of edges equals the number of retained labels. Adjacency in the *group graph* and incidence between two *plane labels* are different relations.

**Local pruning.** No edge joins vertices both of original degree at least three. Otherwise the two alternatives at either end force a plane K₂,₂ by cross-incidence, contrary to codegree one. If v has original degree at least four, choose, at each neighbor wᵢ of original degree at least two, an edge yᵢ other than xᵢ=vwᵢ. The graph is simple, so yᵢ is not incident with v. Two distinct edges xⱼ,xₖ at v remain after excluding xᵢ and xᵢ′. Cross-incidence makes yᵢ and yᵢ′ neighbors of both their distinct plane labels; hence yᵢ=yᵢ′. One edge has only two endpoints, so v has at most two neighbors of degree at least two. After deleting all original degree-zero and degree-one vertices simultaneously, the remaining maximum degree is three and its degree-three vertices form an independent set. For a degree-three count a and remaining vertex count v', degree summation gives 3a≤2(v'−a) and 2e'≤2v'+a, hence 5e'≤6v'. An edge removed with both endpoints of degree one counts only once in the bound of one removed edge per removed vertex. Restoring these edges and the isolated single-endpoint labels yields 5|R|≤6|tops|.

Native additions needed beyond the verified 401 components are the arbitrary fixed-alternatives version of the masking lemma, the label-to-unordered-pair counting after removing single-endpoint components, and the two local degree constraints plus induced-subgraph edge ledger (unless supplied by an existing abstract graph theorem). The physical endpoint enumeration, path uniqueness, heat propagation, changed-output shielding, signed sparse tests and original-ID transport already exist.
