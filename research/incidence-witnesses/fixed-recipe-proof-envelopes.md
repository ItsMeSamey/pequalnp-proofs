# Exact common-ledger recognition for fixed simultaneous replacements

Choose a collection V of distinct gates of a monotone circuit and independently available monotone replacement recipes r_v. The inputs to the recipes are fixed signals whose original cones survive every edit. Replacing v means redirecting all its uses, including the designated output if applicable. Tests are the positive singleton flags and maximal independent payloads for every legal query set. Let L and U be two evaluations. Keep the raws unchanged. At every gate compute its original operation on the corresponding parent values; at v in V additionally AND this result with r_v for L, and OR it with r_v for U. Recipe values here are the actual values of the fixed, unaffected library.

The following are equivalent for an originally correct circuit.

- Every subset of V can be replaced while preserving the full promise.
- The L output accepts each native flag and the U output rejects every listed negative.
- One common ledger of original accepting and rejecting proofs satisfies all visited-site obligations: on every flag, each visited v in V has r_v=1; on every listed negative each visited v in V has r_v=0.

For any fixed test and any chosen edits the physical gate values lie between L and U by monotonicity. Each entire extreme vector is simultaneously attainable on that test: in topological order choose the old operation or the recipe according to which attains the required minimum or maximum. This works for nested replacements and arbitrary reconvergence. The subset is allowed to depend on the test in proving necessity, since all subsets must be correct on all tests.

At a lower-true gate its old operation on lower parent values is true and any local recipe is true. At an OR choose one true child, at an AND keep both; recursion yields an original accepting proof (old values dominate lower values) with the required visits. At an upper-false gate use the dual rejecting proof, choosing one false child at an AND. These choices can be fixed per physical gate and test. Conversely truncate each proof the first time it encounters a substituted gate along each branch. The checked recipe values and surviving raw leaves certify the same output. Monotonicity extends flag acceptance and same-query maximal rejection to the full promise. Correctness for just one designated set of simultaneous edits need not imply correctness for its subsets.

**Strictness in a minimum donor-free example.** Use a bipartite graph with point raws p,r and query raws x,y and the three native flags (p,x),(p,y),(r,y). The circuit

    F = [p AND (x OR y)] OR [r AND y]

has four gates. Replace g=x OR y by the separately computed recipe (p AND x) OR (p AND y). The full output function stays identical, but on flag (r,y) this replacement is zero while old g is one. The common ledger chooses the right output branch on (r,y); the full local positive-direction requirement would fail. Both original and modified circuits use only the raw signals for this example. It claims no size saving. The original four-gate circuit is minimum: three binary gates connecting four essential raws to one output must form a read-once tree. A top OR in such a tree separates the edges into disconnected variable classes. At a top AND every accepted two-raw flag must put a raw in each child (neither child accepts the empty input). Every vertex belongs to a flag, hence its raw alone satisfies its own child. All cross pairs would be edges, whereas this connected path is not complete bipartite. The same strictness occurs on any projective plane by gating one query-pool in the ordinary point-star decoder with its own owner, using an independent copy of the recipe. A different point on a line of that pencil has a native flag on which the old pool was one and is now zero.

**Channel interpolation.** Given the common ledger, write P_v and N_v for its positive and negative visits at a physical channel. A monotone extension on fixed bank signatures σ exists precisely if no σ(e)≤σ(n) occurs on P_v times N_v. The OR of positive conjunctions, one conjunction of the true coordinates per positive signature, proves sufficiency, with the usual convention for constants. Any common term bank plus per-channel merges can be physically costed once and used with the semantic-library cut ledger. A KW trace supplying an eligible distinguishing coordinate for every crossed pair in this product establishes existence, without a quantitative size bound. Testing only matched history pairs can miss a collision: take native flags on distinct queries x,y with maximal negatives at y,x respectively. In the bank of just those two query bits both matched pairs have a distinguishing bit, but crossed positive and negative signatures at the identical query agree.
