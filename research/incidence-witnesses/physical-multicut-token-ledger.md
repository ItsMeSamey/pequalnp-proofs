# Physical multicut accounting for flag comparisons

Partition the actual gates of a trimmed single-output binary DAG into nonempty disjoint blocks, leaving the raws outside. No restriction on block order is necessary. For block i let I_i be the distinct external gate outputs occurring as inputs inside the block, U_i the distinct raw labels read directly there, and E_i the gates in the block which are the root or have an external direct consumer. These ports denote physical connections; a wire which skips a block is absent from that block's ports.

For each exit e in E_i compare its actual value on any chosen flag with its value on any chosen legal negative. If e distinguishes (one on the flag and zero on the negative), and every external gate inlet reaching e inside the block is coordinatewise dominated on the two tests, at least one upward-discrepant raw endpoint is directly read in the block. Otherwise monotonicity inside the subcone of e would give domination there too. The allowed raw choices for this comparison are precisely the endpoints of the flag absent from the negative, optionally narrowed to raw ports reaching e. Full outgoing-cut signatures are unnecessary. Let h_i be the minimum number of raw labels meeting all these one- or two-element obligations at block i. Every singleton is forced; remove the pair obligations already met by the forced labels. The residual pairs form a bipartite graph whose vertex-cover number equals its matching number. In particular |U_i|≥h_i even though comparisons differ between blocks and ports.

Build the undirected graph of the block's gates and all its distinct formal inlets U_i and I_i, with edges from its operand occurrences; retain parallel occurrences or ignore duplicate undirected edges in the connectivity count. If c_i is its number of components and s_i its gate count, there are at least s_i+|U_i|+|I_i|−c_i edges and at most 2s_i. Every component contains an exit by trimming, so c_i≤|E_i|. For each nonroot gate v serving some external block write t(v) for the number of distinct external blocks it serves. Summing over gate-disjoint blocks yields

    s ≥ Σ_i h_i − 1 + Σ_v (t(v)−1) + Σ_i (|E_i|−c_i).

The minus one counts the root as one exit without an external consumer. All other exits occur once in their own block while each enters exactly t(v) sets I_i. If an obligation for the same raw is independently established in multiple blocks, it counts once in each because different gates read it there. A raw found to bypass many cumulative cuts may have only one actual reader, for instance one point read solely at an early gate whose output fans out. Those cumulative findings cannot substitute for block obligations.

## Alternative label routing inside a block

For each line l assign distinct native point labels on l to exits accepting their bare points and rejecting the bare complement of l. Route one token per label backwards within the block until a raw or external gate inlet. At an AND choose a fixed complement-rejecting operand for l; at an OR choose an operand accepting the token's singleton. The used gates distinguish on the same bare negative and respective singletons. Let k_l and j_l count used distinct exits and terminal inlets. In the reversed union of selected paths, outdegree-two branching occurs only at ORs. There are at least j_l−k_l branches, since each terminal needs a predecessor except where it is itself a starting site, and the excess of ends over starts cannot exceed the excess over one in the reverse outdegrees. Equivalently add k_l source edges and inspect indegrees and outdegrees in the acyclic path union. Each branch routes two distinct points and rejects the complement of l, so no other line can charge the same physical OR. Thus the block contains at least Σ_l max(0,j_l−k_l) OR gates. An external inlet can carry many tokens. As a route-independent relaxation one may lower bound j_l by the minimum cover of assigned labels using eligible singleton supports of actual incoming ports and raws. This bound may be used instead of the mixed comparison bound in chosen disjoint blocks, or combined by taking the larger in each. Adding both demands a proof that the physical charges are disjoint.
