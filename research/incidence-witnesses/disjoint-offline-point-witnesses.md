# Shared off-line point corrections of width one or two

Work with an ORIGINAL sparse-query, unrestricted-point circuit of O(N) physical gates on planes of growing order q. For a physical signal h and a line l, call a set S of one or two points outside l an off-line witness when h is true on the single query l together with S. The following conclusion concerns disjoint witnesses; it does not discount repeated occurrences by assuming any underlying gate-disjointness.

Choose point raws R independently with probability θ=(log q)/(4q), and let U be the query lines disjoint from R. Conditional on p not being in R, the q+1 lines through p have disjoint q-point remainders. Its degree d_p among U is therefore Binomial(q+1,ρ), ρ=(1−θ)^q=(1+o(1))q^(−1/4). Chernoff's bound and a union bound show uniformly for every p outside R that d_p=(1+o(1))qρ, whp. Also |R|=o(N) whp. Counting incidences gives M=|U|=(1+o(1))Nρ.

For each (h,l) with at least ceil(100N/log q) disjoint witnesses choose that many in advance. Independence across the disjoint sets bounds the probability R contains none of them by exp(−θ² ceil(100N/log q))≤q^(−6) for sufficiently large q. Similarly with ceil(24q) different singleton witnesses the failure probability is at most exp(−24qθ)=q^(−6). There are O(N²) signal/line pairs. Fix a good R for which these hitting properties hold for every eligible pair simultaneously, as well as the degree estimates. In particular every such pair has h(R+l)=1. An empty witness h(l)=1 always has this property.

Uniformly choose q−1 lines W in U, and let P* comprise points outside R and all W-lines. For a flag (p,l) of this unhit point set and U, the three tests from near-unit-feeder-intervals.md now include the common baseline R at each input. They are P=R+W+p, F=R+W+p+l, Z=R+W+l+(all P* points off l). Both negatives are legal, and F contains the flag. Each unhit p has all its d_p query neighbors in U outside W. Its unhit probability is C(M−d_p,q−1)/C(M,q−1)=exp(−1+o(1)) uniformly, so expected flag count is (1/e+o(1))Nqρ. The single-stage proof with these common pinned coordinates incurs at most 2 #OR flags with any rich witness. (No raw already pinned true can reject Z.)

For the oriented point-owned ANDs, count H(W), the distinct flags admitting a qualifying sibling h which is true also at R+l. The proof of random-query-hot-feeder-entropy.md works with line singleton support L_g(R)={l∈U:g(R+l)=1}. A traced OR rejects R+W+p, hence W must avoid its whole support in U. At threshold d=ceil(q^(1/2)) its expected charge is O(M/q): if m=|L_g(R)|≤d²/2, charge is ≤2m and the avoid probability is ≤exp(−m(q−1)/M); for m>d²/2 use N(q+1) exp(−d²(q−1)/(2M))=o(1). The raw endpoint case is unchanged: a raw point already active in R cannot reject R+W+p. Each assigned label (p,l) follows only one path. Thus E[H] log₂(E[H]/(2 #AND d))≤O(#OR·M/q), giving E[H]=O(Nqρ/log q) when s=O(N). For instance split at E[H]=Nq^(5/8); above this threshold the logarithm is Ω(log q).

It follows that for some W there are (1/e−o(1))Nqρ surviving flags for which a point-owned physical receiver is required and every qualifying sibling h has h(R+l)=0, even though it is true on F and Z. For each such receiver, h(l)=0, there are fewer than ceil(24q) singleton off-line witnesses, and there are fewer than ceil(100N/log q) pairwise disjoint witnesses of sizes one or two. The union of any maximal disjoint collection of these witnesses is a set of at most 2 ceil(100N/log q) points meeting all of them. This transversal is local to (h,l); different receivers can share physical producers arbitrarily. The argument gives no upper bound on the number of disjoint witnesses of size three or on triggers requiring multiple selected queries. In particular it does not itself exclude a linear-size circuit.
