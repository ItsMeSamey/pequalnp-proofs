# Sharp endpoint entropy for any pencil-local decision

On all same-query maximal negatives, suppose the selected point-versus-line endpoint of flag (p,l) depends only on the set A of queried lines through p, under an arbitrary deterministic legal strategy. The rule may be different at every flag and need not be monotone in A. At A empty the point is present on the maximal negative, so the line must be returned. At l∈A the line input is present, so the point must be returned. The threshold construction in all-negative-endpoint-entropy.md is such a strategy and is physically implemented by the edge formula.

Fix t=floor(sqrt(q)). Call (p,l) cheap if some subset of at most t lines through p, excluding l, causes point return on that flag. Call it expensive otherwise. At a point with d expensive flags, query an arbitrary subset of at most t of their lines. On the expensive coordinates the resulting point-return signature is exactly the chosen subset: presence forces return, and absence with this many queried neighbors cannot activate it. Therefore the number of signatures is at least Σ_{j≤min(t,d)} binom(d,j).

At a line l with d cheap flags, take any subset U of ≤floor(q/t) of their points. For each p∈U query its selected cheap witness (at most t lines through p other than l). The union uses at most q queries. Since every query meets l at exactly its chosen point, each selected point sees exactly its own witness and every other point on l sees the empty pencil. Pencil locality forces the point-return set on l to equal U. Thus the number is at least Σ_{j≤min(floor(q/t),d)} binom(d,j). These are original maximal negatives, not an enlarged query domain.

Either at least half of all flags are expensive or at least half are cheap. In the first case at least N/3 points have at least (q+1)/4 expensive flags, by averaging degrees between zero and q+1. In the second case the analogous statement holds for cheap flags at lines. For sufficiently large q these ≥N/3 sites have log-support Ω(sqrt(q) log q), from the binomial coefficient at floor(sqrt(q)) on at least (q+1)/4 coordinates. The threshold strategy gives O(sqrt(q) log q) at every site, so the order of the worst-site minimum is sharp across ALL pencil-local strategies.

Arbitrary negative-side gate decisions can inspect the entire query set. The simultaneous witness argument uses pencil locality exactly once, to prevent the witnesses selected at other points of l from changing the local decision. No physical gate lower bound follows from endpoint support alone.
