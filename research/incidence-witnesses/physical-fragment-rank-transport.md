# Physical fragments and local Karchmer–Wigderson correlations

Let a binary monotone scalar circuit of s gates meet the original incidence promise. Trim it. For each flag e=(p,l), its positive input is the singleton pair, and its tested negative S_e takes the q other points of l and the q other lines through p. These sets are independent. Run a deterministic memoryless monotone KW traversal from the output on each positive e and negative S_f: the OR decision depends only on the positive flag and current gate and selects a true child; the AND decision depends only on f and the current gate and selects a false child. A traversal ends at a raw true on e and false on S_f. Define M_g(e,f) as 1 if that pair's actual traversal visits physical g.

Fix the output and each internal gate having at least two outgoing physical pin occurrences as fragment roots. Cutting uses of these shared signals leaves tree fragments. For every physical pin reading raw v there is one fragment root b and a unique downward path from b in its fragment to that pin. Its actual reached-pair matrix is M_b restricted by a row mask and a column mask along the path, because every decision constrains one party only. This remains true for shared roots with many possible incoming histories. Count tied pins independently. Let r(v,b) be the real rank of M_b restricted to the square indexed on both axes by the flags at v. The rank of the pin's corresponding square is at most r(v,b).

All pin receipts returning v are disjoint pointwise and sum to its return indicator. On the star square, off-diagonal entries are one: the other endpoint of e belongs to S_f if e≠f share v. A diagonal entry is one exactly when v was the chosen output on (e,S_e). If t_v star diagonals are zero, the rank is t_v+1 if there is an unaffected diagonal, otherwise q+1 (J−I is nonsingular). Thus the sum of these ranks over all v equals E plus the number of vertices receiving at least one diagonal output, with E=N(q+1). The receiving vertices cover the flags; at least N are needed. Consequently

N(q+2) ≤ sum over original raw pin occurrences v→h of r(v,b(h)).

Every physical pin appears once, regardless of how many queried cuts it bypasses or how often a raw reappears in unfolded proofs. If all those ranks are ≤100 for even one allowed set of tie decisions, s≥N(q+2)/100−1 since there are at most s+1 raw pins. This implies the target for q>100. A formula has one root fragment and rank one. High local ranks are still possible for genuinely shared mixed circuits; there is no rank cap asserted in general. This version uses the physical traversal's own reached-pair matrices; sharing-signatures.md instead routes to a first separating fragment and bounds ranks by the number of distinct shared-gate evaluations.

**Exact gate-by-gate identity.** On the full E×E test matrix, let r_g=rank(M_g). For each of the two reversed pins at g, the reached-pair matrix is obtained by a row or column mask; write ranks r_g0,r_g1 for them. The split surplus d_g=r_g0+r_g1−r_g lies between 0 and r_g. At each nonroot physical node u (including raw nodes) define merging loss m_u as the sum of its incoming pin ranks minus rank(M_u); this is nonnegative by disjointness and rank subadditivity. Canceling every physical pin in a topological sweep gives sum_raw rank(M_v) = 1 + sum_g d_g − sum_nonroot m_u. In particular the split surpluses must total at least N(q+2)−1 plus the merging losses. This is a single conservation law across the whole DAG; an edge crossing several frontiers is never charged repeatedly. Both raw-local and global ranks are semantic history measures; their size is not a count of physical gates.
