# Smaller negative-transversal certificates and exceptional flag repair

In a trimmed size-minimum scalar incidence DAG let G be an antichain of k≥1 distinct shared nonroot gates with at least two distinct direct consumers apiece. Write f for their number of distinct direct consumers, necessarily at least k. Pin all outgoing uses to zero and denote the actually lost native flags by L. For each i assume that on every promised independent input its activation implies that the selected queries meet a prescribed bank B_i. No disjointness among banks or necessity of meeting a bank on a positive input is assumed.

Suppose J⊆G has j≥1 indices whose banks have transversal number greater than the query cap q. An empty bank is allowed with no transversal possible. Their conjunction rejects every independent input. Let E_J consist of the flags of L where at least one member of J was zero. Write v(E_J) for the minimum vertex-cover size in the bipartite graph of these flags. Then

    f + k + 𝟙[f = k] ≤ 2j + |E_J| + v(E_J).

Indeed the retained original gates of J can feed their conjunction, costing j−1 gates, merged with the zero-pin residual by one OR. Restore E_J using v stars by assigning each edge to a cover endpoint. A star with a assigned edges takes a gates (a−1 raw ORs and one central AND), and merging the v outputs with the current result takes v further ORs. Thus the additions cost at most j+|E_J|+v(E_J), including the empty-exception case. All f original consumers disappear and all k−j omitted selected gates disappear independently because none can feed the retained J cones. At f=k every consumer has two distinct selected operands and folds to zero. At least one such physical consumer is not the original root (the singleton k case cannot attain equality); a downstream direct successor is outside the direct consumers and outside all protected cones and also disappears. No counted successor can be a member of G by incomparability. The corrected circuit accepts every flag and rejects every promised negative. The inequality follows by minimality.

If all B_i have size ≤r and the entire bank family has transversal number greater than q, a certificate with j≤binom(q+r,r) exists. Choose an inclusion-minimal subfamily. For each of its indices i, a transversal H_i of the other banks with at most q elements must avoid B_i. For i≠j we have B_i intersect H_j nonempty. In a uniform random ordering of the finite query universe, the events that every member of B_i precedes every member of H_i are pairwise disjoint: the two crossed intersections would have to precede each other. Each such event has probability 1/binom(|B_i|+|H_i|,|B_i|)≥1/binom(q+r,r). This is the set-pairs inequality. The certificate cardinality is sharp for all r-subsets of q+r labels whenever these fit the query universe: deleting one r-subset leaves its complement as a q-transversal. With r=2, at most (q+1)(q+2)/2 gates need retention.

A sampling variant handles wide banks. Assume every q-set misses at least γk banks, 0<γ≤1. For h independent uniform draws from G the probability that the sampled banks have a q-transversal is at most η=binom(N,q)(1−γ)^h. Every smaller transversal extends to size q. For each e∈L let z_e denote the fraction of all G that were zero on e. Its probability of requiring repair is 1−(1−z_e)^h. If η<1, conditioning on a sampled family with no q-transversal gives some such family for which

    |E_J| ≤ (Σ_{e ∈ L} [1 − (1 − z_e)^h]) / (1 − η),    |J| ≤ h.

Insert these quantities in the physical inequality, using v(E_J)≤|E_J| if no sharper information is available. This variant prices the actual loss after one common cut and needs no independence between the positive exceptions and the bank tests.
