# Earlier mixed receiver witnesses on a chain of query backgrounds

Let a monotone binary circuit with s=O(N) gates compute plane incidence on arbitrary point sets and query sets of at most q lines, where N=q²+q+1. Select a uniformly random ordered list of q−1 distinct lines; write W for its full set and X for the points off every line of W. Put T=⌊(log q)^(1/3)⌋ and d=⌊(q−1)/(T+1)⌋. At stage i=1,…,T take the first (i−1)d lines as V and the first id as W_i. Rounding and the unused tail do not affect the estimates. Use natural logarithms for sampling bounds.

For each p in X, each of its q+1 incident lines l is outside W. The stage tests are W_i+p+l (positive), W_i+p (negative), and W_i+l plus all points of X off l (negative). Use the output trace in near-unit-feeder-intervals.md. At most 2s distinct flags can have a rich traced feeder anywhere on the chain; discarding their entire histories costs at most 2sT flag-stages. Assign each remaining flag at each stage to one oriented physical AND with children g,h: g has singleton point support exactly {p} on X at W_i, h(W_i+p)=0, and h(W_i+p+l)=1. The same orientation serves at most one point across the chain, by monotonicity of g's supports. All counts below are of assigned flags, even if other orientations also qualify.

Discard a flag-stage if its assigned h(V+p+l)=0. For any fixed orientation and flag, the increasing prefix makes the 0-to-1 transition of h on that flag possible in only one of the disjoint stage increments. Thus at most 2s(q+1) such assignments are lost over the chain.

Next discard assignments with h(V+l)=1. Conditional on the fixed prefix V, its increment is uniform among d-subsets of the N−|V| other lines. Apply the prefix-code proof of earlier-query-hot-feeder-entropy.md (SHA-256 f64bc2d847ccd3f2e776f64df58c60b774cb8ba376d02323092e413d1371afd9) with singleton support evaluated at V and threshold D=⌈q^(2/3)⌉. Only the increment is required to be random: every charged OR rejects W_i+p and hence its shifted line support avoids that increment. Assignment and X may depend on the later random lines, since the charge bound applies simultaneously to every allowed assignment given the increment. Per OR the expected charge is O(N/d)+N(q+1)exp(−D²d/(2N))=O(N/d). If H is the number of such assigned flags at this stage, Jensen gives E[H] log₂(E[H]/(2sD))≤O(sN/d), with H=0 when s=0. Splitting at s q^(5/6) yields E[H]=O(s[q^(5/6)+N/(d log q)]). This is uniform over prefixes.

The expected initial total is (1/e+o(1))TN(q+1), by the untouched-point probability for W. The three discarded counts are o(TNq) for s=O(N). Moreover |X|=(1/e+o(1))N with probability tending to one by common-near-unit-extension.md, and Markov’s inequality makes the discarded fraction o(1) with probability tending to one. Thus (1/e−o(1))TNq assignments remain. On each, h(V+p+l)=1 while h(V+l)=h(V+p)=0; the latter follows from h(W_i+p)=0. This is a mixed response relative to V, not necessarily a mixed response on the empty query background.

For the same experiment, with probability tending to one, simultaneously for every stage, gate h and point p with h(W_i+p)=0, the set
     
    {l outside V : h(V+p+l)=1}

has size at most 10(N/d)log q. Conditional on V this set is fixed and the increment must avoid all its members. If its size exceeds the threshold, avoidance has probability at most exp(−10 log q). A union bound over O(sNT) choices suffices. This remains valid for a gate and point selected after seeing the whole chain.

Select a stage with Ω(Nq) retained assignments. They are distinct flags at that stage, assigned among at most 2s orientations. For a sufficiently small constant η>0 depending only on the bound on s/N, orientations serving fewer than ηq flags account for fewer than half these assignments. Each remaining orientation owns one point and serves Ω(q) distinct incident lines inside a set of O(q log^(4/3)q) candidate labels. Since each point accounts for at most q+1 distinct flags, a positive fraction of all points have such an orientation. This is a necessary profile for a linear-size original-promise circuit; it does not itself rule one out.
