# Forcing a lower bound on the number of wide shared gates

Work in the bilaterally hoisted circuit on the q-1 capped sample; its sole-use private ANDs are necessary for the initial h400 shared-credit cap. Suppose q=401, N=161203, and size s<=646424. Let M be the count of distinct shared internal gates with raw zero-certificate width at most w. The h400 flag argument in shared-zero-certificates.md gives M<=3010 when w=8, and M<=2964 when w=2.

Choose an inclusion-minimal certificate of at most w raw variables for each of the M gates. Fix all D distinct raw variables in the union to zero. The restriction loses at most 402D incidence flags, or D units of flags divided by 402. It kills at least I=max(M,D-M) original gates. Indeed choose a proof of each zero by tracing both operands at a zero OR and one at a zero AND, evaluating all other raw inputs to one. By minimality each root uses every raw variable in its certificate. All internal gates of the union of proofs vanish. This union has at most M connected components, D raw vertices and at most two incoming wires per internal gate, so it contains at least D-M internal gates as well as the M roots.

After eliminating those gates and folding constants, the residual number of shared gates is at most the number of original shared gates of width >w. Removing a shared constant before unary bypasses ensures strict shared-count decrease; bypasses cannot increase the count. Apply size-sharing-interpolation.md to the residual good flags. With T further eliminations and A leaves in their source cones, available final formula leaves are at most s-I+T+1-A. The edge unit loss from the source steps is at most C=A/8+1206T and the maximum extra positive pins on a shore is at most floor(C/804). Thus required leaves are at least (402-floor(C/804))(N-D-C). For the parameters below the first factor stays at least 200. After replacing it by 200, the margin over available leaves decreases as A increases, so it suffices to compare at A=s-I+T, where only one leaf is budgeted.

The worst D+C is bounded by substituting the largest D=wM, then the largest M: for fixed M the saving I/8 grows at slope at most 1/8 in D, and at D=wM the net effect grows with M. Taking T=48,w=8,M=3010 leaves at least 1059.75 edge units (D=24080,I=21070); taking T=62,w=2,M=2964 leaves at least 62.75 (D=5928,I=2964). Both give contradictions. Hence at least 49 original shared gates have minimum raw zero-certificate width >8, and at least 63 have width >2. These counts apply to the hoisted architecture; the unrestricted q401 gate lower bound remains open.

A separate saturation obstruction applies on the arbitrary-point, at-most-two-line domain. To accept every positive flag and reject a fixed maximal negative N_T, a monotone proxy must depend essentially on every point in the union of T and on each line outside T. For p on a selected line compare flag (p,t) to input t alone; for m outside T take a point p on m outside the selected union and compare (p,m) to p alone. Both smaller inputs are dominated by N_T. Consequently a binary proxy needs at least |union T|+N-|T|-1 gates. A single OR of these raw coordinates attains the exact lower bound. This does not rule out sharing across proxies or targeting only some flags.

The finite lower counts also apply to the original sparse circuit: the literal unmerged bilateral hoist introduces no shared gates, preserves or decreases retained gates' numbers of outgoing occurrences and weakly lowers each retained internal function. Thus every original zero certificate still works after hoisting. See near-linear-wide-sharing.md for the detailed transfer.
