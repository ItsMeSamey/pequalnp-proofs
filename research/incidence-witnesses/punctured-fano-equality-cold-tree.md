# Cold merger types in a 17-gate dense parent

Assume a parent accepts all flags, rejects at least twenty of the twenty-one maximal negatives, and uses exactly seventeen gates. The first-fork proof in punctured-fano-almost-full-forks.md forces exactly six hot raws, all on one shore, each with its own first branching wire. If a hot x and cold y share a first branch, take a retained maximum containing x and a neighbor of y. The shared first branching wire is already one from x, so adding y leaves the output unchanged despite forming a flag. For nonincident opposite-shore ordered pairs the numbers of witnessing maxima are two (16 pairs) or three (24 pairs). For incident pairs use the rejected singleton x and its positive extension by y. Thus no cold raw reaches a first branching wire shared with a hot raw. The six distinct first forks exhaust all six surplus output occurrences. Every cold site reaches the output along a unique path.

The six cold paths form a tree toward the output, with five binary mergers after suppressing single-child nodes. At a merger with two child sets S,T of cold leaves, choose s in S, t in T. If they share a hot neighbor p, the singleton p is rejected and p+s and p+t are both accepted. Adding s or t changes only its own operand at the merger; the unique path property forces the merger to switch in either case. An AND would require both baseline operands already true and could not switch. Thus an AND merger requires that every cross pair of leaves have disjoint neighbor sets. Exactly one unordered pair per shore has disjoint neighborhoods (indices 2,3 on the point side, 8,10 on the line side in the punctured-Fano checker). The only possible AND merger joins those two individual leaves. At least four of the five physical mergers are OR. Single-child gates suppressed for this count may use either operation.
