# Punctured-Fano circuits with pure-shore preprocessing require 21 gates

Delete the incident point labelled 1 and line labelled 2 from the binary Fano incidence matrix, using nonzero three-bit vectors with mod-two dot product zero for incidence. Six inputs remain per shore, with sixteen flags, two stars of size two and four stars of size three on each shore. Consider circuits that first use arbitrary binary AND/OR gates and fanout separately on each shore, then take products of a point-only and a line-only signal, and OR the products. Products need not have a common orientation.

Every product contributing a flag has singleton acceptance sets on its two shores forming an incidence rectangle. The nonincident one-point/one-line negatives suffice for this assertion. Because the graph is C4-free, one set is a singleton. Replace that factor by the raw center: its value can only decrease on general inputs and it preserves every originally accepted flag of this product. Remove products serving no flags. Group all products assigned to a given center by ORing their opposite factors before taking the product. This does not increase the cost, even when a pure preprocessing gate still has other uses: for k products now assigned one center, k−1 new payload ORs suffice while k−1 product gates and k−1 final joining slots disappear. A single-output OR network covering t distinct productive products originally requires at least t−1 slots.

For each remaining center let e_i be the size of its opposite-shore singleton acceptance set. Each such set is a nonempty subset of that center's star. The number of distinct pure gates on its payload shore is at least the sum of e_i−1 over those centers. To prove this finite claim even with arbitrary meet, join and reuse, first observe that all requested masks of size two or three need distinct physical gates, since two stars have at most one common vertex. Let t be the number of requested triples. It remains to force t additional gates on that shore. Give away all two-bit masks contained in every star other than those t triples. Also give away the six raw masks and allow each of the t triple outputs to be adjoined freely whenever it is the meet or join of two available masks. Search through at most t−1 further meet/join adjunctions, closing under all freely obtainable requested triples each time. This relaxation includes any circuit with fewer than the claimed gate count: a repeated, empty or already free mask cannot create any new singleton capability. For t=1,2,3,4 the maximum numbers of obtainable specified triples are 0,1,2,3 respectively. With t=0 distinctness alone suffices. The checker repeats this finite computation on both shores; no unrestricted synthesis solver is used.

If c centers appear, the total gate count is at least sum(e_i−1)+c+(c−1) = sum(e_i)+c−1. The e_i cover all sixteen flags. The centers form a vertex cover and a verified six-edge matching forces c≥6. Thus 21 gates are required, attained by the ordinary row-star circuit (ten payload ORs, six ANDs and five joins). Any 20-gate witness would have to go outside this separated-product architecture. This conclusion holds already on singleton flags and nonincident singleton pairs; it does not decide the unrestricted 20-gate problem on the larger promised sample.
