# Two-leaf witnesses in sparse incidence circuits

Let G be a simple bipartite graph with no four-cycle. Let C be a scalar monotone binary circuit on vertex variables, correct on all assignments of weight at most two for the predicate that the selected vertices contain an edge. Propagate constants before counting witnesses. Let F be the set of edges with an accepting parse using exactly two variable occurrences. Then the gate count is at least |F|.

For each wire v, form S(v), the set of variables reachable down from v along OR gates alone. Input wires have their own singleton, and AND outputs have empty S. Every two-leaf parse has exactly one AND gate g, connected by ORs to the output. Conversely, every pair in S(left(g)) times S(right(g)) forces acceptance through g. Hence if both sets are nonempty they are disjoint (singletons are rejected), and every cross pair is an edge. Bipartiteness makes the sets opposite-sided, and four-cycle freeness implies at least one is a singleton. Assign each such g to the vertex of its singleton side, choosing either side for singleton by singleton. The sets on the other side are called leaf sets.

At each vertex x, let k be its number of assigned ANDs, and let U be the union of their leaf sets. Each parent producing a leaf set has an OR-only cone. Discard branches with empty S. Identify every wire whose S is a singleton with the corresponding input vertex and stop descent there. What remains is a directed acyclic graph containing all |U| input vertices and m internal nodes with |S| at least two. Every vertex is reachable downward from one of at most k roots. The underlying graph has at most k connected components and at most 2m edges. Hence |U|+m−k ≤ 2m. Each internal node belongs to at most one account. Its support contains two different neighbors of that center. Centers on the same side cannot have two common neighbors, and centers on opposite sides have disjoint neighborhoods. The charged nodes are ORs, disjoint from the assigned ANDs. Summation gives s ≥ sum_x |U_x| ≥ |F|.

In a syntactically multilinear circuit every edge has such a parse: children of AND gates depend on disjoint variable sets, so a parse contains no repeated variable. A parse accepting a two-variable edge uses both variables because the singleton inputs are rejected. Thus s ≥ |E| for this circuit class.

The sparse incidence promise implies the weight-two assumptions for q ≥ 2. For arbitrary circuits at least |E|−s edges require a repeated variable in every accepting parse. The witness statistic depends on the representation, so this does not resolve the general bound.

A related multilinear-circuit lower bound appears in [Jukna, Remark 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.3065#page=19). The argument here counts the edges that admit a two-leaf parse even when other edges require repeated variables.
