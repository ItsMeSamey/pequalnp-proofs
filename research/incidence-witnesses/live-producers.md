# Removing sharing through components with private variables

Use the producer-rooted read-once components of ../anchored/README.md. A live variable v is the sole local ordinary leaf of a nonoutput component whose exit evaluates to one on v alone. All other variables are called nonlive; let H be their count. Only nonempty producers with live variables can have a nonzero singleton support, and then that support is precisely their own variable. Every incidence neighbor of a local variable is exterior, as established by the OR-LCA lemma.

The following construction retains internal sharing only through producer components without ordinary local variables. Replace the exit supplied to the other components as follows.
* A live producer supplies its raw ordinary variable.
* Any other nonempty producer supplies zero.
* Empty producers still supply their original formula evaluated on the replaced entering signals.

The replacements are monotone lower approximations on every input: if v is selected, its live producer's exit is one, and if v is absent the replacement is zero. For each nonempty nonoutput component add a detector: evaluate its local formula at these replaced inlets with all its own ordinary variables set to zero. The scalar result is the OR of all detectors and the substituted output component with all its own ordinary variables set to zero. The resulting circuit takes only live variables. Each raw leaf occurrence is an original port emitted by a live producer. Nonempty producers never feed their computed detector values into another component, so only empty producer exits can fan out among the internal gates.

On any positive incidence whose two endpoints are live, the two owning producers' replacements are exact because their local variables are selected. If any other nonempty producer's actual bit differs from its replacement, choose the first in topological order. Its replacement is zero and its actual value is one, and no local variable of that producer is selected. All its replaced inlets equal the actual inlets (empty computations are unchanged on matching predecessors), so its detector accepts. Without such a producer, all inlets reaching the output are exact. All output-local variables are nonlive and therefore unselected on this assignment, so the output computation accepts.

Consider any live edge-free input (P,T) with at most q−1 entries on each side. The substituted output is zero by lower approximation and correctness. For a detector in C, remove all C-local variables from (P,T), an operation that leaves its inlets unchanged: no local variable influences an entering port. The detector is bounded by the actual exit on the resulting input. Choose any locally owned variable v. All q+1 neighbors are exterior and there is a neighbor y that avoids all the remaining selected vertices of v's side, because v was removed, each other same-side vertex shares at most one neighbor with v, and at most q−1 were selected. Augment the input by y. It is still edge-free, and it has at most q lines. If the exit of C were one, this legal negative would dominate the accepted (v,y) on the exit and every outside input, which is impossible. Thus every detector vanishes on the required negatives.

## Counting with bounded sharing of empty components

Let F be the total number of outgoing port occurrences of live producers; then F≤(2N−H)+e=s+1−H. Let M≥1 bound the number of directed port paths from the exit of each empty producer until their first entrance into a nonempty component or the output component. Parallel port uses count separately. All such target components are terminals. If there are no empty producers, take M=1. The restriction does not bound the number of paths or uses of nonempty producers. In particular arbitrarily many internal nonempty gates may each have unbounded fanout.

Unfold only the empty producer components. Each live raw port entering a terminal contributes at most one leaf occurrence. A live raw port entering an empty producer component contributes at most M leaf occurrences because that component is a tree and its exit has at most M routed paths to terminal formulas. Thus the detector OR, after constants are folded, is a formula with at most MF ordinary leaves.

Also (q+1)H≤MF. Each nonlive variable has a distinguished port-only AND sibling that accepts every one of its q+1 neighbor singletons (see ../anchored/weighted.md, including its inactive-owner extension). They are disjoint subtrees within the terminal components. Trace these selected siblings down through empty producers, cutting at every nonempty producer. Each cut nonempty port has singleton weight either zero or one, and only live producers can contribute one. Every unfolded empty subtree obeys subadditivity of singleton support. The total of all sibling weights is therefore bounded by the number of unfolded live leaves, at most MF by the very same path counting. This calculation uses the unpruned terminal forest, so it does not require every such sibling to survive pruning in the detector OR.

Start with any perfect matching of the regular bipartite incidence graph; it exists by Hall's theorem. Remove its edges, then remove all nonlive vertices. At least Nq−(q+1)H test edges remain in a bipartite graph J of maximum degree q. Although J need not be induced, the proper-neighborhood negative for each e=(p,l), with points Adj_J(l) minus p and lines Adj_J(p) minus l, is independent even in the original plane. Any cross incidence would complete a 4-cycle through e. Both sides have at most q−1 variables. Thus the detector formula passes every positive and every proper-neighborhood test required for J.

The formula must have at least |E(J)| leaves by the Karchmer–Wigderson proper-neighborhood rank argument from formula-leaves.md. For clarity, at each v the output-v matrix on its incident edge square is all ones except t_v diagonal zeros, where t_v counts edges whose diagonal return is the other endpoint. For degree ≥2 its rank is at least t_v. For degree one, at least one occurrence is required by essentiality (positive edge vs rejected opposite singleton), so it still has ≥t_v leaves. This covers arbitrary degree profiles. The ranks are bounded by the number of occurrences since each occurrence traces a disjoint rectangle. Summing t_v gives |E(J)|.

Consequently Nq ≤ 2MF ≤ 2M(s+1−H). In particular when H≥1 and M≤50 the desired bound Nq≤100s holds at every order. If H=0 the stronger formula bound Nq≤MF≤M(s+1) applies, implying the same conclusion for M≤50 and s≥1. With no empty producers we have Nq≤2(s+1−H); thus arbitrary internal sharing through components owning ordinary variables still costs at least Nq/2−1 gates.

## Sparse singleton supports and negative tests without a path bound

There is another sufficient condition with unrestricted depth and fanout. For every empty producer g let S_g be its singleton support on ordinary inputs. Assume |S_g|≤r and, for each proper-neighborhood negative D_e used for J above, g rejects D_e with all members of S_g removed. There are only polynomially many tests on the supplied circuit. It is sufficient as a structural condition that g rejects every edge-free mask disjoint from S_g with at most q−1 selected variables per side.

Use the same detectors for nonempty components. Replace every incoming producer port, including an empty one, by the disjunction of its singleton support restricted to live variables. Each replacement is below the original signal on every assignment, by monotonicity. Add a detector for each empty producer g as well, evaluating its local formula on these disjunctions with variables of S_g omitted from every disjunction within that detector. If the original output component is empty, keep its ordinary substituted output, without suppressing any support there.

On a live positive pair, if a true producer output is missed by its substituted singleton disjunction, neither endpoint lies in its support. At the first such producer all entering values are exact. If empty, suppressing S_g therefore does not change that positive assignment. If nonempty, both endpoints are exterior exactly as before. Thus the missed positive is detected, or the substituted output itself accepts it. On any D_e an empty g's detector is at most the original g evaluated on D_e minus S_g, hence zero by hypothesis. Nonempty detectors and the original output reject as before.

Write W for the sum of |S_g| over all producer port occurrences, now measured on all ordinary variables. The flattened detector OR is a formula with at most W ordinary leaves, because each port expands into its disjunction independently, and each port belongs to just one component detector. Also (q+1)H≤W: the disjoint chosen sibling subtrees for the H nonlive vertices need that much total singleton weight from their entering port occurrences. There are at most 2e port occurrences, every nonempty producer has support size ≤1, and the empty-producer bound is r. For r≥1, W≤2re.

Removing the perfect matching before the H nonlive vertices gives the improved |E(J)|≥Nq−qH. The same KW bound therefore yields

    Nq ≤ W+qH ≤ (2−1/(q+1))W ≤ (4−2/(q+1))r e.

This proves the target for r≤25 irrespective of how many empty producer gates occur or how often they are used. The condition tests the zero restrictions of D_e; small singleton supports alone do not establish it.

The primary correctness checker is check_live.py. A fixture gives each nonzero-index point a shared internal proxy of the form x_i OR (x_0 AND y_l), where (0,l) is incident. Supplying an independent copy of the extra pair to each proxy creates unboundedly many nonempty internal producers and no shared empty producer. Removing the original (0,l) output term ensures the reduced circuit needs to catch that positive through a detector. Sharing the same extra pair tests nontrivial empty-producer fanout too. All live positive flags and every live edge-free combination of up to q−1 points and q−1 lines were tested at orders two and three.

## A contamination accounting inequality with no soundness hypothesis

Flatten all ports into singleton-support disjunctions as in the preceding section. A nonempty detector and the (possibly empty) original output are mandatory. For every other empty producer g define its optional detector with S_g suppressed. On the live graph J trimmed by a perfect matching, let a_g count the test edges on which this optional detector accepts, and let b_g count the proper-neighborhood negative tests D_e on which it accepts. These are evaluations of the flattened detector itself, so earlier mixed false positives cannot be counted twice merely by propagating along a path. Let w_g be its formula's raw variable leaf count after suppression and constant folding, and w_base the total raw leaf count of the mandatory detectors/output. All these quantities can be computed by evaluating the flattened local formulas directly; there are polynomially many tests.

Choose any subset U of the optional detectors, and keep them along with the mandatory ones. If any positive test edge is missed, look at the first producer whose actual one on that input was replaced by zero. Its two endpoints lie outside S_g, its replaced inputs were exact, and its local detector necessarily accepted. It must therefore have been an omitted optional producer. The number of lost positive flags is at most sum_{g outside U} a_g. A negative test can fail only because a kept optional detector accepts, spoiling at most sum_{g in U} b_g flags. Discard all spoiled or lost flags to get J'. For every surviving flag its new proper-neighborhood negative only shrinks from its old D_e. The detectors remain monotone and reject that smaller mask. They all reject singletons: at every optional detector its own singleton support is suppressed, and at every mandatory detector the earlier domination lemma applies.

The rank argument now gives the unconditional bound

    Nq ≤ qH + w_base + sum_empty g min(a_g, b_g+w_g).

Indeed one may independently omit each optional g at cost a_g or retain it at cost b_g+w_g. One can further replace the sums counting lost/spoiled flags by the exact cardinality of their union. This inequality measures first-missed edge capacity and tested negative contamination without asserting either quantity is small for general circuits. In particular, a purely point- or purely line-dependent empty producer has a_g=0 and requires no optional detector at all.

The optional costs also yield a dirty bounded-width subclass without any negative-test assumption. Suppose empty producer supports have size at most r≥1 and each empty producer component has at most b entering port occurrences. Every optional detector then has raw support size at most w_g≤br. It can accept an uncovered positive edge only if both its endpoints occur as raw leaves: it rejects singletons by its own support suppression. Its accepted edges induce a four-cycle-free graph on at most w_g nonisolated vertices. A graph on m vertices with no four-cycle has at most m(1+sqrt(4m−3))/4 edges when m≥1: sum binomial(degree,2) is at most binomial(m,2), then apply the quadratic mean bound to the sum of squared degrees. Thus with D=max(1,(1+sqrt(4br−3))/4) we have a_g≤D w_g. Since all raw occurrence budgets come from W≤2re and qH≤qW/(q+1), omission of every optional detector gives

    Nq ≤ (D+q/(q+1))W ≤ 2r(D+1)e.

In particular r=1 and b≤1024 imply Nq<35s, regardless of empty-gate depth, fanout, number, or contamination. For fixed r, any b=o(q²) forces superlinear size. The bounded-width hypothesis is imposed on the local component before expanding the ports, rather than on an unfolded tree.
