# Refactoring shared signals in a size-minimal incidence circuit

Assume binary monotone gates and fix a downward-closed sparse-input domain on which the target is exactly incidence. It suffices to preserve all positive flags and all maximal negative inputs. On a chosen negative test family alone, conclusions below concern only its downward closure. Replace gates by constants by bypassing their consumers and folding constants. Count outgoing wire occurrences after normalizing repeated inputs.

**Shared signals must be impure and incomplete.** If a nonoutput shared wire h is zero on all maximal negatives, replace all of its outgoing occurrences by zero, retain the circuit producing h and append h by an OR at the output. On a flag with h=0 nothing changes, while on one with h=1 the new output accepts directly. On negatives h is zero. Each of the f distinct consumers loses its gate during constant propagation, while restoring h costs at most one gate. Hence a size-minimal circuit cannot have such an h with f≥2. Dually, if h is true on every flag, replace its uses by one and append h using AND. Original negatives with h=0 now reject directly and those with h=1 retain their original value. Therefore every shared signal in a size-minimal circuit must accept some negative and reject some flag.

**Actual output obligations.** Form downstream cofactors C₀ and C₁ by replacing all outgoing uses of h by the corresponding constant. A candidate monotone replacement h′ whose definition preserves acyclicity works exactly when it is one on the flags rejected by C₀ and zero on the maximal negatives accepted by C₁. Indeed the output lies between C₀ and C₁ and, when they differ, equals the substituted bit. This compares h′ to the required output behavior rather than to the full truth function of h. The cost comparison uses every dropped now-dead gate as well as every added gate. Different wire replacements must generally be evaluated jointly or applied sequentially with recomputed obligations.

**Zeroing an entire cut.** After setting any chosen set of internal gates to zero, fold constants and prune unreachable logic. Let the saved gate count be Δ and the graph of lost positive flags be J. Negative outputs stay zero. An OR of oriented incident stars restores J and cannot accept any independent input. Assign each flag to a covering endpoint in a minimum vertex cover of J; this needs at most |E(J)|+ν(J) leaves by bipartite matching duality. Including the final output OR costs at most that same number of gates, and costs zero if J is empty. Thus every minimum-size circuit obeys Δ≤|E(J)|+ν(J) for every cut. Existing residual wires may make a repair cheaper. This prices the whole discarded cone against actual lost flags, including losses from simultaneous upstream deletions, with no requirement that individual witness cones be disjoint.

**Closed local regions.** Keep external entering wires, including h, as shared inputs. Suppose all internal fanout of a region goes to its designated exits g₁,…,g_b and no other outside outputs. Simultaneously compute the two cofactors of every exit at h=0 and h=1. Fold constants and reuse identical surviving gates across the two circuits. If this needs K gates, then every original exit can be reconstructed as g_j(0) OR [h AND g_j(1)]. This needs at most K+2b gates total by monotonicity. For a single exit with g(1)=1 it uses just g(0) OR h. If the original region contained f distinct direct consumers of h, restricting h to zero removed all f gates, so the new computation saves at least f−1. The dual statement holds for g(0)=0. Wires leaking outside the region must be counted as exits.

For the five-gate stages in irredundant-wide-rank-chain.md choose h=F_(i−1). Each stage has one exit and its formal one-cofactor is identically one. Its zero-cofactor is x_i AND (a_i OR b_i), computable with two gates. Reconstruction costs just three gates in total, a saving of two per stage despite the failure of every individual operand-forwarding test. No unrestricted lower bound follows from these refactorings alone.
