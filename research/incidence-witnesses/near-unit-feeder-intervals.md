# Distinct rich OR costs across a chain of near-single-query negatives

Use a monotone binary circuit correct on the ORIGINAL sparse-query, arbitrary-point tests, or one independently verified correct on every background below. Correctness on singleton flags and a sparse point promise alone does not suffice. Let Q* be any at most q−1 query lines, P* any set of points outside their union, and W₀⊆⋯⊆W_h⊆Q* an arbitrary chain. Consider the flags (p,l) with p∈P* and l outside Q*. At stage i use F=W_i+p+l and the two negatives P=W_i+p and Z=W_i+l+(all P* points off l). The original promise gives these values: Z is independent and has at most q lines; P is independent; F contains a flag. Evaluate all tests in the same physical DAG.

Trace back from the output preserving F=1, P=Z=0 for the current wire. At an OR descend to a child true at F, which is necessarily false at both negatives. At an AND descend to a child false at both if there is one. Otherwise its children have opposite negative evaluations: orient them g,h with g(P)=1,g(Z)=0 and h(P)=0,h(Z)=1. Both are true at F. The descent terminates this way since every raw active at F occurs at one of the two negatives. Define A_g(i)={u∈P*:g(W_i+u)=1}. Then p∈A_g(i)⊆l. Call the feeder rich if |A_g(i)|≥2.

For a rich feeder trace farther backward through wires false on Z and true at W_i+p. At an AND choose a child false at Z, which still accepts all singleton points of its parent on this background. At an OR choose a child accepting W_i+p; it too rejects Z. A raw at the end has at most one singleton point in this trace (a raw already active at W_i cannot reject Z). At some step a rich parent becomes a thin child whose support is exactly {p}. The step is an OR, as a chosen AND child contains its parent's support. Charge its physical operand slot. For a fixed OR slot the child singleton identity cannot change across the chain: its supports A_child(i) only increase. When the parent is rich and rejects Z its support is contained in l. At any later qualifying time the parent retains those same two or more points, which determine the very same line. Thus even choosing different stages for different flags, a single physical OR slot is charged by at most ONE DISTINCT (p,l).

It follows that at most twice the number of OR gates distinct flags have a rich witness at ANY stage. Every other flag must be served at EVERY stage by an oriented physical AND g,h satisfying

    A_g(i)={p}, g(Z)=0, h(P)=0, h(Z)=1, h(F)=1.

These conditions are necessary for the extracted receiver, but a gate satisfying them need not occur in an output trace. A fixed orientation is eligible for at most one point p on the whole chain. For a fixed flag its eligible stages form an interval, because each original test input increases along the chain (the P* points off l are fixed), each gate value is monotone, and the condition A_g(i)={p} is an intersection of monotone lower and upper restrictions. For every flag without a rich witness those intervals cover the entire chain. This counts distinct physical OR slots across all stages together, including in mixed, reconvergent DAGs.

In the single-stage case W=∅, P* all points, if every oriented singleton-specific AND covers at most w incident lines of its unique point, then N(q+1)≤2 #OR + 2w #AND. This is a conditional consequence. The unrestricted estimate requires additional control of these receiver intervals; a singleton feeder can serve many geometric lines.
