# A dominating theta restriction and its excess ledger

Choose any nonincident point x and line Y. Retain x,Y and their complete neighborhoods: all q+1 lines through x and all q+1 points of Y. Each retained non-pole line meets Y at exactly one retained point, giving a bijection between the two neighborhoods and q+1 internally disjoint paths of length three from x to Y. The induced graph has no other edges. Its binary monotone circuit size on two-by-two tests is exactly 4(q+1) by theta-residual.md, with arm count q+1. Every deleted vertex has a retained neighbor (the line joining it to x or its intersection with Y).

Apply the elimination lemma of two-stage-theta.md to the single batch of all other vertices. The degree hypothesis holds when q≥2; the starting three-by-three tests are available for q≥3. The residual has an edge and the private compensators are all distinct. Thus s≥2(2N−2q−4)+4(q+1)=4N−4 in one batch. The all-retained-one evaluation is used only for zero propagation. In particular the original order-401 promise suffices. This does not assume that projected full point assignments are legal after a bilateral hoist.

On one already bilateral-normalized circuit retaining the necessary two-by-two tests, take x,Y uniform among nonincident pairs. Each raw is retained with marginal (q+2)/N. Every deleted sole-use raw has a distinct private AND exactly-one-zero compensator, while every third and later outgoing occurrence contributes beyond the two-per-raw charge. The original zero-flow identity then adds the entire low-zero internal fanout excess and nonprivate exactly-one-zero ANDs above 4N−4.
