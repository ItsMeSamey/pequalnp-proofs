# Two simultaneous uncertainty bounds on the same mixed flags

Fix 0<c<1, N=q²+q+1, and a binary monotone circuit computing incidence on arbitrary point masks and at most q query lines. Draw an ordered uniform list A of n=⌊cq⌋ distinct points and, conditional on A, an ordered uniform list W of q−1 distinct lines avoiding A. Put X equal to the points outside A off W. Write T=⌊√log q⌋. Grow both prefixes together through T disjoint blocks each, of lengths d_A=⌊n/(T+1)⌋ and d_W=⌊(q−1)/(T+1)⌋. At stage i write B and C for the joint backgrounds immediately before and after the block. For p in X and l incident to p missing A, the positives C+p+l and negatives C+p, C+l+(X outside l) are legal.

With high probability the degrees outside A into lines avoiding A are (exp(−c)+o(1))q uniformly. One proof uses iid sampling of points with probability n/N, Chernoff on the disjoint remainders of a pencil conditional on its center's absence, followed by conditioning on exactly n sampled points, an event of probability Ω(1/√q); the exceptional probability before conditioning is smaller than any inverse polynomial using tolerance q^(2/3). Thus the pool of available query lines has M=(exp(−c)+o(1))N. Given such an A, sampling W leaves (1/e+o(1))N points in X with high probability by hypergeometric avoidance and bounded differences as in bilateral-earlier-mixed-receivers.md. The initial flag-stage count is (exp(−1−c)+o(1))TNq.

Apply the monotone chain trace from near-unit-feeder-intervals.md to the simultaneous increasing backgrounds. Each trace starts on the displayed three tests; a raw cannot accept the positive while rejecting both negatives. All point-singleton supports are computed by adding singleton points of the fixed X to that stage's background. Hence the same rich-OR charge persists over stages and at most 2sT flag-stages are lost. Assign the remainder to oriented ANDs (g,h) with unique singleton point owner p across the assigned stages, h(C+p)=0 and h(C+p+l)=1, where s is circuit size.

Discard h(B+p+l)=0 at cost ≤2s(q+1) across stages by one transition per orientation and incident line. Discard h(B+l)=1 by the earlier entropy proof: conditional on the ordered A and past query prefix, B is fixed and line support at B avoiding the next query block is necessary for each charged OR, since it rejects C+p. Thus for each stage the expected loss is O(s(q^(5/6)+M/(d_W log q))) using D=⌈q^(2/3)⌉. The rich-pencil inequality is deterministic for every set of lines. Finally discard h(B+l)=0, h(C+l)=1 at a further total cost ≤2s(q+1), again by a single transition per owner orientation and incident line. Every retained flag now has h(B+p+l)=1, h(C+p)=h(C+l)=0. The earlier bounds give (exp(−1−c)+o(1))TNq such assignments with high probability if s=o(N√log q), and Ω(TNq) with positive probability for s≤c₀(c)N√log q with a sufficiently small constant.

For each retained flag the following two candidate sets are simultaneously small. The first consists of lines j missing A, outside the preceding query prefix, for which h(B+p+j)=1. The second consists of points u off W, outside the preceding point prefix, for which h(B+u+l)=1. Neither set can meet the corresponding next block, since h(C+p)=h(C+l)=0. The query bound is O((M/d_W)log q) for all h,p and stages with high probability, conditional on A and each past query prefix before sampling its increment.

For the other direction, condition on the entire ordered W. On ordered lists A with all points off W, their relative weights are proportional to 1/(M_A)_{q−1}, where (x)_k is a falling factorial and M_A counts lines missing A. Every such list satisfies M_A≥N−n(q+1)≥(1−c+o(1))N. Replacing one point changes M_A by at most q+1. Thus any two lists differing by one replacement have weight ratio in [1/K,K] for a fixed K=K(c), by bounding the log ratio of falling factorials by (q−1)(q+1)/(N−n(q+1)−q). Fix any already exposed point prefix. For two remaining point choices u,v, transposing their names in all completions gives a bijection of next-choice events with weight ratio in the same range: if both appear, the underlying set does not change. Each eligible next point therefore has conditional probability ≥1/(KN). For a candidate set of m points fixed at the start of the block, conditional avoidance through j steps leaves all m available, so its probability of missing the entire block is ≤exp(−md_A/(KN)). This applies even though eligibility is defined using W. Taking m=10KN(log q)/d_A and union bounding over sNT choices proves the simultaneous point bound.

Consequently at one stage Ω(Nq) distinct mixed flags obey both candidate bounds O_c(q(log q)^(3/2)). Each earlier pair response remains positive despite its two individual arms remaining false through the entire step. For any such receiver the full off-line payload still makes it true at C+l, by complementary trace. This last condition distinguishes the receiver from the exact incidence row p AND (OR of its pencil), which rejects that off-line payload. The argument gives no additive physical cost for enforcing the two bounds, and leaves open how to exclude a row equipped with a separate many-point correction.
