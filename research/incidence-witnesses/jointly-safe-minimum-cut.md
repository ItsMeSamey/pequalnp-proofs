# A minimum-circuit test that prices jointly safe uses

Let C be a constant-free trimmed scalar monotone DAG meeting the native flag and independent-negative promise. Let G be k≥1 distinct nonroot gates forming an antichain in physical reachability. Keep their original computations but pin all their outgoing uses elsewhere to zero; constant-fold the residual output C0. Suppose every native flag lost at C0 originally activates every g in G, and no promised negative originally activates every g in G. Then C0 OR (AND over g in G of g) meets the same promise.

Indeed C0≤C so all negatives reject C0, and the conjunction rejects every negative by hypothesis. All lost flags are restored, and retained flags remain accepted by C0. There are at most k new binary gates (k−1 conjunctions plus output OR). Every original direct gate consuming a member of G vanishes from the residual on folding its zero input, even if it received several G inputs. Count distinct physical consumers, not pins. None can be in the kept original ancestor cones of G: a path from a consumer back to some selected input would contradict acyclicity or the antichain. All surviving residual gates can replace their original physical rows; no downstream copy is needed for the stored G computations. Thus if f distinct physical gates consume G, the new size is at most s−f+k. In every size-minimal candidate, f≤k for every such jointly safe, covering antichain. At equality the change is size-preserving. A flag common to all G has a new proof of length no larger than the sum of the individual minimum leaf counts. When G shares the same flag signature, proofs of other flags use no true G signal and remain available after zero folding. These last statements do not require original proof paths to have been private.

For the dirty-incomplete Fibonacci family (frozen source dirty-incomplete-fibonacci-completion.md, 10c0e9aa), take S as the bank's seed and T as its terminal selector. Both signals are true on ordinary and special flags and both are false on the private matched flags. On any independent negative S=true requires the line l and one of x,z; all a_i including a0 are then absent and b0 cannot be present, so W is false, the second completion's ghost is absent, and T is false. All private matched flags survive the double-zero cut by their private exits. S,T have separate downstream cones at the cut and are incomparable; the two base gates and terminal AND are three distinct consumers. Adding S AND T and one final OR costs two. This gives a strict improvement even though every internally shared gate accepts some promised negative and misses some native flag. On the generic ordinary flags each selected signal has a two-leaf proof through its own completion, so generic proofs shrink from exponential size to at most four leaves. This is a necessary condition on a minimum circuit; no argument here finds such an antichain in every low-size candidate.
