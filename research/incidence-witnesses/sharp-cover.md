# Sharp reuse bound for a gate with a rectangle cover

Let \(A_p\) be the set of lines through point \(p\), and suppose any two distinct points have at most one common line. Write the singleton-input matrix of one gate as an exact union of rectangles \(R_i\times C_i\). Let \(K_p\) denote its row support. A row qualifies when \(|K_p|\ge2\) and \(K_p\subseteq A_p\). Discard rectangles with an empty factor. Suppose \(k\) remaining rectangles have singleton column sets and \(b\) have at least two columns.

**Bound.** At most \(b+\binom{k}{2}\) rows qualify. Indeed, if a qualifying row belongs to the row set of a rectangle with at least two columns, the point is incident with every line in that column set. At most one point can satisfy this condition for each such rectangle, accounting for at most \(b\) points. Every other qualifying row uses two singleton-column rectangles with distinct columns. Charge it to one such unordered pair of indices. The two lines determine the point, so no pair receives two charges.

A cover of size at most \(r\) consequently supports at most \(c_r=\max\{r,\binom r2\}\) qualifying rows. Indeed, \(b+\binom k2\le r-k+\binom k2\), and the discrete differences of the right side are \(k-1\), so its maximum on \([0,r]\) occurs at an endpoint. If all but at most \(h\) gates have such covers, counting the at least \(q\) qualifying gates per point yields \(Nq\le Nh+s c_r\). For \(r=10\) and \(2h\le q\), this gives \(Nq\le90s\).

**Sharpness.** In a projective plane of order \(q>100\), let \(3\le r\le14\). Choose two distinct lines and then successively choose a line avoiding every intersection of two earlier lines. For \(2\le j\le13\) earlier lines, this excludes at most \(\binom{j}{2}(q+1)\le78(q+1)<q^2+q+1\) lines. It excludes each previous line as well. The resulting lines have \(\binom r2\) distinct pairwise intersections. Use one rectangle for each selected line, with that line as its singleton column and all its incident points as its row set. Exactly the pairwise intersection points qualify. This witnesses equality for a single matrix and does not impose the cover condition on every gate of a scalar circuit.
