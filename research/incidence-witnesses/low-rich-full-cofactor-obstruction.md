# Bounded-query full cofactors with thin physical singleton supports

For prime q take its coordinate projective plane with N=q²+q+1. For 1≤b≤polylog(q) there are monotone fanin-two scalar circuits of N polylog(q) gates correct on the entire point cube and every query of ≤b lines. All gates have a false singleton-pair vertex cover of size ≤q+1, all pure point singleton supports fit on a single geometric line, and on all but q+1 geometric lines the maximum intersection with any such support is ≤1. The construction does not establish correctness on typical maximal q-line negatives.

Partition points into their q vertical affine columns plus the infinity row. Each group X lies on one geometric line and has ≤q+1 sites. Partition lines into the q classes of nonvertical affine lines of fixed slope, one vertical class, and the singleton infinity line. Encode a line by O(log q) binary field-coordinate and type bits. For each point p a Boolean circuit of size polylog(q) checks if the encoded line contains p. For finite objects this uses integer multiplication and addition modulo q with fixed point coordinates and comparison; grade-school binary arithmetic and division by the fixed q have polynomial bit complexity. The vertical and infinity cases require only type and coordinate comparisons.

Choose O(b log N+1) maps from all lines into 2b² buckets so every at-most-b subset is injected by some map. Independent uniform maps suffice since a given subset collides under one map with probability at most 1/4, and there are ≤(N+1)^b subsets. For each hash, bucket, and bit form two rails indicating presence of a selected line having the respective bit value, as well as a nonempty-bucket bit. A singleton bucket yields its precise binary word. Dualize the Boolean predicate circuit in the usual way: complement swaps rails, AND and OR each compute both rails by their original and dual operators. Gate the answer by bucket nonemptiness. A true incidence yields a positive answer even with bucket collisions by monotonicity from the singleton input. Taking OR across buckets and AND across maps therefore computes, for each p, its exact query incidence when at most b lines are selected. To see rejection use the map separating the selected lines. No assumption about the number of selected points enters this reasoning.

Implement the rails locally to enforce both physical cover constraints. Precompute each rail and presence bit separately within each query class, using ORs of directly named raw lines. For each point group X form B_X, the OR of its raw points. For each rail compute the AND of B_X with each class-local value, then OR those mixed values across query classes. This is the intended rail whenever B_X=1. When B_X=0 all such rails vanish. Independent copies of the decoder for points of X read only these X-local mixed rails. Use bucket presence as the active constant one where needed, and zero for a constant zero, or fold constants in the predicate circuits; no global constant-one signal is necessary. Finally AND the decoded answer with its raw p and OR over p. If p is present, B_X=1, proving the claimed full point-cofactor semantics.

There are O(b³ log² N) rail indices. For an index, class-local preprocessing costs O(N) gates. Generating its mixed versions costs O(q²)=O(N) across all pairs of point and query classes. The decoding circuits cost N poly(b,log q) gates. Thus the full construction costs N poly(b,log q).

Every pure point intermediate is supported in X on its single geometric line. Every pure line intermediate is confined to one query class of ≤q+1 lines. All decoder intermediates after the conditioning ANDs require a selected point of X and a selected line, so X covers their false singleton pairs and they have empty pure point supports. The complete per-p contributions and their final ORs are already exact on singleton pairs and accept no pure point or pure line input. Thus every wire meets the announced normalizations. Pure raw singleton inputs are included in the bounds. The only potentially rich geometric lines are the q column lines and the infinity line. In particular even the mean, over all geometric lines, of the maximum point support on that line is bounded by an absolute constant.
