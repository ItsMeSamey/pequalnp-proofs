# A guard for every maximal negative with few uncovered points

In PG(2,q), let N=q²+q+1, and let 1≤C=o(√q). There is a binary monotone flag-complete guard rejecting every maximal negative (X,Y) with at most q active lines and |X|≤Cq, where X consists of all points uncovered by Y. Its size is O(N (C+1)³ log q) when (C+1)²=o(q). Constants are absolute. In particular for C=q^(1/3−δ) with fixed 0<δ<1/3 this is o(Nq). This guard simultaneously covers every automorphism orbit satisfying the stated size condition.

**Geometry of small uncovered sets.** Put k=|Y| and let m be the largest number of Y-lines through one point o. For the line multiplicities r_x at covered points write E=Σ_x(r_x−1)=k(q+1)−N+|X|. Then k≥q−C and E≤Cq. Pair counting gives k(k−1)≤m E. Adding the m pencil lines first creates excess m−1; each remaining line meets the pencil on m distinct points and creates at least m more units. Hence k−m≤E/m. First m≥q/(2C) for large q, so k−m≤2C²=o(q), hence m≥q/2. Now k−m≤2C and q+1−m≤3C+1. Every uncovered point lies on one of the missing pencil lines, and o is covered. Set h=⌈3C+3⌉. There are at most h missing pencil lines and at most h−1 off-pencil query lines.

**Hash test for a bounded cell.** In F_q fix additive coordinates over F_p. There is a family of O(h log q) multipliers t≠0 and, for each multiplier, a partition of the field into O(h²) buckets for t times a value. Given two sets of field values P,Q, test whether some occupied P-bucket intersects the translation of some occupied Q-bucket by t z, for a hardwired z. AND these tests over multipliers. Genuine y=b+z always passes. Whenever |P|,|Q|≤h and there is no genuine match, some multiplier rejects.

Here are details with no assumption on the characteristic. Take B=⌈100h²⌉, smaller than q for large q. Choose d with p^d≤B<p^(d+1), keep d full coordinates and partition the next coordinate's cyclic list into u=⌊B/p^d⌋ consecutive nearly equal intervals. Ignore all remaining coordinates. Thus p^d u is between B/2 and B. Translation of a bucket can intersect at most four buckets: full-coordinate values are prescribed and the shifted interval on the cycle crosses only a bounded number of the near-equal intervals. For a fixed nonzero Δ=y−b−z, spurious overlap requires the first d coordinates of tΔ zero and its next cyclic coordinate within twice the largest interval length of zero. Indeed take witnesses y',b' from the two original buckets after multiplying values by t, with y'=b'+tz; their coordinate discrepancies from ty,tb have absolute cyclic size at most the largest length. For uniform t≠0, tΔ is uniform nonzero. The interval length is at most 2p/u and the fraction of eligible differences is at most 40/B for large q (count at most min(p,4⌈p/u⌉+1) next-coordinate choices and account for excluding zero). Thus the false-overlap probability for any pair of disjoint shifted sets of sizes ≤h is at most 40h²/B≤0.4. Independently repeating O(h log q) times and union bounding over ≤q(q+1)^(2h) choices of P,Q,z gives the fixed family. Each multiplier needs O(B) gates per cell to OR the products of compatible occupancy pairs; their table is hardwired.

**Two verdicts per point.** For each of three nonconcurrent candidate infinity lines L build one affine branch; their AND is the guard. Within a branch handle ideal-point incidences exactly by testing the direction bins of active affine lines and whether the infinity line is active, using O(N) gates. Use two distinct fixed choices of vertical direction on L, with corresponding affine coordinates. For each choice form point column x and nonvertical query slope a groups. Precompute per multiplier the occupancy of the buckets of ty for the ordinates of active points in each column, and of tb for the intercepts of the active queries of each slope. Here the buckets are applied after multiplication exactly once. Aggregation costs O(N) per multiplier and direction since the groups partition the relevant raws. For each x,a apply the bounded cell test at translation z=ax. OR its result over a, including also the raw query for the vertical line at x, to give the column verdict. In this construction a cell with an empty group always rejects. For each affine point AND its raw with both column verdicts, one from each direction. OR these terms and the exact ideal-point case. All true flags pass in every infinity branch, including a vertical query in either direction.

For the intended negative choose L avoiding o, which exists by nonconcurrency. In either direction every nonvertical slope group has ≤1 pencil query plus at most h−1 outside queries. Take an active affine point v. Of its two columns, at least one differs from its line ov, since the directions differ. That column cannot be a missing pencil line: if it contained o it would equal ov. It thus meets the at most h missing pencil lines in at most h points. Its payload has size ≤h. All its slope cells reject by the hash guarantee and its vertical query is absent because v is uncovered. So one of v's two column verdicts rejects. The ideal-point branch also rejects by genuine independence. This proves correctness on the whole small-uncovered family, without requiring both directions to be globally light. Six affine coordinate systems cost only a constant factor. With q² cells per direction, O(h log q) multipliers and O(h²) gates per multiplier per cell, the claimed bound follows.
