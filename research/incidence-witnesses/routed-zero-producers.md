# Physical OR producers for zero certificates

In a live constant-free binary circuit retain both operands at every OR and one globally fixed operand at every AND. Let C(g) be the raw labels reaching g in this pruned DAG. Zeroing C(g) forces g zero by induction, so its cardinality is at least the minimum zero certificate size z(g). Merge occurrences of each raw into one input vertex.

For one target the retained ancestor graph is connected. With k raw vertices, a unary ANDs and o binary ORs its operand-slot multigraph has k+a+o vertices and a+2o edges. Hence o≥k−1≥z(g)−1. For a union of cones replace 1 by the number of connected components and count only distinct raw vertices. For nonnegative target weights summing to at most one on targets using each physical OR, summing gives at least Σ weights times (z(g)−1) distinct physical ORs. Cone counting independently audited.

For positive integer c and |C(g)|>c, stop at boundary vertices with |C(h)|≤c. They cover C(g) and number at least ceil(|C(g)|/c). Treat them as terminals in the connected multigraph count. The internal cone therefore contains at least ceil(z(g)/c)−1 ORs whose routed sets have size greater than c. If routed target sets intersect pairwise in at most c labels, none of these ORs is common to two targets, so the counts sum. Independent minimum certificates chosen separately at each target need not arise from a common routing.

Construct a graph on raws with at most one edge per physical OR. For g=a OR b choose a raw from each operand's routed set and join them (skip loops). Every C(g) induces a connected subgraph using ancestor edges alone, by induction. For each target partition C(g) into t nonempty parts and give the partition weight λ≥0. If each unordered raw pair has weight ≤1 among partitions separating the pair and containing both endpoints, at least Σ λ(t−1) distinct ORs are required. Partitions may overlap arbitrarily. These are total producer ORs; adding them to a deletion ledger requires separate proof.
