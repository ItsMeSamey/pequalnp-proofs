# Eighteen gates for twenty maximal negatives

A binary monotone DAG accepting the sixteen flags and rejecting at least twenty of the twenty-one punctured-Fano maximal negatives requires at least eighteen gates. Use punctured-fano-almost-full-forks.md and punctured-fano-equality-cold-tree.md. All hot singleton inputs reject. Each opposite-shore nonincident pair lies in three maximal negatives, so it rejects too. The six cold leaves therefore have exactly the prescribed, incomparable singleton-hot neighborhoods, of sizes two and three, with pairwise intersection at most one.

Suppose seventeen gates suffice. The six-leaf cold tree has five binary mergers. All are OR except possibly the cherry joining the unique pair of disjoint neighborhoods. Other gates are hot-only or unary (one cold-dependent operand). Call leaves outside an exceptional AND cherry ordinary. Every merger above an ordinary leaf is OR. Its neighborhood is exactly the intersection of the supports of the hot operands of unary ANDs along its path: passing filters transmit a true leaf; a failed filter erases its effect, and the hot singleton baseline rejects. Before its first merger each ordinary leaf needs a private unary AND, since otherwise its neighborhood would contain any sibling leaf's neighborhood. The designated private hot operand contains its entire star.

The four degree-three ordinary stars cover all six hot raws and connect them through their intersections. Their private hot operands' cones therefore connect all six raws. Each binary hot-only gate merges at most two components, hence at least five hot-only gates are needed and at most seven unary gates remain. With an AND cherry, each of its two branches requires a unary OR so that the opposite cold leaf can activate the cherry while this leaf is zero. Designate one per branch. In either architecture six unary gates are designated, leaving at most one extra.

If there is no extra unary inside an exceptional cherry branch, each leaf's neighborhood is a designated hot support filtered either by the full set or one common mask G from the extra unary AND, if present. For ordinary leaves this follows from the path intersection. For a cherry (y OR U) AND (z OR V), the designated signal for y is V, and conversely. Under any singleton hot input, changing y alone makes the cherry true precisely when V passes; if a later filter masks it, the output equals the rejected baseline. A possible OR additive cannot create a positive on its own. The two possible filters are nested, so two equal designated signals would give comparable neighborhoods. All six signals must be distinct nonraw gates. This exhausts the remaining six hot-only slots, leaving only the designated unary gates. Thus all six hot signals equal the six target stars. No size-three star can be the first such target obtained from the raws and other stars: two singletons cannot union to a triple, no other nonsingleton target is contained in the triple, and no earlier target strictly contains it.

If the seventh unary is inside an AND cherry branch, the four ordinary triple neighborhoods are unfiltered private hot operands. There are at most five hot-only gates. Four distinct triple outputs leave at most one auxiliary. No triple can be obtained by intersecting two strict supersets as only the auxiliary could be a strict superset. To obtain each by union the auxiliary must supply a two-element subset, since other triples are incomparable. A single pair cannot be a subset of two target triples. This excludes the remaining case. The argument allows unrestricted fanout in the hot-only DAG.

If a minimum circuit of size at most twenty has an AND root with one parent rejecting just one maximal negative, the dense parent costs exactly eighteen gates, the circuit costs exactly twenty, and the correcting parent is a single OR of two wires from the dense cone. This follows from root incomparability and the exclusive-operand argument in punctured-fano-root-interfaces.md. The unrestricted twenty-gate decision remains open.
