# Punctured-Fano root interfaces

Use the 12 raw sites, 16 positive flags and 21 maximal negatives of punctured-fano-almost-full-forks.md. Count physical binary AND/OR gates with arbitrary fanout. Assume minimum size at most 20.

**AND root.** If one parent B rejects at most four maxima, the other A rejects at least 17 and accepts all flags. The cited first-fork lemma costs A at least 17 gates. Every singleton belongs to six maxima, so A rejects each singleton and every raw is essential by its incident flag. At most two gates besides the root are outside A.

A wire on an all-AND path to the root has only one use. Keep its occurrence on the chosen path, replacing every other outgoing occurrence with true. At wire value one nothing changes. At zero the chosen path still forces zero. Every replaced slot simplifies a reader, contradicting minimality. Repeated operands are redundant. Consequently root parents are incomparable. B is nonraw since it accepts all flags. Were B an AND, its two distinct operands would accept all flags and be nonraw. Their AND-only paths imply neither can occur in A, since that would give a second use. With B this would use three exclusive gates. Thus B is OR. Its at most one additional exclusive ancestor leaves just u OR v, (u OR v) OR w, and (u AND v) OR w, with u,v,w from A's cone including its raws. Circuit simplifications may identify some boundary wires.

**OR root.** Assign each flag to an accepting parent, yielding E1,E2. For each nonempty assigned E, let r count its incident sites and let D consist of edges whose endpoints have assigned degree at least two. A child rejects all original negatives and depends on its r assigned sites. By the first-fork partition in projective-cold-empty.md, a raw reaching no branching wire is cold. Two cold endpoints cannot form the middle edge of an induced four-vertex path: distinct groups give the cold-edge obstruction; a common group allows a forbidden interchange. Every D edge is such a middle edge, since the projective incidence graph is C4-free. Hot endpoints of D have distinct first-branch groups: same-shore endpoints each have two assigned neighbors and swapping changes an assigned edge to a nonedge, and across shores swapping on another assigned neighbor gives a forbidden same-shore pair. Thus the hot endpoints cover D and require at least its matching number distinct branching wires. The child size is at least r−1 plus that matching number by the physical fanout identity. Add both bounds and one for the root, subtracting the actual number of shared physical ancestors. The checker check-fano-or-splits.py finds 734 out of 32767 unordered nonempty splits with combined bound at most 20 before subtracting overlap. This exclusion applies directly to gate-disjoint cones only.
