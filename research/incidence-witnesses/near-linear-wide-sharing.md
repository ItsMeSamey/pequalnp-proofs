# Near-four-N size forces many shared gates of large zero-certificate width

Fix a nonnegative constant C. For all sufficiently large plane orders q, any binary monotone incidence circuit of size s<=4N+Cq on the original sparse sample has more than q/1024 distinct shared internal gates each requiring more than floor(q/[16(C+8)]) raw inputs to force zero. Write k=q+1, N=q^2+q+1. The statement is conditional on this size window, rather than a separation of complexity classes.

Take the single-wave bilateral OR-spine hoist, giving a live circuit with at most s gates computing the requisite q-1 capped sample and reserving a distinct private AND for each fanout-one raw input. Use the h=q-1 all-zero balanced flag restriction. It deletes exactly D=N-k inputs on each shore. Zero flow and the private ANDs guarantee expected removals at least 4D-2(q-1) plus the shared-gate excess credits: every deleted vertex except the two sets of q-1 free vertices has a surviving neighbor. The residual output is nonconstant (its graph consists of q length-three paths between the two poles). Since the removals cannot exceed s, expected shared credits alone are at most
    s-4D+2(q-1) <= Cq+4(q+1)+2(q-1) <= (C+8)q.
There is no need to charge any residual flow lower bound for this estimate.

For w=floor(q/[16(C+8)]) let M be the number of shared gates with a raw zero certificate of size at most w. Every raw vertex survives with probability k/N; the union bound gives every such gate a zero with probability at least 1-wk/N>=1/2. As every counted gate contributes at least one excess use, M<=2(C+8)q. Fix to zero the union of one such certificate per counted gate. This sacrifices no more than wM<=N/8 units of incidence flags (one unit is k flags) and removes every narrow shared gate. The remaining shared-gate count is bounded by the original number of wide shared slots, as unary simplification cannot increase this count.

Suppose that at most q/1024 wide shared slots were present. Eliminate all remaining shared slots by the source subformula argument in size-sharing-interpolation.md. Its nominal budget B is at most sqrt(2k(q/1024)(s+q/1024)). For sufficiently large q at fixed C this is at most N/8 (the limiting ratio to N is sqrt(8/1024)<1/8). At least 3N/4 units of good flags survive the two operations. At most floor(B/k) positive sites are pinned in total, leaving a matching-degree cap d=q-floor(B/k) of at least q/2. By the proper-neighborhood rank bound the final formula needs at least (d+1)3N/4 leaves but has at most 4N+Cq+1. This contradiction proves the conclusion for the hoisted circuit.

The conclusion transfers back to the original circuit if the hoist is implemented without simplifying or duplicating internal gates except the stipulated unbranched spines. Each retained old internal wire used as an off-spine sibling has that use moved once from an OR spine into a new output collection tree; its other uses are unchanged or removed. A removed exit signal is replaced only by its own raw variable. Consequently no retained internal wire acquires additional uses, and every new output-collection gate has fanout at most one. Induction shows that every retained nonoutput gate's function after substitution is pointwise at most its old function. A zero certificate for the old gate is therefore a zero certificate for the new. Thus every distinct wide shared gate of the hoisted circuit was already a wide shared gate before hoisting. Work with the unpruned hoist when applying this transfer; merely discarding unused gates cannot increase any fanout.

With the stated width threshold, if along any subsequence the wide-gate count t/q has limsup at most alpha<49/512, the normalized pinning cost B/N has limsup at most sqrt(8 alpha)<7/8. Because the narrow union costs at most N/8 edge units, a constant fraction of the flags survive, and the positive pins spend less than a fixed fraction of q. The matching degree stays linear and yields the same contradiction. Thus the liminf of the number of original wide shared gates divided by q is at least 49/512. Rank independently audited both the transfer and this strengthened constant.

More generally, fix 0<beta<1 and set width w=floor(beta q). If s<=4N+Cq, the h=q-1 residual lower bound at minimum degree two is 4(q+1)-1-floor(4(q+1)/3). Together with the expected private loss <=2(q-1) and baseline 4D this bounds all shared credit by (C+10/3)q+O(1). The narrow count divided by q is therefore at most (C+10/3)/(1-beta)+o(1), and the union of its zero certificates divided by N has limsup at most b=beta(C+10/3)/(1-beta). Whenever b<1, the very same elimination shows a lower limit (1-b)^2/8 for the wide shared count divided by q: any strictly smaller ratio leaves positive fractions of both the edge units and the matching-degree budget. The formula comparison rules it out. This last parameterized statement uses the residual weighted-flow bound as well as the sharing interpolation.
