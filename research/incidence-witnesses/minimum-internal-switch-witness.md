# Internal private switches in a lexicographically selected genuine minimum

Among circuits of minimum gate count on the original promise first minimize the sum of the alternation-switch-charge.md parameters c_e, then minimize D, the number of ordered pairs (physical OR u,physical AND v) with a directed path from u to v. The finite optima exist. Let a marked OR g=A∨B have a single physical reader slot in h=g∧K. The rest of the circuit and the location of h are unrestricted.

For EACH arm, say B, some same-query maximal negative n satisfies B(n)=1, K(n)=0 and C[h←1](n)=1. Here the last expression forces every use of h (or the output itself) to one. Otherwise replace g,h by j=A∧K and h'=j∨B, redirecting all previous h readers to h'. Every old positive survives since h'≥h, while a newly accepted maximal negative would necessarily have B=1,K=0 and forced output one. Thus the full promise survives at no added gate cost. If trimming decreases size, that already contradicts minimum.

For every kept OR u, any reachability to an AND in the new graph maps to old reachability, identifying new AND j with old h. Further any old AND reaching u in the new graph maps to an old AND reaching u: j can reach an old downstream wire only if h could, and new A,K,B-to-downstream paths had counterparts in the old circuit. Consequently no kept OR acquires new marked status. For a proof of a flag not visiting h retain its old choices. For a proof visiting h, if it used arm A, use j and the old A,K proofs; if it used B, take B at h' and omit the K obligation except where used elsewhere. Old choices on shared wires remain consistent. The only possible newly charged OR in these translated proofs is h', and any such proof previously visited the removed marked OR g. Thus all c_e weakly decrease, regardless of further downstream ANDs.

Finally D strictly decreases. Contributions of kept ORs do not increase by the reachability mapping. The new OR h' reaches only original ANDs strictly downstream of h, whereas g formerly reached every such AND and also h itself. This contradicts the tertiary optimum if the secondary sum stayed equal. The conclusion gives a witness for each arm. Neither distinctness between witnesses nor an additive switch-to-gate payment is asserted.
