# What the sparse negative tests force

Let Γ(Y) be the points on lines in Y and let V(Y) select precisely the points outside Γ(Y) and the lines in Y. Every V(Y) with |Y| ≤ q is globally maximal among edge-free assignments. Adding a point touches a selected line. Adding a line outside Y touches one of its q+1 points outside Γ(Y), since each selected line meets it in at most one point.

It follows that a monotone clause accepting every positive pair and rejecting V(Y) is unique after duplicate literals are removed. It must mention only coordinates zero at V(Y), and it must mention every such coordinate since flipping any of them creates an incident pair. Call this clause C(Y). It ORs all point coordinates in Γ(Y) and all line coordinates outside Y. Different sparse canonical negatives require different clauses. The conjunction of the canonical clauses passes all positives and rejects each chosen negative. In particular the minimum number of clauses is precisely the number of tested sparse masks.

For a circuit with a AND gates, fix for each AND a choice of one parent. Replacing each AND globally by a copy of its chosen parent gives an OR-only function greater than or equal to the original. For every negative assignment choose a false parent at every original AND that evaluates false, arbitrarily choosing elsewhere. By induction all originally false wires remain false, so the enlarged function rejects that assignment. It accepts all positive pairs and hence is its unique canonical clause. At least as many distinct choice vectors as negatives are required. In particular 2^a ≥ Σ_{j=0}^q binom(N,j). Also q lines through one point cover q²+1 points, giving a pruned clause with (q²+1)+(N−q)=2N−2q literals. Its OR requires at least 2N−2q−1 binary OR gates. The two counts concern disjoint sets of gates.

Conversely, for any m≥1 sampled negatives, a circuit can implement their canonical clauses with O(N m/log₂(m+1)+m) total gates when m≤2^(2N). Set b=max(1,floor(log₂ m)). Partition the 2N input bits into blocks of b and precompute every nonempty subset OR in each block using at most 2^b gates by stripping one bit per subset. For every clause OR its nonempty block pieces, using fewer than ceil(2N/b) further ORs. AND the clauses. For m=o(q log q) this gives o(Nq), and tagging all gates eventually fits the prescribed budget. The Fano-plane script check-sampled-clauses.py verifies maximality, uniqueness and the resulting circuit against all inputs of line weight ≤2.

These claims cover fixed sets of negatives. A lower-bound adversary that picks different sequences of tests for different circuits need not be equivalent to one common fixed sample.
