# Sparse partial stars and the AND/OR dichotomy

The raw inputs are query labels, and each target A has q+1 labels. Distinct targets intersect in at most one label. A head for A is a wire rejecting every disjoint input mask of size at most q. Join all pairs of raw singleton labels accepted by a common designated head for A. The sum of the graphic ranks of these graphs is at most the circuit size. There is a gate-type refinement.

Mark for A the gates whose singleton support has size at least two and lies in A. Every head contributing an edge is marked. Take connected components of marked gates using undirected physical parent-child adjacency. Boundary wires entering a component C either have singleton support of size at most one or accept an outsider singleton. This holds also for raw label wires. The components of different targets are disjoint because two labels determine at most one target.

Evaluate the internal circuit of C with a formal boundary assignment: give value one to every boundary wire with an outsider singleton, zero to all others. This evaluation is well-defined even if the boundary combination never occurs on actual inputs. For heads rejecting it, their combined support graph has rank at most the number of ORs in C. To see this, maintain a union-find partition on A. Every boundary wire rejecting the artificial input is thin. At each artificially rejecting internal gate join the labels in its actual singleton support. An AND has a rejecting operand whose singleton support contains that of the output; an OR has two rejecting operands and can join at most two existing classes. Thus only ORs can increase rank, by at most one each.

If a head instead accepts the formal assignment, choose a proof DAG for this evaluation with one fixed true predecessor at each OR and both at each AND, stopping at the boundary. If it visits a distinct physical AND gates, connectedness bounds its number of distinct boundary wires by a+1 (edge count after making ORs unary). Each true boundary wire has an outside singleton. Taking one such label per visited boundary wire gives a real disjoint mask T on which all needed wires are one by monotonicity, and the head must accept. Hence a≥q, irrespective of dependencies among different boundary wires and repeated uses. Thus exceptional components pay at least q ANDs each; the OR charge for their rejecting subfamily can still be made separately. Since the entire component contributes at most q graphic rank, the total rank over components is bounded by the total gates. Distinct components of the same target may cover overlapping labels; graphic rank is subadditive.

This proof generalizes the single-head scarcity result in lean/Research/SparseQueryCut.lean. It allows arbitrary-depth monotone DAGs and arbitrary many overlapping partial heads. It applies only to heads with an actual disjoint-mask rejection guarantee; a synthetic singleton intervention alone need not provide that guarantee. The unrestricted scalar q401 problem remains open.
