# Physical scalar charge for simple flag proofs

Use binary monotone gates, constants folded, unlimited fanout and the original scalar plane promise. A positive proof chooses one OR operand and both AND operands and unfolds sharing. Let D be the native flags admitting a proof with exactly two raw leaf occurrences. The circuit has at least |D| physical gates.

Every such proof contains exactly one AND g, with an OR-only path to output and OR-only paths from raw leaves to its two operands. For each qualifying physical g collect all raws OR-reachable into each operand. Every pair across these feeder sets propagates to output. Same-shore assignments of at most two sites and nonincident singleton pairs reject. Hence the two raw sets are pure opposite-shore supports forming an incidence rectangle, necessarily a star. Choose its center v once for g, orienting single-edge ties arbitrarily.

Let k_v be the number of ANDs assigned to v, and d_v the union size of their noncentral raw feeder sets. Explore backward from those operands through raw-reaching ORs, stopping when a signal has only one reachable raw label, discarding any raw-free branches. The resulting DAG has at most k_v designated roots and at least d_v sinks since their labels cover that union. It has ≥d_v−k_v binary branching vertices by counting edges versus vertices. Each such OR has at least two reachable raw labels on the opposite shore and all lie in N(v). It cannot serve another center on this shore by codegree one, or the opposite shore by purity. The k_v actual ANDs pay the remainder. Summing covers D. No designated row signal or syntactic bilinearity was assumed.

A semantic variant allows any mixed computation inside the two operands. Consider ANDs with an OR-only path to output whose inputs accept bare singletons of opposite shores. Their bare singleton supports again form clean stars. For any such product centered on line l, its payload operand h rejects the full bare complement of l: otherwise adding singleton query l makes its companion accept and violates the original promise. Assign each of its d_l distinctly covered point labels to one of the k_l products at l. Send these tokens into their assigned operands, traversing the common physical DAG in reverse topological order. At an AND follow one fixed child rejecting the bare complement. At an OR route each token into an accepting child. Aggregate tokens at a shared wire before proceeding and stop at singleton-token sites. Every charged branch OR accepts at least two points of l and rejects its complement, hence is unavailable at another line. Edge counting gives ≥d_l−k_l branch ORs; the k_l actual products pay the balance. Thus the physical size is at least the number of distinct flags covered by these line-centered semantic products.

The point-centered variant holds when each relevant query operand accepts only if it accepts a submask of size ≤q on bare-query inputs. This ensures complementary rejection by the scalar sparse tests. It holds in particular for AND-depth ≤floor(log₂q) in those operands. The two semantic shore bounds may overlap and only their maximum follows without further accounting. These estimates leave open the flags with no shallow proof and no such separating product.
