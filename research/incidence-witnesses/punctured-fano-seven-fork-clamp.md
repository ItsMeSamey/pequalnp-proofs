# Seven singleton-hot first forks cannot coexist with five independent cold raws

Assume all sixteen flags are accepted and at least nineteen of the twenty-one maximal two-by-two punctured-Fano negatives reject. The twelve singletons and all nonincident opposite-shore pairs reject (they lie in six and three maxima respectively). Suppose the only branching wires are seven distinct fanout-two first forks f_y owned by seven distinct singleton-hot raws y, with no cold raw reaching any of them. The five cold raws form an independent set C. Require the ordered separation test also on hot-hot pairs: for each opposite-shore nonincident hot h,y, a rejected mask contains h and some neighbor of y. Under these hypotheses the circuit cannot exist.

Every path from y goes through f_y, which y alone activates. The fork rejects every other hot singleton h. For incident h,y use the rejected h and positive h+y. On the same shore choose a neighbor z of y not adjacent to h, using incomparability of their stars. Then h+z rejects and h+z+y accepts. For opposite nonincident labels use the ordered separation test in the same way. Were f_y already one from h, adjoining y could change nothing. Each f_y depends only on hot inputs.

Replace all forks f_y by their respective raw y and trim. This decreases values, since each original fork dominates its owner. Every hot/cold flag retains its original value because its pure-hot portion is a singleton: all original fork values agree there with the substituted wires. The resulting circuit is a formula with each cold raw read at most once and each hot raw at most twice. Cutting the incoming edges of all forks shows this directly: if f_y was nonraw, the owner y had a unique path to it and no path bypassing it. Every remaining nonraw is unbranched.

If a flag h,l is lost, it is hot-hot. Take the earliest original fork f_z active on h,l even though its owner differs from both endpoints. Without such a fork all substitutions would agree with the old evaluation. Cut its ancestry at the first upstream forks one encounters. These earlier atoms agree with their owners on the pair and both singleton tests. Any raw other than z reaching f_z must have already encountered its own fork or f_z would also be its first fork. Raw z is zero on all three tests. Since f_z rejects each singleton but accepts the pair, the cut computation must use both f_h and f_l. Each spends an outgoing occurrence in a private path to f_z with no intervening fork. Clamping f_z deletes both occurrences. Hence both lost endpoints occur at most once in the clamped live formula.

If such an endpoint v has two cold neighbors c,c', choose another hot neighbor w of c (every cold has degree at least two, and C is independent). The pair c',w is nonincident by absence of four-cycles. All three flags vc,vc',cw survive because they are hot/cold. In a formula where v,c each occurs once, their least common ancestor must be AND on the empty background to accept vc but reject both singletons. It must be OR on the background c',w to reject the baseline but accept each extension by v or c. No fixed gate can do both. Thus each lost endpoint has fewer than two cold neighbors. No edge satisfies this in the four mixed cases. At most one does in each of the twelve pure cases, as independently evaluated by the checker.

Use KW traversal on the surviving flag rows of this formula. A good column e is a surviving flag whose proper-neighborhood mask (all neighbors of its endpoints except the endpoints themselves) lies below a retained maximum. Orient e toward the endpoint returned on the diagonal. For distinct good edges on a common star at v, the only possible returned variable is v. Each physical leaf occurrence of v gives a rank-one rectangle of its returns; their disjoint sum gives the return matrix. On the square of d good edges incident to v the matrix is all ones except t diagonal zeros for the edges directed away. If t<d its rank is t+1: subtract an all-ones row from each of the t exceptional rows. If t=d and d≥2, the matrix J−I is invertible over the reals. Consequently when d exceeds the leaf budget c_v≥1, at most c_v−1 edges may be directed away. At d≤c_v impose no restriction. Use budgets one on C and two elsewhere, additionally lowering both endpoints of the possible lost edge to one and removing that row and column.

The companion check-fano-seven-fork-clamp.py constructs the graph, its maximal negatives and the sixteen candidate C directly. For every set of zero, one or two omitted maxima it enumerates the allowed loss scenarios and tests all orientations of the good edges with pruning. It rejects 24, 504 and 5040 systems respectively. The orientation calculation covers all omissions, while the circuit inference requires the ordered directed witnesses above. In the prior classification these hold for every single omission and 198 of 210 double omissions; the twelve remaining doubles are outside this application. The theorem does not cover other fork configurations without a separate reduction.
