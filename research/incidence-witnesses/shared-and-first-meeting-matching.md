# Residual matching flags can first meet at one shared-product junction

Choose a line a, a point o outside it, and m≥4 points p_i on a with lines l_i=op_i, 2^m+1≤q. These flags form an induced matching. Take all 2^m OR banks choosing exactly one literal of each pair. Their conjunction is the OR of the matching products on the full cube. Its unique singleton-query witness for edge i is the bank B_i selecting just query l_i and all other points. Its unique singleton-point witness is the bank C_i selecting just p_i and the other lines.

Let R be the conjunction of all C_i and H the conjunction of all remaining banks. For b=0,1 let U_b be the OR of the corresponding parity half of the points, with both halves of size at least two. Use distinct top gates T_b=H AND (R AND U_b). Their OR computes the full matching: a true matching flag supplies one parity guard. Both partial products H,R are physically shared and dirty: an opposite transversal for any omitted prime clause falsifies only that clause, and is legal and independent. Each T_b is indispensable for its own native flags and has width at most 2^m+1.

Complete the plane by ordinary point stars, omitting the m designated flags. None of these flags is globally marked by a projected OR support of size ≥2 confined to its owning pencil or line. Among matching or guard banks the only queries are l_j, of which only l_i passes through p_i; only p_i among matching points lies on l_i. In the completion p_i's query OR excludes l_i, and other owners' query ORs cannot have two lines through p_i. Point factors are raw. For each designated flag only its parity term accepts. Its B_i and C_i are in separate subproducts until the final T_b; U_b is not a singleton witness. At each of the two junctions both directional certificates first coexist for at least floor(m/2) distinct assigned residual flags. Taking m=floor(log₂(q−1)) refutes any fixed debit per first meeting, even under the full cube and with necessary output terms. The large producing cones still pay for this construction, which is not gate-minimal. No constant global-amortization counterexample follows.
