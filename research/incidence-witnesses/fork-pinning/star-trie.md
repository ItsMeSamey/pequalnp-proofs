# Eligible OR weight from an exact point star

Assume a line m has an OR-only computation of precisely the q+1 point singletons on m from those raw point inputs. The computation may share gates and be embedded in a larger circuit. More generally it suffices that the chosen singleton paths branch only at OR gates whose point-singleton supports lie on m. Pick one path to its own raw leaf for every singleton label, unfold their union, and suppress unary vertices. The resulting full binary tree has 402 leaves at order 401. Its total branch incidence count, summing label counts at each branch, is the external path length. The least possible value is 3508, since a minimum-length tree's depths differ by at most one (replace a deepest sibling pair by their parent and split a shallowest leaf); 110 leaves have depth eight and 292 depth nine.

A label x assigned to a branch occurrence of physical OR gate g activates g. It is eligible if each child accepts some singleton on m other than x. If ineligible, one child accepts only x among m's points: it has a routed label because this is a genuine branch. Thus this child routed exactly x. It is a leaf after unary suppression. Charging that branch-label occurrence to this leaf is injective, even if both children of a branch are leaves. There are at most 402 losses. For fixed x its selected path meets any physical gate at most once by acyclicity, hence summing physical eligible weights cannot undercount the remaining branch-label pairs. This proves eligible weight at least 3106 per star. Different stars cannot share a counted physical gate when its actual point-singleton support is contained in the corresponding line and has size at least two. Containment is automatic for OR-only cones with an exact-star output; restricting a general gate to the line alone would not suffice.

At the one-sided h=267 schedule, 982 stars yield 3050092 and 983 yield 3053198, straddling the sufficient 3052338 bound.
