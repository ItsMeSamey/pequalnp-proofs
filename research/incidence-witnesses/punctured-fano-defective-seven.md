# Seven hot owners under a direction-defective double omission

Use the punctured-Fano flag tests and retain nineteen of the twenty-one maximal negatives. All hot-hot interchange witnesses, the induced-P4 tests for the first-fork ledger, all singleton negatives, and all nonincident opposite-shore pairs survive. For the twelve omissions missing one or two ordered directions we extend the seven-fork clamp. A failed direction (h,x) means no retained maximum contains h together with any neighbor of x. A hot owner's first fork f_x can accept another hot singleton h only in such a direction, by the channel domination argument. A cold y can first branch at hot f_h only if (h,y) is a failed direction. An incident pair cannot yield either failure because the active singleton is rejected.

Suppose seven distinct binary first forks have seven hot owners; they exhaust branching in the putative eighteen-gate circuit. Their five cold complements are independent. The checker enumerates all sixteen such complements. For each of the twelve omitted pairs, at most one additional hot singleton t at one hot owner f_x is possible, and at most one cold y can reach any hot owner f_h. Enumerate actual presence or absence independently. When cold contamination is present pin y to zero; every remaining cold input has a unique path to output. Pinning preserves the truth values on all surviving tests, and no hot-singleton footprint changes. All clamped collectors formerly receiving y see its fixed zero.

Replace every f_z by raw z, except that a genuinely impure f_x is replaced by x OR t. These lower approximations agree with original values on all hot singletons. After the cut all nontrivial fragments below the sink are disconnected. The routed output is a formula on the remaining raw inputs, expanding each inserted OR separately at each use. A remaining cold leaf occurs at most once, and a hot raw at most twice except t. Indeed each raw has to pass through its own first fork before reaching any other collector. A port emitted by f_t must enter the f_x collector, because with t alone it activates f_x, and there is no other earlier collector with singleton t. The local raw x is zero there. At most one direct t port can reach the sink; at most two more t occurrences come from the f_x substitution. This yields cap three for t. All surviving hot/cold flag inputs are correct since on them the hot collectors see only their prescribed singleton hot input. Every negative stays negative.

Two losses of available occurrences sharpen the caps.

* When cold y entered f_h, its private path before that fork has an AND: its singleton does not activate the fork, whereas an OR-only path would. On every y+p flag this AND's sibling is one, since y must affect the output through the AND. That sibling cannot depend on y. Evaluated on the singleton p, it therefore receives a true local input or a true inlet. The only other local raw is h, which is distinct from p because h and y are nonincident. Thus at least one inlet carrying singleton p entered this collector. Cutting the collector wastes that inlet. Decrease the cap for each p in the original neighborhood of y by one, except that the extra t-to-f_x inlet might already have been counted if h=x and p=t. The checker omits this latter debit.
* For a lost hot/hot flag {a,b}, take the first collector whose original value one at that flag is missed by its substituted value. Earlier inlets then agree on the flag. Neither endpoint can be the owner of the missed collector, since an owner activates its own replacement. Every earlier inlet that is true on the flag is represented by its singleton footprint there. If no incoming port carried a, the collector would already be one on singleton b, contrary to the missed substitution. Thus a port carrying a is wasted, and similarly for b. For t this debit is distinct from the original f_t-to-f_x routing debit: f_x cannot be missed at an input containing t. Decrease each endpoint's original cap by at least one, without double counting another lost flag or the cold-contamination debit.

Enumerate every subset of the surviving hot/hot flags as possible losses. For each resulting positive graph, if an accepted edge has two once-read endpoints and both have at least two accepted neighbors, it is impossible. The two other neighbors and these endpoints give an induced P4 because the original graph is bipartite and four-cycle free. On the empty background the two endpoints must meet at an AND; on the two outer vertices their pair of unary responses forces an OR. The outer pair rejects because it is a nonincident opposite-shore pair. The same two fixed single-occurrence leaves cannot meet at both types of gate.

For the remaining patterns use the star rank test on exact, retained proper-neighborhood columns, dropping lost flags. To certify a negative, it suffices that some retained maximum contains the two proper neighborhoods after removing pinned y. The rank lower bound on each square used by the checker remains valid if some rows are discarded: use just its surviving good edges as both row and column indices. The square off the diagonal forces return of the star center. Its t diagonal choices away from the center require rank t+1 if some diagonal points toward it, and rank d for d=t≥2. Thus at a vertex with good degree exceeding its cap, at most cap−1 diagonals may point away. The checker attempts every allowed orientation by branching on each edge. Unclamped cases, contamination only, impurity only, and both give respectively 192, 104, 40, and 8 infeasible systems of branch choices (each system checks every candidate loss set). Run check-fano-defective-seven.py.

For the contamination debit, the cold singleton y cannot activate the hot-owned fork f_h. Otherwise both the direction (h,y) and its reverse (y,h) would fail, since the owner's own paths pass through f_h. No pair of opposite directed failures occurs among the 210 two-row omissions, as checked directly from the directed witness masks. This gives the required zero baseline on the private y-arm.
