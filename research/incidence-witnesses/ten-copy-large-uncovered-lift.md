# Ten copies lift negatives with at least q√q−q uncovered points

For every sufficiently large integer plane order q, suppose a binary monotone circuit H of size s accepts every singleton flag and rejects each maximal (X_Z,Z) with |Z|=q and |X_Z|≥q√q−q. Here X_Z denotes all points outside the union of Z. Ten restricted copies with at most 10s+9 gates compute exact incidence on arbitrary point payloads with at most q active queries. The proof uses only the axioms of a finite projective plane.

Split the query lines into two colors such that each color meets every pencil in at most 2q/3 lines. Independent fair colors have this property with positive probability for sufficiently large q by a binomial tail and union over N=q²+q+1 pencils. Put L=⌊3q/4⌋.

Use the five point blocks of five-copy-maximal-negative-lift.md. Each block B has a fixed pool F_B of exactly q distinct lines missing B with every pencil containing at most ⌈q/2⌉+1 of the pool. Indeed for each affine quadrant choose floor(q/2) available outside horizontal and floor(q/2) available outside vertical lines, and add the infinity line if needed. For the enlarged small/small quadrant containing some ideal points, choose floor(q/2) outside horizontals and ceil(q/2) outside verticals instead (at least ceil(q/2) of both are available). For the block of two ideal points take floor(q/2) and ceil(q/2) lines in two other directions respectively. A finite pencil contains at most two of the selected lines; an ideal pencil sees at most a single parallel group plus the infinity line.

Form one copy of H for each point block B and each line color by setting the remaining raw inputs to zero. OR all ten outputs. Every flag survives some copy. At an independent test let Y' be the retained queries of one copy. Extend Y' to any q-set Z by adding members of F_B; this is possible since |F_B|=q. If a pencil centered at o has more than L members in Z, it has a removable member in Z outside Y', since |Y'∩pencil(o)|≤2q/3. There is an unused member of F_B off that pencil: the pool has at least q−⌈q/2⌉−1 such members, while the current Z has fewer than q−L. Exchange the two lines. Repeat until the o count is L. During every exchange each other pencil has at most q−L+1 members because it shares at most one line with the o pencil. For large q this is less than L. Thus a completion with maximum multiplicity m≤L exists, using only filler additions.

For this q-set let E be the sum of excess multiplicities on covered points. It equals |X_Z|−1. Counting intersecting pairs gives E≥q(q−1)/m. Starting with the m most concurrent lines and adding the others also gives E≥m−1+m(q−m). If m≤√q the first bound gives E≥q√q−√q. Otherwise √q≤m≤L≤q−√q, so by concavity m(q−m)≥√q(q−√q)=q√q−q. Thus the promised H rejects (X_Z,Z). The copy's retained payload lies in X_Z because Y' is independent of it and all added lines miss B. Monotonicity gives rejection at the actual copy input; this applies in every copy. The fillers and comparisons require no physical gates.

The result allows all smaller query cardinalities and all q-sets with fewer than q√q−q uncovered points to be exceptional. It does not turn a uniform average bound on the remaining q-sets into a pointwise guarantee.
