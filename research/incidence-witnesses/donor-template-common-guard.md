# Exact common-guard extraction using four donor templates

Suppose OR_p (p AND H AND C_p) computes incidence exactly on arbitrary point payloads and at most q queries in a plane of order q; N=q²+q+1. Let h be the physical gate count of H's cone. Assume at least αN distinct donors p, each with ≥βq pairwise disjoint nonempty sets K of ≤r queries outside its pencil satisfying C_p(∅,K)=1. Here α,β>0 and r≥1 are fixed. These empty-background hypotheses can come from genuine-cofilter-negative-packings.md when its richness and size conditions hold. All singleton flags activate H since H is required by every original output term.

Choose four distinct donors p_i with no three collinear. At most 3(q+1)+3 owners are forbidden at any step, so αN>3(q+1)+3 suffices. Choose one K_i per donor with the four templates pairwise disjoint: ≤3r previously used labels meet at most 3r candidates in any one disjoint family, so βq>3r suffices.

Copy H four times. In H_i set to zero every point on the union of K_i and every query through p_i. For an independent input (A,Y) with |Y|≤q−r write (A_i,Y_i) for the resulting filtered mask. Its extension (A_i∪{p_i},Y_i∪K_i) is independent: A_i avoids K_i, p_i avoids Y_i∪K_i, and the retained inputs inherit independence. It has at most q queries. If H_i accepted, monotonicity would make H and C_{p_i} both true on this extension, contradicting exactness of the original circuit. Thus each copy rejects all required negatives.

Given a flag (x,l), at most two donors lie on l. For every other donor, H_i still accepts the flag unless x lies on a member of K_i. A flag missed by every copy therefore has x at an intersection of two lines from distinct templates. Those lines are distinct. All such x belong to one explicit set E of at most 6r² points. OR the copies and append x AND (OR of the q+1 lines through x) for every x∈E. The result has size at most L=4h+3+6r²(q+2), accepts every flag, and rejects every independent input of ≤q−r queries. Zero restrictions need no new gates after constant propagation.

Use D=⌈log₂ N⌉ binary digits to assign the N query lines distinct labels. Each digit gives one explicit bipartition. Assume q−r≥rD. For any k selected lines with q−r<k≤q, form the coordinatewise majority vector of their labels, breaking ties arbitrarily. At most one selected line has that vector, so the sum of all coordinate minority counts is at least k−1. Some digit has minority size at least (k−1)/D≥r. Its larger part has at most k−r≤q−r selected lines, and hence both parts fit. If k≤q−r every split works. Conversely, for any family of D binary partitions splitting every q-set with both sizes at most q−r (r≥1), each common signature cell contains at most q−1 labels: q labels of one signature would never split. Thus D≥⌈log₂(N/(q−1))⌉ for this type of binary splitting family.

For each digit OR two copies of the L-gate circuit with query masks restricted to its two parts, preserving the whole point payload. AND the D results. All flags pass every partition regardless of overload, by monotonicity; each legal negative rejects on a good digit. The exact full-promise circuit uses at most 2DL+2D−1 gates. The partition family is explicit; choosing donors and their certificates uses the stated structural hypotheses.
