# Finite restrictions of the two-pole graph

Take the two rows to be parallel horizontal affine lines and the two directions at infinity to be distinct nonhorizontal directions. Each direction gives a perfect matching of the two rows, by its joining lines. Alternating between matchings translates the first row by a nonzero field element, hence for prime q the union is one 2q-cycle. Subdividing its matching edges by their joining lines gives the generic 4q-cycle. The four generic types in cyclic order attach to four distinct poles, periodically. The six special vertices additionally induce the five edges O–L, O–Y0, O–Y1, L–X0, L–X1.

Keeping six consecutive generic vertices and all six special vertices leaves 12 vertices and 16 edges. This is also the order-two Fano incidence graph with two adjacent generic vertices deleted: the eight generic vertices there have identical periodic attachments. For a concrete matching, denote successive retained generic vertices 0,...,5, with attached poles A,B,C,D,A,B respectively. The pairs 0–A, 1–B, 2–C, 3–D, 4–5, O–L match all vertices. Either shore supplies a six-centre star cover, giving a monotone circuit with 10 neighborhood-OR gates, six AND gates and five output-OR gates. In the outer graph every omitted generic has degree three and a retained pole neighbor. The one-batch deletion lemma buys two gates per omitted vertex using the outer three-by-three tests and retains the required two-by-two tests. A circuit lower bound of 21 on the finite promise would therefore give 2n−3 for the outer graph. The matching, size and 21-gate upper bound alone do not establish that circuit lower bound.

For an alternative identification minor, keep nine consecutive generics and identify the endpoints. This gives the full Fano graph with 15 physical inputs and 14 semantic inputs. A lower bound 27 would be needed, and two selected semantic variables can activate three physical inputs on one shore. Keeping eight consecutive generics without identification gives Fano minus one edge with 14 physical inputs, 20 edges, and target 25.

## Physical cost of basic identification minors

Suppose unused inputs are fixed to zero and there are m retained physical inputs. A threshold-two minor on t≥3 semantic variables has at most two singleton-copy labels: they must form a clique in the bipartite host since every pair triggers the function. Consequently m≥2t−2. The prefix-OR circuit costs 3t−5, at most 2m−4. For t≥5 in the outer graph every singleton-copy vertex must have degree at least four, and the four candidate poles are pairwise nonadjacent. Thus m≥2t−1 and even an additional distinct gate per identification, m−t in total, does not give strict surplus.

For a one-hot t-crown on 2t semantic variables, m≥2t. Taking the OR of corresponding left/right variables and testing threshold two costs 4t−5 on one-hot-per-shore inputs. Even two further distinct gates per identification yield at most 2m−5 when added to this upper bound. This statement concerns the one-hot promise only.
