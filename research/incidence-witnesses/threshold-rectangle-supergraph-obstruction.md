# An intersection of polynomially many flag-complete rectangle filters

Consider monotone scalar circuits on a plane of order q; write N=q²+q+1. Each filter is an OR of k rectangle products, with point and line factors computed by shared pure OR networks. Suppose a conjunction of polynomially many filters accepts every singleton flag and rejects at least a constant fraction of maximal q-line negatives. A monotone augmentation by terms that vanish on singleton flags may be globally set to zero, even when some of their internal gates have other uses; this preserves every flag and cannot weaken negative rejection. The assumptions refer to the surviving filters.

Some filter rejects at least the original rejection probability divided by the number of filters. Its false singleton-pair graph has a vertex cover C of size o(N): apply the capped-false-pair-matching estimate, for instance with threshold ceil(q log³ q), and then bipartite matching-cover duality. No iterative pinning is needed. Setting all raw sites of C to zero removes every false pair at the selected filter while retaining at least N(q+1)−(q+1)|C| genuine flags.

Write s_P,s_L for its counts of pure OR gates. Retain its point-factor DAG. View the line-factor DAG backwards, injecting the point factor of each product at the line-factor node. At each node OR all arriving contributions and pass the result to each predecessor. Raw line sites then give precisely the unions of point factors associated with that line. The total number of incoming contributions across all nodes is at most k+2s_L. The resulting point-to-all-stars OR circuit therefore needs at most s_P+2s_L+k≤2s gates where s is the physical size of the original full DAG, irrespective of sharing across filters.

In an OR circuit, an output with d distinct raw points requires at least d−1 gates with nonsingleton support in its cone: suppress singleton-valued gates, then use the connected graph wire count joining d distinct sources to one output. Such a gate cannot serve two distinct surviving lines because their stars meet in at most one point. Hence 2s≥Nq−(q+1)|C|. For this architecture the asymptotic constant is at least 1/2.

The conjunction assumption forces every selected filter to accept every flag. For threshold combinations of partial filters with threshold strictly below the number of filters, a filter with small false-pair cover might accept very few genuine flags; the proof does not supply a flag-complete sparse filter. Arbitrary monotone feature factors with accurate singleton profiles may also accept unrelated multisite masks; they do not give the OR reconstruction above.

## Partial filters under a monotone combiner

The same asymptotic constant holds for a threshold (m−r)-of-m of polynomially many partial filters when r=o(q/log³ q). More generally, suppose every rejected evaluation of a monotone combiner admits a zero certificate consisting of at most b filter positions, and b ceil(q log³ q)=o(N). A zero certificate means that setting those positions to zero forces rejection even when every other position is one.

Put M=ceil(q log³ q). With probability 1−o(1) all filters whose false-pair graphs have matchings of size ≥M evaluate to one on the same random maximal negative, by the capped-matching estimate and a polynomial union bound. As the combiner rejects a constant fraction of those negatives, pick one rejected input on that event. Choose its zero certificate J. Each selected filter is zero and has a vertex cover of its false pairs of size <M. Their union C has size ≤bM. Every flag must be accepted by a filter in J, since otherwise the certificate forces that flag to reject. For the threshold take b=r+1. After pinning C to zero, the union of the selected filters therefore has exactly the surviving incidence pairs. No filter alone must cover many flags.

Collect the distinct physical rectangle-product gates reaching the OR frontiers of J. A product shared by several filters is taken once. Distributive expansion of pure factors is unnecessary. Write k for the number of retained products, s_P for the number of distinct relevant point OR gates, and s_L for the corresponding line OR gates, before restriction. All three classes are disjoint physical gates. Restriction is done by zero-valued raw annotations; no structural simplification or multi-certificate duplication is required. Ignore zero or empty factors or leave a zero annotation. Direct raw point factors are free, as are direct raw line factors. One may prune an originally empty constant branch; a safe rectangle with a constant-one side cannot yield a nonempty product on the empty opposite shore in a literal two-shore edge-existence filter. This theorem concerns pure nonempty disjunction factors, or identically zero ones, and ordinary flag products; independent extra singleton terms are outside this rectangle model.

Seed one contribution per product at its line-factor node, even when that node itself is raw. Process the line OR DAG from outputs to inputs. At a node combine arriving contributions by OR (zero if none, no gate if one); for a binary OR gate pass that combined wire once down each input incidence. An input repeated twice contributes at most two arrivals and may be deduplicated. Only retained raw line sites are reported; restriction of removed raw lines to zero can equivalently be implemented by discarding their readouts. Induction on the reverse order identifies each node's combined wire with the union of the point factors of all products whose line factors have a downward path to the node. This gives, for each surviving raw line, the OR of precisely its surviving incident points. Number of arrivals is at most k+2s_L, even with raw seeds, zero gates, multiple roots or reconvergence. Thus the constructed circuit size is ≤s_P+k+2s_L≤2s without charging OR collectors or forming the scalar union physically.

Deleting C loses at most (q+1)|C| flags. Summing max(d−1,0) for the resulting stars loses at most another N from the remaining flag count; this includes zero and singleton stars and applies on the original full projective boundary. Accordingly

    2s ≥ Nq − (q+1)b ceil(q log³ q).

For an arbitrary monotone combiner with no bound on zero-certificate size the simultaneous cover union may encompass all vertices. The earlier warning about incomplete individual filters still applies; the certificate is what permits an exact union.

## Exact reverse merge count

Tighten the factor two by taking just the downward closure of the line factors of the distinct collected products. Let l be its binary line OR gates, u its distinct raw line nodes, p the needed binary point OR gates, and t the distinct product seeds. For t>0 every node in this closure receives at least one arrival, either from a seed or from a predecessor in reverse order. Before any constant or duplicate simplification, there are exactly 2l+t arrivals and l+u destinations, even with duplicate operands counted as separate incidences. A destination with a arrivals costs a−1 binary OR gates. Thus the reverse merges cost exactly l+t−u. Constant raw restrictions can be applied after this construction and never increase its size. The point-only multioutput circuit uses at most p+l+t−u≤s−u physical gates. Unused raw lines have zero readout and contribute zero lower-bound demand. If there are no seeds, the demand is zero. In particular the final certificate inequality is

    s ≥ Nq − (q+1)b ceil(q log³ q).

This last inequality supersedes the factor-two estimates above. In fact if all d>0 output lines are among the u counted raw line nodes, the sharper lower bound ∑max(d−1,0)=|E_surv|−#{d>0}, together with the −u reverse saving, gives s≥|E_surv|≥N(q+1)−(q+1)b ceil(q log³ q). The inclusion holds because a positive induced edge has a witnessing retained product whose line DAG reaches that raw line.
