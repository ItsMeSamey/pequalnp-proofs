# Affine block circuit on a bounded-occupancy promise

Let q be prime, k fixed, and N=q²+q+1. In the affine chart partition points (x,y) by x and nonvertical lines (a,b), with equation y=ax+b, by slope a. Suppose each such block contains at most k active sites. There is a monotone binary circuit of size O_k(N log² q) for incidence under this promise. Every physical wire admits a vertex cover of at most 2q for its accepted nonincident singleton pairs.

Take a family of O_k(log q) functions from the q coordinate values to O(k²) colors such that every subset of at most 2k values is colored injectively by at least one function. Independently random colors in a palette of at least twice binom(2k,2) make each fixed set injective with probability at least one half. The union bound over all subsets yields this family; it can also be selected by finite search. No uniform generation cost is charged in circuit size.

For each hash, block and color bin, form an occupancy bit and two ORs per coordinate bit, for sites whose bit is one and zero respectively. Reuse them for all partner blocks. Building all these ORs separately over all bins costs O(q² log q) per hash since the bins partition each block. For each pair of blocks (x,a) precompute d=ax modulo q. Each color pair tests y=(b+d) modulo q from the four collections of rails. Binary addition of a known constant, comparison and one conditional subtraction, and equality take O(log q) two-input Boolean gates. Compile to monotone dual rails by exchanging rails at negations and using De Morgan for complementary outputs. AND the true output with both bin occupancies. All constant gates can be folded. OR these results over colors, AND over hashes for this pair of blocks, then OR across block pairs.

Any true flag activates its exact coordinate rails in every hash and remains accepted by monotonicity, even with collisions. For a negative choose, separately for each block pair, a hash injective on the union of its active y and b values. Both occupied bins then give true binary encodings. Empty bins cannot activate a test. Thus each block pair rejects. There are q² block pairs and O_k(1) tests per pair per hash. The cost including bin preparation is O_k(q² log² q).

Vertical line x=c contributes its selector times the OR of points in its column. Each point at infinity contributes its selector times the OR of affine lines of its direction, including the vertical direction as one group. Finally the line at infinity contributes its selector times the OR of all points at infinity. All cost O(N) gates, and these terms are exact on the full cube.

Before a color-pair test is occupancy gated, all its nonconstant internal signals vanish at empty and depend only on at most q raw points and q raw lines. These sites cover its false singleton-pair graph. Each gated test is exact on its singleton-pair inputs even in a hash with collisions, because at most one active site occurs in either bin. Its accepted pairs are therefore flags, as are those of all collectors above it. A boundary pure OR has at most q+1 sites, and its gated term has no false pairs.

This applies in particular to at most k actives on each shore. Concentrating arbitrarily many points in a single column or all q active lines in one slope block is permitted in the official scalar problem and is outside the bounded-block proof. The O_k(N log² q) upper bound is eventually below Nq/100 for fixed k; this asymptotic comparison gives no such numeric conclusion at q=401.
