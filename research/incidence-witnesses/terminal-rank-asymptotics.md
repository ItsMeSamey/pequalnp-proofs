# Linear semantic rank on an increasing chain of incidence-negative inputs

Assume a fixed C>=0 and s<=4N+Cq along any sequence of growing plane orders. Each geometric pin selects an independent mask and zeros all its neighbors. The nested accumulated positive sets P_i are independent. At each step ignore constant shared wires and pick a currently nonconstant shared gate true on a proper-neighborhood KW negative or endpoint singleton. It was zero at P_(i-1), else monotonicity would make it constantly one on all surviving extensions. It becomes one at P_i for all future rows. Distinct chosen columns have a unit lower triangular evaluation matrix at these negative inputs. See terminal-dirty-pinning.md for the zero-cost hoist which guarantees such a choice whenever the matching test requires more than s+1 leaves.

Suppose Q raw sites were fixed to zero in advance, with limsup Q/N<=b<1. At a current cap of z positive pins per shore, choose equal-sized surviving vertex sets on each side of size m=N-(q+1)z-Q. Isolated pinned positives may be included. If m/N stays bounded below and d is fixed, d successive maximum matchings furnish d(m-o(N)) edges. For example the maximal-matching bound in terminal-dirty-pinning.md gives at most N(i+sqrt(q))/(q+1+sqrt(q))=o(N) unmatched vertices when i prior matchings have been removed. Thus for x=z/q any fixed d>=2 with (d+1)(1-b-x)>4 forces a new dirty column, at per-shore pin cost at most d-1. For x bounded away from 1-b this also leaves the q-1 cap needed for the d-1 extra tested sites.

Consequently the liminf of the number of nested independent dirty steps divided by q is at least the integral from 0 to 1-b of 1/(floor(4/y)-1) dy. For rigor take finitely many intervals 4/(d+1)<y<4/d, bounded away from 0, trim arbitrarily small open neighborhoods of their endpoints, and apply each fixed-d threshold with uniform strict slack. The gap between two such stages can be crossed using the larger neighboring d; integer rounding costs only O(1) steps per interval. Then send the small trims to zero and the finite cutoff to infinity. At b=0 the lower bound is sum over d>=4 of 4/[(d-1)d(d+1)]=1/6. This conclusion applies directly to original sparse circuits. In particular the number of original shared gates divided by q has lower limit at least 1/6, witnessed by a triangular submatrix on negative inputs.

For the bilateral hoist and width w=floor(q/[16(C+8)]), near-linear-wide-sharing.md shows that simultaneously removing every width-at-most-w shared gate costs at most Q=N/8 raw zeros. Thus b=1/8 is valid. The initial partial interval contributes (7/8-4/5)/3=1/40 and the full intervals for d>=5 sum to 1/10. The wide-column rank in the hoist has lower limit at least 1/8 after division by q. Each wide hoisted shared gate is a distinct original shared gate of at least that width; its accepting independent witness also transfers. Earlier zero values need not transfer, so the wide-column rank statement concerns the hoist while the count applies to the original. This is a necessary architecture for the stipulated size window and does not prove an unrestricted order-401 gate bound.

Keep original gate IDs during pinning: annotate constants but do not bypass nonconstant unary identities. Cutting remaining original shared IDs produces formula fragments with disjoint original raw occurrences. After constant folding and final OR assembly their total leaf count is at most U=s+1-F, where F is internal excess fanout. Thus for s/N<=4+o(1) and F/N>=eta-o(1), replace the asymptotic target 4 by gamma=4-eta in the stage integral (using d>=2). In particular eta=1 gives rank/q lower limit at least 1/4 and eta=2 gives at least 1/2. This couples duplication to semantic rank without requiring disjoint gate sets. Rank independently audited the original-ID convention and the 1/6 and 1/8 claims.
