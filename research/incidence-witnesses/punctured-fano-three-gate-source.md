# One unsafe source needs more than two private feeder gates

Use the retained productive safe source cones and the physical counts d,h in punctured-fano-mixed-and.md. Delete silent subgates on the singleton flags within the safe cones and purify them as in that note. Doing this on all uses of these cleaned wires preserves every flag evaluation, including uses at other gates, and only decreases negative evaluations. The remaining accessible safe-cone wires, and all original raws, are pure-shore signals. Their singleton masks completely determine their behavior on a flag.

Suppose d=1 and h≤2. If the single other source is productive, it and its outside feeder gates are a one-output cone on pure boundary signals with at most three binary slots. To be unsafe, at least one feeder must have been a warm mixed AND. After cleanup, either the structure simplifies into a separated cone already or the terminal AND plus a feeder AND remain. Repeated internal wires in a three-gate one-output cone either give an idempotent gate or a gate of the form g op (g op' v), which can be simplified to g or the second operand gate by absorption or idempotence. All-AND trees regroup by shore without increased cost. With one OR and two ANDs the remaining tree is C AND (A OR B), where A,B,C are conjunctions on disjoint wire occurrences, and one of the three conjunctions contains two occurrences. A prefix AND above an OR with its second AND in a different branch has this same normal form by associativity. Nonproductive terms in the distribution may be suppressed. A productive single term must contain leaves on both shores; a one-shore term accepting a flag would accept a forbidden singleton. Every productive resulting rectangle is a star, by C4-freeness and the rejection of nonincident flags. Statements below about sets refer to singleton supports. Replacing a pure factor whose support is a singleton by its raw center preserves its contribution on flags and can only decrease its value on all inputs.

If C has two leaves on the same shore, both productive alternative leaves must be on the opposite shore, giving a separated expression by grouping their OR. If C has opposite-shore leaves P,L and A,B have the same shore, again group their OR before the shorewise intersection. In the remaining case the stars are (P∩A)×L and P×(L∩B), with A on the point shore. If |P|,|L|≥2, both contracted sides have size one and the two raw-centered products and one join cost three gates. If |P|=1, either the first rectangle is empty or it contains the entire second; similarly when |L|=1. In the single surviving term at most two pure intersections/products are required.

If A has the two leaves (the case of B is symmetric), write D for C. The term DB must have B on the opposite shore if productive. If both leaves of A are on B's shore, group A OR B on that shore; if both are on D's shore then AD cannot be productive. Otherwise write U for the A leaf on D's shore and V for its other leaf. The stars are (D∩U)×V and D×B. If D is singleton the first is either empty or all of D×V, and the products combine at the common raw center. Otherwise B is a singleton b. If V⊆{b}, keep only D×B. Else choose v∈V other than b. The set D∩U is in both b's and v's neighborhoods, so contains at most one label. Use the resulting raw center against V, and b against D, plus an OR. With only one productive distribution term in any of the cases, at most three single-shore intersections/products suffice directly.

Thus the sole unsafe source can be replaced with at most its three slots, using separated products. Fresh intermediate ORs of products feed the final collector and may be flattened. If fewer than three slots were present, the two-gate AND-only case is already separated by regrouping, and a single feeder OR with no mixed AND is safe. This contradicts the separated-product lower bound for the size-20 candidate. Consequently d=1 implies h≥3. Reusing centers in the separate flag-repair bound also permits counting only the vertex cover of missing edges with both endpoints outside all existing oriented safe centers.
