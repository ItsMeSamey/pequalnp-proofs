# Six hot owners under a direction-defective double omission

Use the same eighteen-gate hypothesis and nineteen retained maximal negatives as in punctured-fano-defective-seven.md. Six distinct owners must fill one shore, since the six other raws are independent. On either choice of hot shore H there is at most one cold variable y that can first reach a hot fork f_h, and its degree is two. If no such contamination occurs, the unowned-fork projection and pins of punctured-fano-two-omission-projected-clean.md apply directly since they use only clean cold paths and retained maxima after this initial separation. The same direct enumeration with the initial directional filter omitted verifies these clean-owner cases even for the twelve exceptional pairs. If all forks are owned, or the seventh unowned fork X has no cold raws, pinning y leaves five unique cold paths and their eighteen-gate lower bound, against at most seventeen gates.

Suppose y actually enters f_h and a nonempty set S of other cold raws first meets the unowned fork X. First pin y=0, saving at least one gate. If X ceases to branch, retain bypasses temporarily as unary identity wires to track paths through the original graph. A remaining raw c formerly entering X could have two paths only by subsequently reaching a still-branching old hot-owned fork f_a. Every path from c would then pass through that fork, which is already true on the surviving owner singleton a. The directed test for (a,c) forbids this because y is the only defective cold target. Contraction of unary wires cannot create two paths absent in the identity-wire graph. Thus all five remaining cold variables have unique paths and cost at least eighteen. Otherwise relocate X as before by cutting both distinct uses and adding its signal at the output through one OR. This costs at most sixteen gates, with five unique cold paths. Its projected retained maxima are computed on the pinned domain (all y coordinates zero). The checker tests 496 profiles, comprising sixteen orientation/omission combinations and all 31 nonempty S for each. For 400 profiles the certified hot singleton and opposite-shore pairs are exact, contradicting the five-clean eighteen bound. For sixteen further profiles, exactly one nonflag lacks coverage. Whether or not that nonflag is accepted, the resulting five stars remain incomparable, have common neighbors for each pair, and the requisite hot cones connect all six hots. The sixteen-gate singleton-support relaxation in five-cold-lattice.md fails also when the additional entry is accepted. The 80 others have singleton S={c}.

For each such c, X rejects every hot singleton. The only possibly missing ordered test in this orientation has target y. Thus for h' outside the star of c there is a retained negative containing h' and a neighbor p of c. On c+p, the circuit must distinguish c+p from p solely through X. If X(h') were one this negative would dominate the accepted flag on X and all exterior variables. For h' inside the star take the negative singleton h' itself. This argument works after y is pinned, and X then depends only on c and hot variables.

After pinning y, force X to zero rather than relocating it. This removes at least its two readers and one cold gate in its cone: a bare c wire could not be an unowned fork. It preserves every remaining flag outside c's star since X sees a single hot input at each; all required negatives persist. If c also has degree two (16 profiles), the four remaining triple stars have unique paths and require at least fifteen gates, against at most fourteen. To verify the lower bound by the existing singleton-hot relaxation, their four private ANDs and three OR mergers leave seven slots at budget fourteen; at most two extra unary ANDs fit, and the 1, 104, 560 relaxed tuples for j=0,1,2 all fail. The remaining 64 profiles have degree-three c, leaving three triples and one pair. These four require exactly fourteen gates by punctured-fano-stray-pin.md, so all the savings just counted are exact.

In this equality case the y pin removes exactly one gate. No gate on the c-to-X path can be that sole lost gate: it would have to be the direct reader of y as well as a c descendant, putting y and c on the same path before their different first forks. More precisely y enters its direct reader before meeting f_h, unless that reader itself is f_h; in either case any c arriving there would encounter f_h rather than X. After pinning y all the c-to-X gates disappear on cutting X (c has no other path), and there is room for only one. Thus already in the original circuit c feeds X directly. Its operation is AND because X(c)=0. In particular X stays zero at c=0 even under an override of f_h: f_h differs from X, and c is a raw input.

Pin c=y=0 in the original circuit, propagate constants and bypass copies to obtain the same optimal fourteen-gate residual F (it takes only these syntactic operations and dead-gate deletion). All three binary mergers of the four cold paths are OR since the surviving star pairs intersect. No unary OR with a cold-dependent child remains: bypassing that OR to its cold child preserves exact singleton-hot/cold pair tests by the clean-tree path-intersection argument, violating the fourteen-gate lower bound. Thus any cold-dependent gate in F has value zero when all remaining cold inputs are zero, by induction at OR mergers and unary AND gates. This remains true if any hot-only signal is forced to one.

Now choose a neighbor p of y. The original flag y+p accepts. Since all paths from y to output pass through f_h, setting f_h to one makes p alone accept with c=y=0. On the ordinary unforced restriction, f_h(h)=1 and hence its forwarded signal is nonzero. Under the zero/copy propagation just used, forcing that forwarded signal high dominates forcing f_h high: each bypass transports the signal or ignores a constant zero; overriding a shared earlier signal may raise additional uses. If the signal is dead, overriding f_h has no effect on the output. In the surviving circuit its value on hot singleton h is one, so it must be hot-only. But the preceding cold-gate induction says even overriding it cannot activate the output on p alone. This contradiction excludes the tight case.
