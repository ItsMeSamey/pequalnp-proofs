# Two-shore OR splits on one actual independent input

Fix a legal independent negative n and let I be its active point and line sites. For each physical OR g=a OR b mark plus flags as native singleton flags accepted by a but not b and minus flags symmetrically. Define K_g to consist of vertices on either shore whose incident flags include both signs.

For each active v in I, at least q distinct physical OR gates g satisfy g(n)=0 and v in K_g. Start q+1 tokens, one per singleton flag incident to v, at the output. Process wires in reverse topological order, collecting all demands at a shared wire. Each visited wire is true on its demanded flags and false on n. At an AND send all demands to one low child. At an OR, if one child accepts every demanded flag send all to it. Otherwise each child has a demanded flag private to it and each token is sent to an accepting child. Each flag's only upward raw discrepancy from n is its endpoint opposite v, since v is active and n is independent. The resulting connected binary DAG must end at q+1 distinct raws, hence has at least q branching vertices, all ORs with the stated sign property.

Thus q|I| ≤ sum over physical ORs g false on n of |K_g intersect I|. No two active vertices share a flag because I is independent. Each term is bounded by the smaller of the two numbers of plus and minus flags touching I. A broad mixed OR can serve many active vertices; the footprint is explicit, not counted as as many physical gates.

Take n_l=(all points off l,query l) in turn for each l. An OR charged in two rows cannot accept two distinct bare point singletons, since rejection on n_l puts all its bare point support in l. Therefore among the qN row/gate branching obligations, at most the number of physical ORs with at least two bare point activations can be placed on those rich gates. All remaining obligations occur at ORs whose bare point support is empty or a singleton. At least one private flag of each such split genuinely requires both endpoints at this OR, since the bare query also lies in n_l. This counts distinct gate/line pairs and does not double-count cumulative upstream cones. A local semantic sufficient condition for Nq≤100s is that no OR split on more than 100 different anchored lines in this sense. This extra restriction does not follow from unrestricted scalar correctness.
