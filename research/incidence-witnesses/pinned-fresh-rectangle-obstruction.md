# An indispensable AND can create almost all flags after a large pin

Fix h≤q−2 query lines through O, to be set true; delete all covered point sites. Of the remaining pencil lines choose T of size r=q−h. Put A equal to their points other than O (size rq) and U equal to the q² nonpencil lines together with T. Every one of the q+1 neighbors of each p∈A is in U. Choose J∈T. The set J* consisting of J without O and one representative on each other T-line meets every member of U. Both T and J* use only surviving sites. At fixed h/q<1 these sets give the following mixed gate on a pinned residual:

    B=(OR of all point inputs in A) AND (OR of all query inputs in U).

B first accepts all |A||U| opposite-shore pairs from its point-only and line-only operands. This includes |A|(q+1)=Θ(Nq) flags and Θ(N²) nonflags. The constructions below use B to compute exact scalar incidence on A×U. For the original or full residual graph add the ordinary row-star output for points outside A. There are no missing flags from A to queries outside U.

**Linear overhead relative to row stars.** Select distinct anchor flags (a,t₀), (b,m), with t₀∈T, b∈J*, m∈U. Form

    Hₗ = t₀ AND [AND over t∈T−{t₀} of (x_a OR t)]
    Hₚ = x_b AND [AND over p∈J*−{b} of (m OR x_p)].

The first equals the anchor flag OR the conjunction of all T queries. The second equals its anchor flag OR the conjunction of all J* points. Let C be their disjunction with the row-star computation of all other flags on A×U, omitting both anchors from that computation. Every constituent gate accepts an actual flag. Then B AND C computes incidence there on the full cube: when both shores are nonempty all T queries hit every point in A, and all J* points hit every query in U. Its extra cost over the row-star upper bound is O(N). Each operand of B is indispensable: with exactly T and no A-points, C is true but the output must reject; with all J* points and no U queries, C is again true but incidence is false. Both tests remain legal after pinning, since h+|T|=q. They may be extended to maximal negative point sets. This efficient variant contains locally resolvable gates inside its auxiliary factors.

**Resistance to arbitrary single-parent resolutions.** There is also a larger construction where every physical gate in the A×U decoder resists replacing it by either operand on the residual promise. Use the bipartite incidence graph on A∪U. For each maximal independent set M meeting both shores, let K_M be the OR of the raws in its complement. Define C as the AND of these K_M. Each flag satisfies all clauses. Every independent input with both shores nonempty extends to a mixed maximal independent set and hence fails a clause. Thus B AND C computes incidence. Discard redundant clauses until the remaining family is inclusion-minimal under the actual sparse residual promise with B present. It is nonempty when the graph contains a nonincident A×U pair.

For each remaining clause there is a legal negative where B and every other clause are true. Removing any AND subtree of clauses therefore changes a legal output. Every literal v in K_M has a neighbor w∈M by maximality, and the flag (v,w) satisfies the clause using only v. Hence dropping either side of any binary OR inside a clause loses a singleton flag. The two pure OR pools defining B have the same indispensable-leaf property. For each mixed M, some query of T lies outside M because M contains a point in A, and some point of J* lies outside M because M contains a U query. Thus C accepts both pure T and pure J*, again making both operands of B indispensable. At the final AND, dropping B fails on those pure examples and dropping C fails on a nonincident pair with B true. Every intermediate gate is flag-live.

One may share a physical nonraw OR between the two branches reconverging at B AND C. Choose a surviving clause. Its independent set contains some u∈U, so its complement contains all A-neighbors of u, of which there are at least r≥2. Join two such points by one OR and use the same gate in both the clause and B's point pool. Its one-parent resolutions still lose flags. A full pre-pin circuit can start with all mixed clauses (correct without any query limit), followed by the stated redundant-clause removals after pinning. This clause construction can be exponential and does not imply any improvement in the unrestricted upper bound or assert global size minimality.
