# Eliminating a singleton or a mixed-degree doubleton at the unowned fork

Use the dense 18-gate parent with all flags, at least 19 retained maximal negatives, six singleton-hot raws on one shore, and one unowned fork X of fanout two. Let S be the cold raws first branching at X. Assume every other cold raw has a unique path to the output. A pair of distinct hot vertices belongs to exactly one maximum, since all maxima contain at most two hot vertices and the punctured-Fano incidence list gives exactly one such maximum for each hot pair. In particular different pairs containing the same hot vertex have distinct witnesses. All singletons and all opposite-shore nonflags reject.

If S={c} and c has three neighbors, pinning it suffices after at least two gate savings. If c is itself the fork, both distinct readers simplify. If the sole first reader is AND, pinning kills it and simplifies at least one subsequent consumer. Otherwise that reader is c OR g. The operand g has only hot ancestors: another cold reaching this operand would reach X, and a second path from c is impossible before its first fork. The operand rejects each singleton h. Indeed select a neighbor p of c with h+p rejected. For h in the star choose p=h, and otherwise the three pure pairs h+p have distinct witnessing maxima, so at least one survives the omissions. If g(h) were one, the reader and all exterior variables on h+p would dominate their values on c+p, contradicting rejection. Bypass the OR to c. This lowers the circuit and preserves every original flag on a singleton hot input because g is zero on these hot singletons. Pin c to zero and at least one further reader simplifies. The remaining five clean cold paths with their singleton/pair tests need seventeen gates by five-cold-triple-pin.md. The supposed eighteen-gate circuit has at most sixteen left, a contradiction. A degree-two singleton instead is already excluded by one gate saving and the eighteen-gate five-cold bound.

If S={a,b} with degrees two and three, X rejects every hot singleton h. Choose p in the union of their neighborhoods with h+p rejected, using the same argument since the union has at least three elements. On p alone the output rejects, whereas p+c accepts for a neighboring c in S. All the paths carrying this change pass through X. A positive X(h) would let h+p dominate that accepted assignment at X and all exterior variables. The inputs to X depend only on the hot shore and S.

The unique routes from a,b before X meet at an OR: their stars have a common hot neighbor, and from that hot singleton either leaf alone switches their merger and then the output, impossible at an AND. Each has a private AND before this merge. If the path from a had only ORs, then a would force the merger high on a hot neighbor of b outside a's star, and a would substitute for b on a required flag despite rejection. The argument for b is identical. These three distinct gates have no exterior use except through X.

Pin a,b to zero and force X to zero. This keeps every remaining cold/hot flag because X evaluates to zero on its singleton-hot input and cannot see the other cold leaves. All required negatives remain rejected. The three private/merger gates disappear (including X if the merger is X); both distinct downstream readers of X also simplify. Thus at most thirteen gates remain. All four remaining cold variables still have unique paths. Their three triple stars and one doubleton star have pairwise nonempty intersections, forcing all three mergers to be OR. The four private filters and three mergers leave at most six hot gates and extra unary filters; the three triple supports connect the six hot raws, requiring at least five hot gates. The same singleton-support lattice test as five-cold-lattice.md with j≤1 extra filters and hot budget 6−j rejects the 1 and 186 relaxed target tuples for every such pin pair. Run check-four-cold-mixed-pin.py. The upper bound is fourteen gates by the direct star formula. This contradicts the residual thirteen-gate budget.

All 16 previously surviving choices of (one omitted maximum, two-element S) in each orientation of punctured-fano-unowned-fork.md have degrees two and three. The mixed-degree pin argument excludes them without further assumptions about which maximum was omitted.
