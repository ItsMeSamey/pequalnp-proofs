# A single linear-size mixed bank on an independent random chain

For prime q let N=q²+q+1. Fix the circuit first, then draw the ordered q−1 line background W uniformly without replacement, as in earlier-mixed-receiver-tradeoff.md. Write X for the points missed by W. With probability tending to one, all but o(N) affine points of X have the same physical thin-oriented receiver at every stage for all q of their nonvertical flags. The common earlier mixed sibling has candidate row size at most 2q at every earlier prefix for all these points. This realizes the numerical 1/e fraction of all points and misses only o(N) surviving points. The bank costs O(N); the exact full-plane completion costs Θ(Nq). This example imposes no geometric cover bound on the intermediate global point-coordinate signals.

## Fixed physical bank

Choose m a power of two in [q^(1/3),2q^(1/3)). Write each representative intercept b as three base-m digits (b0,b1,b2), padding by zeroes. For every affine slope a make two collections of pure ORs on query inputs. A group in the first collection fixes (b0,b1), and one in the second fixes (b0,b2). Each collection partitions the q intercepts; building both costs at most 2q gates per slope. Two specified groups with the same prescribed triple intersect in just its one possible line.

From the affine point inputs compute nonemptiness and the existential 0/1 rails for each binary coordinate bit. To obtain x rails, OR the q points within each x column, then build aligned dyadic OR trees on these q indicators padded by zero. For bit j and either digit value OR the corresponding aligned intervals of length 2^j. Summing over j costs O(q) extra beyond the column ORs. Repeat for y using rows. Thus the entire point encoder costs O(q²), and on a single affine point it supplies its exact coordinates.

For each slope a compute t=y−ax mod q using hardwired standard binary arithmetic on both rails. Negation swaps rails, and De Morgan implements the complement of each gate. This costs polynomial in log q per slope. Test each of the at most m² group addresses in both collections against the appropriate two digit fields of t. AND the matching indicator with its query group OR and OR the results within each collection. The slope verdict requires both collections to match. Gating by point nonemptiness and ORing over slopes gives H0. Addresses cost O(q m² log q)=o(q²), including the ORs that collect them. Put H=H0 OR [at least two global points]. The latter costs O(N) by maintaining nonemptiness and a two-point flag in a binary tree. For each affine p form the physical receiver R_p=p AND H. The bank including all these receivers costs O(N). All gates are monotone; multiple active points may produce spurious coordinate encodings.

## Probability and candidate labels

For a fixed affine p=(x,y) and slope a, let S1,S2 be the two raw query groups addressed by t=y−ax. Their sizes are at most m and their only common element is the unique slope-a line through p. If p belongs to X but H(W+p) is true then in some slope both groups are hit by distinct elements of W. The probability of containing any prescribed pair is at most q²/[N(N−1)]. Summing over q slopes and at most m² pairs per slope yields O(m²/q)=O(q^(−1/3)). No conditioning on a later choice of the chain is taken. Hence with high probability only o(N) points of X are spoiled, by expectation and Markov. All other points satisfy H(V+p)=0 at every prefix V of W, by monotonicity. Also H(V+l)=0 for every query l, while H(V+p+l)=1 for every incident nonvertical l, since the two addressed groups contain it.

Uniform candidate widths also hold. For a good p and slope a not already matching both groups, a new l must equal the common incident line if neither group has been hit. If either group has been hit, at most m new labels can supply the missing group. The union bound gives probability at most 2m(q−1)/N that either group is ever hit for this fixed p,a. Define D(p) as m times the number of slopes whose groups are ever hit at p. Then E D(p)=O(m²). For every good point, q+D(p) bounds all earlier candidate widths simultaneously; adding a vertical or boundary label cannot affect H0. The expected number of points with D(p)>q is O(Nm²/q)=o(N); Markov again shows that whp all but o(N) points qualify with width ≤2q. Since |X|=(e^(−1)+o(1))N whp in the random-background experiment and only O(q) points are at infinity, (e^(−1)−o(1))N affine receivers qualify throughout.

## Exact completion and forced trace

For each affine p put Y_p equal to the OR of its q incident nonvertical lines, and include R_p AND Y_p as its row. OR the rows and add exact flags involving the line at infinity, a point at infinity or a vertical line. Every genuine affine flag forces H on its singleton pair, and monotonicity preserves acceptance on larger payloads. The factor p AND Y_p ensures no independent input is accepted. Thus this completion is exact on the full cube even though the shared bank on its own is approximate.

At any stage W_i and affine flag (p,l) above, F=W_i+p+l accepts only the indicated row. At P=W_i+p, Y_p=0 and H=0. On Z=W_i+l plus X off l, p=0, Y_p=1, H=1 by the two-point gate, since whp |X|≫q. The same holds with the full maximal independent point payload. Consequently the row's AND has only R_p as a child rejecting both negatives. At R_p the raw p and H have opposite negative evaluations. The same orientation is therefore forced physically throughout all stages. At the earlier prefix V, H is mixed and its candidate row is bounded as above. Sharing H across all p is counted exactly once. The terminal star ORs, not the row bank, account for Θ(Nq) gates in this explicit full decoder.

The construction settles neither arbitrary sharing among the terminal filters nor the additional restriction that every intermediate point pool have a small geometric cover. Its binary coordinate rails are unions of many rows or columns. All asymptotic claims refer to random backgrounds sampled independently of this fixed circuit.

## Square orders with normalized intermediate signals

For q=p², p prime, retain every estimate above and also impose one geometric line for each pure point singleton support, and a q+1 vertex cover for every false singleton-pair graph. Start with the O(N) cap-one circuit K in low-rich-square-order-decoder.md. That construction has the stated intermediate properties, accepts every singleton flag, and rejects all pure singleton inputs and nonincident singleton pairs. Its behavior on multiquery negatives is unrestricted here.

Replace affine point inputs u to the global coordinate encoder by u AND K. Use the resulting gated affine nonemptiness to guard H0. At every singleton flag K=1; on W+u either the same coordinates are seen or no point is seen. Thus the earlier ghost counting only improves. For the candidate bound, adding a query can switch K to one; even then a positive verdict requires the original ungated digit test to match, and the count was proved for points where the ungated digit test rejects on the entire W. Keep that stronger definition of good. With no point the occupancy test always fails. Replace the global threshold-two of H by the OR of tests for two distinct points in one affine column. Every threshold subcircuit can be built with only column-local nonempty ORs, and the final column verdicts are zero on all singleton pairs. Any sizeable Z with more than q affine points triggers it, as required with high probability here.

All global point-feature wires start with the gated points, which are zero on every pure singleton and every nonincident singleton pair. Constant folding preserves this fact for nonconstant monotone functions of those signals alone. Every comparator for a digit-pair address with a nonempty query group is nonconstant (vary y for fixed x,a); it is therefore also zero when the gated point signals vanish. The product of that comparator and its pure query group vanishes on all false singleton pairs, as do ORs of these products. Any pure group or partial OR on the raw query side uses at most m labels and lies in one parallel pencil. The local threshold-two subcircuits have cover at most q and pure point supports confined to a column. K has its original normalized signal properties; all final affine and boundary completions can be built with pure stars of size ≤q+1 and exact branch outputs. Thus the geometric and false-pair conditions apply also to the completed circuit.

To evaluate y−ax in characteristic p, encode a field element by its two coefficients in a fixed F_p basis. Binary arithmetic on coefficients takes polynomial in log q size per slope, including conversion of the residual to its label integer in [0,q−1] (first coefficient plus p times the second) and vice versa. The three digits are taken from this integer. Coordinate-group preparation works on either representation: first obtain raw ORs per x and per y value; binary digits of the integer labels use aligned intervals, and changing labels to coefficient pairs can instead be implemented by a binary conversion after the dual rails. Labels remain in bijection with field elements, so the fiber sizes and unique-common-line proof are unchanged.

Only the point encoder, including its grouped nonempty gates, is gated. Raw p in R_p remains unchanged, so the split at R_p is still forced. This normalized linear-size bank is asserted for the square orders supported by K. The unguarded prime-order construction proves the random-chain row profile without the intermediate normalization.
