# Hoisting the OR spines of sole-use variables

The unrestricted point domain matters. Fix a point x and a line m avoiding x. Let B be the AND of all point variables on m. On a mask with B true, every line through x already has an active incident point on m. Thus substituting x OR B for x in an exact incidence circuit preserves correctness on every mask. The only raw use of x is this OR. The sibling rejects all singletons, yet hoisting B to the output would accept the independent mask consisting of all points on m and no lines.

There is an exact positive result on bounded masks. Suppose the original circuit is correct on the sparse promise through at least three selected lines, with arbitrary point sets, and q≥2. For an ordinary x with only one outgoing occurrence, follow its OR spine as long as each intermediate signal has a single consumer that is an OR. Stop with the first OR signal of fanout at least two, immediately before the first non-OR consumer, or at the output. Only inputs with an initial OR are under discussion. Each off-spine sibling is independent of x since no path from x can escape the preceding single-use signals. The spine exit equals x OR B, where B is the disjunction of the off-spine siblings.

On every independent mask with at most two points and two lines, B is zero. Delete x if present without changing B. Choose a neighbor y of x avoiding the at most two selected vertices on x's side, using codegree at most one and degree q+1>2. Adjoining y preserves independence, and then adjoining x creates an incidence. Both masks have at most three lines. If B were true, both would fix the spine exit to one, violating correctness since x is invisible outside that exit. In particular no two distinct sole-variable OR spines merge: a shared OR would give one sibling the other variable's pure singleton.

Bypass all spine gates and use each x in place of its old exit. Accumulate all off-spine sibling signals into an OR appended to the output. If the spines contain M OR gates, there are M sibling occurrences and the extra output disjunction needs at most M binary gates. All old signal evaluations weakly decrease after bypass substitutions, taking bypassed signals to mean their corresponding raw variable. So every appended sibling is zero on every small negative. If an originally accepted flag loses acceptance before the appended OR, take the earliest changed signal in a topological order. It must be a bypassed spine OR; its raw variable is zero and either its own sibling or an earlier true sibling on the same spine accounted for its old value. Earliest change implies this true sibling remains true. The updated output accepts. Monotonicity completes the proof on the entire two-by-two domain. A sole-variable spine cannot end at the output, since the raw singleton would then be accepted.

Each affected raw variable now has at least two uses if its old exit branched, or its unique consumer is an AND. If an old exit was an off-spine sibling of another selected spine, its use transfers to the appended OR aggregation rather than disappearing. Occurrence counts here refer to the literal unpruned construction; any later simplification and any loss against a desired fanout threshold must be accounted for. When the output supplements are added without deleting dead logic, an affected wire's raw uses stay at least as numerous as its old exit uses.

The construction gives no automatic large-point-promise preservation. If the original circuit is assumed correct only through two lines, even the small-domain conclusion fails. Take B to be the AND of two nonincident-to-x line variables. Then x OR B is an exact proxy through two lines, since no line through x is present when B is true. But the zero-point assignment with these two lines is a small negative activating B.
