# Beyond q banks using sparse transversals and physical OR gates

Use the layered model of shared-bank-sparse-product.md. Let q>100 and t≤2q+2. Globally mark a flag (p,l) if l belongs to the query support of some original bank-layer OR gate having at least two distinct queries all through p, or by the dual projected point support within l. The same singleton-frontier counting as in that paper gives at most 4O distinct marked flags, even when marking every such OR gate. Assign each other flag to one accepting product T.

For an assigned (p,l) let k be the number of distinct banks of T lacking raw point p. All k contain l. At least one other bank contains p, since otherwise T accepts query l with empty payload. Thus k≤t−1. If some of the k banks has its query support contained in star(p), it must have support exactly {l} for an unmarked flag. The analogous point support witness at line l always exists by the original unrestricted payload complement; for an unmarked flag it is {p}. Assigned flags with both witnesses are an isolated matching in their product as before.

Otherwise each of the k banks has a nonempty set of query labels outside star(p). These outsider sets have transversal number greater than q: a smaller transversal together with payload {p} makes T accept a legal negative. In their intersection graph a matching of m edges would give a transversal of size at most k−m by picking one common outsider for each pair. Hence k≥q+1 and m≤k−q−1. Remove both endpoints of a maximal matching. The remaining banks have pairwise disjoint outsider sets and number at least 2q+2−k≥d=2q+3−t.

For each such bank trace a physical OR-DAG path from raw l to its bank output. Along the path the query support starts as {l} and first grows to size ≥2 at an actual OR with an incoming operand of support {l}. Its support is contained in the bank's support, and has an outsider because the flag was unmarked. The first-growth gates are thus distinct for these d banks: a shared gate would put the same outsider in two disjoint sets. Across assigned flags any one physical OR can serve at most two distinct singleton line labels, each incident to q+1 possible owners. Each flag is assigned once. The number of these unwitnessed flags is therefore at most 2(q+1)O/d. When t≤q+1 there are none.

Consequently E≤[4+2(q+1)/d]O + min(t,4(log₂t)²)A with arbitrary sharing, and E≤[4+2(q+1)/d]O+2A for private product cones. The logarithmic matching estimate remains valid here: take min(m,q) pairs from an induced matching of size m and use its distinct transversals; log₂t<q. The branch-excess refinement is E≤[4+2(q+1)/d]O+2A+min(t,4(log₂t)²)F with F as in shared-bank-branch-excess.md. Thus for private product cones the official bound holds whenever 2q+3−t≥(q+1)/48, including t≤1.97q for q>100. No repeated enumeration of AND subproducts as separate physical costs is involved.

At width q+2 a strictly collinear direct witness bank can genuinely be absent. Take the product of raw p and q+1 banks, each OR of all incident queries and one distinct outsider z_i. On the promised line masks these compute p times its exact query star because no q-query input contains all q+1 outsiders. Dropping any one of the q+1 banks permits a legal independent payload {p} with the other q outsiders queried. The term can replace p's ordinary term in the exact scalar star formula on the promise. This shows why the extra physical accounting is needed; it does not assert gate minimality.
