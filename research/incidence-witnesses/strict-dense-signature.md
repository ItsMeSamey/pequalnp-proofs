# Equality in the full-plane theta bound is impossible at order 401

Write N=161203. Suppose an original scalar circuit has size 4N−4. Use the line-then-point bilateral sole-use OR hoist in sparse-point-cold.md. Its circuit computes all singleton flags and enough sparse negatives for the subsequent full-plane arguments. It has at most 4N−4 gates and at least this many by the dominating-theta bound, so equality holds. Every all-domain simplification decreasing its size is impossible. Remove constant and dead code. Every sole-use raw feeds a private AND whose sibling accepts all 402 plane-neighbor singletons. Write S for the number of sole raws. The bilateral census of mutual-cold-points.md gives S≤6461+3382=9843. Line-then-point hoisting keeps the line use counts and gives the cited sparse point cap in this normalized circuit.

For any set D of at most 399 raws, delete exactly D in the first batch. A full dominating theta avoiding these raws exists. Candidate point poles avoid the deleted points and the union of the deleted lines; symmetrically candidate line poles avoid the deleted lines and every deleted point. Each candidate set has more than two members: the union of j≥1 lines covers at most 1+401j points, and at most 399−j further points are forbidden. A complete incidence rectangle between these two candidate sets would violate codegree one. Choose a nonincident pair, giving the required full theta K. Each deleted raw has a surviving neighbor in K and each surviving raw has at least 402−399=3 surviving neighbors. The first batch uses literal zero-flow in the normalized circuit. The second deletes everything outside K, preserving its two-by-two tests. The exact theta lower bound pays the 4N−4 baseline in both batches combined. Every additional first-batch physical credit must vanish.

At the mask with exactly D deleted and all other raws true, no shared internal gate can be zero and no nonprivate AND can have unequal operand values. A deleted raw cannot have more than two outgoing occurrences, so testing all singleton D bounds every raw fanout by two. Write F for internal fanout excess, summed over nonoutput internal gates. The raw-use identity gives F=S−3. All gates are physically indispensable even on the post-hoist sparse promise, since the same 4N−4 lower bound applies there.

On all these dense masks each signal is either constantly true or evaluates as the OR of a nonempty, unique set of at most 399 raws. Call these signatures large and small, respectively. An OR unions signatures, with size at least 400 promoted to large. A private AND has the small singleton signature of its sole raw because its sibling accepts all 402 neighbors and is large. A nonprivate AND has identical operand signatures by the equality condition. No internal gate with a small signature can branch.

In fact no nonprivate AND can have a small signature T. Take the earliest such AND. Its two small operands expand through ORs of small predecessors only, stopping at ordinary raw leaves or private ANDs (the latter are tokens labeled by their sole raw). Both expressions have exactly the same label set T. A sole raw label in T would require its unique private AND token to reach both operands using only small gates. This needs a shared small gate, which was excluded. Thus both operands are full-cube ORs of ordinary raws with identical support T. Their AND is redundant, a contradiction.

Consider the DAG induced by the large gates. A source here is an OR with two small inputs whose union has at least 400 labels. Its two small predecessor expressions are private trees of ORs over ordinary raw occurrences and private AND tokens. Distinct such sources cannot share any small internal gate. Each ordinary raw supplies at most two physical input occurrences and each private AND token supplies at most one. Thus the sources number at most 4N/400. If n_i counts large gates with i large operand slots, there are n_1+2n_2 large-to-large slots, at most the number of large gates plus F by the physical fanout bound. Consequently n_2≤n_0+F. Every nonprivate AND has two large operands. The total number A of AND gates is at most S+4N/400+F.

Finally partition the OR gates into trees by cutting at each shared OR's output and every AND. Count one group per tree top: a top is the output, a branching OR, or an OR feeding an AND. The group count is at most 1+F+2A. A raw with two readers must feed two ORs, since no nonprivate AND has a raw operand and a private AND's sibling is large. Its two occurrences belong to different groups: a repeated raw inside one associative OR tree can be removed with a gate saving. Consider such a raw x and the sets of other ordinary raw labels entering its two groups. If both sets have at least two members, choose one label from each so the two do not form an incident point-line pair. Such a choice exists, for otherwise they would be disjoint opposite-shore parts of a K₂,₂. On those two labels add a plane neighbor of x avoiding conflicts with selected same-shore labels; each of at most two such labels forbids at most one of the 402 neighbors. This gives an independent negative mask. Both OR groups are already true, so adding x leaves the output unchanged, although its neighbor requires a positive flag. The negative mask uses at most three sites on either shore and the added-x mask is positive by monotonicity. The argument works with nonraw terms and when one group feeds the other: every path from x passes through a saturated group output.

Each of the 2N−S ordinary raws therefore has an occurrence in a group with at most two ordinary raw labels including itself. At least (2N−S)/2 groups are needed. Combining the inequalities, with S≤9843 and F=S−3, requires

    (322406−9843)/2 ≤ 1+(9843−3)+2(9843+644812/400+9843−3).

The left side exceeds 156281 and the right side is below 52432. This contradiction raises the unconditional order-401 bound to at least 4N−3=644809. It does not reach 646425.
