Here is the stronger residual lower bound for 1≤h≤q−4. Pick two distinct unpinned axis points x,O, and a line Y through O other than A. This Y survives; every surviving point lies outside the pinned point set and every surviving line meets A at an unpinned point. The 2(q+1) theta vertices for x,Y, excluding O and A as usual, survive. In the first batch delete all remaining surviving axis points other than x, including O, and all surviving lines through O other than Y. Every first-batch vertex has a surviving neighbor: O sees Y, all other removed axis points retain nonaxis lines through themselves, and each removed line sees points off A. These first-batch vertices have degree at least q (the point A-neighbor line already deleted and no further distinct line through an unpinned axis point meets a pin). The step of two-stage-theta.md preserves three-by-three and pays two slots per raw. After this batch every other noncore point was initially of degree at least q+1−h and loses at most one more line; remaining noncore lines initially have all their q+1 points and lose at most one point on A. Each now has a theta neighbor by joining to x or intersecting Y. Thus the second step preserves two-by-two and pays two per raw, and the terminal theta costs 4q. If the residual has n vertices it therefore costs at least 2n−4. No pin phases enter either deletion batch: apply both steps to the already compiled residual graph.
