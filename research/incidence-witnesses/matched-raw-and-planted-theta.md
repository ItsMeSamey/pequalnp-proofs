# Opposite matched raws and excess readers in one planted theta

For the bilateral normalized order-401 circuit, 1625 distinct raw-pair AND gates suffice for the scalar 646425-gate bound. Select m=1625 different operand pairs, forming a simple graph on its v nonisolated raws; a minimum circuit has no duplicate raw-pair gates. Take a maximum matching of size ν. Give each matched pair opposite survival bits with independent fair orientation per pair, and give all other raws independent fair bits. Independently plant K and D₀ from planted-theta-credit.md, overwriting the corresponding raw bits.

For an active raw x write d for its degree in the selected graph. At d<402 there is an incident plane flag (x,y) whose second endpoint y is not one of the d partners. If the d selected ANDs were x's only readers, all would stay zero when x is activated at y, contradicting the flag and singleton tests. Thus its excess above two physical uses is at least d−1. At d≥402 that excess is at least d−2≥(400/401)(d−1). Summing gives at least (400/401)(2m−v) excess units. The ν matching edges and one incident edge per uncovered vertex are all distinct since the matching is maximal. Hence m≥v−ν and there are at least (400/401)(m−ν) units. For the count select only d−1 units per low-degree raw and d−2 per high-degree raw. At most 2m units are selected.

Each raw is planted with probability 803/N for N=161203, with 401/N forced deleted and 402/N forced retained, so its deletion marginal is p=(1−1/N)/2. For every raw-pair type the probability both endpoints are planted is at most δ=.000032 by the pair counts in planted-theta-credit.md. Write a=1−1606/N. The probability both are free is at least a. For nonmatching edges with at least one free endpoint, disagreement is fair, since their original bits arise from independent blocks. For matching edges with exactly one free endpoint disagreement is fair, and with both free it is certain. Expected credit, before discarding bad outcomes, is therefore at least

     m(1−δ)/2 + aν/2 + p(400/401)(m−ν) ≥ m(1−δ+a)/2.

The last inequality uses p(400/401)>a/2. On a good outcome, disagreement and deleted excess are separate physical zero-flow terms. Every selected AND is nonprivate: a sole-use raw's AND sibling must accept every one of its 402 neighboring singletons, impossible for a raw sibling.

Conditional on the planting, consider the q=401 available neighbors used in the original planted-theta union bound. These are free or permanently retained. If at least three are permanent, failure is impossible. Otherwise discard at most two permanent neighbors. If at least three complete free matching pairs lie in the remaining neighborhood, at least three survive. Otherwise discard their at most four endpoints: at least 395 free bits from distinct independent blocks remain. Thus the bad probability is bounded by b=(2N+802) Pr[Binomial(395,1/2)≤2]<10^(-100). This bound suffices for both the three-neighbor and one-neighbor requirements; the two exceptional O,g have permanent neighbors. On bad outcomes at most 3m chosen credits were counted. Consequently expected credit on good outcomes exceeds 1625(1−.000032+1−1606/161203)/2−3mb>1616.879. A good outcome earns at least 1617 and the planted two-stage ledger gives 4N−4+1617=646425. The existence of enough such AND gates remains a circuit-profile hypothesis.
