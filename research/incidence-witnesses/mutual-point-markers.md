# Reciprocal selection for the sparse point orientation

In the bilateral normal form of sparse-point-cold.md, an active point is the query and the payload is a line mask. The circuit is correct through at most q points and q−1 lines here, with q=401. Let Uₓ(T) be the first-fork value when x is the only selected point. An owner x is intrinsically cold if no line mask of size at most 199 avoiding x activates Uₓ; assign it witness {x}. For every other sole-read owner add an arc x→y to each other point for which no mask of size at most 199 avoiding both x and y activates Uₓ. Owners without any arc are globally warm. All nonintrinsic owners have distinct first forks: if another owner shared the fork, the common downstream cofactor would accept an outside line singleton through the other owner, which combined with a nonintrinsic ≤199 witness would violate correctness when only x is active and the union of the two payloads avoids x.

Use the witness choice of mutual-cold-markers.md: minimize selected reciprocal pairs then maximize the sum of their later fork indices. Edit all globally warm owners and the later owner of every selected reciprocal pair; the rest are charged. Each bad neighbor y of an edited point x is an earlier unedited nonintrinsic owner choosing x as its witness partner. Otherwise switching x's choice either removes a reciprocal pair or improves the secondary objective.

On a negative test with at most two active points J and at most two original lines T, all disjoint, suppose each active edited point has a ≤199 witness avoiding J. Their union with T has size at most 400; on this extended negative each raw-to-fork injection is dominated by the original fork value. Monotonicity proves rejection on T. Otherwise J={x,y} consists of the bad pair just described. Point x alone is actively edited. The earlier fork of y has value U_y(T)=0 because T avoids both x,y and contains at most two lines; it precedes the only active injection. Turning off y leaves the edited output unchanged because all paths from y pass this zero fork. Now the output is Vₓ(T), the zero-point circuit with x's fork forced to one. Choose a witness W, |W|≤199, avoiding x with Uₓ(W)=1 by nonintrinsicness. On the independent input with only x active and payload T∪W the original fork is true; the true output is zero, hence its dominated forced cofactor at T is zero. The warm splice preserves singleton flags and saves at least one gate per edited owner as before. Thus M≤3213 in a below-target circuit.

For every remaining charged point use its chosen singleton or paired blocking witness in the ≥199-slot connected marked-region argument in sparse-point-cold.md. Each charged gate accepts more than two singleton lines through that owner and has its whole singleton support inside the pencil union of its chosen witness points. If the same gate were charged for distinct x and y, both owners would have to choose each other: any third pencil intersects the two covering pencils in at most two lines. Each reciprocal pair already has one edited endpoint, so all these gate sets are disjoint. Therefore 199C≤s≤646424, giving C≤3248, and H_P=M+C≤6461. Taken with H_L≤3382 and preservation of raw line occurrences under the point hoist, bilateral internal fanout excess satisfies F≤1613+3382+6461=11456.
