# A residual bound from singleton transforms

For a live constant-normalized binary monotone circuit on v occurring inputs and s gates, put e=s−v+1. Suppose its graph G of accepted pairs is bipartite and excludes 4-cycles, and it rejects empty and singleton assignments. There is a set M of at most 3e+1 variables such that the sum of degrees of unmarked vertices is at most v+2u, where u is their count. Extra nonoccurring variables may be included in u as isolates.

Keep one outgoing wire per nonroot node and substitute a fresh placeholder at each removed use. Wire counting shows there are exactly e placeholders. The retained edges form a tree. For each distinct producer of a placeholder sever its one retained outgoing edge, leaving its producer as the root of its own component. Remove the placeholders (regarded as external input ports) and treat the other ends of the severed retained wires as input ports too. There are at most e+1 components and at most 2e ports in total. Every influence from any component's local variables reaches the exterior through that component's root. No signal entering a component can depend on its local variables, since that would give a directed path through the component's root back to itself.

Consider the read-once monotone formula F on the component's variables and its t distinguishable ports. For each local singleton x let f_x be the function of the ports obtained by selecting just x. Let f_0 be the all-zero local assignment's function. By monotonicity all f_x≥f_0. The set of distinct functions strictly larger than f_0 has antichain width at most max(1,t). For zero ports this follows because the only strictly active transform is constant one. For a leaf the claim is immediate. At a binary gate with two positive port counts, distribute antichain members between the child in which the chosen x arose. The child transforms furnishing incomparable active parent transforms are themselves incomparable and active, since composing with the fixed sibling under AND or OR preserves order and maps the baseline to the parent baseline. Thus the bound is the sum of the two port counts. If instead one child has no ports, its baseline is a constant. A nonneutral constant can prevent any change in the other child from becoming active, and a change in the constant child then gives at most one active transform. A neutral constant lets the other child's changes pass through; any active change in the constant child must be 0→1 under OR and then produces the maximum transform 1. Thus the width is at most max(1,t).

For each local singleton x, its outside neighborhood is monotonically determined by f_x: fixing any particular outside singleton y fixes the incoming bits, and the downstream result is a monotone function of the root exit at that assignment. The baseline f_0 has no outside neighbors. Hence local vertices with at least two outside neighbors have pairwise distinct and incomparable active transforms: inclusion of one such neighborhood in another would give two different common neighbors, violating the bipartite 4-cycle exclusion. Mark all such vertices, at most max(1,t) per component, totaling at most 3e+1.

The graph induced on one component at external variable assignment zero is a star forest. Its ports are fixed, and the scalar output is a monotone unary function of the component root. Simplify constants in the resulting read-once formula. If constant, only the empty graph is possible. At an OR root the pair graph is the disjoint union of the two child graphs; both reject singletons. At an AND root the children have disjoint variables and both reject zero after constant simplification, so accepted pairs form one complete bipartite graph between the singleton supports of the children. The absence of a 4-cycle makes this a star. This proves the claim inductively. An unmarked vertex of total degree at least 3 has at most one external neighbor, so its internal degree is at least two. The sum of internal degrees of all such centers across the star forests is at most the total internal edge count, hence ≤v. Low-degree unmarked vertices have degree at most two. This proves the degree-sum bound.

# Incidence application

Set d=q²−32q, N=q²+q+1 and V=2N−d. The usual adaptive argument gives cost ≥2d−t after d deletions, with t+z≤floor(qd/(q²−d)) for cheap deletions t and residual isolates z. Every nonisolated residual vertex must occur, so if e is the excess of the live residual circuit, the original cost is at least 2N+d−1−floor(qd/(q²−d))+e.

All V surviving vertices are counted, adding any nonoccurring isolates to the unmarked set U. Write u=|U|. Because at most 3e+1 are marked, V≤3e+1+u. Let L be the incidence count from U to deleted vertices. Then L≥(q+1)u−(V+2u)=(q−1)u−V. For each deleted vertex count its neighbors in U. The counts sum to L, and their squares sum to at most u(q+1)+u(u−1) because any two vertices have at most one common neighbor (and opposite-side vertices share none). Cauchy gives L²≤d u(q+u). Regardless of the sign of the lower bound, L²≥(q−1)²u²−2(q−1)uV. When u>0 rearrange and divide by u to obtain

    u≤floor([qd+2(q−1)V]/[(q−1)²−d]).

The same bound holds when u=0. Together these statements prove the desired Nq≤100s through q=320 by finite arithmetic. Run check.py to verify the full range from 289 onward.

The deletion step and prefix accounting use the [adaptive restriction and neighborhood second-moment argument](../incidence-deletion.md). For the final deleted set D, both the cheaply deleted vertices and the surviving isolates belong to the same monotonically increasing depleted set B(D), and the two groups are disjoint. The bound on |B(D)| therefore bounds their sum. This structural argument is on paper; [the finite arithmetic check](check_threshold.py) covers exactly orders 289 through 320.
