# Seventeen gates even after four negative rows are omitted

Keep all 16 punctured-Fano flags as positives and any ≥17 of the 21 maximal two-by-two negatives. All 12 singleton inputs reject by monotonicity, since each occurs in six maximal negatives. All raws are essential by comparing a flag to its opposite endpoint alone.

Apply the first-fork groups of research/one-hot-circuits/fork-graph/projective-cold-empty.md. Each raw follows its unique initial path to a first branching wire, or to the terminal group. Two singleton-hot labels reaching the same first fork are interchangeable on arbitrary fixed backgrounds: either raw makes the shared wire true, and all its paths go through this wire. Cold labels in different groups cannot have an accepted two-label input, since the later label cannot affect the earlier cold wire. A positive edge cannot belong to one group if it has an induced P4 negative background that turns positive on adding either endpoint: the first merge would have to be AND on the empty background and OR on that negative background. Separate singleton acceptance at the fork is defined at this actual gate, not by the promised output.

Each edge has its induced P4 background covered by five or nine of the original maximal negatives, so all survive four omissions. Cold vertices thus form an independent set of the actual graph. Any two same-shore labels have distinguishable single-raw backgrounds witnessed on eight or ten original negatives, guaranteeing at most one hot of each shore per group. A two-hot group must also consist of nonincident labels, and those labels must remain interchangeable when its two-raw swapped inputs lie on the same shore. The accompanying exhaustive integer checker counts these witnesses and the independent sets.

There are at most two ambiguous point-line pairs after any four omissions. If at most one such pair can actually be grouped and h≤5 forks, there are at most six hot labels; the ≥6 cold labels must comprise one entire shore, since the two shores are the only independent sets of size six. The six hot labels on the other shore then need six groups. If two pairs are grouped, only two omission patterns can permit them. In both they are disjoint and the eight remaining vertices have independence number four. There are at least eight hot labels, exceeding the capacity of five groups plus two pairings. Hence in every case h≥6. The live slot count is ≥12−1+h≥17. This finite argument does not assume a spectral bound for a partially specified matrix.

For any two maximal negatives n,m, the exact minimum accepting all flags and rejecting n,m is min_W |W|−1, over retained raw sets W for which W minus n and W minus m each cover every flag. Any live input support must have this property. In a minimum W drop inputs in both negatives. The OR of W00 inputs together with (OR W10) AND (OR W01), subscripts denoting membership in n,m, is a read-once realization; omit empty terms. The 210 costs are 8 for 36 pairs, 9 for 58, 10 for 76 and 11 for 40.

In a trimmed minimum circuit of size s≤20 with an AND root, the children accept every flag. Their nonempty sets of rejected maximal negatives cover all 21 (a parent rejecting none could be bypassed). If the smaller set has 1–4 members, the larger has ≥17, requiring 17 gates. The smaller requires at least six for one row and eight for two or more. If both sets have at least five, each contains a row requiring seven ORs and the two incompatible families need at least three and four ANDs respectively since the larger has ≥11 elements. The root plus parent-cone lower bounds are thus ≥24 for a single smaller row, ≥26 for two through four, and ≥22 otherwise. Subtracting the actual size shows at least four, six, or two physical gates respectively occur in both parent cones. Shared ancestors may be numerous behind one branching wire; these counts are not a count of independent forks or a contradiction to size 20.
