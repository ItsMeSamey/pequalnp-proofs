# Projecting the unowned fork onto the cold inputs that reach it

Consider an 18-gate dense punctured-Fano parent with seven branching wires, six singleton-hot raws on one shore H, and a single unowned branching wire X. Assume the cold raws which encounter a fork all meet X first; all remaining cold raws reach the output on unique paths with no fork. Let S be the nonempty set of cold raws first branching at X and put U equal to the union of their flag neighborhoods on H. The gate X depends only on H∪S: any other cold raw reaching X would meet a branching wire.

For p in U choose c in S incident to p. The negative singleton p turns positive on adding c, and every path influenced by c passes through X. Consequently the downstream circuit on background p, with the wire X forced to one, accepts. Every retained negative maximum z containing p must therefore have X(z)=0, by monotonicity. More generally a retained maximum n has X(n)=0 whenever n∩(H∪S) is contained in any such z.

In the original circuit replace both reader occurrences of X by zero, retaining X's computation, then append X at the output by one OR. Distinct nonredundant readers both simplify, so at most 17 gates remain. All positive flags survive by case distinction on X. Every certified maximum still rejects because it has X=0. The retained negatives need not each use two hot raws: six of the maxima have only one site on a specified shore.

The companion checker compares the certified maxima with the universal eighteen-gate lower bound for at least nineteen maxima in punctured-fano-nineteen-maxima.md and the sufficient conditions of punctured-fano-eighteen-deletions.md for at most four omitted rows. With all original maxima present this excludes |S|≥2, on either choice of hot shore. With one original omission, the exclusions per hot shore are 299 of 315 choices at |S|=2 and every choice for |S|≥3. No singleton S is excluded by this relocation test. These are counts of (omitted maximum,S) for a fixed orientation.
