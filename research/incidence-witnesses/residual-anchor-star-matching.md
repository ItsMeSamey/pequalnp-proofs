# A matching forced by retained physical ANDs

Pin gates of a minimum native circuit to zero, simplify and prune to a circuit with live output, d fewer physical gates, and lost flag set L. Select surviving AND gates h=x AND b with designated raw guard x and a path to the output consisting only of ORs. The path may be empty. Choose at most one guard per gate and call their union R. Then d≤|L|+ν(L[V minus R]), where ν is bipartite maximum matching.

Assign each lost flag touching R to one anchor at one endpoint x. Add its opposite raw to the sibling pin b with one new OR per flag. Shared old siblings keep their other readers. On a legal negative all original residual gate values remain identical by induction: if x is true every newly assigned neighbor is false. On flags the edits only increase values and each assigned flag activates its anchor and the OR-only exit. Repair the uncovered lost graph with fresh stars at a minimum vertex cover: one gate per edge toward its star and one OR per used center joining to the surviving output. König's theorem proves the bound. If the original circuit also minimizes AND count among size minima, equality requires that at most ν ANDs were removed. More generally, an earlier surviving wire w with x AND w false on every legal negative in the residual can be added for one OR and restore all flags activating both. The same simultaneous topological induction applies.

Choose k distinct point owners and physical terms t_x = x AND OR(D_x) on d_x≥1 distinct incident lines apiece. Each displayed binary OR tree is private, including its root, and the trees are disjoint. The term ANDs may have other readers. Each has a positive-length OR-only exit outside the selected banks. Suppress all k terms and assume the residual output survives. Then all D=sum d_x bank flags are lost, and the lost graph on vertices outside R has a matching saturating all k owners with distinct unanchored lines.

Indeed the private stars save D gates. Fix one successor towards the output for each vertex in the OR exit paths. Route each zero star until its first nonzero OR successor. That OR is removed by its zero input, and each earlier OR on the trail has two zero operands and is also removed. Their union is a directed forest. A stopping vertex receives at most one arriving edge because its other operand is nonzero. Each interior vertex receives at most two. With r stops there are exactly k−r interior vertices with two incoming edges, by the degree sum. These vertices and the stops are k further distinct gate removals. Lost singleton flags must activate a suppressed star, so |L|≤D. The residual lost graph has at most k point owners. Thus D+k≤d≤|L|+ν≤D+k, proving both assertions.

For the high-congestion formula using every point star on a single axis and the opposing line stars off that axis, suppress all q+1 point terms. Every line except the axis remains anchored, leaving matching size at most one. When the complementary points are a union of pencil lines, all line terms remain as anchors; the matching is empty. The rewrite strictly shortens both constructions. This supplies a minimum-specific explanation of these high-load examples. It does not show that every high-load original OR exposes private stars or retained anchors.
