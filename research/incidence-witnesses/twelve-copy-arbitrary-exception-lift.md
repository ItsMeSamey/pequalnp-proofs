# Twelve restricted copies remove exponentially many arbitrary maximal exceptions

Let q be a sufficiently large integer plane order and N=q²+q+1. Let a monotone binary circuit H of size s accept all incident singleton flags. At query cardinality exactly q let at most exp(q/100) sets Z fail the required rejection on the maximal negative (X_Z,Z), where X_Z consists of all uncovered points. No guarantee is needed for smaller cardinalities, or on the structure and automorphism orbits of these exceptions. Then a circuit of size at most 12s+11 computes incidence exactly for arbitrary point payloads and all query sets of size at most q.

**Coloring.** Fix the exceptional family E. Independently color all query lines with two equally likely colors. The probability a fixed Z of size q contains more than 3q/4 lines of either color is at most 2 exp(−q/8) by Hoeffding. Some coloring therefore keeps both color loads ≤3q/4 in every exception.

**Six point blocks.** In any affine chart take horizontal and vertical directions. Divide the horizontal parallels into groups of sizes (5/12)q+O(1) and (7/12)q+O(1). Split the first horizontal strip into two nearly equal vertical groups and the second into three nearly equal vertical groups. These are five disjoint affine rectangles. Each uses at most (11/12)q+O(1) horizontal and vertical lines *in total*. Thus at least (13/12)q−O(1) lines in those two directions avoid each rectangle, more than q+q/15 for large q. Add all infinity points except the horizontal and vertical ideals to one rectangle; the same outside horizontal and vertical lines still avoid it. The sixth block consists of the two remaining ideal points and is missed by all 2q lines in any two other parallel classes. Each block B hence has a filler pool F_B of at least q+q/15 lines missing B.

**Circuit.** Use one copy for each block B and color, suppressing raw inputs outside that block or color. OR the twelve outputs. On incidence positives a copy containing both endpoints accepts. On an independent input take any copy, let Y' be its n retained queries, and consider extensions to q queries only by labels from F_B. If n>3q/4, no extension belongs to E because Y' is monochromatic. At least one extension exists. If n≤3q/4, put r=q−n≥q/4. The pool has at least r+q/15 fresh lines, giving at least binom(r+u,u) choices where u=⌊q/15⌋. Since r≥u, this is ≥2^u>exp(q/100) for large q. Thus again some extension Z lies outside E. The retained points lie in X_Z by independence and the filler property. Monotonicity forces each copy to reject, using solely H's exact rejection on one good q-query maximal test. The extensions are logical witnesses and cost no gates.

**Whole-orbit version in all planes.** Any collineation group of a finite projective plane has size exp(O(log q log log q)). Start with a quadrangle and close it under joins and meets; in a finite plane the stabilized incidence structure is a nondegenerate subplane. If this subplane is proper, adjoin an outside point and close again. A subplane of order r properly contained in a plane of order m satisfies m≥r². To see this, take an outside point on the extension of a subplane line. The r² subplane points off this line lie on distinct other ambient lines through the chosen point: otherwise two distinct subplane lines would meet outside the subplane. There are at most m such lines. Consequently at most O(log log q) points were adjoined before reaching the entire plane, since the orders square at every strict step. A collineation is determined by the images of the chosen generators, giving the claimed group bound. In particular even exp(q/200) completely exceptional orbits contribute at most exp(q/100) maximal masks for sufficiently large q, so the twelve-copy result applies irrespective of their internal geometry.

The exceptional family may also depend on H and need not be explicitly listed for the nonuniform existence statement. The quantitative premise concerns its absolute count at cardinality q; inverse-polynomial error under the uniform q-set law alone does not establish it.
