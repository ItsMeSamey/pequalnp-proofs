# Exact simultaneous face-tree LP for a canonical physical formula

Fix a common exempt OR set J. For each proper face f let T range over the finite physical trees of flexible-face-or-trees.md. Minimize B with x_fT≥0, Σ_T x_fT=1 for EACH f and Σ_{f,T:u∈T} x_fT≤B for each u outside J. The dual maximizes Σ_f d_f over λ_u≥0, Σλ_u≤1, d_f≤Σ_{u∈T outside J}λ_u for every f,T. Thus dual prices sum the minimum tree price per face. Fractional choices are simultaneous; restricting them to integral choices is a different problem.

For any plane q≥2 take the point-star formula with its N−1 main collector ORs exempt. Use binary caterpillar query trees: one bottom OR (cherry) has two raw query children, each other OR has one raw child. Obtain two disjoint perfect matchings of the regular incidence graph by Hall; put the two matched incident line labels at the cherry of each point. Each line now has exactly two cherry owners.

At face (p,H), exactly the q owners x∈H except p retain their entire query pools; all other owner terms reduce to distinct free raw points. Among the surviving pools the only duplicated query is H itself, present in all q. Route H via one winning owner x. Each nonwinner drops precisely the OR immediately above its H leaf, and all its other ORs remain. The resulting distinct-raw tree is physically admissible. An owner's pool is eligible on N−1 faces. A single-raw-parent OR (raw child H) has load N−1−q=q² plus wins at that owner-label pair. A cherry OR has load q²−q plus wins at its two pairs.

At each H prescribe 1/q total wins for each of its q−1 noncherry owners and (q+1/q)/2 total wins for each of its two cherry owners. Sum is q+1. Each possible excluded p has one unit to route to winners other than itself. Fractional Hall gives the desired allocation: each single winner has demand ≤q and any two or more winners see all q+1 rows. Do this on every H. Loads are q²+1/q at EVERY outside OR, since the owner cherry has two such labels and each other gate only one.

Uniform dual prices 1/(Nq) on the Nq unexempt ORs are tight: every tree has z−1 branches, at most N−1 exempt, so its unexempt count is at least N−2q. The E faces give dual value E(N−2q)/(Nq)=q²+1/q. Hence this is the exact fractional optimum. An integral solution choosing each p's cyclic successor on each H has every owner win once per incident H; maximum load is q²+1. Rounding up the fractional optimum proves integral optimality. At q=2 the optima are 9/2 and 5. Other query-tree shapes and cherry placements can have different optima. No general congestion bound follows for arbitrary shared circuits.
