## Explicit infinite-order consequence of the coarse fixed-width argument

For every finite plane of order q≥40000 the original scalar promise requires at least 4N−3+floor(N/61) gates. This uses no resets, repeated census or improved group coefficient. Put k=floor(N/61), t=30. Since W≥m(d−2), h≤q/2. The point additive terms are ≤4q+9q and the line additive terms ≤9q+h, giving S₀≤2k+23q. These estimates follow from N=q²+q+1 and q≥40000; the floor in the point denominator is at least (q−4)/2.

We have T=30(k+1)≤0.493q², H=q−31≥0.999q; consequently H²>T and J≤q. Thus P≤2k+8q, S≤4k+23q, G≤5k+23q. Demand is at least N−18k−15.5q−15. Supply is at most 8N/31+27k+124q+1. Their difference is at least (8/1891)N−140q−16>0 for q≥40000. Every stage can therefore earn one extra gate. The terminal baseline contradicts the proposed cap after k+1 stages.

This explicit finite-onset inequality is weaker asymptotically than the separately derived 4+55/364 liminf in weighted-envelope-ceiling.md. The constants here give no assertion below 40000; the fixed q401 release and source-level parameter implications through 410 have separate proofs. None of these linear-in-N guarantees yields Nq/100 on an unbounded set of orders. In fact the displayed coarse proof itself requires t(k+1)<H²≤q² before the group test; with the official k/N growing as q/100−4, its geometric sufficient condition eventually fails for any t≥1. No special Desarguesian coordinates or plane-existence assumption enters the infinite-order implication.
