# One first pair-creation gate on the full scalar plane

Say a cross pair first arises at a binary AND when the AND is true on that pair and false on both singleton endpoints, and each operand is already true at one of the two singletons. Let N=q²+q+1 and q≥2. Compute the query star S_p for each point as the OR of its q+1 incident line raws. Make P and Q the nonemptiness signals on their respective shores, G=P AND Q and m_p=p AND G. Output the OR of m_p AND S_p over all points. This computes incidence on the entire Boolean cube. The only AND whose operands can both receive truth from bare singletons is G. At every other AND one operand already rejects every pure singleton. The same assertion excludes newly created same-shore pairs at later gates as well.

Every native singleton flag (p,l) selects just its row. All accepting proofs there include G exactly once, p twice and l twice: one copy of each endpoint is used within G and the other is used by the row constraint. G accepts all nonincident pairs as well. For gate accounting use Nq star ORs, N−1 point nonemptiness ORs, and compute Q using q additional ORs over the q+1 already formed star outputs of the points of one projective line. Their pencils cover all query labels. Include one AND for G, N masks, N row ANDs and N−1 joins. Total Nq+4N+q−1, or 43 gates on the Fano plane. No unrestricted minimum is claimed.

## Bypass expectations under the original promise

This construction has a safe bypass, and every universal first creator of this kind must have at least one safe parent replacement on the promise of arbitrary point inputs and at most q lines. To see this, let g=a AND b create every incidence flag in the stated sense. At every raw singleton exactly one child is true: there is an incident flag providing one true child, while g itself rejects that singleton. Across every incidence edge the two singleton types differ. The bipartite incidence graph is connected, hence up to exchange a accepts every point singleton and b every line singleton.

Replace all occurrences of g by b. This only increases the circuit and preserves all positive inputs. An independent input with a nonempty point payload already has a=1, so nothing changed on it. If an empty-payload negative with |Y|≤q began to accept, add any point outside the query union (at most 1+q² points are covered). The enlarged assignment is still independent and the bypassed circuit still accepts; but a=1 there and the old circuit must reject. This contradiction proves the bypass is safe for arbitrary fanout, even with shared descendants. Thus no gate-minimum sparse-promise circuit has a single universal first creator. Removing a redundant universal creator can increase the count of distinct first creators while shrinking the circuit.

The opposite direction can genuinely fail. In the displayed construction choose a line J and add D=AND_{z on J} m_z by OR with the old output. When D fires all points of J and some query are present, hence there is a flag. But G→P would now falsely accept J with no queries. The modified circuit has no additional first pair creator; its new AND operands reject pure singleton inputs. The cost increases by q+1 to 46 Fano gates.

For a flag-live version pick an exterior point p₀ and a line l₀ through it. Omit that flag from the ordinary star of p₀, take h=m_p₀ AND l₀, and instead use D=AND_{z on J}(m_z OR h). This accepts the omitted anchor privately and has the same full-cube exactness and unsafe bypass to P. Every displayed gate activates on a flag; all new ANDs still have an operand rejecting both bare singleton shores. Choose the line supplying the shared star ORs for Q not through p₀, so its computation is unchanged. The total is Nq+4N+3q+1, or 49 Fano gates. Proofs of nonanchor flags retain their original minimum; unfolding the anchor proof repeats h once for each blocking point.

These controls concern first creation in one particular physical circuit. They do not invalidate receiver lower bounds that also assume negative-mask rejection at each actual receiver. The only unconditional impossibility established here is the minimum-circuit bypass for a universal first creator under the stated promise.
