# Batch bypass with a common side mask

Let a physical binary monotone circuit compute incidence on arbitrary payloads with at most q queries. Select m distinct internal gates e_i to replace everywhere by named raw inputs p_i. Let B be a signal accepting all singleton flags. Suppose on each flag the original value of e_i is at most p_i, and on every prescribed negative where B=1 one has e_i=p_i, for every i. The first hypothesis holds on the full cube for any AND-run endpoint e_i reached from p_i via AND-only gates.

Append B as a conjunct of the rewritten output, supplying the original B independently. On a flag every rewritten signal is at least its original value by induction, including at nested replacements since they now receive their raw values directly. On a B-passing negative each retained signal equals its original value by induction. The other negatives fail B. All positives dominate a flag. With a separate circuit of a gates for B the saving is at least m−a−1. With an existing B whose original cone is protected, bypass only e_i outside that cone, leaving protected e_i unchanged also for other consumers: the saving is at least |{i:e_i outside Anc(B)}|−1. The cone includes B itself. An existing upstream B costs only the final AND.

There are two further ways to handle an existing B computed using some e_i. First, retain its original cone as a separate copy, and bypass all m gates in a fresh version of the output. Only changed gates in the guard cone can need duplicate computation. More precisely, bound the added bill by the number of gates in Anc(B) reachable from any replaced gate including paths of length zero. Gates outside that intersection have exactly the original computation and may be reused in both versions. The resulting saving is at least m minus this bill minus one. Second, reuse the rewritten B' directly if every negative where B=0 has B'=0 or has a rejecting rewritten output. The flag and B-passing negative inductions already give correct values there. When reuse is justified, it costs at most one extra gate. Without such evidence it can fail: consider output p AND y on the two-raw one-flag function, let B be this very gate, and replace it by p. Original B accepts the flag and B-passing negatives are absent. Reusing B'=p then falsely accepts the point alone.

More generally for flag preservation it suffices that each flag have an original accepting proof such that every first replaced site reached from the output along this proof receives a true replacement raw. Stop the proof there, since its children will not be consulted. The proof certifies the new output on that flag even if another replaced site outside its visited branches would switch off. The guard must still accept the flag. For negatives, exact sufficient and necessary checking with a fixed independent guard is rejection of the rewritten output on each B-passing negative. It in fact suffices to check the equality hypothesis on B-passing maximal negatives only: the induction there proves final rejection, B rejects B-zero maximal negatives, and every eligible independent input extends to its maximal payload while keeping its original query set. Equality itself need not extend to smaller payloads.

In the full-plane example of multiquery-first-run-shared-control.md the gates e_p=p AND B use B equal to the OR of all query raws. It is a physically available upstream signal accepting every flag, and e_p=p on every B-passing negative. Hence a common side mask permits simultaneous savings even though both individual parent substitutions have counterexamples. In the limited-pencil version this particular B misses exterior flags, so this global rewrite requires a different guard.
