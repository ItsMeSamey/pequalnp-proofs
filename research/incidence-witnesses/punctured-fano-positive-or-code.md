# Positive flag code

At each physical OR choose one operand independently and uniformly, retaining both operands at each AND. For each of the 16 positive flags e, let d(e) count physical OR gates with precisely one true operand on that flag. Fix at all such gates the choice of the true operand. Every gate's original value on e is then preserved by induction. The oriented root is a conjunction of a nonempty set of raw variables. On a flag it must use both endpoints since the original circuit rejects each singleton and orientation only decreases the output. Thus the 16 subcubes of orientations defined above are pairwise disjoint. Their total measure is at most one. Convexity gives sum d(e) at least 64.

Suppose some live gate has a consistent local orientation whose resulting raw conjunction includes two nonincident sites (including two on the same shore). Extend that orientation by following a path from this gate to the output, choosing the path predecessor at each OR above it. Those ORs are strictly downstream, so choices are consistent, and the root conjunction retains both sites. This orientation lies outside every flag subcube. Strictness forces sum d(e) at least 65.

If equality 65 holds with strictness, integer balancing forces fifteen 4s and one 5: the next possible mass after the balanced 31/32 is at least one (replacing 4,5 by 3,6, or two 4s by 3,5). In each half of an orientation coordinate the unused mass is between zero and 1/32. The difference of the signed masses of flags fixing that coordinate is therefore at most 1/32 in absolute value. All such flags weigh 2/32 except at most one flag of weight 1/32. Thus the two sides' counts differ by at most one. A coordinate with imbalance two or more and a consistent nonincident witness force sum d(e) at least 66. This argument counts truth patterns across flags. It gives no automatic conversion of their sum into a physical gate count.
