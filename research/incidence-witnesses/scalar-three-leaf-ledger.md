# A physical lower bound for flags with a proof of at most three raw occurrences

Let s be the number of binary AND/OR gates of a monotone scalar circuit satisfying the original incidence promise for a C4-free plane of order q≥2. Constants are folded. Let D3 be the set of flags with a native accepting proof tree using at most three raw leaf occurrences, counting repetitions. Then |D3|≤21s. In particular a counterexample to Nq≤100s would require more than 79 percent of all flags to use at least four raw occurrences in every proof. The proof uses only negatives with at most two raws on either shore.

Write O,A for the physical OR and AND counts. A raw pool of a wire on a chosen shore consists of the raw labels on that shore reaching the wire through OR gates only (a raw reaches itself). A flag is rich if, on one of the two shores, some raw pool of size ≥2 contains its endpoint and is entirely contained in the opposite endpoint's neighborhood. The number of rich flags is at most 4O: for each owner explore all distinct such pool roots down their raw-reaching OR cones, stopping at singleton shore marginals. The number d of distinct reached labels is at most the number of roots plus the number of branching ORs by counting edges. Each distinct root is itself an OR with a ≥2-label marginal. Every OR counted in either role is exclusive to one owner on that shore, by codegree one. This gives ≤2O flags per shore; the other-shore raw marginal of a wire is irrelevant.

Count two-leaf flags by s using scalar-first-product-ledger.md. A remaining three-leaf proof has two AND occurrences separated by OR-only paths; the higher g has an OR-only path to the output and exactly one of its operand subproofs uses the lower h. They are physically distinct by acyclicity. Write r for the repeated raw label and t for the opposite, single-occurrence label. Exclude all rich flags.

First suppose h receives r and t on different operand slots. Let U be its r-shore raw pool at the r slot, V its t-shore pool at the other slot, and S the r-shore raw pool on the direct operand of g. Both U and S contain r. For every t' in V the pair r,t' activates g, hence V is contained in N(r) and V={t} since the flag is not rich. If U is contained in N(t), then U={r}; at most two flags per physical h can be of this kind, since fixing which operand carries the point fixes both singleton marginals. This costs at most 2A across the two choices of repeated shore combined.

Otherwise U contains u nonincident to t. Call h a source with token t whenever its t slot's marginal is {t} and its opposite slot has a nonneighbor u of t in its r-shore marginal. Fix a repeated shore and the physical choice of t slot at h. Each source has at most one token. For any outer AND g on an OR-only route to output with direct r-shore raw pool S on the designated slot, every token t' reached by its other operand along OR-only paths from these sources is incident to every r' in S: select r', its source's off-t' witness u', and t'. If r' missed t' this legal negative would enable g. For the associated flag this implies S⊆N(t), hence S={r} when not rich.

In any such routing class, assign each outer g with singleton direct pool to its unique r. The distinct token labels reaching its other operand through ORs lie in N(r). For k_r outer gates at r covering d_r labels, their union of OR-routing cones, stopped at singleton token label sets, needs at least d_r−k_r physical OR branch sites. Every branch has ≥2 distinct labels in N(r) and cannot occur for another r. The sources are AND gates with one token each, so no branch is hidden inside them; repeated sources with equal tokens do not increase d_r. The k_r physical outer ANDs pay the remainder. Each class accounts for at most s flags. There are 2 choices each of repeated shore, inner token slot and outer direct slot, yielding at most 8s.

Finally suppose h receives two occurrences of r. Its two r-shore pools U,W both contain r. The direct t-shore pool V at g lies in N(r), so V={t}. Either U⊆N(t) or W⊆N(t): otherwise one nonneighbor from each, together with t, enables g on a legal negative. The contained pool is {r} for a non-rich flag. For each choice of inner slot, use h as token r if that slot's r-shore pool is {r} and the other slot also contains r. This makes h accept bare singleton r. Therefore every such token reachable by an OR path into an outer g with direct singleton t is in N(t); otherwise that nonflag pair enables g. The identical OR-routing count gives at most s flags in each of 8 classes (repeated shore, inner singleton slot, outer direct slot).

Adding yields |D3|≤s+4O+2A+16s≤21s. No previous row signals, bounds on fanout, or exclusive assignment of gates across routing classes were assumed; the factor 16 explicitly allows reuse. The common-occupancy control has four occurrences at every flag, so it is consistent with this inequality.
