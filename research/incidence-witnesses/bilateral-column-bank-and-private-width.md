# Testing the mixed row bank with random pinned points

Use the experiment of bilateral-earlier-mixed-receivers.md with fixed c>0. The original global mixed bank fails: with high probability the random pinned set R has two points. The normalized same-column threshold-two also fires with high probability, since q disjoint q-point columns have independent pin counts and each has probability bounded away from zero of at least two pins. Once this guard fires H(R+W_i+p) and H(R+V+l) are one on every stage. Ordinary repetition of this bank does not remove that failure. A predetermined support S is pin-free with probability (1−c/q)^|S|. Thus no polynomial number of predetermined supports of size Ω(N) supplies a pin-free copy with appreciable probability. This calculation does not impose pin-freeness on other possible circuit designs.

## A bilateral bank with polynomial logarithmic overhead

For prime q build one H_x per affine point column. On its singleton point encode y in dual rails. For each affine line slope a compute t=y−ax modulo q, with x,a hardwired, by polynomially many bit operations in log q. The goal is to recognize t among the active intercepts of slope a. Fix L=ceil(10 log N), and choose a power of two r between 4(L+1)log₂N and 8(L+1)log₂N. Give the q intercepts distinct polynomials of degree D=ceil(log_r q)−1 over the r-element field, using base-r digit coefficients. For each slope, argument z and output u, compute the OR of all raw lines with intercepts evaluating to u at z. These query-only features are shared across all columns and cost O(q²r). For each cell use dual-rail binary field operations and finite address comparisons to require that every z admits an active query matching the target polynomial's value at z. This costs polylog N gates per cell, even if all r values are explicitly tested for each z. Gate by nonemptiness of column x. If at most L+1 queries are active at this slope, r>D(L+1) implies that a target absent from the active set fails some test by the polynomial root bound. Any true target passes regardless of load. OR slope results, then OR with the threshold of two points in column x; call this H_x. Use R_p=p AND H_x for each affine point in x and complete by the AND of each R_p with its exact nonvertical query star and by exact boundary terms. Full-cube correctness follows because singleton flags pass and completed rows are subsets of flags. The bank costs O(N polylog N); the explicit full completion costs Θ(Nq).

Condition on a typical R with |U|=(exp(−c)+o(1))N and retained degree (exp(−c)+o(1))q uniformly for points outside R. For any slope there are at most q available lines; hence a fixed set of L lines of that slope occurs in W with probability at most (q/|U|)^L. A union bound over slopes and choices gives q(C/L)^L=o(1). All slopes are then safe even after one extra query, throughout the nested chain.

There are (exp(−c)+o(1))q affine columns with no point of R, by independent binomial counts. Conditional on R, each of their points survives W with probability exp(−1)+o(1) by the retained-degree estimate. The total number surviving in these columns concentrates within o(N) of its conditional mean by the line-swap exposure argument (each change modifies at most 2(q+1) points). Discard columns with fewer than four such points; this loses O(q) points. Thus (exp(−c−1)+o(1))N eligible points remain. In their columns R is empty, so H_x(R+W_i+p)=0 and H_x(R+V+l)=0, while H_x(R+V+p+l) accepts exactly the q incident affine query choices when labels are unrestricted, or their retained subset when l ranges over U. On the large comparison negative Z, at least two surviving column points off the tested line remain, so H_x(Z)=1. The root trace is forced through R_p provided no other row accepts at F; this holds because all queries avoid R, and W misses p. The probability statement is joint for the fixed bank and the single random experiment. Each cell has a point-pool cover of one column and false-pair cover by its ≤2q local sites; partial clean star outputs and boundary processing preserve the same order of cover.

## Lower bound for disjoint cell processors

Suppose each H_x is an OR of private processors C_xa with point-feature inputs and query features local to slope a, together with any point-only guard inactive on the good p. Count only true flags (p,l) at the fixed stage for which H_x(R+V+p)=0 and H_x(R+V+p+l)=1. Suppose there are Ω(Nq) such distinct flags, and the entire candidate row has width at most q polylog q for each good p. All raw queries in V are held fixed; changing a candidate of slope a affects only its corresponding processor. Sum the per-slope candidate widths over all good p: the total is at most O(Nq polylog q). Since at most one true flag per p per slope is present, choosing a sufficiently large cutoff w=polylog q leaves Ω(Nq) true flags whose slope-cell candidate row has size at most w. For eligible query sets U only, row sizes and target labels are simply restricted to this common U.

For fixed x,a let m_xa count the distinct physical point-feature signals on the live input boundary of the private cell, including direct raw point inputs. Each counted signal feeds a private gate in the ancestry of the cell output or is itself that output; merely available or unused features are excluded. Their producers may be precomputed and shared across cells. Points with the same m_xa-bit signature at R+p have identical candidate rows for that cell. A row of width at most w can account for at most w true flags at slope a in column x because the map from p to its true label is injective. Thus at most w·2^m_xa good flags can use that cell. Each cell accounts for at most q flags, so Ω(q²) cells must account for Ω(q) good flags. Each requires m_xa≥log₂q−O(log log q) point-feature inputs and at least (m_xa−1)/2 private binary gates. Summing gives Ω(N log q). This lower bound concerns the private cell realization, not arbitrary shared mixed receivers.
