# Paying all 1617 selected raw-pair ANDs by an independent survivor set

Let N=161203, k=402. In a bilaterally normalized order-401 scalar incidence circuit, suppose there are at least 1617 different physical AND gates on distinct unordered raw pairs. Choose m=1617 of them, with simple operand-pair graph H and at most 2m=3234 active vertices. The following restriction earns at least m extra physical credits.

Independently choose a full dominating theta K as in dominating-theta.md: a uniform nonincident point x and line Y, their full neighborhoods, and the two poles. Each raw lies in K with probability 403/N. The probability any two distinct specified raws both lie in K is at most δ=2/N+k²/[N(N−k)]<.000019. Indeed same-shore pairs can both be on the one line Y (probability 1/N) or can include the pole x (at most 2/N); the dual argument applies. Here 3/N≤δ since k²≥N−k. For an opposite pair, charge occurrence of either prescribed pole at most 2/N; the remaining event has at most k² choices among the N(N−k) nonincident pole pairs.

Call a geometry vertex risky if at least 400 of its 402 plane neighbors are active in H. At most eight vertices per shore are risky: nine such neighborhoods have at least 9·400−36>3234 distinct active vertices, since any two on the same shore share just one neighbor. For each risky vertex form a candidate set from its full 402-neighborhood by excluding its intersections with all other risky neighborhoods on the same shore. The candidate sets are disjoint and each has size at least 395. Independently in each set choose three candidates uniformly without replacement, giving a reserve R independent of the random choice of K. Each raw belongs to R with probability at most t=3/395. Each pair of distinct raws belongs to R with probability at most t², also when in the same block by sampling without replacement. The chance that K∪R contains a chosen AND pair is at most m(δ+2·403t/N+t²)<.19. Fix an outcome avoiding all such pairs.

Extend the active vertices of K∪R to a maximal independent set I of H. In a first deletion batch keep all inactive raws together with I, and delete the remaining active raws D. K survives, so every deleted vertex has a retained plane neighbor. Every surviving vertex has at least three surviving neighbors: a nonrisky vertex has at least three inactive neighbors; a risky vertex has the three reserved neighbors. The original bilateral normal form gives two slot credits per deleted raw plus its physical fanout excess above two and the nonprivate exactly-one-zero ANDs at this restriction. This first step is literal zero propagation and thus retains the available three-by-three negative tests. Now delete the surviving vertices outside K. Each has degree at least three before that batch and a surviving neighbor in the dominating theta. The two-stage-theta.md deletion lemma preserves its two-by-two tests and buys two per removed raw. The full theta requires 4k gates on 2k+2 raws, by dominating-theta.md.

Every edge crossing from D to I buys one exactly-one-zero AND credit. The AND is nonprivate because its raw sibling cannot accept all plane-neighbor singletons of a sole-use raw. No edge lies inside I. For each deleted raw v of H-degree d, maximality of I gives at least one neighbor in I, hence its internal degree in H[D] is at most d−1. If d≥3 then its fanout excess is at least d−2≥(d−1)/2. At d=2, choose a plane-neighbor singleton different from its two H-partners. Adding v at this singleton must trigger a flag, while the two raw-pair gates remain zero, requiring a third reader. Its excess is therefore at least one. At d=1 its internal degree is zero. In all cases the deleted raw provides at least half its internal H[D] degree in excess slots. Summing pays all internal edges of H[D] in a ledger disjoint from the crossing-AND credits. Thus the first stage adds at least m above its two-per-raw credit. Telescoping both batches and the exact theta residual gives 4N−4+m=646425.

All arguments use finite projective-plane incidences, including the uniform pair counts. The raw-pair criterion is sufficient, and the proof does not assert that every normalized circuit has 1617 such ANDs.
