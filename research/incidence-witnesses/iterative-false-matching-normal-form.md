# A normal form with a small false-pair vertex cover at every wire

Assume a polynomial-size circuit on the original order-q promise. Choose a common integer m≥2 satisfying m ≫ q(log q)²/log log q. While some current live gate accepts m mutually vertex-disjoint nonincident singleton pairs, deterministically pick one such gate, substitute one, propagate constants and trim. Keep the original raw universe throughout. Every step removes a different physical gate, so there are at most s edits.

For each step apply capped-false-pair-matching.md to the current monotone gate on the SAME uniform random maximal q-line negative. Its failure probability is at most δ=q^(−ω(1)); the bound uses its pair profile alone and needs no correctness of that intermediate circuit. When the gate is one, performing its edit leaves the output unchanged on that test by topological induction. Thus the final circuit rejects at least 1−sδ of the original uniform maximal negatives. Each edit only increases the Boolean output on every mask, so all flags survive. Every independent fixed-size negative has an extension of mass greater than sδ for large q, hence is still rejected. A constant-one root is impossible provided sδ<1. Constant wires can be folded out. Physical savings are at least the number of selected edits.

In the final circuit every actual wire's current false-pair graph has matching number <m. This includes raw inputs, whose false-pair matching number is one. By the bipartite vertex-cover theorem each wire admits fewer than m exceptional points and lines combined which meet all of its accepted nonincident singleton pairs. Outside those exceptional vertices every accepted singleton pair is a genuine flag. The exceptional sets can differ by wire. This conclusion includes the near-unit rejection probability on the original large negative masks; it does not preserve exact correctness on every such mask.
