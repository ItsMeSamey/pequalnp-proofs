# Specializing actual cold operands with alternative point pools

For each line l let n_l select that single query and every point outside l. Call an old scalar gate v cold at l if v(n_l)=0. Write S_l(v) for the points p on l passing v on the native singleton flag (p,l). Under the one-query fixing the full payload function at a cold v has no accepting bare point outside l. All outputs at n_l are cold, with S_l(output)=l. Internal point cofactors need not be ORs.

Compile a monotone OR-only multiple-output circuit. Its library contains zero and all point raws initially. For a cold gate occurrence (v,l), an already available point-pool OR with support P is admissible if S_l(v)⊆P⊆l. This is a directional approximation sufficient for this compilation; it need not equal the old cofactor. Process gates in a common topological order. A cold raw is an appropriate point singleton or has empty required support. A cold AND has at least one cold operand, whose required support contains S_l(v), so forward any admissible realization of that operand. For a cold OR both operands are cold and their required supports have union S_l(v). Forward any existing admissible library signal if one exists, regardless of which gate or query produced it. Otherwise choose admissible realizations for the two actual operands and add their binary OR to the common library. It is admissible. One can keep all available alternatives as a menu; the union of alternative indices is free but the Boolean OR of their signals incurs its ordinary physical cost. Equal outputs need only be added once. The roots realize the exact line ORs since their intervals have only the support l.

For each true output compare its singleton points to its bare complement. In the compiled OR-only network every rich OR supporting at least two points in an output cone belongs to only that line. Contract singleton/empty supports to their respective raw/zero and count the connected cone from the q+1 distinct point raws to the output. It contains at least q ORs. Thus the library necessarily pays at least Nq new ORs overall. Even free ANDs cannot reduce this bound in an arbitrary joint monotone network: route the q+1 singleton tokens from each root, choosing a single complement-rejecting operand at AND and one true operand per token at OR. Reaching q+1 distinct raw leaves from one start requires ≥q physical branching ORs. Each accepts at least two bare points and rejects the complement of that line, hence charges only one line.

A readily computed upper screen for the constructed library is the sum, over original ORs v=a OR b, of the number of lines l where v is cold and S_l(a), S_l(b) are incomparable. Comparable supports permit forwarding an operand even when its compiled version overshoots. The actual number of paid merges can be smaller because a third existing library signal suffices. In particular a bound of 100s on this screen or on the explicitly counted paid merges would imply 100s≥Nq for the original candidate.

For another screen, within each operand slot of v record the distinct rich supports (size at least two) on its candidate cold lines, and the names of the singleton supports. Each rich support determines one line. When both supports at an incomparable occurrence are singletons, the two distinct names determine their join line. The split-line count is therefore at most the two rich roster sizes plus the number of distinct joins between the two singleton-name lists, excluding identical points and optionally filtering by actual co-occurrence. This is an input-table condition on the original mixed operands. No uniform constant bound on the resulting roster follows from scalar minimality here.
