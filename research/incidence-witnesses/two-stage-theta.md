# Two-stage elimination to a theta core

A useful elimination step applies to a bipartite graph of codegree at most one. Suppose a current circuit is correct on independent inputs with at most b+1 vertices of each shore and on all singleton incident pairs. Let D be a batch of raw variables to delete. Require each deleted v to have degree greater than b, and to have at least one neighbor outside D. Then after hoisting sole OR prefixes only for variables in D and setting all D to zero, one obtains a circuit for the residual graph correct on b-by-b tests and singleton flags. If the residual graph contains an edge and deleted raws are essential, at least 2|D| slots have been saved.

Indeed on an independent b-by-b input, turn off v and choose a neighbor y avoiding all selected other vertices of v's shore. Codegree and the degree bound permit this. Adding y remains independent, while adding v gives a positive by monotonicity from an incident pair. An off-spine sibling of v's initial OR prefix is independent of v, so that sibling must reject the original independent mask. In particular it rejects every other singleton and the selected OR spines cannot merge. Bypass the OR spines by their raws and append their off-spine siblings at the output, using at most the number of removed slots. The earliest-change argument retains all positive pairs. The appended siblings and the old output reject every required negative. In the literal hoisted circuit every deleted raw has at least two uses or has a sole AND reader. The latter's sibling accepts a surviving neighbor singleton, since this singleton rejects at the output but adding v accepts, and the sibling cannot depend on v. Two compensated ANDs cannot be the same: a sibling made solely of the other deleted raw would not survive. At the all-surviving-raws-one evaluation the output is nonzero. The exact zero-flow count gives at least two removable slots per deleted raw (uses plus distinct exactly-one-zero compensations). Reapply separately after each batch; the slot savings telescope.

## Balanced pencil residuals

At h=q−r≥1 the h-on-each-side balanced flag restriction leaves r point poles xᵢ on an axis g outside O and r line poles Yⱼ through O different from g. Its generic points are the q points of each Yⱼ outside O; its generic lines are the q lines through each xᵢ different from g. Every generic point has exactly one generic line neighbor from each of the r pencils and every generic line has exactly one generic point neighbor from each of the r point rows. There are n=2r(q+1) surviving vertices.

Keep one point pole x and one line pole Y. In the first batch delete just the 2(r−1) other poles. Each has q surviving generic neighbors, so from a four-by-four promise the step with b=3 pays two per removed pole for q≥4. In the second batch delete every generic point outside Y and every generic line not through x. Every such deleted vertex has at least r generic neighbors in the current graph, and one of them is retained in the theta core: join its point to x, or intersect its line with Y. For r≥3 apply the step with b=2, paying two per deleted generic. The core induced on x,Y and their q respective generic neighbors is exactly q disjoint length-three paths between x and Y. It requires 4q gates even on two-by-two tests by theta-residual.md. The starting residual circuit therefore needs at least

    2(n−2(q+1))+4q = 2n−4.

This includes every r≥3 under the four-by-four input promise; r=1 is the direct theta equality. Positive pins are not needed. At order 401 with h=200 the already bilaterally hoisted starting circuit provides the four-by-four promise after zero restriction. The expected sole free-pin deficit is at most 200·9843/161203. The expected extra-credit hurdle is 1617+200·9843/161203≈1629.212. A raw-pair AND with distinct inputs is nonprivate and has exactly-one-low-zero probability at least 0.4975155 under this balanced restriction. This follows by subtracting twice the largest same-shore, incident or nonincident joint deletion probability from twice the common marginal, as in balanced-flag.md. Therefore 3275 distinct such raw-pair AND slots suffice, independently of total raw uses.

## Full projective plane

In the original incidence graph choose O on Y and x outside Y, and put g=xO. Retain the same theta core: poles x,Y, all points of Y except O, and all lines through x except g. First delete the q points of g other than x and the q lines through O other than Y. Each removed vertex has a surviving neighbor, including O with neighbor Y and g with neighbor x. All have degree q+1. The step with b=3 applies for every q≥4 on the original sparse promise. Every vertex still present has at least q neighbors: points outside g lose at most their line to O, lines avoiding O lose at most their intersection with g, and each surviving pole loses just one neighbor. Now delete all remaining noncore raws. Every such point lies on a retained line through x and every such line meets Y at a retained point. The step with b=2 applies. Combining its two-per-raw credit with the 4q-gate core proves the unconditional bound s≥4N−4 on the original circuit for q≥4. At q=401 this is 644808 gates, short of 646425 by 1617.
