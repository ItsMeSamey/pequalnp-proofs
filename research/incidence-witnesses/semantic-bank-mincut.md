# Semantic bank recognition and best simultaneous deletion for a fixed library

Let D be a trimmed binary monotone circuit for the original promise. Tests are all native flags and, for every line set L of size at most q, the negative with L and all points outside its union. The latter dominate all required negatives. Choose a fixed library of new gates of cost k using signals in the original DAG whose entire upstream cones must survive; keep those cones fixed. New library outputs have no other old dependencies. For each other old gate v, admit at most one chosen replacement recipe, of cost c_v, using only the fixed signals and library outputs; some gates may have no recipe. Include all gate-to-gate uses and a permanently retained output sink.

A sufficient and exact test for the local directional specification is that the proposed recipe is one on every native positive where v is one and zero on every listed negative where v is zero. These conditions mean new≥old on the positive tests and new≤old on the negatives. They can be checked on the actual realized signal values, allowing arbitrary correlated, mixed upstream gates. No claim of necessity for global output equivalence. For row signal G and column pairs (u_i,Y_i), a tap recipe of the form OR_{i∈J} [(G∨u_i)∧Y_i] exists precisely when the following covering test passes: discard every i whose term is true on a listed negative where v is false; remaining terms must cover all old-positive flags of v. This describes nonempty J if at least one eligible column remains; the empty set is usable only if zero is implementable in the model. Any choices of covering subsets can share a union library as in tapped-differentiating-bank.md. No syntactic pattern is required inside the circuit.

For old gates S chosen to delete, let B(S) consist of v∈S with some original use outside S, including the sink. Require a recipe at each such v. Net savings are at least |S|−Σ_{v∈B(S)} c_v−k. Connect every surviving use of each boundary node to its replacement. Topological induction through surviving gates preserves the positive and negative directional inequalities because each replacement is computed from fixed unaffected signals, and all gates are monotone. An arbitrary original promise circuit is thus preserved on its specified tests, and monotonicity extends rejection to all smaller independent masks. Multiple consumers or deleted cones in series cost the boundary node only once.

The best value of |S|−Σ c_v is a single directed minimum cut for fixed library and recipes. Use a vertex for each deletable old gate, arc source→v of capacity one, and auxiliary z_v. Give v→z_v capacity c_v or infinity if no recipe, and arcs z_v→u of infinite capacity for every physical reader u; treat permanent readers and the output sink as the cut sink. The source side gives S. For fixed S the best side for z_v charges exactly c_v when v∈B(S), and zero otherwise. Thus the cut capacity is n−|S|+Σ_{B(S)} c_v where n is the number of deletable gates. Infinity may be n+k+Σ_{finite}c_v+1. A minimum original circuit must have every such cut capacity at least n−k. The algorithm is polynomial in the graph and explicitly given test tables; there are exponentially many original masks in general. The result detects profitable semantic banks even when their internal physical taps and fanout overlap arbitrarily. Rank alone supplies no library or covering test.
