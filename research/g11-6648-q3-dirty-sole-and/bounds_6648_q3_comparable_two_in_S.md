# Three direct entrances: both secondary gates upstream of S, comparable case

Suppose q=3, j≥3, g=x∧S is the sole dirty reader of x=3, with sole consumer M=g∨D. The two additional charged OR gates Q and T each directly read a dirty AND. Both feed S, with Q upstream of T. This allows shared donor ANDs, repeated ancestor occurrences, Q feeding both dirty and raw-facing arms of T, and shared uses in D. We prove U≤j+2c+3 using the existing 21/19-card and no-x-star footprint hypotheses.

S accepts singleton 6 and singleton 7. Every member of its AND-only input frontier therefore accepts both. No raw literal does. A Lambda frontier OR would propagate both singleton positives to the same side of its ordinary witness; the opposite nonempty raw support would have to consist of neighbors of both vertices, namely x only. Raw x cannot reach an ordinary top since it has sole dirty AND reader g. Thus the only possible frontier signals are Q,T. The later T must occur, since otherwise its path to S would pass the earlier Q. T is therefore singleton-positive on both 6 and 7, regardless of whether its raw support is empty or positive. In particular no original ordinary top or pretop Lambda OR can receive it on an OR-only path. A pretop OR directly reading a dirty AND after T would also be charged, and only M is a possible later charge. All earlier circuitry in Q's donor or donors consists of pure anchored input clauses and AND products: no dirty-AND-to-OR entrance precedes Q, and nontrivial earlier pure pretop ORs are Lambda.

Set x=false. Exactly g,M disappear and M aliases D on the least size-19 card. D is singleton-negative, so cannot equal T. A direct pretop OR reader of M was impossible since M is raw-empty and there is no later charge. Any new pin with supplier D after the alias thus goes directly into an AND before the clean aggregation. Every surviving path after T to the original top boundary is AND-only. In this least card T and each AND on such a path has at most one direct reader before the boundary: if there were two, removing their T (respectively AND) pins by cofactoring true and restoring a single copy of the factor around the affected top union would strictly save a gate. This uses only the multiplicative forward paths, not private donors. Thus T affects precisely one original top, a centreless one (an ordinary top directly receiving T would violate the singleton-pair argument; an intervening AND has empty raw support). Original ordinary/centreless labels are kept even if gates become clean after the restriction.

At this unique root split T into its two alternative pins. Each resulting term implies the old clean root by monotonicity; their union is exact since all downstream T occurrences are conjunctive. One can equivalently make the same pin choice at every T occurrence in this root and retain its other paths. No root except this one needs to split T. Each resulting expression is a conjunction with just the single possible remaining OR switch Q; the AND before an input of T can be expanded into the same product. Any OR-only Q path into a T inlet remains accounted for in the next operation.

If Q has positive raw support it is K∨R, where K is a pure anchored-clause conjunction and R a pure anchored disjunction or literal, with R nonempty. In each expression set only the K pin at Q to false for the raw branch, or to true while conjoining the original K for the other branch. Both branches are clean, the latter because it vanishes for K=false and otherwise the substitution agrees, and they cover the whole expression. Every mixed OR ahead of an AND frontier now either projects to its original raw support or becomes true. The original Q itself projects to R; other such positive ORs are original Lambda supports. A chosen raw OR pin of T is handled by exactly this rule (and if T reads Q itself, by R or true). Direct K occurrences elsewhere are retained. Thus at most two pure anchored products result per expression. For ordinary roots potentially influenced by Q, aggregate their K corrections into one product with the union of all opposite pure guards. Both ordinary sides cannot inherit Q since their original raw supports would both contain R. Any member of R is an original anchor for that guard union. T cannot contribute to this correction.

If Q has empty raw support it is the union of two pure anchored-clause AND products. After the unique T split there is no OR causing a raw-positive restoration of Q, nor a further raw-empty OR: the only other charged pretop gates were T and the removed M. Thus every occurrence of Q in each expression is a multiplicative factor. Split that single factor into its two arms, even if several paths carry it. Each term is a clean pure anchored product. Ordinary roots give no correction in this case, since raw-empty Q cannot reach their raw-positive inlets through ORs other than T, which cannot feed them.

Consequently at most four batches are needed at the T-root and at most two at each of c−1 other centreless roots, plus at most one ordinary correction. All clauses are original anchored supports or literals on the surviving domain. Assign original surviving residual edges exclusively once. For U≥8 the no-x-star footprint gives at most one double globally, and adding the two x edges gives U≤2+[4+2(c−1)+1]+1=2c+6≤2c+j+3. For U≤7 one has c≥1 and j≥3.
