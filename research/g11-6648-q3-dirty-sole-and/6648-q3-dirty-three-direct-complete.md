# q=3 dirty sole x-AND with all three exceptional ORs directly reading dirty ANDs

Under the original least size-21 G11 TopForm assumptions, j≥3, raw x=3 has sole dirty AND g=x∧S, and all three physical exceptional ORs read at least one dirty AND pin. We prove U≤P=j+2c+3 over the original residuals. The mandatory M=g∨D is raw-empty, D is singleton-negative, and x=false deletes only g and M, identifying M with D in the least 19-gate card. No original star contains x. The two original x-edges are budgeted separately. Let the other paid gates be Q,T. The argument permits shared donors, AND-mediated paths from one gate to another, reconvergence, and all placements of T in relation to D and M. The end-of-proof small integer reduction uses the reviewed one-star P=14 exclusion, not an unconditional U≤14 claim.

## Frontier and the both-in-S dispatch

Every signal in the OR/input frontier of S, found by expanding only ANDs, accepts both singleton 6 and singleton 7, by the sole x port. A raw input does not. An original Lambda OR cannot do this: it propagates both singleton positives to the same side of its witnessing ordinary clean top. An opposite raw vertex must neighbor both (it cannot equal either, by singleton rejection), so it would be x, impossible by the dirty sole reader. Every frontier wire is therefore charged and is not the later M. At least one of Q,T feeds S.

If both feed S and are incomparable, both must occur as frontier wires and each accepts both singletons. Neither can feed a Lambda OR or ordinary star on an OR-only path. After x=false all their paths to the original top boundary are AND-only, and both go via S to one common centreless top. Indeed each such AND-only signal with two readers can be factored once around its affected root union, saving a gate in the least 19-card. Expand both two-input selectors at that root into at most four anchored conjunctions, and use one at each other centreless top. If one precedes the other, name the later T. T occurs on the frontier and likewise has only AND paths to a single centreless top in the card. Split T there into its two actual pins. Each resulting product, or an unaffected top, has only the earlier Q selector: for Q+ use its original dirty donor times the true branch, or the branch given by its raw-facing pin; for Q0 use either conjunctive input arm. One pooled ordinary correction suffices for positive Q, none for Q0. There are at most 4+2(c−1)+1=2c+3 batches. The no-x-star exclusive-double bound gives U≤2c+6 for U≥8; otherwise c≥1 suffices. Detailed arguments with card aliasing are in bounds_6648_q3_incomparable_two_in_S.md (107d18db…) and bounds_6648_q3_comparable_two_in_S.md (854b704b…). In particular no assumption of distinct dirty input donors across Q and T is used.

## Exactly one in S

Name that gate Q. Its sole occurrence as a distinct S-frontier signal implies S=Q physically by leastness. Q accepts both 6 and 7 singletons, so cannot feed a Lambda or ordinary top by OR-only paths. No earlier mixed OR feeds Q, since T does not feed S and M is later. Q's two inputs are thus pure anchored conjunctions (this includes an input literal or single pure Lambda disjunction). For Q+ write K∨L with a dirty AND K and pure raw-positive L. For Q0 its two inputs K1,K2 are distinct dirty ANDs, since no prior mixed OR can supply an empty OR and duplicate pins are redundant.

Interpret the following tests in the **actual 19-card**. M→D cannot introduce a new raw support, but a pretop OR formerly reading M (necessarily charged) now has D in that pin. All original non-x pairs are still realized, and the aggregation can be arranged as an OR tree on the unchanged original top boundary. D cannot equal Q because D rejects every singleton and Q accepts two.

| Mutually exclusive card test | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Q has no forward path to T | Q is terminal before the top boundary except through ANDs. |
| Q reaches raw-empty T | T is terminal before the top boundary except through ANDs. |
| Q reaches positive T and OR-reaches its raw-positive pin | This pin must equal Q (no intermediate Lambda can inherit both 6,7 singleton positives); T is terminal by the same paired-singleton test. |
| Q reaches positive T and does not OR-reach that pin | T=V∨R with R pure and V=Q·A. Use the numerical budget below. |

A terminal signal here cannot return through an OR after an AND, as this is a paid entrance, nor via uncharged ORs if it is raw-empty or accepts both singletons. In the first row no such route can end in the only other surviving charge T. By factor extraction on the least card, the terminal and each subsequent AND have a unique reader before the original top boundary; multiple direct AND readers save a gate upon factoring once around their affected aggregation. The terminal reaches just one centreless top. Split it into either original OR pin at that root. Substituting either arm only decreases the top and the two terms cover all its positives. The remaining selector's two branches reduce each product to at most two clean anchored conjunctions. In the first row Q,T are incomparable (T→Q is prohibited by its original absence from S), and in the next two the remaining Q is earlier. For a positive remaining selector, guard its true branch by the actual pure dirty AND and evaluate the other branch by substituting only its raw-facing pin. A mixed Lambda in the latter branch gives its original anchored raw support; the paid Q itself gives its physically pure raw-facing pin, singleton or Lambda, never an assumed anchor on Q. In the true branch OR-only descendants of Q are true. For an empty selector there is no raw-positive restoration after the terminal is split: all remaining occurrences are conjunctive and its two pure-product arms suffice. This also handles a chosen terminal pin which is itself Q (or a Q-containing AND/OR), since the same remaining selector is used throughout. For ordinary tops one positive selector supplies at most one additional clean product with all opposite pure guards pooled, anchored by any raw vertex of that selector; two affected sides would activate that vertex's singleton. Empty selectors contribute no correction. Thus the first three rows each have at most 2c+3 batches; U≤2c+6 as in the both-in-S proof.

## The multiplicative route to positive T

In the last row R is a pure raw-facing wire. Q cannot enter another pre-T OR (paired singleton obstruction and no remaining charged gate). Consequently every route from Q into T's AND donor V is multiplicative and V=Q·A, where A is a conjunction of pure originally anchored clauses or literals (true if empty). In the card V is still the original dirty AND: an original positive direct entrance T could not read empty M on its raw-positive side. Each pre-T impure primitive is Q itself. This accounts for any new path through the M→D alias; after it there is no other surviving charge between Q and T. No privacy of the pre-T gates is needed.

On V-negative pairs set T to R: Q stays as a multiplicative factor or is irrelevant at each centreless root, so splitting Q into its two pure arms gives at most two clean batches. On V-positive pairs V implies Q and T, so set both true and multiply each resulting pure conjunction by V. Expanding V=Q·A gives at most two further batches. These latter terms are below the original clean root since V ensures both switches agree with the true substitution. Direct factors sharing the inlets of either gate are evaluated with the same global flag; their pre-T factors are pure or Q. All surviving raw ORs in the R projection carry their original raw supports, with T's own value taken from R, not from an asserted anchor on T. On ordinary tops only T can alter the raw-only evaluation: Q cannot reach an ordinary OR-only inlet except after the dirty V and its return via T. Opposite guards at T-receiving ordinary tops are pure and their union is originally anchored by any member of raw(R). Therefore the sole correction V times this union costs at most two batches. Each centreless root costs at most four.

If such a root reads V directly it has no V-negative residuals, saving two batches. If it reads a dirty input Ki to Q directly (K when Q positive), retain Ki and cofactor Q=true, since Ki implies Q. This makes V=A, T=A∨R; expanding this one selector in the retained product costs at most two clean batches. The Ki differ from the later V. Choose a direct raw-empty operand at every centreless root. These operands are distinct: two roots sharing one direct pin could be factored immediately in the minimum 19-card by reassociating their top aggregation. For Q+, the candidates are the j−1 surviving original dirty AND gates, including good operands K and V. For Q0, the candidates are the same j−1 AND gates plus Q, including three good operands K1,K2,V. No clean original AND can be an operand by TopForm. Both counts ensure at least m=max(0,c−j+3) roots with the two-batch saving. Also Q+ gives c≤j−1, while Q0 forces j≥4 because g,K1,K2,V are distinct dirty ANDs.

Consequently for U≥8 the exclusive anchored-double lemma gives U≤4c−2m+5, including two x edges, two ordinary batches and one possible double. At c=0 the two ordinary batches and x edges give U≤6 even if U<8. At c≥1, U≤7 suffices.

For completeness, a strict failure with P≥14 is already excluded: if any ordinary top exists there is at least one star, hence U≤15. For P≥15 this suffices; for P=14 the only possible failure has U=15 and exactly one starred edge, ruled out by the reviewed P14 one-star theorem. If there is no ordinary top, least size 21 and minimal output aggregation give P≥22 (6648-regime-index.md). Thus only P≤13 is needed here, whence c≤3 and j≤10−2c. At c=1 the bound 4c−2m+5 is ≤2c+j+3, with m≥1 when j=3. At c=2 the bound suffices for j=3,4 and all j≥6. At j=5 a failure would give U≥13, so at most three original stars; any anchored double requires at least four distinct star edges. Thus the surplus vanishes and U≤12, as needed. At c=3,j=3, Q+ would give c≤j−1 and Q0 would require j≥4, so this cell cannot occur. At c=3,j=4, m≥2 yields U≤13. All cases prove the original U≤j+2c+3.
