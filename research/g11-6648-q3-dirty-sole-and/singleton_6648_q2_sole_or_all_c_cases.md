# Size 21 with sole OR port of vertex 3, q=2, arbitrary c

Use the donor safety argument in singleton_6648_q1_sole_or_all_c.md: x=3 has sole reader M=x OR w; w is independent of x, singleton-negative, and every independent input activating w contains both 9 and 10. It is clean after either individual deletion. In the following, residual status and Lambda star anchors always refer to the ORIGINAL circuit. The official hypotheses include j>=3.

**One exceptional pure clause.** Suppose w=D is a dirty AND and the other charged OR E is pure. Before and after globally replacing M by 3, every nontrivial pure boundary support is anchored unless E itself. Its two immediate children are anchored pure ORs or raw inputs, because E has no dirty ancestry and there is no other charged pure OR. Apply the following lemma on the full graph or on a vertex deletion, always retaining the original anchor. A clean positive conjunction with at most one exceptional primitive E accepts at most TWO residual edges in G11 if E's children are individually anchored or singleton. For a matching of three marked edges, one first needs absence of ambient crosses: a cross between ab,cd forces every anchored clause to have trace ac or bd after relabeling the cross ac. At least one anchored clause exists (a single disjunction cannot cleanly accept a matching). A second cross of the opposite parity makes the anchored traces inconsistent; a same-parity second cross creates a four-cycle. Hence the two opposite cross pairs are nonedges that hit every anchored clause, and one exceptional clause cannot miss both, as it hits ab and cd. Thus every matching is induced. Eight independent orientations of three marked edges require eight distinct rejecting clauses. At least seven are anchored and the corresponding cube vertices are connected even after omitting one. Adjacent vertices give the same anchor by their two common mates. All six endpoints therefore neighbor one vertex, contrary to maximum degree four.

If there are two edges hu,hv, every anchored clause contains h (otherwise its anchor would be h and star-cover the spokes). Hence h misses E by singleton rejection and neighbors every surviving vertex of E by pair activation. Each of E's two children with two or more surviving vertices must then have anchor h and cannot supply either residual neighbor. As both u and v are required, each child has precisely one surviving vertex, E={u,v} on this domain. All accepted edges must hit u or v, and neither u nor v can have residual degree at least two since such a pivot must miss E. Hence there are only two. Empty or singleton E or a removed child causes no exception to these arguments.

The D cone has no mixed ORs preceding M; its clauses obey the lemma, including on each 9/10 deletion. Since 9,10 are nonadjacent, at most four original residuals satisfy D. When D=false the projection M->3 preserves the whole circuit on that input; it decreases the function elsewhere. As in the q=1 proof, every projected centreless top is a clean conjunction of pure raw supports anchored except possibly E. Projected ordinary tops contribute only original star edges. Thus U<=4+2c<=j+2+2c. This proves the entire single mixed charge plus single pure charge case, with E anywhere in D's cone or outside.

**Raw-empty sibling w.** If w itself is OR, its raw support is empty and it is dirty by TopForm as in the q=1 proof. The charges are w and M. Both pins A,B of w are dirty ANDs (a prior OR pin with no raw support would also be dirty and charged). All earlier primitive clauses of the two arms are anchored. Each arm inherits the 9/10 safety condition since it implies w. Its total residuals number at most three: the 9-deleted restriction gives an induced matching of size at most two by degree-four homogeneity; all further edges are incident to 9 and belong to the 10-deleted restriction's matching, hence contribute at most one. Both arms thus cover at most six when j>=4. For j=3 the two arms must be incomparable in their pure multiplicative ancestry, since otherwise w duplicates one operand. Each has at most two dirty ancestors, at most three clauses, hence each restriction's matching has at most one edge by the orientation count; two arms cover at most four. When w=false, project w->false and M->3. There is no remaining charged OR contribution. A centreless top is either killed or projects to a clean anchored conjunction; ordinary tops yield only original stars. Thus U<=j+2+2c here too.

**Second mixed OR Q outside the upstream cone of dirty AND D=w.** All D primitive factors are anchored, and the two restrictions as above give at most three D-positive residuals (also <=d+1 by the orientation bound). If a dirty AND pin Z of Q implies D, project M->3 and Q->its other pin, preserving all evaluations when D=false. If Q originally had nonempty raw support, its other pin retains an ordinary raw disjunction after M->3. If Q originally had empty raw support, no uncharged OR could directly read it, and Q cannot go through D to M; hence substituting even another dirty AND pin only inserts that conjunctive cone along downstream AND paths. All projected tops are clean anchored conjunctions and ordinary projected tops yield only original stars. U<=3+2c suffices.

Alternatively suppose that Q is raw-empty regardless of whether either dirty pin implies D. There is no other OR directly reading Q before a clean AND, and any downstream dirty AND must also continue through ANDs until first clean top (it cannot feed M's D pin by the hypothesis, nor feed Q). Factor Q out of the OR aggregation of its downstream tops by setting Q symbolically true on its AND descendants, aggregating the cofactors, and restoring Q once. All its direct AND readers disappear, so minimality forces at most one; the same reasoning applies to its dirty AND descendants. Thus it reaches just one affected centreless top. After M->3, both arms of Q become pure anchored conjunctions (or a constant false if a simplification kills a term); the unique affected top is Q times a conjunction of guard clauses. Splitting the two arms gives two clean anchored projected conjunctions, hence at most four residuals at this top. Unaffected ordinary tops cover only stars and other centreless tops cover at most two. Together with the three possible D-positive residuals this gives U<=2c+5<=2c+j+2. Shared dirt is never charged separately in this count.

These analytical subcases do not close a second nonempty genuinely independent mixer or a raw-empty secondary OR inside the protected D cone. No numerical enumeration is used.
