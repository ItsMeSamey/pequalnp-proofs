# Free neighbors in the saturated endpoint cofactor

**Scope.** In the single-star-edge P14 slice of L-meta-6648 assume a least top-form binary AO realization on G11 of size at most 21, exactly one starred edge ab, and 2t+λ=8. Here t counts ordinary tops and λ counts Lambda gates; m_a counts singleton-a ORs with one raw-empty pin. The preceding endpoint and Lambda cycle-rank arguments in this package leave only t=2 at edges 59 or 89, or t=3 at edge 89. All three possibilities are impossible.

**Endpoint rigidity.** Use an endpoint a with the lower bound f(a) on setting a=true, and suppose f(a)=14−m_a. Let v∈N(a) differ from b. Assume the further restriction v=false has lower bound f(a)−1. Then v has exactly one direct reader in the original circuit and it is OR.

Here are the details of the transfer, including the effect of changes to gate indices. Normalize the clean output OR aggregation to a tree on all clean top outputs as in 6648-p14-single-star-cycle.md. An original raw-v reader was never part of the clean OR aggregation (it would accept singleton v), and no new raw-v use is created in this step. At a=true remove t ordinary ANDs and all L_a singleton-a ORs, where L_a counts their physical gate indices. None directly read v: the raw sides of each ordinary top are {a},{b}, and a singleton-a OR with raw v input would also have v in its raw support. Forwarded ordinary tops point to b-side wires, never raw v. Retain all gates not expressly removed at this stage, allowing symbolic constants. Reconnecting to a b-side wire cannot newly expose a raw v-pin. This last point concerns immediate wire labels, not semantic singleton dependence.

Let O be the maximal output OR region after these prescribed changes. Flatten it using the distinct non-OR boundary wires, applying the external-use false substitution justified in the endpoint note. The rank saving is ρ=|O|−h+1, with h the number of boundary wires. The connected paid subgraph K given by the b-OR paths and upper aggregation subtree has rank ρ₀≥λ_b+t−1. Boundary vertex v is absent from K: the lower paths have raw support {b}, and the upper tree uses ordinary b-side terminals or clean aggregation gates. It is not necessary that v be absent from the rest of O's boundary. Original raw-v reader rows not inside O remain explicitly present, with operation and raw pin unchanged, before final simplification. If substitution or aliasing makes any such retained row removable, this is an *additional* gate saved.

The gate count is at most 21−t−L_a−ρ ≤ 14−m_a=f(a). Thus this construction has no spare row: all counted inequalities are equalities and any additional deletion or bypass is impossible. If at least two original reader gates of v are in O, the vertex v is on its boundary with at least two incident pin edges. Removing v from the region graph leaves a connected graph containing K: every OR still has a forward OR path to output, and every other boundary wire still feeds such a path. Restoring v and its at least two edges increases its cycle rank by at least one. Hence ρ≥ρ₀+1, contradicting saturation. This proof counts distinct pin occurrences even if readers reconverge at the next gate.

If one original v-reader lies in O, the flattened output includes v as an explicit OR term. Choose an ordinary binary aggregation tree of the distinct boundary wires. There are at least two meaningful boundary terms if v is present: the restricted function also accepts inputs with v=false. Thus v has an OR reader in the new circuit. This remains true if the reader is the output itself. Any original v-reader outside O surviving as well gives a second distinct use. If none lies inside O, all original reader rows survive outside O with the same operation. Removed top rows cannot have exposed v directly, so these conclusions hold before any further folding. A constant/alias simplification that deletes another row contradicts the floor as above.

In this optimal f(a)-gate realization the free variable v has just one reader, an OR. Two distinct readers disappear when v=false, violating the f(a)−1 floor. A sole AND becomes false; if it is the output the remaining function would be identically false, and otherwise a live reader is deleted as well. A sole OR reader which itself is the output is permitted by this argument. Nonconstancy and essentiality ensure the reader exists. It follows that with one inside reader there are no original outside readers, and the original inside reader is OR. With none inside, the unchanged surviving original readers force precisely one original OR reader. This establishes the claimed rigidity.

**The additional floors are explicit.** For a=9, N(a)={0,5,7,8}; choose v=7. The core left after setting all four neighbors false has a 7-gate lower bound by L-meta-927 on the labels (10,4,6,2,1,3). For a=8, N(a)={1,4,5,9}; choose v=4. The core has an 8-gate lower bound on labels (6,3,7,10,0,2) by the same result. Both cores contain edges (respectively 14 and 36), take value zero at the empty input, and are nonconstant. The graph at a=true is the OR of its four free neighbor variables and that core. Fixing any one neighbor false leaves the other three free; fixing those three false successively costs at least one gate each (each remains essential by singleton vs empty). The core bounds hence give respective f(a)−1 lower bounds 10 and 11, matching full floors 11 and 12. Equality of the former minima, if needed, follows from the saturated realization by deleting its essential v once. The included source crosswalk identifies these cores directly from the issued graph.

**Forbidden original sole OR.** For both selected v there is a neighbor u with N(u)⊆N[v]: for v=7 choose u=3 with N(3)={6,7}; for v=4 choose u=1 with N(1)={4,8}. Suppose the sole reader is v∨D. Its other input is independent of v (raw duplicates would be redundant) and is not a true constant. If D is true on any independent T, strip v without affecting D. T cannot meet N(v), since toggling v would create an edge without changing its only channel. Nor can a neighbor u have no neighbor in T, since then T∪{u} is independent, D is true there, and toggling v once again creates an edge. With u as above, an independent T outside N[v] cannot dominate u. Consequently D is below the graph function. No raw input is below it (singletons are independent); following clean OR pins backward must therefore encounter a clean AND in D's ancestry, possibly D itself. The sole reader v∨D is dirty on singleton v. It is live, and some downstream path must pass an AND before output since any all-OR path would deliver that singleton to output. The clean AND below that downstream AND contradicts original TopForm. No TopForm property of the cofactor is used.

**Applying the contradiction.** For t=2, λ=4. At 59 one has λ_5=0, and at 89 one has λ_8≤1. Thus λ_9≥3; the donor-subset bound forces m_9≥3, and its floor 11 forces m_9≤3. Apply rigidity at 9 with the forbidden v=7. At t=3 and edge 89, three distinct 8-side top operands require at least two nonraw singleton-8 wires, impossible with m_8≤1. The floor 12 forces m_8≤2, so apply rigidity at 8 with the forbidden v=4. This exhausts the single-star-edge P14 slice; it does not exclude other parameter slices or establish the official conjecture. The argument is mathematical, with no new Lean or native run.
