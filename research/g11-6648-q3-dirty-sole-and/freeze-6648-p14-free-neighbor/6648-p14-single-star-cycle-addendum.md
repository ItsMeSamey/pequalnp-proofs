# Additional cycle rank from Lambda

Use the endpoint lemma in 6648-p14-single-star-cycle.md. Write L_r for all physical OR gates with raw support exactly {r}, m_r for those with one raw-empty pin, and λ_r for Lambda gates with raw support {r}. Since an ordinary top can only have opposite singleton sides {a},{b}, all Lambda gates have one of those labels. We have L_a≥m_a+λ_a, allowing singleton OR gates with two nonempty pins outside Lambda. All L_a gates disappear when a=true without touching b-side OR paths or clean output aggregation.

Trace all singleton-b OR paths backwards from the t top b-pins to b, retaining only parents of raw support {b}. Each reached gate has one or two such parents. A gate with two is Lambda because it reaches an ordinary top's pin. Conversely every b-Lambda has such a witness and is reached on these paths. Therefore the connected subgraph rooted at b has rank exactly λ_b, counting its two parent arcs separately even if a parent repeats. Attaching the disjoint upper aggregation tree as in the endpoint lemma increases the rank by t−1. The true-a cofactor therefore has size at most 21−t−L_a−(λ_b+t−1)≤22−2t−λ−m_a. At P=14, 2t+λ=8 and hence m_r≤14−floor(r) for either endpoint, where floor(r) bounds the true-r cofactor.

For completeness each singleton-r OR describes r OR a union of raw-empty donor wires from its m_r mixers. A two-nonempty-pin OR in a least circuit has incomparable child donor sets, since containment would make it semantically equal its larger child. Each Lambda therefore represents a donor subset of cardinality at least two. Distinct gates represent distinct subsets, by leastness. Thus λ_r≤2^{m_r}−m_r−1. Also t distinct r-pins demand at least t−1 nonraw r-wires. With m_r=0 there are none, and with m_r=1 there is at most one, since a first two-nonempty-pin gate would have comparable codes.

Using independently supplied true floors for G11, vertices 0 through 10 respectively have lower bounds 14,16,15,17,13,13,14,14,12,11,12. Exclude 1,2,3 from every star. Only 8,9,10 can host any Lambda, with individual caps 1,4,1. They span just edge 89. If t=1, λ=6 exceeds every two-endpoint cap. If t=2, λ=4 forces endpoint 9 and an opposite endpoint with at least one mixer, leaving 59 or 89. If t=3, λ=2 forces endpoint 9 or edge 8–10, which is absent. The opposite endpoint needs two nonraw wires, leaving 89. For t=4, the 3t−2 bound requires both endpoint floors at most 11, whereas only vertex 9 qualifies. These necessary conditions do not establish existence of the remaining cases.
