# Dirty sole x AND, positive Q in S and inherited W=M OR a pure arm

Use the G11, size-21 least TopForm and j≥3 premises of 6648-q3-dirty-sole-and-dispatch.md. Write x=3 with neighbors y=6,z=7. Its sole reader is the dirty g=x∧S, with sole reader M=g∨D. The three exceptional ORs are M, a raw-positive direct exit Q=K∨L in the ancestry of S (dirty AND K, positive raw-facing pin L), and a nondirect inherited W=M∨R with R pure and positive. In particular the R inlet has no separate dirty route from Q. D may or may not use Q, and no private-use assumption is made on D,K,S,M or W. We prove U≤2c+j+3 with the ORIGINAL residual and star labels.

1. Neither clean ordinary tops nor Lambda can be supported at x: the only original raw reader of x is dirty g. Both xy,xz are residual. Singleton y and singleton z activate S by the sole-port comparison, and S is independent of x. Expand S through ANDs to its input or OR frontier. Every frontier wire accepts both singleton y and singleton z. No raw input can do that. A Lambda OR positive on both such singletons is also impossible: at its witnessing clean ordinary top each singleton propagates to the same inlet by OR paths. Let w be the raw opposite center. It cannot equal y or z (else the clean top accepts that singleton), and it must neighbor both, by evaluating the two pairs. The unique common neighbor of y,z is x; x cannot be a raw star center. This argument uses actual singleton evaluations, not a presumed raw reach for both on the frontier wire. A singleton-positive OR is dirty and in orN. Among Q,M,W only Q is not strictly downstream of S. Hence each frontier occurrence is the very same Q, so S computes Q and in a least circuit is physically Q (otherwise substitute earlier Q and remove the duplicate). In particular Q cannot OR-reach any ordinary top, by the same paired-singleton test, even through later ORs. Any pre-M OR directly reading Q also accepts both singletons and cannot be Lambda; there is no such distinct exceptional OR. Every pre-Q OR in the ancestor cones used here is pure and Lambda if nontrivial: an earlier dirty-AND-to-OR injection is unavailable, and a clean AND upstream of a later AND contradicts TopForm. L and each primitive in K are thus singleton or originally anchored pure clauses.

2. Take the original x=false restriction; g disappears and M forwards D. The restriction uses 19 gates as certified, but the following modal counting invokes neither leastness nor new anchors in its projections. The originally inherited W now computes D∨R, NOT this value on the original x-edges. Pay those two edges separately. All later assertions about decrease, exactness and root modes concern x-free assignments. Each old ordinary or centreless root is retained as a labeled output or simplified to its restriction. D was already independent of x, singleton-negative, and every independent input activating it contains both 9 and 10 (sole-port shielding at M, as in singleton_6648_size21_c0q2_sole_and_dirty.md). It therefore becomes clean after either separate deletion of 9 or 10. There is no edge 9–10, so every residual belongs to at least one of these domains. D cannot be an OR: before M every raw-positive OR is singleton-positive, and no raw-empty OR is possible with Q the only earlier charged gate and raw-positive. Thus D is a dirty AND. Its AND-only primitive frontier consists of pure anchored clauses and possibly Q itself. Indeed a distinct impure pre-M OR would need an OR path from Q; the first OR reading Q was excluded in (1). Consequently D is either pure, or D=Q·B with B a pure anchored product, where repeated Q factors are redundant. Direct K factors count as their full conjunction and are permitted.

3. For D-positive original residuals use D itself if pure, otherwise the two pure anchored subterms L·B and K·B. Each implies D, hence is clean on either single-vertex deletion. Assign each D-positive residual once to one term and one deletion on which it survives. This gives at most four clean anchored batches (two when D is pure). Anchor edges in the full original G11 remain starred even if an anchor is deleted. For each allocated double the induced-matching and four-corner conditions use those original anchors, as in singleton_6648_q2_upstream_complete.md. The no-x-star lookup allows at most one double globally when U≥8 (6648-all-raw-empty-charges.md, §1). The lookup condition is a property of the labeled G11 star set and exclusive allocations, not a condition on the remainder of the gate signature.

4. On D-negative x-free graph pairs set D=false everywhere in the restricted circuit, including all external uses and the former M aliases. W reduces to pure R, the output is unchanged on the designated pairs, and every restricted clean root only decreases. If the D suppression affects K, the projected K is either zero or the same pure anchored product: pre-Q dirty AND uses in K are multiplicative, and no other dirty-to-OR exit precedes Q. A surviving Q now computes K'∨L, with L still pure. Every centreless root supplies at most two clean anchored products by the raw-Q and positive-K' modes. More explicitly, before the first original clean top no OR directly reads Q: such a gate is neither Lambda as shown in (1), nor M (fixed pins), nor W (its original inputs M and pure R). Nor can a new OR-only Q path arise by M→D after D is set false. Every Q occurrence on a root's AND frontier is therefore the original Q itself, and any remaining non-Q primitive is pure with an original anchor or singleton (at W use R; a zero dirty factor kills the product). Distribute the one factor K'∨L. The resulting products imply the original clean root on x-free assignments. Ordinary roots have no Q on their OR-only inlets, since any such original route forces the common center x. W is pure after the projection; every other surviving OR-only raw pathway is its original raw support, or a subset after deletion. Thus no ordinary root owns a D-negative residual. This counts shared or reconvergent dirty subcircuits by semantics; it does not remove multiple physical uses independently.

5. For U≥8 the two paid x edges, up to four D-deletion batches, at most two batches per centreless root and at most one total double give U≤2c+7. This suffices for j≥4. For j=3 the only dirty ANDs are g,K,D unless K=D. In the latter case D is pure and only its two deletion batches are needed. More generally if D is pure it has at most three residuals across the deletions (at most two after deleting 9, at most one additional 9-edge in the induced matching after deleting 10). In the Q-dependent case K,D,g are distinct. D directly reads Q and one other pure clause T, since no other dirty AND is available, g cannot enter D, no OR reads Q, and a K pin would make D=K by absorption. Repeated Q is impossible by leastness. Thus D0=L·T has only two pure anchored clauses and D1=K·T at most three: K is a single AND on two primitive clauses since there is no earlier dirty AND available.

For clarity, on either deletion a clean anchored product with at most three clauses accepts at most one original residual: two would form an induced matching, whose four independent orientations require four different rejecting clauses. Hence D1 has ≤2 residuals jointly. D0 has ≤1 jointly: if there were two, the two deletion bounds force them to be 9b,10a (9–10 is a nonedge). If D0 were globally clean it could not have two. Otherwise an independent positive of a two-clause product has a minimal subassignment of size at most two; by protection that subassignment must be {9,10}. Singleton negativity gives disjoint clause supports, so put 9,a on one side and 10,b on the other. They are distinct endpoints (otherwise singleton positivity or the absent 9–10 edge). The cross ab also activates D0 and must be a graph edge, or else it is an independent positive missing both protected vertices. Vertex b is adjacent to 9 and a; the original anchor for the nonsingleton clause containing 9,a must equal b by C4-freeness. Thus 9b is starred, contradiction. In particular the protected union at j=3 has at most three edges. Charge these directly together with the x pair; allow the one possible surplus only on the remaining centreless batches, giving U≤2c+6. At c=0,j=3 no outside batches remain and U≤5 even when U<8. In all other cases U≤7 is already sufficient (j≥4 or c≥1). The selected balance yields the issued inequality.

This is a source-only mathematical result conditional on the cited site graph/card lemmas. It uses no numerical search and does not treat the joining-secondary case with a separate dirty Q route into the R inlet of W.
