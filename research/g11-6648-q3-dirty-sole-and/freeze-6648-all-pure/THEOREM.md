# All-pure exceptional ORs in the size-21 G11 surplus problem

Assume every pre-top OR in the issued least TopForm circuit is a pure OR of primary inputs. Equivalently it suffices that every charged exceptional OR be pure: the first OR reached from any pre-top AND would have a raw-empty operand and would be charged. Let x=3,y=6,z=7; the remaining eight vertices form H. We prove U≤j+2c+q under the size-21 and critical-card hypotheses already used in 6648-size21-sole-and.md.

## The ports

Vertex x has a unique reader by the size-21 reader theorem. That reader cannot be a pure OR x∨D. If a variable w in D is y or z, the corresponding singleton masks switching x; otherwise testing w together with either y or z forces w to neighbor both. Their unique common neighbor x cannot occur in a nonredundant such donor. A dirty AND sole reader cannot occur: x=false removes it and one live consumer, exhausting the two-gate savings allowed by the false-card lower bound 19. An AND consumer would itself become false and require a third removal, so the unique consumer would be an OR, impure before a later filtering AND. Therefore the sole reader A=x∧S is clean. S must survive x=false: otherwise deleting S,A and A's unique OR consumer would save three gates.

If S has empty raw support it must be a pre-top AND (a pure OR has nonempty raw support). It is dirty by TopForm. Such a gate has at most one direct reader: all of its pre-top descendants are ANDs, each affected clean top factors S, and cofactoring just its direct AND pins true and reinstating S once around the affected top union saves a gate if two readers existed. Thus S would be dead after deleting its sole reader A, a contradiction. Now S is a pure input disjunction and cleanliness at A forces its support contained in N(x)={y,z}. Both y and z must activate S because x has only this reader. Leastness eliminates duplicate pure functions, so S is physically the gate h=y∨z: any genuine binary proper subunion is a raw literal.

Invoke the three-reader (common gate) case of 6648-size21-sole-and.md. In the x=false card h has one outside reader, an OR E, and in the original its additional reader is A. The other two direct ports are one individual OR for y and one for z. Write the former y∨K. On an independent assignment containing z but omitting x,y, the common h is true, and switching y creates yz. Thus K must be false there. No raw variable in pure K is y (redundancy), z (singleton obstruction), or x (sole x reader). Any other must be adjacent to z; the only possibility is 9. Thus the individual ports are y∨9 and z∨10. They need no restriction on which later gates read them. E is pure, and its support has the form {y,z}∪T with at least one vertex in H; otherwise E would duplicate h. The proof does not require its other pin to have disjoint support from h: it uses only existence of an outside vertex. In particular the verifier deliberately permits all eight choices of that vertex, including 1,9,10.

No ordinary star other than A uses x,y,z. A raw path out of y or z to another ordinary top passes first through its individual port, or through E. A pure clause containing {y,9} can be anchored only by z, and {z,10} only by y; neither can be a singleton opposite operand as neither has a direct AND reader. If two raw vertices have at most one common neighbor, the opposite nonempty support would have to equal that singleton. A clause containing {y,z} could be anchored only at x whose sole reader A already uses exactly h. Raw singleton ORs duplicate literals in a least circuit. In particular xy and xz are starred and the remaining 14 graph edges include every residual edge.

## The cost of one centreless top

Pure ordinary tops accept exactly their own starred edges. Assign each residual edge once to an accepting centreless top. Write R for the assigned family. Each such top is a conjunction of nonempty pure primitive clauses. With d private dirty AND ancestors it has at most d+2 distinct primitive factors. Dirty ANDs have at most one direct reader by the extraction above; hence their ancestor sets are disjoint between different tops. A pure primitive OR has at most one direct AND reader as well. Cofactor its direct AND inlets true, retaining its other uses unchanged, and restore the primitive once around the affected top union. Each affected top originally implied the primitive via AND-only paths. When it is true nothing changes, and when false the restored terms are false, so two direct AND readers would save a gate. Thus each nonanchored nonsingleton primitive costs a distinct charged OR globally. A physical Lambda primitive always has an ordinary top anchor, even if it has additional readers.

Every primitive hits every edge in R. For every maximal independent set I of G11 at least one primitive is disjoint from I, since the top is clean. Its support contains no x by x's unique reader. If it contains y it contains 9 or else contains z together with some H vertex; if it contains z it contains 10 or else y with some H vertex. Indeed h's only non-A exit is E, and a centreless top cannot receive raw y,z directly. Any nonsingleton anchored primitive at such a top lies in N(v) for some v in H, contains neither x,y,z, and omits each w with vw∈R because anchor-to-support edges are starred. These are only necessary conditions; the search permits all possible supports obeying them.

The verifier enumerates every nonempty R among 14 edges. It builds all eligible nonempty supports hitting R. A singleton or any support contained in one permitted anchor set costs one slot; all others cost two, one primitive and one charged OR. Each option covers the maximal independent sets on which it is false. Options with the same coverage use the cheaper cost, and a no-cheaper option with smaller coverage may be discarded. On nonempty unmet mask M, choose a set I∈M and minimize cost(C)+DP(M minus the covered mask of C) over supports rejecting I. Empty M costs zero. Hence the returned minimum is a lower bound on the actual distinct primitive count plus actual nonanchored primitive count. There are 16,383 nonempty R. Only R={(1,8),(4,8),(5,8),(8,9)} has cost strictly below |R|, with minimum 3. In that case singleton 8 is rejected by a primitive omitting 8, which therefore contains all four listed neighbors. It is nonsingleton and unanchored since its only possible anchor is 8 and all four spokes are residual. A centreless top has at least one dirty AND child, giving d+2≥3. Thus its actual allocated d+2 plus one distinct charged primitive still pays 4. For all other R the DP pays |R| directly. Summing proves the result.

The calculation uses plain Python only, without a compiler, solver, desk submission or independent replay. `receipt.txt` records the local result. The use of size-21 and card structure is conditional on the existing source proofs. This package does not contain a combined Lean verification.
