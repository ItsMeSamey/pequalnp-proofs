# Three direct entrances: the two incomparable secondary gates both reach S

In the q=3, j≥3 least size-21 G11 circuit let x=3 have sole dirty AND reader g=x∧S, with unique reader M=g∨D. The two other paid ORs Q,T each directly read a dirty AND, both belong to the ancestry of S, and neither belongs to the ancestry of the other. Raw signs are unrestricted; dirty donors or earlier AND/OR ancestors can be shared. The goal is U≤2c+j+3.

S is positive on both neighbor singletons 6,7 by the sole x reader. Follow its ANDs back to the OR/input frontier. Every frontier signal is positive on both singletons, so is not a raw input. A Lambda OR positive on both singleton inputs would send those positives along an OR-only path to one side of a witnessing clean ordinary top. Each vertex w in the opposite raw support would need to be adjacent to both 6,7, by singleton rejection and pair activation. Their only common neighbor is x, which cannot raw-reach a clean top (its only reader is dirty AND g). Every such frontier OR is therefore charged. M is later, leaving only Q,T. Both occur as frontier signals since they are incomparable and both feed S. In particular each is positive on both singleton inputs. Semantically S=Q∧T. Neither Q nor T can OR-reach an original ordinary top or be directly read by an original Lambda OR by the same singleton argument. A pretop OR immediately reading either or any downstream dirty AND along its influence would be charged: an OR reading an AND has an empty-raw pin, and a direct OR reading Q or T inherits both singleton positives. The only potentially later charged gate is M; the other secondary is incomparable.

Set x=false and use the actual minimum size-19 card, deleting g and aliasing M to D. Both Q and T, and S, survive (no further deletion fits the 19 floor). Since M was removed and both secondary gates precede it, every remaining path from Q or T to an original top boundary is AND-only; a new edge out of D is possible, but D cannot equal singleton-positive Q or T, and the original readers of M before the top boundary were ANDs (a raw-empty M would charge any OR reader, and no later paid OR exists). Regroup original clean aggregation as an OR tree. For either Q or T and every subsequent AND, two direct AND readers can be cofactored to true and its factor inserted once around the OR of affected roots, saving a gate; all affected roots imply the factor through entirely AND-only routes. Minimum size 19 prohibits this. Hence each secondary signal has a unique forward path to a single original top. Both paths pass through the surviving gate S and thus reach the same original top. S is an AND, not a raw gate or Q/T (both incomparable and feeding it), so this top is centreless. No other original top receives either selector. This argument uses no uniqueness for the selectors' input wires or their shared ancestors.

All gates before the two secondary ORs are pure ORs and AND products on each respective ancestral cone: any other pretop impure OR would require another charged entrance, contrary to incomparability and M being later. Their two pins thus each have the form of an anchored pure-clause conjunction, including a single literal or anchored disjunction as a one-clause case. On the unique affected top expand Q and T into their two alternatives each, simultaneously at every occurrence, yielding at most four pure anchored conjunctions. All other centreless tops use just one anchored conjunction, and ordinary tops yield only original stars. Direct uses of D or of shared ancestors are included in those same conjunctions; setting the alternative at a physical OR only decreases signals. Each expanded term is clean on G−x, with original star labels. Allocate residual pairs exclusively. For U≥8 the established no-x-star anchored matching footprint gives at most one extra edge across all batches, in addition to the two x edges. Thus U≤2+(4+c−1)+1=c+6≤j+2c+3. For U≤7 use c≥1 and j≥3. This is a source argument with no new native or Lean run.
