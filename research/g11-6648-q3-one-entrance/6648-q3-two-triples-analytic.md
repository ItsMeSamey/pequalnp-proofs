# Two global exceptions cannot support two disjoint triples on H8

This is a proposed independent paper proof of the single entry F(2,2)≤5. H is G11−{3,6,7}, with 8 vertices and 11 edges. Only 8 has H-degree four. The three deleted vertices have at most one neighbor each in H. A hypothetical coverage of at least six marked edges by two batches restricts to six exclusive marks U. Each effective exception is a union of up to two guarded terminals, or the later one may be a union of the earlier exception and one guarded terminal. A guarded terminal is a singleton or a subset of the H-neighbors of one original anchor, all its corresponding spokes outside U. A batch is accepted by a positive conjunction clean on all independent H inputs. Physical claims are unnecessary after this abstraction.

Use the separately established one-batch upper bound F(2,1)≤3, so both batches must own exactly three disjointly allocated marks. Use also the established one-binary-exception upper bound F(1,1)≤2. These are prerequisites; this note removes only the F(2,2) computation. Every guarded terminal meets at most one marked neighbor of any vertex h: two such neighbors in a nonsingleton terminal force its anchor to be h by four-cycle freeness, contradicting their marked spokes. Thus a union of k guarded terminals meets at most k marked neighbors of h.

**Matching trace rule.** Suppose one clean product owns disjoint marks ab,cd and ac is a cross edge. Any guarded clause hits both marks and cannot contain a,d (the common neighbor c would be its unique anchor, starring cd), nor b,c (anchor a would star ab). Its trace on the four endpoints is therefore ac or bd. If another cross exists, oppositely placed crosses form a four-cycle; after interchanging labels an adjacent second cross is ad. Now a,c have common neighbor d (starring cd), and b,d have common neighbor a (starring ab), so no guarded clause can hit both marks. In a triple using two binary supports E,F as indispensable clauses there must be a guard: otherwise the entire clean product is E times F. Overlap would give a positive singleton, while distinct disjoint supports of size at least two would induce a four-cycle. A singleton factor would itself be guarded, leaving only one binary exception. In the one-arbitrary-exception setting a single clause alone is positive on some singleton and likewise cannot be clean. Thus each pair of matching edges within a triple has at most one cross. If ac is the only cross, both ad and bc are independent and hit every guarded clause, so two distinct exceptional clauses are necessary to reject them. These clauses must take complementary traces bc and ad, since both hit ab and cd.

**Nested exceptions.** Let E be binary and F=E union a guarded terminal. Any triple including E reduces to at most one binary exception since F is redundant, and a triple omitting F is equally impossible. Thus only F matters in both triples. By the trace rule, a matching triple with at most one exceptional clause is induced. The eight orientations selecting one endpoint per pair are independent. No accepting clause can miss two orientations, since their union contains a mark. At least seven orientations must miss guarded clauses. Each missed clause contains every opposite endpoint, so clauses missed at adjacent orientations have their anchors at the same vertex by two common neighbors. The cube with at most one vertex removed is connected, and the opposite endpoints of its remaining vertices include all six endpoints. This would give a single anchor degree at least six, impossible in G11. Thus each triple has a pivot h incident to two of its edges. Every guarded clause contains h, because otherwise it would contain both marked mates. A clause must reject singleton h, so F omits h, contains both mates, and is contained in N_H(h) by cleanliness on the pair consisting of h and any member of F. Both triples force the same h by C4. Four distinct marked neighbors of h would have to belong to F, though F is a union of at most three terminals.

**Independent binary exceptions.** Denote the two supports by E,F. Both must occur in each triple or the one-binary-exception bound applies. In particular each hits all six marks. If a vertex h belongs to both, it cannot be a within-batch pivot: any clause missing it would be guarded and contain both marked mates. Such a vertex has marked degree at most two across the two batches. A vertex missing one support has all its marked mates in that binary support, hence also has degree at most two by the terminal observation. Thus the entire six-edge marked graph has maximum degree two. At any pivot the two mates belong to every clause omitting h, so all guarded clauses contain h. If only E omits h, then E⊆N_H(h) as above; if both omit h, then E∩F⊆N_H(h).

The three edges in a batch cannot form a triangle: every pivot must be omitted by one of the two exceptions, but either exception, being a vertex cover, omits at most one of its vertices. Nor can they form a three-edge path a−h−k−b. Its adjacent pivots have opposite membership patterns because hk must hit both exceptions; thus E⊆N_H(h) and F⊆N_H(k), with labels interchanged if necessary. Any edge hitting both supports either meets h,k, meets a common neighbor of h,k, or joins distinct neighbors of h and k to form a four-cycle with hk. There is at most one common neighbor. All three marks of the other batch would have to meet it since h,k are saturated. This contradicts degree two. Thus each batch is either three disjoint edges or a two-edge path plus a separate edge.

**Excluding a matching triple.** Every guarded clause of a three-edge matching batch must hit all three disjoint marks, so its anchor z has at least three incident edges in T=H minus U. It is in H, given the exterior neighborhood sizes, and outside the six matching endpoints since its own marked edge could not hit its guard. There cannot be two such anchors: T has just five edges, so two anchors of T-degree at least three must be adjacent and both have T-degree three. Their joining edge would leave each at most two neighbors among the six matching endpoints. Hence all the guarded clauses have the same z. Its H-neighborhood of size at most four meets all three pairs, so at least two pairs have a unique representative in N_T(z), say u1 and u2 in u1v1 and u2v2. Every guarded clause contains both representatives. Write cd for the third pair.

If u1u2 were an edge, it would be the sole cross between its two pairs. The complementary traces required by the trace rule put u1,u2 in opposite exceptional membership classes. Because every guard contains u1,u2, cleanliness implies E⊆N_H(u1), F⊆N_H(u2) after a possible swap. Consequently all six marks would be incident to u1,u2 or their unique common neighbor z by the four-cycle argument for an adjacent pair. Their total marked degree is at most 2+2+1: z has at least three unmarked H edges and H-degree at most four. Contradiction. The crosses u1v2 and v1u2 are also impossible via z,u1,u2 and the matching edge. Only v1v2 can cross these two pairs.

Of the eight orientations of all three matching pairs, six select u1 or u2, so all six hit every guarded clause. The two exceptions can reject at most two orientations, so at least four of these six must be nonindependent. The potential cross v1v2 accounts for none. By the trace rule each of the first two pairs has at most one cross to cd, and each cross covers only two orientations. To account for four of the six, both must use their forced representative (a cross from v1 or v2 also covers an orientation avoiding both representatives), and they must use different endpoints of cd or the orientations overlap. Up to swapping c,d the crosses are u1c, u2d. Since z has a representative in cd, either zc or zd exists. Respectively z−c−d−u2−z or z−d−c−u1−z would be a four-cycle.

**Two remaining pivots.** Each triple now has a two-edge pivot, and these pivots h,k are distinct by the global degree bound. Record membership in E,F as 00, 01, or 10. Two same-type pivots are impossible: for 01, E lies in both neighborhoods and contains two mates; for 10 use F; for 00 use E∩F, which contains both mates of either pivot. A 00 pivot cannot coexist with 01 or 10 either, since both its mates lie in E∩F, contained in the other pivot's neighborhood. Hence the pivots have opposite 01/10 patterns. Then E⊆N_H(h), F⊆N_H(k), after swapping, and membership of k in E gives hk∈H. Again all marks meet h,k or their unique common neighbor. To account for six marks at marked degree at most two, this common neighbor must exist, all three vertices have marked degree two, and no marked edge joins two of them. Their triangle therefore supplies two more H neighbors per vertex, requiring three vertices of H-degree at least four. H has only one.
