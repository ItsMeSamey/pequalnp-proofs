# Counting paid ORs with AND ancestry versus direct dirty-AND readers

Let I be the set of paid ORs (orN minus lambda) with a proper AND ancestor. Let B be the set of physical OR gate indices having a direct dirty AND input. In a least live TopForm circuit, B is contained in I. Indeed a dirty AND accepts an independent input, so its OR consumer is dirty too. A direct AND inlet has empty raw support, whereas lambda requires two raw-positive inlets. The consumer is therefore paid, and has the AND as an ancestor. Count the physical OR index once even if both inputs are dirty ANDs.

Conversely, each Q in I has some P in B upstream or equal to Q. Since Q is dirty and the graph output is clean, a live downstream path from Q to the output must encounter an AND. A clean AND ancestor of Q would then violate TopForm; all its AND ancestors are dirty. Choose a directed path from one such ancestor to Q, and take its last AND. The next gate on the path is an OR directly reading that dirty AND, and the remaining path to Q is OR-only. Thus the set of distinct last-AND-to-OR gates in the entire cone of Q is nonempty and contained in B. Different paths, different AND pins and different paid descendants can all give the same physical index.

In particular I is empty iff B is empty, and |I|=1 implies |B|=1. The reverse count does not follow from the path argument: a further OR on an OR-only continuation of a direct reader can have AND ancestry while reading two non-AND pins. If that further OR is paid it belongs to I but not B. A concrete conditional instance is the raw-empty sibling E=A OR B at M=x OR E: when E is the sole direct reader and no further direct reader occurs, both E and M are paid and have AND ancestry, but only E directly reads a dirty AND. This is a valid distinction in the classification, not an assertion of existence of a least G11 circuit with that topology. Consequently a theorem for |B|=1 covers the whole |I|=1 case, including arbitrary other paid pure ORs; it may cover more.
