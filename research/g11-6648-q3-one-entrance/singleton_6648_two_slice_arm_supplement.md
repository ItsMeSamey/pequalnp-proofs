# Independent check of the two-slice arm bound

For a protected pure conjunctive arm T before E, suppose T is clean on G−9 and G−10. Its primitive supports are originally anchored or singleton except for at most one common physical pure OR F with two individually anchored or singleton children. Original stars define all residuals. Then T accepts at most three original residual edges.

The one-exception lemma gives at most two per deletion. If there are four total, each belongs to exactly one deletion because 9 and 10 are nonadjacent. Thus on G−10 there are two distinct spokes at 9; on G−9 two distinct spokes at 10. On either slice let h be the surviving centre, u,v the two mates. Any nonexceptional primitive omitting h must contain both u and v. Its original anchor must then be h, by full-graph C4 uniqueness, contradicting residual status. Consequently all nonexceptional primitive clauses contain h, so F occurs and misses h by singleton rejection. Every surviving p in F lies in N(h), since {h,p} activates T. An F child with at least two surviving leaves consequently has original anchor h, and cannot supply u or v without starring the corresponding spoke. Since both mates are in F and there are just two children, each child contributes precisely one mate and nothing else on this deletion. The trace of F is exactly {u,v}. This allows an additional original leaf at the deleted vertex at this step.

The first slice excludes 9 from F globally, as 9 survives, and the second excludes 10 globally. Neither deletion can therefore hide any original leaf of F. Both exact mate pairs agree and are two common neighbors of 9 and 10, contradicting C4-freeness. With no exceptional primitive the shared-spoke stage already gives a contradiction.

Hence both A,B before E contribute at most six residuals jointly. After E=false the remaining projected centreless products have at most one two-child exception and ordinary projections give original stars, so U≤6+2c.
