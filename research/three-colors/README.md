# Three simultaneous odd color classes

Let A be symmetric and alternating over GF(2), with three independent nonzero marker columns a,b,c. Assume every vertex carries one of these three labels, and seek target a+b+c. Thus each of the three color classes must contribute an odd number of vertices. Assume each color class induces either a clique or an independent set. Edges between classes are arbitrary. A witness exists precisely when some transversal triple induces an independent set or a triangle. Checking triples gives a deterministic cubic-time witness algorithm.

To prove necessity, let S induce even degrees and have each color count odd. Within a selected vertex's own color its degree is even, either zero or one less than an odd number. Thus at each selected vertex the degrees to its two other color classes have equal parity. The three cross-edge counts on S all have a common parity r, by summing the local identities in each color. For the three cross-edge bits e,f,g of one transversal triple, the homogeneous-edge indicator over GF(2) is 1+e+f+g+ef+fg+ge. Sum this identity over all triples of S. The constant contributes one because all three counts are odd. Each linear term contributes r because every cross-edge is counted once per vertex of the third class. Each quadratic term contributes r because the product of the two cross-degrees at a common vertex equals either one's parity. The total is 1+3r+3r=1. At least one counted triple has all edges or no edges. Its induced degrees are even and it realizes the three markers.

A singleton color may be a forced apex. The conclusion applies to arbitrary cross adjacencies, even if a bipartite graph between two of the classes has unbounded cut rank.

Dropping the restriction on intra-class edges destroys the transversal conclusion. On any odd cycle of length at least five, give two adjacent vertices their own colors and every remaining vertex the third color. A feasible support must contain the adjacent pair, which forces their remaining neighbors and then the entire cycle. The full cycle realizes the requested counts. It can be arbitrarily large.

The analogous four-color transversal conclusion already fails on independent classes. On a six-cycle give the three vertices of one bipartition distinct singleton colors and give all other vertices the fourth color. The full cycle works; any single vertex of the fourth color produces odd degrees at two of the forced singleton vertices.
