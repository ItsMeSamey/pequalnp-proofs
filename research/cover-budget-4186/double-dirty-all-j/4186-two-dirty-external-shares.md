# External dirty-wire sharing in the split circuit

Split the unique affected clean top (A∨B)W into R_A=AW and R_B=BW; constructing W once gives a circuit at most one gate above minimum, with only ANDs between dirty products and clean tops. As usual its top aggregation can be reordered without increasing OR count. Only gates that reach the output need be considered. For a dirty wire X let R(X) be its nonempty set of reachable tops. Call X branching if at least two distinct AND gates read it. Duplicate pins do not count twice.

One-factor extraction implies each branching X has exactly two readers. If two branching gates have disjoint reachable-top sets, their disjoint downstream regions can be factored independently, saving at least two gates. This contradicts the one-gate slack.

**Laminar factorization.** Suppose R(X)⊆R(Y) and X,Y are incomparable in the dirty DAG. Keep them both, substitute true for both in all downstream ANDs, and reconstruct outputs. Group projected roots in R(X), restore X once by AND, join the group with roots of R(Y) outside R(X), restore Y once, then join with unaffected roots. This restores both factors exactly, uses at most the original aggregation OR count and at most two new ANDs. A symbolic true marker absorbs its OR subgroup and introduces no physical constant. All direct readers of either X or Y disappear. Thus disjoint reader sets give two gate savings. If X reaches Y, R(Y)⊆R(X); use the same reconstruction with Y' equal to Y evaluated under X=true, restoring Y' for R(Y) and X for R(X). Keep the projected wire Y' when processing Y, and substitute true in its readers. No extra computation of the original Y is required.

**Antichain.** If comparable branching X→Y have no common direct reader, the preceding savings contradict slack. A common direct reader Z equals X AND Y, which is Y since their connecting path is all AND. Bypass Z, saving one. Z is dirty and has a direct AND reader. If the bypass leaves Y with at least two distinct readers, extract Y and save a second gate. Otherwise Y's other original reader V was the only reader of Z, so V originally computed Z AND Y = Y and can also be bypassed. Thus distinct branching gates are incomparable.

**Two-root incidence.** As no branching gate can be downstream of another, each of the latter's two readers has a unique forward path to its clean top. Hence |R(X)|≤2. Different two-root gates cannot realize the same two-root pair. Without a common reader this follows from laminar factorization. If they have one common reader Z=X AND Y, it reaches precisely one root R; both other readers must lead to the other root S. If Z is dirty, simultaneous projection deletes Z, both other direct readers, and the first direct reader of Z, which cannot be either reader leading to S. Two restorations yield at least two savings. If Z is a clean top, S implies Z; dropping S and an aggregation OR saves two. If both their readers coincide, these compute two equal copies of X AND Y. If dirty, merge them, then extract the resulting shared wire whose readers lead to the two distinct roots. If clean, drop the duplicate and one aggregation OR.

**Application to external shares.** An original dirty factor anywhere in W cannot leak outside H. All roots depending on this factor Z imply it (inside H this follows from W). Substituting true globally in Z's dependent region and restoring Z once across affected roots is exact. For monotone Boolean F depending on Z and possibly correlated underlying raw variables, this assertion uses the stronger syntactic factor property: H has a multiplicative W path through Z, and every other dependent clean root has an AND path through Z. Thus each is identically Z AND its Z=true cofactor, independent of correlations. Each direct reader is AND or the special OR, and both simplify upon substituting true. This forbids multiple readers by original leastness. The same argument forbids branching anywhere downstream in its path to H.

Any genuinely external arm-share in the split has the root pair {R_A,E} or {R_B,E}, where E is an unaffected centreless top. Its dirty ancestors cannot belong to the other arm or the guard. These share pairs are pairwise intersecting and different gates cannot have equal pairs. In particular, if both arms leak then every leakage meets the same one external top, and there are at most two leaking gates. Otherwise the sharing pairs form a star from one arm to distinct external tops. The dirty ancestor cones of these branching products are disjoint: any dirty ancestor shared by two incomparable branch gates must itself branch before they diverge, contrary to the antichain lemma.

The remaining star case cannot be excluded by root-set overlap alone. For k distinct root pairs {R_A,E_i}, every two intersect, neither contains the other, and all sharing products may have disjoint ancestor cones. Applying k separate factorizations to such root pairs would reuse the central output k times. This is a combinatorial obstruction, not a globally least graph example. It leaves the graph-specific charge unresolved.
