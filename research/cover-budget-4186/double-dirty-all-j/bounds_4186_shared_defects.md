# One shared dirty subtree of at least three primitive leaves

Use three projected roots AW, BW, Y, with Z shared solely between A and Y and W shared between the first two projections. Write k for the Z leaf slots, x for A's further slots, z for Y's further slots, b for B's slots, w for W's slots. Assume k≥3,x≥0,z≥1,b≥2,w≥1. The available local allowance is L=k+x+z+b+w+1. Assign residuals exclusively to the three roots, in batches r,s,h respectively. Each batch is an induced matching; n slots require 2^r≤n for a nonempty r-batch. We show that edge cost plus cover additions is at most L.

The charge before cover savings is 2(r+s+h). With m the largest batch, no positive deficit is possible for m≥5 because one root alone has at least 2^m slots and 2^m≥6m. Thus a positive deficit has m≤4, L≤23 and sum of slot counts≤22. Exhausting this finite simplex yields 258 positive-deficit profiles and maximum deficit three.

Apply the following filters to each sharing pair. Denote their individual slot counts by n,n', assigned batch sizes by u,v, and number of common slots by l. Different roots may also use equal raw inputs independently; n+n'−l still upper bounds the number of distinct supports in their union.

1. For u≥2, at most 2(n−2^u) clause slots can fail to be the unique rejecting slot of a designated transversal. A slot rejects at most one of the independent transversals, and each transversal has a rejection; a transversal rejected by m≥2 slots uses m≤2(m−1) such slots. Any unique rejecting slot has support exactly its u omitted endpoints. An extra vertex outside the matching would hit this slot and leave all others hit on the associated transversal, so it would need to neighbor a selected endpoint. The slot's anchor already neighbors that endpoint's omitted mate and the extra vertex, contradicting C4-freeness (anchor equal to the selected endpoint would star-cover the residual). Thus if l exceeds the bound, a shared support of size u covers both batches. It forces v≤u and saves v cover additions. Apply in both directions.
2. Suppose u≥3. All the first batch's leaf supports lie in the neighborhood of a common centre p. Indeed the rejecting clauses on neighboring transversals contain at least two common omitted endpoints, so their anchors coincide; cube connectivity and the fact each clause hits every assigned edge pin down all anchors. If v≥3 too, both centres agree, since any common slot hits all u edges and thus has a unique anchor. Then the entire union of batches is an induced matching inside N(p); the clause count below applies to both in full.
3. Otherwise classify the v batch into v−e edges internal to N(p) and e crossing edges. All hit any shared clause and thus meet N(p). Every crossing edge has endpoint x in N(p) belonging to every shared clause, since its other endpoint is outside N(p). If some such x is outside the u matching's endpoints, it is adjacent to none of them: N(p) induces a matching. Appending x to every u-transversal preserves independence and hits all common slots, forcing at least 2^u private first-root slots. This case can be admitted with no cover saving.
4. Otherwise all e crossing endpoints x belong to the u matching. They are distinct and lie on distinct matching edges: if a pair used both mates then the second batch would have a graph cross edge between distinct assigned edges, contradicting inducedness. Select these x in covering the first batch; this saves e additions on the second. Furthermore at least 2^u−2^(u−e) transversals contain one of the e vertices and hence hit all shared slots; that many distinct private first-root slots are necessary.
5. The u matching together with the v−e internal second-batch edges is an induced matching in N(p), even if other residuals elsewhere intersect. For two nonempty such batches of sizes u,i the union of primitive supports has size at least 2^u+2^i: use a uniformly random global transversal. Each clean root rejects it, and a shared clause hits every designated edge of both batches, so its rejection probability is at most 2^−(u+i). Weight the two roots' rejection requirements by 2^u and 2^i respectively. Each private clause contributes at most one and each shared clause contributes at most (2^u+2^i)/2^(u+i)≤1. If i=0 only 2^u is required.

The arithmetic script enumerates all admissible savings for each pair, takes a conservative minimum over alternatives and maximum over independent pair bounds, and tests whether that single saving suffices. It leaves two profiles, both r=s=h=2, k=3, x=0, z=1, with (b,w)=(2,2) or (3,1). In either case Y has four slots, all of them distinct unique-rejection two-vertex transversals. Three occur in Z, so two of them are disjoint. Any AW edge satisfying both has its endpoints among the four Y matching vertices and hence equals an assigned Y edge by inducedness. This contradicts disjoint assignment. Thus every profile meets its budget. This is a finite integer enumeration plus structural proof, without a Lean verification.
