# Rich central root with arbitrary disjoint shared dirty subtrees

Use central root A=AW, partner B=BW and external roots E_i. External i shares l_i≥2 primitive slots with A and has z_i≥1 private slots. B shares w≥1 guard slots and has b≥2 private slots. Shared slot blocks are disjoint as occurrences; duplicate raw wires on independently paid pins may remain separate. The total allowance is one per slot after identifying the prescribed shared occurrences, plus one per external root, since each k-leaf sharing saves k−1 gates. Assign disjoint edge batches to the roots. Each is an induced matching in the graph.

If A owns r≥3 edges, all its primitive supports are anchored at a common vertex p. N(p) induces a matching plus isolates. Every root shares at least one clause, so every designated edge meets N(p). An edge with both endpoints there is internal; for a crossing edge its inside endpoint belongs to EVERY shared clause of its root. A second rich batch has the same anchor (any common slot has a unique anchor) and is internal-only. Thus every crossing batch has size at most two.

Choose a random independent set inside N(p), selecting uniformly one endpoint per edge and using fair coins on isolates. A and every internal-only nonempty root reject this assignment. Weight each rejection obligation by 2^h for its h owned edges, enough to pay residual count and one covering endpoint per edge. A slot hits all h designated edges so its weighted rejection contribution is at most one. A slot shared by two such roots hits h+h' distinct matching edges and contributes at most (2^h+2^h')/2^(h+h')≤1 total. Empty roots have no obligation. The same argument bounds the fraction of other shared slots used by A as follows.

For a crossing batch of size h, let a≥1 count crossings, and d count those inside endpoints lying on A edges. They lie on distinct matching components by inducedness and avoid the components of its h−a internal edges. Every shared slot meets all r A edges, all h−a internal edges, and contains every crossing inside endpoint, so its weighted charge from A is at most 2^−(h−d). Inconsistent endpoint requirements only make the charge smaller. Thus at least 1−2^−(h−d) per shared slot remains. At least 2^h−2^(h−a) private slots are needed: that many transversals of the root include some inside crossing endpoint, hitting ALL shared slots. Each transversal must miss a clause and each private slot can be missed by at most one.

For a crossing external root with h=1, z_i+1≥2 suffices. If h=a=2, z_i≥3 and the structural +1 suffices. If h=2,a=1, z_i≥2, and z_i+1 pays three; the remaining unit comes from the two or more shared slots each at least half available.

For a crossing partner B the private-slot estimates alone give b≥2 when h=1, b≥3 when h=a=2, and b≥2 when h=2,a=1. Thus its edge-plus-cover cost of 2h never exceeds b+2. The central rich obligation has a surplus of 2^r−2r≥2 beyond its actual cost, paying these two possible extra units without any fictitious charge to the budget. If B is internal-only it was already included in the joint weighted estimate. This closes every partner size and every number or size of external batches. No simultaneous cover-choice optimization is required. No conclusion here concerns a central batch with at most two edges.
