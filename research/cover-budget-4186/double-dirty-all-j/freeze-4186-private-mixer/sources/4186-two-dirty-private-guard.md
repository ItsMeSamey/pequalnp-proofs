# Two dirty arms with a common guard

Assume the sole excluded OR is M=A∨B and both operands have empty OR-reach. All proper pre-top uses of M are ANDs. Factoring any such wire out of the OR of all its dependent top outputs costs one AND and removes its direct AND readers; leastness forces M and all post-M dirty ANDs to have one reader each. Thus exactly one centreless top is affected, H=(A∨B)W, for a conjunction W of primitive raw OR clauses. The arms A,B are conjunctions of primitive clauses. Their dirty ancestries are disjoint: otherwise take a latest common dirty ancestor Z. An arm containing the other would make M redundant. Otherwise both arms are multiples of Z, so Z factors even through M, and all dependent tops factor as Z times their Z=true cofactors. A latest common dirty ancestor then cannot branch toward both arms without contradicting leastness. A primitive OR clause with at least two raw inputs has one ordinary-star anchor whose star edges connect it to every clause vertex.

## Projection and sharing

Replacing the M-dependent cone by its M=true cofactor realizes W, removing M and at least one direct AND reader. W cannot be clean, since then the same deletion letting W replace H preserves the graph output. Create AW and BW, and aggregate the one extra output. This constructs a circuit with at most one more gate than least size, in which all pre-top conjunctions use pure raw clauses. This statement concerns size and the resulting wiring; it does not assume the competitor is least. Every dirty AND in the split circuit has at most two distinct AND readers by the same factor argument. Any two such branching dirty gates have overlapping sets of reachable tops: otherwise their downstream AND cones are disjoint, and both one-gate savings can be carried out with a common reordered output aggregation.

## Private cone bound

Suppose the dirty ANDs of the ordinary q=0 portions can be charged to external centreless tops without overlapping either arm or guard charges, and the arms and guard are distinct private trees with a,b,w primitive leaves respectively (a,b≥2, w≥1). Pay the unaffected roots by their existing private q=0 charges. The merged root has remaining allowance a+b+w: it uses a+b+w−3 dirty ANDs, one centreless allocation of 2 and the exceptional OR allocation of 1.

Assign outstanding residuals exclusively to the two projected roots, r and s respectively. Each batch forms an induced matching by the established anchored clause lemma, so 2^r≤a+w and 2^s≤b+w. The bound 2r+2s≤a+b+w follows arithmetically except for the triples (a,b;w), up to exchanging arms,
(3,3;1), (2,2;2), (2,3;2), (2,2;3), (3,3;5), (2,2;6), (2,3;6), (2,2;7).
For example the short finite arithmetic check can be bounded from above using increments of the floor-log function: increasing a or b by 2 increases its twice-floor-log by at most 2 once the sum with w is at least 2. For w≥8, increasing w by 4 increases each twice-floor-log by at most 2, so only four starting w need be considered for this tail. To retain all exact exceptional triples, check a,b=2..7 and separately use the same monotonicity from a≥6 or b≥6.

In the last four triples both branches need three assigned edges to exceed the allowance. For an induced matching of size at least three, all endpoint-choice complements occur in anchored clauses: each independent choice is rejected, and its missing clause must contain the opposite selected endpoint of every edge. Two choices differing at one position have at least two common complementary endpoints; hence their anchors coincide by four-cycle-freeness. Connectivity of the cube gives one center for all endpoints. Every guard clause meets every edge of both batches; at least two of its vertices from each matching pin its anchor to that matching's common center. Both batches therefore lie in the same open neighborhood. The graph induced by that neighborhood has maximum degree one by four-cycle-freeness, so the entire assigned set is an induced matching.

On an induced union of r+s disjoint residual edges, the clean merged root (A∨B)W rejects all 2^(r+s) endpoint selections. A rejected assignment either misses one of w guard clauses, or misses both an A clause and a B clause. Every guard clause hits each assigned pair. An A/B clause union hits each assigned pair too. Thus each clause or union rejects at most one endpoint selection, giving 2^(r+s)≤w+ab. This excludes all four large-w exceptions. The same bound excludes all remaining cases whenever the four residual edges form an induced matching.

Remaining private obstructions require four residual edges, two per projected root, with w≤3, and a union that fails to be an induced matching. If two assigned edges share a vertex, their joint endpoint-cover charge saves one; the deficit can reach two only at (2,2;2). For unrestricted external dirty-wire sharing, the projection above only shows proximity to leastness, not a disjoint private charge partition.
