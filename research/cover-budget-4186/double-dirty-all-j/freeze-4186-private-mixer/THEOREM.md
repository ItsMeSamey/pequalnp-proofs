# Private two-dirty-arm exclusion for L-meta-4186

**Statement.** Take n,G,C under all four hypotheses of the positive statement inside the issued negation (no four-cycle, nonempty edge set, least binary AO circuit, top form). Use t for ordinary tops, c for centreless tops, j for junk AND gates and u for the number of residual graph edges Ustar. Assume exactly one dirty OR outside Lambda. It is M=A∨B with both pins reading dirty ANDs. Assume the private cone decomposition below. Then there is a vertex cover K with |K|+u ≤ t+2c+j+1, for arbitrary j. This excludes counterexamples within this slice of the issued negation.

**Private decomposition.** The affected centreless top computes H=(A∨B)W. Count primitive clauses at the boundary of the binary conjunction trees, with multiplicity of leaf positions. The two arms consist of private binary conjunction trees with respectively a,b≥2 primitive leaves. At H the common guard consists of w≥1 such leaves: reassociation of its AND cone places M as one further leaf. Internal dirty gates of the two arms and the guard region are pairwise disjoint, and disjoint from the charges of the unaffected roots. Equivalently for this accounting, require a+b+w−3 distinct dirty gates locally and disjoint budgets d_i for the other centreless roots with at most d_i+2 pure primitive leaves per root. Each primitive is a nonempty raw disjunction, either a singleton or contained in the star-edge neighborhood of its anchor. Pure clauses can be reused. The cover construction assigns each residual edge exclusively to an accepting projected or unaffected root and can choose either endpoint of each externally owned residual. No restriction on graph order or overlap of accepted edge sets is needed.

**Reduction.** Dead gates contradict leastness by deletion. The clean output is the OR of all clean AND tops: recursively expand its clean ORs, whose operands must be clean. Raw input wires fail cleanliness by singletons, and TopForm forbids clean ANDs under further ANDs. A dirty AND read directly by an OR forces that OR outside Lambda, since its AND pin has no raw OR reach. M itself has empty raw reach; any OR reader of M would be a second dirty OR outside Lambda. Thus all pre-top paths after M use only ANDs. For any dirty wire on these paths including M, replace it by symbolic true in its downstream ANDs through the first clean tops. Every distinct direct reader disappears. Each affected top was the original wire AND its projected cofactor; a true cofactor is impossible by dirtiness. Aggregate projected tops, AND the result once with the preserved wire, and aggregate unaffected tops. The clean OR aggregation needs no more gates than before (m tops require at least m−1 binary ORs). Two distinct readers would save a gate. Hence M reaches exactly one first clean top, which is centreless; conjunction associativity gives H above. The empty guard would make H=M dirty. Every ordinary top has two pure sides: no OR with raw reach can contain M, and all other dirty AND/OR boundaries are excluded. Its cross edges form a star: sides are disjoint by singleton rejection and two vertices on each side would create a four-cycle. One chosen center covers that top. A pure nonsingleton primitive belongs to Lambda, delivering its ordinary star anchor. Literal private-tree counts spend (a−1)+(b−1)+(w−1) dirty gates, including the w−1 ANDs below the clean top in the guard region. Splitting H into AW and BW is a logical expansion; no leastness of a split competitor is needed.

**Local accounting.** Every residual pair activates a centreless top. Each projected or unaffected conjunction is clean and every clause hits each of its accepted residual pairs. These pairs form an induced matching. If xy and xz both activate the conjunction, a clause missing x must contain y and z. Its anchor must then be x by four-cycle freeness, making these star edges. If disjoint pairs xx′ and yy′ have cross edge xy, no clause can contain x′,y (its anchor must be x), nor x,y′ (anchor y). Each clause therefore hits both cross pairs xy′ and x′y, making both graph edges and giving a four-cycle with the original pairs. For k>0 owned edges, all 2^k endpoint transversals are independent. Each misses a clause, and a clause can be missed by at most one transversal. Thus 2k≤2^k≤the leaf count. Empty batches require zero charge. For each unaffected root charge 2 per owned edge, at most d_i+2, and retain free endpoint choice. If the affected batches have r,s edges then r≤floor(log2(a+w)), s≤floor(log2(b+w)); these upper bounds also hold for empty batches. The affected budget after the exceptional OR and centreless-top credits is a+b+w.

**Exceptional counts.** Twice the sum of the two log bounds is at most a+b+w except for the eight triples in profile_receipt.txt, up to arm exchange. The finite checker uses the fact that increasing an arm count by 2 increases twice its floor log by at most 2. Increasing w≥8 by 4 increases each twice-floor-log by at most 2. Reduction to base cases is therefore valid. In the four profiles with w≥5 the budget can only fail when r=s=3. Every choice of one endpoint per three owned edges is rejected by a clause containing their other endpoints. Its anchor is unique. Orientations differing in one position have two common opposite endpoints and thus the same anchor. Consequently all six endpoints lie in one neighborhood. Any guard clause hits all three edges, pinning its anchor to this center; the same guard pins the other batch to that center. Inside a common neighborhood the graph has degree at most one, hence the combined six distinct edges are an induced matching. On such a union, every rejecting orientation misses a guard clause or simultaneously an A clause and a B clause. Each guard or union of one clause from each arm hits all assigned pairs, so rejects at most one orientation. Thus 64≤w+ab, impossible in these profiles.

The four smaller profiles can only fail with r=s=2. For a projection with exactly four clauses, each independent transversal of its two edges misses a distinct clause. Each clause contains precisely the opposite two endpoints among the four. If the clause for endpoints x,y contains an outsider z, the opposite transversal plus z now hits every clause. Its only possible graph edge is z to an opposite endpoint. Such adjacency yields a four-cycle using that endpoint's mate, the clause anchor, and z; residual status excludes the anchor being either opposite endpoint. Thus every tight clause is exactly its specified two-set.

* At (2,2;2) and (2,3;2) the tight first projection has two distinct guard two-sets. Both hit both second-batch edges; hence at least three endpoints belong to both induced two-edge matchings. Three shared endpoints contain an entire common edge, contrary to exclusive ownership.
* At (3,3;1) the single tight guard has two vertices covering all four assigned edges, so the edge-plus-cover charge is at most six.
* At (2,2;3) the budget is seven. If two marked edges meet, choose their shared endpoint and save one. If they form a globally induced four-edge matching the orientation argument gives 16≤3+4, impossible. Otherwise some cross edge joins disjoint marked edges (within-batch crosses already impossible). If this edge belongs to an ordinary star, its selected center is one of the two endpoints; choose that endpoint for its marked edge as well. If residual it was exclusively assigned elsewhere, to an unaffected root, since the two affected batches are fully enumerated. Choose a common endpoint for it and the incident marked edge. This global overlap saves one without changing external per-edge budgets.

Combining the ordinary centers, the unaffected choices and the affected choices gives the stated bound. Shared dirty nodes that defeat the private charge partition are outside this result. The separate one-pure-arm c=1 theorem is not used here.
