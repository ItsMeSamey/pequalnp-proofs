One-junk 3-clause incidence classification. Write the universal residual term as (OR A) AND (OR B) AND (OR H), below the graph function. Its true pairs include all residuals, though some may also be ordinary star edges. Label every vertex by membership in the three supports. The full type 111 is empty by rejection on singletons. Let D_i be vertices of doubleton type missing clause i, L_i of singleton type i, and N_i = L_i union the other two D classes. A pair satisfies the term precisely when it is in D_i x N_i for some i, taking unordered pairs. Type 000 plays no role. Each product is a complete bipartite subgraph with disjoint sides. If both sides are nonempty, at least one has size one by C4-freeness.

With zero nonempty D classes there are no accepted pairs; with one the accepted pairs form a star or are empty. With two nonempty classes D_i,D_j, if either has size >=2 its opposite N_i must be the singleton d_j. Then L_i is empty, and every pair contains d_j. If both are singletons, they are adjacent, with pendant leaves L_i,L_j, giving a double star. With three nonempty classes each D_i is a singleton d_i since N_i already has size >=2. These three centers form a triangle, with pendant leaves L_i at d_i in the accepted-pair graph. All three leaf groups cannot be nonempty: selecting one l_i per group satisfies the full term, hence there is a graph edge l_i l_j among them. That edge, together with l_i d_i, d_i d_j, d_j l_j, contradicts C4-freeness. Thus the triangle has pendant leaves at at most two of its centers. Selecting these centers, and padding within the triangle to two where necessary, covers all accepted pairs.

Consequently the residual graph has a vertex cover of size at most two, and tau(G)<=t+2. This uses the full-cube implication of the three-clause term for the triple of singleton representatives. Pair-only acceptance would permit a triangle with leaves at every center. A single star of arbitrary size can occur for a three-clause term below a C4-free graph, so the incidence argument alone gives no absolute bound on accepted-pair cardinality.
