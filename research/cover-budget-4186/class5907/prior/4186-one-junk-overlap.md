# One dirty AND with overlapping raw clauses

Let J=(OR A) AND (OR B), let Z=A∩B be nonempty, and let H be the union of affected tops' pure guard supports. Ordinary tops contribute t star centres. Affected tops are J OR raw leaves on one side and a pure guard T on the other; an edge in U must meet H and activate J. Write q for the outside ORs excluded from Lambda. No pure OR gate has singleton support in a least circuit: it could be bypassed to that raw input. The sets A,B are incomparable by inclusion, since otherwise J could be bypassed. In particular both have at least two elements.

A charging fact used below is that a pure Lambda OR below an output with support V contained in N(v) cannot contain a residual neighbor w of v together with any other vertex in V. Its Lambda centre also neighbors both, and would coincide with v by C4-freeness, making vw covered. Without requiring V contained in N(v), a Lambda gate still cannot contain two distinct residual neighbors of v. Thus a raw OR cone containing k residual neighbors needs at least k−1 charged pure ORs, and if V is contained in N(v) and contains an additional vertex then it needs at least k. For multiple outputs covering k distinct residual tokens with m roots the first bound becomes k−m. For a direct counting proof, truncate the backwards cones at Lambda gates and at inputs, discard branches carrying none of the designated tokens, and count binary mergers. A terminal supplies at most one distinct token, and a binary DAG with m output components and k distinct terminals requires at least k−m internal gates. In the strengthened version designate one additional V-vertex as a token.

Z is disjoint from H by singleton rejection and complete to H by activating J on singleton z and a top by its guard h. If H is empty, U is empty. If H={h}, each residual is h-w. When h belongs to A, all w belong to B; symmetrically for h in B. If h belongs to neither then w belongs to Z. A single raw clause cone thus contains all u residual neighbors; q≥u−1 when u>0. If c=0 select h as the centre of an affected ordinary top. Otherwise add h to the t centres. The available vertex allowance beyond t is 2c+1+q−u≥2c.

Suppose now |H|≥2. C4-freeness gives Z={z}. Also A∩H has at most one member x since every such member neighbors all of B, which has at least two elements. Similarly B∩H has at most one member y. Every ordinary affected top has singleton guard: if |T|≥2 then every raw mixed-side leaf neighbors all T, forcing it to equal z, since z also neighbors all T. In that event the mixed operand computes J OR z = J, and its last OR is bypassable. Distinct ordinary guard singletons require distinct first OR mixers directly reading J: if two guards shared such a mixer, its raw support would be contained in {z} for the same reason, making it redundant. The raw support of a first direct mixer can be taken nonempty since a wire with J and no raw supports computes J and is redundant.

For Rz residuals of the form z-h, choose one guard for each h. If m distinct guards are selected, at most c are centreless, and the others demand at least m−c distinct charged mixers. The union of their pure cones costs at least Rz−m charged pure ORs. Together with c, these costs cover Rz, including the case Rz=0.

Every remaining residual belongs to x-(B without z) or y-(A without z). For the former family, all of B neighbors x, and z is an additional support token. Therefore the pure B cone pays its cardinality in full. The A cone similarly pays the latter family, even if an edge xy is counted in both families. These two raw cones and the union of the guards have pairwise disjoint gate sets: their underlying supports intersect in at most one vertex in each case, and a least circuit has no singleton-support pure OR. They also exclude the direct mixers. This proves 2c+1+q−u≥c+1, with a further unit when xy is residual.

For c=0 all affected guards are singleton centres, giving tau≤t. For c≥1, the general one-junk incidence classification gives tau≤t+2, which fits c+1. Alternatively cover remaining residuals by adding z, x and y when present. This needs at most three additions; for c=1 with both x,y present, xy is an edge. If residual, the double count gives the extra unit. Otherwise its star centre is x or y, already covered, giving at most two additions. Consequently all overlapping one-junk cases satisfy the proposed inequality.
