# Common ordinary guard and two raw mixer vertices

Use the physical private-D hypotheses of 4186-common-guard-large-support.md with P={p,q}. Every mixed pin has raw support S between P and the ordinary-star neighborhood N(a), where a is the unique ordinary guard. Define eligible vertices to lie outside P and neighbor either p or q. A vertex other than a neighbors at most one of p,q. If K (the pure boundary factors of a mixed centreless top) accepts an independent input away from a, that input avoids P and has an eligible vertex of each label: otherwise adding a missed p or q would keep it independent and activate the top. K therefore rejects independent singleton inputs away from a. All K clauses are singleton or star-anchored.

Choose the ordinary star centers including a, and assign residuals exclusively. Give any available pure unaffected witnesses their edges first. At most one residual incident to a remains: it must satisfy D on its pair, because any raw activation would involve an ordinary star edge at a. A clause of D missing a must contain every such mate; two distinct mates would force its anchor to be a and both edges to be star-covered. This residual costs only one additional edge unit.

For each mixed top its remaining assigned edges avoiding a form an ambient induced matching. Two meeting residuals would force the anchor of a K clause missing the common endpoint to star-cover them. If xy,uv are disjoint assigned edges with cross xu, every K clause traces either xu or yv (a clause containing x,v or y,u would star-cover a residual by anchor uniqueness). A second cross is impossible, either by C4 or incompatible clause traces. The two independent pairs xv and yu both satisfy K. Both pairs thus avoid P and each of their vertices has a P-neighbor. If an endpoint w lay in N(a), its assigned mate z would have a neighbor p' in P distinct from w; a,w,z,p' form a C4. Hence all four lie outside N(a), so both assigned edges satisfy D. The anchored clauses of D obey the same trace constraint, making the clean conjunction DK true on the independent opposite pairs, a contradiction.

Separate all these mixed assignments into far residuals with both endpoints outside N[a] and near residuals meeting N(a). All far residuals satisfy D. The restriction of D to outside N[a] is clean since aD is clean: adjoin a to any independent input there. Restricted clauses which remain nonsingleton retain their original star anchors. The anchored conjunction argument makes the far batch an induced matching and has 2^f<=d+1 primitive slots for f>0 far edges (d dirty ANDs in D's cone). Thus 2f<=d+1.

For a near matching at one root, each edge has at most one eligible endpoint. A near endpoint w outside P prevents its mate from neighboring either p or q by C4, and if w is itself in P it is ineligible. Moreover at most one eligible endpoint per label occurs throughout this batch. For label p it is either the unique possible neighbor of p inside N(a), or it is outside N(a) with mate p; those two edges could not coexist in an induced matching because p and its N(a)-neighbor give a cross. Hence any K-positive orientation must include two specified eligible endpoints, or none is positive. For r>=2, at least 3*2^(r-2) orientations are rejected and each clause accounts for at most one. For r>=3 this is >=2r. For r=1 both singletons are rejected. If r=2 the bound is three; physical mixed-pin count contributes at least one additional slot beyond the k pure clauses and always pays 2r. For r=0 a mixed top has at least one mixed pin and a nonempty pure conjunction. Therefore each mixed root has at least one unit of slack except possibly r=2, k=3, and exactly one mixed pin.

If f=0 the d+1 budget is enough for the a-incident charge. If f>0 and this charge is needed, choose a mixed top owning a far edge uv. Local slack there finishes the accounting unless it is the tight two-near-edge case. An endpoint or ambient crossing between these edges saves a global cover vertex: for an exterior residual cross use the same endpoint to cover it and a marked edge, and for a star cross choose its center as that marked edge's endpoint. In fact the earlier per-root inducedness already precludes all these crossings within this top. In the tight case exactly one of its four near orientations is K-positive, with one eligible vertex per label. Let I select both ineligible endpoints. Exactly one of the three K clauses misses I (each of the three rejected near orientations needs its own distinct clause). Both I union {u} and I union {v} are independent and must still be K-negative: if I meets P that is immediate, and otherwise I has no P-neighbors while the additional endpoint can have at most one. The same unique clause missing I must therefore omit both u and v, contradicting K(uv). This proves the required one extra unit.

Sum with the unaffected q=0 charges, using the existing disjoint-boundary accounting under private D. It gives the proposed inequality for all j with |P|=2 in this slice. With leaks the local and orientation results persist; this note does not provide disjoint physical charges for leaked dirty ancestors.
