# Single pure-sided mixer with a common ordinary guard

Assume the sole exceptional OR is M=D∨P, D a dirty product and P a pure raw union with at least three vertices. Write a for the ordinary singleton guard witnessing the mixed lambda ORs, S* for the largest raw support in that ordinary leaf, and assume the bounds P⊆S_i⊆S*⊆N(a) for each mixed pin D∨S_i. The ordinary top a(D∨S*) accepts aD. Every ordinary affected top has guard a, since its guard vertex must neighbor all of P, and two distinct vertices have at most one common neighbor. A mixed centreless top H factors as K times a product of D∨S_i, where K is a conjunction of pure primitive clauses (unfolding all AND factors). The primitive clauses of K and D of size at least two are lambda-anchored by ordinary star edges. K cannot be the empty conjunction since the top would dominate D.

**Edges at a.** Any ordinary projected a-edge is a star edge. Any residual containing a that activates a mixed top must therefore have D=true, unless its other endpoint is in a raw S_i, which again makes it star. Since aD is clean, D has a clause missing a. This clause contains every mate of an a-incident residual on which D=true. If it held two such mates its anchor would equal a by C4, making those edges star. Thus at most one such edge needs this special handling, using one q token for its edge count, and a is already in the ordinary cover. Edges admitted solely by unaffected pure centreless roots may instead be charged there.

**Independent tests.** If K accepts an independent set I avoiding a, every p in P must have a neighbor in I: otherwise adjoin p and every mixed pin is true, violating cleanliness. Every vertex other than a neighbors at most one p by C4. Hence K rejects independent sets of size less than |P| away from a. In particular it rejects singleton and independent pair tests. Let r be any exclusive batch of residual edges away from a accepted by H. Every clause of K meets every batch edge. These batch edges form an induced matching: shared vertex x would require a clause omitting x containing both mates, whose anchor equals x and star-covers both. A cross edge xy between distinct batch edges xx′,yy′ forbids any clause from containing x′,y or x,y′, again by C4 and the star anchors. Then the two opposite selected pairs activate K; pair rejection forces both to be edges, violating C4.

**One center.** For |P|≥4 and r≥3, take any three batch edges. All eight endpoint orientations are independent and K rejects them. Their missing clauses contain the three complementary endpoints. Adjacent cube patterns share two endpoints, hence all these clauses have one center s, and all six endpoints are in N(s). Every remaining K clause meets each of those edges and therefore has the same center. Every endpoint of any other batch edge must be in N(s) as well, by applying the same three-edge argument to that edge and two already considered.

For |P|=3, r≥4, consider the set of centers of all K clauses. For two centers s,t their neighborhoods can share at most one vertex, so on at least r−1 batch edges they necessarily take fixed opposite endpoints. A third center would choose endpoints on at least three of these fixed edges, sharing two with s or t; impossible. With only two centers, choosing the s-end of one fixed edge and the t-end of another hits all K clauses on an independent pair, impossible. Thus all clauses have a single center. In this and the next paragraph, when all K clauses have one center, each endpoint of every batch edge lies in its neighborhood: if only one mate lay there, all clauses would contain it and K would accept a singleton.

**The three-edge exception at |P|=3.** Suppose r=3 and centers are not homogeneous. Two centers s,t must share a vertex on one matching edge, or else they take fixed opposite endpoints on all three, precluding a third center just as above. On the other two edges their supports choose fixed patterns 00 and 11. The four independent pair tests on these two edges require all four complementary patterns; clauses of patterns 01 and 10 have new centers. Each of those centers already intersects both s and t at one of the two fixed-edge endpoints. Thus neither can see the shared vertex of the third edge and both must see its mate. In turn neither s nor t can see that mate. There is only one center per two-edge pattern because two common neighbors determine its uniqueness. Every K clause therefore uses exactly one of the four same-parity triples on the three edges. Four opposite endpoint orientations activate K (the complements of the other four clause patterns). Each positive orientation must dominate all three elements of P, so its three vertices have three different P-neighbors. Every pair of endpoint choices from different matching edges occurs in one of the four positive orientations. Thus each edge's two endpoints have the same P-neighbor, different for different edges. None of these endpoints lies in N(a): if x∈N(a) and x and its mate y neighbor p∈P, then a and y are distinct common neighbors of x and p. Neither x nor y equals a. Hence none of these three edges activates any raw mixed-pin support; all must have D=true since H accepts them. Every primitive clause of D meets all three edges. Its choices on the two fixed edges pin its center to the corresponding K center, and its choice on the third is then fixed by that center. D must accept the same four independent orientations as K. This contradicts cleanliness of DK. Nonhomogeneity is therefore impossible.

**Cost.** For r=1,2 there are at least 2^r≥2r primitive K clauses, by rejection of all r-vertex transversals. For r≥3 with common center s, each p∈P other than s has at most one neighbor in N(s) by C4. A K-positive orientation must select that vertex for each such p. These vertices are distinct (none is a) and unless the requirements are incompatible they fix different edge choices. Thus at most one fourth of the 2^r orientations is K-positive. Every K clause hits all batch edges and can reject at most one orientation. There are at least 3·2^(r−2)≥2r primitive clauses. This counts both raw and D contributions without splitting or duplicating shared guards.

**Scope of summation.** If D's dirty ancestor cone has no outside leakage, all dirty factors belonging to the K cones outside D have a single direct AND reader, by factoring a shared dirty product across its dependent top outputs. Thus distinct tops have disjoint dirty K ancestry. A top with k K slots and at least one mixed pin has at least k−1 dirty AND gates below its clean root, so its j plus 2c allocation pays k. Pure unaffected tops receive the usual q=0 allocation. Ordinary centers and one endpoint per remaining residual produce the desired cover charge; the at-most-one exceptional a-edge uses q=1 alone. With arbitrary leakage the local primitive-clause results still apply, but adding their charges needs a distinct-node argument.

At |P|=2, independent singleton rejection still excludes incident residuals. Independent pairs may be accepted. Any cross edge between two batch matching edges cannot occur if both are D-positive: the two fixed clause pattern classes also constrain D, and D and K would together accept an independent opposite transversal. The case with a raw-only batch edge is not settled here.
