# Direct eight-gate lower bound for the six-vertex net

A singleton-rejecting nonconstant graph function computed by ORs and at most one AND must be a single product of two raw disjunctions. To see this, expand OR cones: any raw disjunct at the final output would accept its singleton; the one AND, if present, has pure OR operands. Where there are fixed constants, false disjuncts can be erased, true disjuncts make their OR constant, and a constant operand in a product makes it zero or a pure disjunction. Thus the same conclusion holds for a nonzero graph function after fixing any input constants. For a product with raw supports A,B, singleton rejection makes them disjoint, and its edges form exactly their complete bipartite cross product. In a four-cycle-free graph this is a star (or empty).

In particular the triangle needs at least two AND gates. Its three-variable Boolean function is majority and is self-dual: taking the complement after complementing all inputs gives the same function. By De Morgan duality swapping AND and OR in the whole circuit preserves this particular function. Thus at least two ORs are needed as well. A four-gate implementation exists, for example xy OR [z AND (x OR y)]. Every size-four triangle circuit therefore has exactly two ANDs and two ORs.

Suppose the six-vertex net had a circuit of at most seven gates. Prune dead gates, then successively set its three pendant inputs to false using the restriction lemma from net-proof.md, pruning any new dead gates as desired. Each deletion loses at least one gate, and the remaining triangle requires four. Therefore the starting size is exactly seven and each deletion loses exactly one gate. This deleted gate must be an OR: a live direct AND reader of the fixed input becomes identically false and cannot itself be the output, since the restricted function is nonconstant. Thus at least one subsequent direct reader would simplify away as well. Every gate left by the projection retains its type. The purported net circuit consequently has the same two AND gates as the four-gate triangle.

It remains to show no circuit with at most two AND gates (and any number of ORs) computes the net. Its vertex cover number is three by its three disjoint pendant edges and the cover of all triangle vertices. One AND is already impossible. With two AND gates, the earlier one is D=A AND B for pure disjunctions A,B. The other has the form (P OR δD) AND (Q OR εD), where P,Q are raw disjunctions, possibly empty, and the flags indicate whether a copy of D is present. This is obtained by expanding each OR backward up to an input or an AND. The output OR closure contains no raw variable by singleton rejection and so selects from the two ANDs. If D alone reaches the output the one-AND case applies. If D is a final disjunct in addition to the second gate, absorption gives D OR (P AND Q). If D is included in both second-gate operands, distributivity gives the same form. If no second-gate operand includes D, only one or two products occur. In all these cases the output is a union of at most two pure products, each clean and therefore each a star, contradicting the three disjoint pendant edges.

The only remaining output is (D OR P) AND Q, after possibly exchanging the second gate's operands. If P is empty, this is a conjunction of three raw clauses A,B,Q. Each clause hits each of the three pendant edges. The assignment consisting of all three pendant vertices, and the three assignments obtained by substituting just one corresponding triangle center for its pendant, are four independent assignments. Each must miss a clause. No clause can be missed by two different assignments: on a pair where those assignments make opposite choices, that clause hits at least one of the two endpoints. Three clauses cannot suffice.

If P is nonempty, Q's support meets every graph edge and so has at least three vertices. P AND Q implies the net function, forcing their supports disjoint and complete to each other. Every p in P must neighbor every vertex of Q. The net's maximum degree is three, attained only by its triangle vertices, and no two vertices have three common neighbors. Thus P consists of a single triangle vertex s and Q is exactly its three neighbors. Set s=false and its unique pendant=true. P is false and Q is true. The graph restricted to the other four vertices is a four-vertex path, whereas the circuit is just D with constants substituted in its two pure factors. The path's two disjoint end edges and lack of a four-cycle exclude a single biclique. This contradiction proves the eight-gate lower bound in the unrestricted exact binary AO2 model.
