# The net with pendant classes attains the full excluded-OR allowance

Take distinct triangle vertices a,b,h and pairwise-disjoint nonempty sets P,D,Z of sizes p,d,z. Each P vertex has sole neighbor a, each D vertex has sole neighbor b, and each Z vertex has sole neighbor h. There are no further edges. This graph is four-cycle-free: every pendant has degree one and the only cycle on the three remaining vertices is their triangle.

Use these circuit wires, where a disjunction is an exact binary OR tree on the displayed distinct wires:
- E = b OR every vertex of P, and B = a OR every vertex of D.
- K = a OR b, J = E AND B, and H = J AND K.
- S = K OR every vertex of Z, O = h AND S, and the output is H OR O.

The K wire is computed once and reused. In the six-input labeling from the earlier fixture, a=1, b=2, h=4, P={0}, D={3}, Z={5}. In the replay code a,b,h are 0,1,2 followed by contiguous P,D,Z input ranges; this is the same graph up to renaming. The gate count is p+d+z+5: p and d ORs build E,B, one builds K, z build S, one aggregates, and three gates are AND. Expanding H by distributivity and absorption gives the OR of ab, all aP edges, and all bD edges. In particular every apparent P-D monomial is absorbed after multiplication by K. O contributes ha,hb and hZ. The output computes the graph on the full Boolean cube.

## Exact lower bound for unrestricted AO2

**Restriction lemma.** Suppose an exact binary earlier-wire AO circuit on n inputs computes a function depending essentially on input v. Suppose the restriction v=false is nonconstant. Then there is an AO2 circuit on n−1 inputs computing the restricted function with at least one fewer gate. This applies to arbitrary gate order, sharing, duplicate operands, dead gates, and arbitrary natural output index as allowed in the root circuit model.

Map v to a symbolic false marker and each other input i to i if i<v and i−1 if i>v. Traverse all original gates in their given order, recording for each old wire either the false marker or one new wire index. For an AND with a false argument record false without creating a gate. For an OR with one false argument record the other argument (including false when both are false). In all other cases append the same binary operation on the two mapped indices and record its new earlier-wire index. Induction preserves each wire's restricted Boolean function. At least one gate directly reads v: otherwise no later gate depends on v, and only output v itself could depend on it; such an output would restrict to the constant false. Every direct reader of v is omitted. A missing original output denotes false and is excluded by nonconstancy. A valid original output maps to the restricted function and cannot be the marker; it need not be a gate, because returning a mapped raw input wire is allowed. No physical constant gates, unary gates or extra output gates are required. Thus size drops by at least one, irrespective of other simplifications.

**Six-input base.** The net needs eight gates. The [direct lower-bound proof](analytic-base.md) derives this without enumeration: a seven-gate net would collapse to a four-gate triangle with the same two AND gates, while no two-AND circuit computes the net. The following independent finite check corroborates that lower bound.

Each of the six net inputs is essential: a vertex and any of its neighbors together activate an edge while the neighbor alone does not. On six essential variables a binary circuit needs at least five gates. In its output cone, the underlying connected graph has at least (number of gates)+6−1 links and at most twice as many links as gates. The canonical mask of the labeled net is 691 under zero-based lexicographic pair positions and minimization over input permutations.

The variable labels are named in order of first occurrence; the graph test explicitly includes every labeling and canonicalizes afterward. This lower bound is computational and is not a Lean certificate.

**Lift.** In an arbitrary circuit for the larger net repeatedly fix all but one member of each class to false. Every vertex being deleted is still essential at its deletion: turn on just that pendant and its triangle neighbor, then turn the pendant off. The restricted graph retains the entire triangle and at least one vertex of each class, so is nonconstant after each step. There are exactly p+d+z−3 restrictions, leaving the six-vertex net up to a permutation. Any original circuit has size at least 8+(p+d+z−3)=p+d+z+5. The displayed circuit is therefore globally least among AO2 circuits; this lower bound assumes no top form of the competitor.

## TopForm and sharpness

The sole junk AND is J: one P vertex and one D vertex activate it on an independent input. The clean tops are H and O, and neither has any AND descendant. H is centreless, since its J operand has empty OR reach; its other operand has raw support {a,b}. O is ordinary, with sides {h} and {a,b}∪Z. Its cross edges are exactly ha,hb and hZ. Thus Ustar consists of ab, all aP edges and all bD edges.

Every pure OR in E,B,K,S is outside F because it accepts a singleton. The final aggregation is below F. Lambda contains the entire S cone, including K. Each OR there has two raw-reaching operands and lies in the OR closure of O's leaf side opposite {h}. No OR from E or B can occur there. H witnesses no additional Lambda gate: its J operand has empty raw closure, while the other operand has a two-member side. Consequently q=p+d, j=1, ordinary count t=1, centreless count c=1, and u=p+d+1. The three core vertices cover the graph, and choosing one pendant edge in each class gives three vertex-disjoint edges, so τ=3. Both sides of the requested inequality equal p+d+4. In particular q can be arbitrarily large, and dropping a single unit from its allowance makes the inequality false on this globally least family.

For p=2k, k≥1, take E=(b OR ⋁L) OR (⋁R) for disjoint classes L,R of size k. Replacing E by its left child loses precisely the k edges aR at two-true input assignments. Replacing it by its right child loses aL, ab and all bD edges, totaling k+1+d. Both replacements thus lose at least k original residual edges even when the better child is selected. This compares evaluations of the split expressions on marked pairs; a projected expression need not itself compute a graph function on all assignments. If both branches are retained with their shared factors, the separate gate and budget accounting is a different question.
