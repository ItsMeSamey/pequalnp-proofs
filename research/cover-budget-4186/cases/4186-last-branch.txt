Graph G nonempty C4-free, exact least AO2 circuit C computing its edge function in TopForm. t counts noncentreless tops, c centreless, j junk AND, q outside ORs not in lambda, u residual graph edges. In this note c=0,j=q=1,u=2. Aim tau<=t.

Every gate reaches output (dead-gate deletion). Expand output through clean ORs to see it is an OR of all clean AND tops. A raw vertex is never below F (singleton); clean OR has clean children. No top's operands have clean AND ancestors (TopForm). The sole dirty AND J is (OR A) AND (OR B), for nonempty sets A,B of raw vertices: before J there are only pure ORs in its operand cones. Each top operand is a disjunction of raw leaves and possibly J. Both operands cannot include J, since J is true at a false-F input. Call a top with J in one operand affected, and call its other pure operand's support T its guard. All affected mixed operands contain raw leaves, by c=0. In each there is an OR-only path from J to the operand with a first OR directly reading J. Every such first OR is outside F and not lambda, since the J pin has empty orIn. Therefore they are the same unique charged OR M. Its other operand has nonempty raw support S: if empty it is J, or an OR with J antecedent (which would be an earlier charged gate); in either event M duplicates J or must later be fed into a second charged OR before reaching any raw leaf. In the case M=J OR J, deletion contradicts leastness. Hence M=J OR (OR S), S nonempty, and S is contained in each mixed operand's raw leaves.

Each noncentreless top gives a nonempty complete bipartite star: disjoint side supports by singleton rejection, both cannot have size>=2 by C4. Choose one center from a singleton side, preferring the pure guard whenever singleton on an affected top. Their union covers all star edges and has <=t vertices. Suppose a residual edge e is still missed. It activates an affected top (otherwise it is a star edge); J true and an endpoint lies in its guard T. Since e missed the chosen center, |T|>=2 and the mixed raw support is exactly {s}, hence S={s} globally. All affected guard vertices neighbor s, through each such top's raw cross pairs, and none equals s.

If A and B intersect at z, J is true on singleton z. The above top then forces z adjacent to every member of T (if z in T that singleton already activates the clean top). Thus C4 gives z=s, because s also neighbors all T. Since J is monotone, x_s implies J, so M=J OR x_s computes J. Delete redundant M and redirect to earlier J, contradicting leastness. Hence A,B disjoint.
Consequently e joins some h in T and A to b in B, or with A,B interchanged. Since h in T and A, h neighbors every b' in B by activating the top. If |B|>=2, the pure OR root producing B is outside F (true on a singleton), differs from the mixer M, so is in lambda. Its lambda witness is a top with singleton opposite side {v}; the entire B lies in that top's other raw side. Thus v also neighbors all B, and C4 forces v=h. This puts hb in a top's cross edges, contradicting residual membership. Therefore B={b}.
Every residual edge now contains b since J is true on it. No affected guard can contain b: if one did then every a in A would be adjacent to b (test {a,b}), ensuring J<=F and contradicting its being junk. Each of the two distinct residuals b-h1,b-h2 therefore has its other endpoint in an affected guard, and both h1,h2 neighbor s. If b=s then J implies raw x_s and M=J OR x_s is just x_s, contradicting minimality. Otherwise b,h1,s,h2 are four distinct vertices on a cycle. This proves tau<=t.
