Assume exact 4186 hypotheses with j=1. The sole junk J has form (OR A) AND (OR B) with nonempty A,B. All affected clean tops compute (J OR R) AND (OR T), R their raw mixed leaves and T their pure guards; otherwise a clean AND would occur strictly upstream of a clean top, or a top would contain J on both OR sides and inherit a dirty witness. H is union of all T. All residual edges satisfy J and meet H. The term (OR A) AND (OR B) AND (OR H) is below F. Every noncentreless affected top receives at least one first OR reading J with nonempty pure support S, a charged mixer. A first OR whose other input contains J would duplicate its other wire and is nonleast. For all tops reached via that mixer, S times their guards consists of actual star edges. Every star edge can be covered by one of t ordinary centers (one per noncentreless top).

Suppose A,B disjoint. For any h and pure wire L, count k distinct pure ORs reachable by expanding before stopping at raw inputs and lambda ORs. There are at most k+1 distinct stopping wires by connectivity and two inputs per gate. No stopping wire contains two residual neighbors of h: the lambda witness center would neighbor those same two vertices, so C4 forces it to equal h and both edges would be within that star. Hence the residual neighbors of h in L number <=k+1. Write delta=tau-t. We need u+delta<=q+2c+1.

If e.g. B={b}, all residuals have form b-a with a in A. Count on A gives u<=k+1, and delta<=1. If c=0, since u>0 some affected ordinary top requires a mixer, q>=k+1. If c>=1 the result follows immediately. For both |A|,|B|>=2, there is at most one p in A intersect H: all such vertices neighbor all B, hence C4. Symmetrically at most one v in B intersect H. If only one exists all residuals form a single star from that pivot into the opposite operand; same bound. If neither exists u=0.

Suppose p and v both exist. Every residual is a p-B or v-A spoke. Let k=k_A+k_B be the disjoint charged nodes in the two pure operand expansions (no shared nonempty support). Let I=1 if pv is residual and 0 otherwise. Since pv always is an actual edge, u<=k+2-I. If I=0 choose the center of one star covering pv to include a pivot; adding the other gives delta<=1. If I=1 add both, delta<=2. Thus u+delta<=k+3. This suffices if c>=1 or if there are >=2 distinct mixers.

Remaining c=0 with unique mixer. Pick s in its raw support; all H neighbor s via star edges and s outside H. If a in A outside {p,s} and b in B outside {v,s} exist, ab is a nonedge as a-v-p-b would otherwise yield C4. For any h in H outside {p,v}, the cube input {a,b,h} activates the three-clause term; h cannot equal a or b, since ab is a nonedge. Edge ah forces cycle h-s-v-a; bh forces h-s-p-b. Therefore H={p,v}. Replacing centers of affected tops by p,v covers both these stars and all residuals, costing at most one extra vertex. If pv is nonresidual, this costs at most zero: either at least two affected tops are replaced, or exactly one has both p,v in its pure guard and hence its own cross-star cannot contain pv. In the latter case select a center equal to p or v at an unaffected top covering pv and add only the other pivot in replacement. Thus delta<=I and u+delta<=k+2, paid by k pure charges plus one mixer plus one junk.

If the indicated a,b fail to exist, e.g. A={p,s}, v-s is already star. The v-A spoke count is <=k_A, improving the first k+3 bound by one: if pv residual then A's root cannot be lambda (its witness center, sharing p,s with v, would equal v), so k_A>=1 and there is only one residual; if pv nonresidual there are none. The same holds for B={v,s}. This completes the disjoint case.
