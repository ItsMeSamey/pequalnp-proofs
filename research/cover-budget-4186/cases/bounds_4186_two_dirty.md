# Two uncovered edges, two dirty ANDs, one outside OR excluded from Lambda, no centreless tops

Write t for the number of noncentreless tops. A strict defect in this branch requires vertex cover number t+2. Choose any centre of each top cross star. These t choices and one endpoint of each uncovered edge give a cover. Equality implies that the uncovered edges are disjoint, no endpoint is a permissible centre, and no two top stars have a common permissible centre. The four endpoints induce exactly the two uncovered edges since every other edge is covered by a top cross star.

Every nontrivial path carrying a dirty AND value through OR gates has to start through the unique outside OR M excluded from Lambda. Indeed a direct OR reader of a dirty AND is dirty and has an operand reaching no raw input. A direct top reader has an empty side, which is excluded. On a path to an active top, M's other operand must reach some raw input s. Otherwise the first OR above M on the feeding path is a further outside OR excluded from Lambda, or the top directly has an empty side. The OR M is dirty because it reads a dirty AND. Its raw-reaching operand is a pure raw OR expression, since any dirty AND feeding this operand would have had to cross M earlier and a clean AND cannot be below another AND in top form.

On either uncovered edge choose an active top by following true OR inputs down from the output until an AND is reached. The route starts at a below-F output and remains below F through OR, hence this AND is a top (a raw input cannot be below F). At least one side of this top activates via M, since purely raw activation of both sides would give a cross edge. Both sides cannot receive M through OR paths, since both would reach s and the top would activate on singleton s. The opposite side supplies a raw endpoint of the uncovered edge and cannot be singleton, since that endpoint would be a permissible centre. The M side is therefore singleton by the C4-free biclique property, with sole element s. Both edges must activate the same top, since otherwise two distinct tops would admit centre s. Denote its leaf side by T, choosing endpoint names ab and cd with a,c in T.

Write h for the dirty AND feeding M. Its raw-reaching sibling in M cannot activate on either edge, since this would put an endpoint into the singleton side {s}. Thus h accepts both ab and cd. Upstream from h no dirty AND passes through an OR: its OR reader would have to be M, which lies after h. Top form excludes clean AND ancestors. The two dirty ANDs and exact binary fan-in therefore express h as a conjunction of at most three raw disjunctions (discard repeated operands). Each disjunction meets both ab and cd and consequently rejects at most one of ac, ad, bc, bd. At least one such cross pair activates h. It must be bd: all four are graph nonedges and each of the other three activates T and hence the top. In particular neither b nor d lies in T.

Any additional z in T activates the top on {b,d,z}. Since bd is a nonedge, z is adjacent to b or d. If z is adjacent to b, z,b,a,s form a 4-cycle, since s is adjacent to every vertex of T. If z is adjacent to d, use z,d,c,s. Thus T consists of a and c alone. Retaining all other centres and using a,c in place of s covers both uncovered edges and the entire star at this top, using at most t+1 vertices. This contradicts the required t+2.
