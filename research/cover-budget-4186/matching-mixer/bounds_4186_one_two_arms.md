# One-plus-two mixed arms for a residual matching

Assume sole nonempty raw mixer D OR P. Fix p in P and assign distinct, vertex-disjoint residual edges to clean projected roots V=D AND K and W=K AND all mixed raw supports S. Every S contains p. The owned two-edge batch is locally induced; different batches may have ambient crosses.

Use the AND regions outside D's ancestry. At a genuine mixed centreless top with n private dirty ancestors, the n+2 potential boundary slots comprise k private pure K slots, m≥1 mixed S slots, l proxies for escaped D-ancestor subfactors, plus any unused allowance. Use the original D-cone primitive pool for subfactors rather than expanding them twice. Outside D all dirty ANDs have unique forward AND readers, so private regions are disjoint. Independent boundary occurrences can be separately paid even if their raw supports coincide. Genuine mixed centreless roots have n≥1: a top with only raw-reaching boundaries cannot itself be centreless. In the following k+m+l≥3 bounds, any shortfall from counting only distinct actual boundary slots is available directly as unused n+2 budget.

Run the local-centre lottery for all rich roots (two or more assigned residual edges) included in the joint estimate. It needs 2^h for h owned edges and charges all rich users at most one unit per slot. A slot containing an additional singleton-batch endpoint, distinct from all the rich batches' endpoints, retains at least half a unit after their combined charge. On a private slot of a size-two root, a clause containing at least three vertices spends at most half a unit. These are the established local-centre miss-probability bounds (zero for an adjacent support, at most 2^−|support| otherwise).

If V owns two edges and W one, each K slot hits all three and retains half its unit. The S and proxy slots are unused by V, so at least m+l+k/2 units remain to pay W. This is at least two if l≥1; if l=0 use the three-unit private allowance and m≥1. This handles any number of such arms at once.

Suppose W owns two, V one. If l=0, all W primitive occurrences are entirely within the private slot allowance. Every K clause hits all three edges. It cannot be a unique rejecting slot for any of the four transversal assignments on the W pair: such a clause would have to be exactly its two designated mate vertices by the tight-support lemma. A five-slot W needs at least three distinct unique rejecting slots on the four assignments. These must all be S slots and hence contain p, whereas three different two-vertex transversal supports on two disjoint edges have no common vertex. Four or fewer slots are likewise impossible. The private six slots therefore pay all three edges including cover additions, without any D charge.

If l≥1 and neither W edge owns p, each S and each K has at least three vertices. After the rich W charge, at least l+(k+m)/2 remains, which pays the singleton by the private three-unit bound. If W owns a p-edge, there is at most one such arm. The bound l+k/2≥2 already suffices except for l=1,k≤1. Zero k is impossible: K would consist only of escaped factors implied by D, so clean V would equal dirty D. For l=k=1 the private slots still leave 1.5 units. If D accepts the p-edge, assign it to V instead and apply the rich-V case. If D rejects the p-edge, choose a D primitive missing both endpoints. It is unused by W (all its clauses hit this edge), and does meet the marked singleton V edge. The rich charges of all other users together leave at least one half unit. This completes the singleton charge. This exceptional half is needed at most once globally.

These calculations pay arbitrary many 1+2 and 2+1 arms simultaneously, even with nested leakage into the D cone. Rich-only obligations can use the same pooled lottery. Independently introduced singleton-only charges require separate bookkeeping, in particular on the single exceptional D half-slot.
