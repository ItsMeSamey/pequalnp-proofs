# Native guards for a ternary flow vertex

Assume for this local calculation that the three incident edge labels A,B,C are already one-hot over Z/3. Write A_i for the indicator of value i, and likewise for B,C. Reversing an incident edge negates its label by a permutation of the three coordinates. Moving a fixed charge to one incident label is also a permutation. Thus every charge and every orientation reduce to the relation a+b+c=0.

This relation holds exactly when the labels are all equal or pairwise different. It has the following six-row native implementation on the promised one-hot domain:

    A_i (B_i + C_i) = 0,     B_i (A_i + C_i) = 0     (i = 0,1,2).

If a=b, the first active row forces c=a. If a and b differ, the two active rows prohibit c=a and c=b, respectively. The third label is otherwise unguarded at this vertex. This works over GF(2) and the masks contain at most two variables. Its soundness outside the promised one-hot domain is not asserted.

**Exact local cost.** Consider any guard with one of these nine indicator bits as pivot and an arbitrary affine mask in all nine bits. For a fixed pivot value a, its mask on the active slice has the form u(b)+v(c)+d with arbitrary Boolean functions u,v on three labels. For it to vanish on all nine legal triples, v must be u transported by c=−a−b, up to the constant d. A constant u gives no rejection. Every nonconstant u or its complement cuts a singleton from the other two labels. Thus for each pivot there are precisely three useful masks up to their values on the domain. Each rejects four of the eighteen illegal triples, so four guards never suffice. A five-row cover with distinct pivots would choose five among nine pivots and one among three masks at each. The accompanying exact enumeration checks all 30,618 choices; none covers every illegal triple. The displayed construction therefore has minimum size six. Enumeration at six gives 603 covers, counting ordered labeling of each edge's three symbols and fixed names for A,B,C, with pivot subsets counted just once.

**Incidence budget.** In a cubic loopless graph on n vertices, one ternary edge supplies three pivot bits, for 9n/2 in total. If each vertex relation must be exactly represented on promised one-hot inputs by its own rows on its incident labels and each pivot row is assigned to a single vertex, at least 6n distinct pivot bits are needed, even before enforcing one-hot. This leaves a shortage of 3n/2 rows. The argument applies when a single specified vertex bears a nonzero charge and all others have charge zero, because the charge is a relabeling. Even removing the local constraint at one vertex saves only six required rows. Auxiliary Boolean pivots, guards mixing several vertices, and weaker encodings that need to preserve only global satisfiability can evade the counting premise.

**When an ordered guard enforces matching.** Independently, let H be a simple conflict graph on candidate hyperedges, joining any intersecting pair. For an order, give vertex v the guard x_v times the sum of all earlier neighbors' bits. Its Boolean models are precisely independent sets if and only if every vertex's earlier neighbors form a clique. If the clique condition holds, the earliest selected vertex with a selected earlier neighbor sees exactly one, since previous selected vertices are independent. Conversely, two nonadjacent earlier neighbors together with their center give a three-element model despite both conflicts. This proves the equivalence for that order, and existence of such an order is the reverse perfect-elimination characterization of chordal graphs. In particular concatenating two independent predecessor pools for a 3-uniform hyperedge can admit a false selected triple. This result uses each distinct conflict once in the mask; repeated appearances need separate accounting.

**One-edge additive potentials on the legal relation.** Let A be any abelian group, allowing integer, real or GF(2) capacities. Suppose f(a)+g(b)+h(c) is constant on every triple of one fixed charge. Absorb that charge and the sign into h to write f(a)+g(b)=H(a+b). Subtracting the cases a=0 and b=0 gives H(a+b)−H(0)=(H(a)−H(0))+(H(b)−H(0)). Thus each nonconstant part comes from the same homomorphism Z/3→A, up to the absorbed changes of sign. If A has no element of order three, all three potentials are constant. In particular a per-edge additive conservation identity over the integers, reals or GF(2) cannot distinguish even a single fixed charged root from its uncharged copy using these unchanged potentials. A ternary-valued or nonlinear invariant falls outside the lemma.

A matching-count version also applies if the local rule uses nonnegative slack rather than equality. At each vertex the uniform distribution on its nine legal labeled triples has uniform one-edge marginals, for every charge. Consequently these distributions agree on incident-edge intersections even with a single fixed defect of nonzero charge. Summing any vertexwise valid inequalities with coefficients canceling on edge singletons cannot give a strict contradiction: expectation under these local distributions would preserve every summand. This statement requires only local star distributions and is independent of any proof-degree lower bound.
