# Refining a field-valued uniform hyperedge network

Assume the first-stage chart certifies the full degree-at-most-d polynomial over K, with |K|>d. In hidden coordinates the degree-d part Q consists of products on distinct simple d-edges, up to invertible K-linear changes. Compute the formal derivatives of Q in direction Sx and require each output component to belong to the K-span of all component polynomials of Q. Compare coefficients on the union of their monomials and every derivative monomial, including repeated-variable monomials. Powers with even exponents differentiate to zero in characteristic two, but remain present in the comparison space.

In hidden coordinates S_ki contributes to an edge E containing k by replacing k by i. For i different from k this produces a unique term in that edge's derivative. It must be an existing simple edge, excluding i already in E. Conversely each globally permitted replacement yields a derivation. The pairs form a preorder: given allowed k→i and i→j, take any edge containing k, replace k by i and then i by j. If j=k the reflexive relation suffices. Consequently the input derivations form the incidence algebra with full diagonal matrix blocks for the equivalence classes. The natural-module K-trace radical is the strict triangular ideal. Split the quotient center by binary idempotents, lift orthogonal atoms in complementary corners, and choose a K-basis of each image.

The resulting input change preserves the edge-product span. The lifted projections are conjugate to the original class projections by an incidence-algebra unit: the sum of products of corresponding new and old projections is identity modulo the radical and intertwines them. Within-class basis changes are also units. In expanding an edge product under an incidence matrix, apply the selected nontrivial replacements one at a time. A nontrivial legal replacement cannot target another original vertex in the edge. Each next replacement consequently acts on an original vertex still present and gives another simple edge, so two completed replacements cannot collide either. Applying this to the inverse unit gives equality of spans.

The mixed difference on each d-subset of recovered vertices gives its squarefree top coefficient. The nonzero coefficients' K-expanded bases must span the entire raw output independently. For edge E and v in E, take the mixed difference on E minus v and read its E-coordinate. For a translated product it equals the missing coordinate's field translation. Require agreement across its incident edges, then evaluate at the shift to obtain the output offset. Equality on binary inputs of weight at most d verifies every raw Boolean ANF coefficient. Complexity is polynomial in explicit binary input size for fixed d. A recognition failure is inconclusive.

On a chart, the product target is a system of affine binomials allowing zero edge values. Interpolate a separate degree-k raw Boolean predicate on the translated binary frame, substitute the linearized trace expression for each bit, then aggregate field monomials without turning positive exponents into zero on the affine field. At most O(n^k m^k) candidate terms are emitted for fixed k and field degree m. The one-inequation algorithm decides the target and bit without constructing a general witness.

The repeated-variable control is a smooth cubic xyz+x³+y³+ωz³ over F4. Its gradient equations have no common nonzero solution even over the algebraic closure: for nonzero xyz their product would require ω=1, and any zero coordinate forces them all zero. A product of three linear forms is singular, so this cubic cannot become an edge product.
