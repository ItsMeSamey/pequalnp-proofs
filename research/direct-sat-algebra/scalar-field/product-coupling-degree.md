# Binary polynomial coupling across independent solution fibers

Fix a product of nonempty sets X_i⊆GF(2)^{n_i} with one baseline point in every set. Let a Boolean polynomial P on the whole vector have degree d. If P ever differs from its baseline value, it already differs at a point changing at most d factors. To prove this, take a point x where values differ and interpolate on the tiny binary switches s_i that choose between baseline and x_i in each whole block. Each original bit is affine in one switch, so the resulting polynomial has degree at most d. Its squarefree coefficients are determined by evaluations on switches of weight at most d. Agreement there with the constant baseline would force a constant polynomial everywhere.

For d=2, suppose the baseline fails and no one-block change passes. Write u_i for a feasible displacement. All pure one-block contributions vanish. A switch of two blocks can therefore pass only through the mixed bilinear term, which is nonzero somewhere exactly when it is nonzero on a pair of basis displacements of their GF(2)-spans. Those bases can be built from actual local feasible points: repeatedly take a basis of the annihilator of the current span and ask for a feasible displacement on which any one of these linear functionals is one. Each success strictly increases dimension, and if all fail the span is complete. A side-condition solver returning actual witnesses is sufficient for these queries. This yields a deterministic polynomial-time decision and witness using local degree-two and degree-one test oracles. A successful point consists of the baseline in every other block. All calculations use binary coordinates, even if local charts have unrelated field degrees. The global field compositum can thus be avoided.

For general fixed d the cardinality-of-changed-block lemma alone does not bound the number of choices inside each changed block. One constructive approach is to build, separately for each local fiber, a basis of evaluation vectors of all squarefree monomials of degree ≤d, using local degree-d polynomial nonvanishing and witness queries as above. Each basis vector is represented by an actual local point. On any selected set of at most d changed factors, enumerating products of these representatives suffices by multilinearity of the coefficient pairing in the local evaluation vectors. For fixed d this is polynomial in the binary dimension under the stated local-oracle assumption. Multiplying k bounded-degree side indicators charges degree proportional to k. No uniform polynomial bound follows for unbounded k.

Equivalently, merge these representative sets two at a time and retain a basis of degree-at-most-d evaluation vectors after each merge. The corresponding state of two joined assignments is a fixed bilinear function of the child vectors, since every monomial separates into its two restrictions. Basis compression is therefore exact and retains representatives for lifting. All intermediate dimensions are at most the number of global monomials of degree at most d, giving n^O(d) bit work apart from the charged local oracle costs.
