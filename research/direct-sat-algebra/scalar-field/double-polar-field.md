# Recovering an affine restriction of a scalar field quadratic from its binary tuple

An explicit binary quadratic tuple F has n input bits and m output bits. Suppose it is an affine restriction of a nondegenerate scalar quadratic form on E=K^d, K=GF(2^m), with arbitrary binary affine recodings of both input and output. Its hidden direction space W has binary codimension h=md−n; neither h nor a field presentation must be supplied. Under this promise exact target counting has deterministic bit complexity 2^O(h) times a polynomial in the explicit input size. General SAT remains outside this statement.

**Recover a field chart when m>6h.** Work with the output-valued polar B. A nonzero p∈W has a nonzero pairing with some r∈W: otherwise W lies in p's full K-orthogonal hyperplane, of binary codimension m>h. Compute two binary orthogonal complements in W using all output coordinates. If a binary subspace of a K-space has codimension <m, it K-spans that space because every proper K-subspace has binary codimension at least m. Since W intersects p's K-orthogonal hyperplane there in codimension at most h, it spans the hyperplane. Consequently

    P=(p^perp_W)^perp_W=W intersect Kp,   R=(r^perp_W)^perp_W=W intersect Kr.

This also proves the claim for d=2, when p's K-orthogonal hyperplane is Kp itself. Each line intersection has binary codimension at most h in its K-line. Compute e=B(p,r)≠0 and H₁=B(P,r), H₂=B(p,R) in the output. Both have codimension ≤h. The two displayed linear identifications are injective, and μ(B(x,r),B(p,y))=B(x,y) is the restriction of the original output-field multiplication normalized to unit e. Indeed on x=αp,y=βr it multiplies the coordinate vectors for αe and βe to αβe, including after an arbitrary binary output recoding.

Use the partial product completion of bounded-punctured-chart.md with I=H₁∩H₂ of codimension d_I≤2h. For each basis x∈I, J={b∈I:μ(x,b)∈I} has dimension at least m−2d_I. Any nonzero binary linear output functional yields a perfect pairing on full field multiplication. Thus μ(J,I) spans all Y whenever m>3d_I, by dimension, and M_x is forced by M_x μ(b,c)=μ(μ(x,b),c). The span of the matrices M_x M_y is the entire regular field algebra, since μ(I,I) spans Y. Evaluate at the known unit e to construct all multiplication operators. Check the field axioms, unit, Frobenius and partial product. No search over input planes, idempotents or possible scalars is needed.

**Restore the source form and the restrictions.** In the recovered output field, consider the alternating n×n matrix of observed polar values on the binary input basis. Extend it linearly over K, and take the quotient of K^n by its K-radical. In the true chart this is the pullback along K^n→E induced by the original n direction vectors. Those vectors K-span E since h<m. Nondegeneracy of E implies the radical equals the kernel. Gaussian elimination produces the full nonsingular form with coefficients b_ij in dimension d', coordinates for the n original directions, and an explicit binary subspace embedding W. Verify injectivity on the n binary vectors. Absorb the observed output constant into the target.

For the centered tuple, subtract the known cross terms sum_{i<j} b_ij x_i x_j. Fit the remaining values on W using sum_i A_i x_i² + sum_i C_i x_i; the coefficients are unknown K elements and these are linear equations. This exactly captures an arbitrary homogeneous quadratic refinement and a translated origin. Because the polar is nondegenerate the K-linear part can be removed by a translation with explicit corresponding constant. Recover the binary affine constraints from the embedded W and the translation. Verify all affine parameters and the complete tuple identity by evaluation at input weight at most two. Any valid completion gives the actual fiber count on its verified affine restriction, regardless of whether a different hidden completion might exist.

**Uniform control and backends.** Run the polynomial chart attempt on every input; ambiguous candidates must pass the entire verification. If it fails, direct sum over all 2^m binary output characters yields exact counts on any input using polynomial-time quadratic Gauss phases; binary pinning gives a witness with at most n additional sums. A true chart with m>6h satisfies every recovery test. Therefore fallback on a promised input costs at most 2^(6h) poly(input). An accepted chart supplies its own computable h'=md'−n. On a promised large-m chart the partial products reconstruct the true field action and linear reduction of the K-Gram matrix gives the true d, so h'=h. To preserve the 2^O(h) bound when m≤6h as well, always cap the chart algorithm by choosing the cheaper of 2^m and 2^(h') enumeration. Exact large-chart counts use the phase formula in scalar-field-pins.md and either the native fixed-degree Kloosterman evaluator laurent_carry.py or the published characteristic-two point-counting algorithm detailed in punctured-field-target.md. The native implementation independently realizes the polynomial bit bound by finite-ring carry traces, with small-field benchmarks.

Decision needs no fast point-count backend. For a certified chart of dimension d≥4, the Fourier count differs from q^(d−1)/2^h by at most q^(d/2)(q−1)/q, since the nonsingular scalar Gauss magnitude is q^(d/2). Thus m>6h makes every target attainable. In dimension two at nonzero translated target the Kloosterman bounds give error at most 2√q; for h≥1 and m>6h the main term q/2^h exceeds it. If h=0 use the unrestricted field solver directly. At translated target zero in a nonsingular plane, test the origin and the at most two isotropic K-lines. Find finite slopes by solving the binary-linearized affine quadratic aX²+bX+c=0 with b≠0; test the line at infinity separately. Intersect each candidate line with the binary affine chart by linear solve. When m≤6h the binary character enumeration supplies the same deterministic decision bound. For d>2h the native unrestricted-plane construction in scalar-field-pins.md returns explicit witnesses for every target. The decision bound alone is not a deterministic witness claim for arbitrary h.
