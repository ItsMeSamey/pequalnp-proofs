# Sparse torus inequations on a supplied binary separation tree

Use the same full HNF membership matrix T and ambient multiplicative signature coordinates as torus-overlap.md. For a subtree with leaf set S, restrict T to the corresponding block columns and let σ_S be their contribution. Compute its exponent rows on the original occupied torus by summing the appropriate columns' coefficients times the within-polynomial exponent differences. Feasible signatures P_S are computed by successive signed monomial pins. Constant-one coordinates and duplicate exponent rows are reconstructed after enumeration; nonidentity constants forced by the base remain in the table.

Write J for the joint homogeneous image and I_S and I_C for its projections to S and its complement C. On I_S, the kernel of σ_S consists exactly of the tuples u with (u,1) in J. This follows from the defining full T test with all base ratios and all complement ratios equal to one. The number of such u is |J|/|I_C| by projecting J onto I_C. Thus each occupied coset has exactly D_S=|I_S||I_C|/|J| distinct signatures. Row-lattice indices compute all these orders before any state enumeration. If a cut exceeds the supplied cap the solver returns UNKNOWN.

The table retains extendible signatures with a passing tuple on all subtree leaves. At a leaf use the single sparse inequation routine on its signature-pinned occupied torus. For children U,V multiply their retained signatures componentwise and keep each product in P_S for S=U∪V. Necessity follows by restriction of any passing point. For sufficiency, choose passing tuples in each child coset fiber and choose a full base point attaining the parent product (it exists by membership in P_S). Replace its child tuples by the chosen passing tuples and retain its complement tuple. The complete signature product has not changed, so the full HNF image test supplies a single common base point with those difference tuples. In particular the parent signature alone preserves compatibility with the outside. At the root this is the desired decision. Hash-table joins need at most D_U D_V candidate products; only matched products are stored. Complexity is polynomial in the explicit input length and the numerical maximum cut index, including the cost of constructing the supplied tree's cut descriptions. No efficient search for a good tree is implied.

Over F4 take triangles sharing a single central vertex, otherwise disjoint, with edge inequations x_u+x_v≠0. Each triangle has three marginal edge ratios with one independent cycle constraint and thus contributes factor 3 to the global index. Because central scaling can be fixed and all other vertices are private, triangle ratio images vary independently. In a tree that groups each triangle's three edges before joining triangles, any cut splits at most one cycle; its index is one or three. The five-triangle control obtains global index 243 and maximum cut index three. The root test decides feasibility and does not produce a common point or counts.

The group state-space interpretation and exact tree inference are established graph-realization techniques. See [Forney and Gluesing-Luerssen, Codes on Graphs: Fundamentals](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.6264) and [Kashyap, On Minimal Tree Realizations of Linear Codes](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.1383). Here augmented Hermite matrices construct states without discrete logarithms, and deterministic root splitting plus sparse nonvanishing generate and filter them without scanning the ambient field.
