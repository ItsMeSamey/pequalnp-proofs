# Recognizing untwisted field-product networks on arbitrary simple graphs

Let a full binary input/output affine recoding conceal the edge products x_i x_j, with one K=GF(2^m) input per vertex and one field output per edge, m≥2. Arbitrary connected simple graphs with an edge are allowed. In the connected untwisted case the polar centroid is K: comparing B(Sx_i,y_j)=B(x_i,Sy_j) at a k-neighbor kills every off-diagonal S_ki, using TB(x_i,y_i)=0 if j=i. Each edge synchronizes the two remaining scalar actions. Recover a polynomial field representation and K-bases of the input and output modules. The same promise holds if twists can all be removed by vertex/edge re-encoding, including on a tree.

With λ distinct from zero and one, set F_c(x)=F(x)+F(0). Recover Q(x)=(F_c(λx)+λF_c(x))/(λ²+λ). Translation adds only a K-linear term to the homogeneous quadratic. Check this field refinement. Arbitrary binary-linear drift would not suffice.

Compute the K-linear input maps S admitting a K-linear T with B(x,Sx)=TQ(x), comparing all ordinary quadratic monomials including squares. In graph coordinates S_ki is allowed iff N(k)⊆N(i). For each neighbor j of k, output kj includes S_ki x_i x_j. If i=j this is an unavailable square; for distinct i,j,k the monomial is available iff ij is an edge, and no other contribution cancels it. The diagonal case is unrestricted. Conversely all allowed terms belong to the edge-monomial span. Thus the projected input space A is the associative incidence matrix algebra of the neighborhood-inclusion preorder.

Equal-neighborhood classes give full diagonal blocks, and strict inclusions give a triangular ideal J. On the natural K-input module tr(SR) pairs each diagonal block by the nondegenerate matrix-trace pairing. Its radical is exactly J. Compute the center of A/J, isomorphic to K^h for h twin classes. Splitting its binary idempotents z²=z recovers all primitive central idempotents. Lift them to complete orthogonal idempotents e_i in A. Sequentially choose a preimage in the corner complementary to earlier lifts and raise it to a power of two at least the input dimension to kill its nilpotent idempotence defect. The final complement completes the family.

Let p_i be the true twin-block projections paired by residue class. Then u=sum e_i p_i equals the identity modulo J and satisfies e_i u=u p_i. Thus u is a unit carrying the hidden decomposition to the recovered one. Units preserve the edge-quadratic span: for edge kj, a term x_i x_l in (ux)_k(ux)_j requires N(k)⊆N(i) and N(j)⊆N(l), which imply edges ij and il, so i≠l. Apply u⁻¹ for equality. Arbitrary further K-basis changes within twin blocks are also units. Every K-basis selected in each lifted block therefore gives valid product coordinates after output re-encoding. Recover edge images by polar evaluation, solve for a shift, and check all binary coefficients. Stars, complete graphs, false twins and one edge are covered. Genuine Frobenius holonomy may shrink the centroid and is outside this guarantee; the separated-neighborhood recognizer handles many such cases.

Independent coefficient checks covered every connected labeled simple graph on two through five vertices, including 674 with nonzero trace radical and 306 with nontrivial twin classes.

The degree-zero derivations here are those preserving the graph's squarefree monomial ideal. The general structural description of derivations preserving such ideals predates this reconstruction; see [Brumatti and Simis, The Module of Derivations of a Stanley–Reisner Ring](https://doi.org/10.2307/2161115). Algebraic rigidity for Stanley–Reisner rings is treated by [Bruns and Gubeladze](https://www.home.uni-osnabrueck.de/wbruns/brunsw/pdf-article/v3n4-2.pdf). This procedure starts from affine binary data, constructs the field presentation and coordinate frames, and checks the raw map.
