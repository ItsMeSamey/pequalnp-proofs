# Minimum scalar polar rank separates the standard CNF gate lift

Take a nonzero binary linear combination of the output bits of a product network. Some edge e has nonzero coefficient functional Tr(a·) on its output field. Restrict all vertices outside its two endpoints to zero. The resulting scalar quadratic polar has two diagonal zero blocks and nonsingular m×m off-diagonal blocks: a scalar multiple of the trace pairing and a Frobenius automorphism preserve nondegeneracy. Thus every nonzero scalar polar has rank at least 2m. An output functional supported on one edge attains rank 2m. This invariant is unaffected by invertible binary affine changes of either input or output. It requires neither graph girth nor the derivation argument.

Every nonlinear gate row emitted by cnf_quadratic_interface.lift has the form ab plus an affine term, where a and b are binary affine linear forms. Consequently its polar rank is at most two and exactly two when the restricted row remains nonlinear. Affine input restriction preserves the upper bound. As long as a genuine gate row remains in the binary output row span, the raw CNF gate lift cannot be one of the extension-field product networks with m≥2. This holds even after dense invertible mixing of all gate outputs. Deleting equations or adding auxiliary variables with a different encoding may change the invariant.

The usual two-bit determinant graph color encoding has minimum rank four, so this rank-two obstruction need not apply there; its noncommuting derivations give the separate exclusion in hidden-product-network.md.

For m=1, the implemented recognizer also accepts prime-field networks. The real DIMACS 2-CNF cycle (¬x_i∨¬x_{i+1}) gives edge monomials x_i x_{i+1} after the ordinary lift. check_product_network_cnf.py applies dense reversible input and output changes, asks for x_0 x_j=1 as a raw quadratic side bit, and obtains UNSAT at j=1 and SAT at j=2. It separately tests ordinary three-literal gate lifts. This positive control starts at clauses rather than constructing an ANF from a planted extension-field law.
