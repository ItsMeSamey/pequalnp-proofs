# A witness boundary at ternary products

Given a positive binary exponent k and nonzero a∈K, finding a kth root of a reduces to finding an assignment to simple three-uniform product equations. The construction costs O(log(k+1)) vertices and edges. Solvability of the root equation already has a polynomial gcd-and-power test.

Use four anchor vertices and impose product one on all four triples of anchors. All anchors equal some u with u³=1. Divide every vertex value by u to normalize the anchors while preserving all cubic constraints. For every wire w in a binary addition-chain circuit computing x^k introduce a distinct inverse wire and impose w inv(w) u_0=1. For gate w=p q take fresh copies p' and q', imposing p' inv(p) u_1=1 and q' inv(q) u_2=1, then p' q' inv(w)=1. This also handles p=q with simple hyperedges. Impose output times two distinct anchors equal to a. In normalized coordinates these equations implement the entire powering circuit. Every kth root extends by evaluation and inversion, and each returned assignment yields a root by normalizing the input wire. This reduction makes no hardness assertion. It shows that universal witness recovery on ternary product networks includes general binary-exponent roots, beyond the graph-specific plus/minus Frobenius cycle argument.
