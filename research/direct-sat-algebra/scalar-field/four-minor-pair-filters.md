# A deterministic five-decoy class for exact binary plane traces

Represent U by its four low bits, changing coordinates so H=<e0,e1>. In lexicographic exterior-coordinate order H has Plücker vector 1. A full-rank private projection of an r+2-element independent set gives one nonzero trace point p among the 35 points of Gr(2,4)(F2). Call any attained non-H point a decoy.

There are 16 decomposable binary two-row projection functionals f that accept H. They are precisely the decomposable six-bit covectors with first bit one. For every unordered pair f,g, including repetitions, test whether the two matrices obtained by appending their respective two rows to the common private rows have a common size-r+2 independent set. Both accept every H witness. A negative common-basis answer therefore proves absence. A positive answer gives physical IDs and their actual trace: either H, or a decoy simultaneously accepted by f and g. This uses ordinary maximum-cardinality matroid intersection, including its constructive augmenting-path algorithm. [Lawler, Matroid Intersection Algorithms, §2](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1971/Archive/ERL-m-333.pdf).

After discovering decoys, pick any pair accepting none of them. Each iteration either settles the instance or discovers a new decoy, so at most 35 intersection calls suffice. If no pair remains, report UNKNOWN with explicit bases realizing decoys that hit every pair. Every such certificate contains at least six distinct decoys. Consequently this algorithm always settles instances with at most five attained incorrect plane types, at arbitrary private rank. It can settle other instances as well.

The number six is a sharp covering constant for these local filters. Enumerate the 35 nonzero wedges a∧b for distinct nonzero four-bit a,b. Delete H to index 34 decoys. For each of 136 filter pairs, let the corresponding hyperedge consist of the 34-bit mask of decoys accepted by both. No hitting set of at most five elements exists. The exhaustive recursion in four_minor_pair_filters.py takes any unmet edge, branches once for each element of that edge, deletes the edges hit, and descends to depth five. The six exterior points encoded as 2,3,4,5,6,7 do hit every pair. Sharpness also occurs on a physical instance. At private rank zero take the four physical vectors encoded by 4,5,6,7, the affine coset e2+H. Their six pairs attain precisely the six non-H planes inside the three-space <e0,e1,e2>. Every pair of linear accept-at-H tests must accept another point in this Fano configuration: for extension tests compose each with a binary functional taking its H-value to one, then the joint passing set in a binary three-dimensional vector space has dimension at least one and excludes zero. Thus every common-basis call is feasible on this NO instance and the adaptive solver returns UNKNOWN with its six bases. Adding vectors e0 and e1 gives a YES instance with the same entire second-degree lift span, since all seven Fano lifts sum to zero. Independent private-only columns pad this example to arbitrary private rank. The replay also compares 150 small physical instances with complete candidate-set enumeration.

## Relationship to weak-map results

The GF16 zero-test in four-minor-zero-test.md produces absence exactly when every basis of the binary M1 is a basis of the freer GF16 M2 (assuming their ranks agree). Lucas's binary rigidity theorem concerns a proper rank-preserving weak map *from* a binary matroid. In this application the binary hypothesis on the freer matroid is not guaranteed. Geelen, Oxley, Vertigan and Whittle explicitly state the orientation and binary theorem in their introduction. Oxley and coauthors' Tutte-polynomial inequalities also start from the assumption of a rank-preserving weak map; they give consequences and do not provide the present identity-map containment test. [Geelen et al., Weak Maps and Stabilizers of Classes of Matroids](https://homepages.ecs.vuw.ac.nz/~whittle/pubs/weak_maps_and_stabilizers.pdf). [Weak Maps and the Tutte Polynomial](https://www.math.lsu.edu/~oxley/freedom_paper_final.pdf).

The space-cover theorem for fixed-rank perturbations of a supplied graphic matroid charges the solution size k (and, for the dual algorithm, perturbation rank). In the exact-plane problem the common private rank is unrestricted, the private matroid need not be graphic, and the physical witness size is r+2. The perturbation bound alone therefore does not apply. [Fomin et al., Covering Vectors by Spaces in Perturbed Graphic Matroids and Their Duals](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2019.59).

## A second quadratic-span certificate

For the six coordinate covectors indexed by i, let M_i consist of the private rows and the corresponding two named rows. For a B of size r+2, write p_i(B)=det(M_i[B]); a zero vector can be discarded. The 21 Cauchy–Binet polynomials det(M_i diag(X) M_jᵀ), i≤j, have joint coefficient vector φ(p)=(p_i p_j) indexed by B. Their coefficient span V over F2 can be learned with high probability by evaluation over a sufficiently large binary extension and expanding evaluated vectors back into binary coordinates. Every resulting vector belongs to V. For each of the fewer than 2^21 binary linear queries on those 21 coordinates, a nonidentity has probability at most (r+2)/|K| of vanishing under independent uniform field inputs. Thus a union bound controls failure of the sampled span to equal V; no probability claim applies to formal identity tests without this randomization.

Enumerate the 35 candidates Λ={p:φ(p)∈V}. When V is correct, H outside Λ gives absence; if φ(H) is a coloop among the candidate lifts and belongs to V, then it must be attained, since actual lifts span V. In particular rank(V)≤5 suffices for completeness. Any dependence of at most six distinct nonzero lifts would give a set T of at most seven points (adjoining the origin in case of odd cardinality) annihilating all degree-at-most-two polynomials. A nonempty such set has at least eight points: split T by a varying binary linear coordinate, multiplying indicators of each side (degree one) by any affine linear function. Each nonempty side annihilates all affine linear functions and hence contains at least four points (even cardinality, zero vector sum, distinct elements). Thus a target in the span of at most five actual basis lifts must coincide with one of them. This criterion is an auxiliary relaxation; the five-decoy algorithm above supplies deterministic certificates.
