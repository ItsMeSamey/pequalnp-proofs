# Finding a point on the compiled affine plane curve

Let P(X,Y) be the verified compiler's output over K of cardinality q=2^m, with numerical degree D. Deterministic native counting decides emptiness. The following separate Las Vegas search on a nonempty locus costs expected poly(D,m) bit operations, and every returned point can be checked against the original chart. The chart adapter invokes this algorithm in field_plane_root.py and rechecks the resulting input. A matching previously computed exact count can be reused.

Handle zero and constant polynomials directly. For q<16 max(1,D)^4 enumerate the plane. Otherwise compute the squarefree radical. For a nonconstant P let H=gcd(P,P_X,P_Y), A=P/H. Perfection implies each irreducible has a nonzero derivative coprime to it in at least one direction. Thus H has the largest even multiplicity below or equal to each original multiplicity. Recursively take rad(P)=lcm(A,rad(sqrt(H))), with constant radical 1. A square has only even exponent tuples, and coefficient square roots use inverse Frobenius. Remove the content in Y. A K-root of the vertical content supplies a witness with any Y; vertical factors without a K-root can be discarded. Handle constant residuals directly.

For the primitive squarefree remainder F, choose c with gcd(F,F_X+cF_Y)=1. Each geometric component excludes at most one c by squarefreeness over the perfect field. Scan D+1 values. Compute the nonzero Y-resultant R(X) of F and this derivative, of degree O(D²). Find all rational x-roots of R and test the corresponding F(x,Y) for a rational root. This includes every singular rational point, including intersections of conjugate components. Degree drops on specialization can add extraneous candidates, which direct checking removes. A constant-in-Y derivative is allowed. Rational univariate roots are isolated by taking gcd with T^q−T and splitting using traces of basis multiples of T, evaluated by modular Frobenius. These traces distinguish field elements by nondegeneracy of the trace pairing.

If this step returned no point, nonemptiness gives a smooth rational point. Its unique geometric component is Frobenius invariant and hence defined over K. The affine part of an absolutely irreducible degree-d plane curve has at least q+1−(d−1)(d−2)√q−d points by the arithmetic genus bound and at most d points at infinity. This exceeds q/2 at our cutoff. Each vertical fiber has at most D points because F is primitive. Uniform x and the deterministic univariate rational-root test therefore succeed with probability at least 1/(2D) per trial. All preceding gcd, resultant, and field operations use polynomials of bounded variable count and degree polynomial in D. The compiler's coordinate map sends each parameter point back to an original point, where the target and pins are re-evaluated.

The singular projective curve estimate is due to Aubry and Perret, [A Weil theorem for singular curves](https://www.math.univ-toulouse.fr/~perret/Fichiers/Scan-Weil.Singulier.pdf). The compiled plane promise charges the numerical degree, including growth from Frobenius layers and pin substitutions.
