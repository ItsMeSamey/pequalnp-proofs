# Full affine drift on stars: surjectivity and center pins

Assume a supplied K-coordinate chart for a forest of stars. Each edge output is center times leaf plus an arbitrary K-affine functional on all vertices, with independent prescribed target values. With centers fixed the leaf matrix is M=C+diag(s_owner(e)); the right side includes the given target, constant and center drift. Index each row by its corresponding leaf. Starting with the empty principal minor, adjoin one whole center block of degree d at a time. As a polynomial in that center's value, the enlarged determinant has degree d and leading coefficient equal to the previous nonzero principal minor. At most d field values fail. Thus when every degree is less than q, construct a witness deterministically by trying at most d+1 values per center, then solve for all leaves. This works with arbitrary cross-star K-linear drift. The fixed-field star hardness at maximum degree q proves the all-target degree bound is sharp.

More generally, impose jointly consistent binary affine constraints Hs=b on center coordinates, with H_i the binary columns for the ith field symbol. For an order with remaining tail T after center i, put ell_i=rank(H_{T+i})−rank(H_T). Given an extendible prefix, the feasible values of s_i form a binary affine subspace of size 2^(m−ell_i). It is obtained by Gaussian elimination modulo the image of H_T. Whenever d_i is strictly below this size, try d_i+1 distinct allowed values to keep the enlarged principal minor invertible while retaining extendibility. Thus an arbitrary number of center pins is allowed under this per-block hypothesis.

Recognize whether any order meets the bound by reverse greedy. Starting with suffix T empty, choose any remaining i with d_i<2^(m−rank(H_{T+i})+rank(H_T)) and place it immediately before T. If a valid ordering exists, moving this chosen i to that position from earlier cannot harm intervening centers: their future-variable spans only grow, so each rank loss ell_j weakly decreases. If no candidate exists, no such ordering exists. This failure does not itself prove unsatisfiability.

For one nonconstant center bit, it suffices to place a participating degree-d center last with d<q/2 and have all others of degree<q. For every fixed q≥4 the q/2 threshold is sharp in the worst case even for one pin on one center. Pin Tr(t)=0. Using q/2−1 reciprocal leaves forbid every nonzero a in its trace-zero hyperplane by (t+a)c_a=1. Its remaining leaf enforces td+b_1=0, giving b_1=0. For each affine check L_i=0, give b_i a leaf with b_i u_i+L_i=0 and, except at the last check, one with b_i v_i+b_{i+1}=0. Under the single pin all b_i vanish. Thus independent degree-two stars implement arbitrarily many checks. Booleanize master x using xy+x=0 and the tie y=x among those checks. Isolated edges supply AND gates with private operand copies, and affine checks handle the copy and NOT constraints. The full vector map is surjective before the side pin, because all degrees are below q. Yet its zero-target feasibility with the single prescribed binary linear bit is NP-complete. No counting reduction or claims about pins on leaves are required.
