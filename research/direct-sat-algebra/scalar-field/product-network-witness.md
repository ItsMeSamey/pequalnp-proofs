# Deterministic witnesses on the recognized edge-product family

Start from the verified chart and sparse field polynomial P representing the desired raw bit. For each monomial seed its variables as nonzero and add all endpoints of nonzero edge targets. These are the candidate least supports from binomial-inequation.md. For each candidate test the full polynomial on that support, including all terms active there. At least one candidate succeeds whenever the inequation is feasible, by the support-closure argument. This works for arbitrary edge targets.

On a fixed support product-network-basepoint.md constructs a particular solution and expresses each connected component using one cyclic multiplicative parameter h of known order. Substitute these expressions into P, folding all exponents modulo their corresponding orders and combining equal exponent tuples. This expansion does not increase term count. Distinct tuples of monomial characters are linearly independent, so the empty dictionary is precisely the identically zero case on that support.

For a nonzero dictionary of at most T entries, assign one parameter at a time, keeping its residual dictionary nonzero. For the relevant group order d, enumerate nonzero packed field elements a in order of increasing polynomial-basis degree. Test the first min(T,d) powers of a^((q−1)/d), computing the restricted dictionary directly. Shoup proves that some degree-O(log m) polynomial represents a primitive element over GF(2) in any given polynomial field presentation of degree m. Therefore within polynomially many trials a tested base generates the order-d subgroup. Fixing any live choice of the remaining variables gives a nonzero sum of at most min(T,d) distinct univariate characters; the Vandermonde matrix on the first that many generator powers is nonsingular. Thus at least one trial retains a nonzero residual dictionary. The algorithm stops as soon as it finds one and never needs to identify a primitive element or factor q−1. Final field coordinates are lifted through the chart; the original side bit and the chart's verified vector output are checked.

Both decision and original-input witness recovery in this particular hidden product-network family are deterministic polynomial bit time. The cycle-solving and particular-witness proof uses the special exponents of edge products. It does not establish this witness bound for arbitrary sparse affine binomial systems. The plane-curve witness bound belongs to a separate model.

[Victor Shoup, Searching for Primitive Roots in Finite Fields](https://shoup.net/papers/primroots.pdf)
