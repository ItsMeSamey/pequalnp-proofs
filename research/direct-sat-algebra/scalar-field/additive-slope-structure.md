# Subfield-line hitting and a hidden low-rank shift

If A is linear over K₀⊆K and h=[K:K₀], then for s=b+uv with v≠0, u in K₀, the K₀-determinant of A+M_b+uM_v is an ordinary degree-h polynomial with nonzero leading coefficient. Any h+1 distinct points of this line therefore hit a safe slope when |K₀|>h. For the linearized form A the largest scalar subfield is obtained by taking the common fixed field of the Frobenius powers j with a_j≠0; commutation with M_c gives a_j(c^(2^j)−c)=0 by uniqueness of the linearized representation. Its binary basis can be computed by solving F^k(x)=x. At h≥|K₀| a diagonal K₀-linear operator containing every K₀ scalar among its diagonal entries can make the entire scalar line unsafe. Over all binary-linear A, no input-independent proper subset of K hits universally: A(x)=x²+cx has unique safe slope c, movable to any field element.

Another sufficient condition is a verified rank-r shift A=M_c+UV, where the columns of U span the binary error image and V gives their r binary coordinates. Seek a nonzero u such that V M_u U is strictly triangular. The forbidden triangular entries impose at most r(r+1)/2 homogeneous binary equations on m unknown bits. If m exceeds that number, any nonzero kernel vector gives a safe s=c+u⁻¹: multiplying A+M_s by M_u reduces to identity plus M_u U V; the determinant equals det(I_r+V M_u U)=1. This argument certifies a single scalar directly and does not rely on a fixed hitting list.

When c is hidden and 2r<m, solve for a monic linearized P of Frobenius degree r and Q of degree at most r with P(A(x))=Q(x) on a binary basis of K. An annihilator of an r-dimensional extension of the error image gives a solution. For any solution, Q(x)−P(cx) vanishes on a subspace of dimension at least m−r>r and must vanish identically by the ordinary polynomial degree bound 2^r. Its top coefficient recovers c^(2^r); invert this Frobenius and verify rank(A−M_c)≤r directly. This is the scalar special case of the existing rank-drift.md interpolation argument, related to Pierre Loidreau's “On the reconstruction of linearized polynomials” (2004). Neither structural criterion holds for all A. These notes provide proofs, not a separate implementation.
