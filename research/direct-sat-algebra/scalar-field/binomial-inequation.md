# Sparse nonvanishing on affine binomial models

Let X be the rational solutions of a binomial system over K=GF(q). To decide P≠0 for sparse P, group its monomials by the least Horn closure C containing their supports. Discard impossible closures by affine-binomial.md. Each group sum depends only on C and vanishes when C is absent. If the sum is zero on the torus fiber on C, truncation shows the group vanishes everywhere. Conversely, if P vanishes everywhere, restriction to C and induction on strict subclosures show that the group alone vanishes there.

On viable C the active equations define a coset of a subgroup H. Append the difference w of two monomial exponents as the last equation and treat z=x^w as unknown. With N=q−1 and B the column Hermite basis of [augmented exponent matrix | NI], put T=NB⁻¹. Possible z satisfy z^(T[j,last])=product_i c_i^(-T[j,i]) in every row, where c are the original coefficient ratios. At least one z exists. The number of distinct z equals the gcd of N and all last-column entries. When it is one, Bezout coefficients recover the unique z multiplicatively from those right sides. Distinct characters on H are linearly independent as K-valued functions: translating a minimal dependence and subtracting a scalar multiple eliminates one character without eliminating every character. Thus a group vanishes iff sums of coefficients with corresponding ratios vanish within each class. The algorithm uses polynomially many HNF computations and modular powers. This argument needs no generator of K*.

For multiple inequations, multiply their left sides along a supplied binary tree. Reduce after each merge by the preceding rule, retaining one representative per surviving closure and proportional-character class. Every replacement is a global function identity by truncation. Let W be the largest surviving class count at any tree node. Each internal merge needs at most W² raw terms. With leaf sizes charged separately, total bit cost is polynomial in input length and W. The reduced root is nonzero iff the inequalities are simultaneously satisfiable. This is a parameter of the supplied representation and tree. Over GF(2), even connected constraints (1+y x_i)≠0 have a root with 2^n distinct support classes, although y=0 immediately satisfies them.

Unrestrictedly many constraints are NP-complete even on the full nonzero torus over GF(4). Impose x_i³=1 and x_i+x_j≠0 for each edge. Solutions are precisely proper colorings with three colors. In this example there is only one support closure on the models, so closure count alone cannot ensure tractability. On the binary field, a satisfying assignment for the bounded-W case can be recovered by coordinate restrictions, but those restrictions require their own width accounting if the products are rebuilt at each step.
