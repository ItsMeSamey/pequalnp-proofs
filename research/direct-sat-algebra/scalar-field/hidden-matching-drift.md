# Recovering matching products with binary-affine drift

The input is the constant, binary linear columns and squarefree quadratic columns of a vector-valued Boolean map. The promise is that invertible binary affine input and output coordinates turn its quadratic part into independent field products, one pair of field inputs and one field output per edge. The fields may differ. Any binary-affine map from the entire input to the entire output may be added. Translations contribute only affine terms. Successful recognition certifies this representation even on an unpromised input.

Polarization removes the entire affine drift. On a single edge the polar is the field determinant on two field coordinates. Its simultaneous input/output centroid is precisely scalar multiplication: applying the centroid identity to pairs on each coordinate axis forces the input action to preserve that axis, and on one vector from each axis the two restrictions and the output action satisfy T(ab)=S₁(a)b=aS₂(b). On a direct sum of edges the nondegeneracy of each block and the independence of their outputs kill every off-diagonal input action by testing pairs from distinct summands. Since products span each output, output actions also preserve summands. The centroid is therefore the product of the scalar fields. Its primitive binary idempotents separate the edges.

Within each recovered block pick p,r with nonzero polar pairing e. Their perpendiculars are their distinct field lines. Pairing the first line with r and the second with p identifies each line bijectively with the output. Under these identifications their cross pairing is multiplication normalized to unit e. The implementation certifies the resulting field table, converts it to power coordinates, and recovers the full drift from values on individual binary basis vectors. Equality on inputs of weight at most two proves equality of all ANF coefficients. Recognition returns no chart if these tests fail.

Fix the left inputs in order. The right-input linear system has one diagonal multiplication operator per edge plus an arbitrary binary matrix. Eliminate previously fixed invertible leading blocks from the next diagonal block to obtain its Schur complement A before adding the new multiplication. A slope s with A+M_s invertible extends the invertible leading matrix. Repeating the step gives an invertible full matrix. Evaluate at the chosen left inputs and zero right inputs to remove the affine offset, solve the matrix equation, then replay the witness in raw coordinates.

The keyword `slope_finder` on both `solve` and `Chart.witness` accepts a callable taking the field and the coefficients of the linearized polynomial A. The default remains `additive_safe_slope.find`, polynomial in the local degree and number of safe slopes. Passing `additive_slope_prefix.find` selects deterministic worst-case time polynomial in the packed input size times 2^(0.78 M), where M is the largest field degree. In the prefix backend h=floor(0.22m) parity bits leave at most 2^(m-h) candidates. Products of at most h trace factors only require exponents of binary weight at most h after cyclic folding; their number is at most a polynomial times 2^(H₂(0.22)m), with H₂(0.22)<0.78. Dickson principal-minor moments and binary elimination have polynomial overhead per exponent/candidate. All slope outputs are independently checked for invertibility. The recognition and Schur operations outside the slope finder take polynomial time.

Run `python hidden_matching_drift.py` for raw recovery, degree-mixed examples, injected prefix pivots, and hostile controls. The keyword can be used directly as `solve(n,m,c,linear,pairs,target,slope_finder=prefix_find)` after importing `find as prefix_find` from `additive_slope_prefix`.
