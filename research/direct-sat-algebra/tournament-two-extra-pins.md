# Two compulsory vertices preserve the arbitrary mixed tournament problem

Use the outgoing row convention and let (d,beta) be original loop and mark labels. Keep the original tournament orientation on vertices Z, and give all Z loop zero. Add w1,w2 (both prescribed present), and transitive marked a1,a2,a3 in that order. Give the five new vertices loop one. Only A={a1,a2,a3} is marked. Direct w2 to w1; all A to w1; w2 to a1,a3 and a2 to w2. All Z dominate A. Set w_j→z iff lambda_j(z)=1 for lambda1=beta and lambda2=ones+d+beta.

For any feasible new support A has odd selected population. Each selected z therefore sees external parity 1+(1+lambda1(z))+(1+lambda2(z))=d(z), giving precisely the original requirements on its old neighbors. Since w1 sees no other new vertex it enforces the original beta mark. Conversely, take any feasible old support. If its lambda2 readout is one, add both pins and a1. If it is zero, add both pins and all A. The selected a rows see respectively one external outgoing edge for a1 alone, or 1,2,1 such edges plus 2,1,0 internal outgoing edges when all A are selected. The w2 row sees w1 and respectively 1 or 2 vertices of A, giving outgoing parity one after its lambda2 readout is added. This is a linear-overhead decision and witness reduction. It requires two physically pinned vertices and establishes no hardness result for the unconstrained single-mark problem.
