# Original-source comparison for joint quadratic image recovery

Cheraghchi and Indyk, Theorem 1 of *Nearly Optimal Deterministic Algorithm for Sparse Walsh–Hadamard Transform*, give deterministic recovery with nonadaptive transform queries and an ℓ₁ error proportional to the best k-sparse tail. Consequently a genuinely k-sparse target is recovered exactly. The theorem states a bit-length hypothesis polynomial in the logarithmic dimension; its proof accounts for integral coefficient magnitude and the normalization of the transform. [Original ECCC paper, Theorem 1 and §4](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2015/076/download/)

**Explicit reduction.** Let a(y)=#{x:q(x)=y}. Under their normalized transform, the queried coordinate is 2^(−M/2) Σ_x (−1)^(s·q(x)). Choose even M≥max(m,d,2), where m is the number of ports and d the dimension of the affine input domain. Implicitly append zero output bits to embed a into dimension 2^M without changing sparsity. The normalized oracle value is then dyadic and has O(M+d) bits. Each oracle query is answered by a quadratic Gaussian sum. For known K, Theorem 1 with k=K therefore recovers the exact histogram in deterministic polynomial bit time in the explicit input size and K. For unknown K recover successively the support of output prefixes: after j−1 bits with K' known values, the next prefix has at most 2K' values, a valid sparsity bound. Apply the theorem with that bound, capped by the ambient dimension. Every prefix has at most K points. Repeat on pinned input domains, whose support can only shrink, to recover a representative for each requested output. This is our reduction to the earlier theorem, not an attribution of the quadratic SAT application to its authors.

Exact polynomial-time counting for one binary quadratic and hardness for cubics also predate the present work. Ehrenfeucht and Karpinski established the degree distinction; Montanaro gives an alternative proof and a bounded-occurrence strengthening for the cubic case. [Original 1990 report](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/ftp/reports/cs-reports/1990/8543-CS.pdf) · [Montanaro, §3.1](https://people.maths.bris.ac.uk/~csxam/papers/lowdegree.pdf)

Accordingly neither the output-sensitive existence theorem for joint quadratic histograms nor the cubic counting barrier should be presented as a new general algorithmic complexity theorem. The elementary hashing implementation, directly checked unknown-support stopping criterion, Gaussian target quotient/lift, budgeted witness recovery and tests against specific SAT representations are the local contributions. This limited comparison asserts no priority for those combinations. The available original source already suffices; heavy-coefficient learning theorems are unnecessary for the deterministic claim.
