# Exact quadratic width of the odd-parity one-hot colour chart

At each vertex the three labelled Boolean colour indicators satisfy odd parity. Use the first two as x_v,y_v and write the third as 1+x_v+y_v over GF(2). All three internal pair-exclusion violations become x_v y_v by Boolean idempotence. Requiring this to vanish excludes exactly (1,1), and each remaining evaluation denotes one distinct colour. Each edge has simultaneous-colour violation equations xx′, yy′, (1+x+y)(1+x′+y′). Thus target zero is in bijection with labelled proper three-colourings; isolates also have exactly three choices.

The polar space has dimension n+3|E| after deduplicating the internal rows. On each edge block use the polars of xx′, yy′ and xy′+yx′, the third obtained by summing the three original edge polars. These matrices are independent and have support disjoint from all other edge and vertex blocks. This basis change concerns polars; counting uses the actual constant and affine terms as well.

For w=min_T(codim(T)+dim(P restricted to T)), every optimal split can be replaced with an equally good coordinate split. Use the formal degeneration in colour-determinant-exact-width.md with weights 3v,3v+1. Each chosen edge polar is homogeneous (the two cross terms have the same weight), and each vertex xy is homogeneous. The matrix at generic t is obtained by nonzero edge/vertex row and basis-pair column rescalings. Ranks cannot rise at t=0. This proof applies to any invertibly recoded chart by transporting the subspace back first.

Let F,S,D have two, one or zero live physical bits. Codimension plus the dimension of surviving vertex polars equals |S|+2|D|+|F|=n+|D|. For each edge with both endpoints outside D there are live endpoint slots; at least one of its three polar generators has a nonzero coefficient on those slots. Restrictions to a coordinate space preserve disjoint supports among edge blocks and vertex blocks. The total cost is therefore at least n+|D|+e(G−D). Taking every nondeleted vertex to retain just x attains this lower bound for every D. Adding an endpoint of each remaining edge to D gives a vertex cover; choosing a minimum cover as D proves

    w = n + τ(G).

In particular the weaker n bound from disjoint rank-two vertex polars is valid. This width is specific to the chart above; the single-determinant edge encoding is a different polar space.

**Other preprocessors.** The deduplicated vertex forbidden planes are the mutually independent two-dimensional P_v. Every external scope is spanned by one line from each endpoint plane, and at any vertex with a neighbor the three incident colour types give all three nonzero lines. Thus each individual scope is contained in the span of all others, and private-scope elimination makes no initial progress in a connected graph with at least two vertices. Direct sums respecting each whole scope cannot separate endpoints of an edge; connectedness gives one factor. This does not prohibit conditional, adaptive or differently grouped elimination.

For completeness, a space W hitting every plane in positive dimension must choose a nonzero h_v∈W∩P_v at each vertex, costing n independent directions. Modulo their span K, the vertex planes descend to independent single coordinates. For each edge choose a colour line at each endpoint from one matching colour that is neither h_u nor h_v; at least one of the three colours works. The corresponding external plane meets K trivially. Its nonzero hit in W therefore projects to a vector supported nontrivially on those two quotient coordinates. The leading pivot coordinates of W/K consequently cover all graph edges, costing at least τ(G) further dimensions. Conversely take both directions on a minimum vertex cover and one direction at every other vertex. This hits every plane. The exact hitting dimension is n+τ(G).

The common radical vanishes because the vertex polars are nondegenerate within each vertex, and the edge/vertex independence shows full polar output after removing redundant output coordinates. For a centroid pair (A,B), alternatingness gives [B Pol(z,z)]_v=Pol_v(Az,z)=0. Here Pol_v is the standard alternating form J on the v block. Varying inputs outside v with z_v fixed forces every off-diagonal block into v to vanish. In dimension two J(A_v z,z)=0 on the two basis vectors and their sum forces A_v to be scalar. On an edge Pol(Ax,y)=Pol(x,Ay) equates its endpoints' scalars. Thus on a connected graph A is scalar and fullness forces B to equal that scalar. There is no nontrivial polar-centroid split, and for output dimension >1 the one-dimensional centroid fails the full-output field recognizer. Duplicated gate rows before preprocessing add only consistent affine output relations; an invertible reordering of equations or invertible affine input reparametrization cannot change these facts.

An independent bounded run enumerating all 2,898 joint input directions in dimensions 0,2,4,6 checked the exact width against every labelled simple graph through three vertices (12 graphs total, 0.143 seconds). This validates a structural exponent. It supplies no lower bound for deciding or counting colourings by other algorithms.
