# Exact semilinear subfield-line search

Interpolate the linearized coefficients of the d original binary columns. Remove the scalar coefficient b, obtaining B. If B=0 any nonzero additional scalar works. For the active exponent set J choose j in J, let e=gcd(d,{i−j:i in J}), and g=gcd(e,j). Every term acts on L=GF(2^e) via the same automorphism θ. Hence B is θ-semilinear over L. Put F=GF(2^g), m=e/g, n=d/e. The iterate B^m is L-linear on an n-dimensional space.

The norm-zero slope is t=0 and is tested by original rank. For t≠0, Bx=tx implies B^m x=Norm(t)x. Thus all bad norm targets are roots of the monic degree-n characteristic polynomial of B^m, including zero when B is singular. Two scalars of equal nonzero norm have ratio θ(z)/z for some z in L*, by cyclicity. The change x→zx identifies their binary kernels. One lift per norm therefore decides an entire class.

Conversely, suppose B^m has nonzero eigenvalue ν=Norm(t). On that eigenspace C=t^(-1)B satisfies C^m=I. For x nonzero, Σ C^i(αx) cannot vanish for every α in L: in an L-coordinate nonzero on x it is a nonzero linear combination of distinct automorphisms θ^i, or equivalently a nonzero polynomial of degree less than |L|. Some choice gives a nonzero fixed point of C, hence Bx′=tx′.

Test at most min(|F|,n+1) targets, counting zero. If |F|>n, success follows; otherwise every class is checked and failure certifies only that all b+L slopes are singular. Recheck the final original binary columns. Previous scalar-commutation sufficient criterion required |F|>nm.

Raw-input cost: recover L,F as binary kernels of Frobenius minus identity. Obtain field coordinates on L by binary linear algebra and power_chart (factor the small integer e by trial division and find a full-degree generating element by Frobenius fixed spaces). Invoke deterministic_norm_lift.norm_preimage on the degree-e power basis for each nonzero target. This uses only primes p dividing m, found by trial division up to d. Project into the p-primary component by repeated division, use Shoup's unconditional degree-O(log e) primitive-candidate list and a single p-primary order test, then recover base-p logarithm digits by an at-most-d-entry table. Lift the complementary component by exponentiation. No group-order factorization and no scan of |F| or |L| is charged beyond the stated n+1 targets. Each stage is polynomial in d, and each norm and returned scalar is replayed.

For a strict separation take K=GF(4096), L=GF(64), F=GF(4), θ(z)=z⁴, and an L-basis (1,η). B(u+vη)=ηθ(v) is bad exactly on the norm classes zero and one. This covers every scalar in F; the other norms in F* give safe slopes in L. The checker found scalar 32, and also passed 40 independent comparisons with entire subfield-line scans. For semilinear background see Dempwolff, Fisher and Herman, https://doi.org/10.1017/S0017089500020164 .
