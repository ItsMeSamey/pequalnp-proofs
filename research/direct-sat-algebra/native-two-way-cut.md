For a binary matrix G use V_i=span(e_i,G[:,i]). These spaces span the ambient n-space. On side X their dimension is |X|+rank G[barX,X] by projecting away identity columns from X. Thus the cut dimension is exactly rank G[X,barX]+rank G[barX,X]. Jeong–Kim–Oum (https://doi.org/10.1137/19M1285895) construct a width-w tree or certify failure at their fixed-parameter cost.

At node X store counts indexed by s in im G[X,barX], t in im G[barX,X], and parity. Count x_X with outgoing response t, that mark parity, and every selected row seeing zero in G[X,X]x_X+s. Singletons test both values. At a join X=L union R fix s,t_L,t_R. The child inputs are s|L+t_R|L and s|R+t_L|R. Add products of matched child counts into output t_L|barX+t_R|barX and XOR the parities. Original assignments determine their split outputs uniquely. At root use its unique response pair, mark one. Positive table predecessors reconstruct the original support. Cost at most 2^(3w+2)poly(n) per node; <=2^(w+1) entries per table; counts <=2^n. No symmetry or normal-coordinate compiler is assumed. Independent accounting must include the subspace tree recognition cost if no tree supplied.
