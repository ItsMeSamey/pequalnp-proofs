# Recovering independent target factors after dense linear changes of coordinates

Let Q:V→Y be an explicit centered quadratic map over GF(2). A constant is absorbed into the target. Let B(u,v)=Q(u+v)+Q(u)+Q(v). Assume the values of B span Y and its common radical in V vanishes. Both tests are Gaussian elimination. Define C as the pairs (S,T) of endomorphisms of V and Y satisfying B(Su,v)=B(u,Sv)=T B(u,v). These are linear equations tested on basis pairs.

In C, two elements (S,T) and (A,D) commute: evaluating B(Su,Av) in the two orders gives TD B(u,v)=DT B(u,v). Fullness implies TD=DT and nondegeneracy then implies SA=AS. Coordinatewise composition preserves C, and the identity belongs to C. On this commutative binary algebra squaring is additive. The idempotents therefore comprise the kernel of the linear map E↦E²+E. Within this space require Q(Su)=TQ(u) for each input basis vector u. This suffices for all u, since the polar of the left side is T²B=TB. The constraints are linear on the idempotent space: B(Su,Au)=TD B(u,u)=0. Thus a second Gaussian elimination returns a basis of exactly the quadratic-compatible idempotents.

An idempotent other than zero or identity splits V into im S and ker S, and Y into im T and ker T. Fullness and nondegeneracy ensure both component maps have positive-dimensional domain and codomain. For x,y in different input components B(x,y)=0 by the defining centroid identity. Compatibility puts Q(x) and Q(y) in the appropriate output components. Conversely a direct decomposition with complementary input and output spaces has precisely this projection in C. Consequently one detects a split iff the compatible-idempotent space has dimension at least two. Recurse on its factors; the hypotheses pass to each factor by the direct sum. Repeated Gaussian elimination finds a finest linear direct decomposition in polynomial time. Since all splitters commute, the coarsest common refinement of all projections into simultaneous binary eigenspaces gives the same indecomposable factors up to ordering. A scalar idempotent signal cannot be inferred from poles alone: for instance Q=(x₁x₂+x₃, x₃x₄) has the same polar as two unrelated monomials, but the cross linear term forbids the two natural complementary projections.

For target t each factor receives its component of t. Multiply its exact fiber counts; sum embedded witnesses using the domain direct decomposition. Using Fourier sums on each factor costs a polynomial times the sum of 2^dim(Y_i). A tuple of independent full-rank scalar quadratics on disjoint input blocks therefore has polynomial recognition, counting and witness recovery after arbitrary invertible input/output basis changes. Its image can have 2^dim(Y) elements. Translation x↦x+u changes the centered tuple by B(x,u), which commutes with every compatible projection; the existence of the decomposition survives translations as well.

Direct decomposition by a bilinear centroid and primitive idempotents has prior art in James B. Wilson's [Finding direct product decompositions in polynomial time](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.0548). The binary quadratic compatibility equations above explain how the linear terms enter this solver. No priority is asserted for the centroid or its direct-product interpretation.

For every connected simple graph with an edge, the raw color-determinant encoding has only scalar centroid. Here V is the direct sum of two-dimensional vertex spaces and Y has one basis vector per edge. B spans Y because a pair of single-vertex inputs on the endpoints of an edge realizes that edge vector; the common radical vanishes because no vertex is isolated and each edge polar is nondegenerate on its two endpoint spaces. For such single-vertex inputs u,v with B(u,v)=e, the vector B(Su,v) is supported on the star of the second endpoint while B(u,Sv) is supported on the star of the first. Their equality places Te in the one-dimensional intersection spanned by e. Write Te=t_e e. The resulting scalar-form identity B_e(Sx,y)=t_e B_e(x,y) forces (Sx) on both endpoints to equal t_e x there, by edge-block nondegeneracy. Adjacent edges thus have the same t_e; connectivity gives S and T scalar. This obstruction applies to the determinant representation on both satisfiable and unsatisfiable connected graphs and survives invertible recoding.

## Reduction of degenerate inputs at a fixed target

The hypotheses can be enforced with polynomial preprocessing. First center Q and translate the target. If U=span B is a proper subspace of Y, then Q mod U is affine. Solve Q(x)=t modulo U, reject inconsistency, and parametrize its full affine solution space. The residual Q(x)+t now lies in U, so pass to its coordinates and target zero. The coordinate map is bijective on the parametrized domain; no multiplicity changes. This step strictly decreases output dimension.

If the common radical R is nonzero, decompose V=H⊕R. The map on R is linear and Q(h+r)=Q(h)+Q(r). Project the target and the H-map modulo Q(R). Each satisfying h produces exactly 2^(dim R−rank Q|R) solutions r, and recovering one takes a linear solve. This strictly decreases input dimension. Recenter as needed and repeat both operations, recording affine maps, multiplicities and lifts. Each iteration strictly decreases the sum of dimensions; dimension-zero residuals are affine base cases. At termination the reduced problem has full nondegenerate polar. Thus hidden product recognition can be applied target by target after all affine constraints and output shifts have been accounted for.

For the general bilinear factor theorem see also [Kreuzer, Miasnikov and Walsh, Proposition 5.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.01735), which treats primitive scalar-ring idempotents for full nondegenerate bimaps.
