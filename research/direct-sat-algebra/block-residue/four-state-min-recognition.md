# Four-state multisorted chain-minimum recognition

Choosing a total order on each site so every binary row relation preserves coordinatewise minimum is NP-complete, even with full unary projections and a known solution. Verification enumerates pairs of tuples. For hardness reduce monotone NAE-3SAT on distinct triples. Give each variable site states 0,1,* and interpret 0<1 as its truth bit. Give each clause site states a,b,c,* and associate the three literals with its cyclic comparisons (a,b),(b,c),(c,a). For a comparison (i,j) and its variable, take the relation {(i,0),(j,1)} together with every pair containing a wildcard at either site. The minimum of the two ordinary rows is allowed only if i<j agrees with 0<1. The three directed comparisons extend to an order on a,b,c exactly when their bits are not all equal. Conversely extend a satisfying assignment to those orders and put every wildcard first. Then the two ordinary rows are comparable, and any minimum involving a wildcard-bearing row retains a wildcard. The original row instance always has the all-wildcard solution. [NAE hardness](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04198)

For physical realization, compute four one-hot outputs from two private input bits per site. Expose every indicator by a binary exterior clause (NOT indicator OR fresh unconstrained primary), assigning every observer primary one whenever needed. This step keeps wildcard indicators exposed. For variable sites pin the unused fourth color false. On constrained pairs forbid precisely the missing color tuples by exterior binary clauses. Every allowed pair of actual row states extends by using wildcards everywhere else; every disallowed pair violates its exterior clause. Thus the physical unary/pair tests recover exactly the reduction, including full relations between nonadjacent sites. The three surviving unit rows at a variable site still fail Boolean majority closure, as do the four clause rows.

Chen and Larose Theorem 6.11 proves general binary conservative semilattice recognition NP-complete on a growing common domain. The construction above uses per-site orders, at most four states, and exact physical row pairs. It does not classify chain-median recognition. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.00932)

Any single full disequality relation on q colors, q at least three, excludes both chain-minimum orders and unrestricted multisorted majority operations. For minimum take a color c maximal in neither order; larger a on the left and b on the right give allowed (a,c),(c,b) with forbidden minimum (c,c). For majority, take three distinct right colors and call their operation output c. Put c on the left at every position with a different right color and a color different from c in the remaining position, if any. The left majority equals c and the diagonal is forbidden. The physical one-hot four-coloring relation with its binary exterior realizes exactly these edge constraints, including on connected complete bipartite graphs and on complete graphs of arbitrarily large treewidth.
