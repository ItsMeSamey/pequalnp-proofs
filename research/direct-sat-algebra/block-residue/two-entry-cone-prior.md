# Two-entry cones and earlier circuit decompositions

In structural LUT mapping, a cut for a fixed root meets every path from a primary input to that root. A K-feasible cut has at most K leaves. Mishchenko and coauthors describe bottom-up enumeration by combining cuts of the two fanins. Teslenko and Dubrova study the related separator question for one fixed vertex and give a linear-time algorithm for a compact representation of all its minimal double-vertex dominators. [Mishchenko et al., cut enumeration](https://blif.org/~satrajit/files/2006_FPGA_Improvements_to_FPGA_Tech_Mapping.pdf) · [Teslenko and Dubrova, double-vertex dominators](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.04994)

The [two-entry cone theorem](tied-free-laminar-nand.md) fixes a seed pair of physical wire IDs. Its forward closure takes every gate whose operands become available, across all possible outputs. A gate can expose a result to multiple exterior consumers. The theorem compares closures for different seed pairs and proves their gate sets are disjoint or nested. Disjoint gate sets may share an entering wire. The proof uses acyclicity, a unique producer per derived wire, and two distinct physical operands per gate. The maximal closures then give the coarsest admissible partition. Optimality for the specified number of non-majority-closed tables additionally uses exact joining on shared wire IDs and existential projection.

Classical single-vertex dominance gives a tree, and canonical single-entry/single-exit regions give a program structure tree. Their boundary conditions differ from two entering wires with unrestricted exposed outputs. The cited papers establish these related constructions. The cross-output laminarity and partition-cost claims here rely on the linked proof. [Lengauer and Tarjan, dominators](https://doi.org/10.1145/357062.357071) · [Johnson, Pearson and Pingali, program structure tree](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=2e3b19aa1df44df18435900d3deeb11a547d193e&repid=rep1&type=pdf)
