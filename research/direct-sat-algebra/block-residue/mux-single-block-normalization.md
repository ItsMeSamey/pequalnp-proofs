# Connecting a normalized circuit's comparison forest

A shell is a disjunction of two signed comparisons, each of the form x+y=d over GF(2). The contracted comparison graph retains both edge occurrences, independently of their shell pairing. Root-relative Boolean truth is measured against a rail o. Every comparison has a physical K2 realization: for a pair (a+b=d,c+e=f) create two new raw endpoints with colors (a,c), (b+1+d,e+1+f), using fresh physical bits and signed copy pins for their indicated potentials. A raw matching comparator then accepts exactly when at least one comparison holds. Pins for multiple occurrences of each potential may form a path with the indicated signs. If comparisons are required to come from unsigned copy pins alone, this particular one-block conclusion does not follow by merely introducing complement vertices; doing so changes the comparison graph.

Start with AND gates and signed wires, absorbing NOT into literal signs. Each AND gate z=x AND y is encoded by (x∨¬z) and (¬x∨[z=y]); signs on inputs are carried into both uses. The new variable z has a rail spoke and one nonrail comparison to its designated parent y. Thus after removing the rail the underlying simple graph is a forest on all wires, rooted at the primary inputs. The output pin can be encoded by one shell with duplicated true-output literal (two parallel signed spokes). Canonicalize constant circuits separately; remove unused primary wires, or account for them explicitly. This statement assumes nonconstant literal inputs to the AND gates. At least one primary root has a rail spoke: take the designated first operand of the earliest AND. Repeated input variables do not create a nonrail loop because gate outputs are fresh.

Choose such a spoked root r. To ensure at least three other roots for the construction add three unconstrained padding roots. For every other root u introduce a fresh h and the two shells

    (s=o OR h=u),  (s≠o OR h=r).

Select s by cyclically permuting the list of the other roots with no fixed point. Every other root is then used once as selector, each contributes two opposite-signed spokes and s differs from both branch roots. For every old assignment and each choice of the three padding bits there is exactly one h per connector. Every returned satisfying potential assignment restricts to a model of the original circuit relative to o. Fixed rail gives an eightfold multiplicity after padding; an unfixed physical rail gives a further factor two when counting raw copied-coordinate assignments. Distinguish this from auxiliary factors in a particular NAND source wrapper.

The nonrail graph adds the subdivided tree links u−h−r, retaining a tree. All original and padding vertices now have a spoke; each h has two nonrail neighbors. Deleting the rail leaves a connected tree. Deleting any other vertex leaves every surviving spoked vertex adjacent to the rail and each surviving h adjacent to at least one spoked neighbor. Thus the multigraph is vertex-biconnected; it has a single graphic cycle-matroid component including its parallel spokes. Opposite spokes at a selector contradict if all comparisons are required to hold, so the block is unbalanced. Its cycle rank exceeds one. Each of the nonhub roots has three distinct neighbors (or only two if originally isolated: its connector and rail); counting edge occurrences it has degree at least three even in the latter case. For arbitrarily many distinct degree-three neighbors in the underlying simple graph, use hard circuits in which every nonhub input is designated y in at least one gate, or choose a path of root links instead of a star: all internal old roots along that path have two distinct connectors plus the rail. For that version assign each initially unspoked root a distinct link not incident to it. With k≥5 roots the k−1 nonhub roots can be matched to the k−1 links: each excludes at most two links and each link excludes at most two roots. Hall follows for sets of size ≤k−3 from the minimum allowed degree k−3, and for sets of size ≥3 each link has an allowed root in the set. The existing three padding roots guarantee k≥5 whenever the original has at least two roots; add one further padding bit if necessary.

The pin contraction, singleton guard convention and tree arrangement describe a normalization. The parity disjunctions comprising a mux are each essential on its four distinct potential vertices, but eliminating the private mux outputs existentially recovers the original problem. Identification of these auxiliaries invalidates any stronger claim of resistance to semantic preprocessing. Where original-NAND acceptance is required, compute the color bits using the actual signed pins, construct the declared guard/suffix, evaluate every original physical NAND gate, and decode the circuit-wire potentials relative to the rail. For an exact canonical matching parse those wires satisfy each original comparator by the construction.
