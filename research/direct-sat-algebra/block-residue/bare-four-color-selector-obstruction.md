# Pairwise selector filtering on a bare one-output NAND DAG

For a finite simple graph without isolates, allocate two private raw bits per vertex. Compute four minterms as its four color indicators. For every edge uv and color c compute NAND(indicator(u,c),indicator(v,c)). Fold the distinct test results in a chain of binary AND gates, beginning with two tests. Assert just the final output. Ordinary tied NANDs implement all inversions. The original raw circuit has output one exactly on proper four-colorings. K5 with a path attached at one endpoint gives a connected negative family of unbounded order.

Under generic signed alias normalization, each vertex retains four distinct NAND results on the same two base bits with different input polarities. Its actual indicator values complement those outputs. Each edge test retains one signed NAND, and each binary AND retains one signed NAND with complemented interpretation. There is no common-subexpression merging. This uses no exterior-objective recognition.

Every nonempty two-seed closure starts with a gate whose distinct operands are its two seeds. Starting with a vertex's input pair gives its four gates but no edge test since an indicator of another vertex is unavailable. A seed pair enabling a test gives just that test: its indicator pair is unique, and its result is combined only with another test or a fold result, neither of which is among its operands. A pair enabling a fold likewise gives only that fold: its result is next combined only with a fresh test, and no other gate reads a pair from the triple of its operands and result. Therefore the maximal signed cones are the vertex blocks, singleton edge tests and singleton folds. This structure is asserted after signed normalization or its prescribed private-inverter materialization.

All four vertex outputs are externally read and its raw inputs are private. In indicator polarities its exposed relation is the four unit vectors. The binary hull admits the all-zero ghost. Each selector side contains two colors. At every edge test and fold both entering bases and the result are exposed, with the final result observed. Each signed NAND table has four rows and fails majority closure, so its selector has a side containing any specified local row.

Freeze every test/fold selector to the side containing its row under the wire assignment in which all vertex indicators are zero and all tests and folds are true. Translate signs back to actual base IDs as necessary. Both sides at every vertex remain available. For any of the resulting selector strings, enforce only an arbitrary chosen pair of its sites along with the full physical binary base and output pin.

* At two vertex sites choose different colors from their respective two-element permitted sets. Leave all other vertices at the ghost. Every test actually evaluates to true, as does each fold.
* At a vertex and an edge-test site choose a permitted color different from the test's named color. Keeping all other vertices at the ghost preserves the designated test's exact ghost row and all tests are true.
* At a vertex and a fold, either permitted color works and all folds keep their exact ghost rows. For two suffix sites the all-ghost assignment itself works.

Each tested pair therefore extends, possibly by a different assignment. On the connected negative family at least 2 to the power of the number of vertices full selector strings survive all unary and pairwise checks. There is no physical witness. This does not assert that the full side-conflict graph is empty on the suffix choices excluded from this family of strings. More generally the bare circuits realize NP-complete four-colorability, via addition of a universal vertex to a nonempty 3-colorability instance. All witnesses replay on the original single-output circuit.
