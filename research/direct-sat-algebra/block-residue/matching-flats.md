# A near-linear NAND circuit defeating the projected-term compiler

Take q=2^d disjoint K_{d,d} blocks, h=2dq vertices, and optionally add a simple bridge between successive blocks. Give each vertex two original prefix-bit tracks X_v,Y_v and take one additional prefix bit t. On edge e=ij form z_e=(1+X_i+X_j)(1+Y_i+Y_j) over Boolean values. The actual circuit requires all z_e equal and a suffix y with y1 XOR y2=1 and 1+y2+t=1. Use an AND chain on these two affine predicates and on the affine comparisons 1+z_0+z_e for all e other than one designated edge. The suffix may have b≥2 bits: for each further bit y_j append the affine equality 1+y_j+X_v=1 for a chosen original vertex. For b=Theta(h) these constraints can involve all additional named suffix bits. This is an explicit circuit of O(h log h+b) fanin-two NAND gates after constant-size compilation of the sparse XOR and AND operations. The first two suffix bits always equal (1+t,t), so all zero-suffix anchors fail. Both t values occur in feasible prefixes, ruling out a single universal suffix.

In the reviewed affine-plus-AND normalization expand [z=ab]=(1−z)+(2z−1)ab. Pinning the final conjunction high forces every outer AND to take its second branch: from its value 1 both inputs must be 1, recursively. The sign factors at these outer gates are 1. Let T be the set of inner edge gates taking their first branch and let U=E minus T. If T is nonempty, all named z are forced zero. Each e in U now requires both its operands high, imposing equality of the two endpoint X bits and separately the two Y bits. Every compatible prefix has one suffix and one assignment to the other internal bits in this term. Its exact contribution is the affine indicator of equality within each component of U in the two tracks times (−1)^{|U|}. If T is empty, all edge equalities are imposed. The common z may take either value, giving coefficient 1+(−1)^{|E|}; the resulting support is different or the coefficient vanishes.

For U equal to a choice of one perfect matching in every K_{d,d} and no bridge, each connected component is a pair. In a simple graph exactly that edge set induces this partition. The projected flat determines the partition already on the X track, by which two named coordinates must agree. Hence each such support has a unique contributing expansion index and signed coefficient ±1. They all survive modulo 2^b, even after combining EVERY identical projected affine support first. All meet at X=Y=0, a genuine satisfying prefix (actual z_e=1). The number of surviving classes is (d!)^q; its binary logarithm is (h/2)(log2 d−O(1)). With b=2, or b=h, this exceeds the total number of raw input bits n=2h+1+b for all sufficiently large d. Thus materializing even one record for every distinct surviving flat can cost more than enumerating all 2^n raw assignments on O(n log n) gates.

The projected-flat uniqueness statement uses the supplied affine+AND macros. One can verify each macro by its constant-size NAND table before normalizing. It does not assert the same support identities for all alternative rewrites of that circuit. For strict literal NAND expansion a separate refinement obstruction applies: take many distinct NANDs on pairs of prefix bits, require all outputs equal, and use the same nonzero suffix guard. At all-ones prefix each nonempty designated first-branch set, forcing some NAND output 1 in the complemented gate expansion, has an odd partial term after recombining every other gate; hence at least one distinct fully expanded term is odd there. This argument proves term multiplicity, not equal-support uniqueness for the literal expansion.

The actual circuit is easy to satisfy. This is a counterexample to the stated deterministic compilation clock, not to a deterministic SAT algorithm permitted to exploit identities among different flats or the original gate structure. It is a prescribed split and gate presentation. It supplies no lower bound against choosing a different split.

## Actual SAT and UNSAT source controls

Alternatively require every 1+z_e high in the final AND chain. Each z_e detects equality of the two endpoint colors encoded by the two raw tracks. The projected fiber function becomes the proper-four-coloring indicator. Its expansion for each U is (−1)^|U| times the component-equality flat, directly by multiplying the equality complements. This is the pointwise version of the classical chromatic subset expansion. [Stanley, Theorem 2.5 and Whitney attribution](https://math.mit.edu/~rstan/pubs/pubfiles/100.pdf).

Join any simple nonempty graph H to the block chain by a bridge. A proper four-coloring of H extends over every bipartite block by choosing its colors to avoid its bridge conflict; restriction proves the converse. For U a perfect matching per block and no bridge or H edges, the equality classes are exactly the actual pairs and singleton H vertices. Simplicity forces that unique edge set. All (d!)^q support keys still have odd coefficient on either decision. H=K4 gives SAT; H=K5 gives UNSAT. A supplied H of O(h) vertices and O(h log h) edges preserves O(h log h+b) NAND gates, O(h+b) raw bits and an additional logarithmic factor for explicit fanin indices. The all-zero colors meeting these flats form an infeasible prefix in this variant. A bridge-aware solver can separate the decision core; this is a limitation of enumerating the stated support keys.
