# A single-output hard core for arbitrary two-entry NAND groups

Given strict signed 3CNF with C≥1 clauses on declared master primary bits X, add two fresh primary bits p,q for each clause (a OR b OR c), where a,b,c are signed literal values. Use the following physical NAND circuit, with fresh gate outputs and no Boolean rewrites or hashing. NOT is one tied-input NAND.

* Make t=NAND(p,q), s=NOT(t), v=NAND(s,NOT(c)). For a negative third literal use its master directly for NOT(c); otherwise make a fresh inverter on its master.
* Check p=NOT(a) and q=NOT(b). For a private primary p and its master X use h=NAND(p,X), j=NAND(p,h), k=NAND(X,h), w=NAND(j,k). This w equals p XOR X. For a positive literal the check output is w; for a negative literal use NOT(w). Use separate gates for q.
* Conjoin all 3C check and clause outputs in a binary tree of fresh ANDs, each implemented by NAND and NOT. Pin the single resulting output to one. The source has no exterior equality or clause promise.

If the root is one, both private bits take their designated complements, t=a OR b and v=a OR b OR c. Every original model extends to exactly one satisfying primary word and one deterministic trace. Project any accepting word onto its master inputs to recover the original model. With P positive third literals and J negative first and second literals, the gate count is 17C−2+P+J≤20C−2. The private clause tests use 3C+P, copy checks use 8C+J, and the conjunction uses 6C−2. There are exactly 2C additional primary bits.

Consider any full partition into arbitrary groups with at most two distinct incoming physical wires. In a group containing the physical gate t, its two private primaries p,q necessarily occupy both entry positions. A topological scan shows that all group gates lie in the gate closure generated from p,q. In this netlist that closure contains precisely t and s beyond the primaries. Every copy check begins with a mixed gate needing a master. The clause gate v needs a signed third master. No input to the final conjunction is generated by p,q alone, and the other bundles use distinct private primaries.

Both p and q remain exposed through the first gates of their respective copy checks. If s is outside, t is exported to it; if s is inside, s is exported to v. The exact port relation therefore projects onto the full graph of NAND(p,q) or its complement AND(p,q). The triples for primary corners 01,10,11 have coordinatewise majority with primary coordinates 11 and the wrong output. Projection preserves majority closure, so the whole group fails closure. Distinct clause gates t must lie in distinct groups. Thus every admissible full partition has at least C bad groups, regardless of its topological order, contiguity, group cardinalities, or shared master fanout. This applies directly to the physical port conventions and unconditioned local relation used by the pairing solver.

Recognizing the copy checks and eliminating private inputs recovers the source formula, and assigning all master inputs permits direct original evaluation. This result bounds an exposed structural parameter only; it supplies no solver-runtime or arbitrary-conditioning lower bound.

For a seed with k disjoint blocks covering g gates, all candidate unions together contain (k−1)g gate IDs. Their explicit sorting and scoring costs O(kg log(g+2)) indexed operations with the usual hash-table costs. There can be quadratically many stored positive gains: g copies of NAND(a,b), all outputs externally observed, give bad singletons and bad two-gate unions. Every pair has gain one. The whole group has cost one, and fixing the common bit a makes all gates unary. That high-fanout example concerns one-round pairing and is separate from the hard-core reduction above.
