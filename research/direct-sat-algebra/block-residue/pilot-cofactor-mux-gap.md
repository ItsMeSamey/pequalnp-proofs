# One primary cofactor removes the signed-cone cost of a live mux array

Take m≥2, one pilot h and 2m distinct primary payloads x_i,y_i. Produce a single tied-input inverter of h and for each i compute

    A_i = NAND(h,x_i),  B_i = NAND(NOT h,y_i),  Z_i = NAND(A_i,B_i).

The designated positive output is the left-associated conjunction of Z_1 through Z_m. Implement each binary AND by NAND and a tied-input NOT. This is an original single-output NAND circuit with 2m+1 primary bits and 5m−1 gates. Its output selects AND of all x when h=1 and AND of all y when h=0. Every payload and the pilot affect the output on some raw words. There are 2^(m+1) accepting primary words.

Before conditioning the signed normalizer folds all explicit NOTs. In the resulting topology each operand pair generates only its own gate: starting from h and one payload cannot generate the other half of its mux; the two mux entries reach their own Z but cannot generate another accumulator input; each accumulator pair reaches only its own gate. The maximal cones are singletons. Each first-layer gate has a private payload, so its port relation has at most two coordinates. Each Z has both computed entries visible and an exported output. Each conjunction node has two computed entries and its output is exported or observed at the positive pin. These latter full signed NAND relations fail majority closure. The unconditioned optimal score is 2m−1.

For each actual cofactor replace h by a fixed Boolean constant before canonicalizing. One first-layer gate per mux becomes one, the other becomes a complemented payload, and Z becomes the selected payload. Only the left-associated AND chain remains. Its first node hides both raw entries; every later node hides a fresh primary entry. The singleton partition costs zero, hence is optimal. Merely including h=b as a unit after unconditioned normalization need not give this reduction. Restore the pilot and replay the original netlist to verify any witness.

For a NO companion make NOT(x_1), NOT(y_1) and an extra mux Z'_1 with those complemented payloads and the same pilot. Start the conjunction with Z_1 AND Z'_1 and append Z_2 through Z_m. There are 5m+6 gates. Unconditioned signed reads in the two first muxes differ, so the normalizer retains their distinct outputs. The cone on h,x_1 has two outputs: the exposed h,A_1,A'_1 range over 011,101,110 and miss their majority. The h,y_1 counterpart is a signed copy and is also bad. Every other first-layer cone remains good. The m+1 mux gates and m conjunction nodes are singleton bad cones by the same closure argument. The exact unconditioned optimal cost is 2m+3. After either pilot cofactor Z_1 and Z'_1 become opposite reads of the same selected primary. The first conjunction becomes zero and propagates zero to the root. The positive output pin rejects, with zero bad cones on both branches.

In contrast, conjoin two variable-disjoint copies of the clause-private construction in signed-cone-hard-core.md. Any one primary cofactor touches at most one copy. The other retains each of its C mandatory bad singleton cores, with two private inputs exposed at its mixed checks and output exposed at its mixed clause test. Their closures cannot cross the final conjunction. This concerns the current normalizer, which scans even disconnected gates. Constant-root detection may still decide a branch. The source core can have either verdict and conjoining the two copies preserves it.

For a chosen pilot the two solves cost at most (2^b0+2^b1) times a polynomial in W and use polynomial space sequentially, where W charges numerical primary count, gates, exterior data and reconstruction of a complete word. Scanning N pilot choices costs up to 2N normalizations and score computations before solving a chosen chart. This is ordinary Shannon expansion with constant folding; its gain here is a verified property of both branches on the same original input. The earlier pair and signed-normalization gaps compare unconditioned representations. The structural scores do not bound decision time from below.
