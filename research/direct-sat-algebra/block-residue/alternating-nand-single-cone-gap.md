# A NAND corridor interleaved with live gates separates cones from pairs

For k≥3 take raw signals a,b and fresh cᵢ,eᵢ, 1≤i≤k. Set p₀=b. In the supplied order alternately produce pᵢ=NAND(a,pᵢ₋₁) and dᵢ=NAND(cᵢ,eᵢ), for increasing i. Then produce every tᵢ=NAND(pᵢ,dᵢ). Finally produce s₂=NAND(t₁,t₂) and sⱼ=NAND(sⱼ₋₁,tⱼ) for 3≤j≤k. Assert the single positive output sₖ. There are 2k+2 raw inputs and 4k−1 gates. All NAND operand pairs are distinct, including up to exchange of operands. Each distractor dᵢ is live even semantically: set a=0, making every pᵢ=1. Now tᵢ=¬dᵢ and these t bits can be assigned independently. To propagate a change in tᵢ, set its other input at the first affected NAND to one (a preceding partial result can be made one), then set all subsequent t bits to one. The all-zero raw word gives sₖ=1. Under a=0 one can also obtain sₖ=0 by setting tₖ₋₁=0 and tₖ=1.

For singleton blocks p₁ hides private b; the projection on a,p₁ has three rows and costs zero. Each later pᵢ exposes a and the incoming gate signal pᵢ₋₁, which may take all four entrance assignments in the unconditioned block test. Its output is visible at tᵢ. The full three-coordinate NAND relation fails the majority test, giving cost one. Each dᵢ hides both private raw inputs and costs zero. Every tᵢ and all k−1 aggregators have both incoming gate signals and their produced output visible; the designated final output is retained as well. Each costs one. Singleton cost is exactly 3k−2.

The earliest gate of any admissible group forces its two distinct operands as entrances. With these as the only outside signals, the group can contain only gates computable from them in order. From cᵢ,eᵢ only dᵢ can be reached; each tᵢ still needs pᵢ. From pᵢ,dᵢ only tᵢ can be reached, since pᵢ₊₁ needs a and the aggregator needs another signal. From the two operands of an aggregator, its successor still needs a new t bit. From a,pᵢ₋₁ one can compute pᵢ onward but no d, t or aggregator. Skipping any intermediate p breaks this chain. Thus a nonsingleton admissible group must be a consecutive run of p gates in the p sequence. No such run is contiguous in the supplied gate order, since dᵢ intervenes. The interval optimum equals the singleton score 3k−2.

A pair in one round can therefore contain only neighboring p's. Both produced signals remain visible at their t consumers; raw a remains visible because k≥3. For p₁,p₂ the projected rows on a,p₁,p₂ are 011,101,110, whose majority 111 is absent. Hence this pair costs one and gains zero. A pair starting at pᵢ with i≥2 retains a,pᵢ₋₁,pᵢ, whose projection is the full NAND graph, so this pair costs one and gains one. The positive-gain edges form the path on p₂,…,pₖ. An optimal one-round matching has floor((k−1)/2) such pairs, for a score of 3k−2−floor((k−1)/2). Greedy matching cannot improve that bound. The optimal interval seed is identical to the singleton seed.

The full p sequence has only two physical entrances, a,b, both now private. Its k output bits remain visible at all t consumers. When a=0 the outputs are all one. When a=1 they alternate, with pᵢ=b XOR (i mod 2). Thus the pair of odd-position and even-position output values ranges over 11,10,01; repetition of each value into the appropriate coordinates preserves majority closure. Equivalently, the majority of the three distinct output words is the all-one word. The merged cost is zero and its saving is k−1. No other single cone can save more since every nonsingleton admissible union contains only p gates. The best single-cone score is exactly 2k−1, improving on the best one-round pair score by ceil((k−1)/2).

These scores give exponents in the respective partition algorithms' deterministic 2^score polynomial-space upper bounds. They do not lower-bound actual decision times. The construction avoids duplicate NAND gates, but its fixed-pilot corridor has a closed form with just two parity signals. A corridor-aware simplifier can exploit that fact directly.
