# A single positive NAND output with a linear interval-versus-pair gap

For k≥3 use raw bits a,b₁,…,bₖ. First produce all zᵢ=NAND(a,bᵢ), then all wᵢ=NAND(a,zᵢ), both in increasing order. Next produce s₂=NAND(w₁,w₂), followed in order by sⱼ=NAND(sⱼ₋₁,wⱼ) for 3≤j≤k. For a fixed target polarity p append the following suffix in the specified gate order.

1. Produce u=NAND(a,a).
2. If p=0 produce v=NAND(sₖ,sₖ); if p=1 use sₖ directly as v without adding a gate.
3. Produce h=NAND(u,v), then o=NAND(h,h). Require the single positive output o=1.

There are 3k−1 prefix gates and three or four suffix gates. The output means a=0 and sₖ=p. With a=0 every wᵢ=1, so s₂=0 and the remaining steps toggle: sₖ=k mod 2. The instance is SAT precisely when p=k mod 2. All-zero raw inputs give a witness in that case. In the other case no choice of raw bits works. This uses no exterior input pin. Ordinary propagation of the positive output fixes a=0 and simplifies the circuit in linear time; the gap below concerns only the specified unconditioned partition scores.

Every adjacent pair wholly in the prefix has at least three outside input signals. At the first two phase boundaries these include a,bₖ,z₁ and a,zₖ,w₁,w₂, respectively. Adjacent aggregators read at least three distinct predecessor signals. The pair sₖ,u reads the two inputs of sₖ and the distinct raw a. For a fixed starting gate, extending an interval cannot remove an already read outside signal. Therefore every prefix gate is a singleton in every admissible fixed-order interval partition. The singleton zᵢ hides private bᵢ, so its projection on a,zᵢ is majority-closed. Each wᵢ and each of the k−1 aggregators exposes both operands and its output, giving the full three-coordinate NAND relation. The majority of the rows (0,1,1), (1,0,1), (1,1,0) is the absent row (1,1,1). Hence every interval partition costs at least 2k−1.

Among pairs wholly in the prefix the only admissible ones are {zᵢ,wᵢ}. Unrelated gates have two distinct operands each and different operand sets, so they need at least three incoming signals. For a directly connected pair the consumer's other operand is fresh with respect to the producer's two operands, except when zᵢ feeds wᵢ and their other common operand is a. Each allowed pair hides bᵢ and zᵢ, leaving (a,wᵢ) with three rows and reducing cost by one. These k pairs are disjoint. The suffix adds at most one bad singleton: its tied NANDs are unary negations and only h can be bad. Thus singleton cost is at most 2k.

Consider greedy selection of positive-gain disjoint pairs on these singletons, continuing until maximal. Each of the k original improving pairs is selected or meets a selected pair. Each selected positive pair meets at most one original improving pair: a pair containing a suffix gate has at most one prefix endpoint, and a pair of prefix gates must itself be an original pair. Thus the selected gains total at least k. Costs are additive because each selected union's mandatory ports depend only on uses outside that union, regardless of how those outside gates are grouped. The greedy score is at most k, giving a gap of at least k−1 from interval optimization on both SAT and UNSAT positive-output instances. Taking the best of the greedy singleton pairing and other candidates preserves this upper bound.
