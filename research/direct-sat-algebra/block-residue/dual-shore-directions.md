# Linear directions from dual quadratic equations

Fix a stage and let U_j and V_j be the two current shore spans modulo the already guaranteed space. A binary covector lambda annihilates at least one allowed orientation sum exactly when, in each j, it annihilates one of U_j,V_j. This is equivalent to the vanishing of all products (lambda.u)(lambda.v) for opposite shore basis vectors u,v. The common span of all orientation sums is the annihilator of this set of covectors. Every polynomial equation obtained by a binary row combination of the squarefree products is valid on it. If the quadratic terms cancel, the remaining linear coefficient vector belongs to that common span. Insert these guaranteed directions into the existing quotient, repeat, and solve on the selected original columns to lift.

For d explicit binary coordinate positions the quadratic coefficient vectors use at most d(d-1)/2 off-diagonal bits before augmentation with linear bits. There are at most the sum of the products of opposite shore ranks many rows per pass, bounded by the explicit opposite-shore input pairs. Binary elimination costs polynomial bit time and space in the explicit source length; the rank and the number of fixed components bound the number of passes. Exponential orientation enumeration remains possible at the end. Public dual_directions inputs are nonnegative integer masks in one shared coordinate system.

The theta source demonstrates a strict improvement over local intersections. The checker also exhibits strict incompleteness: the system x(x+y)=0, y(y+z)=0, xz=0 forces x=0, yet distinct off-diagonal terms prevent any linear combination of the three products from yielding a nonzero linear row. Hence the degree-two cancellation certificate does not in general compute every guaranteed direction. This is a known type of Boolean polynomial linearization, applied here to a dual certificate that permits original primal witness recovery.
