# Signed alias normalization and optimal two-entry cost

For every admissible original NAND partition, local signed normalization gives an admissible retained-gate partition with no larger count of non-majority-closed exposed tables. Consequently the normalized optimal count is at most the original optimal count. This applies to [the implementation](signed_cone_nand.py), which gives each surviving NAND a fresh output and maps each original wire to a constant or signed representative. An eliminated nonconstant gate copies a signed operand. A constant fold is valid for every assignment of its operand representatives under their recorded signs. There is no common-subexpression merging between unrelated gates.

Use the [physical exposure rule](pair_optimizer.py): externally observed wires, wires read outside the block, and gate-output entering wires are exposed. An unobserved primary input read only inside the block is existentially projected away. Exterior observations are taken from the original literal occurrences before alias substitution; normalized exterior rows substitute aliases and may discard redundant literals or tautologies.

**Boundary provenance.** Assign each retained gate to its original block B and call the resulting set B'. Drop empty sets. Every nonconstant original alias of q has a directed carrier path from the raw primary or retained producer of q, obtained by following a selected operand of each local projection backwards. All wires on the path represent q up to sign.

For any distinct normalized entering representative q, trace a retained B' consumer's operand alias to q's source outside B or to the primary input. The last entry of this path into B, including a direct primary-to-gate read, comes on an original entering wire representing q. Different representatives require different original entering wires. Thus B' has at most the two entries of B. A carrier path may leave B and reenter it.

For a produced B' output read externally, trace a carrier path from its retained producer to the external retained consumer. Its first exit exposes an original produced wire carrying that representative. For an output observed in a surviving normalized exterior row, take an original occurrence yielding the normalized literal. Its carrier either exits B, exposing the carrier at that point, or ends at an originally observed wire inside B. Simplification of rows introduces no occurrence with no such antecedent.

Every exposed normalized entry also has an *exposed* original alias. If its original entering witness is a gate output, the physical port rule exposes it even when it has no further readers. If it is a primary input p, normalization leaves p as its own representative. A normalized primary is exposed only by an outside reader or exterior observation. Trace the original carrier from p to that outside reader or to the original observed occurrence. If its first reader lies outside B, p was already exposed. If the observed occurrence is p, p was observed originally. Otherwise the carrier enters B and either exits on an exposed B output or ends at an observed B output. Thus even a primary that was originally private as an entry supplies an originally exposed signed coordinate. These arguments permit sharing the same primary many times.

**Tables.** With at most one entering representative the new deterministic block has at most two exposed rows. Any three rows have a repeated member, hence majority closure. This covers collapse of originally distinct entries or an alias feeding back into the same block.

If B' has two entering representatives, all original entering wires are exhausted by the two distinct signed witnesses found above. The four normalized input valuations correspond bijectively to the four original entering valuations, whether or not those input coordinates are exposed. Evaluate B in gate order on the corresponding original entering values. Simultaneously evaluate B' on the normalized inputs. By induction, every locally read or produced original wire has its signed canonical value: the boundary supplies precisely its nonconstant aliases, each retained NAND performs the same operation on signed operands, and each fold obeys its gate-local identity. Every nonconstant intermediate representative here is an entering representative or an already produced retained B' wire, by following its operand provenance back through B to an original entry if necessary. This argument works on all four rows without extending them to a whole-circuit input.

For every exposed normalized coordinate use the corresponding exposed original coordinate, correcting its sign. Applying these coordinate maps to original exposed rows yields exactly the normalized exposed table: corresponding input valuations produce corresponding projected rows, and every valuation is included. Signed coordinate maps commute with majority. Thus a good original block remains good, and an originally bad block contributes at most one bad block. Summing over B and minimizing gives the count comparison. The [laminar cone theorem](tied-free-laminar-nand.md) identifies the normalized optimum. Materializing a negative retained operand as a private inverter inside its consumer's block leaves this table unchanged; each added inverter has only its intended reader and no exterior observation. The solver replays original gates and exterior rows on recovered raw inputs.

The proof uses fresh surviving gate outputs, carrier paths for every eliminated nonconstant output, and provenance for every surviving exterior observation. It does not justify common-subexpression merging or adding observations on other internal wires.
