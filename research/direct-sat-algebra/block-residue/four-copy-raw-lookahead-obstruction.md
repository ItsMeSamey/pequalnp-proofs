# Both raw cofactors retain root-reachable majority obstructions

Take any strict signed 3CNF F with C≥1 clauses on three distinct variables per clause. Let u count its master variables occurring in exactly one slot and H=7C−1−u. The indexed NAND reduction of noncontiguous-nand-hard-core.md produces a single output; signed-cone-hard-core.md proves its unconditioned normalized cone score is H. Its root is structurally a nonconstant signed produced gate for every F, even if F is unsatisfiable. It computes F together with the specified private-raw consistency checks. Unused master variables may be omitted and restored arbitrarily.

Construct four disjoint copies with outputs A,B,C′,D, including fresh masters and fresh clause-private raw inputs for each copy. Let the sole designated positive output be

    (A OR B) AND (C′ OR D).

Implement each OR by NAND on complemented child signals and the final AND by NAND followed by a tied NAND. No new primary inputs are required. This is a linear-size decision reduction: on YES, choose any source model and lift it in each copy. On receiving an accepting raw word, replay the four original copies, choose any one with true output, read its own original master bits and check F. A true copy always exists; projecting an arbitrary fixed copy would not be justified. If F is NO no copy can return true.

Fix any single appearing raw bit to either value. It belongs to one copy, say A after permuting groups and siblings. The other three copies are indexed exactly as before up to renaming. The forward simplifier replaces equal operands, complemented equal bases, and constant inputs, and preserves gates with distinct nonconstant bases. The cofactor's constant-prefix gates introduce no retained base. No changing branch in A can acquire an internal base from B,C′,D. In the first OR, even the extreme simplifications are harmless: a constant-zero A leaves B, a constant-one A leaves true, and a nonconstant A produces an OR with B on distinct bases. Consequently the first OR never becomes syntactically false. The second OR retains both fresh signed nonconstant outputs C′ and D. The last AND thus always retains the second OR, either as its result or as an input to a surviving binary gate. This argument still works if a reachability sweep drops folded-away subcircuits, and applies on NO inputs without assuming that logical satisfiability is known to the simplifier.

Every normalized block of the two untouched copies C′,D retains its original boundary. Specifically, the operand pair of any internal retained gate in one of these copies can generate no signal in the other, hence cannot complete their OR. No condition outside enters either copy. Conversely starting from the two copy roots at their OR generates no internal predecessors. At the final join, if present, the two incoming bases likewise have disjoint provenance. Thus the maximal two-entrance cones inside each untouched copy remain the cones classified in signed-cone-hard-core.md. Only the original output observation changes to a mandatory consumer at the OR, leaving the copy's final gate output exposed. All masters and other external readers used in that classification are unchanged. Each of the two copies contributes H bad blocks to both child charts. Even without the full classification, each contributes at least C separate private-core NAND graphs.

For every raw candidate i one has b(i,0),b(i,1)≥2H and the standard aggregate split budget is at least 2·2^(2H). The unsplit chart likewise contains these copies; in fact all four roots are live before cofactoring. Repeating source clauses if desired gives u=0. On a YES input both cofactors remain SAT, even if the fixed bit prevents its own copy from accepting: its sibling and the opposite group can use independent source models. On a NO input both cofactors remain UNSAT, while their charged bad tables stay root-reachable. The conclusion bounds the structural exponent used by the algorithm's upper estimate. It gives neither an execution-time lower bound nor an obstruction to semantic preprocessing, early witness discovery, or source recognition.

The construction uses independent occurrences of an entire raw NAND instance in one ordinary Boolean root. It does not identify shared selector choices or average cofactor evidence. Earlier archive examples such as incidence-witnesses/cofactor-double-star-duplication.md measure surviving incidence defects in overlapping subinstances; those defects do not imply this cone-score bound.
