# Directed Hamiltonian cycles as a projection of homogeneous marked cycles

For an undirected simple graph G with marked edge e and a partition of its indexed edges into pairs let Z_G(Y) sum the products of edge variables over simple cycles containing e and taking at most one edge per pair. Count each cycle once as an undirected edge set. Give every indexed edge an indeterminate, including bridges.

**Uniform graph.** For n≥3 form a chain of n stages and a closing edge e joining its two ends. At each stage give each directed arc (u,v), u≠v, an internally vertex-disjoint branch path between its successive terminals. At the first stage only allow u=1. Two choices at different stages conflict if their tails agree, or if their stages are consecutive cyclically and the earlier head disagrees with the later tail. Include the transition from stage n to stage 1. For every conflicting pair insert a dedicated edge into each of the two paths and pair these two edges. Distinct conflict pairs use distinct physical occurrences.

There are B≤n³ branches. Reserve one separate weight edge per branch. Every branch needs at most B−1 conflict edges plus its weight edge. Pad every branch to the same L=B+1 edges with ordinary edges; all internal vertices are fresh. Pair every edge that still lacks a mate, including e and every weight and filler edge, with its own fresh pendant bridge at the first terminal. The result is simple and has M≤2(BL+1)=O(n⁶) indexed edges and M indeterminates. Nested loops over branch IDs and their pairs suffice for O(n⁶) word operations on O(log n)-bit indices and O(n⁶ log n) explicit output bits. Unused bridge variables cause no change.

**Bijection and projection.** Every marked simple cycle takes exactly one whole branch in each stage. Absence of a pair conflict requires n distinct tails and compatible consecutive heads and tails. These conditions are also sufficient and give a single directed Hamiltonian cycle. Conversely, reading any single-cycle permutation from vertex 1 uniquely fixes the branch choices. Reversing traversal of the resulting undirected cycle does not duplicate its edge set. All feasible marked cycles have degree D=1+nL≤M. Put X_uv on the weight edge of each corresponding branch, and 1 on every other edge. With at most n(n−1) distinct target indeterminates, reused across stages, this uniform projection π takes Z_G to HC_n with coefficient one per monomial, including in characteristic two. The unused diagonal matrix coordinates may be included in the target ring. For the permanent use n row stages with n column choices each, conflict exactly when two choices use the same column, assign X_ij to the relevant weight edge and use identical padding and bridge mates. Each permutation including its fixed points yields exactly one contributing marked edge set.

**Formal circuit and border consequences.** The November 24 preprint's Theorem 1.1 states a lower bound b_n=exp(c(log n)²/(log log n)²) on the degree-n component border size of HC_n for all sufficiently large n over any field. The threshold depends only on characteristic. It gives the same bound for the permanent outside characteristic two. An exact arithmetic circuit for Z_G specializes directly to one for HC_n by relabeling inputs. Hence the exact Z_G circuit size is at least b_n. [Primary theorem and model](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/92b516ef8e2abf66c3db7aa31438d3010bc7956f/preprints/Circuit-lower-bounds-for-Hamiltonian-cycle-and-the-permanent-November-24-2026/build/intro.tex)

To transfer the stronger model, denote projection onto homogeneous degree k by [k]. Work in the finite-dimensional spaces of coefficients over the algebraic closure specified in the primary theorem. The map T(q)=[n]π(q), for homogeneous source q of degree D, is linear and Zariski continuous, and T(Z_G)=HC_n. Suppose Z_G belongs to the closure of degree-D components of outputs of circuits of size s. For each such circuit compute its homogeneous components of degrees 0 through D at every gate. Addition and subtraction are componentwise; for a product its component k is the sum of component products at indices i,k−i. Binary arithmetic cost is O((s+1)(D+1)²), independent of the unrestricted syntactic degree of intermediate polynomials. Scalar and variable leaves are free. Select component D first, then substitute π. The degree-n component of the resulting circuit is T(q). Taking a continuous image of the original closure therefore places HC_n in the closure of those degree-n outputs. It follows that b_n≤O((s+1)(D+1)²). Substituting constants before stripping extraneous source degrees would not justify this argument.

Since n≤M≤O(n⁶) and D≤M, division by this polynomial overhead leaves exp(Ω((log M)²/(log log M)²)) for the source degree-component border size along the constructed family. Exact circuits enjoy the same bound directly. This concerns the explicit indexed sizes M(n), with no assertion about every intervening size.

**Canonical multilinear predicate.** Give each edge an enabling bit and take the unique multilinear polynomial f_G agreeing with feasible-cycle existence on the Boolean cube over the chosen field. No accepting enabling set has size below D. At size D the accepting sets are exactly the feasible cycle edge sets. Boolean-lattice inversion therefore gives [D]f_G=Z_G over every field. The same component lower bound applies when a circuit must compute this exact canonical polynomial. An arbitrary Boolean decision circuit can represent a different formal polynomial modulo the Boolean equations. The construction yields no lower bound for the running time of physical span decision or witness recovery. In fact the complete instances above always have a feasible marked cycle, whereas setting all weights to one gives (n−1)!, zero in characteristic two for n≥3.
