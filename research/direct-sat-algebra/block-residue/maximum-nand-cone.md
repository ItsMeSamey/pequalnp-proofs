# Optimal single merge of arbitrary size in an original NAND partition

Fix an acyclic NAND netlist, its original raw input and gate-output wire IDs, and the exterior observations. Each seed block is a set of original gates with at most two distinct entering wires. The seed blocks partition the gates; their ordering or contiguity is immaterial. A block exposes gate-output entering wires, shared or observed entering primary inputs, and produced wires consumed outside the block or observed externally. Count repeated occurrences of one physical ID as one wire. The exposed table R(U) consists of the interface assignments that extend to all gates in U. It has at most four rows because entering values determine every gate by the original topological order. Enumerate all entering valuations and project away private, unobserved primary inputs.

Write c(U)=0 when the table is closed under coordinatewise ternary majority and c(U)=1 otherwise. These costs use the original consumers and observations; repartitioning the complement does not change c(U). A single merge replaces any collection of at least two seed blocks by their union U with at most two entering wires. Its saving in the total bad-block count is

    S(U) = sum of c(B) over seed blocks B in U, minus c(U).

## The largest union for a set of possible entering wires

Enumerate every subset P of zero, one or two physical wire IDs. For each P start with all seed blocks. Mark for removal each block consuming a raw input outside P. Whenever a marked block is removed, mark the remaining consumers of each of its produced wires outside P. Continue until no more blocks are marked. Only a block's own producer-consumer incidences are internal. A retained producer of a wire in P may belong to the final union. P licenses an entering value if needed; membership in P never forces an internally computed output to be free.

Let T be the union of surviving blocks. Every operand consumed in T that is neither produced in T nor in P would have triggered removal. Thus the actual entering wires of T form a subset of P. Any competing union U with entering wires in P survives: it uses no raw wire outside P, and when an external block is removed, a wire supplied to U by that block must belong to P. Induction on removals proves U is contained in T. Therefore T is the largest eligible union for P. This proof also handles a cycle in the directed graph of seed-block dependencies. For singleton seeds, a topological scan starting from P gives the same gate set, adding a gate only when all its operand IDs are available, including when its output ID was seeded.

## Why the largest union attains the optimum

For any such U inside T, let k be the sum of the seed costs in T outside U. The difference in savings equals k+c(U)-c(T). If k is at least one, or if c(U)=1, the difference is nonnegative since c(T) is at most one. In the remaining case R(U) and each additional seed table are majority-closed.

Take the natural join of R(U) with all the additional seed tables, identifying coordinates by original physical wire ID. Project to precisely the exposed ports of T. This produces exactly R(T). In one direction, restricting a T assignment supplies compatible constituent rows. In the other, take extensions witnessing the constituent rows. A wire produced in one constituent and read in another is exposed by its producer and is an entering coordinate at its consumer. Inputs shared between several constituents are exposed and identified as well. A hidden input is an unobserved primary read only within that constituent, so its private witness cannot disagree with another constituent. A hidden constituent wire has no reader in another constituent. Thus the extensions agree wherever they overlap, giving one assignment of every original gate in T. Acyclicity of the original netlist also permits checking that assignment in original gate order. Cyclic block dependencies, repeated gate operands, exterior observations, and initially seeded outputs preserve this argument.

A coordinatewise majority of three joined assignments restricts to a valid row in every constituent and still agrees on each common wire. Projection preserves closure by taking the majority of three arbitrary preimages. Hence c(T)=0 in the remaining case. This proves S(T) is at least S(U). Taking P to be the actual entering set of an optimal U proves that the best enumerated T is exactly optimal among single merges. Skip T with fewer than two seed blocks. Include the unchanged partition with saving zero when declining a merge is allowed. The statement optimizes one merge from the supplied partition; successive greedy merges pose a different optimization problem.

## Cost and witness

Let h count original wire IDs and let W cover the gates, input declarations, seed memberships, exterior occurrences, and wire incidence lists. Precompute consumer lists, producer-block IDs, and the seed costs. For each of the 1+h+h(h-1)/2 possible P, worklist deletion touches each incidence a constant number of times. Recompute the actual interface using original IDs. Scan T in original gate order on at most four entering words and test the at most 64 triples of interface rows. Each triple takes O(W) indexed operations, including comparison with the at most four candidate rows. Each seed's table also takes at most four evaluations, and its interface occurrence lists have O(W) total length across the partition. Precomputing the seed costs can use the same indexed lists and each seed's original-order gate list.

With dense wire IDs and linear indexed adjacency this gives O((h+1)^2 W) indexed operations and O(W) working storage, retaining a single best union. In a bit-cost account charge for indices of O(log(W+2)) bits and the chosen deterministic array-access model; even sequential simulation gives polynomial overhead. Explicit unused input bits count toward W when an original input assignment must be returned.

Apply the winning union to the original seed partition and pass the exact exposed tables to the existing bad-block decision procedure. For each compatible interface row take a local preimage for its private raw inputs, which occur in no other block. The resulting raw assignment evaluates the original gates consistently in topological order, including gates of noncontiguous blocks. Return the original raw inputs and replay the requested observations on the original circuit. The scoring step identifies a partition, while satisfaction and raw-witness extraction use the existing solver.
