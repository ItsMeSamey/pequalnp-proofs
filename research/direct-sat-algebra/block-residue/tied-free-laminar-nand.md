# Globally optimal two-input blocks for tied-input-free NAND circuits

Consider a finite acyclic original NAND netlist with distinct physical operand IDs at every gate, including gates whose operands happen to compute the same Boolean function. Each gate produces one fresh wire ID. A block may be noncontiguous and has at most two distinct entering wire IDs. Its cost is zero or one according as its exact entering/exposed table is closed under coordinatewise majority or fails that test. Exposure uses all original consumers and exterior observations.

For a set A of at most two distinct wire IDs, initially make exactly A available. Scan the gates in topological order, selecting each gate whose two operand wires are available and making its output available. Denote the selected gate set by T(A) and all available wires at termination by V(A). An internal wire named as a seed is initially available independently of its producer. If B is contained in V(A), closure under every gate gives V(B) contained in V(A) and T(B) contained in T(A). A block with entering set A is contained in T(A), by induction in the original gate order. Each nonempty T(A) is itself admissible, as its entering wires are among A.

## Distinct operands force laminarity

If A has fewer than two members, no gate can be selected. If A has two members, every newly available wire outside A is a strict descendant of both seeds. For a selected gate either its operands are the two seeds themselves, or at least one operand outside A was previously derived from both seeds. In the first case the two dependency edges give the claim; in the second extend the earlier paths through the gate. A selected gate could not produce a seed, since the same path argument would create a strictly positive directed cycle to that seed. This reasoning uses physical dependency paths, independently of any Boolean identities.

Consider two seed pairs A,B for which neither A is contained in V(B) nor B in V(A). There is at most one common wire belonging to their union of seeds. Indeed each pair can contribute at most one common wire; if two distinct common wires exist, no original seed can be shared by both pairs. One common wire x must then lie in A outside B and the other y in B outside A. As x was derived from B there is a strict path y to x. As y was derived from A there is a strict path x to y, contradicting acyclicity.

There can be no common wire outside the seed union either. The earliest such wire would have been derived in both closures through its unique producing gate, so both distinct operands would be earlier common wires. Only one common seed is possible, giving a contradiction. A gate in both T(A) and T(B) would likewise require two distinct common operands. Thus these two cones are disjoint as gate sets.

In the remaining cases, seed containment in the other availability implies cone containment. Hence the family of all nonempty computed cones on pairs of physical wire IDs is laminar. This assertion concerns selected gates; disjoint cones can share an entering wire. Every gate belongs to the cone seeded by its own two operands. The distinct inclusion-maximal cones therefore partition all gates. Zero gates give the empty partition.

## Global optimality and witness

Let any admissible partition of the original gates be given. Each of its nonempty blocks U lies within T(A) for its entering set A and hence in a maximal cone. Laminarity and nonemptiness make that maximal cone unique. Consequently the maximal-cone partition coarsens every admissible partition.

For each maximal cone T, a positive sum of costs of its constituent blocks bounds its cost from above since c(T) is at most one. For zero sum, all constituents are majority-closed. Their natural join on original physical IDs, projected to T's true interface, is exactly R(T) and remains majority-closed, giving c(T)=0. The [original-wire gluing proof](maximum-nand-cone.md) handles repeated uses, outputs, and cycles between noncontiguous blocks. Summing proves that the maximal-cone partition attains the minimum bad-block count among all admissible gate partitions. The same exact interfaces allow lifting a satisfying assignment and replaying the requested output on the original raw inputs.

With h wire IDs and g gates, enumerate at most h(h-1)/2 pairs and scan g gates per pair, plus the indexed cost of initializing the availability array (or use timestamps). Store nonempty gate sets as bit vectors; pairwise inclusion tests and deduplication suffice to find the maximal sets in polynomial bit time and space. An explicit conservative bound for these inclusion tests is O((h+1)^4 (g+1)) bit inspections. Recompute each retained cone's entering and exposed table on at most four assignments. This optimizes the specified partition exponent; the residual exponent can still grow with the input.

If a gate ties its operands, one shared available wire suffices to activate it. For example, take raw x,y,z and gates p=NAND(x,x), q=NAND(x,y), and s=NAND(x,z). Seed pairs {x,y} and {x,z} give intersecting cones {p,q} and {p,s} with neither containing the other. Distinct physical operands are therefore essential to the laminarity claim as stated.
