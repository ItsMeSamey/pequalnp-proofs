# Two quantifiers for incident paired comparisons

Let indexed edges of an undirected multigraph G be partitioned into pairs; the two edges of each pair share at least one endpoint. Write H for a binary matrix whose row space is the cycle space of G, and h_e for its marked edge column. An orientation O picks one representative per pair. There is a deterministic polynomial-time test for existence of an O with h_e outside span(H_O). In contrast, existential satisfaction of signed paired comparison shells on the same graph promise is NP-complete.

## From exclusion to compatible cycles

A separating binary linear functional on columns yields an Eulerian edge set through e disjoint from O. Decompose it into edge-disjoint simple cycles, retaining the cycle containing e. Conversely a simple cycle through e using at most one member per pair permits choosing an orientation disjoint from it; its incidence vector belongs to the row space and separates h_e. Here a simple multigraph cycle includes a singleton loop and a length-two cycle on distinct parallel edges.

If e is a loop its singleton cycle suffices, irrespective of its distinct mate. If e is not a loop, delete all loops from consideration: no simple cycle through e contains one. A surviving edge paired to a deleted loop becomes unrestricted. For every surviving pair choose one common endpoint as its owner, including when both endpoints are shared. Group their two incidences into one local color at that owner only. Every incidence not grouped this way is a separate color at its vertex. Thus two distinct edges violate the paired constraint precisely if their incidences have equal local colors somewhere.

## Perfect matching

At each vertex v install two centers a_v,b_v joined by an edge. For every local color k install a port p_k and a mate q_k. Join p_k to q_k, and q_k to both centers. For each original nonloop edge connect the corresponding ports at its endpoints by an external edge carrying that original index. Require the external copy of e in a perfect matching: reserve that edge, delete its two port endpoints, and apply ordinary general-graph perfect matching to the remainder.

In any perfect matching, a port with no selected external edge must match its own mate. Each externally occupied port therefore leaves exactly one mate to be matched to a center. With no such mates the centers match each other. With two such mates they must attach to the two different centers. One mate leaves one center stranded, and more than two cannot be matched. Hence each vertex has exactly zero or two selected incidences, always in distinct local colors. The selected external indices form disjoint vertex-simple multigraph cycles, including the one containing e. Conversely any compatible simple cycle through e lifts by matching its two exposed mates to opposite centers at each visited vertex and filling all other local pairs. Parallel external edges may be kept indexed; an ordinary implementation may coalesce interchangeable parallel edges after the forced edge is reserved. Graph size is linear in the number of indexed incidences plus vertices. This is a deterministic polynomial-time decision and search reduction. If desired, orient each pair away from the recovered cycle and return it with its original indices as a separator certificate.

## Existential signed shell constraints

A shell on incident edges vu and vw asks whether at least one of X_v+X_u=d_1 and X_v+X_w=d_2 holds over GF(2). Given a signed NAE-3SAT clause on distinct variables with literal values X_v+s_v, X_u+s_u, X_w+s_w, choose d_1=1+s_v+s_u and d_2=1+s_v+s_w. The two failures then mean exactly that all three literal values agree. Allocate fresh indexed edges for every clause, retaining common variable vertices and permitting parallel edges between repeated variable pairs. Every shell pair shares its designated center v. Correctness holds for the same variable assignment in both directions, establishing NP-hardness from signed NAE-3SAT and NP membership by replay. Equivalently, in syndrome notation the signed target is the cycle syndrome of the requests: failures supported on some O exist precisely when that target belongs to some oriented span. This is a different quantifier and target from a marked column outside some oriented span.

For a physical matching implementation, use two fresh endpoint colors per shell with its two coordinate comparisons carrying the required endpoints; realize each occurrence as its vertex potential plus its chosen offset and join occurrences of each vertex by a signed copy-pin path. Each of the four physical slots is distinct even for the shared center. After contraction the common endpoint is recovered exactly. This NAE argument establishes the multigraph promise, without asserting hardness for an additional promise that forbids all parallel edges.
