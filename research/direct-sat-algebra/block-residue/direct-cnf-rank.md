# Exact ranks of the two-row signed 3CNF compilation

Let m be the number of nonempty source clauses and k the number of source variables actually used among n declared variables. Reject any empty clause directly, and pad a unit or binary clause by repeating its last literal. Write A,B,C for the three evaluated literals. With one private bit z per clause, use two shells

    A OR (z differs from B)
    B OR (z equals C).

If A is false, the first row forces z to complement B. If B is false, the second row forces z to equal C. Both requirements coexist precisely when at least one source literal is true. A deterministic extension is z = complement(B) when A is false and z = C otherwise. This existential reduction need not preserve the number of models.

Rail-relative potentials realize a literal with a rail comparison, and its sign adjusts the target of its private comparison. Pin all occurrences of each named potential in a chain with their prescribed XOR offsets. With a rail representative r, read a source occurrence as its recovered potential XOR r. The pin chains make this value independent of occurrence. Unobserved variables can take any value during source decoding (the canonical decoder uses zero). A supplied satisfying assignment may equally be extended using rail zero. Decoding such a lift reproduces its observed coordinates, but may change a free coordinate. Every original shell and pin and every original clause can be replayed.

For m>0 the indexed comparison multigraph uses 4m edges. The active vertices are exactly the rail, k used variables and m private bits. It is connected: every A and B has a rail spoke, every private bit reaches its B, and every C reaches its private bit. Its cycle rank is exactly 3m−k. Including all n declared variables as vertices merely adds n−k isolated components. There are 8m physical raw ports, and the chain pins have rank exactly 7m−k−1 because they are a forest with k+m+1 components. For no clauses all the source variables are free, both ranks are zero, the raw word is empty, and decoding to a length-n word costs O(n).

A different exact count applies when merging equal signed requests on the same unordered endpoints. Let R count distinct signed literals among all padded first and second positions and E count clauses whose padded second and third literals are complementary. There are R distinct signed rail spokes. Each private vertex has two distinct incident signed requests except when B and C are complementary, in which case their two targets on the same geometric edge are equal. Private vertices of different clauses are distinct. Consequently the grouped graph has R+2m−E edges and cycle rank R+m−k−E. The automatic backend may revert to the indexed chart if the grouped conflict components are not bicliques. Neither rank is automatically the dimension left after further free-direction reductions.

Removing tautological clauses first is an optional satisfiability-preserving preprocessing for general signed sources. Recompute all parameters after removal, and retain the original formula for witness replay. Then E=0 and every private request atom appears in just one shell. Its sole conflict neighbor is that shell's rail atom. Hence the grouped conflict graph is a disjoint union of exactly R stars, even for padded units or repeated equal literals. There are at most 2^R shore orientations. Alternatively a syndrome table on the grouped rank has at most 2^(R+m−k) entries, with R≤min(2k,2m). This charges the backend separately; compilation, lifting a supplied model, decoding and original replay require O(n+m) word operations with indexed arrays (or expected time for dictionary indexing). An explicit solver returning an original witness incurs both the chosen search cost and this reconstruction cost. Direct enumeration of active source assignments gives a separate 2^k polynomial-space fallback.

The archived incident-pair notes give the signed NAE interpretation for comparisons that share a center. The cographic-pairs archive also solves a matched-pivot signed-NAE subclass. Here an individual pair uses one rail comparison and one private comparison and can have disjoint endpoints. These rank identities and private-leaf stars describe this particular ordinary-OR frontend. No unrestricted polynomial algorithm follows.
