# Selector conflicts on two original positive-output NAND chains

Use the physical four-row split of corridor_nand.py: sort the two physical entrance IDs, enumerate 00,01,10,11, project and deduplicate preserving encounter order. For each full bad NAND or signed NAND graph both entrances are exposed, hence all four rows remain. The first two rows fix the first physical entrance base to zero; the last two fix it to one. A signed alias can complement a logical value relative to the selected physical base. Common constraints below include all unary and binary projections of the full bad tables, the good-table hulls, and the positive output pin.

**Unbounded score, one or zero compatible side words.** For k≥3 use raw a with the smallest ID, independent raw x₀ and private c₁,…,cₖ₋₁. In increasing order form fᵢ=NAND(a,xᵢ₋₁); if i<k form xᵢ=NAND(fᵢ,cᵢ). Append the one positive output o=(¬a AND [fₖ=p]), using ordinary NAND and tied-input inverters, where p is a chosen constant polarity, not another raw input. In the signed maximal-cone chart every fᵢ for i<k and each xᵢ has its own block. From (a,xᵢ₋₁) the intervening private cᵢ is missing, and from (fᵢ,cᵢ) the next gate also needs a. At the end the cone from (a,xₖ₋₁) also includes the normalized output gate, whose two inputs are signed forms of a and fₖ. No earlier cone generates those two bases.

The f₁ block hides x₀ and the xᵢ blocks hide cᵢ, all giving at most two-port relations. The final block exposes a,xₖ₋₁,o up to fixed signs; for p=1 it computes o=¬a, and for p=0 it computes o=0. Its relation is majority closed in either case. The remaining k−2 blocks f₂,…,fₖ₋₁ expose both inputs and output and have the full NAND graph. Each side fixes the same physical entrance a. Thus the score grows as k−2. Without the output pin, differing sides already conflict in pairs. For p=1 the common hull and output force a=0 and every side zero. This one side word really extends (set every raw to zero). For p=0 the common constraints are inconsistent even with no sides selected. These examples are easy through ordinary output simplification and have bounded pathwidth; surviving side words do not count raw witnesses.

**All pairs survive, yet the full choice fails.** Use raw IDs a₁,a₂,a₃,y₀ in this order. Form yᵢ=aᵢ AND yᵢ₋₁, i=1,2,3. Then form L=a₁ AND a₂, M=a₃ AND y₀, T=L AND M, and o=T AND ¬y₃; assert only o=1. Every AND is an ordinary NAND plus tied NAND, and ¬y₃ may be implemented by a tied NAND. After signed normalization the retained gate bases, in order, are ¬y₁,¬y₂,¬y₃,¬L,¬M,¬T,¬o. Each operand pair generates just its own retained gate. Every raw is consumed elsewhere, and each other operand is a gate crossing a block boundary. All seven gates therefore have four visible rows and are bad.

Select side one on all three chain blocks. Choose the four aggregation sides consistent with a₁=a₂=a₃=y₀=1, y₃=0, L=M=T=o=1. The first physical entrance in the four aggregate blocks is respectively a₁,a₃,¬L,¬y₃. The selected word in gate order is 1111101. For an unselected AND table yᵢ=aᵢ AND yᵢ₋₁ the full binary hull in logical coordinates is exactly yᵢ→aᵢ and yᵢ→yᵢ₋₁. Any selected chain side one, when all controls are one, imposes yᵢ=yᵢ₋₁. To meet any proper subset of the three chain sides, let the sequence y start at one, drop to zero at an omitted edge, and stay zero afterward. The omitted transition 1→0 respects both binary implications. All three common hulls, all four exact aggregation gates, and all selected chain constraints hold simultaneously. Consequently every single or pair restriction of the seven-bit word passes, even with the full common relaxation. With all three chain sides selected the three equalities force y₃=y₀, contradicting the output. In fact the original NAND circuit is UNSAT by direct substitution.

The signed controls are essential for that particular counterexample. A missing ordinary NAND inversion edge in a chain pinned zero at both ends can need a 00 transition, which violates the NAND table's binary hull. The AND construction instead permits the one downward transition at *every* possible omission. Ordinary Horn propagation on the source AND requirements decides this example; the pairwise relaxation loses a conditional implication and its selector model requires further checking.
