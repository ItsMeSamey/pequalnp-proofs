# Binary perfect-matching hull on indexed edges

For an explicitly presented loopless undirected multigraph with n vertices and m indexed edges, matching_hull.py returns a basis of the binary span of (1_M,1) in m+1 coordinates for all perfect matchings M. The final bit is a homogenizing coordinate c. A basis row can be a sum of matchings. The empty-order input has exactly one empty matching and yields the c unit; a nonempty graph without a perfect matching returns no rows. Vertex IDs for this routine are integer labels. The ordinary endpoint-matching callback also accepts general hashable IDs.

**Preprocessing.** Test perfect matchability by ordinary maximum cardinality. Edge i=uv is allowed exactly when deleting u and v leaves a perfect matching. Force every disallowed or previously deleted edge coordinate to zero. Each connected component of the allowed graph is matching-covered: restriction of any global matching containing e gives a local matching containing e. Conversely local matchings in different components combine freely. Local homogenized hull equations with a common c are exact globally. To see this, express any feasible component vector as a list of local matchings added modulo two. The list lengths have common parity c. Pad shorter lists with repeated pairs of an actual matching until all lengths agree, then combine lists position by position. This proof does not require outputting the lists.

**Tight-cut restrictions.** At every nonbipartite node larger than ten vertices the actual splitter tries vertex-pair deletions. A deletion without perfect matching yields a nontrivial barrier shore by Gallai–Edmonds; a disconnected deletion with perfect matching yields a two-separation shore. Otherwise the node is bicritical and 3-connected and hence a brick. Details, including proof that each returned shore is tight, appear in tight-cut-finder.md. Bipartite nodes are handled directly, so its optional bipartite shore finder is not invoked by the hull routine. [Edmonds, Lovász and Pulleyblank](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02579233)

For each nontrivial tight cut, both contractions retain the same original IDs for crossing edges. Local matchings select exactly one indexed crossing edge and glue precisely when the selections agree. Each contraction is connected and matching-covered by restriction. Local binary hull equations on shared crossing coordinates and c suffice as well. In two lists of summand matchings, count occurrences separately for each crossing edge e. The two counts for e have the same parity. Pad the smaller count by duplicate copies of any matching containing e on that side, using allowedness. Pair the equalized lists separately for each crossing edge, then glue. This recovers the original binary vector with the prescribed c. In particular, existential gluing over real coefficients is not being reduced modulo two.

**Leaf equations.** Write C for the unsigned vertex-edge incidence matrix of a connected matching-covered node. Every matching satisfies Cx=c1 and the integer identity sum_e x_e=(n/2)c after integer linear combination. On bipartite nodes Cx=c1 alone suffices over GF(2). One proof starts with an actual base matching M. Contract its pairs, and direct every nonmatching edge from its left pair to its right pair. Allowedness puts each arc on a directed cycle, since M symmetric-differenced with a matching containing the arc decomposes into alternating cycles. The connected contracted graph is therefore strongly connected (one-vertex and parallel-edge cases included). For any solution of Cx=c1, subtract c times M. The remaining nonmatching coordinates form a binary circulation; the matching-edge coordinates are determined by the vertex equations. Actual directed simple cycles span the binary circulations: choose rooted directed paths to and from every vertex, add the closed walk going out to the tail of each chosen arc and returning from its head, and add the to/from-root closed walks at vertices of odd incoming degree. The path terms cancel by binary flow conservation. Each closed walk decomposes into directed simple cycles, each giving an actual alternating flip. The bipartite augmented dimension is m−n+2.

For a simple non-Petersen brick, the perfect-matching *integer lattice* is all integer edge vectors having a common vertex sum, by Lovász's matching-lattice theorem. This is the essential stronger input about the lattice. [Lovász, Matching structure and the matching lattice](https://doi.org/10.1016/0095-8956(87)90021-9)

Here is the precise passage to binary equations. Given binary x,c with Cx=c1 and sum x=(n/2)c modulo two, take integer representatives x,c. Set r=(c1−Cx)/2. Its coordinate sum is even by the second check. In a connected nonbipartite graph the integer column lattice of C is precisely the vectors of even total: along a walk, an alternating signed sum of edge columns gives a root vector plus or minus its endpoint vector, and an odd closed walk gives twice a root vector. These generate every even-total vector. Choose integer y with Cy=r. Then C(x+2y)=c1 over the integers. Apply the lattice theorem and reduce an integer combination of perfect matchings modulo two. Its coefficient sum is c because every matching has degree one at any fixed vertex. The augmented binary dimension is m−n+1: the connected binary incidence has rank n−1, and the extra total row is independent since an all-one edge row is a cut exactly in a bipartite graph. No claim about the integer lattice of an arbitrary real subspace is used here.

Every simple Petersen exception has ten vertices. Rather than recognize it, the implementation enumerates *all* perfect matchings of every nonbipartite simple node of order at most ten (at most 9!!=945 complete pairings), computes their augmented annihilator, and includes its equations. Parallel classes, also on large nodes, introduce no further unknown parity restrictions: projecting to the parity of each parallel class maps onto the simplified matching hull. Its kernel, the even subspace within each class, is generated by swapping two copies in an actual matching containing that endpoint pair. Thus every annihilating class coefficient is pulled back onto every copy. For large nonbipartite nodes the simple underlying graph is still a brick since simplification preserves cut tightness and matching coverage. The implemented star and total equations pull back exactly. For small nodes enumeration is bounded in vertex count regardless of indexed multiplicities.

**Basis and witness.** Concatenate all equations in the global edge IDs, then compute their binary right nullspace by packed Gaussian elimination. For an actual matching of prescribed red-edge parity p, the functional (red,1−p) equals one exactly on a matching with the requested parity. It is nonzero on some basis vector precisely when it is nonzero on an actual summand. Greedily delete every edge whose deletion preserves this existence test, maintaining original width and IDs. The final minimal support is one actual perfect matching of the requested parity: select any passing matching supported there, and any unused edge could also have been deleted. This does not require that an arbitrary returned basis row be a matching.

**Charged bounds.** Write N=n+m+2. Across the contractions at a single connected root, the potential |V|−2 is additive: its two child orders are |S|+1 and n−|S|+1. Both children of a nontrivial split have at least four vertices, so a split root has O(n) leaves and internal nodes. Separate initial components of order two contribute only O(n) additional nodes. Each node has at most n vertices and m indexed edges, though crossing edges are copied; O(nm) total edge appearances is a safe bound. The root performs at most m+1 ordinary matching calls. Each nonbipartite tight-cut search tries O(n²) pair deletions and only one terminal barrier search with O(n) additional single-vertex deletion tests. Thus O(m+n³) ordinary matching calls suffice per hull. The stdlib general_blossom.py searches one exposed root at a time, restarting after augmentation. One root search costs O(m+n²) indexed operations and O(n+m) storage by at most n contractions and one enqueue per vertex. At most O(n²) root searches suffice. [Edmonds, Paths, Trees, and Flowers](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1965-045-4)

At a coarse indexed-operation level the blossom calls therefore cost O(N^7) per hull, plus label lookup and sorting costs. There are O(m+n²) global check rows of m+1 bits: small nodes emit at most 46 each, large nodes at most n+1. Packed elimination needs O((m+n²)(m+1)) pivot reductions and O(m²) nullspace substitutions, each on O(m+1) bits. Other graph splitting, copying and indexing are polynomial and within the same coarse indexed bound. To make bit complexity explicit let B include the explicit vertex and edge encodings, separators and N (charge n even with the integer-order convenience API). All source integer IDs have O(B) bits and no packed field vector exceeds O(B) bits. Live storage is conservatively O(B^4) bits allowing all queued graphs, dictionaries, equations, and Python-style indexing overhead. Even charging a dictionary lookup a full linear scan (O(B) items and O(B)-bit key comparisons), direct indexed bit RAM time is O(B^10) per hull and O(B^11) for a parity witness. Alternatively simulate each abstract operation by full sequential scans of the stored data, charging O(B^5) bit steps per operation; O(B^12) and O(B^13) respectively are deliberately loose deterministic tape bounds. Parsing and sorting vertex/edge labels are included by this slack. These bounds are polynomial in the explicit input and are not practical-time forecasts.
