# Three-coloring on the actual CNF and quadratic interfaces

On a connected simple graph G with n≥2 vertices and m edges, give each vertex three bits. Impose odd parity and all pairwise internal negative clauses, and forbid each equal-index pair along edges. Models biject to proper named three-colorings. Binary clauses introduce no auxiliaries. Under the XOR chart (x,y,1+x+y) the internal violations all equal xy in the Boolean ring. Each edge uv contributes x_u x_v, y_u y_v, (1+x_u+y_u)(1+x_v+y_v). After internal duplicates are removed, the n+3m polars are independent by disjoint vertex/edge supports and independence of the three rows per edge. No additional affine output dependencies remain.

No private-syndrome elimination can start. Each edge scope is spanned by endpoint local scope vectors, and all vectors of each local scope occur in edge scopes because every vertex has a neighbor. The rank-drop plus exception parameter p is at least n: the retained local vertex planes are disjoint direct summands, each must meet the conditioning space in at least one independent vector, and each discarded plane costs at least one.

The common polar radical is zero by nondegeneracy of each local xy on its vertex block. Every centroid input action S preserves the radical of each local form by separate scalar self-adjointness, hence acts blockwise. For each local polar B_v the identity B_v(Sa,a)=0 forces its two off-diagonal entries zero and both diagonal entries equal. Each edge equates the two endpoint scalars. Fullness then forces the output action to be the same scalar. Thus the entire centroid is GF(2), there is one block and the implemented scalar-field chart of dimension equal to the output dimension fails.

The exact optimum of codimension plus residual polar dimension is n+τ(G), where τ is minimum vertex cover. Replace the third edge polar by the sum of the three, obtaining homogeneous generators xx', yy', xy'+yx' in weights 3v and 3v+1 for the two vertex coordinates. Each local xy is homogeneous as well. Over GF(2)(t), scale coordinates by t to these weights. Put any direction-space basis in echelon form in weight order, and divide each scaled row by its pivot power. At zero it becomes the pivot unit vector. The matrix of restricted polars specializes polynomially, so its rank cannot increase; at the generic point scaling preserves the original polar span and restricted rank. Consequently a coordinate split attains the optimum, also after invertible recoding by pulling candidates back.

Let D consist of the vertices with neither coordinate retained. The codimension plus surviving local polar rank is exactly n+|D|, since each singly or doubly retained vertex contributes one. Each edge in G−D contributes at least one independent polar, on its own cross-pairs. Retaining just x at every vertex outside D attains equality. Thus the optimum is n+min_D(|D|+|E(G−D)|)=n+τ(G), by adding one endpoint per uncovered edge, and taking D to be a minimum cover for the reverse inequality. Computing this exact structural parameter therefore encodes minimum vertex cover.
