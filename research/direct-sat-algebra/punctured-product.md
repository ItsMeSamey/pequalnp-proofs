# An exponential character-dictionary obstruction for a quadratic target

Let K be a supplied binary field of dimension m >= 2, with a polynomial basis and a supplied irreducible modulus. Pick a nonzero linear functional and let A be its affine level one. Using 2m-2 free input bits, form Q(a,b)=ab on A x A and ask whether its m-bit output is zero. Multiplication is bilinear in field coordinates. Substituting affine parameterizations produces a quadratic Boolean tuple of polynomial size. No irreducible polynomial construction algorithm is needed for this family, since a modulus is part of its description.

Zero is absent. For each nonzero y, both A and {y/b : b in A} are subsets of K* of size 2^(m-1). Since |K*|=2^m-1 they intersect. Every nonzero output therefore has multiplicity at least one.

Put q=2^m. For any d distinct output character masks, the unnormalized Gram G of their signs over all inputs dominates the unweighted Gram over nonzero field elements in positive semidefinite order. The latter is qI-uu^T by character orthogonality, where u is the all-ones d-vector of evaluations at zero. For d<q the latter matrix is positive definite. Its eigenvalue in the u direction is q-d. Consequently E=u^T G^(-1)u <= d/(q-d). For 1 <= d <= q/2 the feature-span obstruction fails and the integer upper bound floor(1/E) is at least one. Verification of a positive target count must fail since zero is absent. The conclusion includes every adaptively chosen dictionary of these sizes at the root. It does not constrain algorithms inferring absence from additional algebra, querying multiple input restrictions, or combining information from different Gram matrices by a new rule.

At the same time the unrestricted affine-restriction / polar-span exponent is large for this map. Write V=ker(functional). Each nonzero scalar output combination is a nonzero linear functional applied to field multiplication. Its cross-block pairing restricted to V x V has rank at least m-2, because it is nonsingular on K x K and removal of one dimension per side costs at most two in rank. Thus its full alternating polar has rank at least 2m-4. If T has codimension a and the restricted polar span p is less than m, a nonzero scalar combination vanishes on T x T, forcing a>=m-2 by the isotropic dimension bound. If p=m the sum a+p>=m already. In every case a+p>=m-2. This lower bound holds for every direction subspace, before exploiting target-specific equations.

The field encoding itself gives an immediate target-specific certificate, since the factors are nonzero. No hardness of general SAT follows.

## Affine branching cannot repair small dictionaries quickly

On every affine input slice of codimension k, each nontrivial scalar phase has polar rank at least 2(m-2-k), since restriction by one linear equation drops rank by at most two. Its normalized bias is therefore at most epsilon=2^(-(m-2-k)) in absolute value by quadratic Gauss summation. For any d chosen masks the normalized Gram has diagonal one and off-diagonal entries of absolute value at most epsilon (the difference of distinct masks is nonzero). Its least eigenvalue is at least 1-(d-1)epsilon. If k <= m-3-ceil(log2 d), this is at least 1/2. The unnormalized Gram then has least eigenvalue at least 2^(2m-3-k), giving E <= d/2^(2m-3-k) <= 1. Every such slice is still UNSAT, but its Gram rule cannot certify emptiness. Consequently a binary affine decision tree whose only rejecting leaves use this Gram rule with at most d characters needs exponential size when d is polynomial in m. This statement permits the dictionary and affine branch directions to depend on the entire path. It concerns this decision-tree model, not DAG reuse, symmetry arguments or other rejection tests.
