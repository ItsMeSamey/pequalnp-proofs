# Recovering hidden vertex planes from a binary quadratic graph map

On two-bit vertex planes P_i let the edge coordinates be det(x_i,x_j) for a connected simple graph. Dense invertible input and output changes preserve the polar bimap B up to isomorphism; translations only affect affine terms. Suppose for every k distinct from i some neighbor j of k is distinct from and nonadjacent to i. Minimum degree two and girth five suffice: adjacent vertices have no common neighbor, and nonadjacent vertices have at most one.

Compute all pairs S,T of endomorphisms satisfying TB(u,v)=B(Su,v)+B(u,Sv) on basis pairs by linear elimination. At single-vertex inputs on nonadjacent i,j, the left side vanishes and the summands on the right are supported on disjoint endpoint stars. With j chosen as above this forces S_{k,i}=0 by the nondegenerate edge pairing on kj. Hence S is vertex-block diagonal. For adjacent vertices i,j, T acts on their edge line by a scalar t_e. In common palette coordinates S_i^*+S_j=t_e I, where the determinant adjoint of a binary two-by-two matrix M is M+tr(M)I. Connectivity implies all S_i agree modulo scalars. Conversely every fixed M and independent binary c_i give S_i=M+c_i I and T acting on edge ij by tr(M)+c_i+c_j.

The members of the input projection of this derivation space commuting with every member form a linear subspace. Their common M commutes with every two-by-two matrix, so they are exactly the independent vertex-scalar operators. Simultaneous binary eigenspaces for a basis recover every vertex plane. Polar images of plane pairs are the edge lines, including a zero image on nonedges. Over the binary field the nonzero edge representative is unique. Verify independent edge representatives form an output basis.

Choose any basis on one vertex plane. Propagate bases by imposing the standard determinant pairing on each tree edge. They agree globally under the hypothesis because every binary invertible two-by-two matrix has determinant one. Verify all pairings on nontree edges. Any affine input translation appears as a linear discrepancy B(x,u); solve for u by Gaussian elimination. Verify the complete proposed affine reconstruction by evaluating at weight at most two. Its output offset and recovered input translation must both be incorporated into the target. A bipartite decoded graph with an all-proper translated target immediately supplies a witness by assigning distinct nonzero palette codes to its two parts. This is polynomial recognition and coordinate recovery; general three-colorability of the recovered graph remains open to this method.

## Arbitrary target fibers on a recovered graph

Write y_e for the requested edge determinant. Mark all vertices incident to a one-edge as active. They must receive a nonzero input. On active vertices each zero-edge forces equal colors; contract the connected components of the zero-edge subgraph induced by the active vertices. One-edges now impose inequalities on the contracted vertices. A one-edge within a contracted class certifies emptiness. Otherwise a satisfying input exists precisely when the resulting graph admits a proper three-coloring. Necessity follows by restricting any input to its forced nonzero vertices. For sufficiency, lift a proper coloring to active vertices and assign zero to every other vertex. This constructs a witness and the reduction uses only graph traversal after decoding. For example, a bipartite contracted graph is immediately tractable for any target, including cases where the full input graph is non-three-colorable. Arbitrary inactive extensions can change the fiber count, so the equivalence alone is for decision and witness recovery. If every vertex is active, the count equals the number of labeled proper colorings of the quotient graph.

Graph recovery from related Lie algebras has primary precedents. Mainkar proves an isomorphism theorem for the graph-associated two-step Lie algebra with one generator per vertex. The binary determinant construction above uses a two-dimensional vertex plane and its palette pairing; the proof explicitly computes its derivation center and chart. [Mainkar, Graphs and two-step nilpotent Lie algebras](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.3414).

For the all-zero target with unrestricted graph, existence has the all-zero witness, but its exact fiber size is #P-complete to compute. It counts homomorphisms to the four-vertex reflexive star, whose center is code zero and whose other three codes permit only themselves or the center as neighbors. This is a connected reflexive graph that is not complete, so the Dyer–Greenhill dichotomy applies. Equivalently the fiber size is the sum of 3 raised to the number of components induced by S, over all vertex subsets S chosen nonzero. This explains why discarding inactive vertices is exact for decision and can lose counting complexity. No restriction on source degree or girth is asserted for this counting consequence. [Dyer and Greenhill, The complexity of counting graph homomorphisms](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~jyc/880/Dyer-Greenhill.pdf).
