# A stabilizer moving infinity gives an original safe slope

Let U={(x,A(x))} for an arbitrary raw binary-linear operator on the supplied K=GF(2^d). The vertical K-line intersects U only at zero. If an invertible two-by-two K-matrix T composed with coordinatewise F^j preserves U, its image of the vertical also has trivial intersection with U. Writing T=[[a,b],[c,e]], that image gives the finite original slope e/b whenever b≠0.

For every 0≤j<d, solve the binary-linear constraints A(a F^j(x)+b F^j(Ax))=c F^j(x)+e F^j(Ax) on each binary basis input. These equations permit singular matrices during computation. On their binary solution basis the K-valued test b(ae+bc) has Boolean degree at most three. If it is nonzero anywhere, some assignment of weight at most three is nonzero: otherwise subset inversion on small supports makes every coefficient vanish. Probe all singleton, pair and triple basis sums. Every accepted T is invertible and gives equality of finite subspaces from containment. Replay e/b on the original columns. Failure determines exactly that every semilinear graph stabilizer fixes the vertical; it does not exclude safe slopes, which always exist.

This method handles projective semilinear recodings without field-algebra recognition, factoring, eigenvector assumptions or extension parameters. There are d binary systems, each with d² equations on 4d unknown bits, and O(d³) fixed-size matrix probes per system. Thus bit time and space are polynomial in d. The test covers a strict automorphism case: over the degree-six polynomial modulus 67 the columns (39,10,27,9,41,6) admit a certificate at j=2 giving original slope 23, while no invertible mover occurs at j=0 or j=1. Tests include 24 further random projective recodings, check containment on every field value and compare the bounded-support search to full enumeration of the computed stabilizer spaces on their small fixtures.
