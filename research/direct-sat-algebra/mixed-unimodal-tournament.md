# Mixed-loop tournament scan

An input is a tournament, a loop bit d(v), and a set A of marked vertices. Seek an induced subset of odd marked population in which each selected vertex has incoming degree of parity d(v). Let B be the unmarked vertices. Assume A is transitive in dominance order. Order the strong components of B by dominance. The B predecessors of each marked vertex must form a prefix of whole components. In A order their prefix lengths must weakly increase and then weakly decrease. All tests and orders are computable in polynomial time.

Split at a maximum into two arms. Process the increasing arm forward and the decreasing arm backward, merging by increasing prefix length with arm order preserved, processing B components as soon as their position is reached before the next marked vertex that sees them. Track three parity bits for selected populations of the left arm, right arm and B. A selected left vertex sees preceding left and B parities. A selected right vertex sees preceding right and B parities plus tau+1, where tau is the promised final marked parity: among other marked selected vertices, the earlier-index and later-index parities sum to tau+1. In both formulas unprocessed same-arm vertices lie on the opposite side of the current index; the other arm lies entirely there as well. Enforce the promised parity at termination. An entire B component sees a common exterior incoming parity equal to all three processed populations summed. Its even transition always admits the empty set; its odd transition admits a singleton when any vertex has the required loop after this exterior shift. Otherwise all vertices require odd internal degree. A directed triangle works exactly when one exists; a transitive tournament admits none because its first selected vertex has zero internal indegree. Trace back selected vertices and verify against the input. Every step admits skipping. A smallest witness can visit at most seven nonempty blocks by deleting repeated-state intervals, so 21 vertices suffice in the base class.

For exact counts require B transitive, in which case count independent skip/take transitions at singleton stages with ordinary integer multiplicities. For automatic deletion to this narrower class, branch on a monochromatic directed triangle, a one-mark directed triangle witnessing prefix failure, or three marked vertices and one unmarked vertex witnessing a high-low-high threshold pattern. Each is hereditary and excludes the class. Each obstruction has at most four vertices, so depth-k discovery costs 4^k times a polynomial. For the first successfully obtained deletion set Z, condition all its selected memberships. Shift residual loops by incoming selected Z, adjust tau by the selected marked population of Z, track one extra parity per selected z for its incoming residual neighbors and enforce z's row. Since B now consists of singleton stages these updates are exact. Summing all conditioned runs costs at most 3^k times a polynomial as the mask has only one bit per selected z. Add the discovery cost rather than multiplying: every valid deletion set suffices, even for a negative answer. Counts have at most n+1 bits. Failure to find a modulator within budget declares the promise unresolved. No implication for general tournament marks follows.
