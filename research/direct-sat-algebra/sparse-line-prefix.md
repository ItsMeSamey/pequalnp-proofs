# Sub-square-root deterministic search on an even-degree self-adjoint field pencil

Input is a supplied binary field K of even degree d=2a, given in a polynomial basis, and original binary columns of a trace-self-adjoint operator A. The output is s with A+M_s nonsingular. The runtime is O*(2^(11d/25)) bit operations; the implementation uses exponential space for its sparse prefix polynomials. Self-adjointness and the field structure are essential here.

Center A by the trace diagonal equations. If the center is good, return it. Otherwise the kernel-selected-half-line lemma gives λ such that the centered Dickson determinant Δ(λt) has odd sum over t in L*=GF(2^a)*. Compute an odd binary-coordinate prefix of t of length h=floor(3a/25). Its indicator is a product of h terms each equal to a constant plus a trace of a field multiple of t. In K[t]/(t^(2^a−1)−1), each monomial exponent is a sum of at most h cyclic powers of two. Cyclic carries only decrease Hamming weight; use exponent zero for residues represented by all ones. Thus at most Σ_{j≤h} binom(a,j) distinct exponents ever occur. Compute indicators by sparse multiplication with each trace and obtain their parities from the moments below. At each step one child has odd parity; after selecting h bits, scan at most 2^(a−h) candidates and verify the original binary rank.

To obtain the moment Σ_t t^r Δ(λt), the Dickson determinant coefficient for a diagonal subset is its complementary principal minor. A monomial survives the torus sum when the lower and upper a-bit halves of its exponent add to −r modulo H=2^a−1. At digit i the selection count u_i is 0, 1 or 2. For any fixed digit word the choices at u_i=1 sum in one determinant: D is alternating, so every retained principal determinant is a Pfaffian square. Multilinearity of the Pfaffian puts the two singleton choices into one column with square-root weights. Double-retained or double-omitted positions are fixed. The implementation checks odd-order contractions separately, enumerates cyclic binary carries with infeasible prefixes pruned, and includes the endpoint cases. No individual choice among the potentially exponentially many singleton subsets is enumerated.

For nonzero r write the target bits as the bitwise complement of r. A zero target bit permits carry paths 0→0, 0→1 or 1→1; a one permits 0→0, 1→0 or 1→1. Weight each zero by z. The sum over cyclic paths across all targets is trace(T(z)^a) for T=[[1+z,z],[1,1+z]]. Its eigenvalues over the reals are 1+z±sqrt(z). Therefore the total paths for all r of weight at most h are at most 2 z^(-h)(1+z+sqrt(z))^a plus polynomial overhead for the separately evaluated zero moment (one by selected-line parity). Choose z=1/25. Since (31/25)^25·25^3<2^22, the cost is O*(2^(22a/25)). The trace expansion bookkeeping fits the same bound since there is at least one carry path per target. Each path costs polynomially many bit operations; each moment is cached and queried only once. Exhaustion of the residual coordinates costs O*(2^(22a/25)) as well. No density assumption enters this guarantee.

The sparse prefix implementation and original-coordinate replay are in sparse_line_prefix.py. The contraction and dense comparison tests are in half_line_carry_moments.py and check_half_line_carry_moments.py. A separate low-rank algorithm can improve the exponent when the fixed top block has small rank. Neither theorem requires equality to a smaller-field safe-indicator, which fails even after arbitrary relabeling on an eight-dimensional control.
