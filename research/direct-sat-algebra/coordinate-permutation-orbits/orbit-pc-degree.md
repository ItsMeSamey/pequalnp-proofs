# PC degree on the physical four-position orbit

Work over F2 with ordinary polynomial calculus (formal linear combinations and variable multiplication, plus Boolean axioms). On the actual four-color orbit in four-color-orbit-boundary.md grant every degree-at-most-d polynomial identity of the entire unpinned orbit, and add the original zero pins Y_{uv,0}=0. A degree-d refutation translates into a unary four-color refutation of degree at most max(2,2d).

Use unary variables X_{u,c} for c in F2², with sum_c X_{u,c}=1, Booleanity and pairwise exclusion on each vertex. Substitute Y_{uv,a}=sum_c X_{u,c}X_{v,c+a}. This is the actual indicator of the difference on each one-hot coloring. If odd vertex count required an isolated padding vertex, specialize its color to zero before substitution. Edge pins become sums of the four forbidden monochromatic edge products. Every supplied orbit identity becomes a polynomial vanishing on the full product of unary domains, of degree at most 2d.

Any such vanishing polynomial of degree e follows at degree at most max(2,e). Eliminate X_{u,0} with the linear sum equation, replacing factors one at a time. Reduce squares using Booleanity and products of different colors at the same vertex using pairwise exclusion. Each replacement is the corresponding axiom times the other monomial factors and does not increase degree. The remaining tensor monomials are linearly independent on the product domain: at each vertex 1,X_{u,1},X_{u,2},X_{u,3} are a basis of the functions on four states. Since the remainder vanishes, it is syntactically zero. Thus the orbit axioms are genuinely derivable at the claimed degree. Linear-combination proof steps transfer directly, and a multiplication by Y is simulated by multiplying the substituted line by each of the at most four quadratic monomials and summing. This costs at most twice the degree of the original unreduced product, handling zero separately.

Lauria and Nordström, CCC 2017 Theorem 3.6 (full-length Theorem 3.7), construct bounded-degree non-four-colorable graphs on N vertices requiring unary ordinary PC degree Ω(N) over F2. Hence even this strengthened orbit calculus needs d=Ω(N)=Ω(sqrt(D)) on those physical reduction images, D=4 binom(m,2), m=N or N+1. This statement concerns degree, without charging the initial semantic axiom bank. It makes no lower-bound claim for other representations or arbitrary decision algorithms.
