# Translation-block sizes two and four for subcube orbit search

Let R,F be explicit permutations of D positions, with F an involution and R fixing the start bit vector p. Every state in the generated orbit of p is obtained from the subgroup generated by the conjugates R^j F R^{-j}: push R powers to the right in any permutation word and apply R^k p=p. The action convention on vectors sends a high coordinate i to coordinate R(i).

The class where that subgroup has only singletons and pairs as coordinate orbits is recognizable and solvable in polynomial time without listing all conjugate generators. Form the union of all shifts by R on unordered pairs transposed by F. Follow the permutation induced by R on unordered pairs until all reached pairs have been visited (at most binom(D,2)). A coordinate orbit of the conjugate subgroup is exactly a connected component of the undirected graph of these swap pairs. Accept the class when that graph is a matching. On its m disjoint pairs every conjugate is encoded by its swap mask. The full subgroup is the binary linear span of successive rotations of the initial mask under the permutation R induces on these pairs. Gaussian elimination needs only the initial independent Krylov sequence, of length at most m, since the first linear dependence gives an invariant span. An unpaired position or a pair carrying two equal start bits is constant. Every other pinned bit prescribes one linear equation on the coefficients of the chosen swaps. Solve them jointly, multiply the selected conjugates, and replay the resulting subcube witness. Inverse permutations are allowed in a straight-line witness, or can be represented by repeated squaring of R using its order computed from its cycles. This is an algorithm for arbitrary starts satisfying the given R-invariance and arbitrary zero pins in this class.

Already when translation-orbit sizes are at most four, the same decision problem is NP-complete even though the conjugates commute. Reduce ordinary 4-colorability of simple graphs. Add an isolated vertex if needed, making the total m odd. For every unordered pair u,v of distinct vertices and a in F2² make one coordinate. A coloring x has precisely the coordinate (u,v,x_u+x_v) marked for each pair. Start with all colors zero. R renames vertices by cyclic rotation and simultaneously swaps the two color components. F adds (1,0) to vertex zero. Both act by explicit permutations on the 4 binom(m,2) coordinates, and R fixes the start. Among 2m successive conjugates the flipped vertex is specified by j mod m and the flipped color component by j mod 2; since m is odd all choices occur. Thus the exact orbit consists of all color-difference states, with size 4^(m-1), and the conjugates commute. Every coordinate orbit under just the conjugates has four positions (when m≥2). Pin (u,v,0) to zero for every graph edge. This has an orbit solution exactly when the graph is four-colorable. Recover a coloring by setting vertex zero to zero and reading every color from the coordinates for its pair with zero, then discard the padding vertex. The full group may also permute whole blocks; the parameter here always refers to the subgroup of conjugate flips. NP membership follows from polynomial group straight-line certificates as in projection-pin-orbits.md. Both permutations depend only on m. Raw graphs with self-loops are immediate NO cases. The width-four hard class has no known general polynomial solver; the stated result is NP-completeness and does not prove P≠NP.

For the original 3SAT triple-observation construction, a separate Gaussian relaxation takes the affine hull over F2 of the start orbit. Its homogeneous span has dimension Σ_{j=0}^{min(3,n)} binom(n,j) for n≥1: coordinate functions are indicators of patterns on at most three bits, span all those multilinear monomials, and the squarefree monomials are independent. With one forbidden pattern per distinct triple, each pin indicator has its own cubic leading monomial, so these indicators together with the constant 1 are linearly independent as functions. Their evaluation column vectors span the corresponding entire binary space, including (1,0,...,0); hence an odd sum of orbit points hits every pin. The accompanying 8-variable, 18-clause UNSAT example explicitly has v(0)+v(16)+v(18) hitting all pins even though none of the 256 original orbit points does.
