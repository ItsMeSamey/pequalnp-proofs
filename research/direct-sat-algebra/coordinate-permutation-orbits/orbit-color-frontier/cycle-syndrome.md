# Cycle-syndrome trellis for the canonical four-position orbit

The raw arrays and start mask are compared with the exact canonical construction. A zero pin forbids one of the four possible endpoint XOR values on its edge. Unpinned edges need no variables. Choose any supplied constrained-edge order, defaulting to lexicographic, and take the fundamental cycle parity-check matrix H over GF(2) for this edge set. Its kernel is the space of vertex differences, since each fundamental cycle checks that the chord difference agrees with the XOR along its forest path. Component roots and isolated vertices may independently take color zero.

At a cut P|S, each bit of an extendible syndrome lies in span(H_P)∩span(H_S). Write λ for its dimension, equal to rank(H_P)+rank(H_S)−rank(H). The two-bit trellis retains reachable syndrome pairs in this intersection. It enumerates the at most four allowed values of the next edge, tests membership in the next suffix span, and stores one predecessor per surviving pair. Prefix-span membership holds by construction. These tests discard no accepting path; continuation feasibility depends only on the syndrome. The final suffix forces both syndromes to zero. For each cut, space for its layer is at most 4^λ times a polynomial factor; each outgoing layer takes at most four times the previous state count times polynomial work. Backtracking stores preceding layers. The implementation charges polynomial preprocessing and polynomial work per retained state. It does not optimize the supplied edge order.

Backtracking returns actual edge differences. Integrate the forest from zero roots. For physical vertex v and desired toggle bit b, take the unique 0≤s<2n with s=v mod n and s=b mod 2 (the physical n is odd). A flip conjugated by s rotations acts there with the requested bit. The solver replays its full word against both original permutations and checks the entire physical final mask, including every zero pin.

The cut-state dimension is the standard syndrome trellis / represented-matroid connectivity baseline. See N. Kashyap, “Matroid Pathwidth and Code Trellis Complexity,” SIAM Journal on Discrete Mathematics 22 (2008), 256–272. Here the two binary tracks share a four-valued edge list, and the recovered word is certified against the given raw orbit.
