# Deterministic typed compression for prescribed principal-minor syndromes

For symmetric binary W, let P=Σ_J det(W[J])z^J in the commuting square-zero algebra z_i²=0. Use the independent syndrome-row Euler operators E_h and right sides b_h from the directed-syndrome addendum. Q=Pπ_u(P) is nonzero precisely when some principal set of syndrome u is feasible. To compute Q start with 1 and apply (1+b_h)I+E_h+mult(G_h) successively, where G_h=P E_h(P). This follows by conjugating the syndrome projection by multiplication by the unit P=P^−1.

After eliminating dependent syndrome equations, there are g≤2^w vertex types a, and p=g(g−1)/2 distinct-type pairs. Let ℓ_a be the degree-one form summing the coordinates of type a with W_ii=1. Write β_ab for the degree-two form on variables of two distinct types a,b with coefficient matrix W_ab. Cycle reversal cancels all longer cycles, and same-type pairs have zero syndrome. Thus G_h is the sum of the ℓ_a with h(a)=1 and the β_ab with h(a)+h(b)=1. Each generator is an eigenvector for each E_h. The product rule shows that Q belongs to the algebra generated by these forms. Restriction to a typed linear subspace commutes with E_h, since variables of each type are replaced by linear combinations of that type.

**Rank separation.** Initially retain the ℓ_a as linear covectors and let d be the sum of dimensions of their typed spans. In increasing matrix-rank order, declare a pair low if rank(W_ab)≤4d+4p. For a low pair retain its row and column spaces as typed covectors and update d. A rank-r block is a sum of r outer products from these two spaces, so its form lies in their exterior algebra. Each low step increases d by at most 8d+8p. There are at most p steps, giving d≤9^p(g+p). Once a pair is high, all subsequent pairs are high, since d then stays fixed. Every high rank exceeds 4d+4p using the final d.

**Private markers.** Choose a typed d-dimensional subspace U0 such that all retained covector bases restrict independently, using pivot coordinates in each type. Restriction is injective on their exterior algebra. For each high pair (a,b) select one new primal vector of each type. Require both to annihilate the retained covectors, and to pair to zero with every earlier chosen vector (including a typed basis of U0) under every cross form. An earlier vector imposes at most one equation on each candidate type, since its own type identifies the only possible cross form. The codimension on either side is at most 2d+2p. Restricting a bilinear matrix on the two sides drops its rank by at most the sum of their codimensions, so β_ab still admits a pair of candidates with pairing one. Gaussian elimination finds them. Their distinct types and their nonzero pairing ensure they increase dimension by two, since earlier vectors are orthogonal to both in the cross form.

On the resulting space of dimension k≤d+2p, low forms restrict to U0 alone. Each high β_j restricts to B_j+X_jY_j with B_j supported on U0 and X_j,Y_j its two private variables. Restriction is injective on the algebra generated by the input forms. Indeed expand any nonzero expression as a squarefree polynomial in high symbols with coefficients in the low algebra; positive-degree elements square to zero. Among nonzero coefficients select an inclusion-maximal high support T. In the restricted expression the coefficient of the private monomial ∏_{j∈T}X_jY_j is exactly that restricted low coefficient and is nonzero. Supports not containing T cannot contribute.

Compute the Euler recurrence on at most k variables. Sparse squarefree coefficients or a full table give 2^{O(k)}poly(input size) time and space, with k≤9^p(g+p)+2p; the rank computations use polynomial time in input size. To recover an original support, try deleting each vertex from the current principal matrix, keeping each deletion that preserves a yes answer. Feasibility is monotone under enlargement of the allowed ground set, so the final inclusion-minimal ground set itself is feasible. Recheck its label sum and determinant. Witness extraction uses at most n additional decisions.

Identifying every variable of a type with the same variable can lose a live fiber. For W=I₂, label both vertices one and prescribe syndrome one. Either singleton works, yet Q=z₁+z₂ disappears under direct color identification. Retaining the nonzero loop covector avoids this cancellation.
