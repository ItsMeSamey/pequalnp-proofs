# Audit of the group-algebra determinant and a polynomial-space variant

The historical slope_group_algebra.py freezes the off-diagonal syndrome and sums columns independently using determinant multilinearity. A pinned column still carries its group weight. Constant nonsymmetric drift affects the frozen bits only. Conditional parity needs no symmetry; only inference of an odd initial parity requires a separate promise. Its Berkowitz recurrence uses the successive quantities b B^j c in the bordered characteristic polynomial. The determinant ring has zero divisors, making unqualified Gaussian division invalid; this division-free recurrence applies. The implementation uses O(n⁴) ring operations by successive matrix-vector products. Its variable-size XOR convolution uses a sufficiently large power-of-two group containing both packed supports; transform and inverse are exact over the integers before reduction mod two. Transform temporaries need O(w+1) bits, so time O*(4^w) and space O*(2^w) follow for off-diagonal rank w. [Berkowitz (1984), original division-free characteristic-polynomial algorithm](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0020019084900188)

**Streaming exact character determinants.** Let the entire frozen matrix D(u) be any polynomial-time evaluable binary matrix, even nonlinear or nonsymmetric; the true pencil is D(Ht)+diag(t). Remove coordinate-pinned bits from all checks and absorb their values in affine offsets. Put p=rank of the remaining additional affine pin rows, and r=rank of those rows together with the restricted H rows. Reject inconsistent pins. Choose an independent basis of the stacked checks with independent pin rows first. Only 2^(r−p) values u' of the combined syndrome are consistent; its restriction determines the original Ht. For each such word hold D fixed and weight assignments of remaining bits by the character signs of the combined syndrome.

For a character indexed by a binary word c of length r, let e_i∈{−1,1} be its value on the i-th free column of the combined check matrix. Multilinearity over the integers makes the weighted sum of integer determinants a single determinant. A coordinate-pinned row is evaluated directly, including its diagonal flip. In a free row multiply all off-diagonal bits by 1+e_i; replace the frozen diagonal bit b_i by b_i+e_i(1−b_i). This uses integer lifts of both realized Boolean rows, so reducing each actual local determinant modulo two recovers its binary determinant. Sum each character determinant with sign (−1)^(c·u') and divide exactly by 2^r before reducing mod two. Character orthogonality then retains exactly assignments with the required combined syndrome. The proof is the elementary identity Σ_c (−1)^(c·a)=2^r if a=0 and zero otherwise.

Entries have absolute size at most two; Hadamard bounds each integer determinant by (2 sqrt(n))^n. Signed sums need O(n log(n+1)+r+1) bits. Fraction-free integer elimination uses polynomial bit time and space, and the loop streams characters and frozen words, giving polynomial space and O*(2^(2r−p)) bit time (plus oracle costs). With coordinate pins only this matches the historical O*(4^w) exponent at the root while reducing its exponential workspace. With many arbitrary pins, scanning the actual pin solution flat of size 2^(n_free−p) may be faster. All checks are conditioned on the original controls without replacing row-private diagonal variables by arbitrary affine parameterizations inside the determinant.

**Nonlinear oddness sufficient condition.** Now assume the *full* matrix is symmetric with diagonal t_i+d_i. For each off-diagonal Boolean ANF f_ij of degree at most two, impose w_i+w_j>w_k+w_l whenever t_k t_l occurs quadratically in f_ij (k≠l). Feasibility of this finite homogeneous strict system is equivalent, by scaling, to the weak inequalities with right side one. An ordinary rational LP suffices; a rational solution may be scaled to integers. In the pointwise involution determinant expansion, a nonempty matching with j edges leaves n−2j diagonal entries. To achieve full Boolean degree n, each diagonal must supply its own variable and every matched edge must supply two fresh variables. The pairs furnished by the quadratic terms must tile exactly the matched vertices, each once. The summed inequalities would then strictly decrease the weight of that same vertex set, impossible. Only the all-diagonal product contributes, and total parity is odd. Unrestricted linear and constant parts of the off-diagonal ANFs do not affect the argument.

The binary 3×3 example with f_02=t₂, f_12=t₀t₁ and f_01=0 satisfies the inequalities with weights (0,1,2). There are three invertible assignments. Adding just the nonsymmetric constant E₁₀ adds cofactor t₀t₁t₂ to the determinant, leaving two invertible assignments. Thus the oddness claim cannot inherit arbitrary nonsymmetric drift from the affine case. The integer-character parity algorithm itself remains correct for either matrix.
