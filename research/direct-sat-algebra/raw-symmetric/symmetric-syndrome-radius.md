# Sharp proximity for binary symmetric principal minors with XOR labels

Let W be any binary symmetric matrix, H a binary label matrix of rank w on its indices, and u a desired syndrome. If there exists S with W[S] invertible and H·1_S=u, one exists with |S|≤2w. The empty minor is invertible. The bound applies to every inclusion-minimal such S: set B=W[S]⁻¹. For any nonempty J⊆S with H·1_J=0, Jacobi's complementary minor identity gives det B[J]=0, since deleting J cannot preserve feasibility.

In the commutative binary square-zero algebra R on symbols z_i for i in S (z_i²=0), put P=Σ_J det B[J] z^J. Reversal cancels nonsingleton permutation cycles of length at least three. Binary coefficients square to themselves, so
    P=∏_i(1+B_ii z_i) ∏_{i<j}(1+B_ij z_i z_j).
Each factor is its own inverse, so P²=1. For each independent row a of the restricted label matrix define E_a(z^J)=(a·1_J)z^J. This is a derivation, including for products killed by repeated indices. The operator π=∏_a(1+E_a) projects onto monomials of zero syndrome, hence π(P)=1.

Conjugating E_a by multiplication by P yields E_a plus multiplication by P E_a(P). By the factorization, its logarithmic derivative P E_a(P) is the sum of those nonconstant factor monomials whose a-label is one; all have degree at most two. Consequently conjugating each operator in π raises degree by at most two from any input polynomial. Applied to 1, their product gives Pπ(P)=P, of degree at most 2w. Its full coefficient is det B=1, so |S|≤2w.

For diagonal completion of a fixed symmetric D, construct t0 with A=D+diag(t0) invertible by one diagonal pivot choice per leading principal extension. The determinant lemma gives det(D+diag(t0+1_S))=det((I+A⁻¹)[S]). Enumerate at most Σ_{j≤2w} binom(n,j) subsets and check their syndrome relative to Ht0. This is deterministic exact feasibility and witness recovery, including slices of even cardinality. It is polynomial for each fixed w. It applies to arbitrary twists of the feasible-set system by translating the requested syndrome. When D=D(u) depends on the same prescribed syndrome, freeze D at u before applying the algorithm.

Tightness is witnessed by w disjoint 2-by-2 blocks of W equal to [[1,1],[1,0]], giving feasible subsets ∅, {first}, {both} on each pair. Label the second element of pair i with unit i and all first elements with zero. Reaching the all-ones syndrome requires all 2w elements. I+W is invertible, so this also occurs for a diagonal completion with t0=0. By contrast, a nonsymmetric W consisting of a directed cycle on n≥3 vertices plus a loop at vertex zero has only the feasible sets ∅, {0} and [n]. I+W is invertible, and labeling only vertex one forces all n elements at rank-one syndrome. The assertions concern invertible slope completion, not arbitrary target lifts through singular leaf matrices.

For context, group-labeled matroid bases and linear delta-matroid representative sets have related proximity and algorithmic theories. See [Garamvölgyi et al., ICALP 2025](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2025.85) and [Wahlström, SODA 2024](https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9781611977912.31). The proof here uses the binary symmetric principal-minor representation and binary labels.
