# Recovering a diagonal section from Boolean rank-one points

Let T(t) be a supplied binary linear parametrization of a symmetric matrix space, on a basis (so the parametrization is injective). Compute all two-row two-column minors as binary functions of degree at most two. Every rank-one point and zero satisfies all the equations. Restrict repeatedly to the common kernel of every linear consequence in their span, recomputing minors in independent coordinates.

Suppose that modulo the span of the reduced minors every product t_i t_j equals a computable linear form. At every nonzero rank-one point the evaluation vector is then a simultaneous eigenvector of the transposes of all multiplication-by-coordinate matrices, with eigenvalue bits the point itself. Obtain candidate bit patterns by iterated intersections with the zero and one eigenspaces. Simultaneous nonempty eigenspaces with different label words are in direct sum, so there are at most the underlying dimension many live words at any stage. Verify each candidate by evaluating the actual matrix and testing rank one. This proves completeness on acceptance even without assuming the intermediate multiplication tables are associative. All computations are polynomial in the matrix dimensions. Certificate failure is inconclusive.

In a raw symmetric section, choose rank-one symmetric matrices whose diagonal vectors are independent and span the output-coordinate space. Over the binary field these matrices are exactly vvᵀ. Their matrices are independent and their raw control lifts supply a whole diagonal matrix space in the paired dual leaf/output frames. Add back the arbitrary fixed binary leaf drift, select diagonal bits by successive leading principal minors, solve the leaf equations, and check the raw target.

## The complete adjacency example

Use the raw cyclic family with its center-only terms unchanged, replacing the cycle adjacency matrix by the complete-graph adjacency matrix. Its crossing space has independent basis E_ii and J, the all-ones matrix. The rank-one locus spans this entire matrix space, so the earlier linear minor test alone gives no proper hyperplane.

In rank-one coordinates t_1,...,t_{r+1}, the columns representing t_i t_j in the Cauchy–Binet expression for all two-by-two minors are the flattened outer squares of v_i∧v_j, where v_i=e_i for i≤r and v_{r+1} is the all-ones vector. Pairs of standard vectors supply distinct diagonal columns. For each i, e_i∧v_{r+1} has ones at every edge ij and its outer square has a nonzero off-diagonal entry indexed by ij,ik (j and k distinct and different from i), unique to that i. All coefficient columns are therefore independent. Thus the minors span every pair product in these coordinates and the product-reduction test succeeds in every invertible binary matrix-coordinate presentation. Exactly the r+1 coordinate units are nonzero zeros of all minors. Together with the unique full raw symmetrizer this gives a deterministic polynomial-time raw witness construction for the complete-overlap family at r≥7.

For uniqueness of the raw symmetrizer, write its three components as (P,Q,l) in center, leaf, and extra scalar coordinates. Testing the symmetry identity at the i-th leaf requires the matrix e_i P_i+(1+e_i)l to be symmetric (P_i is row i and 1 denotes the all-ones column). Comparing rows and columns outside i shows all l_j are a common bit b. Comparing row i shows P_ij=b for j≠i. At the i-th center coordinate the matrix is e_i Q_i+e_{i+1}P_{i+2}+e_{i-1}P_{i-2}. Choose j outside i and its two neighbors on either side. Its (i+1,j) entry is b while the reverse entry is zero, hence b=0. Comparing its (i+1,i+2) and reverse entries gives P_{i+2,i+2}=0. Thus P=l=0 and e_i Q_i symmetric makes Q diagonal. Symmetry at the last center coordinate makes complete adjacency times Q symmetric; connectedness forces Q scalar. The single nonzero solution is the leaf inclusion. Affine perturbations disappear on polarization, and invertible recodings preserve the solution-space dimension.
