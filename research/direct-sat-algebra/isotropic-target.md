# Exact quadratic fibers by common isotropic restriction

Let k be the minimum codimension of a common totally isotropic subspace for the alternating polar forms of the explicit quadratic tuple over the binary field. A quadratic map restricts to an affine map on every coset of this subspace, including in characteristic two, since repeated-variable monomials reduce to linear terms.

The greedy algorithm starts on the full domain. Pick any nonzero restricted polar B and replace the current vector space V by its radical R. The cost of this step is r = rank B. For any optimal common isotropic space W in V of codimension h, its image in V/R is isotropic for a nondegenerate alternating form, hence has dimension d ≤ r/2 by orthogonality. The next domain R has a common isotropic space W intersect R, of codimension h+d−r ≤ h−r/2 in R. Thus the sum of greedy costs is ≤2k, independently of tie choices. At termination all polar forms vanish. Gaussian elimination both computes the restrictions and supplies a complement at each step. Their concatenation complements the terminal space.

On every coset of the terminal space, evaluate the constant and directional differences of the tuple, solve the resulting binary affine system for the requested output, sum the exact fiber sizes, and return one lifted solution if present. Every count takes at most n+1 bits. Polynomial preprocessing plus 4^k times a polynomial in explicit binary input length suffices. The computed greedy codimension c supplies a checkable upper bound k≤c and a lower bound c/2≤k. A cap on c yields an inconclusive answer on exhaustion. On any affine input restriction the same proof applies in its intrinsic coordinates.

For a single monomial x_1 x_2 the greedy cost is two and the optimum is one. Independent such monomials in disjoint pairs make the gap arbitrarily large additively while preserving factor two: the sum polar is full rank, forcing k≥n/2. Conversely the gated signatures (h x_i)_i have k=1 and exponentially many images. Choosing one nonzero polar kills h on its radical, so the cost here is only two regardless of n. This handles dense invertible input and output re-encodings as well for the 2k bound.

The parameter is the codimension of the maximum isotropic space of an alternating matrix space. Connections with graph independent sets and alternating matrices have been studied by Bei, Chen, Guan, Qiao and Sun. Sparse Walsh output-sensitive discovery is a separate applicable method whose guarantee depends on the size of the entire image (Cheraghchi and Indyk). No priority is asserted for the greedy approximation.

The graph-color determinant tuple gives a complementary lower control. If the graph has matching size t, take the sum of its t matching-edge polars. Its 4t involved input bits split into disjoint four-by-four nonsingular alternating blocks, as each determinant is a sum of two disjoint quadratic monomials. Hence this sum polar has rank 4t, so every common isotropic subspace costs at least 2t. A maximal matching in any six-regular simple graph meets all edges, whence t≥3n/11 by counting at most eleven incident edges per matched pair. This parameter therefore grows linearly even on the raw determinant signatures for six-regular color controls. This concerns the representation before target-specific preprocessing.
