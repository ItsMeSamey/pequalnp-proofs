# Original joint translation with non-binary projected prefixes

join in lift.py takes one raw CNF prefix, two assignments to the same private selector set, a tail CNF, the retained named boundary (including all actual shared raw names), and immutable variables. Its group consists of translations preserving each equal-scope tail table and fixing immutable coordinates. NO_GROUP_LIFT excludes this particular group; SKIP certifies nothing. Two empty prefix projections merge with zero, while an empty and nonempty one cannot merge. The empty boundary has one possible assignment.

Condition each prefix and check its remaining rows are Horn. Existentially eliminate a private variable by resolving its positive against its negative occurrences, discarding tautologies, if its total other-neighborhood has size at most the arity cap a. Stalling declines. This yields explicit Horn clauses on boundary sites with bounded arity. On each scope S of either projected formula, obtain both true projections by at most 2^a Horn SAT tests with pins. A common shift maps one whole relation to the other exactly when it maps all those projected tables: a matching projection guarantees every translated source model satisfies every target clause on that clause's own scope; apply the inverse for the converse. For each scope the permitted shifts form an affine coset or are empty, so convert to Gaussian equations and impose them ON THE SAME global vector, together with tail table invariances and immutable pins. A successful raw shift translates the tail assignment; extend_horn reruns the original pinned prefix with the new boundary to reconstruct a raw prefix witness. Genuinely non-bijunctive Horn ternaries are supported.

join_affine in affine_lift.py recognizes equal-scope affine tables of each pinned prefix. Gaussian projection eliminating private coordinates yields boundary equations which may have unbounded support. Two nonempty projections admit a translation exactly when their normal row spaces agree. For each normal, require its scalar product with the shift to equal the difference between affine constants. Solve jointly with tail and immutability constraints, then reconstruct by extend_affine. Thus full parity relations invisible to binary projections are supported.

At fixed a the Horn eliminator has at most sum_{j<=a} 2^j binom(n,j) distinct clauses. Its greedy scans of neighborhoods and all pairs of opposing occurrences are charged, in addition to n possible elimination rounds. Table recognition costs O(q 2^a) Horn SAT tests for q prefix scopes and O((q+m_tail)4^a poly(n,a)) local translation work plus Gaussian solves. For affine prefixes the only exponential-in-a contributions are O(2^a poly(n,a)) per prefix scope and O(4^a poly(n,a)) per tail scope. The research implementation uses a cap on native probes and reports SKIP on exhaustion. Parsing, per-probe work and Gaussian operations separately take polynomial time in their explicit argument lengths. No recognition claim for all Horn hidden elimination orders or for all semantic tail symmetries follows.

Testing whether flipping a specified bit b preserves ALL models of a strict 3CNF is coNP-complete, even for guaranteed satisfiable formulas. For each strict source clause (u or v or w) of F use a fresh q and clauses (not d or u or q), (not q or v or w) where d is a control common to all clauses. Add (not d or b or z), (not d or b or not z). For d=0, choosing all q=0 gives a model and b is immaterial on every assignment. For d=1 the prefix extends exactly when F is satisfiable and b=1 is forced. Consequently the prescribed flip is a semantic symmetry iff F is unsatisfiable. A distinguishing assignment is a polynomial certificate against a proposed shift. This establishes the membership-test obstruction; it does not directly classify existential unrestricted shift-lift problems.

check.py compares 250 tiny systems against all original assignments and all grouped tail shifts, testing non-bijunctive Horn cases, physical lifts and edge cases. check_affine.py tests joint parity, immutable variables and empty supports.
