# Point-syndrome review and scope

Reviewed mathematical source d85c4339 (the earlier 2451c8a4 was extended with a transition oracle). Equality of H-boundary syndromes places their difference in the IMAGE of a single full physical tail translation. Such a shift preserves every suffix factor simultaneously and fixes all processed selectors. It may change future selectors; they are existential and will be tried afresh. The chosen partial representative already has an original prefix word. Gluing that word to the translated continuation preserves every constraint, because the entire variable intersection is the frontier. The algorithm need not physically apply the shift to its stored witness: each transition uses an actual parity-constrained 2SAT solution anchored on the complete old frontier, and the final path is an ordinary physically consistent sequence of these original words. Empty syndrome sets give a genuine NO only after all branches were completed without cap exhaustion.

The elementary transition solver also satisfies a degree-d moment theorem. Translate one actual 2CNF model to zero. For every subset S of at most d sites, take its implication closure C(S). Drop it if it violates a negative conflict. For every translated model x, the monomial on S either vanishes identically or equals the indicator of C(S) contained in x. The distinct surviving closures, ordered by inclusion, give a unit triangular evaluation matrix for these indicators. Hence evaluating the product of the d affine check-indicators at the genuine closures detects any nonzero value on any model. Undo the translation and replay the original constraints. This proves the n^{O(d)} cost directly; it need not materialize the full moment matrix.

The multicolored-clique reduction is parameter-preserving for the actual recognized chart. Every gate selector is the unique selected hit of a ternary, equal to its original data bit. Odd parity plus the original at-most-one clauses gives exactly one chosen vertex per class. On bank zero, all endpoints and all choice bits cross. For a color, precisely the single parity equation on its initial shift, final shift and raw choice-bit shifts is necessary and sufficient to extend into all later carries and selector shifts. On later cuts the remaining segment of each unfinished chain gives exactly one independent equation with the identical argument. Thus the maximum D is k. Padding classes by isolated vertices is harmless for k≥2; the reduction has polynomial bit size and witnesses transfer to the original selected vertices. This is W[1]-hardness, not a proof of completeness or of an unconditional lower time exponent. The classical source can alternatively be obtained by taking k copies of the input vertices of ordinary parameterized Clique and adding only compatible cross-copy edges.

On the precommitted fixed three-color controls, enumerating the O(h³) candidate triples directly is already a polynomial alternative. The comparison tests the point-clock versus the FD-clock cap, not a complexity separation from clique search, Gaussian elimination, or all two-state methods.

An even closer old baseline eliminates the equality selectors and telescopes their XOR chains by Gaussian elimination. This leaves the original pair-conflict 2CNF with only THREE parity checks. One call to the same degree-three transition oracle on that smaller system solves the entire precommitted family. The full-source execution still tests cap handling and physical traceback across cuts, but cannot establish new tractability on these controls.
