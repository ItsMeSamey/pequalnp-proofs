# Certified conjunctive factors of affine circuit readouts

Let y be the scalar affine readout and z_j an original AND with operands a_j,b_j. For chosen gate indices S compute the squarefree polynomial P = y + sum(z_j + a_j b_j) over S. Raw inputs and original gate outputs are independent formal coordinates during the calculation. Each added equation vanishes on every physical word. A coefficientwise identity P=A B therefore proves that the original output is exactly the conjunction of A and B. Dependencies between chosen gates do not affect this identity. The verifier checks gate and tap bounds and recomputes both sides.

Discovery tries all gate taps in the readout as one packet, then each such tap separately. If P is an affine product with independent nonconstant parts u and v, its symmetric alternating matrix of cross-term coefficients equals uvᵀ+vuᵀ. Its row space is exactly the span of u and v. At rank two the solver enumerates the six ordered bases and four constant shifts, testing the full polynomial including its affine part. This finds every nondegenerate affine factorization in each probed chart until one is accepted. At most t+1 charts are tested for t gate taps. Each formal product has at most quadratic size in n+m. Discovery and certificate verification take polynomial time. An appended AND consumes the verified affine factors. A SAT witness is replayed on the original circuit. The downstream exact two-order solver may decline. Discovery can also decline, for example on the XOR of three independent pair products.

Run python check.py from this directory. The cycle of length six and K4 each use six physical edge bits, with CNF clauses excluding all incident tuples of the wrong vertex parity. Two odd vertex charges give SAT; one odd charge gives UNSAT. All 64 raw words were checked against each actual CNF. The checks cover ordinary AND readouts and the equivalent distributed output A(B+x)+Ax on an existing raw bit. On the four distributed instances, the previous positive-root detector declined, while the certified pair and the complete existing solver both decided all four. The cycle used 12 clauses and 24 distributed ANDs; K4 used 16 clauses and 48 distributed ANDs. Recorded cycle timings were about 3 ms for both methods. On distributed K4 the certified pair took 10.35 ms SAT and 5.98 ms UNSAT, compared with 46.68 and 6.41 ms for the existing complete solver. These are individual observations. Gaussian elimination already decides parity systems in polynomial time. The benchmark demonstrates physical readout recovery, without a complexity separation.
