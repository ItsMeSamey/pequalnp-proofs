# Joint evaluation on differently ordered interval blocks

Two supplied GF(2) matrix programs read the same bits in orders pi and sigma. Both scalar outputs must be one. Suppose every node of a variable decomposition is an interval in BOTH orders. Leaves are single bits; children partition their parent, in possibly different orders across the two programs. Write w for maximum individual width and k for maximum internal arity.

For each block I and assignment to its bits, let A_I,B_I be its two transfer matrices. Store a basis of products A_I tensor B_I realized by actual assignments, with actual factors and witness pointers. Ambient dimension is at most w^4. Leaves check two bit choices. At an internal node enumerate one retained representative from each child, multiply their A factors in pi order and their B factors in sigma order, and form the tensor of the resulting products. Keep a basis of these actual tensors. The parent map is multilinear in the child tensors by expansion of the matrix entries, summing all internal indices of each program. Every independent choice of child words concatenates on disjoint variable sets while both programs read the same physical word. Thus the retained tensors span all attainable products. At the root the product of the two scalar outputs is a linear functional on the tensor; if it is nonzero it is one on an actual retained word. Trace and replay that word. The calculation never substitutes parity of the number of successful words for existence.

Time on a supplied tree is n w^{4k+O(1)} up to ordinary index costs; store actual factors and child pointers polynomially for bounded arity, streaming joins. For fixed k find a tree in n^{O(k)} time: enumerate first-order intervals, test each for contiguity in the second order by its minimum/maximum positions, and check all partitions into 2 through k recognized subintervals. The second ordering of any disjoint common subintervals is fixed by their positions. Handle empty and singleton inputs directly. Binary trees thus include full reversal and recursive independent child swaps, recognized in polynomial time. Original circuit conversion still needs separate costing. Unbounded permutations give NP-completeness already at individual width three.
