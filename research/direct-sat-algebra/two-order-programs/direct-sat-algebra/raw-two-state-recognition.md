# Exact any-order two-state recognition on raw circuits is coNP-complete

Input is a Boolean circuit or an explicitly listed CNF on original raw bits. The question is whether its scalar has a complete deterministic width-at-most-two read-once program in some bit order. By two-state-any-order-peeling.md a coordinate can be peeled exactly when at least one cofactor is constant, or their XOR is constant.

A nonmembership certificate supplies one partial assignment to the original raw bits with at least one free bit. On that subcube, for each free coordinate v give six assignments to the other free bits: two making its 0-cofactor respectively 0 and 1, two making its 1-cofactor respectively 0 and 1, and two making the XOR of those cofactors respectively 0 and 1. The verifier evaluates the original circuit at every specified probe, retaining the same partial assignment throughout. This excludes every possible last variable. Restriction of any alleged original two-state program would realize the forbidden residual. Conversely, if the original function lacks a realization, take any sequence of successful peels until none applies. Nonmembership propagates to the retained cofactor because reversing a successful peel constructs a two-state extension. The terminal residual cannot be a constant (including an empty-domain function). Every remaining coordinate thus has the six witnesses. Only the final restriction and its witnesses are needed: none of the earlier simplifications must be certified. For N active raw names and explicit circuit size S, certificate length O(N^2), verification O(N(S+N)) elementary bit operations up to addressing overhead. Remove inactive names in sparse encodings. This gives coNP membership.

Reduce 3UNSAT by adjoining three fresh variables a,b,c and the three clauses (a OR b), (a OR c), (b OR c). If the old formula F is unsatisfiable the new scalar is zero, with a width-one complete program. Any model of F, fixed on the old variables, leaves exactly majority on the three fresh bits. Each bit's cofactors are AND and OR of the remaining two; neither is constant and their XOR is not constant. Thus no possible last bit exists. This proves coNP-hardness even on 3CNF, with clauses padded by repetition if exact three positions are required. A direct linear-size XOR-AND compilation preserves the same language of functions on the original bits. Hence recognizing the semantic promise on arbitrary original circuits is coNP-complete. A bounded structural procedure may soundly abstain.

A search with SAT oracle can produce an actual order via O(N^2) groups of constant-many queries on cofactor and derivative constancies, retaining the original restricted circuit at each peel. Negative recognition alone never implies that the circuit is unsatisfiable. This is a representation-recognition barrier, not a lower bound against general SAT algorithms.
