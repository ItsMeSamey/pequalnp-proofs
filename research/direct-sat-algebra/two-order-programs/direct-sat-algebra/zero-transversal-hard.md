# Zero transversal dimension already permits NP-complete physical CNFs

For each original 3SAT clause with literals L1,L2,L3 add three new private selectors s1,s2,s3, enforce s1 XOR s2 XOR s3=1 by four ordinary ternary clauses on their one scope, and add three binary clauses s_j -> L_j. Retain all original variables. Each source model extends by selecting one true occurrence per clause. Every accepting extension selects an odd, hence nonempty, set of true occurrences. Projection to physical original variables is exact, though not uniquely counted. Repeat literals to pad shorter nonempty clauses and treat an empty clause by a fixed contradiction. Cost n+3m variables, seven clauses per source clause, polynomial bit encoding.

Every ternary scope is an exact affine plane, so after local affine-hull extraction all its ternary clauses disappear. The remaining binary violations are products of just two affine forms irrespective of how Gaussian elimination chooses coordinates, hence the ternary-transversal parameter b=0. Nonetheless the problem is NP-complete. In the natural chart one may keep s1,s2 and substitute s3=1+s1+s2 per scope. Thus each lifted quadratic violation has one factor on a single original raw variable and one factor on at most two private bits in just one disjoint selector block. This is the established matching-plus-syndrome reduction specialized to the new geometric parameter. It is not a new mixed-XOR SAT hardness theorem.

A guarantee based on small b alone therefore cannot give polynomial SAT unless P=NP. The remaining quadratic rank, chart structure and charged search for them are substantive. Even when every residual depends on only two independent affine forms, a common coordinate chart that makes them ordinary 2CNF is a strictly stronger requirement. This obstruction complements the block-complete family with large b but easy SAT.
