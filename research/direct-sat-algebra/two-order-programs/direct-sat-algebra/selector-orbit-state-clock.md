# A priori history bound from fixed sites and tail translations

Use the exact 2CNF prefix projections and common-translation orbit key on the physical frontier. At a cut of size b let T be the restriction to those sites of the verified whole-tail translation space, with immutable sites fixed, and let r=dim T. Suppose every reachable nonempty prefix relation fixes all but at most c frontier bits. The fixed values and exceptional positions may vary. The number of possible orbit keys is at most
  2^(b−r) sum_{h=0}^{min(c,b)} binom(b,h) 2^(2h+4 binom(h,2)).
Fix the h nonfixed coordinates E and their residual bijunctive relation D. The full relation is the fixed word on the others times D. At most 2^(2h+4 binom(h,2)) possible 2CNF descriptions arise by choosing forbidden unary and binary words on E. Shifts zero on E have dimension at least r−h, and act freely on the fixed-word coordinates without changing D. Hence at most 2^(b−r) inequivalent fixed words are possible (also if r<h). Additional translated equivalences only decrease this bound.

A budgeted recognizer computes the actual tail spaces and their projected codimensions. It checks every generated nonempty child's forced sites by pinned 2SAT, and can decline on a violation rather than infer UNSAT. Every processed layer generates at most twice the previous key count plus initialization, so this charges recognition and failed attempts. The canonical orbit key takes polynomial work per child with no pairwise inclusion tests needed. With n raw/compiled bits, m rows and d=max(b−r), a fixed-order decision and original traceback on the promise cost poly(n+m) times 2^d sum_{h≤min(c,n)} binom(n,h) 2^(2h+4 binom(h,2)). Sorted dictionary logarithmic lookup is polynomial in n. For fixed c and d=O(log(n+m)) this is polynomial despite unbounded physical cut sizes. Solve the accumulated binary constraints for a retained terminal history and replay the untouched raw CNF; separately charge source cover discovery/copy compilation. No all-input width bound or optimal-order finding is assumed.
