# Controls for the quadratic split portfolio

Give each graph vertex two bits and each edge their determinant as output. Target one on every edge enforces proper three-coloring on the nonisolated vertices. For a matching of size ν, a scalar combination of s matching outputs has alternating polar rank 4s by disjointness. If it vanishes on a codimension-a direction space, 4s≤2a. If d independent combinations vanish there, some vanishing word has weight at least d, by prescribing all ones in systematic positions. Thus the surviving polar span has dimension at least ν−floor(a/2), and the joint split optimum is at least ν after arbitrary invertible recodings. At maximum degree Δ with no isolates, a maximal matching gives ν≥|V|/(2Δ) since its endpoints dominate all vertices. This is a lower bound on the split parameter alone.

For a contrasting composition use field multiplication xy over GF(2^m), m≥2, and append k≥1 inputs and outputs. For i<k−1 use z_i+x0 z_{i+1}, and end with z_{k−1}+x0 x1. Every base preimage uniquely determines z for any tail target by backward substitution. The mixed z polars isolate each chain form, the last x-axis polar is absent from field outputs, and the field polars are independent. The common radical is exactly the first remaining z direction: the perfect field pairing forces base coordinates zero, and each mixed term forces z_{i+1} zero. Its affine image selects its own output, so every radical round removes one bit and output until the field base is exposed. These claims are invariant under dense invertible binary input/output recodings and affine input shifts. The residual optimum is m since any lost nonzero field scalar polar has rank 2m and demands codimension at least m. The field recognizer instead uses one quadratic Gauss sum. Zero and nonzero field targets have respectively 2^{m+1}−1 and 2^m−1 preimages with the unique tail extension.

## Exact coordinate optimization on graph determinants

Over GF(2)(t), scale the bit coordinates x_v,y_v by t^{3v},t^{3v+1}. Each edge determinant and its polar scale by the same monomial on their two terms, so the rank of the restricted polar span stays constant for invertible generic scaling. Put a basis of any direction space in RREF in increasing coordinate weight. Divide each scaled basis row by its pivot coordinate scale. Each resulting row has polynomial entries, since all of its nonzero off-pivot entries have strictly greater weight. At t=0 the basis specializes to its pivot coordinate unit vectors. Restricted polar coefficients on pairs of basis rows are polynomials in t; a nonzero minor at zero is nonzero generically. Thus restriction to the coordinate pivot space has no greater rank. This establishes the existence of an optimum among coordinate spaces, including when the original problem is supplied after an invertible recoding.

Let F be the vertices with two free coordinates, S those with one, and D those with none. Align the retained bits at S to one common channel. On edges internal to S this only removes terms. A full-to-single edge and a full-to-full edge always have nonzero polar, and their monomials identify their own edge. Thus the exact optimum is the minimum of
2|D|+|S|+e(F)+e(F,S)
over the ternary vertex partitions. Evaluating all partitions costs O(3^n poly(n)) once the graph is supplied. For a fixed F the optimal contribution of v outside F is min(2,1+deg_F(v)), choosing dead or single. Equivalently the optimum is n+min_F(e(F)+|N(F) outside F|−|F|), computable in O(2^n poly(n)) time and polynomial space. This enumeration is for the structural optimum.

For d-regular graphs with d≥2 denote surviving rank by r. The identity d|F|=2e(F)+e(F,S)+e(F,D) implies 2r≥d(|F|−|D|). If |F|≥|D| then r≥|F|−|D|, else the conditioning cost alone exceeds n. Therefore w=n, with equality attained by keeping only one channel at every vertex. This includes bipartite graphs and UNSAT coloring hosts. No inference about general decision hardness follows from the value of w.
