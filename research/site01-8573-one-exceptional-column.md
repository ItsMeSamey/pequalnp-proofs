# One arbitrary exceptional column

Suppose k≥1 good columns each have one physical input-to-output path and column y is unrestricted. All columns have degree at least two and any two share at most one row. Let m be the edge count, d=|N(y)|. Any constant-free exact-two monotone circuit for the bipartite graph predicate has at least m+k gates. Trim dead gates; paths count operand-pin occurrences.

Call a wire pure if it has no good-column ancestor. The remaining column-bearing part is a tree with pure side operands. At all rows high, y=0, the function is OR of the good columns. On this restriction, each bearing wire must be zero at all good columns zero, or it could not transmit its own good columns. A merger of two bearing inputs must be OR because a singleton good column in one side must pass the gate while the other side stays zero. Exactly k−1 mergers occur. A bearing OR with pure side D is an injection; D vanishes at y=0 and all rows high, hence identically at y=0. Every bearing AND has one pure side, its filter. Along the unique path of z, on y=0 with only z high, the conjunction of these filters is precisely OR of N(z). Each filter accepts all neighbor singletons, and some filter rejects the assignment containing all nonneighbors. That filter equals OR of N(z) on the slice. Select its AND. It cannot also lie on another good column's path since then N(z')⊆N(z). These k distinguished ANDs are on distinct solitary branches before any merger.

For a positive circuit computing OR of h named row variables on a restriction, trace one path from its output for each named singleton. Maintain that each visited wire is false on the maximal complementary row set X and true on the path's singleton. At an AND choose one fixed child false on X, true on all visiting singletons. At an OR each visitor chooses a true child; both are false on X. The paths end at the respective named raws since constants and other raws fail one of the two tests. Their connected union has h leaves and at least h−1 distinct two-child splits, by counting edges even with reconvergence. Each split is a physical OR actually true on at least two distinct named singletons and false on X.

Applying the trace to each distinguished pure filter on y=0 gives |N(z)|−1 pure OR charges. None can be shared across z: its actual singleton support is contained in N(z) by falsity on the maximal complement, and contains two different rows. With the k ANDs and k−1 mergers this counts (m−d)+k−1 gates.

At y=1 with all good columns zero, the root computes OR of N(y). The trace marks at least d−1 ORs. None is a charged pure OR: such a gate is high at y=0 on two rows in N(z), at least one outside N(y), and stays high at y=1, excluding falsity on the maximal complement. The only possible duplicate charges are marked mergers.

Call an injection initial when its bearing operand has no injection ancestor. Some injection exists, since bearing signals would otherwise all stay zero when all good columns are low. The l initial injections form an antichain in the bearing tree. Their bearing operands are identically zero on the y-high tracing face, so no initial injection is a marked two-child split. Nor were initial injections charged in the good-column count. A marked merger must have an injection in both subtrees, as a bearing subtree without an injection stays zero. Both subtrees therefore contain an initial injection. In a tree with l distinguished antichain nodes at most l−1 vertices split them between both children. The l initial injections pay every duplicated merger and provide at least one extra OR.

There must be another AND besides the distinguished k. Otherwise every pure signal is an OR of raw rows and y; an injection's nonempty pure operand vanishes at y=0 and so equals y. Follow any injection toward the output. If no AND follows, y alone forces the output. If an AND follows, it is the unique distinguished filter for the sole column z below it; all subsequent operators are OR. Choose r∈N(z) outside N(y). With just r and y high, the injection forces one, and this filter's pure operand already accepts r at y=0. This is a false positive. Thus at least one additional AND is compulsory.

Total (m−d)+k−1+(d−1)+1+1=m+k. For k=n−1 this is the issued m+n−1. Multiple exceptional columns are not covered.
