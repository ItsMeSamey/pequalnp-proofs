# One extra OR for a single-level monotone threshold-two circuit

Let T₂(n) be the Boolean predicate that at least two of n bits are true. In a binary monotone circuit with unrestricted fanout, call a circuit single-level when no path passes through two AND gates.

**Theorem.** For n≥2, every single-level T₂(n) circuit with at most 2n−3 ORs uses at least n−2 ANDs. The bound is attained for every n≥4. This is an OR–AND tradeoff for single-level circuits.

We use the known universal OR lower bound 2n−4 and the secondary exact-floor theorem that an OR-optimal circuit requires at least n−1 ANDs for n≥6. The latter holds also for 2≤n<6: replace one input by the OR of two new inputs and adjoin their AND to the output by one new OR. This adds two ORs and one AND and preserves the threshold function. Thus a small violation lifts to arity six. The same substitution preserves single-level structure. At arity four the two bipartitions of a two-bit square give two ANDs comparing the two sign groups at each coordinate, with four front ORs and one output OR. Splitting proves attainment.

Suppose a counterexample exists. Minimize n and then the number of ANDs at that n under the OR allowance. We may assume n≥5 because n≤3 is immediate and one AND cannot separate four labels (a single consistent sign axis has at most two pairwise conflicting masks). Drop dead wires and repeated copies. Write a for the AND count. Collect all AND outputs by a tree of a−1 ORs. Every singleton rejects, so no raw reaches the output by ORs alone. All remaining gates are an OR front end from n raws to the 2a signed AND input pins. A raw receiver mask is the set of pins reached from a raw by paths of ORs. No mask contains opposite pins; any two masks conflict on some axis. In particular different masks are incomparable.

If one raw x has a one-pin mask, no other raw reaches its pin, since adding x to that other singleton would change nothing. The pin computes x. Its comparator's opposite input computes the OR of every other raw: it is independent of x, and every pair containing x needs that comparator. On x=false remove this AND and a collector OR. Also remove the opposite input's OR root, which computes the maximum signal of the residual circuit. Its AND uses copy the other operand and its OR uses propagate the maximum. Since the residual output is not that maximum (n−1≥2), its root is unnecessary. Hence at least two ORs and one AND disappear. The induction hypothesis at n−1 rules this out. Assume all raws have mask width at least two.

**Transposition.** Reverse the OR front end, starting at the 2a signed literals and forming its n receiver masks by binary unions. For an OR circuit with p live inputs, q designated output pins and b OR gates, summing fanouts including output taps gives a reverse circuit with b+q−p unions: at each original input or gate with f≥1 outgoing occurrences use f−1 unions. Duplicate wires and redundant unions may be bypassed. Here we get at most (2n−3−a+1)+2a−n unions. Each raw receiver needs its own last union. By incomparability such a receiver cannot feed a different receiver, so take them all as sinks and reserve their n joins. There are h≤a−2 helper joins left.

**Components and colors.** Start with a component for each axis, its two signed literals colored differently. A bank wire means a signed literal or previously constructed helper. On adding a helper that unions two bank wires, merge their components if distinct. By construction every bank wire lies within a single final component. Let c denote the final component count; c≥a−h≥2. In component i with tᵢ axes and hᵢ helpers, exactly tᵢ−1 helpers merged distinct components. Write δᵢ=hᵢ−tᵢ+1≥0; consequently Σδᵢ≤c−2.

The bank wires of component i can be colored in at most 2+δᵢ colors so that same-color masks never conflict. At a merge, permute and align the two existing palettes to give both operands a common color, then give their union that same color. Cross-component masks were always compatible. An internal helper may be assigned one fresh color. Each color class is jointly consistent because its union contains no opposite signs.

The component support of a raw has size one or two. Any two supports intersect, or the corresponding masks would not conflict. These supports therefore have a common component or form the three edges of a triangle. Indeed in the absence of a singleton and common vertex, two meeting edges force every edge avoiding their common vertex to be the edge on their other endpoints.

In the triangle case every component occurs in some raw, so c=3 and the palettes have sizes at most 3,2,2 in some order. At each vertex, the two incident edge classes use disjoint sets of colors, since raws from different classes could conflict only at the common vertex. Within one edge class no two raws can have the same pair of operand colors. The two size-two vertices supply one color per class, and the third supplies one color to each class and at most one additional color. Hence the three classes together have at most four raws. This contradicts n≥5.

**Star reduction.** Write 0 for a common component, and let l=c−1≥1 be the number of others. There are n₀ raws supported only on 0. Every other raw joins one center bank wire and one bank wire in some leaf i. All leaves occur. Let Sᵢ be the set of center operand colors of raws at leaf i. They are nonempty and disjoint, because distinct leaf classes must conflict in the center. Thus

    l ≤ Σ |Sᵢ| ≤ 2+δ₀ ≤ l+1−Σ_{i>0}δᵢ.

Each leaf contains at most |Sᵢ|(2+δᵢ) raws. Within each leaf retain the largest class having one fixed leaf-operand color. The retained leaf operands are jointly consistent, so conflicts among the retained raws must occur entirely in the center, including conflicts with center-only raws. Let k≥l be the number of retained external raws and n'=n₀+k. Their center projections are pairwise incompatible.

If Σleaf δᵢ=1, each |Sᵢ|=1. All ordinary leaves have ≤2 raws and lose ≤1; the unique exceptional leaf has ≤3 raws. If it has three, their leaf operands are pairwise incompatible, hence its axis count is at least two. So total lost ≤Σleaf tᵢ. If Σleaf δᵢ=0, all leaves have two colors and all but possibly one have |Sᵢ|=1. Each of those loses ≤1. The exceptional leaf with two center colors has at most four raws and loses at most two. Total loss again is ≤Σleaf tᵢ except possibly if one single-axis leaf had four and retained two. In this last case the loss is ≤1+Σleaf tᵢ and k≥l+1.

**The smaller circuit.** Retain only center axes used with both signs across the n' projected outputs. This preserves every pairwise conflict. There are t'≤t₀ such axes. We may assume n'≥2: if n'=1 then n₀=0, l=k=1, both components are trees and their color pairs already give n≤4. Hence t'≥1. On projecting the center bank, each of its original t₀−1 component-merging joins either has an empty operand and can be bypassed, or joins two previously disjoint surviving components. Projection cannot create connections between components which had previously been distinct. Thus at most t'−1 such joins survive. At most δ₀ internal helpers survive. The n₀ center-only receivers need at most n₀ additional joins; each external retained raw is already a bank wire in the center. Reverse this circuit from 2t' signed literals to n' designated masks; both signs of each retained axis are live. The OR front of the resulting physical circuit has at most (t'−1+δ₀+n₀)+n'−2t' ORs. Add t' ANDs and a t'−1 OR collector. Pairwise conflict and singleton consistency make the circuit compute T₂(n'). Its OR excess over 2n'−4 is at most δ₀+2−k≤1.

If n'=n, the center circuit has fewer ANDs than the chosen counterexample, a contradiction. If n'<n, induction gives t₀≥t'≥n'−2. In the ordinary loss case, adding the other axes gives a≥n−2. In the exceptional four-raw loss case k≥l+1 gives δ₀+2−k≤0: the smaller circuit is OR-optimal and therefore t₀≥n'−1. This extra AND absorbs the one exceptional lost raw and again gives a≥n−2.

The argument also applies to an all-clean circuit whenever one invokes the established normalization into collected clean terminals at no increase in either gate count. It does not handle arbitrary dirty intermediate ANDs.
