# Three incidence layers for monotone threshold two

Let C(n) be the minimum binary monotone AND/OR gate count for threshold two with unrestricted fanout. For all sufficiently large n the following construction gives

    C(n) ≤ 2n + √(2n) + (5/4) 2^(9/10) n^(3/10) + o(n^(3/10)).

Put t=√(2n), v=ceil(t+2·8^(-4/5)t^(3/5)+t^(29/50)), w=ceil((8v³)^(1/5)), and z=ceil(v^(9/20)). Sample a uniform v-edge simple graph R on w vertices. A candidate input label is an unordered pair of disjoint edges. Keep one candidate for each distinct four-vertex union.

The expected number of intersecting edge pairs is O(v²/w). Distinct matchings on one four-set give a distinct four-cycle for each pair of matchings. Their collision losses are therefore at most the cycle count. Its mean is at most 3 binom(w,4)(v/binom(w,2))^4 ≤ 2v⁴/w⁴·(1+O(1/w)). The probability of a degree exceeding √v is exponentially small: for k=ceil(√v), union bound over a vertex and k incident edges gives at most w·(2ev/(wk))^k. Conditioning on maximum degree ≤√v therefore preserves the stated expectation bounds up to an exponentially small relative error (for integer √v the same estimate also bounds the slightly larger event). Choose one graph with losses at most the sum of these conditional means. Let F be its retained four-sets. A fixed vertex pair occurs in at most v+Δ² of them: either it is one of the two edges of the retained matching, or it meets two separate edges. Thus maximum pair codegree is O(v), and total pair codegree is 6|F|=O(v²).

Uniformly inject the w vertices into the distinct edges of a complete graph on z vertices, possible since binom(z,2)≥w. Discard any member of F whose four image edges intersect each other, losing O(v²/z) in expectation. Keep one remaining member for each eight-vertex endpoint union. For two distinct four-sets sharing r vertices, expose the first four edge images. If they are disjoint, then to collide, the other 4−r images must all lie on the 2(4−r) endpoints outside the common images. The conditional probability is O(z^(-2(4−r))) by sampling without replacement. For r=3 a collision would require repeating the one remaining image, impossible by injectivity. For r=2 the number of pairs is O(v³) by the maximum and total pair codegrees. For r=0,1 it is O(v⁴). Some injection thus loses at most O(v²/z+v³/z⁴+v⁴/z⁶)=O(v^(31/20)) more candidates.

After both selections the surviving count is at least binom(v,2)−2v⁴/w⁴−O(v^(31/20)). Since w≥(8v³)^(1/5), the leading loss is at most 2·8^(-4/5)v^(8/5). Expanding around t, with v=t+O(t^(3/5)), this is 2·8^(-4/5)t^(8/5)+O(t^(6/5)), while binom(v,2)−n is at least 2·8^(-4/5)t^(8/5)+t^(79/50)−O(t). Since 79/50>31/20, at least n labels survive for every sufficiently large integer n. Select any n.

Let v', w', z' count respectively the used first-graph edges, their incident vertices, and the endpoints of the images of those vertices. Compute each used first-graph edge's label OR for 2n−v' gates, each incident vertex's edge OR for 2v'−w', and each used image endpoint's vertex OR for 2w'−z'. A singleton activates eight final vertices. Two distinct labels activate at least nine, so a full threshold-nine module computes the desired function. Tracking the first nine thresholds by their prefix recurrence costs at most 17z' binary gates (constants simplify). Thus the circuit costs at most 2n+v+w+16z. Substituting the parameters gives (v−t)+w=(5/4)8^(1/5)t^(3/5)+o(t^(3/5)), proving the displayed coefficient.

This is an unrestricted upper bound. The companion matching-codegree-constant.md supplies a matching lower coefficient for the separate three-layer ledger with a full threshold-nine module. It charges only active symbols. If the module also has unused inputs, fixing those inputs false recovers the same lower bound on its active-input cost.
