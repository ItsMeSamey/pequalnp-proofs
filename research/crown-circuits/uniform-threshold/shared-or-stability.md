# Stability of the shared-group counting bound

These deductions concern a **regular globally minimum** monotone threshold-two circuit on n inputs, of size 2n+s, with exactly two distinct OR readers at each input. They use the normalization and the star lemma of shared-or-cut.md. No assertion about the existence of regular minimizers at arbitrary n is required. Write T=s+1, h for the number of shared OR groups, d=T−h, and K=4+3T+binom(T,2)−n. Assume h>0. For asymptotic statements s=√(2n)+O(1), hence K=O(h), h∼√(2n), as follows from the first inequality below.

For distinct shared groups let U<W if forcing just U true with all raw inputs zero makes W true. DAG order and monotonicity make this a strict partial order. Denote by p_U,t_U the numbers of strict predecessors and successors. Write q for the number of incomparable shared pairs without a shared raw, e for the number of raw edges avoiding shared groups, b_U for the number of singly-used raw neighbors of U, and a for the number of branching AND gates. Putting back the unused terms in the frozen summation gives

    K ≥ d(d+5)/2 + (4−e) + q + h a
        + Σ_W (f_W−2)(h−1−p_W)
        + Σ_{AND A, f_A≥2} (f_A−2)(h−number of probes activating A).

Indeed the shared base units not used in the flow are exactly the incomparable missing pairs. The unused shared-OR excess counts every probe other than W and its predecessors. Every shared AND spends one fanout-surplus unit per probe simply from f−1 versus f−2. The remaining terms in the frozen inequality are nonnegative. In particular d=O(√h), a=O(1), q=O(h). The pointwise star bound gives deg(U)≤T+2−p_U. Its deficits have exact sum K−d(d+5)/2+q−(4−e), since the number of comparable pairs plus the number of shared raw edges is binom(h,2)−q.

**Common-neighbor obstruction.** If U<W, no raw-incidence vertex V can neighbor both. On singleton x=UV, groups U and V are already true and W is therefore true by probe monotonicity. Adding raw y=WV has no effect at either occurrence, contradicting threshold two. Thus every distance-two nonedge in the shared graph is one of the q exceptional pairs. This graph is within 3q edits of a disjoint union of cliques. At each step take a pivot v and remove its closed neighborhood as one clique, filling missing internal pairs and deleting crossing edges. Summed over possible pivots, each induced path on three vertices contributes once to the fills and twice to the deletions. Choose a pivot with deletions at most twice the fills. Each filled pair was an original distance-two nonedge and is filled only once.

**Loss along comparable probes.** For each probe U let B_U be the entire right hand side of the frozen probe flow inequality, namely f_U plus f_W−1 over strict successors W plus max(f_A−2,0) over active ANDs. In the star proof choose c_U charged singly used neighbors, all but at most one if deg(U)≥3. If deg(U)≤2 choose none. Then b_U≤c_U+2, each charge uses a distinct active-to-dark boundary pin entering a distinct AND, and J_U=B_U−c_U≥0. Summing these definitions using n=e+m+Σ b_U, where m is the shared-edge count, gives Σ J_U≤K+h. In detail Σ B≤2h+binom(h,2)−m+h d, and Σ c≥n−e−m−2h. The stated bound follows by subtraction and h≤T.

For each comparable U<W, c_W≤J_U+a. Every charged neighbor V of W has a dedicated reader AND whose other pin is active under W, hence active under U. By the common-neighbor obstruction V cannot also neighbor U. If V is false under U, the active pin is therefore a boundary pin unused by U's charges. If V is true under U, the AND is active. A singly-used active AND contributes −1 to the exact flow balance, while B_U counted zero. This also holds for an active output AND, whose deficit is two. The rest of the internal active-flow balance has nonpositive discarded terms, so B_U−c_U pays for all the unused boundary pins and all distinct such private active ANDs simultaneously. The remaining readers number at most a. Distinct charges of W give distinct ANDs. Finally B_W≥t_W, giving t_W≤J_U+J_W+a whenever U<W.

Put L=ceil(√(K+h)). At most L groups have J_U>L. Among remaining groups put in H those with t_U>2L+a. No good element precedes any member of H. Thus comparable pairs either touch a bad group (at most hL), start at a good group outside H (at most h(2L+a)), or go from H to outside H (at most |H|(h−|H|)). In particular there are at most h²/4+h(3L+a) comparable pairs. Consequently

    m ≥ binom(h,2)−q−h²/4−h(3L+a).

For s=√(2n)+O(1), at least h²/4−O(h^(3/2)) labels have two shared endpoints. Outside O(√h) exceptional vertices, the probe order admits a partition into two sets with O(h^(3/2)) within-set comparable pairs. The statements characterize necessary structure. They do not give a stronger third-order size lower bound.
