# Sharp AND count for single-level threshold two at OR excess two

A single-level binary monotone circuit for T2(n) with OR count O≤2n−2 requires at least n−3 ANDs. For n≥6 a 2-by-3 grid at six labels followed by raw splits attains (O,A)=(2n−2,n−3). The circuit model has no input-to-output path containing two AND gates. Collected-clean circuits with no singleton-hot AND reduce to this model. Thus an optimal size-27 eleven-input circuit with seven ANDs must contain a singleton-hot AND.

Use the transposition, projection, palette, and e=0,1 bounds of fixed-extra-or-single-level.md, where e=O−2n+4. The only open case is e=2 and, by the previously proved bound, deficit n−a=4. Select a smallest counterexample in (n,a). One-pin deletion gives a smaller counterexample or an impossible deficit at e≤1. Otherwise the a signed axes support n pairwise conflicting receiver masks, each a last join of at most two helper-bank wires. There are h≤a−1 helpers and every axis is effective.

If the helper-bank component supports form a triangle, the existing palette proof gives n≤3+2+1=6 and a≥3, finishing. If they share a common center with at least one peripheral component, use the star projection there. The projected deficit at e'≤2 plus the debit D of discarded labels minus discarded axes bounds the original deficit. For r=2−e' one has D≤r+floor(r²/4). If r=0 the original deficit cannot increase, and projection reduces n or a. If r=1 the e'=1 deficit is at most two and the debit is at most one. If r=2 the e'=0 deficit is at most one. In order to attain four every inequality in the debit bound must therefore be equality and the debit must be three. In the notation there, sum(q_i+u_i)≤2 and sum(q_i u_i) must be one. Thus some peripheral component has q_i=u_i=1. The bound subtracted only one axis per peripheral component in estimating D; equality forces them all to have just one axis. But q_i counts internal helper joins. A single signed axis has only its two opposite literal wires; a consistent nonredundant internal join cannot exist. Contradiction.

If there is just one helper-bank component then h=a−1 and all helpers merged distinct components, with no internal joins. Thus every component encountered in this construction has a two-color palette. Delete the last helper H and the d receivers using it. The standard last-helper lemma gives d≤2 and an e'≤1 projected circuit on N=n−d surviving labels using at most a axes. Since n=a+4 and the deficit at e'≤1 is at most two, equality forces d=2, N=a+2, e'=1, and all a axes remain effective. The pre-H components X,Y therefore both have live axes. Single-component receivers cannot occur in both components, since such a pair would be compatible. Call X the component containing all such receivers (either if none). All remaining receivers span X and Y. They use one existing wire from each, not a join of two within one component. There is at least one cross receiver; otherwise Y has no effective axes.

Partition the cross receivers by the colors of their Y operands. At most two lie in each class because their X operands must conflict pairwise and have a two-color palette. Write k for the larger class size and l≤k for the other. Keep the k receivers in the larger class and the n0 receivers supported wholly on X; then project to X. All pairs still conflict there. The k selected external outputs already exist as single X bank wires. If t effective axes remain, the forest bank needs at most t−1 helpers, and at most n0 final joins are needed. Back-transposition gives OR excess at most 2−k. The degenerate case n0+k≤1 would force N≤2, contrary to N=a+2 with a≥1. If k=1, the N−l survivors have deficit at most two relative to at most a−|Y| axes. Hence |Y|≤l≤1. If k=2, the deficit is at most one, giving |Y|≤l−1≤1. Thus Y is a single axis, and both colors occur in cross receivers (indeed l=k). Both signs of its axis correspond to its two distinct colors.

In the original bank H joins a literal of Y, say positive, to one X wire R. Its two receiver users have other operands conflicting with one another, each compatible with H. Both operands thus lie in X: operands from different pre-H components do not conflict, and two conflicting Y literals cannot both be compatible with H. Retain all original X-only receivers, all t0≥1 pre-H cross receivers using the positive Y sign, and both H users; discard precisely the l0≤2 pre-H receivers using the negative sign. Projection to X retains every required conflict since all kept Y operands are positive or absent. The projected X bank needs at most a'−1 joins on its effective a'≤a−1 axes; at most n0 original X-only final joins, and two additional joins (one with R for each H user), suffice. The t0 external outputs are already X wires. For N'=n0+t0+2=n−l0 labels the new OR excess is at most 2−t0≤1. Therefore N'−a'≤2. It follows that n−a≤l0+1≤3, contradicting the assumed four. Axis permutation and palette use above make no assertion beyond the single-level model.
