# Star-forest restrictions at twelve inputs, 29 gates and seven ANDs

The regularization of twelve-unrestricted-eight-and.md works unchanged at the new 22-OR/seven-AND composition: any three-loss single-raw deletion violates C(11)=27, so every raw reads two ORs and every AND has at least two exclusive singleton labels per side. The decoder has v−2 ORs. The pure-doubleton branching telescope again prohibits six losses on deletion of its two labels. With the only remaining possibility (two OR exits), five ORs disappear, giving T10/24/seven ANDs, excluded by ten-unrestricted-seven-and.md. Thus every pure doubleton is private and clean. These products are different for different pure doubletons, since overlaps violate the exclusive-side lower bound and disjoint shared products save six gates as in twelve-unrestricted-eight-and.md. If every pure group is a doubleton, the P pure groups and M mixed raw-bearing groups cost at least P−1+M decoder ORs, exceeding v−2. In particular some pure group has at least three labels.

A bare d-star has local floor 2d−4. Adding the first remote costs at least order minus one, the remainder full order. This excludes d≥5 from a star forest at v−2. A nonstar component with d≥4 has order-minus-one floor, as does a bare four-star with any remote nonstar, so any degree-four vertex forces a star forest. In a star forest let L be the degree-one vertex count, p the short-path count, m the number of stars of degree at least three. Then v=L+p+m. The L singleton-bearing mixed injections and p short centers cost L+p. For the latter use the distinct mixed groups or the distinct private clean terminals; a pair inside a large star survives without every such terminal.

Pin every other component false and the center of a selected d-star true. Only d+(m−2) ORs can remain. Its raw leaves each occur at precisely one distinct leaf OR. At least the earliest two such gates fold. The first has a constant other operand. Before the second only one raw has appeared; if its other operand were true on that first singleton then adding the second raw would be invisible and the output could not compute threshold two. That operand is thus identically false on its one available bit (also by monotonicity). This remains true with arbitrary additional AND/OR gates between the leaf readers. Therefore 2d−4≤d+m−4, i.e. m≥d. With twelve total edges, a four-star would require three further stars of degree at least three, consuming at least thirteen edges. Thus every candidate (including a nonstar original) has maximum degree at most three.

In a star forest d=3 forces m≥3. The complete star lists left are four three-stars, three three-stars plus a short path and an independent edge, or three three-stars and three independent edges. Any pure three-star with two or more direct OR exits is impossible here: erasing its three leaf injectors and two exits (absorption forbids coincidence) leaves v'−3 ORs on the remaining components, which include two three-stars. They need v'−2 by the six-OR two-star bound and full remote increments. A mixed center already pays a separate OR beyond its bank.

For the seven-AND C14/34 reduction at a branching doubleton, the E=12 residual has decoder allowance N−2. Restoring 24−N ORs gives 22 ORs at most, and C(12)=29 forces 22 ORs with seven ANDs. The preceding restrictions therefore apply to a regularized representative. A protected normalized incidence subgraph must be justified separately before transferring any of its edges into that representative. This does not prove nonexistence of T12/29/A7.

## Dirty minimization and the remaining star forests

Choose a size-minimal candidate with as few singleton-hot ANDs as possible. The regularization and terminalization above preserve the full output and all singleton traces on retained ANDs. If a private pure triple U=x OR y OR z has a dirty sole AND reader U AND P, the two exclusive labels leave exactly one hot label x. On the whole Boolean cube P dominates x by monotonicity. Replace the triple bank and its private product by x OR ((y OR z) AND P). The same two OR gates and one AND suffice, the new middle product is clean, and the old product's downstream value is identical. The old bank's internal ORs have no other users by the flattened-group definition. This contradicts dirty minimality. All private pure groups (now necessarily of size two or three) consequently feed clean products.

No two pure groups can share a private product. They would have to be disjoint, since otherwise the product is hot at their common raw. If the smaller has size two, erasing it saves its bank, both other raw readers, the product, a collector OR and at least one additional bank gate of the now-unused partner, yielding T10 at most 23. For two triples the corresponding minimum loss is 2+3+1+1+2=9, giving T9 at most 20. Reassociate the other raw-bearing groups to expose these distinct tagged ORs, and use a surviving positive outside both groups. Thus if no pure group branches, all P pure vertices require at least P−1 collector ORs, while the M mixed vertices require M other ORs, exceeding v−2. Every selected dirty-minimal regular representative must contain a branching pure triple.

Such a triple U has exactly two OR exits. Erasing its three labels yields at least 6+(f−1)+w gate losses, where f is its fanout and w counts one-sided dark ANDs. A direct AND reader of U is one-sided or redundant, since any partner vanishing when all three raws are false would imply U. The size floor 21 at nine allows eight losses. At f=3 there can be no dark one-sided AND. All three direct exits are ORs and vanish in addition to U's two bank ORs and three external raw-reader ORs (group absorption excludes overlap). This leaves fourteen ORs and seven ANDs, below the secondary floor at nine. For f=2 with a direct AND, there is no remaining dark fanout excess or extra one-sided AND. The only initial outside-bank dark nonraw source is that AND: a distinct pure OR group cannot contain two of the three raws by incidence simplicity, a mixed OR group with no prior dark nonraw operand remains live, and the other exit of U is a live OR (no erased raw can enter it by absorption; a dark nonraw partner would imply U). There is therefore just one dark strand after U's AND reader. At a mixed OR that strand may be joined to an erased raw and continue, but cannot branch because all fanout credit is exhausted; it cannot later enter a both-dark AND because both operands would have to come from this one unbranched strand. Raw inputs all have only OR readers; the other direct consumer of U is live. There is exactly one lost AND and eight gate losses, contrary to the unique four-AND composition at T9/21. Hence f=2 and both exits are ORs, as claimed.

Every previously listed star forest has at least three triples. Deleting a branching one costs five decoder ORs (its three distinct leaves and both exits). The remaining forest includes two triples and needs its order v' minus two, yet only v'−3 ORs remain. Thus no star forest is possible in the regular representative selected to minimize the number of dirty ANDs among all size/composition candidates. Every candidate would imply the existence of such a representative with a nonstar component and a branching pure triple. The equal-cost rewrite is not asserted to preserve a fixed original bank through subsequent normalization. The earlier maximum-degree-three restriction on regular graphs does not use that rewrite. The nonstar may contain that triple or may be disjoint from a three-star centered at it. These restrictions remain conditional on the cited finite smaller-arity bounds and do not decide the unrestricted twelve-input composition.
