# A sharing requirement for the 507 and K4 decoder

The graph on five flags is the complete tripartite graph with parts of sizes 1, 2, 2. The other four flags form K4. Negative inputs are single edges, and positive inputs include the union of any two distinct edges. A putative decoder uses at most 15 binary AND and OR gates, including at most seven ORs.

Pin one entire component to zero. The large component requires at least eight gates. Its central flag participates essentially: a negative edge between the other two parts becomes positive when the center is added. Pinning the center to one leaves threshold two on four independent flags, since the four star edges can be independently selected. Zero rejects by monotonicity. This threshold requires seven gates. The four-flag component likewise requires seven gates: its pairs reject and its triples accept, giving threshold three on four flags by monotonicity and the dual bound.

A wire is bilateral when it can be true in each of the two zero-pinned projections. The first bilateral wire must be an OR of exclusive wires from opposite components. The gate therefore simplifies in both projections. If D gates simplify in both projections and S survive in both, the sum of the two projected sizes is at most 15−D+S. Since the two minima sum to 15, S≥D. In particular some later gate must have two bilateral operands and survive both projections. This holds for either gate type. A pure-component cross AND also simplifies in both projections and requires an additional shared gate. Counting only ORs gives the weaker bound S_OR≥D_OR−1 from the separate four- and two-OR floors. K4 admits a two-OR, five-AND decoder.

For each fixed singleton edge from either component, its two flags may instead be pinned to one and all other flags of that component to zero. The resulting function rejects the opposite zero and accepts all opposite edges. Choose any resolution of every AND into one of its operands for which zero still rejects. Such a resolution exists by following a false operand at each false AND on zero. The resolved function is a disjunction that accepts all opposite edges, so its set of variables is a vertex cover. Both graphs have minimum vertex cover three. Each such resolution therefore retains at least two OR gates.
