# Four remote flags cannot supply the OR saving for a quartic center

Consider a regular size-34 eight-AND fourteen-input host with two components. Suppose one contains a degree-four flag and the other K has four vertices. The decoder on q flags has q−2 ORs. After erasing K, ordinary peeling to the degree-four star costs one OR per central vertex outside its closed neighborhood, and the star needs four ORs. Thus K may cost at most three ORs. The following argument uses the physical roles of flags; it does not assert the nonexistence of arbitrary abstract nine-flag decoders.

No pure group of K branches. If it did, it would be a pure doubleton or triple with two distinct OR readers, by the arity-twelve/eleven deletion bounds. Erase that flag and its pendant neighbors. The two reader ORs are distinct from the pendant mixed injections, since those raw inputs are already covered by the pure flag. What remains in K has at most three vertices and no isolates (the pendant neighbors were removed), so at most one nontrivial component. Each remaining nontrivial component contributes at least its order minus one further OR by general remote peeling. This yields at least four distinct savings already, contradicting tightness.

Private pure groups have distinct dedicated AND products. Overlapping private operands at one product factor their common raw. For two disjoint operands in K (size two or three), erasing one group's labels already saturates the total-gate allowance: its two raw usages per label and one one-sided product cost 2k+1, while the unchanged single-use partner tree becomes dead as well. This also proves that no two private groups of sizes two or three anywhere can feed one product. Restrict to edges of K joining two private groups alone. The only available initial signals are their distinct single-use raw-only flags. No product can first fire, as its opposite input cannot be another of these private flags. All mixed raw injectors see only zero external raw inputs. Thus at most one such edge exists, or its pair with a second would be a positive input falsely rejected.

All private doubletons are clean since each AND operand needs at least two exclusive singleton labels. Every dirty private group in K is consequently a triple adjacent to all three other vertices. Its product G has singleton support exactly one, at a label t in the triple. No AND can directly read it by the same exclusive-shore inequality. Furthermore no mixed raw group of K can receive G along any OR-only path: for that group's raw edge x shared with the universal triple, if x=t then x is already covered by G; otherwise both reader groups of x are already true at t and adding x is invisible.

When K is erased, each mixed flag pays its own raw injection OR. Each clean private flag pays an OR in the clean collector; a central positive pair still requires a surviving terminal. If all flags are covered this way the cost is already four. If there is exactly one dirty private flag, G's direct OR reader also disappears and lies outside both lists (it is positive on t and cannot be a local injection). Again all four are paid.

There are at most two dirty flags, since they are universal and three would make three private-private edges. With exactly two, there can be no other pure flag, since it would give two more private-private edges. Hence K has two mixed flags, and there is room for just one OR besides their two contractions. Apply the preceding reader argument to both dirty outputs: all their readers must therefore be the very same gate H, necessarily their disjunction. Its singleton support has size one or two; it cannot be the clean output. A direct OR reader of H would disappear as a fourth gate and cannot be a local injection because it receives the dirty signals along ORs. If an AND reads H, that AND is clean in the original circuit, because its operand has at most two singleton labels and needs at least two exclusive. Its dedicated collector OR therefore disappears on restriction, separately from the two mixed injectors and H. There is at least one other surviving terminal on central pairs. Both possibilities contradict the three-OR allowance.
