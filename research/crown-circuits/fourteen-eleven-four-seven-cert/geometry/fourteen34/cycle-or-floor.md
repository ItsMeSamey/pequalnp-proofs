# An n-cycle needs at least n−2 decoder ORs for n≥5

The n formal flags are the vertices of a simple cycle. A binary monotone decoder rejects each single edge's two-flag assignment and accepts the unions of any two different edges. AND gates and fanout are unrestricted.

Assume there is a decoder with at most n−3 ORs, and choose one with minimum total size among such decoders. The connected degree-two bound supplies at least n−3 ORs. Every flag x is essential: for a two-step path x,a,b the edge ab and its union with ax differ only at x. Erasing x leaves a connected path on n−1 vertices, which needs at least (n−1)+2−5=n−4 ORs, since it still has degree two. Hence at most one OR can be saved by setting x to zero.

Following each occurrence of x through AND-only paths, stop at the first OR. No such path can terminate at the output beforehand: that would bound the output by x while the surviving path has an accepted pair of edges. Each reached OR loses an operand when x=0, so there is exactly one first OR F. If x directly enters F, a second direct OR use would be another first OR; if x had a further AND use, some AND-only path from that use would reach the other operand of F, bounding it by x and making F redundant. Thus x would be single-use.

If x reaches F only via ANDs and has at least two direct users, set x=true within these pre-frontier AND cones. Every affected wire is x conjoined with its modified version, by induction through ANDs. Each direct reader can therefore be bypassed to its other operand. If only one operand of F depended on x, restore the factor x just on that operand. If both depended on x, restore x once after their disjunction. This changes no external observable because every path from x crosses F first. No external operand into the pre-frontier region can rely on x except through one of the affected AND paths or a previously crossed F; in the latter case it would form a cycle before reaching F again. A constant true, should it arise, is only a simplification placeholder. At least two ANDs disappear and at most one AND is added, at unchanged or lower OR count; this contradicts the secondary size choice. Duplicate pins have already been ruled out by idempotence. Every formal flag consequently has exactly one reader.

The earliest useful gate reads distinct flags x,y. It cannot compute their conjunction: among the two walks of length two starting in opposite directions at x, at least one avoids y for n≥5. Its edge ab versus the union with ax must flip while y remains zero, impossible when x enters only x∧y.

If the earliest gate is x∨y and x,y are adjacent, select an edge xz incident to x other than xy. Adding xy would add only y, hidden by the common OR. At distance two use their common neighbor z: xz versus its union with zy again demands a flip. Thus their graph distance is at least three. Identify x and y as a single vertex of the quotient graph and bypass the earliest OR by that new flag. There is neither a loop nor a duplicated edge; the quotient is connected, has n−1 vertices and the merged vertex has degree four. The reduced decoder still satisfies all edge and edge-union requirements: lift each tested set of quotient edges to the corresponding original edges, where their two x,y bits entered the rest of the circuit solely by their disjunction. The connected layer bound requires (n−1)+4−5=n−2 ORs, but the reduced circuit has at most n−4. This contradiction proves the claimed floor.
