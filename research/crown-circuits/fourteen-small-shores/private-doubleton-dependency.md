# A nine-AND branching quartet has at most one private-doubleton neighbor

Use the raw-regular, clean-terminal incidence decoder for a hypothetical 34-gate fourteen-input circuit with nine ANDs. A private doubleton is a single-use pure flag on two raw labels; its product is a dedicated clean terminal. The products for distinct private doubletons are distinct. These statements, the clean-terminal collector, the decoder budget v−3, and the remote-component charges are dependencies documented alongside this note.

Let Q be a branching pure flag on labels x1 through x4. Fix its flag to true after the standard remote-component peeling, exposing a four-label threshold-two star. Suppose m of its neighbors are private doubletons. If the neighbor of xi has dedicated product D_i∧P_i, then P_i rejects xi by cleanness and accepts each of the other three Q singleton labels. Indeed compare singleton xj with pair xi+xj. Q was already true, the only changed external flag is D_i, and D_i has no other use. Its opposite operand therefore accepts xj. Thus the m different P_i signals have distinct three-element singleton supports on the star.

The first occurrence of each specified three-element support must be an OR. Its only strict superset in four coordinates is full, so an intersection cannot first produce the triple. At least one other OR is needed even to obtain the initial pair on the way to any triple. If there were only one OR beyond the first-producing ORs for the m desired triples, its output would have to be that initial pair P. Until a support of intermediate size omitting a member of P appears, every pair or triple contains P. Intersections of such sets cannot produce another pair, while intersections with singleton or empty sets have size at most one. A first OR producing a triple must therefore use P and introduce a triple containing P. Only two distinct triples contain P. Thus for m≥3 at least two additional ORs are necessary, even allowing later pairs to arise by intersection.

At least m−1 further collector ORs join the m distinct surviving terminal products, each with empty singleton support. They are distinct from all gates that first construct the nonempty supports in the preceding argument. Therefore m=3 needs at least seven ORs and m=4 at least nine. The standard remote-component bound leaves at most 2+c≤5 ORs on a quartet star. Here c counts remote components containing a pure flag of degree at least three; there are only ten edges outside the four-star and each such component needs at least three.

At m=2 the two other Q labels form a positive pair which fires neither designated product. Hence a third surviving clean terminal is required. Two triple ORs, one initial pair OR, and two collector ORs give a local lower bound of five ORs. There must therefore be at least three high remote components. The private doubletons are raw-disjoint, so their two other edges are distinct. The central component uses at least six edges, in addition to at least nine edges in the remotes, exceeding fourteen.

For completeness, two private doubletons cannot share a raw label. Erase their three-label union. Each dedicated opposite remains live, because a terminal has at least two exclusive singleton labels on its opposite shore and the erased set contains at most one label outside the doubleton. Their two distinct products are therefore both one-sided dark ANDs. The raw-regular deletion count gives at least six losses for the six raw occurrences and one further loss for each one-sided dark AND. Eight losses would give a 26-gate eleven-input threshold, contradicting its 27-gate minimum.

The lower bound counts gates in the specialized decoder. Terminal products remain nonzero because each responds on a designated two-label pair, and they still belong to the terminal OR collector. Contracting constant branches cannot turn one of their collectors into a gate with positive singleton support. The preceding upper bound uses exactly the documented remote-flag restrictions and constant bypasses.

In particular a quartet has at least three labels whose deletion yields a fully raw-regular thirteen-input minor after reassociating the two affected OR groups. The remaining option, if present, has one irregular raw at the exterior doubleton.
