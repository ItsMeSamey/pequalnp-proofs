# Hard hot labels at width two

Assume the normalized fourteen-label 34-gate five-AND candidate of reductions/fourteen-five/dirty-conflicts.md has a unique dirty gate of width two. Among size- and dirty-minimal representatives also minimize the number of raws hot at that gate. Write T for its two clean continuation pins. There are two outside clean axes, denoted C and D. An inactive clean direction chooses both receiving signs away from T, leaving four C,D cells. The two hidden dirty-selector choices on an inactive cell have identical observable traces.

A hot label whose inactive rejection face is a half or the whole square can be cleaned at no cost using its existing sink for T or T union one outside pin, as in dirty-conflicts.md. Therefore *every* remaining hot label has a distinct one-cell inactive deficit. They number h≤4. More generally the nonpartner set N, including the hot labels, has disjoint nonempty outside faces in these four cells. At least ten labels are opposite-support partners. If h cells are taken by hot labels, all other nonpartners must fit into the remaining 4−h cells.

Normalize double-tag nodes as in dirty-conflicts.md. Any irreducible double-tag node contains both outside signs specifying its one-cell deficit. Thus its descendants' deficits are subsets of that same cell and cannot be empty at a useful raw. At most one raw H can depend on it, by disjoint rejection faces. If the first such double-tag node is an internal helper, rewire H to T union E, building E as the disjunction of its two outside clean pins in a new helper. H has exactly the same resolved event, but no longer carries both dirty tags. The double-tag helper is now dead and can pay for E. Other raw events and tags are untouched. This contradicts the chosen minimum. Applying the same argument whenever necessary leaves no irreducible double-tag helper. Any remaining hot sink joins two opposite, single-tagged helpers, one specifying its C sign, the other its D sign. Both must reach a different receiver also: if either is private to H, replace H as above and refund E from that child. A preexisting helper containing both required outside signs, at most one dirty tag and only continuation-compatible signs on the receiving axes already allows T union that helper to clean H without any extra charge.

Count distinct immediate tagged child helpers used by the h hot sinks. A helper can serve at most two hot cells in one row or column and cannot serve both dirty-tag orientations. One hot cell needs two helpers. Two neighboring hot cells need at least three, since both child helpers could be shared only by identical cells; two diagonal cells need four. Three cells need at least four, two for each tag orientation. Four cells need at least four, and equality forces the four complete coordinate lines as their scopes, with row helpers all of one tag and column helpers all of the other tag. Indeed all four helpers then serve two cells each; at each cell one C helper and one D helper are required. The source T uses another helper, disjoint from all tagged helpers. These bounds apply to a secondary-minimum representative and do not exclude width two outright.
