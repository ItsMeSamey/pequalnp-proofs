# Small exclusive singleton shores in a 34-gate threshold circuit

Work with a minimum raw-regular fourteen-input monotone threshold-two circuit of size 34. Each raw has two distinct OR readers. Reassociate ORs into the usual pure and mixed groups, with distinct raw inputs in each group. A pure group contains at least two raw inputs. The incidence graph is simple: two raw labels cannot have the same two groups, since then either singleton and their pair would be indistinguishable.

At a physical AND with inputs P,Q choose either orientation. Its exclusive shore is the set of labels on whose singleton P is true and Q false. For any collection of oriented ANDs, erase a set S containing all their exclusive shores, then bypass each chosen AND toward P. This still computes threshold two on the remaining labels. On their singleton inputs, induction through the original circuit preserves every wire value under the simultaneous bypasses. Monotonicity of every bypass relative to the original preserves acceptance of positive pairs. The all-zero input remains rejected.

For s erased labels let k be the number of pure groups wholly erased. Before downstream simplification at least 2s−k OR gates can be removed within the groups: reassociate each nonempty remainder first, and replace an empty pure group with a formal zero pin. If k>0, at least one further downstream OR is eliminated. Indeed after this reassociation trace a formal pin to the output. If it has an AND-only path then zeroing it would force the output to be identically zero, contrary to the surviving threshold. Its first downstream OR can therefore be contracted when the formal pins are zeroed, possibly after propagation through ANDs. Its identity is distinct from the earlier group contractions. Count the designated ANDs on the original graph: identities already lost on zero propagation count as removed, and surviving designated gates may be bypassed. To see validity after simplifications, the pruned-then-resolved circuit lies between the original restricted circuit and the circuit obtained by resolving before pruning, pointwise. This follows gate by gate because simplifying a wire known constant zero before a monotone enlargement can only decrease the enlarged result. These bounds count OR and AND identities separately.

A single erased label saves two ORs. Consequently every shore has size at least two by C(13)=32. For two erased labels, simplicity gives k≤1, hence at least four ORs disappear. No two different ANDs can have the same two-label shore: resolving both would leave at most 28 gates against C(12)=29. Resolving one such shore gives an optimum twelve-input circuit of size 29. In the seven-AND case that circuit has exactly six ANDs and 23 ORs. No additional loss is possible.

For three erased labels, whenever k≤1 at least six ORs disappear. Thus the union of two shores of distinct ANDs cannot be contained in this triple, by C(11)=27. If k≥2, the triple contains two adjacent pure doubletons: two complete distinct groups meet in at most one raw, and each has size at least two. Two private doubletons cannot be adjacent by the independently established private-doubleton minor. Therefore an overlap between distinct two-label shores requires two adjacent doubletons, at least one branching, in their three-label union. Likewise a three-label shore can contain another gate's two-label shore only in this exceptional geometry. For the chronological first AND these shores are the ordinary raw-support differences of its two OR-only inputs.

This applies to physical singleton truth sets throughout. Projected selector deficits require a separate implication before using these cuts.
