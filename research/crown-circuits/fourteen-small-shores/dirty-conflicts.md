# Singleton conflicts after dirty products fire

Consider a normalized size-34 candidate for fourteen-input threshold two with five physical AND gates. Use the terminal-clean representative with the fewest dirty ANDs. The five binary directions select one operand at each AND. The direct continuation T_j of a dirty product is its set of first downstream product input pins reached through OR gates alone. U_j is the event that the backwards output resolution reaches j. The proof uses the established physical reverse representation: the 14 distinct raw receivers each have one paid sink union; a receiver can be replaced by T_j or a union of T_j and a primitive pin without increasing cost. A replacement with a larger acceptance event and a nonempty rejection set keeps all singleton negatives and pair positives. All clean AND outputs feed the terminal collector, so a clean AND becoming hot would contradict a preserved singleton negative.

For raw i first evaluate the circuit forward on its singleton, firing every AND whose two inputs are true. Its exclusive sign on a gate records that exactly that sign's operand is true. Every pair of distinct labels has opposite exclusive signs at some gate. Indeed, compare the input pair with the coordinatewise OR of the two singleton wire evaluations. The first gate where the two evaluations differ is an AND whose operands are separately true on opposite singletons. OR gates cannot create the first difference.

There is a stronger necessary condition. If two labels have no mutual conflict on the clean gates, then at least one of their mutually conflicting dirty gates must, on firing, supply a pin that was false on both singletons. Otherwise add the outputs of all baseline-conflicting dirty gates: every pin they can supply is already in the union of the two singleton pin vectors. No further product can fire, all clean products remain false, and the pair is erroneously rejected. When there is just one dirty gate, this firing must supply one side of a clean terminal whose opposite operand was true on at least one of the labels.

## Width and exclusive ancestors

Let j be any dirty gate. Let k count its direct clean-axis targets, possibly zero if it also targets a dirty product. These clean signs are consistent, because j is hot on at least one singleton, which every clean gate rejects. Delete j and these k clean input ports from the product-level directed graph. Let r count the other dirty gates that now have no path to a clean output. For a direction missing U_j all k clean selectors are fixed away from these ports. Gate j and the r gates are unobserved. Additional restrictions imposed by dirty targets can only decrease the trace count. Consequently the number of distinct observable traces outside U_j is at most 2^(4−k−r), whenever k+r≤4; equivalently at most two traces remain as soon as k+r≥3.

Every j-hot raw's acceptance event contains U_j. If there is only one trace outside U_j, every proper such event is U_j. If two remain, a raw's nonempty deficit either consists of both or of one. In the singleton case choose a primitive operand pin observed on the other trace and absent from the desired rejecting trace. Such a pin always exists in the required direction: at the latest observable difference between two traces both select opposite signs of the same active gate. That gate is distinct from the unobserved j. Thus T_j alone or T_j union that primitive pin realizes exactly the chosen event. Reattach every j-hot raw this way. T_j injects only strictly downstream of j. One additional primitive pin upstream of j cannot fire an earlier AND by itself or activate both j inputs. The rewired singletons therefore keep j cold. Cold labels were unchanged, and no terminal clean gate becomes hot. This contradicts minimum dirtiness.

Hence k+r≤2. A dirty chain of length three with no bypass to a clean terminal is impossible. A terminal-adjacent dirty gate with one exclusively dependent predecessor must have width one. This statement applies even in the presence of other dirty gates with bypass paths.

## One dirty gate

There are four clean axes and the sole dirty gate has width k equal to one or two. Let N contain the labels whose physical singleton makes none of the k receiving terminals' opposite operands true. Every hot label belongs to N. For two N labels a dirty firing cannot produce an accepted clean gate on those k axes, since their opposite operands remain false. An accepting pair must therefore have opposite exclusive signs on one of the other 4−k clean axes. The N labels' ternary words on those axes have disjoint completion cubes. Consequently N contains at most eight labels at width one and at most four at width two. At least six or ten labels respectively make one of the opposite operands true.

A minimum-dirty candidate has a hot label that cannot be cleaned by a T_j-plus-one-pin sink. Each hot label's deficit lies within the inactive clean face and is cut there solely by its direct clean pins outside T_j. Pins on the k receiving axes are already fixed on that face, and j is inactive there. If a hot raw restricts at most one further clean axis, the replacement T_j or T_j union its one extra sign realizes its acceptance exactly. Hence some hot raw has direct pins on at least two of the remaining clean axes as well as both dirty input pins. Its inactive deficit has size at most two for k=1 and exactly one for k=2. These conditions are structural necessary tests and do not by themselves rule out both surviving widths.

## A necessary pair of tagged helpers

For the one-dirty case, call a formal reverse-bank mask double-tagged when it contains both dirty input pins. Remove unused unions first. Every useful double-tag mask X has a nonempty rejection event, since it feeds a proper raw receiver and cannot feed T (T contains no dirty sign). Its resolved acceptance event is U_j together with its direct outside-clean signs. Any clean sign in X at a continuation coordinate has the same orientation as T: the opposite orientation would cover the full direction universe in combination with U_j. In particular these signs may be discarded when U_j is already included.

Process union nodes in any topological order with the existing computation of T granted first. No tagged node can be required to synthesize T. For a double-tag node, if a child with at most one dirty tag already contains all its outside-clean signs, replace that union by T union the child. The semantic event is unchanged, and the physical union now has at most one dirty input tag. This uses at most one union, also when the node is a raw sink. The transformation preserves every subsequent semantic event, never creates an extra dirty product, and can be applied to each useful node. If both tags disappear from every raw, the unique dirty product has become clean, contrary to minimality.

At the first remaining double-tag node both children are single-tagged, with opposite tags. Neither contains the union of their sets of outside-clean signs. Each therefore contributes at least one outside-clean sign absent in the other child, and these two signs are on different clean axes because the node is proper. Both children are charged helper unions: neither is a primitive (each has a dirty and a clean pin) or a raw sink feeding other computations. For width two the two remaining clean axes split exactly one per child. For width one at least two of the three remaining axes have distinct, individually necessary owners. This gives a required signed two-helper pattern without enumerating all-clean banks.

## Two consequences beyond one dirty gate

With a single clean terminal and four dirty products, both operands of that terminal have at least two dirty ancestors. If one operand has fewer than two, at least one dirty product feeds only the other operand. Choose a maximal dirty product among those feeding only that side. None of its descendants can feed the first side, so it is terminal-adjacent and it directly feeds just the other side. To miss its reach the clean selector must choose the first side. At most one dirty gate is then observable, leaving at most two distinct traces, contradicting the replacement argument.

More generally let D be the set of all directly dirtied clean input pins (inputs reached by a dirty output on an OR-only path). If D has consistent signs and touches b of the c clean axes, consider labels for which no opposite input on those b axes is true on the singleton. Any accepting pair among these labels must have opposite exclusive signs on a clean axis outside D. Even allowing every dirty gate to fire adds only pins in D to the original clean-input union. Thus these labels' completion cubes on c−b axes are disjoint, and at most 2^(c−b) such labels can occur. This test applies to any chronological dependence among the dirty products.
