# Shared cycle readers in the cyclic sixteen-flag interfaces

Let g be a cycle flag supplied by the OR of its two incident edge labels. In the ordinary 27-gate T2(11) circuit, the other occurrence of each label enters a different neighboring cycle bank OR; both neighbors remain live after deleting the two labels. All downstream dark signals thus originate only from g. The dark-edge count charges four raw uses and f(g)−1 internal surplus. Six losses would force four ANDs to disappear by the size-21 T2(9) composition.

If f(g)≥3, at least six losses are paid with no room for a one-sided dark AND. There is no nontrivial dark product from g alone. If f(g)=2 and an AND reads g, its other operand must be nondark: any signal vanishing on g=0 is bounded by g and makes that product redundant. This spends the last debit on a one-sided dark AND, so no other dark fanout is possible. The two outgoing dark occurrences from g can pay for at most one additional both-dark merge. Thus at most two ANDs disappear, impossible. Each branching cycle flag therefore has exactly two OR readers.

Flatten the single-use OR-to-OR edges in the decoder. Let b count branching cycle flags and P count maximal OR groups consisting entirely of branching cycle flags. The branching flags have 2b group occurrences; a duplicate in a single group could be removed. Erasing all b flags saves at least 2b−P ORs: put erased terms last in a group with other terms, while each all-erased group costs its term count minus one. Apply these explicit bypasses before trimming. Every remaining cycle flag still spoils a singleton of the remote labels (sixteen-pendant-readers.md), so erasing it subsequently removes an OR while positive remote pairs survive. In total at least 2b−P+l−b ORs disappear for cycle length l. The remaining four P3s or three P3s and one independent K2 require respectively eight or seven ORs: erase one leaf per P3 and identify each remaining edge with a raw, obtaining the four-input OR floor of four. The cap of twelve implies P≥b−1.

Each P group uses at least two branching-cycle occurrences. Thus at most 2b−2P≤2 such occurrences enter a group with any other term. A single-read cycle flag cannot share a maximal OR group with any single-read P3 flag: associate them as the first OR and identify them. The resulting connected cycle-plus-path component has a cubic or quartic vertex and no universal covering flag, so its combined-order-minus-two floor and the remaining remote charges exceed the reduced OR budget. Thus at most two ordinary P3 flag groups contain any cycle flag as a term. For the triangle, any pure group with at least two cycle flags computes the same triangle nonemptiness signal, so P≤1 and b≤2.
