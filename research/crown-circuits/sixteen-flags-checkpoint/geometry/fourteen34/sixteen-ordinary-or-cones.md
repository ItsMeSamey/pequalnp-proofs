# Ordinary leaf OR cones in the cyclic sixteen-flag interfaces

Use the twelve-OR eight-AND residual on C3+4P3 or C4+3P3+K2. The fixed seven-OR incidence bank yields an ordinary 27-gate T2(11) circuit, so decreasing the total gate count is impossible. Among such decoders maximize the number of OR gates with an OR-only path to output. The earlier physical erasure bounds apply to this representative. Call a wire hot if true on some singleton raw edge.

No cold operand can enter a hot OR. Suppose H=A∨B is hot and B cold. Replace every H use by A and append a final OR with B. If B is false nothing changes; if B is true at least two raw labels were present, and the new output is positive as required. Before trimming, this has exactly the same OR and AND counts. H was outside the old collector because its hot singleton would otherwise have reached output. Every old collector gate retains its downstream OR path, and the final OR adds one. Any newly dead gate would contradict size 27. Consequently all wires reaching a hot OR solely through ORs are themselves hot.

For an ordinary P3 with leaves x,y and center g=x∨y, write Ux=x∨P and Uy=y∨Q for its singly-read leaf entries. P vanishes on the entire two-label subdomain: adding x to y cannot change any wire upstream of Ux, since every raw flag except x is unchanged. The accepted pair xy must therefore differ from singleton y through Ux, forcing P(y)=P(xy)=0. Monotonicity gives P(x)=0. Hence P is hot on an external singleton by collector maximality. The same holds for Q. Every companion term in a flattened leaf OR group has such a witness, since it reaches the hot OR via ORs and is bounded by its companion.

If g's single reader is an OR G, Ux and G cannot both be hot on an external singleton z. On addition of x to z the only source flags changing are x and g; their sole direct readers Ux and G are both already true. Every other decoder signal remains unchanged, a contradiction. Likewise for Uy. The leaf entries are incomparable under OR-only reachability because each is false on its sibling singleton. G cannot reach Ux through ORs because G(y)=1, nor can Ux reach G because of Ux's external witness. Symmetrically G and Uy are incomparable.

The OR predecessor cones of G and Ux are disjoint. Any common OR predecessor Z is hot. An external singleton heating Z would heat G and Ux, which is impossible. Neither can sibling y heat Z. The bare leaf x cannot reach G through ORs without passing Ux. Any other predecessor of Ux, excluding Ux itself, cannot read x except through g: on singleton x its raw-source assignment with x suppressed is contained in the sibling-y assignment, so Z(x)≤Z(y)=0. Ux itself was ruled out by incomparability. Thus Z cannot be hot. The same holds for G and Uy. A common OR predecessor of both leaf entries, if any, must be hot externally.

If the center reader is the clean AND g∧W, any external singleton heating Ux or Uy must also heat W. On adding the corresponding leaf to that singleton, its already-hot entry remains true. W cannot depend on g except through the AND it feeds, so W does not change. Acceptance forces the center product to switch, hence W was already true on the singleton. This containment supplements the surviving-representative condition of duality/uniform/sixteen-center-comparison.md; it does not require separate W for separate paths.

For m ordinary P3s the 2m leaf groups are distinct by the earlier cross-group identification and sibling incompatibility. At most two groups can have a direct cycle-flag term by sixteen-cycle-readers.md. Thus at least 2m−2 distinct groups need a non-cycle companion term with an external singleton witness: at least four in the triangle family and two in the square family. Such a term cannot be another ordinary flag directly (within a group by absorption and cross-group by identification). The source must therefore be an exceptional or K2 flag, an internal shared OR, or a hot AND. Multiple groups may share a source when fanout permits.
