# Kind-preserving normalization of raw input fanouts

Suppose a minimum g-gate fan-in-two monotone AND/OR circuit on n≥4 inputs computes threshold two and the one-input deletion minor requires at least g−2 gates. All raw inputs can be made to enter exactly two OR gates with no change in the number of gates of either kind.

After eliminating constants, dead gates, identities and repetitions, each raw has at most two readers: setting it false removes every reader. A twice-used raw x cannot enter an AND D=x∧P. Removing x also makes D zero, and D must have a consumer outside x's two readers, as combining D with x would already be reducible. D cannot itself be the output since the remaining threshold is nonzero. Thus at least three gates would be lost. A sole AND reader is impossible by the three-gate direct-specialization argument in algebra/reverse/eleven-threshold-and-direct.md, lines 19–21. Its other operand equals the disjunction of all remaining variables; this maximum signal, the AND, and a downstream simplification disappear.

If the only reader of x is H=x∨P, then P is independent of x and rejects every singleton y of the other raws. Otherwise y and x+y are indistinguishable. Hence whenever P is true the target threshold is already true. Redirect every use of H to raw x and append one terminal OR of P with the former output. When P=false the result is unchanged. When P=true the new answer is true, as required. The transformation replaces one OR by one OR. P cannot be raw (it rejects singletons) or a constant false (H would have been redundant), so other raw fanouts do not change.

If x remains singly used it now feeds H's former sole successor. The single-AND case remains impossible by the minor bound. Repeat along the old DAG until x has two readers. It cannot reach one of the newly appended terminal ORs directly, since the resulting output would accept the singleton x, so this terminates after finitely many old ORs. More than two readers or a pair containing an AND would contradict the same three-saving argument. Repeat for the remaining singly used raws. The circuit always computes the same function at the original minimum size with the original AND and OR counts.
