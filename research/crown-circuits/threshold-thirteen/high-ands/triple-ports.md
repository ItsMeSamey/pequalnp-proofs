# Three exclusive singleton witnesses per AND

Assume a minimum 31-gate monotone binary circuit computes threshold two on thirteen inputs. Use the regular clean-terminal form with two OR readers per raw. The twelve- and eleven-input minima are 29 and 27.

Deleting any two raw variables saves four ORs even after an arbitrary AND is replaced by one operand. If their reader sets are disjoint, bypass all four readers. If x,y share L=x∨y, then L is single-use and directly feeds an OR. Otherwise L is a pure doubleton, excluded by its five-gate deletion saving. Bypass L, its OR consumer and the two other raw readers. The last two readers are distinct by irredundancy and neither is L's consumer (x∨L or y∨L would be redundant). These are four syntactically distinct ORs. Each bypass computation remains valid after any AND substitution.

Replace any AND by either operand. Zero still rejects and every pair still accepts by monotonicity. A singleton that newly becomes accepted had the retained operand true and the other operand false: otherwise the evaluated AND value stays fixed, hence so does the output. If at most two singletons change, delete their raws, padding to two as needed. The expanded circuit then computes threshold two on eleven inputs with at most 26 gates, contradicting the minimum. Thus each physical exclusive singleton shore of every AND contains at least three labels.

An AND with a pure triple as operand is clean: all three raws of the triple belong to its exclusive shore. In clean-terminal form two such dedicated ANDs cannot share a partner: rearrange the terminal OR collector and factor their common operand to save one gate. Every unshared pure triple requires a shared partner by the tight three-raw deletion bound. Branching triples feed ORs only and cannot be partners. Hence when all p pure groups are triples, p is at most the six units of internal fanout surplus.

For p≥7 all groups must be triples. A quartet requires at least three pure components and at least 3p+1 pure incidences. Hence for p=7 at least nine edges join pure groups, and for p=8 at least twelve. With three or more components, only a 5+1+1 partition can fit nine edges at p=7, and only a 6+1+1 partition can fit twelve at p=8. Its dense component would use at least nine distinct raws and the isolates six more. Larger p needs more than 26 raw incidences. Consequently p≤6.

A quartet leaves exactly one decorated quartet in either of two families. At p=6 there are at least 19−13=6 pure edges in a graph with three components. Only K4 plus two isolates can attain six, with at most 13+6=19 pure incidences. At p=5 there are at least 16−13=3 pure edges, forcing K3 plus two isolates and exactly 16 incidences. At p=4 the plain star bound k+2≤p+1 excludes a quartet.

If all groups are triples, let s count unshared triples. Each feeds a different clean terminal. In a representative with a terminal OR collector the number t of clean ANDs is at most r+1=p−2. For s≤3 there are at least two labels outside the union of the s triples, so those two labels require an additional clean terminal, and s≤p−3. For s≥4 the inequality t≥s forces p=6,s=4 for the remaining cases. That case also has at least two labels outside the four triples unless the four triples are mutually disjoint. If they are mutually disjoint they cover twelve labels, leaving precisely one other raw.
