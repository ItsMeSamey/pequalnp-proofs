# Pair deletion and first-AND separation on thirteen inputs

Assume a minimum clean-terminal binary monotone circuit C computes threshold two on thirteen inputs with 31 gates. The twelve- and eleven-input minima are 29 and 27. Every raw has exactly two distinct OR readers by regularity. A pure flattened OR group has at least three raw labels by the doubleton argument in nine-and.md; that argument uses the total budget independently of the AND count.

**Exact pair deletion.** For distinct raw labels x,y without a common physical reader, setting x=y=0 bypasses four distinct raw-reader ORs. Otherwise their common reader is H=x∨y; a second common reader duplicates H, impossible by minimality. H cannot be the end of its flattened group, which would be a pure doubleton. It has a sole OR consumer B. The two other readers G of x and K of y are distinct. Neither is B since x∨H and y∨H already equal H. Thus H, B, G and K are four distinct ORs, each bypassed upon setting x=y=0. The result computes eleven-input threshold two at 27 gates. Its optimality means that exactly four ORs disappear and every AND survives. No further gate can be simplified.

**Separation.** If an AND reads the raw-OR functions on sets P,Q, then |P∖Q| and |Q∖P| are both at least three. Indeed, deleting a pair covering P∖Q would make the restricted P-side imply the Q-side. This surviving AND would equal its P-side and could be bypassed, contradicting the eleven-input minimum. Exchange P,Q for the other inequality. The two operands may be arbitrarily shared raw-OR DAGs. In particular the first AND has at least six distinct raw ancestors. If it accepts a singleton, it has at least seven, and both operand supports have size at least four. A triple operand forces disjoint supports and a singleton-negative AND. More generally every surviving AND, including those with AND ancestry, admits assignments witnessing each of the two possible strict operand orders after any pair deletion.

**Signed projection.** Every half of each genuine selector contains at least three whole singleton deficits. Otherwise retain the opposite half. At most two raw labels have no rejecting direction left. Specialize those labels to zero, filling the deletion pair arbitrarily if necessary. The other eleven labels still have rejecting resolutions individually. Fixing the selector bypasses the physical AND to its chosen operand, enlarging the function, hence retaining pair acceptance. This would save a fifth gate. A deficit wholly inside one signed half observes its corresponding port at every point, since flipping an inactive gate does not change the outcome. Each signed physical port therefore occurs in at least three distinct observed atoms, and five pairwise-disjoint signed-port events would need fifteen distinct labels. These are conditions on genuine physical switches.
