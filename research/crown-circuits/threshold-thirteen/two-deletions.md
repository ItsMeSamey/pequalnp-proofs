# Three private singleton deficits per selector at size 31

Consider a minimum 31-gate monotone fan-in-two circuit computing threshold two on thirteen inputs. The exact minima for twelve and eleven inputs are 29 and 27 gates. Apply the known kind-preserving normalization so every raw input has exactly two distinct OR readers. All gates are live and the circuit admits no local identity or absorption saving.

Deleting any two distinct raw inputs by setting them false removes four OR gates while preserving every physical AND. First fix x false by bypassing its two OR readers. The resulting circuit has 29 gates and computes the twelve-input threshold. Optimality precludes any new dead, redundant, or duplicated gate. Consider another raw y. If neither original reader of y read x, both readers survive. If one read both, that gate is H=x∨y. The two raws cannot have two common readers: both readers would be copies of H. Let G be the other reader of x and K the other reader of y. H cannot be the final output, since a singleton x would then be accepted. H has a direct consumer J. This consumer cannot be G or K, since x∨H or y∨H equals H. After bypassing H, J reads y directly and K still reads y. If J is a predecessor of G or K by a longer path, the direct edges y→J and y→K persist. Hence y has at least two distinct readers. It has at most two, since three readers would disappear upon specialization, violating the eleven-input minimum. A twice-read raw in this minimum circuit cannot feed an AND: that AND becomes zero, and combining its output directly with y in the other reader would already have been absorbable. Its consumer therefore gives a third saving. Thus both surviving readers of y are ORs. Bypass them on y=false to obtain a 27-gate eleven-input circuit with every original AND intact.

Resolve all ANDs by fixing a parent choice for each. Each resulting OR circuit dominates the original circuit. For a raw i its deficit is the set of full parent choices for which the resolved output rejects the singleton i. A pair is accepted in every resolution, and each singleton has a nonempty deficit. Suppose a selector half contained the entire deficits of at most two raws. Choose x,y to include those labels, and fix both false as above. For each surviving singleton its original deficit intersects the opposite half. Resolve just this AND to the corresponding parent. The partially resolved circuit still rejects that singleton, since it is dominated by its exhibited fully resolved circuit. It accepts the empty assignment and every positive pair by monotonicity and thus computes the eleven-input threshold. Removing the resolved AND leaves 26 gates, contradicting the exact minimum 27.

Every signed half therefore contains at least three entire deficits. At each point of a deficit wholly inside a half, that signed port must be observed. If the selector were inactive, its direction bit could flip without changing the observable trace or any receiver value, giving a deficit point outside the half. Observable atoms are the classes of equal signed-port observations. Distinct raw deficits are disjoint unions of those atoms. Each port consequently contains at least three distinct atoms. Five pairwise-disjoint signed port events are impossible, as they would require fifteen distinct raws.
