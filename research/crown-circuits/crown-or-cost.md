# The monotone OR cost of a crown representation

For n at least 3, write m(n) for the least number of binary OR gates needed to compute disequality on the promised inputs with one active row and one active column. Allow unrestricted AND gates, sharing, and constants. The claimed lower bound is 3n−4. The construction that pairs rᵢ and cᵢ by OR and computes threshold two on the resulting n signals attains that OR count.

**Base.** On the nine promised inputs of the 3-crown, each raw input has a 9-bit mask. With arbitrary free conjunctions, each pin of each OR gate is an intersection of raw masks and earlier OR outputs. The output is one final intersection. Exhausting the Boolean selectors proves four gates cannot give the target mask 238. The modules ThreeCrownFour and ThreeCrownNet check this for actual free-AND nets. The direct disjunction of six positive cells uses five ORs.

**Induction.** Fix n≥4, assuming m(n−1)≥3n−7. Suppose some n-crown representation has ≤3n−5 ORs and choose one of minimum OR count. Expand AND-only expressions on each pin into products of atoms. The output is a product too. Temporarily suppose p chronologically initial OR gates have already paired distinct matching labels, producing zᵢ=rᵢ∨cᵢ. Erase both old raw names of each such label from the tail. To extend this inductive invariant below, the first occurrence analysis must show these names have no OR or output use after their merge. The current atoms are these zᵢ and the unmatched rᵢ,cᵢ.

**Deletion and usage.** Deleting an atom means setting it to false. Every OR directly mentioning it in a product pin loses that pin, so one can substitute the other product throughout the tail, removing this OR. The specialize_saves lemma counts all such gates, including when both pins contain the atom. In a normalized free-AND net, a variable that is absent from every OR can affect evaluation only if read by the final conjunction. A positive input avoiding it rules out that occurrence.

Delete an entire label i. Its remaining n−1-label circuit is valid after retaining the p other pair gates, or p−1 if i was paired. If i was paired, lose its prefix OR and all tail gates hit by zᵢ. If i was unmatched, first lose all tail gates hit by rᵢ. After that, cᵢ still hits some gate. For a retained label j≠i, its single row atom (zⱼ if j was paired) is false by domination by the negative j-diagonal. Adding cᵢ gives a true (j,i) pair. Both witness assignments set rᵢ false. A positive pair avoiding cᵢ rules out its occurrence in the terminal conjunction. Thus the residual gate count drops again upon deleting cᵢ. Reverse the order to cover each unmatched column. The initial and final budgets differ by at most two. Consequently every current variable hits at most one tail OR. Every variable is sensitive and is absent from the final conjunction, so every one hits exactly one.

**First gate.** Its two pins are conjunctions with supports A and B. No current variable in their union is read elsewhere except through the gate output. This is precisely the gate_cut lemma. For any distinct current atoms x and y, there is an input with x=y=false such that turning on x changes the output. Choose a third label k outside the labels of x,y, with its opposite-side atom on (either side works for merged x). Initially the active singleton is false; after switching x it represents a positive pair. This also rules out the possibility that the tail is a lone conjunction. If x occurs on both pins and y is another atom in their union, the gate stays false throughout y's pivot at x=false. If the pin supports are disjoint and x,y belong to the same pin, that pin stays false during x's pivot at y=false and the other pin ignores x. These impossibilities leave only x∨y with distinct x,y, or a constant or copied atom. Remove the latter in a minimum circuit.

Two unmerged row atoms of distinct labels cannot be OR-merged: holding the first row’s matching column fixed distinguishes that diagonal from the pair with the second row. The transpose argument applies to columns. Whenever x,y can represent opposite endpoints of different labels, the negative singleton x and the positive pair x,y become identical downstream. This includes a merged atom with any distinct-label atom and two merged atoms. The only remaining merge is therefore rᵢ∨cᵢ for one unmatched label, and both raw names are absent downstream. Extend the prefix.

Once every label is paired, the original circuit consists of n pairing gates before a tail on z₁,…,zₙ. It computes threshold two on all Boolean inputs by monotonicity and its prescribed values on singleton and two-element supports. Wegener’s threshold-two OR lower bound is 2n−4 even with unrestricted free ANDs. This contradicts the assumed budget. Equivalently, the first-gate argument already gives a contradiction at the all-paired stage, since the nonconstant tail still requires a first genuine gate and no merge is possible.

The base and elementary deletion lemma are kernel-checked. The inductive control of paired prefixes and their quotient semantics still require a Lean proof.
