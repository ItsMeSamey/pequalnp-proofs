# State-deletion bounds independent of the number of clean axes

Take a minimum-size fourteen-input threshold-two circuit of size N≤34 in the regular one-dirty, width-one form. Assume its reverse trace representation has N−13 union gates, fourteen terminal receivers with pairwise disjoint nonempty product deficits, and primitive state acceptances O={I}, P={+}, M={−}, T={+,−}; other primitives are outside literals. At least one raw fires the dirty gate, so it uses P and M. This applies to the six-AND analogue whenever the same structural normal form is established; the number of outside axes is immaterial.

For a state s, let r be its exclusive count and g the number of distinct direct union uses of its singleton state primitive. Deletion gives an ordinary signed cube. Each of the r lost observers is a separate terminal union, and none is one of those g direct users (a direct user accepts s everywhere). After bypassing these g unions the ordinary bank on m=14−r observers uses at most N−13−r−g joins. The transpose construction in one-dirty-width-one-state-minors.md now costs at most N−2r−g, provided at least two observers survive. Only r≤3 requires checking here.

For each active state, g≥1 by the hot raw. If r=0 the transpose is smaller than the original fourteen-input minimum. If r=1 it has size at most 31 at thirteen inputs, contrary to C(13)≥32. Thus both active exclusive classes have size at least two. Every member of either class accepts I on its outside face, so must use O. This gives g≥1 for I too, and the same implications show r_I≥2. For any state with r=2 or r=3, C(12)≥29 and C(11)≥27 force g≤1, respectively. Hence g=1. This use is shared: O has at least four active-exclusive users; P and M each serve a hot raw and an opposite-exclusive receiver containing O. The conclusion is a count constraint, conditional on the stated representation, and does not exclude six-AND circuits.
