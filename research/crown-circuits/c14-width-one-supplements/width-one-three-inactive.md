# The three-inactive-exclusive stratum

Assume the hypotheses of width-one-active-three.md and suppose exactly three receivers are inactive-exclusive. Write H for the four outside assignments where η vanishes, with O∨η the unique O trunk. Each active-exclusive receiver contains O, hence its outside face is contained in H. On each active panel these faces are nonempty and disjoint. Thus both active-exclusive counts belong to {3,4}. A family of four fills the entire H panel with singleton cells. A family of three either occupies three singleton cells or two singleton cells and a two-cell edge.

If both counts are four, all eight active cells above H are occupied and all three nonexclusive receivers avoid H, since their state sets contain an active state. There can be no O-only partner (its deficit would meet both saturated panels inside H). The active-exclusive receivers have all three outside signs. If the counts are three and four there is at most one H-active cell available to any further receiver. In every case a family of three active-exclusive receivers gives the corresponding unique dirty-tag trunk as well: if the positive count is three, the unique P trunk is P∨ξ, with ξ a single outside literal because the negative-exclusive family has at least three members. Those receivers all use O and P, hence their distinct outside points belong to H intersect the ξ-half. Two different outside axes intersect in only two points, so ξ=η. The symmetric statement holds for M.

In particular, when all three counts equal three, the three primitive trunks carry the same outside sign. Any receiver meeting the complement of H uses none of O,P,M, and consequently is either inactive-exclusive via T or has a deficit on all three states. At least three receivers must meet that complement: if at most two do, at least twelve receivers lie in H, and at least three of those are among the five nonexclusive receivers. These twelve then need at least fifteen of its twelve atoms. At least three of the five nonexclusive receivers must in fact meet that complement, since six H-active cells are already occupied by the two active-exclusive families and any further nonexclusive receiver meeting H needs at least one of the two remaining H-active cells. Those three are therefore state-free receivers, with deficits covering all three panels on their outside faces. Their computation uses only outside primitives and outside-only helpers. These conclusions hold before imposing the physical port exclusivity cut.
