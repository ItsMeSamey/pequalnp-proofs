# Clean private products at five pure groups and two branches

Consider the 31-gate seven-AND thirteen-input threshold candidate with two branching and three private pure groups, all private products clean. Every record surviving the preliminary group and probe bounds has two branching triples. The three private products are distinct terminal ANDs. At least two labels lie outside all three private groups, so their pair forces a fourth clean terminal F. The mixed-group budget r=3 permits exactly these four terminals. All three units of nonraw OR excess belong to the terminal collector. Consequently each precollector mixed OR group has exactly one nonraw input. Reassociate its raw members into a chain of tagged binary injections z∨K whose nonraw input is K. Private internal OR-tree nodes have no other consumers, so the change preserves the physical gate counts. Each mixed raw occurrence contributes exactly one injection.

The two branching group outputs have two OR readers each. For either group B its first readers are B∨x and B∨x′ on different injection branches. Minimality forbids x∈B. Deleting the three raws of B produces a 24-gate ten-input circuit with seven ANDs, and the entire deletion preserves every AND. If B∨x had any AND reader, that reader in the actual restricted circuit would take raw x directly, excluded by the T10 raw-reader argument. A singleton-hot B∨x cannot go into the output collector either. Each first injection thus has at least one immediate injection successor. Choose one, (B∨x)∨y. Its raw y lies outside B and differs from x by irredundancy. The four resulting two-step chains are disjoint because nonraw inputs of injections are unique and the two branching roots are independent. The extra injections number 3−q after these eight used slots, where q is the number of pure quartets in the graph. The 22 remaining base configurations have q from zero to two.

Assign two raw occurrences to each chain respecting raw multiplicities and the two outside-label requirements. Erase the labels of the remaining injection occurrences. If a raw labels both a chain injection and another remaining injection, erase that label everywhere. On the resulting subcube every unassigned injection simply relays its sole nonraw parent. The circuit feeding private partners and F is now AND-only on four full chain features and some partial chain features. An intermediate B∨x can contribute to an AND only by also passing through an extra injection on one of its other uses: direct AND reads are impossible, bare B has exhausted both its uses, and the chosen second injection supplies the full feature rather than the partial one. At most 3−q such extra partial features are needed, with either orientation of the unordered pair granted. There are no fresh constant sources. The remaining terms are U_j times one nonempty conjunction of features for each private j, and F equal to one nonempty conjunction of features.

chains_fast.py enumerates these assignments. Labels are interchangeable at the next arm precisely when their original incidence and all three upper partner membership bits agree and they have identical assignment histories. These histories also fix the remaining raw multiplicity. Within an arm the two raw labels are unordered because both possible first-injection choices are granted as independent optional taps. It is safe to test tap sets of exactly min(3−q, number of distinct optional features): extra available features can be ignored. On every surviving singleton and pair the program evaluates the four terms. Each private partner has to reject the singletons in its own group and those excluded by its independently established upper support. F must reject all singletons. For each term discard any valid candidate whose accepted pair set is contained in another valid candidate's pair set. The final finite test chooses one candidate per term and requires all pair positives. If no such choice exists even after the tap relaxation, that physical allocation is impossible.
