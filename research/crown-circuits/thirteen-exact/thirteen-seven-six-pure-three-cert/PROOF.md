# Six pure groups, three branching groups

This census concerns a normalized hypothetical 31-gate circuit for thirteen-input threshold two with seven ANDs. It assumes the two-OR-reader, pure-group degree, deletion, private-product and signed-shore bounds established in degree/t13-seven. There are three branching pure groups and three unshared pure groups. Each branching group has two outgoing occurrences. Each unshared group U_j feeds only its dedicated AND with partner P_j. Each such partner has fanout at least two. A private triple is clean, and a dirty private quartet is hot at exactly one label. Other ANDs are called ordinary below. There are four of them.

Generate the possible decorated pure graphs with quartet_models.cpp. Reconstruct the thirteen labels as pure edges plus one label for each unpaired pure incidence and finally labels with two mixed incidences. Private groups have at most one mutual edge. A branch with several private neighbors can have at most one whose incident product is cold on that branch–private edge: these singleton probes would otherwise coincide, as explained below. Thus at most three plus the number of dirty products are branch–private edges. Canonicalizing by relabeling the branch and private groups gives 257 geometries from the 22,202-row six-group source census.

## Private probes

Guess the hot label in each dirty private quartet. A singleton initially exposes its branching-group pins and its own raw pin if it has any mixed incidence. After a private product has executed, its hot indicator joins the exposed pins. Every other wire is monotone in the initial pins and any earlier exposed product outputs. Two identical final singleton probes are impossible: all unknown operands and all private partners agree, and opposite singleton shores of a private AND require different partner values. If final probe i is below final probe k, no unknown gate can separate them. One must have i in a private own-only shore and k in its partner-only shore. This forces the corresponding disjunction of partner memberships at k. These tests leave 382 hot choices on 153 geometries.

Enumerate all partitions of the three partners by physical equality and all six private product orders. Two clean private products cannot share a partner because they are terminal and factoring saves a gate. At the earliest use of a given partner, monotonicity excludes any singleton probe dominated by a probe of a required negative member of some U that uses this partner. The complement is an upper bound for its singleton support. The maximal possible partner has this entire upper support; all prescribed hot members must be in it, and every opposite exclusive shore has size at least three. Enlargement only adds private partner-side signs. Private product singleton values remain fixed.

The four ordinary ANDs provide four more coordinates. By the physical singleton mass inequality the number of ordinary coordinates whose operands are positive on label i is at most two plus the number of branching groups that contain i, less the number of private own-only shores at i. For a repeated partner add one as a relaxed credit for the unused unit of fanout surplus, and subtract one when the repeated partner is true at i. Indeed a group of m equal partners consumes m input occurrences per hot label and has at least max(m,2)−1 fanout surplus. Three distinct partners and the three branching pure groups exhaust all six surplus units. A repeated partner leaves one free surplus unit.

In each three-sign private fiber, the remaining compatible labels require disjoint four-dimensional partial-code cubes. A label able to specify at most c ordinary coordinates requires volume at least 2^(4−min(c,4)). Sharpen partner upper and lower bounds by monotonicity, by compulsory separation of comparable probes, by every opposite shore's size-three minimum, and by these fiber inequalities. If granting the spare fanout unit on a label is necessary, that label belongs to the singleton support of the remaining shared wire. This wire can reach an AND by an OR-only path, since an OR-only route to output is impossible for a hot wire. Its support is contained in that operand and therefore omits at least three labels from the opposite exclusive shore. These bounds plus optimistic evaluation of all 78 positive pairs leave 628 partition/order cases across 41 geometries.

## Ordinary singleton assignments and physical OR costs

Choose on each of thirteen labels a four-coordinate word in the four states neither, left only, right only, both. The search identifies choices that differ by axis permutation or independent reversals while fixing assigned rows. It enforces all required pair clashes, the per-label incidence caps, three witnesses per signed shore, and the minimum number of dirty ANDs implied by the nonfinal OR input bound in six-pure.md. If one exposed singleton probe is contained in another, both ordinary operand-membership bits are monotone across that pair. Exact code volumes can only increase in an assigned row; check every private fiber during search.

If q pure groups are quartets, their six raw OR trees cost 12+q OR gates. If h ANDs are dirty, the other 7−h clean terminals require 6−h collector ORs. At most 6−q+h ORs remain to construct internal operands. Their initial singleton supports are the three branching supports, each raw with a mixed incidence, and the seven AND output supports. Grant all of them independently of chronology. The eight ordinary operand supports must be obtainable by OR joins. Each distinct nonprimitive support consumes a separate join. When at most two additional joins remain, enumerate their binary recipes while closing under the mandatory supports whenever possible. At each completed library enumerate candidate private partner supports outside their U to check the required shores, repeated-partner identities and credits. This stage is intentionally permissive when more joins remain.

After cutting the named shared sources and all AND outputs, each remaining path contains only ORs. In the distinct-partner case every remaining intermediate has fanout one. Each branch output supplies two tokens, every mixed raw its physical multiplicity, every hot AND output one token, and a distinct partner one token after its dedicated use. The sinks are the eight ordinary AND operands and the partner definitions. Each token can supply only one sink, where its support must be a subset, and the assigned tokens must cover that sink's support. For a repeated partner group of size m, its minimum physical fanout is max(2,m), so only max(2,m)−m additional tokens are supplied. The remaining unit of surplus can be relaxed by waiving any one sink completely: delete one extra outgoing branch and follow that occurrence through the single-use OR forest to its first sink. The free singleton contribution may also be modeled exactly by an extra shared OR sink that has two output tokens, allowing a one-input copy when the original surplus augments a named source. The bounded token-cover backtracker returns feasible at its internal resource cap, so an unfinished search never excludes a case.

As a separate necessary test, simulate each of the 78 pairs. For each ordinary port optimistically OR every primitive or AND output whose singleton support fits that port. Each private own side is the actual fixed disjunction on its labels. Its partner receives the optimistic union under its upper support. Seven rounds dominate the outputs of any physical acyclic seven-AND circuit. Every pair must be accepted by at least one singleton-clean output. This simulation can overaccept and cannot certify a witness. The stronger partial-word causal test admits an AND only when the singleton values it requires can be obtained from primitive inputs and enabled AND outputs.

## Reproduction and status

Run `python3 replay.py` with a C++20 compiler and Python 3. The script reconstructs the decorated graph ledger, recomputes the singleton support restrictions, and rebuilds the finite code search in a temporary directory. Of 628 support/order records, 119 remain after identifying records that differ only in the three order fields: the computed partner bounds have already incorporated that order, and it is not read by the final solver. Every complete record must be rejected with search status zero; a feasibility result or resource cutoff fails verification. The replay checks the full node ledger byte-for-byte.

The computation excludes exactly the case of six pure groups with three branching groups under the cited thirteen-input normalization. It is a finite mathematical computation without a Lean proof of the full exclusion.
