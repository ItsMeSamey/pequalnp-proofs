# Four branching groups among five pure groups

Assume the regular thirteen-input, 31-gate, seven-AND model in census.md and private-probes.md. Denote the unique private pure group by U and its dedicated AND's shared partner by P.

In a branching-center pure star, each absent branching group contributes an extra gate beyond the constant-raw-pin telescope. If U is disjoint from the center, U becomes zero. Either its absorbing product has a single constant input, which saves an extra gate, or P is constant, in which case P pays at least one additional unit through its fanout. For a clean private center the shared partner rejects the retained singletons. Its pre-override expression can be retained as a final disjunct while overriding its occurrences by zero, since the center is forced true; the resulting credit is at least fanout(P)−1, including when the old output collapses. Thus for a branching or clean private center of size k and pure degree d, C(k)≤k+d. A triple needs at least one neighbor and a quartet at least three. A dirty private quartet needs at least two branching neighbors by the original star bound. A branching or clean private quartet forces at least two pure components by k≤H+2; it then has a component of size at least four, leaving an impossible isolated fifth vertex. All four branches are triples. A private dirty quartet has two branching neighbors in components of sizes three and two.

For a shared gate of fanout f true on m≥2 singleton labels, retain these labels, set all other raw inputs zero, and override the gate to true. Nonempty answers stay unchanged; the empty answer remains zero by monotonicity against a singleton. The 2(13−m) constant raw pins give the base saving. The overridden gate previously had at most one constant input under ordinary zero propagation (a nonzero gate with two fixed zero inputs is impossible). Its removal and f constant outgoing pins therefore give at least f extra savings, by the pin telescope. The output is nonconstant. Consequently C(m)≤5+2m−f. Restrict to ten or eight selected singleton labels respectively to obtain a support cap of nine for f=2 and seven for f=3.

The four branching triples credit 38 raw and shared singleton incidences; the exclusive AND shores need at least 49. The remaining fanout surplus two is either one f=3 wire or two f=2 wires. If the f=3 wire is AND its net credit after subtracting its hot mass is at most seven. If OR it has support seven, exactly three singleton-hot ANDs, and zero OR overlap: the excess budget is 38+14−49=3, and there are at least three dirty ANDs. The four clean terminals consume all three ordinary collector ORs. The shared OR therefore has one nonraw source, a branching triple or a singleton-hot dirty AND, and at most four raw inputs by the five-label star. Its support seven forces four distinct raw operands and a branching triple B. The four-label star at this shared OR, charging its own f−1=2 and each branching nonneighbor once, yields C(4)≤5+4−2−(4−d)=3+d for d branching neighbors. Thus d=4 and one raw input also belongs to B, contradicting zero overlap. Finally if either of the two f=2 wires is AND, its own singleton credit cancels its hot debit and the other contributes at most nine; 38+9<49. Therefore both shared wires have fanout two and are ORs, and every AND has fanout one. Their supports sum to at least 11 plus the total AND hot mass and OR overlap.
