# Required mixed attachments in a tight detached component

Let a subcubic ten-input host have exactly two connected components, with a degree-three center in one. The v−3 decoder OR budget equals the baseline from center-layer deletion, a detached component K of order s contributing s−1, and the final three-neighbor threshold. Suppose K has maximum degree two, so each of its pure groups is a doubleton. Write m,u,b for its numbers of mixed flags, private pure flags and branching pure flags.

Set every flag of K to zero. All m raw-injection ORs disappear. The u private doubletons each enter a distinct designated clean terminal that now vanishes, paying u collector ORs; at least one remaining terminal accepts pairs in the center. Each branching doubleton has two distinct OR readers. Reassociate each mixed group by joining all its nonraw terms first, then injecting its raw flag in the last OR. This preserves the 20−v flag factoring. A direct reader of a branching flag can then coincide with a charged remote injection only when the sole nonraw operand in that mixed group is that branching flag itself. A precollector reader of a branching flag is not a collector OR because the branch is positive on its original hot singletons. Occurrences in the other readers can be paired at most two per disappearing OR. If f of the 2b occurrences are in already-charged remote injections, the component costs at least

    m + u + ceil((2b−f)/2).

For b=0 this already exceeds s−1. For b>0 one must have f≥2. Both attachments go to distinct mixed flags within K. An attachment B→Y cannot target a neighboring group, since the raw shared with B would be redundant in Y. Nor can the distance be two, via an intermediate vertex V: let x be the raw on VY, y the raw on VB. At singleton y both of x's groups, V and Y, are already positive; hence adding x cannot change the output. Thus the two mixed targets have graph distance at least three from their respective branching sources.

Additionally any branching doubleton can have at least two remaining nontrivial components after deleting itself and its pendant neighbors: this is the s+1−t excess debit proved in ten-no-quartet.md. It follows that any detached cycle is excluded, irrespective of its length. A detached path with at most four edges is excluded: paths shorter than five vertices have no eligible branch, and in the five-vertex path the only eligible branch is the middle, whose eccentricity is two. For a path on six vertices the only possible attachments are from position 2 to 5 and position 3 to 0 (zero indexing). Both have to occur, and the two other exits must share a single gate having exactly these two branching flags as operands.
