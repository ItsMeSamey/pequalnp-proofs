# Five possible incidence graphs with a degree-four vertex

In the regular 24-gate, seven-AND ten-input circuit, the raw-bearing OR groups form a simple ten-edge graph. After factoring out each vertex flag (the OR of its incident raw labels), the decoder has v−3 ORs on v flags. A mixed flag has a dedicated reader OR where its raw flag is joined to a nonraw expression. Fixing any set of mixed flags to zero eliminates their separate reader ORs while keeping every syntactic path from a pure flag through the other operands.

Choose a degree-d center. First erase every all-mixed remote component, paying one OR per erased vertex. In the center component, peel distance layers to retain the star, paying one OR per discarded vertex. A nontrivial remote component on s flags costs at least s−1 ORs: peel down to one edge, then use a central edge versus its union with that remote edge to witness one final block. Pinning the center true leaves threshold two on d neighbors, which takes at least 2d−4 ORs. Thus at least d−2 detached pure-bearing components are required. Each detached component with a pure flag has at least two edges. Ten edges suffice only if 10≥d+2(d−2), so d≤4.

At d=4 every component has at most six edges and seven vertices. The NetworkX graph atlas lists all simple graphs on at most seven vertices. Enumerating component multisets with ten edges and at least three components containing vertices of degree at least two gives 32 hosts having a quartet. Among the detached components, now discard all mixed vertices. If k nontrivial pure components and h isolated pure flags remain, their savings include one OR when h>0: before peeling the other flags, one isolated pure flag must still reach an OR, since an AND-only path to the output would kill the central star when that flag is zero. The retained decoder bound therefore requires k+h−[h>0]≥2. The script allows every subset of nonleaf remote flags as pure, independently in each component and for each chosen center. This gives six hosts. One of these is a four-edge star with three two-edge paths. The star center would have to be pure by ten-doubletons.md, yet only four vertices have degree two or larger; the pure-group star inequality k+max(d_p,2)≤p+1 requires p≥5 for a pure quartet (d_p counts pure neighbors). Thus this sixth host also fails.

The five remaining types are a four-edge star together with one of the following remote pairs: two four-vertex paths; a four-vertex path and a triangle; two triangles; a three-vertex path and a five-vertex path; a three-vertex path and a four-cycle. In each the quartet is the unique vertex of degree at least three, hence must itself be pure. The four leaves are mixed raw injections. Only this envelope has been deduced; existence of a decoder on any listed host is open.

The layer accounting uses geometry/eleven27-seven/receiver-layers.md. The executable ten-quartet-hosts.py classifies necessary conditions only.
