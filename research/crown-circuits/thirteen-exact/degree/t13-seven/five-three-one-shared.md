# Three branches and a single nonpure shared root

The mixed-roots note excludes an AND root. A sole nonpure shared mixed OR P has fanout four and covers at most six labels (support telescope and C(7)=16). Both private products have P as their partner. They cannot both be clean: factoring P from their terminal union saves a gate. They cannot be adjacent by the indistinguishability cut in five-three-mixed-roots.md.

If a branching group is a quartet, at least one private quartet is dirty, so exactly two quartets exist. The pure graph is the disjoint union of a triangle and an edge. Nonadjacent private groups lie in different components. The other three groups are branches and have exactly one branch–branch edge. Hence their sizes 4,3,3 have union of cardinality nine. Each branch B has exactly one private neighbor, via a distinct raw x, so P must cover B minus x by the private–branch cut. These forced labels number six and are disjoint from both private groups. P must cover one further singleton inside the dirty private quartet, impossible.

Thus each branch is a triple. At least one dirty quartet remains, leaving at most ten mixed raw slots. The support identity gives 49+H≤35+3|P|, with H the dirty hot mass. Therefore |P|=6, H≤4, and the terminal count c is three or four. P has at most three raw terms by its four-label star (5+4−3<7). All ANDs are single-use.

When c=4 there is no available nonraw join before the collector. Each of the three dirty products covers at most two singleton labels. The single nonraw term of P must therefore be a branching triple augmented by three mixed raw terms. All five other branch exits and all three dirty outputs require a raw injection before their first AND, for at least eleven mixed raws.

When c=3 the four dirty products cover one label each, and one ordinary join remains before the collector. If P has two nonraw terms, let y among those be branching sources. Its raw slot count is at least 4−2y. Each of the other 6−y branch exits needs one raw and each of the 2+y other dirty outputs needs at least two raw occurrences, since its next AND operand has three exclusive singleton witnesses and no further nonraw join is possible. Their sum is 14−y≥12. If P has one nonraw term, this is again a branch plus three raw occurrences. One outside join can waive at most two of the nine initial exit payments. At least two of the four dirty outputs must separately pay an additional raw occurrence to reach the three-witness minimum. The total is at least twelve. This excludes all single-root cases.
