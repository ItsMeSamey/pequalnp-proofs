# Five all-triple pure groups, three branches, four terminals

The three branches have six exits; the other two triples are clean dedicated terminals with distinct shared partners. There are eleven mixed raw occurrences. The four clean terminals use all ordinary nonraw joins in their collector. Thus every physical mixed OR tree before the collector has exactly one nonraw source and acquires its further singleton support only by injected raw occurrences. A nontrivial unary-nonraw union tree strictly increases singleton support: if its added raw were already covered by its nonraw source, monotonicity would make that occurrence redundant. Every unspent branch exit needs at least one raw before it can go to an AND, and every unspent dirty output of singleton support h needs at least 3−h raws before its next AND. Here “unspent” means not already consumed as a formal source of a counted shared-root tree. These payments are distinct because there are no interior joins. There are three dirty products. A fanout-two mixed root has support at most eight, a fanout-three at most seven. At those caps it cannot precede another shared root by an OR-only path. The exclusive-shore cost is at least 49 and the raw-plus-branch credit is 35. The preceding five-three notes reduce the remaining cases to the following.

**Two shared mixed ORs of fanouts three and two.** Write M for the sum of their singleton supports, A for the fanout-three support, H for the total dirty hot mass. Then H≤M+A−14. With independent roots, let y of the two nonraw sources be branches and z=2−y dirty products (distinct by single use). If S is their selected dirty mass, the root raw count is at least M−3y−S. The remaining branch and dirty exits require at least 6−y+3(3−z)−(H−S) raws. Thus the total is at least M+9−y−H≥21−A≥14. If the earlier root has support a and feeds the later root of support b, the two trees have one external source, a branch (y=1) or dirty product (y=0). Telescoping the raw counts gives at least b+12−y−H≥b+11−H. If the earlier has fanout three then a≤6 and H≤b+2a−14<b. Otherwise a≤7 and b≤7 and b>a, hence H≤a+2b−14<b. Both alternatives use at least twelve raw occurrences.

**Three shared ORs, each fanout two.** Write M for their total support and H for the total dirty mass. We have H≤M−14. Let t be the number of internal OR-to-OR source occurrences across their three unary-nonraw trees, y the external branch sources, and z=3−t−y the selected dirty sources. If J is the sum of the supports of the internal parents, counted with multiplicity, summing the root raw requirements and all unused branch/dirty exit requirements gives at least M−J−3y−S+6−y+3(3−z)−(H−S)≥20+3t−y−J. If t=0 or 1 this exceeds eleven, since an internal parent has support at most seven. At t=2, a chain has parent supports summing to at most thirteen by strict increase and therefore needs at least twelve raw slots. In the remaining fork the count is at most eleven only for y=1 and common parent support seven; each child then covers eight. The initial branching triple receives four raw additions, and each child one. All five other branch exits therefore receive precisely one raw, exhausting all eleven. The three unused dirty outputs must go straight into subsequent AND operands; accordingly their supports are at least three each. Since H≤23−14=9, all equal three. The five branch exits with one raw each have linear support at most four. None can feed a dirty AND (operand support at least six), or a nondedicated clean AND (each raw-linear operand has support at least five by the four-cover theorem), or a dedicated product with its private triple and bare shared partner. There is no possible next gate.

**Two fanout-two mixed ORs and a fanout-two AND G.** The AND singleton credit cancels its support debit. The remaining dirty debits and the 49-cost give a+b≥16 for the two mixed root supports, hence a=b=8. G is dirty, and the other two dirty products have support one. The two eight-hot roots cannot reach each other by OR-only paths. Each has one nonraw source and at most four raw terms, requiring a source of support at least four. This can only be G, whose support is at most seven by its two disjoint exclusive shores of size three. Thus the roots consume both its exits and at least two raw occurrences. All six branch exits and both singleton-hot dirty exits remain. The former each need one raw, the latter two, giving at least twelve in all.
