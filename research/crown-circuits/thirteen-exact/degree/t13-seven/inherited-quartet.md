# A branching pure triple excludes every quartet in a 31-gate seven-AND thirteen-input candidate

Use the regular, clean-terminal thirteen-input representative. Suppose a raw-bearing group Q contains four raw labels and is not adjacent in the incidence graph to a branching pure triple U. Reassociate Q, joining its four raw inputs in a three-gate OR tree R before joining any nonraw operands. Its proper internal nodes have a single user inside R. Q and U share no label.

Deleting the three labels of U gives the established ten-input minimum, with 24 gates containing seven ANDs and seventeen ORs. All three gates of R survive because both sides of each gate are positive at retained singleton inputs. Every retained raw has two surviving OR readers by the physical-minor result in verification/thirteen-four/minor-components.md.

Apply the clean-terminal normalization to this circuit on ten inputs. It bypasses an OR with a clean and a nonclean parent to its nonclean parent, collecting the clean disjuncts in the output OR tree. This operation preserves all singleton tests at surviving wires. Every gate inside R has two nonclean parents, so none is bypassed. A proper internal node cannot gain an external user through redirection: its only original user was the next tree gate, which is never bypassed. R cannot become unused in the result, since simplification of an unused gate would strictly improve the minimum size. All seven AND gates survive: the operation changes only ORs, its new OR count is at most the old count, and dead or duplicated gates would contradict minimality.

Each original raw occurrence retains at least one occurrence after the bypasses. If its gate is bypassed, the raw is the nonclean input and propagates to a user of the old output. A finite chain of such bypasses ends at a surviving gate, because each nonclean bypassed signal has a path to a use in the minimum circuit and cannot itself be the clean output. Different original reads propagate along disjoint occurrence chains up to their first surviving input positions: every bypassed gate has only one retained predecessor; coincidence at the two operands of one gate would itself permit another gate saving. Thus every raw retains at least two readers. The ten-input physical reader bound (ten-minor.md) forbids three readers or an AND among two. No raw relocation is required after clean-terminalization. Flattening the surviving OR groups necessarily places all four raw leaves of R in one raw-bearing group, whether or not Q was pure originally. This contradicts ten-no-quartet.md and the degree-five-or-higher bound of ten-quartet-hosts.md.

It follows that every quartet is adjacent to every branching pure triple. Fix such a triple U and a quartet Q. In the thirteen-input decoder keep Q, all its neighbors, and all pure flags, bypassing the separate reader OR of each discarded mixed flag. All pure quartets and branching triples are in the retained component of Q: they are either adjacent to Q, or adjacent to U, which is itself adjacent to Q. Under the thirteen-input pure degree bounds, every remote pure flag must therefore be an unshared triple. Each has its own dedicated clean terminal, distinct from the terminals for the other such flags. Set these remote flags to zero. All their dedicated products vanish, and the collector loses at least one OR per product: some independent terminal still has to cover central star pairs. These collector savings are separate from the mixed-reader bypasses. Peel the remaining vertices of the central component outside the closed star in descending distance, saving at least one further OR per vertex. Starting with v−2 decoder ORs, the remaining five star flags have at most three ORs. With the central flag set true this realizes four-input threshold two, requiring four ORs. This contradiction excludes quartets whenever a branching pure triple exists.
