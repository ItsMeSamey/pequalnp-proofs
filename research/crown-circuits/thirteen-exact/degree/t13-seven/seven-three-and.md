# Every 16-gate threshold-two circuit on seven inputs with three ANDs is clean

Use the physical reverse circuit. An AND is a split into the two signed branches of its continuation; an OR produces copies; forward fanout produces reverse unions. A live binary circuit of size g on n raw variables uses g−n+1 binary reverse unions, after counting raw receivers as sinks. This budget is ten here.

Each raw singleton has a nonempty set of rejecting resolutions: select a false original operand at every false AND and either operand at every true AND. These seven deficit sets are pairwise disjoint because every resolved OR circuit accepts every pair. A dirty AND is active on at least one singleton, so in a rejecting resolution for that singleton its output is disconnected from the final output; otherwise that true signal would pass by an OR-only path. Flipping the disconnected decision therefore identifies two global resolutions, without changing the entire set of reachable signed raw inputs. Only one pair of the eight resolutions may be identified. Distinct dirty axes identify different cube edges, so at most one AND is dirty.

Suppose x is dirty. It is inactive on exactly one assignment to the other coordinates y,z. Rename their signs so this is 00. All other signed axes are globally active, since any other inactive axis identifies a different cube edge. Write B0,B1 and C0,C1 for the clean literal branches. The activity U of the dirty axis is B1 union C1, and its two branches A0,A1 restrict U to x=0 and x=1. The seven atoms are the collapsed cell o over 00, and the six separate points above 01,10,11. Each raw receiver is the complement of one atom.

For any one of the six points v, only three branch literals can enter its raw sink without covering v: the opposite A, B and C. All three must enter. The opposite x twin witnesses the need for A. The neighbor with flipped y or z witnesses the need for the corresponding clean literal; if the neighbor is the collapsed cell, neither dirty branch covers it. Thus the physical receiver needs exactly this triple of signs. It contains a binary join, and its ancestry contains an earlier join collecting exactly two of the three distinct signs. Each two-sign subset fixes two coordinates of an excluded point and therefore belongs to at most two of the six required triples. This costs at least three distinct pair joins, none of them a raw receiver.

All seven raw sinks require a join since no single branch is an atom complement. The reverse activity U also needs a binary join before the dirty split: it depends on both clean axes and is neither a constant nor one clean literal. U equals the seventh raw receiver semantically, but these are different physical computations. The dirty singleton must be the row rejecting the collapsed atom, since any hot singleton rejects only when the dirty output is disconnected. Its raw has an OR-only route to each of the two inputs of this dirty AND: if such a hot route crossed a different AND, that AND would also be hot. In reverse, the receiver therefore takes contributions from both signed outputs A0 and A1 after the split. It cannot be the pre-split activity site. U is incomparable with every other receiver and is not one of the pair helpers serving the six triples. We have counted seven receiver joins, three pair joins, and the separate activity join. Eleven exceeds the budget of ten.

The bound is sharp with clean gates. Group a,b, then c,d, then e,f in three pair ORs, and treat g as a fourth group. Distinguish the four groups by their two binary coordinate cuts. The third cut separates a,c,e from b,d,f. Three ANDs and thirteen ORs implement the test. Every pair of different groups trips a group cut and the third cut handles the three within-group pairs.
