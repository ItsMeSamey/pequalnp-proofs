# Five all-triple groups, three branches and three terminals

Two private triples are clean dedicated terminals with distinct shared partners. Call the third clean terminal Z. Four dirty ANDs, six branch exits and eleven mixed raw slots remain. Each mixed group has at most three raw slots by the inherited-quartet result. Only k=2 mixed shared ORs of fanouts (3,2) or k=3 of fanouts (2,2,2) remain: two fanout-two ORs and a fanout-two AND contribute at most sixteen net singleton credits before the three other dirty debits, below fourteen. All ANDs are single-use. Exactly one ordinary nonraw join lies before the collector. Let M be the sum of mixed root supports and H the dirty singleton mass. Then M−H≥14−A at k=2, where A≤7 is the fanout-three support, and M−H≥14 at k=3. Each shared OR preceding another by OR-only paths has support at most seven by the saturated-root clamp.

In the shared OR trees let t count internal shared parent terms (total support J), y count branch terms, and z count dirty terms (total support S). Dirty terms are distinct. With e outside ordinary joins, y+z+t=k+1−e. The trees require at least M−J−3y−S raw slots.

Unused branch exits each need one raw. Each unspent dirty output of support h needs at least 3−h raws before its next AND. One outside join can waive at most three payments: only two singleton-hot dirty outputs could cost four separately, and their union still needs one raw for the next AND's three singleton witnesses. A join reaching a shared OR through ORs is counted inside that shared tree. If e=0, at most two of the 4−z unspent dirty exits enter Z. None enter the private terminals with their fixed bare shared partners. The others enter dirty ANDs of operand support at least four. This gives an extra max(0,2−z) payment. Even when 3−h is negative the stronger bound 4−h holds.

Summing the payments and eliminating y gives R≥M−H+14−4k+4t+z−J+e+(1−e)max(0,2−z). A repeated source within one formal OR tree is redundant. Three distinct internal terms among three roots form a transitive triangle, whose direct initial-to-last term is redundant because the middle OR contains the initial root. Thus t≤k−1 and J≤7t.

For k=2 the bound is R≥20−A+4t+z−J+e+(1−e)max(0,2−z). If e=0 the two z terms combine to at least two, yielding at least twelve for t=0,1. If e=1, t=0 exceeds eleven. At t=1 the bound 25−A−J+z requires z=0 and A=J=7. The earlier unary-nonraw root starts at a branch triple and has at most three raw slots, so J cannot be seven.

For k=3 we obtain R≥16+4t+z−J+e+(1−e)max(0,2−z). With e=0 this is at least 18−3t≥12. With e=1 it is at least 17−3t+z. Only t=2,z=0,J=14 can survive. Three unary-nonraw trees with two internal terms form a chain or fork. Their earliest root is an internal parent and starts at a branch triple, so has support at most six, contrary to J=14.
