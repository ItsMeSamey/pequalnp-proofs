# No degree-four incidence flag in a minimum ten-input seven-AND circuit

Use the regular representative and the five-host census in ten-quartet-hosts.md. A degree-four center is a pure four-edge star, and every remote pure group is a doubleton. There are exactly two detached components. The layer lower bound v+4−5−2=v−3 meets the entire decoder OR budget, so neither detached component of order s can cost more than the baseline s−1 in that peeling argument.

Let w be a branching pure doubleton in a detached component K. Its two readers are distinct ORs by the branching-doubleton deletion lemma. Every pendant neighbor of w is a mixed leaf. Its raw flag is one of the two raw labels already included in w. Thus its flattened group cannot itself read w, since that would make the raw occurrence redundant in the original minimum circuit. Set w and all l pendant neighbors to zero. This removes two distinct OR readers of w and the l separate mixed-flag injection ORs, without overlap. Equivalently these are distinct original gates with a designated operand clamped to zero. With t nontrivial components left in K after those deletions, each can now be peeled down to an anchor edge and its anchor erased by the central-edge witness. Every remaining component has at least two vertices because the only newly isolated vertices were the pendant neighbors removed. The additional debit is (s−1−l)−t. In total K therefore costs at least s+1−t. Since K has no slack, t≥2.

In a triangle, four-cycle, three-vertex path or four-vertex path, removing any degree-two vertex and its pendant neighbors leaves at most one component containing an edge. In a five-vertex path only the middle vertex leaves two such components. The five hosts in the census have at most one five-vertex path in total. Thus there is at most one remote branching pure group.

On the other hand, write b for the number of remote branching pure groups and p for the total number of pure groups. Exactly p−1−b remote doubletons are private. Each has a distinct dedicated clean AND terminal (its shared partner cannot be a second private pure group). All of these terminals vanish on a pair of raw labels in the central star. Some other clean terminal must accept that pair. Hence the number c of clean terminals satisfies c≥p−b. The p−3 nonraw OR mergers must include the c−1 collector ORs, so c≤p−2. This forces b≥2, a contradiction.

Consequently every ten-input size-24 seven-AND candidate has maximum incidence degree three. The previously established all-doubleton exclusion requires at least one pure triple.
