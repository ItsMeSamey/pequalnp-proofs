# Three pure groups with one private triple

All three groups have size three. The private group has a clean dedicated product, and some other terminal is needed since two labels outside the private group form a positive pair. Reassociate the output as F OR (U AND P). This uses the only ordinary OR allowed outside the three pure groups. The two branching groups supply the indicator bits B0 and B1. Only dedicated AND port U can read the private group. The functions F and P are therefore conjunctions of B0, B1 and outputs of surviving marked raw injections. Each retained raw with one mixed incidence contributes one such gate.

In the table, encode pure incidences with weights 1 for B0, 2 for B1 and 4 for U. In each row keep all listed pure-edge labels and the indicated mixed labels. The middle row covers a path with the private group at its center, and the last row covers a path with the private group at one end. Interchanging the branching vertices gives the other orientation.

Evaluate the two branching sources and U on all retained singleton and pair assignments. Starting from the branching sources, close under all conjunctions including constants. For every order of the retained mixed inputs, enumerate each new signal as its specified raw OR an available conjunction. Finally test F OR (U AND P) for every available pair of conjunctions F and P. No formula rejects every singleton and accepts every pair in any row. The independent truth-table implementation three_private.py checks these three cases directly. A separate chronological SAT encoding in few_groups.py and private_three.py gives the same three obstructions.

For three branching groups with at least two internal raw edges, two more restrictions suffice. If the pure-edge graph is a path 0–2–1, retain its two edges plus both mixed neighbors of 0 and both mixed neighbors of 1. The six incidence masks are (1,1,5,2,2,6). For a triangle, retain its three edges and one mixed neighbor of every vertex, obtaining (3,5,6,1,2,4). Allow all three pure indicators as free sources, one arbitrary binary OR, and one marked OR for each retained mixed occurrence, interspersed with free conjunctions. Every schedule can order the first occurrences of identical-incidence mixed labels by relabeling those equivalent inputs. Both resulting CNFs are UNSAT, independently certified by drat-trim. Run three_edge_replay.py with drat-trim and a temporary directory. The edgeless and one-edge graphs remain outside these exclusions.

For the remaining one-edge graph, keep its eight raws meeting at least one pure group: masks (3,1,1,2,2,4,4,4). The sole ordinary OR has one common location and two common operands across all tested assignments. On the three raws of one pure group, restrict all other raws to zero. The pure group indicator is one on each of the seven nonempty assignments. Each pure–pure raw is supplied freely by its other pure-group indicator; each pure–mixed raw can only enter through its own marked injection. Foreign injections reduce to conjunctions and can be absorbed. Sort the equivalent mixed labels within each triple by their chronological injection order. For each possible position of the ordinary OR among these local injections, local_schedule.py exhausts the two ordinary operands and all allowed injections/ANDs. Retain those operand traces that permit threshold two on this triple. Requiring these traces jointly in few_groups.py, on the local singleton and pair assignments, leaves an unsatisfiable CNF for the eight selected raws. Its DRAT proof is verified by hard_star_replay.py with final argument 1.

For the edgeless graph retain its nine covered raws. This yields three disjoint triples. Each individual raw has exactly one marked OR occurrence outside its pure group. A single shared ordinary OR and unrestricted ANDs complete the relaxed model. Permute labels within each triple so its three marked injections are chronological x, y, z. The exact three-variable trace list in local_schedule.py has the following form. The ordinary OR occurs after two or three injections on every triple. The three marked outputs on this triple are exactly x, y, z OR (x AND y). Up to transposing the two ports, the ordinary OR's left input accepts the pair xz and rejects yz, and its right input does the reverse. The checker uses these trace requirements on the six local singleton and pair queries.

There are four global possibilities. If the ordinary gate follows exactly 6+e injections, e triples have completed all three injections and 3−e have completed two. Rename triples to place the e complete ones first, and within each of the complete and incomplete groups order them by their first injection. Choose the left input of the ordinary OR to accept xz on triple zero. These renamings preserve every circuit in at least one search representative. All four cases, e=0,1,2,3, are unsatisfiable.
