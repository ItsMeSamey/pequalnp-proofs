# No seven-pure-group candidate at thirteen inputs, 31 gates and seven ANDs

Use the definitions and arguments in census.md. The nine-input composition theorem tightens the four-variable deletion budget. Every pure group is a triple or quartet. A branching group has fanout two and only OR readers. An unshared group directly feeds a dedicated AND with a shared partner. Distinct unshared groups feed distinct ANDs, and two clean dedicated ANDs cannot share a partner because their outputs are collected at the final OR. Each dirty dedicated AND has a quartet as its private operand and is true on exactly one singleton of that quartet. Every physical AND has at least three singleton witnesses on each exclusive shore.

There are six units of internal fanout surplus. For p=7 let b be the number of branching pure groups and s=7−b the number of private groups. Their partners must all be shared nonpure wires. If all private ANDs were clean, b branching groups and s partners would require seven surplus units. At least one private quartet is therefore dirty. There are at most two quartets in the surviving pure graphs. The private groups induce at most one internal raw edge: restricting to edges between such groups activates nothing other than their sole dedicated AND operands, whose distinct shared partners cannot be the first nonzero signal. If s≥5 this would require at least 3s−1>13 raw labels.

When exactly one private AND is dirty, s−1 clean ANDs require distinct partners, already accounting for all 6−b=s−1 surplus units. Each partner therefore has fanout two and the dirty quartet's AND shares a partner with one of the clean ANDs. When both private quartets are dirty, the graph census below leaves s=2 or 3. For s=2 a single shared partner of fanout two serves both. For s=3 the two available surplus units allow either one common fanout-three partner or two fanout-two partners, one used twice and the other used once at these ANDs. These cases cover all partner groupings up to permuting the private roles.

For each admitted decorated graph the input labels are its pure edges, one further label for each pure incidence not consumed by those edges, and remaining labels with no pure occurrence. Thus at each label i one knows its membership in every private input Uj, and the number hᵢ of branching pure groups containing it. Guess the partner singleton supports P. Each clean Uj must be disjoint from its partner, and each dirty Uj has intersection exactly one. Each opposite shore P outside Uj has size at least three.

Use the signed biclique cover and per-label fanout identity of degree/t11-26/singleton-mass.md. At the s dedicated coordinates a label is constrained to its sign if it belongs to exactly one operand. Let cᵢ be the sum over these coordinates of membership in the union of the two operands, minus the sum over their distinct partner signals of (fanout−1) times membership there. The remaining 7−s coordinates can specify at most 2+hᵢ−cᵢ bits at label i, because all shared wires and their credits have been accounted for. A negative value is impossible. In every one of the 2^s fibers, the compatible labels require disjoint partial-code cubes on the remaining coordinates. Their minimum volumes are 2 raised to the power (7−s) minus the lesser of (7−s) and (2+hᵢ−cᵢ). Reject the partner assignment when the sum exceeds the fiber size.
