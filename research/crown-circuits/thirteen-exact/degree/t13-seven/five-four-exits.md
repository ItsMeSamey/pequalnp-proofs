# Four branching groups among five pure groups

Use five-four-branching.md and the support-eight refinement: four raw deletions save eight ORs by census.md. On nine hot labels an f2 root clamped true gives size≤21 while retaining ≤16 ORs, impossible. Constant-propagation simplifications already available after raw deletion remain available when an originally nonconstant wire is clamped. An eight-hot root cannot lead by OR-only paths into the other shared root: keeping those eight and clamping the first gives the tight size 19 before the second contributes its additional fanout surplus. Each shared root has at most four raw terms by its five-label star. The five pure groups leave at most 26−15=11 mixed raw occurrences.

Let c count clean terminal products, H total hot singleton incidences, and a,b the shared-root support sizes. The exclusive-shore mass gives a+b≥11+H. Thus H≤5, 2≤c≤4. All ANDs have fanout one. Flatten private OR trees up to raw inputs, branching pure roots, ANDs, and shared mixed roots. The two shared OR trees contain y branching-triple source occurrences and z dirty-AND source occurrences. In the serial case one also contains the earlier root. Two occurrences of that root in the same private union tree permit an idempotent deletion, impossible in a minimum. The dirty source occurrences are distinct by fanout one. A clean AND is a collector term.

All 8−y unused branch exits must enter proper ORs. Every unused dirty exit of support ≤2 must enter a proper OR before the next AND, since every AND operand has at least three exclusive witnesses. None of these exits can go to the clean collector. Outside both shared-root trees, such exits each pay a distinct mixed raw occurrence unless a tree with k≥2 nonraw terms absorbs them. Those trees use k−1 ordinary joins each, so at most 2e exits can avoid payment with e outside joins. These occurrences are disjoint from the shared-root trees' raw terms. Private trees ending at an operand with no raw and one nonraw source perform no OR operation.

At c=2, both roots cover eight labels, all five dirty products cover one each, and OR overlap vanishes. The roots are independent by the eight-hot restriction. Each needs two nonraw terms since at most four raw terms and one nonraw term cover at most seven. The two available ordinary interior joins therefore put precisely two nonraw terms in each root and none elsewhere. They need 12−2y raw terms across both. The other 8−y branch exits and 1+y dirty exits each need a raw, totaling 21−2y≥13.

At c=3 there are four dirty products, each covering at most two labels, with total excess beyond one at most one. There is one interior ordinary join. For independent roots, y+z≤3−e and the roots need at least 15−3y−z−[z>0] raws. The unused exits need at least 12−y−z−2e more. Their sum exceeds eleven because 4y+2z+[z>0]+2e≤12. For serial roots the earlier covers at most seven and the later covers eight. Telescoping the root supports gives at least 8−3y−z−[z>0] root raws with y+z≤2−e. Adding the same unused exits gives at least twelve because 4y+2z+[z>0]+2e≤8.

At c=4 there are three dirty gates with total support ≤5, so at most one covers three or more singletons. Each root has one nonraw term, e=0. Independent roots require at least 14−6=8 raw occurrences in their trees plus six unused branch exits. Serial roots require one external term of size ≤3; the later covers at least seven. Hence they consume ≥4 raws, plus ≥7 unused branch exits and ≥1 unused dirty exit of support ≤2. This is at least twelve.

All cases exceed eleven mixed raw occurrences.
