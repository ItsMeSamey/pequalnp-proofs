# Five pure groups with at most one branch

The zero-branch case is excluded by the first-AND argument in census.md. For one branch let q count quartets and d dirty private groups. There are at least 15+q−13=2+q pure edges. The private probe lemma in private-probes.md permits one private–private edge, one nonhot branch–private edge, and at most d hot branch–private edges. Since d≤q, all these inequalities are equalities. Every quartet is private and dirty, the sole branch is a triple, and has degree 1+q. Hence q≤2.

When q=0, the four private triples are clean. They cover eleven distinct labels because there is exactly one private–private edge. Their four terminal products reject the pair of remaining labels, requiring a fifth terminal. The three ordinary ORs can collect at most four terminals.

For any dirty private quartet U, the enhanced star inequality in census.md gives 4+max(2,deg(U))+#(clean groups disjoint from U)≤7. At most one clean group misses U. If q=1, three clean groups exist and at least two must meet U, requiring two private–private edges. If q=2, each dirty quartet needs a neighbor among the two clean groups. These are distinct private–private edges. Both alternatives exceed the unique private–private edge.
