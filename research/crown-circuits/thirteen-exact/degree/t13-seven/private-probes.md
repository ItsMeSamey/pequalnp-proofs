# Singleton separation at private products

For every private pure group U cut its sole dedicated product U∧P_U and treat that product's value as an external signal. The other external signals are the branching pure outputs and the individual raw occurrences in mixed groups. This preserves the singleton values of all uncut wires when supplied the actual external singleton evaluations. Every private U was used only at its own product; no raw enters an AND directly. An unknown AND and both its operands, as well as every shared partner P_U, are thus monotone in the external signals.

For a raw i joining two pure groups, let e(i) record its incident branching groups and the dirty private products hot on i. There are no active mixed raw occurrences. For another label j with e(i)⊆e(j), every unknown AND operand at i is bounded by its value at j; the same holds for every partner. Consequently no unknown AND can separate these two labels in opposite singleton shores. To cover their pair, a private group U must have i in its private exclusive shore and j in its partner exclusive shore. If both i,j are pure-only and e(i)=e(j), even the private products cannot separate them: their partner values agree. Therefore the pure-only probe vectors are distinct.

A branch–private edge which is not the hot singleton of the private product produces exactly the singleton probe for that branch. Hence at most b such edges exist, and the total number of branch–private edges is at most b+d, since the d dirty private quartets each have one hot raw. There are at most one private–private edge and at most b(b−1)/2 branch–branch edges. Counting all pure edges gives

    3p+q−13 ≤ 1+b+d+b(b−1)/2,

where q is the number of pure quartets. At p=6 and b=2 this is 5+q≤4+d, impossible because d≤q. With five or six branches excluded by six-pure.md, every six-pure-group candidate has exactly three or four branching groups. At p=5,b=1 equality requires q=d, so the branching group is a triple and every quartet is private and dirty.

A hot branch–private edge between B,U has partner P_U true when the external B pin alone is supplied: P_U cannot depend on its own product, and there is no other active private or mixed input at that singleton. In particular every singleton on B activates P_U. A nonhot neighbor V of B can separate this hot singleton from its own B edge only if P_V changes from false to true. This requires the product at U to be upstream of P_V, yielding an acyclic ordering constraint on such adjacencies.

For a dirty private quartet U, its singleton-hot product G at x has one OR reader group H. H cannot contain a raw of U as operand. Adding x would be redundant since x≤G. For another raw z in U, re-associate G∨z=U∧(P_U∨z) within H, preserving size and two OR readers per raw. The rewritten AND would have at most two exclusive U singletons, contradicting the physical three-shore bound.

For a fixed topological order of the private products, every other wire can be regarded as a monotone function of the exposed branching-group bits, the individual raw inputs having a mixed incidence, and the preceding private product outputs. Record these bits on the thirteen singleton assignments. On a pair assignment, a monotone partner must be false if the current probe vector is coordinatewise below some singleton probe on which that partner is false. Otherwise optimistically set the partner true. Use its computed value at both ports if the two partners coincide. The private product itself is the AND of this upper bound and its known private-group disjunction. Induction shows the computed pair probe dominates its actual value: increasing a probe can only remove constraints from dominating rejecting singletons. At the end each of the 78 pairs must escape domination by every final singleton probe. The executable probe_envelope.cpp applies this cut to both admissible private orders.
