# Constraints for three branching groups out of five

The raw and branching singleton credits sum to at most 26+12=38. The physical fanout surplus outside the pure groups is 3. At least one mixed OR is shared. Indeed if all shared nonpure gates are ANDs, a fanout-two AND credits its own hot mass exactly and a fanout-three AND nets at most seven after that debit. Three fanout-two gates or fanout-three plus two therefore give total exclusive mass at most 45. A single fanout-four AND with support h nets 2h; seven hot singletons would give C(7)≤5+14−4=15, impossible. Since c≤4 there are at least two other dirty ANDs in this last case. The total exclusive credit is then at most 38+12−2=48, below the required 49.

For a private–private edge x with private endpoints U,V, their dedicated partners P,Q must cover every other singleton between them. If both reject singleton y, adding x changes only U,V until the first change beyond them. Such a change must be through a dedicated product, whose other parent is still false. Thus the output cannot distinguish y and xy. This contradicts threshold two. In particular adjacent private groups cannot have the same partner, since a shared partner covers at most eight labels. This argument permits dependencies between different private products or their partners.

Using the two-remote component bound for pure quartets (receiver-layers.md), there are at most two quartets. With q quartets at least 2+q pure edges exist. If q≥3, a five-vertex disconnected graph with ≥5 edges needs a four-vertex component, yet every quartet must be in a component with at most three vertices. If q=2, four edges are necessary. No four-vertex component can coexist with two quartets outside it; the only feasible component pattern is a complete triangle and a separate edge. These deductions apply to any five-group branch count.

More generally, if raw x feeds private U and a second OR group R, then P_U accepts every singleton y≠x accepted by R. Otherwise on input y+x the additional raw occurrence in R cannot change that already-true signal; the only second route exits U through the dedicated product. Before that product changes, its partner has its old false value by acyclicity and the already-true R route. Thus the product cannot change either, contradicting threshold two. In particular P_U contains all other raw labels of every neighboring branching pure group. The statement also applies to a mixed OR group R and to a second private group (giving the raw members of that group's support).
