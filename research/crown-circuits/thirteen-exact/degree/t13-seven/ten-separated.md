# Two independent two-wire encoders cannot give a seven-AND optimum on ten inputs

Partition the ten primary inputs into two nonempty blocks. Suppose that each is processed by an independent monotone circuit with at most two exported wires, followed by an arbitrary decoder on these wires. Gates are binary monotone AND and OR with free fanout. Assume a 24-gate implementation of threshold two.

For a block of k≥2 inputs, its three weight classes 0, 1, ≥2 have pairwise disjoint images. Otherwise setting the other block to zero or to a singleton distinguishes equal codes. Along every chain passing through 0, a singleton, and a pair, the two-bit encoding rises strictly twice. Hence zero encodes as 00, each singleton as 01 or 10, and every weight at least two as 11.

Write C(k) for minimum threshold-two size. If only one singleton code occurs, the encoder computes OR-all and threshold two on separate wires. Its cost is at least C(k)+1: replace the OR-all site by symbolic top and simplify each downstream use (top OR s = top, top AND s = s). Since the threshold output is not top, the resulting circuit requires no external value for top. If both singleton codes occur, appending an AND computes threshold two, giving cost at least C(k)−1.

For two blocks of sizes k,l≥2, when both codes use a single singleton orientation, the decoder takes at least three gates. When only one block has a single orientation, the decoder takes at least five gates. The adjacent Python verifier exhausts all smaller circuits on respectively 9 and 12 promised rows. When both blocks exhibit both orientations, the decoder computes ordinary threshold two on four free bits, requiring seven gates. Consequently every case costs at least C(k)+C(l)+5. The known values at k=2,…,8 are 1,4,7,10,13,16,19. Thus each partition costs at least 25.

For block sizes 1 and 9, the singleton block already exports its entire information as the raw bit. In the two-orientation case, the decoder is full threshold two on three bits and requires four gates. At total size 24 the nine-block must have 20 gates, so appending an AND yields a 21-gate optimum with four ANDs by the nine-input composition theorem. The encoder has exactly three ANDs. A four-gate majority-three decoder uses two ANDs: with at most one OR its DNF has at most two terms, and the dual rules out at most one AND. The total is five. In the single-orientation case the decoder needs at least two gates, and the encoder needs at least 22. At equality the symbolic-top reduction loses exactly one gate, the OR-all gate itself. Its first realization is an OR since neither input to an AND could strictly precede top without already being top. The encoder retains the four ANDs of the nine-input optimum. The two-gate decoder cannot bring the total to seven.

The hypothesis is a disjoint partition of the actual computation through two exported wires per block. A disconnected graph of raw incidences alone does not assert this interface.
