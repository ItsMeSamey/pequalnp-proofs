# A singleton-support inequality for binary monotone threshold-two circuits

Let S(v) be the set of inputs i for which wire v is true on singleton i. For every AND with inputs u,v form a biclique whose shores are S(u) minus S(v) and S(v) minus S(u). These bicliques cover the complete graph on input labels. Indeed the output accepts any fixed positive pair i,j and accepts neither singleton. At an OR choose a child with the same property. At an AND both children accept the pair. If one rejects both singletons descend into it. Otherwise they must accept opposite singletons and neither accepts the other's singleton, since their conjunction rejects both.

Give each input i a partial binary word, specifying one bit per biclique that contains i and using its shore as the bit value. Distinct inputs clash on some specified bit. Their sets of binary extensions are disjoint, so sum_i 2^(−L_i)≤1, where L_i is the number of bicliques incident to i. By convexity, sum_i L_i ≥ n log₂ n. In particular this sum is at least 39 when n=11.

At an OR the two input support sizes minus the output support size equal the size of their intersection. At an AND this difference equals the size of their symmetric difference plus the output support size. Summing over all physical gates counts every internal wire with its fanout minus one, every raw with its fanout, and the output with support size zero. Hence the total biclique incidence is exactly the sum of raw fanouts plus the sum over internal gates of (fanout−1) times their support size, minus the total support sizes of all ANDs and the total input-support intersections at OR gates. The same equality holds separately for each label with set sizes replaced by membership indicators.

For a regular size-26 circuit on eleven inputs there are 22 raw occurrences and five units of internal fanout surplus. Their support-weighted contribution must be at least 17 plus both the AND-hot and OR-overlap masses. If four branching pure triples use four surplus units, the final surplus must belong to an OR: an AND's own support cancels its entire shared credit. That OR must have at least five singleton witnesses, plus the full AND-hot and OR-overlap masses. These bounds are necessary conditions and do not depend on chronological normalization.

In the p=4, b=4 case with four branching triples, the final OR collector permits at most two clean terminals (r=1), hence at least five dirty ANDs. The one remaining shared wire must be a mixed OR H with at least ten hot singletons. An all-singleton-hot wire computes the full disjunction and can be eliminated in threshold two, so H is hot on exactly ten and there are precisely two clean terminals. The final collector consumes the unique mixed-group overhead. Thus H has precisely one nonraw input, either a branching pure group (three hot labels) or an AND (at most one, since all five dirty tolls must be unit). It needs at least seven raw leaves. Each branching triple has two OR readers; all eight downstream mixed groups are distinct because each has a single nonraw input. Each has at least one raw leaf. Even counting H as one of them gives fourteen mixed raw leaves, but only 22−12=10 are available. This p=4 configuration is impossible.

The plain star restriction applies even to a mixed group carrying k≥2 raw labels. Hardwire its output true; nonempty star inputs already make it true, and the empty input still rejects by monotonicity. There are d different adjacent pure groups giving raw signals, and the other variables have one injection in distinct mixed groups. The first max(0,2−d) such injections cost no OR. The removed group's own overhead t−1 is unavailable, hence k+max(d,2)≤r+5−t. This uses no component pinning.
