# Four pure triples in the thirteen-label candidate

Assume a regular 31-gate, seven-AND minimum circuit. The surviving four-pure types have four branching triples or three branching triples and one private triple. Every receiving group has degree at most three. There are 14 mixed raw occurrences, two nonraw OR joins and six fanout-surplus units. A branching triple has two OR readers and uses one surplus. The c clean terminal products consume c−1 collector joins. Each dirty product has singleton support h≥1 and operands of support at least h+3. Signed mass demand is 49; raw credit is 26, each surplus use credits its source support, and each dirty product debits its support. The support caps at fanouts two, three and four are eight, seven and six. A saturated shared root cannot feed another shared root by OR-only paths. These are the prerequisites in the degree/t13-seven census, inherited-quartet and five-three-three-terminals notes.

With four branching triples baseline is 38 and two excess units remain. A sole fanout-three OR gives at most fourteen less at least four dirty debits, below 49. Sharing an AND does no better since its first extra credit pays its own debit. Two fanout-two ORs are necessary. At c=1 the maximum is 38+16−6=48. At c=3 their sum is at least fifteen and each dirty gate supports at most two. With all joins in the collector the earlier root has one nonraw source of support at most three, plus at most three raws, below seven. At c=2 both roots have support eight and all dirty gates support one. The roots are OR-incomparable by saturation, so both must use two nonraw terms. Only one join is available.

With three branching triples baseline is 35 and three excess units remain. At least two terminals are needed because the private terminal cannot cover pairs outside its triple. Sharing an AND misses 49: two fanout-two ORs and a shared AND give at most 35+16−3=48; a fanout-three AND plus a fanout-two OR gives at most 35+7+8−3; a fanout-three OR plus a shared AND at most 35+14−3; a fanout-four AND at most 35+12−3. Spending more units on ANDs weakens these estimates. A single fanout-four OR works numerically only at c=3 with support six and four singleton-hot dirty products. Its only possible source is a branch, requiring three more raws. Five other branch exits need one raw each and four dirty exits need two each. Sixteen exceed fourteen.

The remaining k nonpure shared roots are ORs, either two of fanouts three and two, or three of fanout two. All dirty products are single-use. Let M be the sum of shared-root supports and H the sum of dirty supports. At k=2 let a denote the fanout-three support, so M−H≥14−a. At k=3, M−H≥14. In the flattened shared-root trees count t internal occurrences of prior shared ORs, with total parent support J counted with multiplicity. Let y count external branch occurrences and z external dirty sources. The trees require at least M−J−3y minus the sum of selected dirty supports in raw occurrences. The 6−y unspent branch exits need one raw each. An unspent dirty source needs at least 3−h raws before the next AND. An outside join can waive at most three payments: joining two singleton dirty sources still requires at least one raw to attain support three. This is the outside-join lemma of five-three-three-terminals.md. With no outside join, any unused dirty exit reaching a dirty destination costs one additional raw beyond 3−h. At c=2 only the two ports of the nondedicated clean terminal can take such exits for the nominal payment, since the private product has fixed operands.

At c=3 there are four dirty gates, no spare join and y+z=k−t. At c=2 there are five dirty gates, one spare join, and y+z=k+1−e−t, where e=1 if the join is outside the trees. In either case before extra dirty-destination payments the lower bound on mixed raws is

    R ≥ M−H+18−3k+3t−y−J.

For example, at c=2 the initial expression is M−H+21−4y−3z−J−3e. No proper unary mixed OR group can add a raw already accepted on its singleton by its nonraw source, since that raw occurrence is redundant. Repetition or a direct transitive occurrence of another source inside an OR tree is redundant.

At c=3,k=2, R≥26+3t−a−y−J. If t=0 this exceeds fourteen. At t=1 a fanout-three parent is unsaturated, with a≤6. A fanout-two parent has strictly smaller support than the fanout-three child because the proper unary child adds a fresh raw. In both cases R≥15. At c=3,k=3, R≥23+3t−y−J. For t≤1 the bound exceeds fourteen. At t=2 the roots form a chain or fork because every tree is unary in nonraw sources. In a chain both parent supports are at most seven and strictly increasing, so J≤13. In a fork feasibility requires parent support seven and y=1, but then this earliest root has only one branch and at most three raws. No case fits.

At c=2 include the extra dirty-destination payments. The strengthened bound is

    R ≥ M−H+17−4k+4t+z−J+e+(1−e)max(0,3−z).

Indeed 3−z counts the unused dirty exits in excess of the two nondedicated clean ports. At k=2,e=0 the bound is at least 26−a+4t−J>14 because t≤1 and J≤7t. At e=1,t=0 it is at least seventeen; at e=1,t=1 at least 28−a−J+z. Reaching fourteen would require z=0 and a=J=7. A fanout-three parent would be saturated, which is impossible. Otherwise the earlier unary root is branch-fed and cannot supply seven.

At k=3, three internal occurrences would repeat a source or include a redundant transitive shortcut, so t≤2. For a convergence into one root the parents overlap by at least J−8 since the child supports at most eight, restoring this wasted payment in the original raw bound and exceeding fourteen. In a chain or fork J≤7t by saturation. At e=0 the strengthened bound is at least 22−3t≥16. At e=1 it is at least 20−3t+z, so only t=2,z=0,J=14 could fit. The earliest unary root would then have a branch source and at most three raws, insufficient for seven. There are no remaining four-pure cases.
