# Three adjacent quartets and an isolated quartet

The nine surviving four-pure-group records without a branching triple all have triangular mask 52 and quartet mask 15. Their pure graph is a triangle plus an isolated quartet U. The triangle quartets touch nine distinct labels: three internal edges and six labels with one mixed occurrence. The four U labels each have one mixed occurrence. These are all thirteen labels.

Set the four U labels to zero. All three triangle quartets remain intact. Dark means identically zero after restriction. Work at flattened OR group interfaces; an internal OR of a positive group cannot supply an external AND. The nine-input restricted threshold is nonconstant.

**Branching U.** Both readers are ORs. A first new dark OR group could only have U as nonraw input and removed raw inputs from U. It would equal U even in the original circuit, contradicting minimum size. A first dark AND cannot directly read U. Thus U is the only dark group root. The grouped restriction loses three ORs inside U, its four mixed raw slots, and its two outgoing nonraw slots. Seven ANDs and 15 ORs remain. At most two positive mixed groups become pure, one per U use.

**Private U.** Its sole reader is D=U AND P. The partner remains nonzero: otherwise P implies U, making D redundant. D is one-sided dark. The quartet-deletion bound in degree/t13-seven/census.md permits at most nine savings: at least eight ORs vanish and a size-21 nine-input circuit needs seventeen ORs. The eight raw reads and D's one-sided charge exhaust this allowance. There is no other dark branching or one-sided AND. The dark signal after D follows one path. No AND on that path can have two distinct dark operands since an earlier dark parent would need a second use. U itself has exhausted its own use, and repeated operands were redundant. Prior to the first positive reader the path therefore uses only OR groups. Its exit is at a positive OR, since any dark operand kills an AND. It must exit because the original gates reached the output which stays nonzero. At most one positive group becomes newly pure. Six ANDs remain. The explicit grouped restriction loses three ORs in U, four mixed raw occurrences, and one nonraw operand at the positive exit. Any vanished OR group along the path had only one nonraw operand, so its cost equals its already counted removed raw slots. Thus 16 ORs remain.

**Star count.** Keep the names of all positive OR groups, including zero-cost one-input groups, and both original raw slots of each surviving variable. Define p' as the number of positive raw-bearing groups with no positive nonraw operands. A group with k raw and t nonraw operands costs k+t−1, so the ordinary budget r'=Σ_{mixed}(t−1) equals the grouped OR count minus 18 plus p'. Identities with no raw slots and one nonraw operand have zero excess. No two groups share two raw variables: otherwise adding the second variable to the first singleton would change neither group, and they are the only readers.

For a surviving triangle quartet W with d pure neighbors, restrict to its four labels and override W true. Every nonempty assignment has unchanged semantics, and empty rejects by monotonicity. That fresh group has at least one surviving raw, already incident to a triangle quartet. The latter then has at least three distinct pure neighbors, contradicting d≤2.

This excludes all nine records even with arbitrary tails. The nine-input composition and physical structural bounds are external premises from the parent census. No ten-input seven-AND classification is needed.
