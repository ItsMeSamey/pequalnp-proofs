# Graph incidence decoder OR bounds

Each edge is a distinct raw label. Vertex flags OR their incident labels. The monotone binary decoder rejects single edges and accepts the union of any two distinct edges.

**Simultaneous charge.** Suppose a set Z of flags is to be fixed zero and each z has a negative and a positive promised pattern differing only at z, with the other Z flags zero in both. Call wires identically zero under the restriction zero wires. For every zero AND select a zero predecessor; for a zero OR keep both. An exit OR has a zero operand and a nonzero operand. An influence path from z on its witnessing inputs leaves zero wires at an exit, since the output remains nonzero. The certificate for its zero operand must contain z. The union of certificates rooted at the exit operands covers at most the number of exits plus the number of distinct zero ORs in leaves (expand in reverse topological order, deduplicating shared nodes). All these ORs disappear. Thus at least |Z| ORs disappear. For pairwise-disjoint blocks of flags, witnesses differing within each block charge one leaf per block.

**Connected components.** For a vertex v of degree d≥2 set its distance layers L_k to zero in descending k, ending with k=2. For each w in L_k take its BFS predecessor a and grandparent b. Edge ba versus ba plus aw witnesses w, avoiding all other current-layer and all farther flags. Finally v=1 leaves threshold two on d neighbors, costing ≥2d−4 ORs. A connected host of order q requires ≥q+d−5 ORs, with no diameter assumption. Any connected induced subgraph with an edge can likewise be retained, charging each outside vertex by a path to that subgraph; at a predecessor within the subgraph choose one of its subgraph neighbors for the grandparent.

In every component outside v's, retain an anchor edge and peel all but its two vertices in the same manner. Then zero the remote anchor pairs simultaneously. A central negative edge versus its union with the remote edge witnesses each disjoint block. A nontrivial remote component of order s therefore charges s−1 ORs, including one OR for a detached 2-vertex component. A host with c nontrivial components and no isolates requires ≥q+d−5−(c−1) ORs.

**Hereditary cut.** Each deleted mixed flag has a separate binary OR reader whose other operand is nonraw. Retain v, its neighbors and all pure flags; delete the other mixed flags by bypassing only their ORs. Each bypass preserves paths from the retained pure flags and loses exactly one OR. A q−2 budget becomes q'−2 on the induced retained vertices. Outside the center component all vertices are now pure. Let them comprise k nontrivial components and h isolated pure vertices. If h>0, first set one retained isolate W to zero. Its syntactic path must reach an OR, as otherwise its vanishing would zero a nonconstant central star. This OR loss is additional; no later promised witness needs W. Peel nontrivial detached components at s−1 ORs each, the central component at one OR per vertex beyond the closed neighborhood, and specialize v=1. Rearrangement gives

    d−3 ≤ k+h−[h>0].

Consequently when d=4, at least two pure flags are outside the center component. Growing that component by unknown mixed edges can only reduce its pure complement. For the weaker distance-only version first fix a single pure flag at distance at least three, if any, and then charge the distance-two layer; the layer's witnesses do not involve that remote flag. A quartet needs at least two pure vertices at distance ≥3, counting disconnected vertices. The component form strengthens the distance-only condition.
