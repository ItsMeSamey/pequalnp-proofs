# Small vertex covers of a clean terminal's private pairs

Consider a regularized minimum 31-gate circuit on 13 labels for threshold two, with seven ANDs, in clean-terminal form with at least two terminal disjuncts. For a clean terminal T put an edge ij in its private-pair graph precisely when the input consisting of labels i and j is accepted by T and by no other terminal. If a set S covers this graph then, after setting the labels in S to zero, the disjunct T can be removed: every surviving pair remains accepted and singletons were already rejected. Monotonicity extends the conclusion to every surviving input.

Every vertex cover of size four contains a whole private pure group feeding T. Use the group and deletion statements in `../../degree/t13-seven/census.md`, the exact ten-input minimum of 24 gates, and the nine-input minimum of 21 gates, which requires four ANDs and seventeen ORs. Pure groups are triples or quartets, and distinct ones intersect in at most one label. A four-set therefore contains at most one whole pure group.

* If it contains none, the four deleted labels save eight ORs by raw-leaf contraction in their groups. Every pure group remains nonzero, hence so does every original subsequent signal on the all-remaining-labels input. Removing T and one final collector OR saves two further gates. This gives at most 21 gates and at most fifteen ORs on nine labels, impossible.
* If it contains a private pure group assigned to another product, that product vanishes. Every four-label restriction saves at least eight ORs. Discarding T gives two distinct lost ANDs, hence at most 21 gates with at most sixteen ORs. This also covers the case where T vanishes automatically. If T is the last surviving terminal, the cover hypothesis itself contradicts acceptance of a surviving pair.
* If it contains a branching triple, deleting that triple saves seven ORs. The resulting physical ten-input, 24-gate, seven-AND circuit has two OR readers at every remaining label. Deleting the fourth label saves two further ORs. If it contains a branching quartet, deleting the quartet saves three internal tree ORs, four external raw-incidence ORs and two distinct ORs at the group's two readers. The latter groups cannot include a direct raw member of the quartet by absorption. In both cases at least nine ORs and T's AND disappear, again impossible.

For completeness, the corresponding three-set classification follows directly. A three-set containing no pure group saves six ORs and then T and its collector slot, giving at most 23 gates on ten labels. A branching pure triple gives an exact 24-gate minor retaining all seven ANDs, and a private triple of another product gives an exact 24-gate minor retaining T. In either case a redundant surviving T contradicts minimality. Covers of fewer than three labels are ruled out by extending them to three-sets that are not a private group of T.

Consequently a terminal dedicated to a private triple has that triple as its unique minimum cover and every four-cover contains it. For a private quartet the minimum cover is uniquely its quartet. Every clean terminal with no private group has cover number at least five. Here a private group's terminal has its other operand shared, so it does not have two different private groups. Its own group always covers its positive pairs. Each label of a dedicated group needs at least two distinct outside partners with private pairs: otherwise swap that label for its unique outside partner (or any outside label if it has none), producing a different cover of the same size.

For a private triple every label needs at least three outside partners. If it has at most two, replace it by those two (padding to a four-set if needed). The other two group labels cover every internal pair, contradicting the four-cover classification.
