# The eleven-input, 26-gate case with eight ANDs

For a regular candidate all eleven raws have two OR readers. Its eighteen OR gates attain the threshold-two OR bound. Flatten single-use ORs into OR consumers. Pure groups have only raw leaves. Every raw occurs in two different groups, and two groups share at most one raw. Write p for the number of pure groups, H for the number of connected components of their mutual raw-edge graph, and d for a group's degree in this graph.

Every pure group with k=2 or 3 raw labels is unshared. Deleting its raws permits only 2k+1 gate savings by the nine- and eight-input lower bounds. A branching U already uses the extra unit, ruling out one-sided dark ANDs. The only exposed dark nonraw signal before a first dark AND is U: a second dark raw-OR seed would put two U labels in a second group, while an OR combining U with a subset of U is redundant. No dark AND can therefore occur. This would remove at least 2k+1 ORs, contradicting the smaller problem's 2(11−k)−4 OR floor.

Distinct pure doubletons cannot be adjacent. If they feed the same AND the common raw factors. For distinct readers, deleting the raws of one consumes all five allowed savings, including its one-sided dark AND. The other reader survives because its overlapping doubleton becomes a nonzero raw and another one-sided dark AND would exceed the allowance. The nine-input minor thereby has a raw entering an AND, impossible at size 21 given the eight-input size of 19. Thus each nontrivial pure component contains at least four distinct incident raw labels, since an adjacent pair uses at least 2+3−1. Each isolated group consumes at least two. Different components consume disjoint labels.

Some pure group has size k≥4. Otherwise all are unshared and the first AND reads two unshared pure groups. If they overlap, factoring their common raw saves a gate; if disjoint, deleting one incurs the full allowance while the other becomes dead.

For this group apply the independent star inequality k+max(d,2)≤p and component-peeling inequality k≤H, obtaining p≥6 and H≥4. When d≥2 its component uses at least six labels: two distinct pure neighbors require at least two additional labels outside U, or they would be adjacent doubletons. The other three components consume at least six more. When d=1 its component uses at least five labels, so equality in the eleven-label budget forces H=4 and three isolated doubletons elsewhere. Within U's component there is just one non-U label. Its unique neighbor uses that label and the raw it shares with U, and a third vertex would need two non-U labels. Therefore p=5. When d=0 the isolated U costs four labels. Four other components would cost eight, and three others with any nontrivial member also cost eight. Thus H=4 and all are isolated, giving p=4. Every case contradicts eleven labels or p≥6.

The regular case covers every minimum 26-gate eleven-input threshold circuit. The now-established 24-gate ten-input lower bound permits at most two deletions on setting one raw false. Thus a raw has at most two readers, and a twice-used raw entering an AND is impossible by its downstream one-sided charge. A once-used raw entering an AND has the usual three-gate direct deletion, while a once-used raw entering an OR contradicts minimum clean-terminal form. Hence all raws have exactly two OR readers.
