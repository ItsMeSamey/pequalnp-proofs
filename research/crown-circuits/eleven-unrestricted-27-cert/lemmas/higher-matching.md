# Distinct opposite-pin witnesses and matching equality

For a globally minimum circuit chosen to minimize dirty gates, both arms of each earliest dirty gate require a shared OR output reaching that arm but not its opposite. The proof uses the private OR tree splitting argument in ../../algebra/eighttwo/straddler.md. In the reversed union network these are paid nonraw helper masks each carrying its particular pin and omitting its sibling. An earliest comparator can be made first by a physical topological reordering. The requirement therefore holds simultaneously for all e dirty comparators whose pins occur in no protected continuation.

Protected cost c consumes at least c helpers which carry none of those 2e pins. Each of the remaining 5−c binary unions can introduce at most two of the required literal pins previously absent from helpers. Thus e+c≤5. At equality each tagged helper must introduce two new pins, so each is the direct union of two distinct literal pins, all these pairs form a perfect matching, and a matched pair may not be the opposite pins of one gate. The matching decomposes into cycles on the e vertices.

The only potentially hard hot receiver for one root is the union of the helpers incident at its opposite signs: using a literal rather than its corresponding helper adds at most one other pin, handled by ordinary cancellation. A two-cycle gives the same forced receiver for two gates and can be replaced by the union of their continuations. Otherwise the forced distinct receiver deficits must be disjoint. higher_cycles.cpp checks each matching of the available signed pins and each minimum pure library for the continuations, followed by all remaining feasible receiver unions. This is the seven-coordinate version of ../../algebra/eightfour/cycles.cpp.
