# Subcube expansion of the raw deficits in a minimum paired circuit

Let twelve nondirect receiver masks R_i be paid sinks of a reverse switch network for a globally minimum T₂¹² circuit, hypothetically of size at most 28. Their nonempty deficits D_i=F ∖ R_i are pairwise disjoint. Let A be the number of switches. Throughout the relevant strata A≤8. Use unrestricted size(T₂¹⁰)≥23. For the stronger one-coordinate conclusion at A≤7 use the separate eleven-input, at-most-six-AND lower bound of 26.

1. For every switch and each of its signs, the corresponding half-cube contains some entire deficit D_i. Otherwise fixing the opposite sign leaves every receiver proper and preserves all pairwise full unions. Physically replacing the chosen AND by that operand saves a gate and still computes threshold.
2. Fix a subcube Q of codimension b≥2 that contains one whole deficit D_i. At least b+1 of the other deficits are disjoint from Q.
3. At A≤7, each signed half actually contains at least two whole deficits.

For (2), suppose only k≤b deficits are disjoint from Q. Set r=max(2,k)≤b. Discard r receivers including all those k, retaining i and at least one other label. Replace these r distinct paid sinks by F and remove their unions. Every retained deficit has some point in Q at which all discarded receivers already were true; these witnesses stay. R_i still covers every point outside Q.

Each coordinate defining Q has a switch whose activation is nonzero on Q: choose a retained p∈D_i. Flipping any one defining coordinate takes p outside Q where R_i is true. An inactive switch cannot distinguish these directions at p, since its incoming activation is independent of its own coordinate and both outgoing branches would be zero in both evaluations. All later masks would also agree. Hence each designated switch is active at p. The proof of the finite-forest restriction lemma in direct-coordinate-restriction.md now applies, using R_i's full coverage outside Q. To see the required ancestry explicitly, reduce its reverse expression to a consistent union of branch literals by replacing every pair of siblings with its earlier activation. The face opposite all remaining signs lies in D_i⊆Q, so every required opposite branch occurs. Restricting directions to Q saves at least b further unions. The resulting paired threshold on 12−r labels has at most 17−r−b+12−r−1≤28−3r gates. Three-gate input splitting would lift it to a ten-input circuit of size≤22, impossible.

For (3), let Q be a signed half and suppose precisely one deficit is disjoint from Q. Part (1) gives a retained deficit contained in Q. Delete the lost paid sink and repeat the preceding restriction with one coordinate, saving another union. The result computes T₂¹¹ at size≤25 with at most six switches, a contradiction. The case of no lost deficit is already ruled out by (1). Replacing a deficit by a smaller one in a specific same-cost, source-realizable helper bank may expose new subcubes; the transformed circuit is itself still globally minimum, so the theorem applies to it afresh.

More generally let k count deficits missing Q, and let q count fixed coordinates h for which some surviving D_i meets Q and is wholly in h's chosen half. Different h may use different witnesses. If q≥2 then k>q. In a contrary case delete r=max(2,k)≤q sinks, retaining one witness label for each h (there are at most eight anchors and at least eleven candidates when an extra deletion is needed). Each of these q switches is active on Q by the flip test at its own witness, and its opposing output is a genuine ancestor of that proper receiver by the one-half face argument. Each also has a path into a wire nonzero on Q, since each retained receiver is true at another surviving representative. In the zero forest choose for each encountered node a successor eventually reaching *any* such nonzero wire. The q−t internal zero mergers and t mixed roots save q unions, as before. One may fix all defining coordinates, including those without a selected ancestor. Source and pair-coverage arguments are unchanged.
