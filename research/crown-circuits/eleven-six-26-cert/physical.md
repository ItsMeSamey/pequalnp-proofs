# Raw OR groups at size 26 for eleven variables

The exact T₂¹⁰ lower bound of 24 allows at most two eliminated gates on fixing any one variable false. In terminal-clean normal form each raw has two OR readers. A one-use raw entering an AND admits the known three-gate specialization, and a one-use raw entering an OR would require an internal singleton-negative operand. A two-use raw entering an AND also loses a downstream gate in addition to the two readers. Flatten every single-use OR whose sole reader is an OR and view each of the eleven raws as an edge of the simple graph on maximal OR groups. Raw supports cannot repeat within a group by idempotence, and distinct raws cannot have identical endpoint groups by singleton-vs-pair distinguishability.

Pure groups of degree two cannot be adjacent. Write their supports U={a,b} and V={b,c}. If both group outputs branch, deleting a,b,c loses at least eight gates by the dark-edge identity (six raw fanouts plus two surplus uses), contradicting size(T₂⁸)≥19. If U branches and V is single-use, the physical reader E of V is an AND. Deleting U loses exactly five gates, since size(T₂⁹)≥21. Four raw incidences and the surplus at U exhaust the allowance. The AND E survives: otherwise its surviving V-input c would make it an additional one-sided dark AND. The V-input becomes the literal c, so the surviving minimal nine-input circuit has a raw-fed AND. Such a circuit cannot have size 21 since size(T₂⁸)≥19; raw fanout at least two plus an AND incurs a downstream simplification, and an only-reader raw AND has the three-gate direct specialization. The case with V branching is symmetric. Finally, if both are single-use and feed distinct ANDs, deleting U uses its unique extra on its own one-sided dark AND, while V's surviving literal again feeds a surviving AND. If they feed the same AND, (a OR b) AND (b OR c) factors to b OR (a AND c), saving a gate. Thus all pure doubletons have disjoint raw support.

For six ANDs, twenty ORs, and p pure groups, the extra nonraw OR cost is r=p−2. In the notation of the ten-input component bound, every pure group obeys k+max(d,2)≤p+2 and k≤2+C, where d is its number of pure neighbors and C is the number of pure components including isolates. The first inequality also applies at mixed centers with the additional saving t_U−1.
