# A four-AND lower bound for eleven-input threshold two

A monotone binary circuit for threshold two on eleven Boolean inputs, with at most four AND gates, needs at least 26 gates. The included upper control uses 27 gates, four of them ANDs. The exact minimum could be 26 or 27. This is a mathematical reduction with executable checks.

Resolve each AND by choosing one of its two operands. On every assignment the resolved circuit dominates the original; on any fixed rejecting assignment there is a resolution preserving all wire values. A resolving mask for label i is the set of four-bit directions on which the resolved output depends on variable i. Each pair of masks has full union, since the corresponding two-true input must be accepted, and each has nonempty complement, since its singleton is rejected. This requires eleven distinct patterns and hence exactly four AND gates. Pick one pattern witnessing each label, giving C of cardinality eleven. On C every raw mask is the co-singleton for its own representative.

Read the circuit backwards from its output, whose initial reachability is all sixteen directions. At OR gates, copy the reach set; at AND gates split it by the switch bit. Gather uses by binary unions. All eleven input variables are essential. With N gates, after removing constants and unused gates the number of reverse unions is 2N−(N+11−1)=N−10. For N≤25 this is at most 15. A split branch lies in a coordinate half of size eight; it cannot supply any of the eleven raw masks, since these each contain at least ten directions. Twelve or more distinct traces are unnecessary here; eleven suffice.

The activation at each split is either the full set or a union of earlier branch masks. The first checker eleven_budget.cpp enumerates the chronological activations in forward order of their selector coordinates and five omitted patterns, quotienting only simultaneous direction-bit translations. It first asks whether an unlimited union of branch masks can produce every required co-singleton in C. In each surviving case it charges the eleven restricted receiver-mask unions, leaving at most four other restricted unions. It requires each nontrivial activated mask on C and recursively covers all required masks by binary unions of strict subsets among the available masks. Branch masks are available from the outset for this cost relaxation. Counts are 124,761 initial combinations, 3,964 passing the unlimited test, and six passing the 15-union test. The axis order is retained throughout, including after symmetry reduction.

The second checker eleven_full.py expands the eight branches of each of these six cases to their complete union closure on all sixteen directions. It selects at most fifteen non-base masks, each generated by two strict available submasks, and requires every nontrivially activated mask to be selected. For each of the eleven co-singletons on C it selects a full receiver extending that co-singleton. Receiver pairs for different labels must have full union. All six instances are UNSAT. Allowing all split branches to be available from the beginning can only weaken the exclusions.

## Consequence for twelve inputs

Suppose a 28-gate, five-AND threshold-two circuit on twelve inputs has a direct raw receiver in its transposed mask network. Reassign that raw to the split branch having exactly its mask. The complete masks of all inputs and all split activations can be preserved, so threshold two is preserved. Indeed two true inputs must cover every resolution, and each singleton has a rejecting resolution.

In terminal normal form, a split genuinely active on singleton k has activation W contained in its raw receiver M_k: every direction reaching the corresponding physical AND sees a true operand and must therefore include k. All raw receiver masks are pairwise incomparable. If a direct raw M_i is a branch of this split, then M_i is a proper subset of W, contradicting M_i⊂W⊆M_k. Thus the direct branch belongs to a clean terminal AND. Equivalently its activation is full, so this AND can feed the final disjunction directly in the reconstructed circuit.

Write x for the raw at this branch. In the forward circuit x enters one arm of that terminal AND through an OR, with no other uses. The other arm K is independent of x. For every other variable y, the singleton y rejects and the pair x,y accepts. Every path from x to the output passes through this AND, even with arbitrary downstream consumers. Hence K must accept each y singleton. K rejects the empty assignment, since otherwise x would be accepted. Monotonicity gives K exactly the eleven-variable disjunction. The x-arm has form x OR P for a signal P independent of x (possibly identically false), and P implies K.

Set x to false. The terminal AND then equals P. If its x-arm was nontrivial, remove the OR combining x with the remaining arm contributions and bypass the terminal AND. If the x-arm was just x, the AND is now false and can be removed together with a downstream gate by constant propagation. Such a gate exists since the remaining eleven-variable threshold is nonzero. In either case two gates are saved. A gate implementing K must also exist and can be eliminated: on the eleven remaining inputs every zero-baseline wire is at most K, so any product with K just copies its other argument, and any sum with K is K. Propagate these substitutions, retaining no copy of K, since the eleven-variable threshold output differs from K. The three removed gates are distinct. The specialization therefore also works before normalization, with arbitrary downstream consumers of the direct AND. This would leave a circuit of at most 25 gates with at most four ANDs for eleven-input threshold two, excluded above.

Pruning after the transformation is harmless. When recomposing the forward circuit from the reverse network, paying B reverse unions on n input ports incurs at most B+n−1 forward gates, after unused outputs and trivial gates are removed. Thus matching an existing raw mask to an actual branch does not increase the budget.
