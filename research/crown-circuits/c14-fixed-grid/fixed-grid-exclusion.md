# Four by four grid with two disjoint missing edges

No monotone binary decoder with at most eight ORs and at most fourteen gates computes the edge promise for this fixed host. In particular, the eight-OR, six-AND decoder is impossible. The inputs to the decoder are the eight vertex flags, each the OR of its incident independent edge labels. It must reject on the empty input and on each single edge and accept every union of two distinct edges. Monotonicity gives threshold two on all fourteen independent edge labels. This theorem uses no SAT search, symmetry quotient, or chronological representative.

Use the threshold size bounds C(12)≥29 and C(14)≥34. Here C denotes the minimum total binary monotone circuit size for threshold two. The incidence bank has 28−8=20 OR gates, since each of the eight flags has positive degree. Consequently any candidate of size at most fourteen is globally minimum in decoder size at this fixed interface and OR cap. Among these candidates minimize the number of decoder gates that are true on at least one singleton edge.

By the companion graph-bootstrap.md, the decoder needs at least eight ORs, and each of the four degree-three flags has exactly one reader in every size-minimal candidate. This uses the seven-OR floor for the three-by-four grid minus one edge, the unique-first-OR factorization, and the archived four-OR impossibility for mask 1459. Two raw flags cannot form the first reader of a hole flag x. For an AND with z, take a path x−a−b avoiding z, so ab and ab+ax give the same reader and other flags. For an OR with a distance-two z, use the edge za and add ax at a common neighbor a. For adjacent z, start with an edge zb distinct from zx and add zx. In each case only x was added and its sole reader is unchanged. The only remaining z is the missing mate; both flags then have the same sole OR reader. Merging gives a seven-vertex connected degree-six quotient requiring eight ORs by the connected layer bound, while bypassing that OR leaves only seven. Repeated raw operands are redundant.

No decoder gate may be true on twelve singleton edges. Keep twelve such labels and set the other two false. Every flag is still nonempty because its original degree is at least three. The new incidence bank costs 24−8=16 ORs. Set the selected gate to true. This preserves all nonempty retained inputs by monotonicity of the gate; at empty the modified monotone output lies below any still-rejected singleton. The gate is internal since the output rejects singletons. Removing its producer and simplifying a consumer saves at least two decoder gates; a dead producer would already contradict size minimality. The resulting threshold circuit has size at most 16+14−2=28, contradicting C(12)≥29.

Write the missing edges as x−y and x′−y′, with x,x′ on one shore. Choose x to be the earliest hole flag read in topological order, exchanging shores and renaming holes if necessary. Its nonraw partner P depends only on the four full flags. On each of the eight edges away from x and y, adding x creates a second edge with the already present opposite endpoint. Since x has no other reader, a reader x AND P requires P true on all eight tests, and x OR P requires P false on all eight. Projected to the four full flags these eight tests consist of the four full–full edge pairs and the four unit vectors: the two edges from x′ to the full columns and the two edges from y′ to the full rows.

For an AND reader, P is true on all four units, hence by monotonicity on every edge incident to any full flag. Those are twelve of the fourteen singleton edges. This contradicts the support bound for the nonraw P. For an OR reader, P is false on all four units and all four full–full pairs, and by monotonicity at zero. Every singleton edge projects to one of these tests or zero. Replace x OR P by x at every use, and append an output OR with P. When P is false the output is unchanged; when P is true the input already has at least two active labels and the appended OR is true. The OR and size budgets are preserved. The removed gate was true on x's incident edges. No old wire gains a singleton positive, and the new output gate rejects every singleton. This strictly improves the minimized count, a contradiction.

This closes this host and gate budget only. The earlier eight-or-six-and.md records intermediate constraints that the present argument strengthens.
