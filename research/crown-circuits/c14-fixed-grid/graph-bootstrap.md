# First-OR bounds for two grid graphs

For a simple decoder host G, consider a circuit with at most k ORs that minimizes the total number of gates among those circuits. Suppose flag v is essential and its zero restriction leaves a nonconstant promise requiring at least k−1 ORs. Every path from v through only ANDs must reach an OR before the output, or the output would vanish identically when v is false. There can be only one distinct first OR across such paths since each loses an operand on restriction. If v enters that OR directly, an additional AND-only route to the same OR would make its other operand imply v, and the OR could be removed. Otherwise, if v has multiple direct AND readers, set v true within its pre-frontier AND cone and restore the common v factor at its unique exit. At least two ANDs are replaced by at most one. Thus v has exactly one physical reader. This is the frontier argument in geometry/fourteen34/cycle-or-floor.md.

If all flags have this property, the first gate takes two singly-read raw flags. An AND of x,y cannot occur if a two-step path x−a−b avoids y, since ab and ab+ax must be distinguished with y zero. An OR cannot combine adjacent flags if one has a second neighbor, nor two flags at distance two. In both cases a rejected edge and an accepting extension present identical values after the sole shared reader. For flags at distance at least three merge their vertices, bypass the raw OR, and retain all other gates. The quotient is simple. Each test of the quotient lifts by taking its unique original edges; the old decoder depended on x,y only through their disjunction.

For K4,4 minus two disjoint edges, fixing any flag false leaves a connected seven-vertex graph with a degree-four vertex. The elementary connected layer floor h+d−5 gives six ORs. Thus at cap seven all eight flags are singly read. Their minimum degree three ensures the required two-step witness avoiding any second flag. Only endpoints of a missing edge have distance three. The resulting seven-vertex quotient has degree six and needs eight ORs, beyond the six remaining. Hence the eight-flag graph requires at least eight ORs.

For K3,4 minus one edge, at cap six each flag is singly read. Deleting on the three-vertex side leaves a six-vertex connected graph with a degree-four center, requiring five ORs. Deleting on the four-vertex side leaves K3,3 with at most one missing edge. With shores 0,1,2 and 3,4,5 and missing edge 03, the vertex map (4,1,2,3,5,0) embeds archived mask 1459, for which four ORs are impossible. Every flag has a two-step witness avoiding any second flag: all have at least two neighbors and all neighbors have degree at least two; when the second flag lies on the same side, one may choose a neighbor of degree at least three. Only the missing-edge endpoints at distance three can merge. That quotient has six vertices with a degree-five center, requiring six ORs, beyond the five remaining. Thus the seven-flag graph needs seven ORs. In any putative eight-OR size-minimal decoder for K4,4 minus two disjoint edges, this forces the four endpoints of the missing edges to have a single reader each.
