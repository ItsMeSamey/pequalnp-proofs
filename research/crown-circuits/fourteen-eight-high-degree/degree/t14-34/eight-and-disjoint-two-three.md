# A branching doubleton and disjoint branching triple force an OR-optimal nine-input minor

Let the branching raw groups be U and V, of sizes two and three, in the regular size-34 eight-AND fourteen-input candidate. Let S be their five distinct labels. Both groups have exactly two OR readers. Erasing S incurs twelve losses from raw occurrences and dark fanout surplus, leaving at most one further loss by the arity-nine size floor 21. Thirteen losses would require four lost ANDs because the only possible size-21 nine-input composition uses four ANDs.

An AND-free dark group must compute an OR of a subset of S. Besides U and V the only entirely erased pure groups W are crossing doubletons, one raw from each bank. They are unshared by eight-and-one-doubleton.md, and therefore feed an AND immediately. Any other dark OR group before the first dark AND must descend from U or V. An independent operand in a both-dark AND must have support at most three (the opposite operand needs two exclusive singleton labels inside S). Neither U nor V can be itself such an operand by their OR-only readers. Thus such an independent operand is either a private W or an OR of U and one V label, called T. In particular a size-three masked operand born from V itself would merely duplicate V. Any T has its own direct U departure: adding its one external raw earlier already realizes T, and no different nontrivial gate before it could have had an intermediate support strictly between U and T. No live signal can feed a dark OR, so these statements describe the full forward Boolean functions.

There is no initial both-dark AND. Two Ws cannot feed one product by the private-doubleton lemma; for W and T the W operand has at most one exclusive singleton; two Ts share both U labels. Suppose nevertheless that thirteen gates disappear. One one-sided dark AND H must start the computation that loses four ANDs. This spends the one remaining surplus unit, so no other dark nonraw gate can branch outside U and V. All later dark products are on the single strand from H, and each of the three required both-dark products consumes a distinct independent W or T. A crossing W consumes the unique exterior occurrence of its V label, as does each T. The three labels so used must be distinct. There are at most two W, since U has two labels, and at most two T, since U has two exits. Thus the possibilities are as follows.

* With one W and two T, all three V exterior occurrences, one U exterior raw occurrence, and both forward departures of U already belong to these three independent operands. Before H, a further dark operand cannot come from one of them (their fanouts are one), from bare U or V (their readers are ORs), or from any other pure pair (two raws of V already share V, and every other V exterior occurrence is spent). Only V plus the remaining exterior U raw can produce the necessary operand. It consumes one exit of V and the last spare raw occurrence. H therefore has at most two hot singleton labels: its four-label operand must have two exclusives. Until the last prescribed product the only unused possible source of added singleton support along the strand is the one remaining V exit, which adds at most three. Three successive products decrease the strand support by at least two apiece. Starting with at most two and adding at most three cannot pay six losses.

* With two W and one T, all exterior raw occurrences are already allocated. The only further dark operand available to start H is the union of U and V, taking the second U exit and one V exit. Hence H has at most three singleton labels. Only V's last exit can supply additional support along the strand. Before that addition at most one both-dark product can be passed, since two would consume four singleton labels. After V's addition the strand contains all V labels. A W now has at most one exclusive label and cannot enter the next product. A T can enter only if both U labels are outside the strand, leaving at most its sole V label in the resulting intersection. No more additions are available. Hence at most one product can be passed after the addition. Three required products are impossible.

If no new source is ever added to the strand, the singleton decrease gives the same contradiction immediately. Thus thirteen losses are impossible. Exactly twelve gates disappear and there is no one-sided dark AND. Since a first both-dark AND is impossible, all twelve losses are ORs. The nine-input residual contains fourteen ORs and eight ANDs.
