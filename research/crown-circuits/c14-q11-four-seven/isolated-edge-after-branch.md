# A lone surviving edge after deleting the cubic branch

In the q=11 four-plus-seven boundary, fix the marked branching flag to zero. Its two distinct OR readers simplify. In G447 mark 2 and G450 marks 3 and 4, the surviving six-flag graph consists of an isolated K2 and a four-cycle (G447) or a paw (G450). The opposite K4 is intact. There are eleven surviving raw edge labels, and all ten residual flags are incident. Their incident-edge OR bank takes 22−10=12 gates. With the residual decoder of at most fifteen gates this computes threshold two on eleven inputs. The minimum is 27, so the full bank-plus-decoder circuit is minimum and all fifteen decoder gates are necessary.

Only the two OR readers of the branching flag can have disappeared. Their nonzero operands propagate to their consumers. An extra zero reader, dead gate, duplicate or idempotent simplification would reduce the size below 27. Each of the two surviving isolated-edge endpoints was essential before fixing the branch, since its neighbor in the original graph has another incident edge. Each therefore has an original input occurrence. Before identifying the endpoints, a surviving reader retains the occurrence; a branch-reader bypass taking that endpoint as surviving operand propagates it to a surviving slot or further bypass. Two different surviving raw identities cannot merge into one formal operand when the only operation is bypassing ORs with a single zero operand. Thus after identifying the endpoints with the same edge label t, it has at least two residual reader slots. If both are at one gate, idempotence saves a gate.

The fifteen-gate decoder has at most seven ORs. Setting t=0 leaves a formal C4+K4 or paw+K4 decoder, which by k4-six-or.md needs six ORs. At most one OR can disappear. The signal t is essential (add its edge to any other single edge), and output is nonzero with t=0. Hence every live path from t meets an OR and all first such ORs simplify on zeroing t. There is exactly one first OR H.

Use the single-first-OR factorization (also in calculus/fourteen34/117-virtual-pair.md). If H reads t directly, another direct reader starts an AND-only path into the other operand of H; that operand implies t, so H is absorbable. Otherwise all direct t-readers are ANDs. Replace t by true at those readers, contracting at least two gates; on every path to H, the original affected value was its new value multiplied by t. Reinsert that factor once on the affected operand of H, or just after H when both operands were affected. No t-path avoids H, so the function is preserved. The operation saves at least one gate, contrary to the exact T11 minimum. This excludes the three marks, conditional on the established original branch and size premises.
