# Counting individually spoiling flags

Split the flags of a monotone decoder into large and small. Call a wire cold when it is false at all large flags true and all small flags false. A lost OR on erasing all small flags has a cold operand. Let m count ORs with two cold operands, and k those with exactly one. Suppose the output is large-live and b distinct small flags each change the decision on some fixed large input when turned on alone.

For each such singleton at least one cold operand of a mixed OR changes from zero to one on its witnessing large assignment. Otherwise induction shows all large-live wires agree with the small-empty case: a live AND has only live operands, and only a mixed OR can introduce a cold wire. By monotonicity that same cold operand is true on the singleton even after raising every large flag to true.

Now keep every large flag true. Every large-live wire is constant true for any choice of small flags. A cold wire can therefore be evaluated with the m two-cold ORs and unrestricted ANDs, substituting true where appropriate. Take the disjunction of the k cold exit operands using k−1 further ORs. Restrict the small inputs to the b spoilers. The result rejects empty and accepts each singleton, hence computes their disjunction. For b positive, k is positive. A circuit for this disjunction costs at least b−1 ORs even with ANDs and constants: starting from the output, retain both inputs of every false-at-empty OR, and at each false-at-empty AND retain a false-at-empty operand. This gives an OR-only relaxation still false at empty and true on every singleton. Its live binary OR graph must have at least b−1 binary joins to connect the b essential sources. Thus k+m≥b.

In the connected eleven-flag degree-three separator cases at the four core-side bridge endpoints, erasing the branching flag leaves a triangle and a connected subcubic seven-flag component with eight edges. The residual decoder has at most fifteen gates and seven ORs. If a triangle flag spoiled none of the eight opposite edges, fixing it true and supplying the other two triangle flags as virtual inputs and the eight real labels via 2·8−7=9 bank ORs computes threshold two on ten inputs. The triangle flag is essential since adding it to the opposite triangle edge creates a positive, so this would cost at most 9+14=23 gates, below the established minimum 24. Thus each triangle singleton spoils. At least three ORs disappear on erasing them. The connected seven-vertex component has a cubic vertex. Preserve its four-vertex star, peel three other flags along a spanning-tree ordering paying one OR each, and clamp the star center true for the two-OR floor of three-input threshold two. That requires five further ORs, contradicting the residual seven. Geometry identified the four endpoints in graph6 table order as 6,9,4,3.
