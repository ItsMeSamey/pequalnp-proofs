# A diamond and K4 cannot share a decoder with fourteen gates and six ORs

Let D be the diamond and R the four K4 flags. The decoder rejects each host edge and accepts the union of any two distinct edges, including cross pairs. Assume the stated budget and choose a minimum-total-size decoder within it. On R alone the function equals threshold three.

Each of the two universal D vertices spoils at least one R edge. Otherwise fix that vertex true: its three neighbors become virtual edge inputs, and the six real R edges generate four flag banks at a cost of eight ORs. The fixed flag was essential (adding it to the opposite edge of a triangle creates a positive), so at most thirteen decoder gates remain. We obtain threshold two on nine inputs with at most 21 gates and fourteen ORs. The fourteen-OR floor at this arity requires at least eight ANDs, a contradiction. The established four-flag-three-lost-ors lemma now removes at least four ORs on setting D empty. R alone therefore has at most two ORs.

For clarity, in a two-OR circuit computing threshold three on four inputs, no raw can have a single nontrivial physical reader. After simplifying constants, call the first OR U=A∨B with pure monomials A,B, and the second OR C U^i ∨ E U^j with pure monomials C,E and exponents zero or one. The final output is the second OR times a monomial Q and optionally U. Four distinct minimal true triples require a total of four distinct products; if no final U occurs both exponents must be one (otherwise at most three products). Factoring U shows in all cases that the output equals Q (A∨B)(C∨E). The common monomial Q is empty. Each raw lies in three of the four expanded products, so lies in precisely one of A,B and precisely one of C,E. These raw occurrences in the second pair are obtained from paths bypassing the first OR. Consequently the sole reader of a raw r cannot be a surviving OR: the first OR must depend on r and nothing involving r can enter C,E through that OR. A sole AND reader must come before the first OR; its other operand is a nonempty monomial on other raws, since r has no other reader. Choose a variable z of that monomial. At z=0 this gate cannot transmit r, although the other two raws set true make r pivotal. This also rules out a sole AND of r in the full decoder: its other operand at D=0 cannot be constant true in a constant-free monotone circuit, and if it is zero r disappears entirely. Repeated identical inputs can be contracted.

Each K4 flag r spoils some D edge. Otherwise pin r true and use its three virtual neighbors plus the five real D edges. The four D flags cost six bank ORs. Threshold two on eight variables needs nineteen gates, so precisely one of the fourteen decoder gates can be lost. Its twelve-OR floor also rules out losing an OR. Thus r has a sole AND reader, already excluded.

Upon fixing any particular K4 flag r false, the other three still spoil on their original witnessing assignments. Erasing those three therefore costs at least three ORs by the singleton-exits lemma. The remaining D-only function needs at least two ORs: it has either the four triples as minimal true sets, or the nonedge pair and two triples. Consequently erasing r alone can eliminate at most one OR. Since r is essential and positives remain at r false, the usual unique-first-OR factoring in a size-minimal decoder yields a single reader. The AND case is impossible, hence this reader is an OR. Its sibling must be cold on R alone: otherwise it remains a nontrivial sole OR on R alone, contrary to the two-OR argument. In applying that argument constant simplification cannot bypass this gate because its sibling is nonzero, independent of r, and the R restriction has exactly two ORs available and requires both. The four readers are distinct: an OR reading two K4 raws would not have a cold sibling.

There are now exactly four exits, one OR r∨H_r for each K4 raw, and no OR with two cold operands, using cold to mean false on the R-full, D-empty assignment. All other R-live gates have only R-live operands. Each cold H_r implies a fixed diamond raw d_r: recurse into a cold operand of each cold AND until a cold input is reached. No cold OR is available. These four chosen raws need not be distinct.

Fix any diamond edge e and a two-element K4 input P. Set S(e) equal to the set of K4 indices r with d_r in e. On the joint assignment (P,e) each exit is bounded above by the respective input bit of P union S(e). Evaluate the decoder instead at R=P union S(e), D empty. Each exit there equals that bit. Every other live wire is bounded by its corresponding value in this alternate evaluation by monotonicity in topological order. This comparison grants upper bounds to exits regardless of dependencies of H_r on earlier live wires. As the cross pair must accept, threshold three on the alternate R input accepts. If S(e) had at most two elements, choose P containing it, a contradiction. Therefore every diamond edge contains at least three of the four chosen occurrences d_r. The diamond has two disjoint edges, so this is impossible.

Dependencies are the established threshold-two optima and OR floors at eight and nine labels, the secondary minimum at the nine-label OR floor, four-flag-three-lost-ors.md, singleton-exits.md and the unique-first-OR factoring argument.

For the original eleven-flag disjoint K3 + D + K4 host, the decoder budget is seventeen gates and nine ORs. If a triangle flag spoiled none of the eleven individual opposite edges, pin it true, introduce its two virtual neighbor inputs and rebuild the eight opposite flags with fourteen ORs. The triangle flag is essential, so the resulting thirteen-input threshold circuit would have at most 14+16=30 gates, below its established minimum 32. All three triangle flags therefore spoil, and their erasure consumes at least three ORs. The surviving D + K4 decoder satisfies precisely the excluded caps above.
