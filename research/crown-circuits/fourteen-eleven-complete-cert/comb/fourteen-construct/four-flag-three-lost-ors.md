# Four-flag hosts need four ORs to receive their signals

Let a binary monotone circuit compute a nonconstant local decision on a four-vertex component R (K4 or a diamond) alongside another component L. Its output rejects each singleton edge and accepts pairs of distinct edges, including cross pairs. Choose two universal vertices y,z of R, with other vertices u,v. Assume each of y,z spoils an L edge: the input consisting of that vertex and one L edge is accepted. At least four physical ORs must simplify on setting R=0.

Indeed let k count ORs with one operand zero in the L projection, m those with both operands zero. Suppose k+m≤3. The circuit has an L-live output; all small dependence must pass through an exit counted by k. Expand the k cold operands at the exits and 2m cold operands at the all-cold ORs through cold ANDs into a fixed conjunction of R raws and earlier cold OR or L-live factors. The output everywhere depends only on L and these k+2m raw monomials, by induction. Raw monomials may be empty.

The local projection F at L=0 satisfies F(u,v,1,1)=u∨v since yz is a negative edge and both extending triples are positive. Moreover F(u,v,1,0)=F(u,v,0,1)=uv since each universal has edges to both other vertices and the triples are positive. No value of F(1,1,0,0) is assumed. In both K4 and diamond the local F needs at least two ORs: if uv rejects then all four triples are minimal true sets; if uv accepts then uv,uyz,vyz are three minimal true sets. A circuit with at most one OR is a union of at most two monomials.

When k=1 the local F is the sole exit's cold operand at L=0, because all earlier L-live signals are pure L and the remaining L-live system can depend on R only via that one bit. Thus m=2 and both cold ORs occur in that operand and must survive at L=0. No earlier L-live factor can enter its cone: at L=0 such a factor zeros a cold AND; following a path of cold gates to the operand, either an OR is bypassed or the operand itself vanishes. Therefore the cold operand globally equals pure F(R), and has the same zero value on every small singleton edge as on empty, contradicting the positive cross tests.

Spoiling makes singleton raw monomials y,z mandatory, since any feature bank lacking singleton y has identical signatures on y alone and empty, and similarly for z. The slice F(u,v,1,1)=u∨v requires an additional monomial containing u but not v and one containing v but not u. Consequently k≥2,m=0 is impossible. The only case left is k=2,m=1, giving four slots. The two remaining monomials are the singleton raws u,v: each must distinguish the positive pair uv from its deletion on both slices (y,z)=(1,0),(0,1), so neither monomial may contain either y or z.

Each exit has its own distinct obligatory singleton monomial. Both y and z must occur as exit factors; otherwise the corresponding isolated small vertex zeros both exit contributions for all L just as empty does, contrary to spoiling. Consequently the unique cold OR has u and v as the respective obligatory factors of its two input cones. Set L=0,y=1,z=0. Before that cold OR, all live wires are independent of u and v: only exits can transmit small inputs into the live system, each available exit has fixed obligatory y or z and earlier live factors and cannot use the later cold OR. Thus this OR computes one of 0,u,v,u∨v; its two inputs are u and v separately masked by fixed live factors. Afterwards all live outputs depend on u,v only via this OR (the remaining exit raw factors are fixed). No monotone unary function of any of these four possibilities equals the required uv. This final contradiction proves the bound.

Applications use only the two spoiling conditions, the local graph, and the number of ORs available after preserving the L-local OR lower bound. In nine-flag mask 511 plus the diamond the budget is seven ORs and the L floor is four. In the ten-flag 4+6 profiles the budget is eight and the L floor is five. For K4 plus eight L edges, either choice of small universal pinned to one with zero spoiled edges would give an eleven-input threshold circuit from the eight L edges and three star edges at cost at most (16−6)+(16−1)=25<27. For the diamond plus nine L edges the same argument gives (18−6)+(16−1)=27<29 on twelve inputs. These q=10 calculations assume six incident L flags, the stated sixteen-gate decoder budget, and the separately verified five-OR floor.
