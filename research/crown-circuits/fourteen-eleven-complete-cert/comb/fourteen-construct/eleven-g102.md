# G102 paired with G49 or G50

Use the verified eleven-flag branch-position ledger: a pure branching flag must lie on the G102 shore and be 0, 3 or 4. In the six-vertex atlas ordering G102 is triangle 1–2–3 with tail 3–4–0–5. Each possible opposite five-vertex shore has eight real edges and needs at least four decoder ORs. A branching flag erasure saves at least two ORs from the original seventeen-gate, nine-OR decoder.

If 0 branches, its adjacent pendant 5 is mixed. Contracting the dedicated leaf injector saves a separate OR: its sole raw is already dominated by flag 0, so it cannot directly read that branch. The surviving paw 1,2,3,4 and the opposite component have at most six ORs. Peeling the paw while opposite edge pairs survive costs at least its order minus one, three ORs, in addition to the opposite four. This is impossible. Equivalently, peeling leaf 4 first leaves triangle plus the large component with at most thirteen gates and five ORs. The triangle's three vertices all spoil: otherwise a two-edge star with eight opposite labels gives ten-input threshold two in 11+12=23 gates. The three spoiled flags then cost three ORs, besides the four opposite ones.

If 3 branches, erasing it leaves edge 1–2 and path 4–0–5. With the eight opposite edges there are eleven labels; the flag bank takes eleven ORs on the opposite graph and one OR on the path. The residual decoder costs at most fifteen gates, so the composed circuit has size 27 and attains the eleven-label minimum. If 4 branches, the residual has edge 0–5 and triangle 1–2–3. The twelve labels then need eleven plus three bank ORs, and with the residual decoder the circuit has size 29, attaining the twelve-label minimum.

In either case the isolated edge is one raw t with its two formal endpoint flags identified. Its original endpoint flags were essential: each has a neighbor with another incident edge. Exactness of the total gate count allows only the two branch-reader OR bypasses, so each endpoint survives in a distinct formal slot before identification. Bypassing an OR on its nonzero operand propagates that operand's identity, never merging two distinct live identities into the same slot. Idempotence if both slots share a reader would itself save a gate. Thus t has at least two distinct readers. After t=0 the remaining P3 or triangle costs at least two ORs by anchoring an edge and peeling away from that anchor while opposite positive pairs survive. The opposite shore still needs four ORs. Therefore at most one of the seven residual ORs can disappear on t=0. Every live path from t must meet an OR since an AND-only path to output would kill the retained positives. Exactly one OR is first on all such paths. The previously verified single-first-OR factorization saves a gate with two or more direct readers, contradicting the exact eleven- or twelve-input lower bound. This excludes both G102 host pairs.
