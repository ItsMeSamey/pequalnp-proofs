# All 411 eleven-flag four-plus-seven host pairs

This packet excludes the complete 4+7 stratum of regular fourteen-input, 34-gate, eight-AND threshold-two candidates having eleven flags in two connected simple components. The decoder budget is seventeen gates including nine ORs. The four-vertex and seven-vertex components together have fourteen edges and maximum degree four. The physical gate model and branching prerequisites matter for the first and last parts of the argument.

## Coverage and proof order

1. The NetworkX atlas ledger records 411 pairs, in edge splits 3+11 (118), 4+10 (158), 5+9 (79), and 6+8 (56). The seven-vertex local OR floors are obtained from the connected degree bound or by peeling down to a checked obstruction in the graph-cut ledger. The atlas source reads the obstruction-loader prefix of the bundled geometry program. The remaining, connected-eleven portion of that program is independent of this packet and need not be run.
2. For 276 pairs in the first two splits, the seven-vertex floor of six leaves at most three ORs for erasing the other component, impossible by the physical four-flag remote argument. For another 131 pairs in the latter two splits the same floor and the abstract four-flag cut give a contradiction. See comb/fourteen-construct/eleven-four-seven.md and its cited physical and abstract sources.
3. The four five-OR exceptions are K4 opposite G446, G447, G448, or G450. All four K4 vertices spoil some large edge by the eight-edge virtual-star bound in eleven-four-seven.md. At most four decoder ORs disappear when K4 is erased. The saturated forest proof k4-four-loss-forest.md yields four distinct singleton entry ORs, with no internal cold OR. In particular a branching pure K4 flag cannot have two first ORs: every path from its flag to the first L-live gate passes its own entry, and before that entry only ANDs are available. The established unique-first-OR factorization then forbids branching there. A branching pure flag must exist, by the earliest private-product argument in degree/t14-34/eleven-branch-roles.md. Choose one on the seven-vertex shore and erase it; at least two ORs disappear by the same branching factorization. The residual has at most fifteen gates and seven ORs.
4. In each of the 28 markings the other six large vertices remain incident, since each initially had degree at least two. There are six large edges after a degree-two deletion, five after a degree-three deletion. All four K4 flags still spoil a large edge: otherwise a virtual three-edge star gives threshold nine in at most (12−6)+14=20 gates, or threshold eight in (10−6)+14=18 gates. Thus deleting K4 must cost four ORs, and the local six-vertex projection may use at most three.
5. Eighteen markings leave a connected six-vertex graph with a cubic vertex, requiring at least 6+3−5=4 ORs. Two leave P6, excluded by algebra/path-six/path.py. Three leave C6, which contains P6 as a spanning subgraph (the independent cycle note is supplied too). These exhaust G446 and G448.
6. The remaining marks yield two three-vertex components or a lone edge and a four-vertex component. The sole-edge factorization in isolated-edge-after-branch.md, using k4-six-or.md, excludes G447 vertex 2 and G450 vertices 3 and 4. The triangle-erasure proof in three-three-four.md excludes G447 vertex 1 and G450 vertex 6. The last deletion removes two raw edges and gives a twelve-input residual. The other four disconnected markings remove three and give eleven-input residuals.

## Replay

Run python audit.py for the exact row crosswalk and python -B algebra/path-six/path.py for the finite OR lower bound. With Python and NetworkX installed, rerun python degree/t14-34/eleven-atlas.py from the packet root and python eleven-marked-branches.py from comb/fourteen-construct, then verify sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS from the root. The generators regenerate their respective bundled TSVs; the atlas also includes unrelated order signatures. The audit uses only 4+7. NetworkX represents triangles as cycles of length three in the marked ledger.
