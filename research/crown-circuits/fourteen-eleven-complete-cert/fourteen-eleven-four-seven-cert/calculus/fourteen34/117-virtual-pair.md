# The two surviving edges after erasing the branching flag of mask 117

Assume the inherited 117 + 511 case with sixteen decoder gates, at most eight ORs, and branching pure flag 1 on the triangle 1,2,3 with tail 1,0,4. We use the physical branching conclusion that setting 1 to zero loses at least two ORs (otherwise the sole first OR permits factoring). Use the decoder on original edge tests as a formal ten-input decoder. Every flag in this small graph is essential: for each, adding it to an incident neighbor's other edge creates a positive.

Set 1 to zero and identify flags 2,3 with one new label A and flags 0,4 with a second label B. On the large shore, implement all nine labels of K5 minus one edge with the five incident-OR flags, using thirteen OR gates. Each of the eleven single labels now produces an original rejecting edge, and any two produce a union of distinct original edges. Therefore the threshold-two circuit is correct on all inputs. Since the minimum for eleven labels is 27, the restricted decoder on the seven formal inputs A,B and the five large flags needs at least fourteen gates (for the induced partial task). This lower bound applies to every equivalent decoder on these tests.

Two ORs disappear on erasing 1, so precisely those two gates disappear and fourteen live gates remain. In particular all gates that directly read 1 are ORs: an AND reading 1 would also disappear. Since the flag branches, these two lost gates are its two readers. Their surviving operands propagate to their users; neither can have only zero inputs, since otherwise it would be a redundant OR of two copies of 1 in the original. At least one residual input slot descends from each of the four original small flags other than 1. Formally, trace any occurrence: a surviving reader keeps the slot; a bypassed OR receiving that flag on its nonzero side propagates the flag to one or more readers. A residual dead gate would give fewer than fourteen gates. Distinct original flag identities reach distinct formal slots before identifying A and B, since a bypass copies one operand and cannot merge two distinct live signals. Thus A has at least two residual occurrences. If both reach the same gate, idempotence already saves one gate.

There are at most six ORs in the fourteen-gate residual. Setting A to zero can bypass at most one OR. Indeed the large component needs four ORs even after subsequently erasing B. This second erasure costs at least one OR: B is essential on the surviving tests (one large edge rejects, the same edge plus B accepts), and there is an accepted input at B=0. An AND-only path from B to output would preclude the latter. Consequently every path from B has a first OR, which simplifies upon zeroing B. The same observation initially gives at least one first OR for A, so there is exactly one distinct first OR F on all live paths from A.

With at least two different direct readers of A, the sole first OR permits a saving. If one direct reader is F, another AND-only route to F enters its other operand, which implies A, rendering F redundant. Otherwise every direct reader is an AND. Substitute true for A at these readers and remove them, contracting each to its other operand. Before F each affected AND-only signal originally equaled A AND its new value. Restore the factor once at the affected operand of F, or after F if both operands were affected. Every path from A hits F and all downstream values are preserved. At least two gates were removed and at most one added. This contradicts the fourteen-gate minimum. Therefore the inherited branching 117 + 511 host cannot meet the budget.
