# Four K4 flags require six ORs alongside a nonconjunctive shore

Suppose a monotone binary decoder has a K4 shore, each of whose four single flags spoils some negative edge of the other shore L. Suppose L alone requires at least one OR. Then five decoder ORs do not suffice.

Assume at most five. At most four ORs disappear when the four K4 signals are set to zero, since at least one must remain on L. Apply k4-four-loss-forest.md: there are four distinct ORs at which the four raw flags enter the L-live subsystem, and no internal cold OR. Each entry has a distinct obligatory singleton raw. All other factors entering its cold operand are earlier live signals. In particular each raw influences the output only via its named entry wire.

Order these four entries chronologically, naming their raws x1 through x4. In the K4-only projection the function is threshold three on these flags. At entry i≤3 fix all i−1 earlier raws true and set exactly 2−(i−1) later raws true. Varying xi changes the output. Consequently the previously live operand of that entry OR is false on the all-ones prefix: otherwise that entry's output is fixed true despite the variation. This operand depends only on earlier raws, and hence vanishes throughout the K4-only projection by monotonicity. The first three entries simplify. Only the fourth entry and one other OR J can survive.

For completeness, a two-OR circuit for threshold three on four flags must reduce to the conjunction of two OR factors each of whose operands is a raw monomial. There is no mandatory raw in the output, because no flag is true at all four minimal positive triples. Write the two ORs in order. If only the second is imposed at output, substituting the first inside just one of its operand monomials yields at most three terms. If both its operands contain the first OR, factor it out, giving the claimed product; if both ORs are imposed at output, remove any occurrence of the first inside the second by absorption. If the second OR has only two raw monomials, it cannot alone give four minimal terms. Thus in every case giving four minimal terms both ORs act as output factors after rewriting, and the first OR's internal signal has been removed from the second. The four expanded terms must be precisely the four distinct triples. A variable confined to one factor would occur in an even number of the four terms (zero, two, or four); each raw occurs in three. Every raw therefore occurs explicitly in both factors.

The fourth raw x4 can enter only its own entry OR. If J is earlier, it has no dependence on x4. If J is later, every occurrence of x4 in J comes through the fourth entry's complete OR output, which the factorization removes. Both orders violate the two-factor requirement.

For C4 or a paw on four L flags, L alone needs an OR: there is no conjunction of raws which accepts every positive and rejects every single edge. In each case all four K4 flags spoil if the total budget is five ORs. Otherwise pin an unspoiling K4 flag true and use its three star labels as virtual raws together with all four real L edges; the four L flags cost four OR gates. This would compute threshold two on seven inputs using at most 4+5=9 ORs, below its ten-OR floor. Thus C4+K4 and paw+K4 both require at least six decoder ORs under the edge-pair promise.
