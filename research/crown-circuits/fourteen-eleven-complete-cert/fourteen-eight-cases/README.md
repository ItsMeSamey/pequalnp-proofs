# Component degree profiles for the fourteen-input, eight-AND search

Every regular canonical candidate induces a simple graph with fourteen edges and no isolated vertices. An edge is a raw input; its two distinct reader groups are the endpoints. The published eight-AND analysis bounds vertex degree by four, or by three for connected graphs. Two nonleaf groups are necessary: each mixed group costs a decoder OR while q flags leave q−2 decoder ORs. At least one vertex has degree at least three. For the last condition, if all degrees are at most two, all pure groups are doubletons. At most one of those can branch (the eight-AND one-doubleton theorem). Private doubletons need distinct dedicated clean products with shared partners. If none branches, the collector budget is too small. If one branches, the seven surplus units bound the number of private groups by six, so at least two labels avoid the private groups and require an additional clean product. Again the collector is too small.

component_rows.py inventories multisets of connected component degree sequences, before any typed flags or decoder choices. Each component is stored as counts of vertices of degrees one, two, three and four. The recursion chooses component types in nondecreasing order and spends exactly fourteen edges. Positive degrees pass the Erdős–Gallai inequalities, and the number of edges must be at least the number of vertices minus one. Conversely these conditions ensure a connected realization. In a disconnected realization with enough edges, select a nonbridge edge in a component with a cycle and any edge of a second component. Switch the endpoints across components. This preserves degrees and joins those two components, including when the second edge is a bridge. Repeat to connect the graph.

There are 10,584 surviving component profiles, comprising 679 pairs of global degree sequence and component count, and 225 global degree sequences. The number of vertices ranges from 9 to 25. The counts for 1 through 11 components are 29, 2172, 3583, 2530, 1279, 584, 247, 104, 39, 14 and 3. Distinct simple graphs can share a profile; a profile admits many possible assignments of pure, mixed and branching flags. This list supplies complete outer coverage. The circuit exclusion additionally requires adjacency, gate topology and Boolean evaluation. The compact table is component-rows.tsv.gz. Reproduce it by running python3 -B component_rows.py. degree_rows.py also generates the weaker 825-row global arithmetic relaxation without connected-realization screening.

## Exhaustive refinement and witness test

For a component degree multiset, label its vertices consecutively in degree order. Starting at the first vertex with unsatisfied degree, choose every subset of later vertices of positive residual degree of the required size, add its incident edges, and recurse. Discard disconnected completed components. Taking products over the component list enumerates every possible simple host up to a vertex relabeling; duplicate isomorphic records are harmless.

For any host with q vertices, assign each vertex one formal input. Enumerate all topological circuits D on these inputs with eight ANDs and q−2 ORs. At gate i choose the operation subject to the remaining counts and any unordered pair of earlier wire indices. Take the last gate as output. All gates are binary. No input or intermediate wire needs a fanout cap for this conservative enumeration. A raw-bearing mixed group's raw terms are grouped together as one vertex flag; its additional nonraw terms and all raw-free gates remain in D.

Test D on the indicator of the two endpoints of each of the 14 edges, which must reject, and on endpoint unions for all 91 unordered pairs of distinct edges, which must accept. Monotonicity then gives the correct truth table on all physical subsets of raw edges: each subset of size at least two contains such a pair, and the empty assignment is dominated by every rejecting singleton. Conversely, any passing (host,D) is a physical realization by computing the OR of the incident raw inputs at each vertex and composing D. The vertex bank uses exactly the sum of degree minus one, 28−q ORs, so the complete circuit has eight ANDs and 26 ORs. The 105-test criterion classifies individual graph-and-decoder records. The full domain has not been searched.
