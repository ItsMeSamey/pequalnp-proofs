# Two five-flag components

Call an opposite edge harmless for y if its two flags together with y reject. If t harmless edges span v flags, use one independent input per edge and one per neighbor of y, of degree d. Fix y true, feed its neighbors their single inputs, and each opposite flag the OR of the selected incident inputs. This costs 2t−v ORs. Every singleton rejects, and every pair of inputs gives two distinct original edges. If l direct OR readers of y vanish, the threshold OR floor gives 2(d+t)−4 ≤ 8−l+2t−v. Hence v≤12−2d−l. Every quartic spoils an opposite vertex star. With one direct OR reader a quartic permits harmless edges on at most three vertices; with two readers it permits at most one harmless edge. A cubic with two OR readers spoils a whole opposite vertex star.

Suppose the opposite decoder needs four ORs. A branching pure w with two distinct OR readers and t pendant neighbors costs 2+t ORs to erase with those neighbors. None of the pendant mixed injectors can read w by absorption. If any flags remain in one nontrivial component, peeling costs its order minus one, for a total of five. No nontrivial component would require four pendant neighbors and already cost six. Thus deleting w leaves exactly two disjoint edges, with no pendant neighbor. A subcubic six- or seven-edge component cannot have such a branch, since its number of edges would be degree(w)+2.

A dirty private triple P has a product G true on one singleton t. An OR-only route from G to a local mixed flag V requires that P and V are nonadjacent: if their raw x is t, then x≤G and the raw is redundant; otherwise adding x on t changes neither of x's group outputs. Let W be the other endpoint of t. V cannot equal W by the first claim. If V is adjacent to W, their raw edge is already covered on t, so its addition to t is invisible. Consequently V misses both P and W and has degree at most two. These bounds apply to the physical groups in the original incidence decoder.

## The pair G48 + G48

The only subcubic connected five-vertex graph with seven edges is the graph obtained by deleting a disjoint edge and a two-edge path from K5. Its graph-atlas designation is G48 and the atlas labeling has triangular mask 478. It contains the archived four-OR obstructors 237 and 254. Therefore neither copy can contain a branching pure flag, by the deletion argument above and the established two-OR-reader property for branching doubletons and triples.

In the absence of any branching pure, the earliest AND has two private pure inputs. An overlapping pair factors out its common raw, saving a gate. For disjoint inputs, erasing the labels of one such private group (size two or three) spends the full arity-reduction allowance of 2k+1 gates; the unused opposite private group loses its root as well. Thus these two copies cannot form the host.
