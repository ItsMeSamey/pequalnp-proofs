# Eleven-flag small remote components

The regular 34-gate eight-AND decoder on eleven flags uses nine ORs. Connected five-/six-vertex archived masks whose verified lower bound is h−1 extend to a connected n-vertex component containing that mask by peeling vertices outside the obstruction, giving n−1. A quartic likewise gives n−1 via its star. The atlas IDs, edge counts and inherited lower bounds for component orders at most seven are tabulated in eleven-atlas.tsv.

On a union of small components with at most one pure flag, contract all mixed flags first. Each is paid through its own final raw-flag injection OR, whose other operand is retained. This preserves a path from the remaining pure flag to the output. A duplicate or dead gate is already an additional saving. Otherwise erasing the last pure flag disables an OR along this path: a path of ANDs alone would force a positive surviving assignment false. A K2 has only mixed flags and a P3 or triangle has at most one pure flag, since any pure there is a doubleton and adjacent pure doubletons are excluded. Thus a lone component of size two or three, two K2s, or the combination of K2 and a three-vertex component all cost their full orders. This excludes 2+2+7 and 2+3+6 since the retained large component must be quartic. It also excludes 2+9 and 3+8: any subcubic exception is among the 19 nine-vertex thirteen-edge graphs, 23 eight-vertex eleven-edge graphs, or five cubic eight-vertex graphs. Every one contains an archived obstruction with bound h−1. The replay in eleven-small-remotes.py enumerates all of these by adjoining a noncut vertex to a smaller connected graph.

For the split 2+4+5, erase the mixed K2, leaving at most seven ORs and fifteen decoder gates. In the two 3+10 edge configurations the five-flag K5 needs five ORs and the other four flags cost at least three. In the six configurations with a diamond or K4 opposite seven or eight edges, the latter has a four-OR floor, and the two universal vertices of the diamond or K4 must spoil opposite edges. Indeed without spoiling its three virtual star edges alongside the seven or eight original edges give a threshold minor below its total gate minimum. The four-flag-three-lost-ors lemma therefore costs four, exceeding seven in total.

It remains to consider four edges on four flags opposite K5 minus an edge. Call the small raw of the K2 x. The union of the K2 and four-flag components must cost no more than five ORs. Pay each mixed injector and each distinct clean private terminal. Every private doubleton is clean. A branching doubleton or triple B has two OR readers, each outside all four local mixed flags: the four-vertex component is a cycle or triangle with a pendant leaf, and every vertex of degree at least two has distance at most two to all local flags. At distance one a direct shared raw is absorbed. At distance two along B–W–V, the first edge would activate W and V at once, concealing addition of the second edge. This applies to any OR-only path. The two readers cannot both be paid K2 injectors, as these would be two copies of x∨B. They cannot be collector gates. Thus every single branching exception pays an extra OR. There is at most one local branch: two branching doubletons are globally excluded and a branching triple and neighboring branching doubleton are excluded. The cycle is settled because it has no triple.

For the pendant triangle let A be its universal triple. If A is private dirty its product G has precisely one hot singleton t, cannot feed an AND, and cannot reach any local mixed flag through ORs by universal adjacency. If there is no branch and no supplemental OR, every reader of G must then be the same K2 injector J=x∨G. An OR reader of J would be extra, since the other K2 injector cannot include x twice. Each AND reader of J is clean by the two-exclusive-shores lemma applied to its support {x,t}. At most one other local private doubleton D exists, because the two degree-two vertices are adjacent. A clean product distinct from D's would give another collector saving. But J cannot be single-use as D's opposite input either: erasing D already spends five gates, and J would become dead, with its two singleton labels disjoint from D. Its K2 injection is untouched by simply erasing D.

Finally suppose both A is private dirty and B is a branching doubleton. They need two supplemental ORs. With at most one H, the two direct readers of B must be H and the K2 injector J=x∨B. Set x false first. This contracts exactly the two K2 injectors and yields a minimum 32-gate thirteen-input circuit. Every remaining raw retains its two OR positions: each contracted gate aliases a nonraw operand. In particular B still branches. If both reader paths merge into one position a bypassed gate would have two different nonzero parents, impossible; coincident input positions on a single remaining gate instead produce an immediate idempotent saving. A branching doubleton in this minimum has no AND reader: deleting its two raws already costs four baseline gates and one fanout surplus, and a one-sided AND would exceed the five-gate gap to the eleven-input minimum. A direct AND with dark second operand is redundant by domination. Thus J has an OR successor in the original circuit, following an alias if needed. It cannot enter the other paid K2 injector since that would absorb x, nor any local paid injector by distance two, nor the clean collector. It cannot be H because H already reads B and B∨J=J. This gives the required sixth saving. A branch of A itself has no second dirty exception and was already covered. All ten 2+4+5 configurations are therefore excluded, with the pendant-triangle argument pending independent review.

For 3+3+5 the large graph is quartic. There are at most two small pure flags, at most one branching, since small pure flags are doubletons and only one branching doubleton is possible globally. If both are private their distinct clean collectors pay after all mixed injectors contract. If B branches and D is private, its clean product D∧P has a shared partner P by the dead-partner debit. At least one outgoing route from P avoids this product, since the product's other operand D is a raw-only private group. When the mixed injectors are bypassed only along their nonraw operand, this route survives, or an additional redundant gate already pays the missing credit. Consequently B has a path avoiding the clean product: any route that enters it must previously have reached P. Pin D false and bypass its collector OR. The disappearing product is a clean terminal and has no downstream ANDs, so the avoiding path survives. Erasing B now costs an additional OR. This gives six losses, leaving fewer than the required four.

For the P3+K4+K4 row, erase the three-vertex component for its full order. This leaves six ORs and at most fourteen gates. Each universal flag y of either K4 spoils some opposite edge. If not, fixing y true (at least one gate lost) and combining its three star inputs with six opposite edge inputs via eight bank ORs yields a nine-input threshold circuit with at most 21 gates and 14 ORs. The established 14-OR floor requires eight ANDs, contradiction. Minimize gates among decoders satisfying the resulting signature and budget. Erasing the four flags of a single K4 costs at least four ORs by fixing them one at a time: each remains essential on a spoiler witness with the other small flags zero and positive opposite assignments remain. The opposite local T3^4 needs at least two ORs since its four minimal triples cannot arise from one OR. Thus each individual flag can lose only one OR if erased first. The unique-first-OR factorization in eight-five-511.md shows every flag is single-read. The first gate cannot be a cross AND, since on one shore alone all its flags are essential. A same-shore AND loses one endpoint on a local assignment with the other false, while a same-shore OR identifies a singleton edge and its union with an adjacent edge. A cross OR identifies its two flags; setting the merged flag to an independent input and removing the gate yields a decoder on the union of the two K4 graphs joined at that flag, with at most five ORs. Fix this degree-six junction true and take its six star edges as inputs: the resulting threshold-two circuit needs at least eight ORs. This contradiction excludes the row.
