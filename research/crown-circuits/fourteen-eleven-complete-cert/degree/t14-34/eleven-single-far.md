# A common far injector in G80 and G103

In these six-vertex components the only possible private dirty flag is a triple P. To fall below six payments, the previous exceptional-flag lemma therefore forces P to be dirty and a different pure flag B to branch. It must be a doubleton, at vertex 2 or 3 of G80, or vertex 0 of G103, by the existing branch ledger. In every case B is adjacent to P. The other two labels of P retain both their raw OR readers in the minor obtained by deleting B's two labels: one is the shrunken center P and the other belongs to a group unaffected by B's two labels. Exactly five ORs are lost, as the twelve-input circuit needs 29 gates. If the shrunken two-label P still had a hot singleton t in its product G, resolve that product to P and erase its one other label. This saves three gates and contradicts the eleven-input minimum. Thus the original unique hot singleton of G is the raw shared by P and B.

At most one mixed flag V can receive B through ORs, because all flags at distance at most two are forbidden. In G80, when B is 2 (respectively 3), V is the opposite pendant 5 (respectively 4). In G103 V is vertex 3. The dirty product G cannot reach any neighbor of P by ORs, and cannot reach a neighbor of B either: on its hot raw the two endpoints of the relevant next raw would already be active. The sets of potential local mixed destinations for B and G are therefore both contained in {V}. This last statement uses the actual hot raw PB. The claims can be read directly from the original atlas vertex labels.

Pay the four nonexceptional flags as mixed injectors or dedicated clean collectors. If there were only one additional OR H, the two distinct readers of B would have to be H and the unique paid injector at V. The direct reader of G cannot be an AND (one singleton is hot); it cannot be a paid collector or the occupied V injector, whose only nonraw operand is B. Hence H=B∨G and its singleton support is exactly the two labels of B. Another OR reader of H is an additional payment, since no further mixed injector is available on a path from B. Any AND reader of H must be clean by the two-exclusive-label bound. If all such readers were already counted local private products, their distinct opposite partners imply that H feeds at most one, contradicting the shared-partner lemma. A further clean collector OR consequently vanishes. This proves the full debit of six. The opposite five-vertex graphs have OR floor four.

The excluded pairs are G51+G80, G49+G103 and G50+G103.

## G51 + G81

In G81 the only possible extra doubleton B is 4 or 0. Flag 4 has no far vertex. From 0 the only admissible mixed injectors are the pendant vertices 1 and 2. Write s for raw 0–5, r for 0–4, and suppose one B reader is a far injector Y=B∨a where a is the corresponding pendant raw of P=3. Erase s and a. Four distinct ORs are contracted: B to r, mixed injector 5 to its prior operand, Y to r, and P to its two remaining labels. In the remaining at-most-30 gates, r retains its original OR reader in group 4, its second B reader H, and all outgoing positions of Y. No Y reader can be H (H=B∨Y would absorb B), injector 5 (s≤Y), the original reader of r (r≤Y), or a P-tree gate. The same adjacency exclusions keep H separate from the other contractions. Group 4 is mixed since it is a doubleton adjacent to pure doubleton B. These surviving positions require no further normalization.

A maximal OR group Y has at least two readers or a single AND reader. Thus r has at least four outgoing positions, or three distinct direct readers including an AND. In the latter case the earliest newly dark AND is one-sided: absent such an AND, an earlier first dark OR would need two copies of r, giving a fourth outgoing pin. The raw-deletion ledger saves at least four gates in either case, contradicting the eleven-input minimum of 27. Therefore neither paid pendant injector can receive B, and its two direct readers pay both exceptions.

## G49/G50 + G99

The only branch positions are the triples 2 and 3 and they are symmetric. Set 2 and its pendant 0 false. Two branch-reader ORs vanish together with the distinct pendant mixed injector. What remains is a star of three edges centered at 3 opposite eight edges on five flags, with at most fourteen gates and six ORs. The center must spoil an opposite edge: otherwise its three virtual star edges together with the eight opposite edges compute eleven-input threshold two at bank cost eleven and decoder cost at most fourteen, below 27.

In the projection deleting the star, at most two ORs may vanish because the dense shore needs four. Write k for ORs with one vanishing operand and m for ORs with two vanishing operands. There is at least one exit k since the opposite output survives. The monomial-slot representation of four-flag-three-lost-ors.md grants at most k+2m≤3 raw star monomials. But spoiling requires a monomial consisting of precisely the center singleton. In the local slice with center true the leaves compute threshold two on three bits. Their incidence columns among the other monomials must be pairwise incomparable: if column i is contained in column j, then fixing j false makes i irrelevant, contradicted by the test with the third leaf true. A two-bit Boolean lattice has width two. Thus at least three additional monomials are needed, a contradiction.

## G49/G50 + G102

There is one triple, at 3, so two exceptional flags require a branching doubleton. Vertex 4 has all flags at distance at most two, so both readers would pay outside. At B=0, pin the branch and its pendant 5 false for three distinct OR savings. The remaining paw on 1,2,3,4 is connected. Opposite positives survive and any connected four-vertex component costs at least three ORs to erase using a remote anchor edge. The remaining OR budget is at most six while the opposite shore needs four. This excludes the last mark. Alternatively, first erase paw leaf 4 (the edge 1–3 changes value on adding it). This leaves a triangle and at most thirteen gates and five ORs. All three triangle flags spoil opposite edges: an unspoiled vertex fixed true would give ten-input threshold two on eight real and two virtual edges at bank cost eleven and decoder cost at most twelve, below 24. Thus the triangle costs three more ORs.
