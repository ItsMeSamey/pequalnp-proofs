# The triangle, diamond and K4 case

Erasing the original triangle in the regular eleven-vertex size-34 eight-AND host costs three ORs by the small-component argument. Suppose its diamond D and complete four-vertex component K have a decoder with at most fourteen gates including at most six ORs.

Each universal vertex of D individually spoils some K edge. Otherwise pin that vertex true, taking three independent star labels and six opposite edges with an eight-OR bank. The essential pin saves one gate. This gives nine-input threshold two in at most 21 gates and fourteen ORs, impossible since at the fourteen-OR floor it needs eight ANDs. The four-flag lemma therefore forces four OR losses on setting D to zero. The K-only restriction is threshold three on four flags and uses exactly two ORs.

By the two-OR factorization in comb/fourteen-construct/k4-six-or.md, its four distinct minimal triples are the four products of one arm from each of two ORs of raw conjunctions. A common raw factor is impossible. Each raw r occurs in three of the four triples, so occurs independently in both OR factors (one factor alone gives even multiplicity). In particular a sole direct reader of r in the full circuit cannot be AND. Pinning D to zero never introduces a positive constant. The first surviving OR already depends on r; the sole earlier AND reader would give r a partner which is a nonempty raw conjunction independent of r. Every prime triple containing r would have to contain that partner, whereas their intersection is r alone. A zero reader would make r unobservable. A sole direct OR reader which survives on K is also impossible, since all occurrences of r would be through that OR, preventing independent raw occurrences in both factors. Thus a sole reader must be an OR bypassed upon erasing D.

Every K flag r spoils some D edge. Otherwise pin r true and take three virtual neighbors plus five opposite real edges at a six-OR bank cost. Eight-input threshold two needs nineteen gates and twelve ORs. At most one decoder gate could disappear, and no OR can disappear, so r would have a sole AND reader, impossible.

Minimize total size for this same full flag function within the six-OR cap. Each K flag can lose only one OR when erased first: erasing each of the other three costs one by the persistent spoiler witnesses, and D alone needs two. The single-first-OR factoring lemma thus forces one direct reader per K flag. Each reader must be an OR bypassed on D=0, and no two K flags can share theirs. Write these four gates as r_i OR P_i. Their second operands all vanish on D=0. They exhaust the four lost ORs; no OR has both operands vanishing there.

Call a wire live if it is true at K all ones and D all zero. Since no OR is nonlive, tracing nonlive AND children backwards from P_i yields some diamond raw d_i such that P_i implies d_i on every input. Every other live gate has only live operands. Fix a D edge e, and let I contain the indices i with d_i in e. On e plus a K edge p, the output is bounded above by the K-only output on p union I. Induct on live wires: at reader i its extra operand can be true only for i in I; other gates are monotone on live operands. If I had size at most two, choose p containing I. The positive cross input would be bounded by the negative K-only edge p. Thus each D edge must contain at least three of the four indexed labels d_i. The diamond contains two disjoint edges, which would require at least six distinct indices. This proves the exclusion.
