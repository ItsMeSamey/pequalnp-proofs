# A complete bipartite five-flag shore costs five ORs

In the regular size-34 eight-AND host let one component be K2,3, and suppose at least two opposite edges survive. Its two centers are triples and its other three flags are doubletons. The claim refers to erasing the five formal flags in the physical incidence decoder.

Pay every mixed injector and every private clean flag's dedicated collector OR. The products are distinct: a shared private product is ruled out by factoring intersecting raw sets or by the private-group deletion bound for disjoint sets. Each clean private doubleton or triple has a shared opposite partner, and these partners are distinct by collector factoring. With at most one remaining exceptional pure flag, the full-five debit follows from the avoiding-path lemma of eleven-role-debits: after the mixed injections contract, an exceptional flag cannot feed only these private products, since the latest reachable partner has an alternate path around all the products. Collector bypass preserves this path and erasing the exceptional flag disables another OR. In the all-clean case the initial five payments already suffice.

Every branching flag has two distinct direct OR readers, both outside any paid mixed injector. Indeed all mixed flags lie within distance two of the branch. Distance one gives absorption of their shared raw. At distance two the singleton on the first edge would activate both flags of the second raw, hiding its addition. No branch feeds a clean collector through ORs because it is singleton-hot. Each dirty private flag is a triple. Its product G is true on exactly one singleton t and cannot directly feed an AND. An OR-only path from G into a neighboring mixed flag hides their common raw or makes it redundant. Its only possible nonneighbor is the other center, which is adjacent to the other endpoint W of t. Heating that center on t would hide the raw between that center and W. Thus G likewise must read into a nonlocal, noncollector OR. These arguments apply also through longer OR-only paths.

With exactly two exceptional flags, any branching flag supplies two extra ORs. Otherwise the two are dirty private centers. None of the three doubletons can be private because it would give two private-private edges, whereas two such raw edges cannot exist: over their joint input no AND can first fire when each active raw flag is consumed by a distinct private product. If the two Gs have only one OR reader in total, it is H=G1∨G2. H has one or two hot singleton labels. An OR reader of H is outside the paid slots, and an AND reader must be clean by the two-exclusive-label bound. There are no locally paid private clean collectors; when H vanishes this terminal removes another collector OR, since an opposite positive still survives.

At most three flags can be exceptional. Three require a branching doubleton B and two dirty private centers (the small-branch adjacency restrictions exclude branching at either adjoining center). The remaining two doubletons are mixed. Erase the two original labels of B. The baseline four raw losses plus branching fanout surplus exhaust the gap from 34 to the twelve-input minimum 29. Explicitly the five ORs are the OR at B, one contraction in each center, and the two nonlocal OR readers. Every AND survives. Reassociate the centers before erasure so each now computes its two remaining raws through a single OR. Each of the four other original raw labels of the centers still has two distinct OR readers: its own center and the unaffected mixed group at its other endpoint. Its remaining occurrence in that group's raw tree is unaffected by either nonlocal B reader. If G_i retained a hot singleton t, resolving its AND to its two-label center can introduce at most the other singleton r. Erasing r then saves its two raw OR readers as well as the resolved AND. This produces an eleven-input threshold circuit of size at most 26, below 27. Therefore the original hot singleton of each G_i belongs to B.

If only two supplemental ORs could be charged, both are direct readers of B and together must directly receive both G_i, so they are precisely B∨G1 and B∨G2. Each has singleton support B, of size two. An OR reader lies outside every local paid slot and cannot be the other one of these two gates. An AND reader is clean, giving a further separate collector debit. This completes the full-five proof. In the eleven-flag ledger every remaining G44 pair has an opposite floor of five, exceeding the nine-OR total.
