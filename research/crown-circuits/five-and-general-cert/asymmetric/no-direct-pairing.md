# A size-40 12-crown without a direct receiver is pairable for any AND count

Here “no direct receiver” means that no selected raw observer mask is equal, on the entire direction cube, to any available split-branch mask. This is a property of the masks, not merely of their wiring. A semantically equal branch can always receive that raw instead.

Normalize comparable row and column receivers to their larger mask. On one rejecting pattern per label every receiver is the co-singleton of its label, so across labels all receivers are incomparable. The remaining distinct receivers form an antichain on the full cube. Let d be the number of incomparable same-label twins. For d≤2 select opposite shores for the two exceptions; these and the equal twins give a paired representation with no cost increase.

If d≥3 then all 12+d distinct receivers are paid unions, leaving h≤5−d other paid masks, since the 40-gate transposition budget is 17. A receiver cannot be an OR ancestor of a distinct receiver. Use the earliest existing copy of any duplicate mask. Count the 12−d paired labels, whose nonempty rejecting sets are pairwise disjoint. Any ordinary two-branch receiver on distinct coordinates misses the whole opposite quarter-cube. There are at most three paired ordinary receivers: four would fill the cube and leave no witness for other labels. Sibling unions simply restore the earlier activation.

Suppose X=b union c is an auxiliary formed by two different-coordinate branches. For a receiver X union b', where b' is the sibling of b, the identity X union b'=U_b union c exposes the activation U_b available before X. If it is full the result is impossible. If it is a receiver then antichain forces equality to that earlier receiver; use its earlier expression. If it is a branch the presentation is ordinary (or a previously available sibling union). If it is a helper the presentation uses an earlier helper instead. A repeated constituent cannot give a new receiver. Hence new paired receivers requiring X with a single branch must use external coordinates. Any two different external coordinates can both have their wrong orientation on the face opposite b,c, making both receivers reject together. At most two such paired receivers are possible: their partner orientations would have to be opposites on one coordinate.

If d=5, no helper is available and seven paired labels are required. Larger d does not fit the union budget. If d=4, at most one helper is available and eight paired labels are required, exceeding 3+2. If d=3, nine paired labels are required and at most two helpers are available. In the independent case order the two branch-pair helpers X,Y chronologically; classify first the ordinary receivers, then those achievable by X with a branch, then the further ones achievable by Y with a branch. A cancellation at Y can rewrite to a presentation using X and a branch. The remaining possible receiver X union Y accounts for only one more. Thus the bound is 3+2+2+1=8.

In the nested case Y=X union t, suppose t has a new coordinate. A partner equal to the sibling of t yields X union U_t, and this belongs to a previous class (the only earlier helper is X). The other potential internal-coordinate partners are just the two siblings of b and c. Among at least three new Y-assisted paired labels, any two partner branches hit a common third label's representative since Y misses every label in their group. Thus opposite branches cannot both occur. Any two distinct external coordinates cannot both occur either: in the face opposite b,c,t their simultaneous wrong orientations cause overlapping rejections. Therefore at most one external partner appears in a group of three or more, and this group has at most three labels. X union Y equals Y. The total is at most 3+2+3=8. If t is the sibling of an X constituent instead, Y=U_b union c with U_b available before either helper. Hence Y is independent, already available, full, or above a receiver (then it cannot help a new receiver). A helper receiving an earlier receiver by a union cannot create a new receiver by further unions, by the antichain. This also covers fewer than two useful helpers.

All d≥3 cases are impossible. The argument uses no upper bound on the number of AND coordinates.
