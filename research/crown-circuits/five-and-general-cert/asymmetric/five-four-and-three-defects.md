# Excluding four defects and the three-defect case without a direct receiver

Use the notation of five-defects.md. All paired receivers have nonempty, pairwise disjoint rejection sets, disjoint also from every other label's rejecting diagonal pattern. Each ordinary union (two split branches of different coordinates) misses an entire eight-point codimension-two face in the full five-cube. The union of sibling branches is the activation, already available. Repeated masks can be bypassed.

## Four defects

There are 16 distinct receivers. Without a direct branch receiver there is at most one auxiliary union X. If a receiver uses X as an operand, its other operand is a split branch. For any subset of at least three distinct labels reached in this way, X misses all their representing patterns, so each partner branch must accept every other such label. Distinct partner branches therefore come from distinct coordinates. There are at most five such receiver masks (also when there are at most two labels, since each has at most two distinct full receivers). Thus at least eleven distinct ordinary receiver masks are required. Each needs a different opposite face containing at most one of the twelve representatives. Write e for the number of empty such faces, u for the number of represented points with at least one singleton face, and v for the number with at least two. At most u+min(4,v)+e ordinary masks can count, since just four labels need two receivers. The C++ program faceprofiles.cpp chooses a coordinate split with minimum minority size, preserving the complement symmetry. It enumerates orbits of each minority shore under the four-cube isometry group and the possible majority shores under that orbit's stabilizer. This covers every twelve-subset, with possible repetitions. Across 108,866 representatives, 59 meet both lower bounds of eleven. One lies in a coordinate half, so all twelve diagonal witnesses are retained on fixing the corresponding selector. Fixing that AND to the chosen parent preserves every positive cell and eliminates an AND. The established unrestricted four-AND theorem excludes this case. As an alternative independent calculation, auxfaces.py on all 58 other profiles returns no survivor.

With a direct receiver, orient its unique outlying representative to pattern 00000 and the corresponding branch to the fifth bit set. All eleven other representatives lie in that half. An outlying representative in the same half again reduces to four ANDs. Permutations of the four remaining coordinates yield 284 unordered classes of the eleven-point subsets of that half. The circuit has at most two auxiliary unions.

On a fixed set of twelve patterns, the source feeding any selector is either full or an arbitrary union of split branches earlier in some total chronological order. The condition is necessary even when auxiliary and receiver unions feed a split, by expanding their unions. For the receiver expressions, any auxiliary reached from a receiver by a path of unions cannot feed a different receiver by unions: it would include the first receiver, contradicting incomparability (or be an avoidable duplicate). Thus auxiliaries that can actually help a receiver are generated exclusively from branches and earlier such auxiliaries, without going through another receiver. Missing auxiliary slots may be padded with an arbitrary binary union. The SAT relaxation low_aux.py represents the values of the branches and auxiliary masks on the twelve representatives, then requires at least one co-singleton receiver per label. For four labels require two expressions that are incomparable as sets of their immediate operand indices, a necessary condition for their full masks to be incomparable. For the direct case require the direct branch as a receiver of the outlying label. The 58 nonconstant indirect profiles and 284 direct profiles were all UNSAT, with no budget exhaustion. Logs are low-nondirect4.out and low-direct4.out. This is an executable numerical proof awaiting independent certificate checking.

## Three defects, no direct receiver

Nine labels have paired receivers. At most two can be ordinary unions: three disjoint eight-point faces and nine reserved points for the other labels would exceed 32. There are at most two auxiliary unions, and thus at least seven of the paired labels must draw on them.

If X is a union of branches on k distinct selector coordinates, at most k+1 different paired labels can use the receivers X union b, provided that at least three labels do so. Their partner branches are distinct, accept all the other labels in the group, and therefore cannot be opposite branches. A partner cannot be a constituent of X, which would give an avoidable copy. The k opposite constituents give at most k options. Two partners on different external coordinates have a joint rejected point in the flat where all constituents of X are absent. This would reject two paired labels on the same resolution, which is impossible. Hence at most one external partner is possible.

If X and Y independently combine two branches on distinct coordinates, at most three paired labels can use each auxiliary with a branch; at most one more has receiver X union Y. To attain nine, there must be two ordinary receivers, exactly three auxiliarily assisted by each X,Y, and one receiver X union Y. This spends at least 16 patterns on the ordinary receivers. Of the three paired receivers for each auxiliary, the obligatory external partner misses a four-point face, and the other two require at least one pattern apiece. Thus twelve more patterns are spent. X union Y needs one and the three exceptional labels need three distinct diagonal patterns. This already accounts for all 32. Nonpairability of the three exceptional labels requires a direction missing two of their row masks and a direction missing two of their column masks, away from all paired receivers and all diagonal witnesses. The former alone gives a contradiction.

For nested auxiliaries write X=b1 union b2 and Y=X union t. A repeated constituent gives Y=X. If t is the opposite branch to b1, replace their union by that selector's activation U; it predates X and Y. If U is full then Y cannot aid a receiver; if U is a receiver then Y cannot aid an incomparable receiver. Otherwise U must be a split branch, rewriting Y as an independent two-branch auxiliary, already excluded. The same applies to b2. Otherwise Y combines three distinct coordinates. Its paired receivers number at most four, while X's number at most three. Since X union Y is just Y, attaining nine requires two ordinary labels, three for X, and four for Y. Saturation of both external-partner bounds forces each group to use the opposite of b1. This produces two different paired receivers, one containing the other, a contradiction.

Thus a genuinely nonpairable five-AND construction would have precisely three incomparable receiver pairs and a direct receiver. In that branch the opposite shore's eleven receivers for other labels must cover the entire direction half opposite the direct branch. None of these eleven can be an ordinary two-branch union: it would have to use the opposite branch of the direct coordinate in order to cover the full half. Its other feature then covers at most eight of the eleven good-half representatives, although ten are required.

## Direct receiver with three auxiliary unions

The eleven opposite-shore receivers each use an auxiliary. Let S be their eleven representative patterns in the direct-coordinate 4-cube. The direct feature itself is true at all eleven points and cannot occur in any of their expressions. The opposite feature is false throughout S. Every auxiliary relevant via union paths to any of these receivers is a union of ordinary branches or earlier such auxiliaries. A raw receiver ancestor would contradict incomparability. Give the search the nine starting features consisting of the negative direct feature and the eight signed branches of the other four selectors. Enumerate three additional distinct unions. Fewer actual auxiliaries can be padded with arbitrary unused unions; repeated formal sets of features are already available.

Each of the eleven expressions must cover the entire opposite 4-cube. If it omits the negative direct feature and contains no opposite orientations of any remaining coordinate, the pattern opposite all its constitutents disproves that coverage. Apply this necessary test, then delete the negative direct feature (it is false throughout S). The remaining expressions are represented by subsets of eight signed features.

For p in S let L(p) contain the four signed features whose coordinate orientations match p, independently of actual split activity. Each p needs an expression E_p rejecting just that point in S. For different p,q there must be a feature in E_q intersect L(p) outside E_p and one in E_p intersect L(q) outside E_q. These constraints suffice for the relaxation allowing arbitrary per-pattern activity: declare L(p) minus E_p active at p. Backtracking checks the pair conditions as it assigns expressions. An initial bipartite matching filters assignments reusing identical expressions.

The file covercompatible.cpp enumerates all 27 eleven-point shapes under sign flips and permutations of the four remaining coordinates. Every family is tested against every shape, so symmetry remains valid for noninvariant families. There are 18,354 sets of three auxiliary expressions obtainable by binary unions, including nested auxiliaries. The weaker matching filter passes 114,775 of the 495,558 combinations (coverfamilies.cpp). Full pair compatibility passes none. This argument never needs the outlying label's exact code.

Consequently any five-AND size-at-most-40 12-crown representation implies a paired representation within the same budget. The unrestricted four-AND case had previously been excluded. Existence of the paired circuit remains a separate problem.
