# An asymmetric five-AND crown of size 40 has at most four incomparable receiver pairs

Consider 12 distinct resolving patterns in the five-dimensional Boolean cube, one witnessing each rejected diagonal of the 12-crown. Reversing the circuit yields five ordered coordinate splits and at most 17 unions. Each receiver for either raw of label i has exactly the other eleven representatives in its mask. All arguments about comparability refer to the complete 32-pattern masks. Replacing two comparable receivers by the larger for both raw ports preserves every positive input, and preserves a rejecting witness for every diagonal. We may use this normalization before defining d as the number of incomparable receiver pairs.

Two distinct split branches cannot both be receivers. Opposite branches of one split are disjoint. Branches from distinct splits share at least ten representatives if both are receivers, whereas two coordinate constraints have only eight joint patterns. Thus there are 12+d distinct receivers, at most one of which is a split branch. At most 6−d of the 17 unions are auxiliary. A receiver union cannot directly consume another receiver: different labels are incomparable on the representatives, and the normalized twins of any one label are either equal or incomparable on the entire cube. Duplicate masks can be bypassed without altering the result. Unioning sibling split branches merely recreates their previously available activation.

**A direct receiver requires at least two auxiliary unions.** Let its label be i. The other eleven representatives S lie in one coordinate half, a 4-cube. Any receiver of j in S made by unioning two split features must use two of the four other coordinates. The direct feature contains j, while its opposite covers no point of S, and no single other feature covers the required ten. The two complementary coordinate values define a 2-face of the 4-cube containing at most one point of S. A distinct receiver construction of this kind uses a distinct face. At most six faces can have this property. To verify, enumerate the 4,368 five-element complements of S. The counts for zero, one, two, three, four, and six such faces are 960, 1536, 768, 896, 192, and 16. The program sparse_faces.py performs the enumeration without external dependencies.

If X is the sole auxiliary union, choose for each of the remaining labels one first receiver. Its operands must include X and another split feature. X misses every label in this group. For any group of five labels, the partner feature for each would therefore have to contain the other four and omit its own label. These five features are distinct and cannot include opposite branches of one selector, since they pairwise meet. The direct selector supplies neither branch: one contains the label to be omitted, and its opposite misses the four to accept. Only four coordinates remain. Therefore at most four more labels have receivers, leaving the required eleven unaccounted for. This excludes d≥5 when there is a direct receiver.

**Without a direct receiver, five defects are impossible.** Six or more would already exceed 17 unions. With five defects there are precisely 17 distinct receiver unions and no auxiliaries. Thus each is formed by two branches of different selectors. They require 17 distinct codimension-two faces of the 5-cube containing at most one of the twelve representatives. There are at most fourteen such faces. Each coordinate pair yields at most two sparse cells, since each cell holds at most eight elements. If it yields two, their union is the exceptional side of a parity character of weight one or two, violated by at most two of the twelve points. If all such near-constant characters have rank at most one, at most four coordinate pairs can have two sparse cells, yielding at most fourteen overall. Otherwise two independent characters have at least eight simultaneous good representatives. Their affine 3-flat has size eight, so all of it is present. Each coordinate pair with no nonzero character from their two-dimensional span supported inside that pair is surjective on the flat, with every cell containing exactly two flat points; it has no sparse cells. At most seven of the ten coordinate pairs can be exceptional. In fact, two singleton characters give seven, a singleton and a doubleton disjoint from it give five, and in a span without singletons at most three weight-two characters give at most three pairs. This proves the fourteen bound.

**Pairing test.** Two representatives chosen from opposite shores of distinct labels always have union equal to all direction patterns. Choosing one receiver per label for both shores preserves the complete crown whenever no two chosen row masks miss the same direction and likewise for chosen column masks. All comparable twins can be fixed to their larger masks. With at most two remaining incomparable labels choose opposite shores. For exactly three incomparable labels, failure of every such normalization requires every pair in the three to have intersecting row complements and intersecting column complements: otherwise select that nonconflicting same-shore pair and the remaining label on the opposite shore. Consequently only three- and four-defect circuits can resist this pairing test.
