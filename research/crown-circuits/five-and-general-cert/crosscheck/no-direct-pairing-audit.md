# Audit of the no-direct receiver pairing argument

The counting argument applies to a reverse network beginning with the full Boolean cube of resolving patterns. Each binary split has a distinct coordinate and emits the two intersections of its activation with the coordinate halves. Paid nodes form binary unions. Each of the 24 raw ports observes a mask. The crown requires full union for each off-diagonal row–column pair and proper union for each diagonal. The network has at most 17 paid unions. The no-direct hypothesis excludes equality of any observed mask with any split branch on the entire cube.

This last hypothesis is semantic. For example, split a branch on a fresh coordinate, union its two children, and place an observer on the union. The observer is wired to a paid union and its mask still equals the older branch. Reassigning the observer to that branch places this circuit in the direct case. A statement based only on inspecting the last wire of each receiver would require an additional argument.

Choose one rejecting witness for each diagonal. Off-diagonal acceptance makes each raw mask contain all eleven witnesses of other labels. The witnesses are distinct. Comparable twins can both observe their larger mask, preserving the diagonal witness and every positive incidence. After this replacement distinct observed masks form an antichain. Distinct already-paired labels have disjoint sets of rejected directions. For at most two remaining incomparable labels, retaining the row mask for one and the column mask for the other preserves all off-diagonal tests, including those involving an already-paired label. This uses the ability to reassign the observers and reconstruct the forward circuit within the original budget.

If there are d ≥ 3 incomparable twins, at least 12+d paid unions are receivers, so at most 5−d others remain. For counting the 12−d paired labels, trace unions backwards only up to split branches. A proper union successor of a receiver cannot be another receiver along a path consisting of unions. A split along that path would invalidate the inclusion argument. Reuse of the earliest copy of a mask covers repeated and idempotent unions. An ordinary receiver with two differently coordinated branch operands misses the full opposite one-quarter cube, regardless of the activations. At most three paired labels have this form, because four disjoint quarters exhaust the cube and leave no rejecting witness for a fifth label.

For a helper X=b∪c on two distinct coordinates, an X-assisted receiver using a sibling of b or c reduces to the corresponding earlier activation union the remaining branch. A full activation is impossible, a receiver activation forces equality with that receiver, a branch activation gives an ordinary or reducible receiver, and an earlier helper activation moves the presentation to the earlier class. Paired receivers newly requiring X therefore need external partner branches. Two such partners on different axes leave a common direction rejecting both outputs by choosing all four wrong orientations. Two on the same axis must be its two opposite branches. This bounds additional paired labels by two.

Two independent helpers yield at most 3+2+2+1=8 paired labels, with the last slot reserved for their union. For nested helpers Y=X∪t on a fresh third axis, unioning Y with the sibling of t reduces to X union the earlier activation of t. Apart from lower classes, the permitted internal partners are the two siblings of b and c. Their own branches are absorbed. Distinct external axes again force overlapping rejections on the cube face opposite b,c,t. If there are at least three Y-assisted paired labels, opposite branches cannot both be external partners: at the third label's diagonal witness Y is false, and each of the other two receivers must be true there. This leaves at most one external partner and at most three new Y receivers. If t repeats or opposes an X constituent, idempotence or sibling cancellation instead returns to the lower or independent case. Thus the nested bound is 3+2+3=8.

The required paired-label counts are seven at d=5, eight at d=4, and nine at d=3, exceeding the respective upper bounds three, five and eight. These counts do not restrict the number of split coordinates. The circuit-size conclusion relies on the transpose/reconstruction gate accounting for this representation. This audit checks the combinatorial argument on that representation.
