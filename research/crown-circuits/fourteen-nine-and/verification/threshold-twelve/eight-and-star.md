# Excluding eight AND gates at size 28 for twelve-input threshold two

Work in a minimum cleaned, clean-terminal counterexample. The lower bound of 26 on eleven inputs implies all twelve raw inputs have two OR readers, as in the eleven-input proof. Flatten binary ORs with fanout one and an OR consumer into that consumer. Each maximal OR group has k raw operand occurrences and t nonraw occurrences, costs k+t−1 binary ORs, and groups partition the OR gates. No group repeats a raw (idempotence), and two raws cannot belong to the same pair of groups (one singleton then already activates both groups and adding the second raw cannot change the output). Thus raw labels are twelve simple undirected edges on groups. A group is pure if t=0. Write p for the number of pure groups. With eight AND gates there are twenty ORs, hence r=Σ_mixed(t−1)=p−4 since the raw degree sum is 24.

**Small pure groups.** At most one pure group has degree two, by small-pure-groups.md. No pure group has degree one, since each binary OR tree has at least two incoming slots. Every pure group U of degree k=2,3,4 must have fanout one. Here is the needed extra argument. Delete its k raw labels. The number of eliminated gates is at least 2k plus the sum of dark-gate fanout surpluses and the number of one-sided dark ANDs, by the exact dark-edge identity. The lower bounds at arities ten, nine and eight allow only 2k+1 total savings. If U branches, its own surplus exhausts this allowance, so a one-sided dark AND is impossible. Nor can there be a fully dark AND. Before the earliest dark AND, every dark nonraw signal is an OR of a subset of U's labels. A dark OR outside U's private tree cannot be the first such outside OR: if its operands are two raw labels in U then they belong to U and this outside OR's group, contradicting simplicity. If one operand is U or is downstream of U, the OR would have the full OR of U's labels as its own function and is redundant. Private proper internal tree nodes feed only the next node inside the tree. Thus the only exposed dark nonraw wire is U. There are no raw-facing ANDs, and an earliest fully dark AND cannot read U twice. No AND therefore disappears. The restricted circuit retains all eight ANDs at size at most 27−2k, but the physical OR lower bound requires 2(12−k)−4=20−2k ORs, a contradiction. This proves every small pure group is unshared and feeds an AND directly.

There must be a pure group of at least five labels. Otherwise every pure group would be small and unshared. Every OR signal before the first forward AND would then have only raw leaves when flattened: a preceding OR entering such a group as a nonraw slot would have to be a shared OR; the earliest shared OR would itself be pure. Thus the first AND reads two pure unshared groups. This was excluded for any two unshared pure operands when at least one has size at most four, by factoring an overlapping label, or by pruning the other operand after deleting a disjoint small operand's labels (small-pure-groups.md).

**Star restriction.** Choose a pure group U with k labels and let d be the number of its neighbors that are pure groups. Keep exactly the k edge variables incident to U; set all other raw labels false and hardwire U's output true. Every nonempty retained assignment already makes U true. The empty assignment still rejects by monotonicity because every singleton rejects. This gives a circuit for threshold two on k variables. Its pure group neighbors represent d distinct raw variables, all other pure groups apart from U become false. By simplicity, each of the other k−d labels enters exactly one mixed group, and no mixed group receives two of these variables.

Counting residual binary ORs by group, a mixed group without such an injected raw uses at most t−1 ORs, and a mixed group with injected x uses at most t. Thus at most r+k−d ORs suffice. Further savings are forced at the beginning. View the pure-neighbor variables as all available from the start. A mixed group with its single injected x computes x∨Q from earlier signals, and no other occurrence of x remains available elsewhere. On every singleton of another retained variable y, Q must be false, since otherwise changing y to x+y could not affect that group or any other raw injection depending on x and therefore could not change the output. As long as at most one retained variable was previously available, monotonicity and singleton rejection force Q identically false. Consequently the first max(0,2−d) mixed injections cost at least one fewer OR each. (Here k≥2, so there are enough injections.) The star minor has at most r+k−max(d,2) ORs. Its 2k−4 lower bound gives

    k + max(d,2) ≤ r+4 = p.

A large group k≥5 now requires p≥7. The other pure groups have degrees at least three apart from at most one doubleton, so 24≥Σpure degrees≥5+3(p−1)−1 and p≤7. Therefore p=7, k=5, and its d≤2. The large vertex has at least three different incident edges ending at mixed vertices. But the pure degree sum is at least 5+18−1=22, leaving at most two of the 24 raw incidences at mixed vertices. This contradiction excludes the case of eight ANDs.
