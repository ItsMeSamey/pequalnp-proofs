# Small pure OR groups in a hypothetical 28-gate twelve-input threshold circuit

Use a minimum clean-terminal circuit. The eleven-input lower bound of 26 implies that specializing any raw false can save at most two gates. Exactly as in the eleven-input argument, all twelve raws have degree two and both readers are ORs. Flatten single-use ORs with OR consumers, as in threshold-eleven/eleven-proof.md. The raw labels form the edges of a simple graph on the resulting OR groups. A pure group U has no nonraw operands and computes the disjunction of its incident raw variables. Two pure groups share at most one raw.

For a pure group U on k=2,3,4 labels, delete its entire raw support. The dark-edge identity lower-bounds the saving by 2k plus (i) the sum of fanout minus one over dark gates and (ii) the number of dark ANDs with exactly one dark operand. If U branches, (i) contributes at least one. Otherwise U cannot be the threshold output and must feed an AND, since a sole OR reader would have absorbed U. For its product U∧P, the other operand P is not dark: otherwise P implies U on the entire Boolean cube and the AND is redundant. Thus (ii) contributes one. The ten-, nine- and eight-variable lower bounds of 23,21,19 respectively imply that the saving is exactly 2k+1. There can be no extra dead-gate removal. The surviving circuit for the corresponding smaller threshold already has minimum size. A branching U has exactly two consumers; a single-use U contributes the unique one-sided dark AND.

There is at most one pure group with two labels. First consider a branching size-two group U. A second branching one would consume two extra fanout charges on a union of at most four labels, when only one is affordable. Consider then a different size-two group V that is single-use, feeding an AND E. On specializing U's two variables, V retains at least one raw as a signal. E cannot die: since V is not dark, E would count as a one-sided dark AND, while U has spent the single extra on its own branching. If V has a label in common with U, V becomes a raw directly entering E in a size-23 ten-input threshold: impossible, since the nine-input lower bound of 21 allows at most two savings on a single specialization. A degree-two input entering an AND saves at least three; a degree-one raw-facing AND also saves three by direct specialization. A degree of three saves at least three directly.

If V is disjoint from U, both of its two raw inputs still enter the surviving cherry V. In the ten-input minor, each of these raw variables must have at least one additional consumer, since otherwise it cannot change the output on the other variable's singleton. The nine-input floor gives degree at most two. Deleting V's two labels therefore has four raw incidences, and the eight-input lower bound 19 allows no additional saving. But the remaining product E=V∧P is irredundant in the size-23 minimum, so P cannot be dark on deleting V. This incurs an additional one-sided dark AND, contradiction.

Now suppose that both size-two groups U,V are single-use. Let their AND readers be D,E. Deleting U's two variables incurs D as its unique one-sided dark AND. If D and E are distinct, E survives as before, because V retains a nonzero signal; apply the same ten-input argument. Thus D=E. If U and V share the label z, the three gates (x∨z)∧(y∨z) are replaceable by the two gates z∨(x∧y). If U,V are disjoint, deleting U's labels incurs the exact five gates already accounted for, but the undisturbed single-use cherry V now has no consumer. Pruning it saves a sixth gate and contradicts the ten-input lower bound. Both cases are impossible.

More generally, no AND can read two single-use pure groups if one has two, three or four variables. An overlap is factored from their two raw-only disjunctions, saving a gate using associativity/distributivity. For disjoint supports, deleting the smaller group incurs the full affordable saving yet its other single-use nonconstant operand becomes dead. This last statement does not bound the size of that other operand.
