# Four-edge remote forks in a tight three-component host

Assume the size-34, nine-AND regular receiver graph has three components including a degree-four center. Each other component must cost at most its order minus one decoder OR to erase: the center and the other remote component already attain the remaining layer lower bound. Suppose the chosen remote component has maximum degree three and at least one pure flag of degree three.

A private dirty triple Q has one hot singleton t and two cold labels. Its product G has a single OR user. Erase both cold labels and bypass G to t, saving four raw-occurrence ORs and one AND. The resulting twelve-input circuit has size 29. A surviving occurrence of t is in its other group, so multiple uses of G would give three raw readers and violate C(11)=27. Bypassed degree-one mixed injectors preserve the nonraw occurrences; merging identical operands would save another gate, violating C(12). A direct AND user of G would give a raw AND reader in this twelve-input optimum with another reader, again exceeding the two-gate deletion budget. An OR group containing G cannot read any raw z of Q. Its hot raw t is dominated by G. For cold z the identity (Q∧P)∨z=Q∧(P∨z) preserves size but leaves the new AND only one exclusive Q singleton, forbidden by C(13)=32.

A bare three-edge star is impossible. Its three pendant mixed injectors disappear and its private dirty product then saves an OR outside those injectors. A clean center instead saves a collector OR. In a four-edge paw, every possible mixed group is adjacent to the unique cubic center Q. The dirty product thus saves an OR beyond the mixed injectors and the distinct private clean collector ORs; again the whole component pays at least its order. If Q is clean the same full cost follows immediately.

The remaining four-edge shape is the star with one subdivided arm. Write the three central labels as t,u,r, where the subdivided arm begins at r and ends at s. Its middle flag B reads r,s. For Q's dirty product to avoid full cost it must enter the far mixed leaf group H containing s: every other possible mixed target contains an incident Q raw. Here H has exactly one nonraw input G. If there were another term, eliminating both s and G would save two ORs within H, whether or not all its terms vanish; that exceeds its one mixed-injection debit. The hot label cannot be r: on r alone the middle B and H are active, so adding their shared raw s would not change the output. Relabel the two pendant central labels to make t hot and u cold. Thus H=s∨G.

If B is pure, it is a private clean doubleton. Erase r,s, eliminating B's bank OR, the r input OR in Q, the s OR in H, B's clean product, and a collector OR. This attains the size-29 twelve-input minimum. Q has shrunk to t∨u, with its surviving AND still hot at t. Resolve this AND to Q, then erase u, the only singleton newly admitted by that resolution. Both raw OR occurrences of u remain: one lies in t∨u, the other in u's own mixed pendant injector. This gives size at most 26 on eleven inputs, contrary to C(11)=27.

If B is mixed, erase r,u and resolve G to t. Exactly four ORs and one AND disappear: two ORs in Q, the r contraction of B's raw bank, and the u-leaf injector. The far group H now computes the pure flag s∨t. The surviving two mixed injectors read t at its old pendant group and s at B. Hence this detached component has become a two-edge path on three flags, down from five. Treat H's existing OR as the path's bank. The total bank size is 24−q' and the remaining ORs total 21, giving q'−3 decoder ORs for the new host with q'=q−2. The graph still has three components, retaining the degree-four center. Since the physical size is the minimum 29, H has an output path. Set both mixed flags to zero, bypassing their two dedicated ORs and preserving every H path by retaining each nonraw side. Setting H false now eliminates a further OR: any path carrying H only through ANDs to the output would force the output zero on the nonconstant central subproblem. This charges three ORs for the detached three-vertex path, whereas the tight layer bound permits only two.

Thus the detached subcubic high-pure component needs at least five edges. In particular if the degree-four center is a branching quartet and the only quartic flag of the host, no such three-component circuit exists. The center's component must contain at least one edge surviving deletion of the quartet and its pendant neighbors, by the layer bound at another surviving cubic center. The three components then need at least 5+5+5 edges.
