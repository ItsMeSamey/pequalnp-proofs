# Maximum raw-incidence degree four at fourteen inputs, size 34 and nine ANDs

The regular raw-incidence graph has v vertices and its decoder uses v−3 ORs. Fix a center of degree d≥3. Each remote component without a pure flag of degree ≥3 consists of mixed vertices and private pure doubletons by small-branches.md. Each doubleton is a clean terminal operand: resolving any AND and deleting one raw shows that each exclusive singleton shore has size ≥2 by the thirteen-input size floor. Their dedicated terminals are distinct. If two single-use doubletons feed the same product and overlap, factor out their common raw to save one gate. If disjoint, erasing one pair already costs five gates (four raw reads plus the one-sided product), saturating the twelve-input size bound. The other doubleton would also become dead, losing its separate unchanged OR root. Thus deleting all flags of such remote components saves one dedicated injection OR per mixed flag and one distinct collector OR per private flag, since some remaining terminal must cover a pair in the central star. These two accounts are disjoint.

Each of the c other remote components costs at least its order minus one OR by the simultaneous-peeling argument in geometry/eleven27-seven/receiver-layers.md. It has at least three edges because it contains a pure flag of degree at least three. Peeling central vertices outside the closed neighborhood costs one OR apiece, and the d-neighbor slice costs at least 2d−4. Thus v−3≥v+d−5−c gives c≥d−2. From the fourteen-edge budget, 14≥d+3(d−2). Hence d≤5.

At degree five equality forces a K1,5 center and three remote K1,3 stars whose centers are pure. Their nine leaves are mixed. Contracting their nine dedicated injection ORs with these flags zero preserves every path from a surviving pure wire, because the nonraw input to each affected OR is retained. One remote pure center can now be set false for at least one further OR saving: a surviving path made only of AND gates after that center would make the output zero, contrary to the central star positives. A first OR reached with a zero operand is removable. The remote graph therefore costs at least ten ORs, giving at most two units of slack from twelve remote vertices, although degree five needs three. This rules out degree five.
