# Eight AND gates at sizes 24 and 31

Assume a minimum ten-input threshold-two circuit has eight ANDs and sixteen ORs. The nine-input floors are 21 gates and 14 ORs. The size-21, seven-AND case on nine inputs is already excluded by verification/threshold-twelve/or-tight.md, since the eight-input 19-gate lower bound forces a regular representative.

Deleting raw x bypasses every reader. Thus x has at most three readers, at most two ORs. A direct AND G=x∧P cannot feed another direct reader of x, by absorption, and G cannot be the output. Thus if x has three readers including G, it also loses a separate successor on deleting x, contradicting the three-gate saving allowance.

Every path from G to output reaches a proper OR: an AND-only path would bound the output by x. A first such OR cannot also read raw x, for its other operand would be bounded by x. If x has one AND and one OR reader, erasing x saves both and that distinct first proper OR. The floors force the forbidden size-21, seven-AND minor.

If x feeds two ANDs, deletion saves both, every intervening dark AND before a first proper OR, and every first proper OR. The three-gate allowance forces both ANDs to feed the same OR directly and exclusively. Factoring x out saves one gate.

If x feeds just one AND G=x∧P, P is independent of x and accepts every other singleton y, since adding x must distinguish x+y from y. Thus P computes the full disjunction on the other nine inputs. Its root is an OR: at an AND both children would also compute the full disjunction and the gate would be redundant. Erasing x deletes G and a first proper downstream OR. Now remove P as well: every P∧Q equals Q, every P∨Q equals P, and the output cannot equal P as it rejects singletons. Topologically bypass these uses and discard P's OR root. This again yields the forbidden 21-gate seven-AND minor.

Every raw therefore has one or two OR readers. Apply sole-OR relocation from verification/threshold-twelve/raw-normalization.md; each intermediate minimum circuit retains the gate counts and is subject to the same exclusions, so ultimately every raw has two OR readers. The general OR-tight theorem excludes such a representative with sixteen ORs. This proves the ten-input claim.

For the thirteen-input 31-gate eight-AND candidate triple-ports.md proves at most six pure groups and at most one quartet; all others are triples. The final collector has t≤r+1=p−2 clean terminals because r=p−3. If no triple branches, at least p−1 unshared triples would each feed a distinct clean terminal, impossible. Delete the three raws of a branching triple U. The dark-edge count charges six raw incidences plus the branching fanout surplus. At most seven gates can disappear by the 24-gate ten-input bound; hence U has fanout two and no one-sided dark AND is possible. A first dark AND would need two dark operands. Before it any dark wire is a disjunction of a subset of U's three raws. Private internal ORs of U have no external readers; a different group cannot contain two of the raws, and combining U with one of its own raws is redundant. Thus U is the only available nonraw dark operand. No raw feeds an AND. Hence all seven deletions are ORs and the remaining size-24 ten-input circuit has eight ANDs, excluded above.
