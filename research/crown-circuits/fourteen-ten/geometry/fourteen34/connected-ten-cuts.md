# The connected ten-flag boundary

A size-34 eight-AND representative permits q−2 ORs in its decoder. If a connected q-flag host contains any connected graph H on h≥2 flags whose edge and edge-union tests require h−1 ORs, then it needs at least q−1 ORs. Keep H and extend a spanning tree of H to a tree spanning the host. Remove the other vertices farthest first. For each discarded vertex w with surviving predecessor a, a has a surviving neighbor b distinct from w, using an edge of H if a is retained. The negative edge ab and positive union of ab with aw differ only at w on the current flags. Clamping w false therefore removes an OR, by the certificate layer lemma. Once all q−h steps finish, the decoder still rejects every edge of H and accepts each union of distinct edges in H. Additional edges among the retained flags cannot weaken those requirements. Thus at least q−h+(h−1) ORs were needed.

For the thirteen connected ten-vertex candidates in degree/t14-34/eight-connected-ten.tsv, connected-ten-cuts.tsv gives explicit injections. Columns are the graph6 host, pattern order h, triangular edge mask, largest excluded OR count, and images of vertices 0,…,h−1. The archived DRAT ledger is degree/t13-seven/graph-cut/initial-drat.tsv. Four masks suffice: 237 (h=5, 3 ORs excluded) and 1214, 1260, 1459 (h=6, 4 ORs excluded). The archived formulas grant arbitrary ANDs. Every candidate therefore requires nine decoder ORs.

Running python -B connected-ten-cuts.py reproduces the embeddings. The images can be checked against the original edge lists independently of the matching implementation.
