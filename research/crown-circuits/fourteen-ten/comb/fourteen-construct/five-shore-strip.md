# Five plus five at eight ORs and sixteen decoder gates

For an eight-AND, 34-gate circuit with ten flags in two five-vertex components, consider the 25 connected graph pairs remaining after removal of the three with a K5 shore. Masks are in the triangular ordering ij at bit j(j−1)/2+i, i<j, minimized under all 120 permutations. The small census five-shore-strip.py checks these masks directly.

Pin a flag x to zero when its remaining four vertices induce at least five edges, hence a diamond or K4. The flag is essential in the original host: select a neighbor with another incident edge; adding x to that edge produces a required positive. Such a neighbor always exists in a connected graph with five vertices unless x is a leaf whose neighbor has degree one, or x is the center of a star. The first is impossible and the star center cannot serve as x because its deletion leaves no edges. Thus a path from x reaches the output. Follow zero through ANDs to the first OR of that path; it must occur because a positive survives. At least one OR vanishes, leaving at most seven ORs and fifteen decoder gates.

Let the other five-vertex shore have m edges and require at least four ORs. Each universal y of the four-vertex induced small graph must spoil an edge on the other shore if m≥7: otherwise pin y true and use its three virtual star edges and all m real edges to compute threshold two with bank 2m−5 and at most fourteen decoder gates. This gives at most 23,25,27 gates for arities 10,11,12 respectively, below their known minima. For m=6 the same construction gives 21 gates but at most 14 ORs; the established nine-input OR-tight secondary bound requires at least eight ANDs at the fourteen-OR minimum, contradiction. For m=5 a retained y of degree four on the original shore must spoil even before x is pinned: all four star edges plus five real edges give a nine-input threshold circuit with at most 5+15=20 gates. The spoiling test itself sets x=0, so this property persists after stripping x.

When two retained universal vertices spoil and the opposite OR floor is four, the four-flag lemma (four-flag-three-lost-ors.md) rules out the pair. All ten seven-plus-seven pairs are excluded: the individual masks 127,223,239,254 each have the four-OR floor and each admits a strip. For six-plus-eight, strip the six side of masks 95 and 119 against either 255 or 507; strip instead the eight side against either 235 or 237. This excludes eight pairs. For five-plus-nine, mask 79 has the four-OR floor and 511 retains two original universal vertices after a suitable strip; this excludes one pair. Floors used here come from a quartic center or an embedding of certified negative mask 237 or 254.

Nineteen of twenty-five pairs are excluded by this test. It leaves {87,94,117,236} paired with 511 and 222 paired with either 255 or 507. It does not assert these six pairs realizable. The result uses the independently established threshold gate minima, OR-tight nine-input bound, and local OR floors.
