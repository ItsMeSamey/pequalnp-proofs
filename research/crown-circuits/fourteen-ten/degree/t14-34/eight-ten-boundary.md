# Ten-flag boundary at 34 gates and eight ANDs

The ten-flag decoder has eight ORs. The archived small graph certificates and the physical remote-component argument in eight-and-four-vertex-remote.md exclude every 4+6 split. For a subcubic six-vertex component the seven embeddings in eight-six-vertex-floors.md prove that it needs five ORs. A six-vertex component with a quartic flag has the same lower bound from the connected degree bound. Only three ORs can therefore be spent by the four-vertex component. This last step uses the physical pure/mixed roles.

In the connected subcubic case, degree sum 28 leaves either two doubletons or one pendant vertex among otherwise cubic vertices. A branching pure flag is required by the private-group first-product obstruction. Deleting that branching flag and its t pendant neighbors saves at least 2+t ORs. A cubic vertex still has all three neighbors, since removing the branching flag reduces the degree of at most three others and any removed pendant neighbor attached only to that flag. If the remaining 9−t vertices formed one connected component, its decoder would require at least (9−t)+3−5=7−t ORs, exceeding the 6−t left. Thus the branching vertex separates at least two nontrivial components, each of order at most seven.

Running eight-connected-ten.py reconstructs every such host by attaching the separator and its pendant neighbors to graphs in the NetworkX graph atlas. It returns 13 pairwise nonisomorphic candidates, twelve with two degree-two vertices and one with a pendant vertex. The generator permits additional branching and all assignments of physical roles.

For two five-vertex components, the connected graph atlas contains 3,5,5,4,2,1,1 graphs with four through ten edges, respectively. The edge totals for a 14-edge host give 28 unordered pairs. Three contain a complete five-vertex component. Erasing the other connected component costs at least four ORs, leaving four, whereas the complete five-vertex decoder needs five. At most 25 unordered pairs survive this test. Neither this count nor the connected count asserts a realizing circuit.
