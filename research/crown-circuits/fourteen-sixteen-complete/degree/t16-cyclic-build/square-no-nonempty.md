# Opposite square vertices cannot form their nonemptiness OR

Name the four cycle vertices A,B,C,D cyclically. With A fixed false the remaining actual edges form a two-edge path, whose middle vertex is rebuilt as the OR of independent endpoint raws. Together with the three other path banks this costs four ORs. The restricted decoder then represents threshold two on nine independent raws. The initial count is 24 gates and eight ANDs, so at most two gates can disappear: three losses reach size 21 with at least five ANDs, contradicting its four-AND composition. This applies to each square vertex. In particular a vertex with an AND reader has no other reader, since a first downstream OR also disappears; that OR cannot read the original vertex as the other operand by absorption.

Suppose N=A∨C is present. Each of A and C must have a second reader since adding either to a singleton containing its opposite vertex can change the answer (BC versus BC+AB for A). Those second readers are ORs, and they are distinct since repeating A∨C is redundant.

Pin B false, set A=x and C=y, and use N=x∨y to supply D without the otherwise required new OR. At least one B reader also disappears, leaving an exact 22-gate threshold-nine circuit with fourteen ORs and eight ANDs. B cannot have two readers or an AND reader: both would remove at least two proper decoder gates in addition to saving the D bank. The first OR downstream of a B AND is distinct from N since N has only raw operands. Thus B has a sole OR reader E. By symmetry D has a sole OR reader G. Neither E nor G can read A or C: adding a singly read vertex to a singleton containing the other operand and its other neighbor must be detected even if that operand was already high. For example if E reads A, DA versus DA+AB is invisible. A common E=G would compute square nonemptiness just like N and be redundant in the original decoder. Neither E nor G can have N as its other operand, since N absorbs every square vertex. Hence in the minor the physical OR U=x∨y has both an original N reader and the distinct surviving reader G. Furthermore each of x and y has exactly one other OR reader, and these external readers are distinct.

For completeness the local cherry argument does not require any degree assumption on the remaining raws. In an OR-optimal threshold-nine circuit that minimizes ANDs, consider a live pure cherry U=x∨y under those reader hypotheses. Each path from U to output has a first proper OR after U, since threshold is not bounded by U. None can also be the external reader x∨P, where the AND-only path implies P≤U: after pinning y=0 and bypassing U and y's external OR, P≤x and this extra OR also disappears, whereas the eight-input OR floor allows only two losses. Similarly for y. Pinning both x,y false now removes U, both external readers, and every first proper OR on such paths. The seven-input OR floor permits at most four losses, so there is only one first OR F. A direct U input into F accompanied by any second path makes F redundant by absorption. Otherwise multiple immediate users of U are ANDs and all routes meet F. Replace U by true in their AND-only cone, removing at least two ANDs; the old value of each affected operand was U AND its new value. Restore a single U factor on the affected input of F, or after F if both inputs were affected. This preserves semantics with fewer ANDs and no extra OR. The fourteen-OR/eight-AND bounds at nine inputs give secondary optimality, contradicting the multiple readers obtained above.
