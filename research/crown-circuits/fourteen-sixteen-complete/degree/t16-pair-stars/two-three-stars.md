# Two disjoint three-stars require six decoder ORs

Let U,V be the centers of two disjoint three-stars. A monotone decoder rejects each of the six individual edges and accepts each union of distinct edges. Assume at most five ORs, and minimize total size subject to that cap and the required values. The singleton and pair tests imply by monotonicity the full threshold-two function on unions of edges. When a flag remains essential and a positive survives at zero, pinning it false saves at least one OR: an AND-only path from that flag to output would extinguish every surviving positive, so a first OR can be bypassed.

Each center U spoils every opposite edge e: U together with e already accepts. Erase the two other opposite leaves successively. Each saves an OR, witnessed by comparing e to its union with that leaf's edge. If U did not spoil e, fix U true, retain its three own neighbors and identify the two flags of e as one independent input. Every singleton rejects and every pair accepts. Four-input threshold two requires four ORs, but only three remain.

No leaf x spoils an opposite edge. Erase its two own siblings successively (the edge of x and the additional sibling distinguish each), saving two ORs. If x spoils an opposite edge, erasing x now saves a third OR while an opposite positive remains. The center U alone now acts as an independent raw alongside the opposite star. It rejects alone and accepts with each opposite edge. This enlarged task needs at least three ORs: the opposite star needs two by clamping its center true, and the independent raw is essential and costs at least one to erase. Only two remain.

Every leaf has only AND readers. Otherwise pin such a leaf x true, saving its direct OR. Then erase both of its siblings successively: with U true, x alone is a negative edge and adding either sibling gives a positive. Now U again acts as an independent extra raw for the opposite star. Its singleton is the negative edge U+x; an opposite singleton rejects by the preceding nonspoiling property for x. Every pair accepts. Three ORs are needed while only two remain.

For a direct leaf reader x∧P with nonleaf partner P (including raw centers), P is false at its own center-alone assignment U. Otherwise monotonicity implies P≥U everywhere and every valid union of raw edges has x≤U. The reader can be replaced by x on every required test, contradicting minimality. Duplicate inputs are also redundant.

Introduce six independent tokens, one per unordered pair of leaves in the same star. Replace each direct AND of two same-star leaves by their pair token, reusing the token across repeat occurrences. At all other gates replace each raw leaf operand by its center. Cross-leaf products thus become U∧V. These modifications cost no ORs.

When all tokens are false, at each center-alone assignment the new and old outputs coincide. Same-star pair products are zero, cross products are zero, and for x∧P with its center present the partner is false on that base by induction and the preceding paragraph. Both center-alone outputs reject. On U plus one token belonging to U, the modified circuit dominates the original positive on U and the corresponding two leaves. The same holds for V. On U+V with no tokens, the modified circuit dominates the original positive on any cross-star pair: no original same-star leaf pair was present. All comparisons propagate in topological order by monotonicity.

Erase the six tokens one at a time. Each remains essential via its own center-alone negative and the variant with that single token true, despite previous erasures. U+V with all tokens false remains a positive. Every erasure thus saves a distinct OR, exceeding five. Six suffice: form the three ORs of a matching of opposite leaves, compute threshold two on those three results with two more ORs and free ANDs, then take the OR with U∧V. The independent control in check.py verified all 64 raw assignments and every CNF clause of the six-OR model; no five-OR solver verdict is used.

In the q=16 all-star residual, keep two three-stars as a base with no universal singleton flag. The other three-star and two-star cost their respective orders four and three: peel each to a single edge, identify its endpoints as an independent extra raw and apply the two-OR independent-raw increment. This requires 6+4+3=13 ORs against the twelve available.
