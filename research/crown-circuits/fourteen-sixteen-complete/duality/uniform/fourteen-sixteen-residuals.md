# Three possible interfaces after a branch on sixteen flags

Use the local OR floors for two triangles, triangle plus square, two squares, triangle plus P4, square plus P4 and two P4s, each at least the combined order minus two. Peeling a path endpoint extends each path bound to P5. Thus any two of {C3,C4,P4,P5}, including repeats, form a strong base with no universal singleton flag. A three-star together with any of these nonstars also has the combined order minus two floor by the full-order nonstar transfer.

At q=16 or 17 no quartic flag is possible. A quartic must lie in a bare four-star component, and its ten remote edges must form a star forest of maximum degree four, by the full-order nonstar transfer. This requires at least three remote components with at least thirteen vertices, hence q≥18.

At q=16 a branching pure flag of degree d≤3 with t adjacent leaves leaves N=15−t flags, 14−d edges and N−3 ORs. All residual components are K2, P3, K1,3, P4, P5, C3 or C4 by the existing local tests. At most one of the four nonstars occurs, and when one occurs all other components are K2 or P3. There are exactly 1+d−t trees, by counting vertices minus edges.

If a nonstar path occurs, the total is at most 5+3(d−t)≤14−3t, below N. If a cycle occurs, at most 4+3(1+d−t) vertices are present, forcing d=3,t=0. The only arrangements totaling fifteen are C3 with four P3s, and C4 with three P3s and a K2. If all components are stars then 15−t≤4(1+d−t), again requiring d=3,t=0. Four stars totaling fifteen flags have orders 4,4,4,3. The third interface therefore comprises three K1,3 stars and a P3. These necessary shapes cover connected and disconnected original hosts. Their physical realization remains open.
