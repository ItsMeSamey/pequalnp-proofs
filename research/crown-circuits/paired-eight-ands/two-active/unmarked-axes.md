# An unused-axis bound for eight ANDs with one dirty comparison

Assume the seven-AND clean and one-dirty exclusions, a twelve-label paired bank with at most seventeen binary unions, and no direct receiver. Five unions remain as helpers after charging the twelve mutually incomparable receivers. Let T be the dirty continuation. A terminal axis is unmarked when its two literals occur neither in T nor in any helper mask.

There is at most one unmarked terminal axis. To see this, take two unmarked axes u,v. Restricting the direction cube to u XOR v = b identifies two clean terminal axes. The resulting seven-axis physical bank has the same or smaller union cost. If every deficit intersects a particular parity plane, the seven-axis bank is valid and contradicts its lower bound. Thus each parity has a label whose whole original nonempty deficit lies in the opposite plane.

For an unmarked pair, each such label is the last union of one literal from u and one from v. Indeed a receiver formed without a literal from either omitted axis has its event invariant under flipping that axis, as the dirty continuation and every helper are independent of it. These two labels occupy unequal-parity quartercubes. They agree in precisely one signed coordinate, so their union is half of one axis, say u. Each of the ten remaining disjoint deficits lies on the opposite half of u. Every such receiver consequently has the fixing u literal as a direct operand of its last union. Its other operand is a helper or a primitive branch. There are at most five helpers. On that slice, each nonempty primitive-branch deficit occupies at least half the slice, including a dirty branch, which is reachable only for one choice of the dirty selector. Thus at most one of the ten receivers can use a primitive: two disjoint such deficits already cover the slice. A repeated helper gives the same deficit and cannot be chosen twice. At most six receivers can fit, a contradiction.
