# Private OR trees and straddling wires

In a globally size-minimal threshold circuit, minimize the number of singleton-positive AND gates subject to size, and collect all singleton-negative gates terminally. Consider a dirty gate P∧Q. Expand P along private ORs up to raw inputs, AND outputs and shared OR outputs of fanout at least two. This is a private OR tree over its boundary signals; repeated leaves may be omitted when rebuilding it.

If every boundary signal has singleton support either contained in the singleton support of Q or disjoint from it, group the two types as H and L. Both groups are nonempty: dirtiness implies a common true singleton, and singleton support containment S(P)⊆S(Q) would let P replace the AND, saving a gate with no change on singletons. Replace the dirty gate at every use by OR(H) and append the clean terminal OR(L)∧Q to the final OR. On all singleton inputs the replacement has exactly the old truth value. On a larger input, if the old gate was true but OR(H) became false, OR(L)∧Q is true; in every other case, monotonicity preserves all old positive outputs. The two private OR groups use one fewer gate than the old single OR tree (or more if some leaf repeated), and the old dirty gate pays for the appended clean AND. The one saved OR pays for collecting the new terminal. This contradicts dirty minimality.

Thus one boundary wire of P has a singleton both in and outside Q, and vice versa. The two witnesses are distinct, since the former has a singleton outside Q and every latter singleton belongs to Q. At the chronologically earliest dirty gate neither can be an earlier dirty AND, and a raw wire cannot straddle. They must therefore be two distinct shared OR outputs.

Use formal OR-only reachability masks on the physical AND-operand pins. The first straddler reaches its own earliest pin but not the opposite pin, because reaching the latter would propagate its exclusive singleton to Q. Every raw singleton activating a pure-OR straddler has an OR-only path into it and reaches every pin of its mask V. Therefore V is contained in a hot raw's formal mask and in an exclusive raw's formal mask. If all of this shared gate's paths reached only the same pin, an outgoing OR incidence could be bypassed while preserving that pin's truth function: any other outgoing edge already carries the same wire to that pin, and no OR-only path can reach the cold output directly from this hot wire. A direct wire into that pin cannot coexist with an alternative route back to it because the pin has a unique input wire. Thus V contains at least two pins and costs a union in the transposed bank. It cannot equal any raw receiver since two distinct raw masks include it and raw masks are an antichain. The opposite straddler likewise yields its own positively charged mask with the opposite earliest sign.

This proves two distinct helper requirements *within the earliest gate*. A later dirty gate may reuse an earlier witness or use an earlier AND output. There is no assertion of a separate pair of new helpers per gate. Both the private-tree argument and the nonraw positive cost were independently reviewed by the duality desk.
