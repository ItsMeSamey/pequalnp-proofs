# Elimination of direct receivers in a paired threshold search

Suppose a paired 12-input threshold-two circuit uses at most 28 gates and at most a ANDs and has a direct raw receiver. The direct specialization in ../../algebra/reverse/eleven-threshold-and-direct.md gives an 11-input threshold-two circuit with at most 25 gates and at most a−1 ANDs. Do not use the separate 11-input lower bound in that document. Fixing the removed variable to zero preserves the values of surviving gates on remaining singleton inputs. Bypassing bottom or top signals likewise preserves the singleton values of surviving gates. Normalize clean terminals without increasing size, AND count or dirty count.

The transposed circuit has at most 25−11+1=15 binary unions. At most one raw receiver can be direct. Its rejection event contains the selector halfcube choosing away from its branch. Two of these halfcubes could be disjoint only if they are opposite halves of the same axis. That would leave no point for any third nonempty rejection event.

Choose the direct receiver m if there is one, and otherwise any receiver. Introduce a fresh clean terminal comparison with opposite literals x,x'. Replace m by m∪{x} and m∪{x'}, keeping its old computation available. This costs at most two reverse unions. The ten old unsplit rejection events extend through the entire fresh selector axis. The new events are the two opposite halves of the old m event along that axis. All twelve are disjoint and nonempty. The two new receivers genuinely depend on the new coordinate, since m has rejecting points, and neither is merely a new branch, since m also has accepting points. The old unsplit observers cannot equal a new branch. Thus there is no direct receiver, even allowing semantic equality of masks.

In forward form split z into z₁,z₂, connect z₁∨z₂ to the old input and add z₁∧z₂ as a new output disjunct. This costs three gates, including one clean AND. The split singletons have the old z singleton values at all old gates. The reconstructed circuit is within the original size and AND limits and has no additional dirty AND. Smaller AND counts are represented by unused clean axes in the packing relaxation. Consequently exclusion of all nondirect paired systems at bounded AND and dirty counts suffices for unrestricted paired systems at those bounds.
