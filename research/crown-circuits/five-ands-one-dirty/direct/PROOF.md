# One dirty AND, four terminal ANDs, and a free receiver

Consider the 12-crown on one-hot row and column inputs. This certificate excludes normalized, size-at-most-40 circuits with one dirty prefix AND, four terminal ANDs, and a raw receiver equal to a free switch source. It also excludes every such circuit with at least three irreducibly unequal row/column receiver pairs, whether or not any receiver is free. A separate check is needed for twelve paired, nonfree receivers.

## Resolution and union budget

Resolve each AND independently to one of its inputs. A monotone circuit output is false precisely if some resolution is false: on a false assignment choose a false parent at every false AND simultaneously. There are four independent terminal directions and one direction at the dirty gate. The dirty gate reaches at most one pin per terminal AND, since otherwise a diagonal on which it is true would also trigger that terminal AND. Its nonempty activation U is therefore the union of one oriented half on each of t distinct terminal axes, with 1 ≤ t ≤ 4. The ten source masks are the eight ordinary oriented terminal halves and the two guarded dirty halves U∩{y=0}, U∩{y=1}. A resolution point covered by either active physical raw receiver accepts. Each diagonal supplies a resolution outside both receiver masks, and two different labels cannot use the same such point.

Expanding all ordinary OR-only pin expressions, then reversing their incidence DAG, gives a union circuit for the raw masks and the dirty-output incidence mask. If q ORs occur in the OR-only portion preceding the final output OR tree, it has 25 sources (24 raw plus one dirty output) and ten named pins. Summing outdegree minus one gives q+10−25 reverse joins. The forward gate count is q+5+3, so a size-40 circuit yields at most 17 joins. Each source is live. In particular omitting a raw row would cause an accepted offdiagonal to depend only on its column and force the associated diagonal positive. Dropping the dirty-output demand gives a relaxation.

Enlarging a comparable row/column pair at one label to its larger existing mask on both shores preserves diagonal rejection and all offdiagonal acceptances. Masks of different labels cannot be comparable, since each rejects its own representative point and covers every other label's point. After equalization, d unequal labels supply 12+d distinct raw masks forming an antichain. At most one is a free source: distinct free sources are supported on distinct directional halves or on complementary halves, hence intersect in at most eight of the 32 resolution words, while each must contain at least ten of the twelve distinct diagonal representatives in common with any other raw. Thus at most 6−d paid nonreceiver helpers remain. The receivers are sinks in the pruned union circuit for these targets.

For d≥2, select the row of one unequal label, the column of another, and all paired labels. These 14−d masks pairwise cover every resolution. At d≥3, available helpers h≤6−d≤3. The independent unrestricted helper search in asymmetric.cpp enumerates them in all four activation families, and finds fewer than 8+h pairwise covering outputs. At h=0,1,2 the maxima are 4,5,6 for each activation. For h=3 they are 7,8,8,7 at t=1,2,3,4. This excludes d≥3. If d≤2, one can choose opposite shores on the unequal labels, producing a fully paired family of twelve masks. To preserve a particular free receiver that is the smaller mask in a comparable pair, leave that one pair unequal. All paid receiver masks are still incomparable, so the same helper count and clique exclusion for d≥3 apply. The free input itself may feed its mate without consuming a helper. With at most two unequal labels, choose the named free receiver as the retained shore at its label. Thus the free case covers any original raw receiver equal to a free switch source.

## Restricting around the free receiver

Let X be the free mask in a paired family. The eleven other paired masks have nonempty, pairwise disjoint rejecting regions contained in X. Equivalent resolution words are counted once when none of the free source masks distinguishes them. Remove gates that do not contribute to these eleven masks. Reasoning about whether a source occurs uses this physical union-expression network; a retained receiver cannot contain X as an input source because it rejects somewhere inside X.

* If X is a dirty branch, flip the dirty bit at a rejected point in X. The opposite dirty branch is the only input that can increase, so every receiver must include it. Delete that branch from the network, saving at least one binary join, and discard X. The ordinary four-axis rejecting region of each resulting raw lies in U, since both dirty branches vanish outside U. At any terminal point in U, choosing the original dirty bit corresponding to X shows these ordinary rejecting regions are pairwise disjoint.
* If X is an activating clean half, toggling its axis from X to the other half at a rejected point can only disable dirty branches. The opposite clean literal must therefore appear in every receiver. Delete that opposite source, saving at least one join, and restrict to X. There U is identically true, so the surviving primitive sources are the eight ordinary signed literals of four axes.
* If X is a clean half on an axis unused by U, toggling its axis leaves the guarded dirty branches unchanged. Again every receiver must contain the opposite clean source. On restricting to X there are three ordinary axes and one guarded dirty axis.
* If X is an inactivating clean half, toggling its axis from X to the opposite makes U true. A receiver lacking the opposite source cannot reject at a point of X with U true: the toggle would give the same or fewer positive sources and the point outside X would still be rejected. It therefore rejects only at U=false, where its two dirty orientations are indistinguishable. There are at most 2^(4−t) distinct such points. Pairwise disjointness bounds the number of receivers not using the opposite source by 2^(4−t). At least one of the eleven receivers uses it, so deletion saves at least one join. On X the dirty activation has t−1 active axes.

In the first two cases the eleven outputs, each now a distinct ordinary Q4 rejecting subcube, cost at most sixteen joins. None is a free literal: a half-cube would leave only eight vertices for ten other nonempty rejecting cubes. Thus there are at most five auxiliary joins. The four-axes.cpp checker finds no such 11-packing among 315,792 attainable five-helper banks. All smaller banks can be padded with unused two-literal helpers. Independent enumeration by anchoring a fully specified output likewise rejected all 11,828 anchored families.

For the remaining clean cases let r be the number of active axes after restriction. If r=0 at most eight distinguishable switch words exist, already insufficient. Otherwise the three-axis guarded bank has 16−2^(3−r) distinguishable atoms: inactive words count once and active words count twice. Every free source leaves at most eight atoms outside it. An ordinary half contains at most eight resolution words across its complementary side; a guarded dirty half has exactly eight atoms outside it (one per ordinary assignment). It is therefore impossible for any projected raw to be free, since it would have to cover ten other representatives, yet each source is also supported on at most eight resolution words. One saved join leaves at most five auxiliary joins. For r=1, exhaustive search rejects all five-helper banks.

**Two saved joins.** If the deleted clean source feeds two gates directly, both become redundant. If it has a unique direct gate, its other operand b survives restriction as a nonempty signal: in the cases r≥1 all other primitive sources are nonempty on the restricted cube, and the receiving gate cannot itself occur earlier in b. Every receiver making use of the deleted source must inherit b through this one gate. The rejecting representative of each such receiver lies outside b, which contains a surviving free source; there are therefore at most eight such receivers. For the unused-axis case all eleven must use the deleted source. For the inactivating case with t≥3, at most two can avoid it. In each of these cases a unique read is impossible and at least two joins are saved. There remain at most fifteen joins for eleven nonfree receivers, hence at most four helpers. The remaining inactivating cases t=1,2 have r=0,1 and were already excluded.

## Finite replay

The reduced checker enumerates all semantic nonfree auxiliary masks in increasing population count. A helper must be a union of two currently available masks. An auxiliary missing only one atom cannot feed a different nonuniversal output, so it can be dropped. For each bank any output is a union of two available masks, allowing equal operands. The checker selects all available singleton rejecting regions first: replacing a larger chosen region containing an available singleton by that singleton preserves cardinality and disjointness. It then searches remaining mutually disjoint regions, removing containing duplicates and pruning by the two-atom minimum. For r=2 the five-helper relaxation actually admits an 11-packing; projected-example.json records all five helpers and eleven pairwise covering receiver masks on fourteen atoms. This explains why the two-join bound matters.

Build the native checkers with C++20. Run asymmetric, four-axes, reduced 1 5, then reduced 1 4, reduced 2 4, and reduced 3 4. The first two programs take no arguments.

At four helpers the reduced search reaches 21,633, 35,580, and 38,842 distinct banks for r=1,2,3 respectively. Each offers at most six singleton rejecting regions and none packs eleven outputs. At r=1 the five-helper layer has 138,512 banks and offers at most nine singleton regions, still short of the ten needed.
