# Simultaneously restricting direct receiver coordinates

Suppose k raw observer branches lie on distinct switch coordinates of the full reverse direction network, each in the sign chosen for a common subcube Q. Assume their incoming activations are all nonempty on Q. Suppose a retained receiver M is nonempty and nonfull on Q and its union with each of the k direct masks is the full cube. Then fixing these k directions to Q removes at least k distinct binary union gates in addition to the k fixed switches.

To see that every opposing branch is an actual ancestor of M, expand the union ancestors of M. Keep split branches as signed literals, but replace a pair of siblings by their earlier incoming activation. Process physical nodes in reverse topological order: at a required union expand both operands, and at a switch retain a literal if exactly one branch is required or expand its activation if both are required. Thus every remaining literal is an actual dependency ancestor, and the literals have consistent coordinate signs. A full-source constituent is impossible because M is nonfull. Their union misses the entire nonempty face opposite all the literal signs. That face belongs to Q, since M covers the complement of every chosen direction half. For each fixed coordinate the corresponding opposing literal must therefore occur; if its coordinate were absent the face would have points outside Q, and with the other sign its entire face would be outside Q. Each such branch is identically zero on Q, but its parent activation is nonzero on Q. Fix a successor toward M for every encountered ancestor (all eventually reach M). From each opposing branch follow these successors until first reaching a nonzero restricted mask. The finite graph of traced edges is a forest oriented toward its roots because each interior node has one chosen successor, and the network is acyclic. Each opposing branch is a leaf: it comes from a switch whose incoming mask is not identically zero on Q. No traced zero path could enter it; tracing stops as soon as a nonzero mask occurs. There are precisely k leaves since every path begins at an opposing branch.

Each of the r roots is a union with a zero operand, since splitting an identically zero mask cannot produce a nonzero branch. Every internal merger of two traced paths is also a union, now with zero output while tracing is still in progress. A root cannot merge two traced paths via distinct operands because both operands would vanish. The forest has k−r binary mergers, since all other nonleaf nonroots have one incoming traced edge, and roots have one incoming traced edge. All r terminal unions and all k−r internal mergers simplify. This counts k distinct unions.

When direct observers are on the same shore at k different labels removed for a minor, their k paid opposite-shore twins may first be replaced by the full source, with all retained witness gate values unchanged and every receiver elsewhere only growing. If the hypotheses above still apply to one originally paired retained M (as they do with a retained rejecting witness), the two savings count on disjoint stages, giving at least 2k unions removed. The number of remaining switches is reduced by k. Pairability of the retained labels and the associated threshold lower bound must be supplied separately.
