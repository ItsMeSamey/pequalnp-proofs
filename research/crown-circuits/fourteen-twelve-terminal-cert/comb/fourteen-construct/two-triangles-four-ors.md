# Two disjoint triangles require exactly four ORs

Assume arbitrary binary conjunctions and at most three binary ORs. Each triangle has three negative edges and a positive triple. Every union of one edge from each triangle is positive. A single flag spoils an opposite edge if their joint input is accepted.

If both triangles have a flag which spoils no opposite edge, set both such flags to one. The other four flags are now independent virtual inputs, each selecting a star edge. Every singleton rejects by the no-spoiling condition, zero rejects by monotonicity, and any pair contains two genuine edges and accepts. This gives threshold two on four inputs with three ORs, contrary to its four-OR minimum. Thus some triangle has all three flags spoiled.

Call this triangle R. Classify wires as cold or live by setting R=0, all other flags one. Write k for ORs with one cold operand and m for ORs with both cold. The three spoiled singleton flags show k+m≥3: trace backwards from each exit's cold operand through both children of cold ORs, choosing any one cold child of cold ANDs. Each raw appears as a leaf since a true exit operand on that singleton must retain it. The distinct leaf count is at most k+m. At a three-OR budget equality holds, so every cold OR is present in every trace and no root or branch has a shared occurrence. The proof in k4-four-loss-forest.md applies verbatim to these three singleton flags up to the earliest cold OR H. For completeness its two children have distinct mandatory raw singleton factors a,b and only earlier live factors. Any cold path from a or b to another exit bypassing H could be kept by the AND choices, giving two occurrences of that raw, since H is mandatory. Thus the only route for both into the output passes through H. Setting the other triangle zero and the third flag of R one makes the required result a∧b, which cannot factor monotonically through the disjunction of separately constant-masked a and b. Hence m=0 and k=3, with a distinct raw factor at each exit.

In the R-only projection each raw is essential when the other two are one. Thus the live operand of every exit vanishes on that projection: at the all-ones earlier prefix, set all later flags one, and varying this raw must change the output. The live operand cannot see that raw or any later one. Each of the three ORs is consequently a birth between an operand true only on the first pure-component restriction and another true only on the second. Each operand of the last OR has an obligatory raw of its own component: follow its AND gates to a unilateral operand, which exists since an AND of bilateral operands would be bilateral. No previous OR produces a unilateral signal. Omit these two obligatory raws by selecting the opposite edge in each triangle. Both inputs to the last OR are false. Its live path to output has only AND gates after it, contradicting the required positive.

The matching upper decoder on triangles abc and def is abc ∨ def ∨ ((a∨b)∧(d∨e)). Each two-vertex cover hits every edge of its triangle.
