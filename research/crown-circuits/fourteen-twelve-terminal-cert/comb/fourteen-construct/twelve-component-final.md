# All 263 residual marks in the connected twelve-flag census

The source ledger is geometry/fourteen34/connected-twelve-subset-stars.tsv, containing 263 surviving marks on 206 graph6 hosts. Its interface fixes a branching flag to zero, leaving at most eight decoder ORs on connected edge-bearing components of orders 3+8, 4+7, 5+6, 3+3+5, or 3+4+4. The marked surviving orders sum to eleven in all cases. The result here uses only those resulting component shapes and the OR cap.

**Removing a remote component.** Let the other component or components have a fixed flag bank with decoder OR minimum q for threshold two on their edge labels. Assume there are at least two such labels and no flag is true on all single edge inputs. For a connected remote component of order s≥2, keep an anchor edge and zero its other s−2 vertices in decreasing distance to that edge. At each step x has a retained neighbor a with a different retained neighbor b (take the next step toward or across the anchor). The edge ab versus its union with ax witnesses that x is essential; some positive stays on at x=0, so an OR vanishes. The resulting two anchor flags can both be set equal to one independent raw representing their one edge. Each singleton and pair of old or new labels then gives exactly the original required values, and monotonicity determines threshold two on every assignment. The independent-raw increment gives at least q+2 ORs on the remaining interface. Thus the full decoder needs at least s+q ORs. This argument does not assert that s physical ORs vanish on zeroing the entire component; it is a lower bound on the original OR count obtained by restriction to an enlarged interface. The same reasoning peels multiple components to different independent raws and then applies the increment once per raw.

For orders s+t=11 with t≥6, retain the larger component. Its minimum is at least t−2. A cubic vertex gives the connected star bound, and maximum degree two leaves a path or cycle of length at least six, with the P6 four-OR floor and peeling the other vertices. In the cycle, after deleting one vertex the residual path lower alone may give t−2: use the C6 floor for t=6, and for t>6 delete one vertex, retaining a path of order t−1, which needs t−3. No single flag can cover all edges of a connected graph with at least six vertices and maximum degree three, since such a graph has at least five edges. The joint bound is at least s+t−2=9. This excludes 52 marks of type 3+8, 157 of type 4+7, and 40 of type 5+6.

For 3+3+5 leave the five-vertex component. Each of the eight rows has a cubic vertex there; the connected star bound gives at least three ORs. No flag hits all its five edges. Peeling each three-vertex component to an independent edge costs at least one OR each, and the two new independent raws cost at least four more in total. The resulting lower bound is nine.

For 3+4+4, each of the six rows has a triangle plus two four-edge components, each a four-cycle or a paw. Leave the triangle and one of these four-vertex components as the base. It needs at least five ORs by triangle-and-square.md when it contains a square. When it contains a paw, its local function needs at least two ORs: the triangle is a forced minimal positive term, and the two positives consisting of the pendant with each triangle edge at its universal vertex require at least one more term. If there were only two terms, the second would have to cover both of those latter positives and hence be contained in the negative pendant edge (their intersection). Thus it needs at least three distinct minimal terms. No paw flag covers all four edges, so the joint lower bound with the triangle is at least 3+2=5 by the remote-component bound. The disjoint base has no flag true on all its singleton labels. The remaining four-vertex component therefore contributes four to the joint lower bound, again giving nine.

All five cases exceed the eight available ORs. This covers the specified ledger. The upstream classification producing the ledger, the regular physical branching premises, and the connected local OR lower bounds remain prerequisites for the result on all connected twelve-flag hosts.
