# A census-free closure of connected twelve-flag eight-AND hosts

The regular connected twelve-flag host has fourteen raw edges and ten decoder ORs. Use the established branching/separator reduction in the opening paragraphs of geometry/fourteen34/connected-twelve-separators.md. It supplies a branching flag w of degree at most three in the subcubic host. Erasing w and its t adjacent leaves removes at least 2+t distinct ORs and leaves at least two nontrivial components on n=11−t flags. There are no isolates left. The residual decoder has at most n−3 ORs and the remaining edge count is 14−degree(w).

If any residual component H has h vertices, local OR floor at least h−2, and no input flag true on every one of its edge singletons, we are done. Indeed peel each other component of order s to one anchor edge at cost s−2 ORs, then identify the two endpoints with one independent raw label. The fixed-bank increment in independent-raw-or-increment.md costs two additional ORs per raw. The initial OR lower bound is therefore (n−h)+(h−2)=n−2. See twelve-component-final.md for the precise peeling test.

Every connected component of order h≥6 meets these conditions. It has at least h−1 edges, exceeding maximum degree three. A cubic vertex gives the connected star bound h−2. A maximum-degree-two component is a cycle or path. Peel down to an induced six-vertex path (or use a spanning path when it is the six-cycle); the six-vertex path needs four ORs, giving h−2.

For exactly two components, t≤1, so n≥10. Only 5+5 avoids an order-six component. In that case w is cubic and has one adjacent leaf, leaving eleven edges in total. One five-vertex component has at least six edges and therefore a cubic. It has floor three and cannot have a flag covering all edges.

For three components w is cubic and t=0, so n=11 and eleven edges remain. A component of order at least six has been treated. If the largest component has order five, the split is 2+4+5 or 3+3+5. The five-vertex component has at least five edges: with at most four the second split would have at most ten, while the first would require a K4 on four vertices. A K4 inside the original connected subcubic graph cannot attach to w. A five-vertex component with at least five edges either has a cubic or is C5. The five consecutive length-two paths on C5 need five distinct minimal true sets, since any two of their supports intersect only within a rejected edge or singleton. Two ORs yield at most four monomials. Thus the floor is at least three, and no flag covers all five edges.

When the largest order is four the split must be 3+4+4. Every four-vertex component has at most five edges by the K4 attachment observation. A five-edge component is the diamond, with a cubic giving floor two, and no flag covering all its edges. Otherwise eleven edges force a triangle and two four-edge components, each a square or a paw. Triangle plus square needs at least five ORs by triangle-and-square.md. Triangle plus paw also needs five: the paw alone has at least three minimal true sets (its triangle is forced, and the positives from its pendant edge and either other edge of its universal vertex intersect precisely in the negative pendant edge). This needs at least two ORs; peeling the triangle to an independent raw raises the bound by 1+2. No paw flag covers all four of its edges. Leave the seven-vertex joint base and charge four more ORs for the last four-vertex component. This requires nine, above the eight remaining.

These arguments exclude all residual allocations, without host enumeration, under the physical branching and separator premises and the stated OR floors.
