# Connected twelve-flag separator reductions

The physical host has fourteen edges, ten decoder ORs and eight decoder ANDs. In a connected host the maximum degree is three. If n₁ is its number of leaves, it has 4+n₁ cubic vertices by the degree sum. Pick any branching pure flag w. It has two OR readers. Its t adjacent mixed leaf flags each pay their dedicated injector distinct from the two readers by absorption.

Delete w and these leaves. If the remainder is connected and a cubic vertex outside the closed neighborhood of w survives, the remaining 11−t flags need at least 9−t ORs by star peeling; only 8−t remain. Otherwise every cubic lies in that closed neighborhood, so n₁=0, w is cubic and its three neighbors are the other cubic vertices. The connected remainder is a cycle on eleven vertices, needing nine ORs by cycle-or-floor.md. Thus deletion leaves at least two nontrivial components.

If there are exactly two components and one is a triangle, call the other L and write h=|V(L)|. Then 12=1+t+3+h and e=|E(L)|=14−deg(w)−3 is 8 or 9. The residual decoder has at most h ORs and h+8 total gates. Each triangle flag spoils an individual edge on L. Otherwise fix that flag true, losing at least one gate: it is essential even with L empty by distinguishing the opposite triangle edge from its union with an incident edge. Use the two adjacent flags as two independent new inputs and reconstruct the L flags from e original inputs using 2e−h ORs. The new circuit computes threshold two: triangle singles correspond to single edges, large singles retain their negative value by harmlessness, and a mixed pair contains the union of distinct edges. It uses at most 2e+7 gates, contradicting C10=24 or C11=27. Erasing the three triangle flags therefore costs at least three ORs by the single-flag spoiling argument. L is connected with h≤8 and e≥8, so either contains a cubic vertex or is a cycle of length at least five. It needs h−2 ORs. Together they exceed h.

If exactly two components remain and one is K2, each K2 flag either pays a mixed injector or is a private clean doubleton. It cannot branch, since its own deletion would leave at most one nontrivial component. The two flags cannot be adjacent pure doubletons. Neither injector is a w reader: an adjacent flag shares a raw with w and would absorb it, and at distance two along w−a−z the raw wa alone would conceal addition of az at such an injector. The clean collector is distinct from w readers. After the two K2 charges, the two w readers and t mixed leaves, only h−3 ORs remain for the h=9−t flags of L. The latter has 14−deg(w)−1 ≥10 edges, hence a cubic vertex and the h−2 lower bound. This also contradicts the decoder budget.

For three components, the branch is cubic with one attachment per component, and t=0. The preceding K2 payment works for any number of K2 components, since each endpoint still leaves at most one nontrivial component when erased and the mixed injectors are distinct across flags. If the cut leaves two K2 components, the surviving seven-vertex nine-edge component needs five ORs; only four remain. If the cut leaves K2, triangle and L, the latter has six vertices and seven edges. After w and K2 the budget is six ORs and fourteen gates. A harmless triangle flag would give T9 with at most 21 gates, of which at most fourteen are ORs. The fourteen-OR lower bound and its secondary minimum of eight ANDs exclude this. Hence the three spoilers and the four ORs on L require at least seven. In these three-piece cuts a K2 contains at most one pure flag, the attachment endpoint, and that endpoint is private clean. If two K2 components both have private endpoints, their distinct private collector payments follow the clean-terminal disjoint accounting. In fact such a pure endpoint would read into an AND, and its other raw is the edge to w.
