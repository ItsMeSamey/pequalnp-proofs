# Excluding seven ANDs in a size-24 ten-input threshold-two circuit

Assume a binary monotone circuit computes T2(10) in 24 gates with seven ANDs. This is a total-size minimum. By degree/t13-seven/ten-minor.md take a regular clean-terminal representative, with two OR readers for each raw and both exclusive singleton shores of every AND having size at least two. Its simple flattened raw-incidence graph has ten edges. Each vertex denotes a maximal OR group bearing a raw operand. If its order is v, preparing the bank at those vertices costs 20−v ORs, leaving 17−(20−v)=v−3 decoder ORs. A degree-one vertex is an actual mixed OR group, not a free singleton bank: it requires at least one decoder OR with a raw operand. Different vertices require distinct gates.

The graph is a forest of stars by degree/t16-cyclic-build/ten-forest.md. By degree/t13-seven/ten-doubletons.md some pure group contains at least three raws. A d-star alone needs at least 2d−4 ORs by pinning its center true. Each remote component except the first costs its full order because two separate components have no universal singleton flag. The first remote costs at least its order minus one, by peeling to its final edge and erasing the resulting essential independent raw. Thus d≥4 would need at least v+d−6≥v−2 if a remote exists, or 2d−4>(d+1)−3 otherwise. Nor can there be two three-stars: their joint six-OR lower (degree/t16-pair-stars/two-three-stars.md) plus full-order remotes gives v−2. Therefore the graph consists of exactly one three-star, p short paths P3 and s independent edges, with 2p+s=7 and 0≤p≤3.

There are L=3+2p+2s degree-one vertices and v=L+p+1. The decoder budget thus has only p−2 ORs beyond the L mixed ORs already required.

Consider each short-path center. If its group is mixed it costs its own decoder OR beyond the bank, distinct from the L groups. If it is a pure doubleton and branches, both exits are ORs by degree/t13-seven/ten-three-groups.md. Erasing the two short-path raws saves both exit ORs and both distinct leaf-group injection ORs, even when the latter are exposed by reassociation: neither exit can enter its own leaf group since the center would absorb that leaf's raw operand, contradicting size minimality. In the graph decoder the rest still needs at least (v−3)−3 ORs: start with the three-star at cost two, charge its first remote order minus one and all later remotes full order. At least one remote remains since s is odd. The four saved ORs contradict that lower bound.

Thus a pure center is private and feeds an AND. Its center side has exactly two singleton labels. Since both must be exclusive by the two-shore lemma, the AND is clean. A single product can serve at most two centers. A positive on two three-star labels survives with all these products false, leaving a further collector term. Thus each distinct marked product costs one collector OR, separate from every charged mixed group. Counting mixed centers and private centers together requires at least ceil(p/2) further ORs. This exceeds p−2 for all p≤3, including p=0. This contradiction establishes the stated exclusion; no other size or AND composition is assumed excluded.
