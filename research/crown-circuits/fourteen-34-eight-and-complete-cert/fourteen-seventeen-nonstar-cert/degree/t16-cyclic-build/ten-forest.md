# A 24-gate seven-AND ten-input minimum cannot retain two neighboring bank ORs

In a regular clean-terminal monotone threshold-ten minimum of that composition flatten the OR groups as in degree/t13-seven/ten-minor.md. The simple incidence graph on v group vertices has ten raw edges, and its relative decoder has 17−(20−v)=v−3 ORs. By degree/t13-seven/ten-doubletons.md a pure group has degree d≥3.

We use the following more general graph bound. If a connected component of order h containing a degree-d vertex w is not a star, its local OR minimum is at least h−d−1+(2d−4)≥h−2. Erase vertices outside the closed neighborhood of w in descending distance; a parent/grandparent edge versus its union with the child edge distinguishes each erased vertex, with surviving positives. Pin w true, set the d neighbors as independent star-edge labels and obtain threshold two. Since the component is not a star, none of its individual flags covers all its singleton edges. Each remote connected component of s vertices can therefore be added at cost s ORs: peel to one edge, identify its endpoints to form an additional independent raw, then apply comb/fourteen-construct/independent-raw-or-increment.md. Thus v−3 rules out that case. If instead the component of w is the bare star K1,d, any remote connected nonstar H of order h' adds its full h' ORs even to this universal-flag base, by duality/uniform/fourteen-cycle-component-transfer.md. Together they require at least 2d−4+h'≥(d+1+h')−2. Their joint bank has no universal flag, so every further remote component again adds its full order. This contradicts v−3. It follows that the regular ten-input host is a forest of stars.

In particular the physical input cannot retain both bank sites u∨v and v∨w for three distinct raw variables u,v,w. They live in distinct flattened groups, as otherwise the raw v occurs twice in a group and a gate can be saved. The distinct raw incidence edges u,v,w thus share endpoints of v on different sides. No simple star forest has such a configuration.

The same prohibition holds before normalization. In the size-24 seven-AND circuit a raw with an AND reader or at least three readers is excluded by ten-minor.md. The raw u cannot have its displayed bank as sole reader: when v is a singleton, adding u would be invisible; likewise for w. The two banks are already the two readers of v. Sole-OR relocation on other raws preserves both raw-input bank sites, since neither could have such another raw as input. Furthermore relocating a raw X∨P uses a nonraw P and does not change these raw inputs. Terminalizing a buried cold W bypasses its reader W∨P in favor of P, reinstating W at the final OR. It cannot bypass either displayed bank, since both its operands are raw and W is a nonraw cold signal. The banks must remain live by size minimality, as must both physical readers of v and the two readers of u and w whenever such a normalization changes another gate. All substitutions preserve exact OR/AND counts. If terminalization creates a new sole OR reader of some other raw, its new companion would be singleton-cold (else adding that raw is invisible), contrary to terminal normal form: its reader would itself be a buried injection of cold into hot. Thus a terminal-normalized representative has all raws regular since direct ANDs and three readers are already impossible, and the two displayed bank ORs survive.
