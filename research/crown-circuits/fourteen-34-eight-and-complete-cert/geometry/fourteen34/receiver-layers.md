# Receiver graphs at size 34 on fourteen inputs

Assume the regular representative has two OR occurrences per raw label, a simple 14-edge receiver graph on q flags, and a clean terminal collector. The separate OR bank uses 28−q gates. With a ANDs, at most q+6−a ORs remain for the decoder. For a component containing a degree-d vertex and c edge-containing components in the host, the center-layer and remote-anchor accounting from eleven27-seven/receiver-layers.md gives at least q+d−4−c decoder ORs when d≥2. Thus d≤10−a+c. A connected host has d≤11−a. In particular no connected host is possible with ten AND gates, and a connected nine-AND host must be a path or a cycle.

The cycle is impossible. Every pure flag is a doubleton. A branching doubleton has at least two OR readers: erasing its two labels would otherwise save three bank ORs, at least two direct readers and a further OR downstream of any AND reader. An absorbing successor is redundant before restriction. The five-gate reduction limit follows from C(12)=29. Alternatively, two direct readers suffice if both are ORs. Delete a branching flag in the decoder alone. Its two distinct OR readers disappear. The remaining graph is a connected path on q−1 vertices with degree two, requiring at least q−4 ORs. The starting budget q−3 permits only one loss.

Hence all p pure groups on the cycle are private. Their sole AND products are clean: each operand requires two exclusive singleton labels, since resolving an AND after erasing one exceptional raw would save three gates versus the two-gate gap to C(13)=32. Two private doubletons cannot feed the same product: they would be disjoint by cleanliness. Erasing one doubleton saves three bank ORs (only its own degree-two flag vanishes), its AND, a collector OR, and the now unused OR computing the other doubleton. Six savings exceed the five allowed. Consequently the collector contains at least p distinct clean terminals. At least p−1 nonraw OR merges are needed; only (34−9)−28+p=p−3 exist. Contradiction.

In the path case the branching vertices lie at distance at least two from an endpoint: deleting a branching flag adjacent to an endpoint also erases that mixed leaf's separate dedicated OR; its raw is dominated by the branch and cannot share a reader with it. The connected residual of order q−2 still retains degree two and requires q−5 ORs, permitting only two losses instead of three. Adjacent eligible vertices cannot both branch. Their common reader would compute B∨B' for adjacent doubletons. Erasing the other label of B makes B≤B' and removes this extra OR in addition to two raw-pin savings, exceeding the gap to C(13). Thus the four readers are distinct. Deleting the adjacent pair leaves two nontrivial path intervals, with maximum degree two, requiring at least q−6 ORs; only three losses were available. No unrestricted nine-AND exclusion is claimed.

## Number of branching doubletons

More generally suppose all pure groups are doubletons, with nine ANDs. Let b of the p pure groups branch, let u=p−b be private, and let c be the number of clean terminals. A private product's other operand must be shared: erasing both labels of the private group saves its contraction and the two other raw occurrences (bank savings plus any lost mixed injections), the clean product, and a collector OR. A single-use partner becomes dead too, exceeding the five-gate savings cap. Its last gate cannot be a counted OR contraction because its singleton support is disjoint from the private doubleton. A partner that is itself an AND can be removed separately as well. Distinct private products have distinct partners by factoring their terminal disjunction, and none of those partners can be a branching pure doubleton (its two readers must both be ORs). Thus b+u≤7, the available nonraw fanout surplus. Since c≥u and c≤p−2, b≥2. At least two labels are outside all private groups, so their pair requires a further clean terminal. Hence c≥u+1 and b≥3.

The minimum total exclusive-shore incidence at the nine ANDs is 54 by the signed singleton cover. Beyond 28 raw occurrences and the 2b credits from branching doubletons, at most 7−b surplus units remain. Each contributes at most ten positive singleton labels, by the shared-support restriction C(m)≤34−2(14−m)−2 at fanout at least two, together with the exact sizes at 11,12,13 and redundancy of the full OR. Each of the 9−c dirty ANDs debits at least one. Consequently the mass is at most 28+2b+10(7−b)−(9−c), with c≤5. If b≥5 the only possibility is equality at b=5, c=5 and p=7. The two private groups force distinct shared partners. Both must therefore be ORs, positive on ten singleton labels. All four dirty gates have singleton support one.

There are exactly fourteen mixed raw occurrences. The ten branch exits each require a mixed OR group, and the four singleton-dirty outputs each require a mixed OR before a next AND, whose two operands require two exclusive singleton labels apiece. The collector consumes every raw-free OR merger, so each mixed group contains only one nonraw term. Those fourteen groups therefore use one raw occurrence each, with a single source of singleton support at most two. No such group can supply a shared OR of support ten. This proves b≤4.
