# Large receiver degrees with eight AND gates

The regular fourteen-input size-34 eight-AND model has 26 ORs, 28 raw occurrences and decoder budget q−2 on q receiver flags. At the degree-d center, remove mixed-only remote components at full cost using their dedicated binary OR injections. The remaining layer argument requires at least d−3 pure-bearing detached components, each using at least two edges. Thus 14≥d+2(d−3), so d≤6.

At degree six the remote pure graph needs slack k+h−[h>0]≥3, where k counts its edge-containing components and h its isolated flags. Among at least three pure-bearing components of at most eight edges total, 2+2+2, 2+2+3 and 2+3+3 fail: a two-edge component contributes only an isolate, and every three-edge component has at most one component induced by its nonleaves. The only survivors are 2+2+4 and 2+2+2+2. Both force a bare six-edge center. In the former the four-edge component needs at least two separated pure components, possible only for a five-vertex path or four-cycle, in either case with exactly two nonadjacent pure flags. Thus at most four remote flags and one central flag are pure. The general star inequality at a mixed or pure center gives d+max(2,d_p)≤p+2 because the unused nonraw OR merger count is r=p−2. Therefore p≥6, contradiction.

Now suppose d=5 and there are exactly three components. The bound q+d−4−3=q−2 is tight. Both detached components must cost at most s−1 decoder ORs at order s. In particular a branching doubleton or triple in either remote component must separate at least two nontrivial pieces upon deleting it and its pendant neighbors: the two direct OR readers and the pendant mixed injectors are distinct, and peeling the remaining pieces at order minus one each would otherwise exceed s−1. The degree bound on small branching groups comes from degree/t14-34/eight-and-overlapping-branches.md.

Consider a remote component with at most four edges. A completely mixed component costs its full order by its separate injectors. A pure flag in a star with mixed leaves also costs its full order after all those leaves have been zeroed: each bypass retains the nonraw side, so the surviving pure flag still has a path, whose vanishing must disable at least one OR. A branching pure triple or quartet cannot occur here: a non-star cubic with only four edges could not leave two distinct edge-containing pieces upon deleting its center. For a branching quartic the only four-edge shape is the star, already handled regardless of its precise reader types. A branching doubleton with two nontrivial pieces requires the five-vertex path with that flag in the middle. The two OR readers of that middle flag cannot enter a mixed group within distance one (its raw is dominated) or two (the singleton raw on the first edge heats both input sites of the second edge). Every vertex of this short path is at distance at most two. Its other pure groups are private doubletons, since there is no second eligible branching position. Consequently the remote mixed injectors and the distinct private clean collector slots account for s−1 losses and setting the middle false removes a further OR reader outside these gates.

The only other possibilities for a private cubic flag are the paw or the star with a subdivided arm. Every remaining doubleton in either graph is private by the two-piece criterion. The paw and both middle-flag cases of the subdivided star are excluded exactly by geometry/fourteen34/remote-fork.md. The exclusive-shore argument, the 29-gate twelve-input minimum and the 27-gate eleven-input minimum used there are independent of the original AND count. In the mixed-middle transfer the decoder budget is now q'−2 and a surviving degree-five center in three components still requires q'−2, so deleting three ORs at the new two-edge detached path still violates the tight bound. If all flags in the tiny component are either mixed or private clean doubletons they cost its full order by the distinct injector and collector charges.

Hence both detached components require at least five edges, and the degree-five center requires at least five, exceeding fourteen. Any degree-five host must therefore have at least four components.

## Four or more components at degree five

Call a remote pure flag exceptional if it branches or has a dirty private product. Every other remote flag is mixed or a private clean flag. The latter products are distinct. Overlapping private operands could be factored; for disjoint private operands erasing a group of size at most three saves 2k−1 bank/external ORs, a collector OR, its product and the other disjoint bank OR, exceeding the size gap. Two disjoint remote private clean groups of size at least four cannot occur except with exactly nine remote edges in two separate four-edge stars and an extra single edge. In that case the center is a bare five-edge star, and at most three flags are nonleaves, contradicting p≥5 at a degree-five center. Thus the clean collector debits are disjoint even if a remote quartet occurs. A private clean product shared with a central group causes no duplicate remote credit.

Erasing simultaneously any remote components devoid of exceptional flags pays their full vertex count by separate mixed injectors and private collector ORs. The collector retains at least one central-only positive pair term. The residual q'−2 decoder on the surviving components still satisfies the layer bound, so at least two detached components must contain exceptionals. These two components jointly contain at least one pure flag of degree at least three, since at most one branching pure doubleton exists (degree/t14-34/eight-and-one-doubleton.md) and a private doubleton is always clean.

Let A be the center component, e its edge count, a its order, s the number of remote vertices and h the number of remote exceptional flags. On erasing all remote flags the preceding accounting pays at least s−h decoder ORs. There are at most nine remote labels, and at least three remote components.

For e=5, A is a bare five-edge star. Each mixed leaf has a unique flag injector. Clamp the center true and use the star slice. The first two leaf controls have at most one prior input, and must reject any other singleton (otherwise adding their own label is invisible); hence they are identically zero and disappear. This conclusion holds before setting the center true by monotonicity. Even for a mixed center, its fixed leaf-slice value is its group OUTPUT fixed true, not merely the raw part: this is sound on all nonempty star inputs as that raw part is already true. One may clamp that group output directly in the decoder. If its own mixed injector is removed this only gives more savings. The four-/five-/higher-neighbor OR floor on the residual uses the nonempty slice; rejection at zero follows from a singleton. In the present case six ORs are needed on five neighbors. Thus the q−2=s+a−2 budget forces h≥4, since a=6 and there are two additional central ORs.

Of any four chosen exceptionals, at most one has degree two and all others have degree at least three. Their degree sum is at least eleven; overlapping edge occurrences are precisely edges joining two chosen flags. If four lie across three or more components there is at most one shared edge, so ten remote labels are needed. Two in each of two components need at least nine labels plus an edge in the third. Thus exceptionals lie in two components with at most one in one of them. Choose three in the larger and one in the second. Their union needs at least eight labels (at most three internal edges) plus an edge in the third component, so all bounds are equalities. One flag is a doubleton, the other three are triples, and the three selected together are mutually adjacent. All edges in their component meet them; hence every additional nonleaf attaches to this single connected pure component. The singleton selected flag has a bare star as its component and is the only possible pure flag there. The extra mixed single edge is discarded. The remote pure slack is at most 2−1=1, below two, a contradiction.

For e=6 the shape is a star with an extended arm or a leaf-to-leaf chord. If present, the far leaf is mixed and pays its distinct injector on the central star restriction. Each noncenter pure flag is a doubleton: unbranched it feeds only its private clean collector product. Consequently no central precollector mixed control can use any such flag except a branching doubleton. With no central branching doubleton the first two mixed neighbor injectors disappear as before (there are at least three mixed pendant neighbors). Together with a−6 far-vertex savings and six residual ORs, the budget still requires h≥4. With at most eight remote edges the preceding four-exceptional count is impossible. With a central branching doubleton there is still one first forced-zero mixed control, since this first injector has at most one variable available. Therefore h≥3. All remote exceptionals now have degree at least three; across at least two components three of them need either nine edges or at least five plus three edges and another edge in the third component, again exceeding eight.

Finally e≥7 leaves at most seven remote edges across at least three components. With only two pure-bearing components, other components are all mixed and may be erased first while preserving pure paths. The required pure high component has at most four edges and all its nonleaves form a clique. The second pure-bearing component has at most three edges and at most one pure component (if it has four instead, the first must be a three-edge cubic star). One pure component is isolated: either a forced P3 center or the sole nonleaf of a cubic star. Thus pure slack is at most one. If three components bear pure flags, the only option containing the required high pure is 3+2+2. The bare cubic star is that high component, and at least one P3 center must be the unique branching doubleton. The other P3 center is private clean. Erase its two raw labels, saving five gates: its bank OR, two mixed injectors, its product AND and a collector OR. C(12)=29 forces its two remaining detached pure centers still to have output paths. The graph has three fewer flags and lost three decoder ORs beside the bank OR, so q'−2 survives. Erasing the remaining remote mixed groups retains the pure paths; both detached pure flags are now isolated. Their slack of one is insufficient for the degree-five center.

Thus a size-34 eight-AND candidate has maximum receiver degree at most four.
