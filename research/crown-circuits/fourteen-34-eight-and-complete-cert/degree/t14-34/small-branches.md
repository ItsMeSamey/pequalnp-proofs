# Branching pure groups at fourteen inputs, 34 gates and nine ANDs

Assume the regular clean-terminal representative for a monotone binary circuit computing threshold two on fourteen inputs with nine ANDs and twenty-five ORs. Raw inputs each have two OR readers; maximal raw-bearing OR groups give a simple incidence graph. Two distinct groups share at most one raw. The regular representative is available because a one-raw deletion losing three gates would leave at most 31 gates on thirteen inputs, below the certified minimum 32. This invokes the same two-reader normalization as in degree/t13-31/ten-tight.md.

There is no pure branching group U of size s=2 or 3. Erase its raw inputs. In the dark-edge telescope the losses equal at least 2s raw occurrences plus f−1 for its fanout f plus one for every AND with exactly one dark operand. The residual size minima 29 on twelve inputs and 27 on eleven inputs allow at most 2s+1 losses. Hence f=2 and no one-sided dark AND occurs. Before a first dark AND the only dark nonraw group output is U. A separate pure group with two erased raws contradicts incidence simplicity, and a mixed OR group receiving U cannot contain an erased raw, since the raw is dominated by U. Proper internal nodes of the chosen raw-only U tree have a single user inside that tree. Thus a direct AND reader of U has a live other operand and cannot occur.

Both distinct readers of U are ORs, in separate groups (a duplicate use of U in one group is redundant). Erase the s−1 ORs of U's raw tree and the s separate occurrences of its labels in their other groups. At any external group containing an erased raw, put it last in the group tree to witness one disappearing OR. Such a group cannot read U, by absorption. The two OR consumers therefore disappear in addition. This saves at least 2s+1 ORs. The remaining OR budget is at most 2(14−s)−4, the Wegener floor, requiring at least 13−s ANDs by degree/t13-31/or-optimal.md. Only nine are available.

In the connected case the factored decoder has v−3 ORs, so the connected layer bound v+d−5 forces every vertex degree d≤2. Every pure group is therefore a doubleton. Their necessary branching established separately in the all-doubleton mass argument contradicts this lemma; the same contradiction excludes disconnected all-doubleton candidates.

A branching group U on four raw labels has exactly two OR readers. The size floor 24 on ten inputs allows ten losses, so f≤3. At f=3 there is no allowance for a one-sided AND. The first-dark-AND argument above forces three OR readers, saving at least ten ORs, contrary to the ten-input OR floor sixteen. At f=2, suppose U feeds an AND G. Its other operand is live before the first dark AND: among groups with an input dark solely from the quartet, only U is available. If there were two initial AND readers, the first needs a live other operand, and the second can have a dark other operand only by consuming a descendent of the first. Every dark signal on this strand is bounded by U (it vanishes whenever all four raw inputs vanish). If the second other operand is dark, it is therefore bounded by U on the full cube, making the second AND redundant. Thus two AND readers would cost two one-sided ANDs, exceeding the budget.

With exactly one AND reader G, the other reader OR is live: it contains no erased raw, and no pre-existing other dark nonraw input may enter it except a descendent of G bounded by U, which would be absorbed. The size budget is exhausted by the eight raw occurrences, the surplus at U and G's one-sided debit. In particular no other dark nonraw output branches. All further dark nonraw group outputs outside U's own tree must lie along a single strand after G: any OR on erased raws alone in a separate group would require two erased raws, and an OR with nonraw inputs must have a dark nonraw predecessor to be dark. Every dark AND must also have a dark nonraw predecessor because raws have no AND readers. Consequently no later AND can combine two distinct dark operands without a dark fanout from that strand. A further one-sided AND is impossible by the tight debit. Exactly one dark AND disappears, and the ten gate losses include at least nine ORs. This yields at most sixteen ORs and eight ANDs on ten inputs, contradicting the secondary floor of nine ANDs. This proves the asserted reader types.

Finally, deleting such a two-OR-reader quartet costs at least nine ORs. The ten-input residual needs at least sixteen ORs and, there, at least nine ANDs. Thus exactly nine ORs and no ANDs disappear. The minor has size 25 and is optimal for both gate types at the OR floor.
