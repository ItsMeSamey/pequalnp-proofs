# The five-edge shore paired with K5 minus one edge

Use the ten-flag decoder with eight ORs and sixteen gates. The small shore is connected with five edges and five vertices. Every subcubic such shore needs at least three ORs. For a cubic vertex, erase its nonneighbor, which costs an OR by connected peeling, then clamp the center true to recover threshold two on its three neighbors (at least two ORs). In the remaining case of a 5-cycle, the five successive three-vertex paths are positive assignments and any two intersect in a rejected edge, singleton, or the empty set. They therefore require five distinct minimal true sets. Any circuit with two ORs has at most four minimal true sets: before the first OR all signals are monomials; after it they are monomials multiplied optionally by that same first OR. Resolving its two choices and the second gate's two choices gives at most four monomials altogether.

Erase any flag x of the nine-edge shore. This leaves a diamond or K4 containing two originally universal vertices y. Each y spoils a small-shore edge: otherwise forcing y true supplies four virtual star inputs plus the five opposite edges at bank cost five ORs, while at least one of the sixteen decoder gates is lost. This computes nine-input threshold two in at most twenty gates, below its minimum 21. The spoiling assignment on y and that edge has x false, so remains available after stripping. The archived four-flag-three-lost-ors.md obstruction now requires four more ORs to erase the surviving small diamond/K4. The five-edge shore needs three ORs. Consequently erasing x alone can lose at most one OR.

In particular x cannot be a branching pure group. Follow every outgoing path through ANDs to its first OR. Such a first OR occurs before output on every path, since an AND-only path to output would force all opposite positives false after x is erased. Distinct such ORs count as independent losses upon erasure. If just one OR F occurs and x has a direct OR reader, it must be F. Every other direct reader is an AND, and each of its routes to F must enter through the other operand by an AND-only path. That operand implies x, making F redundant. If all direct readers are ANDs, cut x out of each such product by substituting true for these uses and retain their other operands. Every affected operand of F had x as an AND factor before the cut. If one operand is affected, restore x once there. If both are affected, restore x after F by distributivity. Every later computation is preserved because all paths cross F. This saves at least one gate when x has at least two readers. Both contradictions are to physical size minimality; these rewrites apply to the decoder's physical pure-group output with arbitrary upstream raw wiring.

The large shore needs at least four ORs by any quartic star. As shown in eight-five-five-stars.md, any branching pure in the subcubic five-edge shore must split it into two disjoint edges upon deleting that cubic vertex. In triangular canonical masks 87,94,117,236, only 117 has this property (at vertex 1). Thus masks 87,94,236 have no branching pure anywhere. The first AND then reads two private pure groups. Overlapping groups factor their common raw. For disjoint groups with one of size k=2 or 3, erasing its labels already spends the full allowed 2k+1 gates; the disjoint unused partner OR-tree loses its root as well. Two private quartets cannot be disjoint here, since all quartets are universal vertices of the same nine-edge shore. This excludes three pairs. The argument forces the unique small branching position in 117 and does not exclude that pair.
