# Five two-edge paths and one independent raw

The decoder cap is fourteen ORs and eight ANDs. Five bank ORs give T11/27. Use the independently established exclusion duality/uniform/ten-unrestricted-seven-and.md: no T10/24 with seven ANDs. In particular a specialization saving two ORs and one AND is impossible. A specialization saving three ORs is impossible by the secondary minimum of nine ANDs at sixteen ORs on ten raws. The usual direct-reader reductions now give the following facts. A short leaf must have a reader besides its bank; otherwise its sibling hides its addition. A mixed AND and OR reading the physical raw costs one AND and two ORs upon erasure (the first proper OR downstream of its AND is distinct by absorption). Three OR readers are impossible. Hence the decoder sees each short leaf exactly once, at an OR. A hot path-center AND would save its bank, the OR reader of its erased leaf and the product resolving to the other leaf, again impossible. Centers with an AND reader are locally cold. The independent raw s has two OR readers by the same argument; for a sole AND use its other operand's full nonemptiness producer, for multiple ANDs use the first-frontier refactorization and for a sole OR use clean-terminal normalization (square-regularity.md).

All physical inputs have two OR readers. In a normalized physical graph the relative OR budget is v−3. No vertex can have degree at least four: a bare d-star at d≥4 already needs 2d−4, and a first remote costs at least its order minus one, others full order; a nonstar high-degree component or star plus nonstar has the stronger ordinary transfer. Any vertex of degree three must lie in a bare star, and all other components must likewise be stars. Two three-stars are impossible by their joint six-OR lower. Each of the five disjoint banks for the short centers belongs to a distinct vertex (two of them together would have degree ≥4). In a star forest these five vertices lie in different components, since none is a leaf. If some center has degree three, the only resulting graph is the q17 K1,3+4P3 on all eleven edges. It has sixteen vertices and the physical relative decoder meets its excluded thirteen-OR/eight-AND budget.

If maximum degree is two, the five protected two-edge paths embed as distinct edge pairs. Joining their endpoints into longer components gives the following alternatives. A path with at least five edges is strong via P6; cycles with at least five vertices are strong. Two nonstars of orders three through five are jointly strong. Thus any newly formed nonstar is alone: a three-cycle must be one pair plus s, giving C3+4P3; C4 must be two pairs, giving C4+3P3+K2 (or other remote joins creating a second nonstar); P4 must be one pair plus s, giving P4+4P3; P5 consists of two pairs, giving P5+3P3+K2. All these interfaces are excluded at their actual v−3 budget by the q16 or q17 cases. With no nonstar, only 5P3+K2 remains. These group vertices are physical: every degree-one vertex needs its own mixed OR injection, since all raws have two OR readers. There are twelve such degree-one vertices (two for each pair and two for s). The twelve injections are distinct. Work in this physical vertex decoder; the nontrivial banks are just the given five pairs.

A center is either mixed (costing an additional OR), pure and private (feeding a dedicated clean AND), or pure and branching. A pure private product is clean because a locally hot product would give the forbidden one-AND/two-OR singleton deletion. Two private centers cannot have the same product: erase the two labels at one center and remove its bank, its two leaf injections, the AND, a collector OR, and the other center's now-dead private bank. Six losses yield T9/21 with seven ANDs, violating the four-AND composition. All are distinct where asserted: the other bank contains only its two disjoint raws, and a local positive remains outside both flagged groups.

If there are no branching pure centers, the five remaining centers cost five additional ORs, or at worst four if every collector term is among those charged. This exceeds the two ORs left after the twelve leaf and s injections. Thus some pure center branches. If b is their number, setting every private-path pair to zero leaves a positive pair in a branching path, so all five minus b mixed/private centers cost an additional OR each, without collector offset. Hence b≥3.

Each branching pure center has exactly two OR readers by the double-erasure proof in sixteen-center-branch.md: the T11/27 to T9/21 four-AND debit applies to a doubleton bank and the two other leaf occurrences; when f=2 a direct AND spends the final debit and aggregation of the remaining dark occurrences contradicts minimality or the required four AND losses. Associate the mixed leaf injections raw-last. For a branching center i, erasing its two raws removes its bank, both leaf injections and its two direct OR readers. An exit entering either of its own two leaf groups would absorb that leaf's raw and contradict irredundancy. Thus these are five distinct physical ORs and the restricted T9 circuit has fourteen ORs and eight ANDs, both optimal. All four deleted decoder gates bypass to their other nonzero operand: an additional dark OR or a redundant fold would incur a sixth loss and leave the size-21 circuit with at least seven ANDs, impossible. A fully dark leaf injection would require an upstream signal bounded by the erased pair; before these gates the only available such signals are the two raws and the pair bank, and an OR between them would be redundant or duplicate that bank. A fully dark center reader is absorbed by that bank.

Fix another branching center j. Its bank still has multiple consumers. Each of its prior use slots entering a bypassed OR is redirected along at least one downstream slot; two distinct surviving routes cannot merge into one slot through a bypass, which has only one surviving input. Coinciding in the two input slots of a single gate would yield a redundancy, also impossible. If both j leaves retain their external OR readers, the shared pure cherry on their two raws contradicts the OR-tight T9 local cherry lemma in square-no-nonempty.md. Its two external ORs are distinct and single-raw. Therefore at least one of j's two leaf injections must be among i's direct OR exits: neither could equal an injection of i since the groups are distinct. Every other branching center demands a distinct such exit, so b−1≤2 and b=3.

Equality in the OR budget assigns one gate to every mixed leaf/s injection, and the remaining two ORs to the two mixed/private centers. Thus the six branching-center exits are precisely the six branching leaf injectors: one from each branching center to one leaf in each other branching path. No leaf injection can take two different center sources since it has only one nonraw operand. Now set both other paths and the independent raw to zero. Their six singleton-bearing injections vanish, as do their two mixed/collector ORs (a branching-only positive still survives). The only remaining OR gates are the six displayed direct injections. Each reads one branching leaf and one different branching center, and thus is true at only three of the six remaining singleton labels. After these injections all downstream computation is AND-only. Any remaining input or injection signal is false on some pair of distinct labels (even a three-label support misses three labels), so no nonempty monomial of these signals can accept every pair. This contradicts the six-input threshold task and excludes 5P3+K2.
