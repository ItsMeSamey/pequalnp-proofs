# Fourteen-input threshold two at 34 gates and eight ANDs

Assume the historical size floors C(11)=27, C(12)=29, C(13)=32, C(14)≥34 and the unrestricted exclusion of eight ANDs at C(11)=27 (duality/uniform/eleven-unrestricted-eight-and.md). First exclude eight ANDs at C(12)=29. Use the usual clean-terminal and raw-reader normalization, as in the eleven-input proof: deleting a raw can save only two gates. Three readers, a mixed AND/OR pair of readers, or two AND readers with distinct OR frontiers save three or more; a common frontier admits factoring. A sole AND has nonempty-anywhere-else as its opposite operand and loses that OR and a downstream OR in addition to its product on deletion. A sole raw OR is eliminated by the standard relocation into a clean terminal. Thus a minimum representative has two distinct OR readers per raw, and deleting one raw saves those two ORs, preserving eight ANDs at size 27, impossible.

Now take a normalized fourteen-input candidate of size 34 with eight ANDs. Its regular simple raw-incidence graph has 14 edges, v vertices and v−2 ORs beyond its raw OR bank. Apply the branching-pure-group existence and branch/pendant deletion lemmas of degree/t14-34/eleven-branch-roles.md and its cited physical reductions. A branch of degree d with t pendant neighbors leaves N=v−1−t flags, 14−d edges, at most N−3 decoder ORs and at most eight ANDs. Reconstructing the bank uses at most 25−2d total ORs. At d=1 this beats C(13); at d=2,3 the corresponding size floor requires all eight ANDs, excluded above. All graphs and gate counts here refer to the normalized representative.

A vertex of degree d≥5 costs 2d−4 decoder ORs on its pinned star. Peeling other flags costs at least v−d−2, even allowing the first remote component discount, contradicting v−2. A quartic in a nonstar component, or a bare quartic with a remote nonstar, costs at least v−1 by the full-order remote peeling lemma (see degree/t16-cyclic-build/ten-forest.md and its sources). Thus the branch is a bare quartic star and the entire host is a star forest. Erase that branch with its four pendants. The residual budget is N−3; a second quartic costs at least N−2 after remote charges, as do two cubic stars together by the six-OR bound for two disjoint three-stars. Thus the other components consist of a≤1 cubic stars, p three-vertex paths and s isolated edges.

Let L count the degree-one flags. These are mixed groups and require L distinct raw injection ORs. Since v=L+1+a+p, at most p+a−1 other ORs remain. Each path center that is mixed costs one more raw injection OR. Otherwise its pure doubleton is private, since a branching doubleton has already been excluded, and feeds a dedicated AND. That AND is singleton-clean: each operand has at least two exclusive singleton labels (otherwise resolve the AND toward that operand and delete the at most one failing raw, beating C(13)); a doubleton thus has no singleton shared with the opposite operand. Two such private path products cannot coincide. Deleting one pair would remove its bank OR, both leaf injectors, the product, a collector OR and the other now-dead bank, six gates against the allowance 34−29=5. The two-label positive inside the quartic star survives without any private path product, so the terminal collector pays at least one OR for each of those products. These charges are disjoint from the mixed injections. Consequently p≤p+a−1, hence a=1, with equality in every OR charge.

Restrict to the quartet's four edge labels, and then hold its center true. Its four leaf flags are four independent bits. The decoder computes threshold two on these bits, since every nonempty subset represents an actual edge subset, and on the empty subset monotonicity and the rejected singletons force rejection. All charged remote leaf injections and mixed path center injections bypass on setting their raw inputs zero. All charged private path products vanish and the collector loses at least their number of ORs, because a remaining quartic pair is accepted. There remain at most four ORs, precisely the four different physical ORs charged to the quartet leaf flags unless fewer already suffice. Each leaf has only this one direct reader after the quartet center is pinned. In chronological order the earliest gate depending on any leaf must therefore be one of these leaf ORs; its other operand is a constant, since another direct leaf would share this reader, contradicting distinctness of the four mixed injection groups. This OR simplifies, leaving at most three ORs to compute four-input threshold two, below its four-OR floor. This excludes the eight-AND composition only.
