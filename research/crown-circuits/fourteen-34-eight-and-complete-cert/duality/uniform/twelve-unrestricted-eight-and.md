# No 29-gate eight-AND circuit for threshold two of twelve inputs

Assume a monotone binary T2(12) circuit on 29 gates and eight ANDs. The known size floor is 29, so it is minimal with 21 ORs. Saving three gates upon erasing a raw would contradict C(11)=27. Therefore each raw has two OR readers after kind-preserving normalization: third OR readers, or one AND plus two first/direct ORs, give forbidden deletions. An AND-only fan to a unique front OR factors if multiple; a sole AND reader has the full nonempty function of the other bits as partner, whose OR root can also be bypassed (degree/t13-31/or-optimal.md). A sole OR is relocated forward with its singleton-clean companion moved to the collector. Normalize clean terms into the final OR collector preserving regularity as in degree/t16-cyclic-build/ten-forest.md. Resolving a side of any AND with at most one exclusive singleton and then erasing that raw (or any raw if none) would save the AND and both raw ORs. Hence every AND side has at least two exclusive singleton labels.

No pure doubleton U=x OR y can branch. Erase x,y. At least four raw uses disappear and a branching U has f−1 excess dark uses, f its fanout. By the dark-edge telescoping identity, the number of lost gates is at least 4+(f−1)+the number of one-sided dark ANDs. The other operand of a direct AND reader of U cannot disappear when x=y=0: otherwise that operand would imply U on the full Boolean cube and its AND with U would be redundant. Thus each direct AND reader costs an additional unit. Six losses contradict C(10)=24. Necessarily f=2 and both exits are ORs. Then the bank OR, the two external raw OR readers and the two exits disappear. These five ORs are distinct: the external readers cannot coincide (a second copy of U), and an exit directly reading x or y would absorb it. The ten-input circuit has at most sixteen ORs and eight ANDs, whereas the sharp sixteen-OR floor requires at least nine ANDs. This excludes branching pure doubletons.

Every pure doubleton is therefore private, feeding an AND, clean by the two-exclusive-shore condition. No two pure doubletons can feed the same such product. Overlapping banks would make the common singleton nonexclusive. For disjoint banks erase both labels of one bank. Its own bank OR, the two other raw OR readers, the product and a collector OR disappear. The partner bank now has no consumer and can be removed. These are six distinct gates, contradicting C(10)=24. A collector gate exists because a positive missing both banks survives; the clean collector gate is distinct from singleton-bearing raw ORs.

Flatten the regular raw graph, whose relative budget is 21−(24−v)=v−3. First suppose every pure group is a doubleton. Let P be the number of pure groups and M the number of mixed raw-bearing groups, so v=P+M. Every mixed group needs a decoder OR beyond the bank. Each of the P private pure doubletons has a distinct clean terminal, needing at least P−1 collector ORs. These collector ORs have clean operands and are distinct from all hot mixed groups. Hence the decoder needs at least M+P−1>v−3. This includes P=0 directly via the M mixed charges. Therefore there is a pure group containing d≥3 raws.

A high vertex in a nonstar component forces its component's order-minus-two floor and full-order remotes. A high bare star and a remote nonstar give the same combined bound by full-order nonstar transfer. Either contradicts v−3, so we have a star forest. A d-star costs 2d−4, and the first remote costs at least order minus one, with all later remotes full; this excludes d≥4. The two-three-star six-OR bound excludes two triples. The graph is exactly one three-star with p short paths and s independent edges. The L=3+2p+2s degree-one vertices each have a paid mixed OR, leaving p−2 beyond them. Each short center is either mixed, paying an extra OR, or pure private, requiring its own distinct clean collector term. An isolated three-star pair survives without any of these products, so all such terms cost one collector OR apiece. The p short centers require p more ORs against p−2, a contradiction. This proof uses neither the proposed eleven-input composition exclusion nor the P5 specialization.

For a C14/34/A8 normalized branch of degree two, erase the branch and any t pendant neighbors as in degree/t18/shapes.md. There are E=12 edge labels and N nonisolated flags with relative allowance N−3 and at most eight ANDs. Restoring 2E−N bank ORs gives at most 21 ORs and eight ANDs for T2(12). Its size floor 29 forces 21/8 exactly, now impossible. This holds for all q,t to which that branch reduction applies.
