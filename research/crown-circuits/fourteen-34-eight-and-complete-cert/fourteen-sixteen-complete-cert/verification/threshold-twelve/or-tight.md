# An OR-tight minimum cannot have two OR readers at every raw

Let n≥6, and suppose a minimum-size monotone fan-in-two circuit for threshold two has 2n−4 ORs, with every raw occurring at exactly two OR readers and nowhere else. Define the flattened OR groups and simple raw-incidence graph as above. At least one pure group exists: the earliest gate cannot read raws at an AND. The budgeting equation for mixed overhead is r=p−4, independent of the number of AND gates. By the star restriction a pure group U with k labels satisfies k+2≤p. Also p≤n because every group contains at least two raw labels if pure, with 2n total occurrences. If k=n−2 then p=n and all p groups must have exactly two labels, impossible since n≥6. Thus k≤n−3.

For each pure U, every one of its k labels has one external OR reader. These external gates are distinct: any two of the labels already share U, so cannot enter any second OR group together. Let F consist of the first proper ORs encountered downstream of U along routes with no intervening OR. Every complete path from U to output must hit F, as an AND-only path implies the output is bounded above by U, yet deleting all k labels leaves a nonzero threshold. If some F gate was also an external reader of x in U, it would have form x∨P with P≤U along the AND-only path. Delete the k−1 labels of U other than x. This contracts k−1 ORs from U's private tree, leaving x, and separately eliminates k−1 external readers. The purported F gate then has P≤x, so also collapses. That exceeds the OR savings 2(k−1) allowed by the OR floor at the residual arity. Thus F is disjoint from the external readers.

Deleting all k labels eliminates k−1 tree ORs, all k external readers, and every OR in F because its U-dependent operand is now zero. The OR floor permits only 2k OR removals, so F consists of exactly one gate. If U had more than one consumer, reduce circuit size as follows. If U directly feeds F and another path to F exists, F's other operand is bounded by U and F is redundant. Otherwise all immediate consumers are ANDs. On the AND-only dependency cone before F, replace U by true, eliminating its immediate AND consumers. Every operand of F depending on U is the product of U and its new value. If a single operand depended on U, restore the factor once on that operand. If both did, restore it once after F. There is no other outgoing path to consider since every path hits F first. The at least two removed gates exceed the one restoration, contradicting size minimality. Every pure group therefore has fanout one.

The first AND in a topological order consequently reads two pure, single-use groups. If an operand was not pure, its earlier nonraw operand must be a shared OR, but an earliest shared OR would itself have been pure. An overlapping label factors from their product, permitting one fewer gate because both input trees are private. For disjoint groups U,V, erase U's k labels. The tree, the external readers and its sole F already use the full 2k OR savings. The opposite pure group V is disjoint, single-use, nonconstant and now dead. Its OR root was not among the already counted gates: it does not read a U label and is on the opposite arm from U, not downstream of it. Its further removal violates the OR bound. This contradiction proves the claim.

In particular a size-21 nine-input circuit cannot have seven ANDs. The lower bound 19 at eight inputs forces, in a representative preserving AND count, two OR readers at every raw; seven ANDs would leave fourteen ORs, exactly the OR floor. The same reasoning removes the seven-AND case at arity ten and size 23. At twelve inputs it reproves the eight-AND exclusion.
