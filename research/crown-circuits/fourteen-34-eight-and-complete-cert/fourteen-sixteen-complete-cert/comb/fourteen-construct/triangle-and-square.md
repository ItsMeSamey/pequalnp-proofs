# A triangle and a disjoint four-cycle require five decoder ORs

Suppose a decoder with at most four ORs, chosen to minimize the total number of gates under that cap. A flag spoils an opposite edge if it turns the negative edge into a positive when added alone.

All three triangle flags spoil. Otherwise clamp a harmless one true, making its two neighbors independent star labels on the unchanged square-edge flag bank. The square needs at least one OR since no monomial computing its local task can accept every triple and reject all four edges. The independent-raw increment gives at least 1+4 ORs, impossible. Similarly all four square flags spoil: for a harmless y, erase its opposite z (an edge at y versus that edge plus z proves essentiality, and positive inputs stay nonzero), then fix y true. The unchanged triangle bank plus two independent virtual labels needs at least four ORs; at most three remain. All these star reductions preserve the proper negative singletons by harmlessness.

Each square flag is single-read. Its first ORs all disappear on zeroing it. Each of the other three can subsequently be zeroed at the cost of an OR since its isolated-flag spoiler witness survived. Thus at most one first OR exists, and the minimum-size first-OR factorization precludes multiple readers.

Set the triangle flags to zero; the square function needs at least one OR, so at most three are lost. The three fully spoiled triangle flags saturate the corresponding cold-forest leaf bound. As in two-triangles-four-ors.md, their pure local function AND3 excludes an internal cold OR: freeze the remaining triangle flag to one and the square to zero at the earliest merger. Consequently each triangle raw enters the square-live system through its unique prescribed exit. Its exit's cold operand is that raw multiplied by earlier square-live factors. Every full path of that triangle raw to output passes its own entry, since any cold-only bypass would yield two occurrences in a saturated trace.

Consider the earliest gate reading any square raw s. It cannot be an AND with a different square raw: s is singly read and could never spoil on its own. An OR with another square raw t would identify the two, yet an edge from t to a neighbor z≠s rejects whereas the triple s,t,z accepts. Both raws have no alternative use. Repeated inputs would be idempotent. An AND with a pure triangle signal is impossible because it would vanish on the entire pure-square projection, where s is essential (add s to an edge avoiding it to obtain a triple). The gate is therefore an OR of s and a pure triangle signal. It is a triangle exit; earlier square-live nonraw factors are impossible since no square raw has yet been read. Thus its triangle operand is exactly its prescribed singleton t. All paths from t enter through that exit, and s is single-read, so the decoder depends on this pair only through s∨t.

Identify s and t as one formal vertex and bypass that OR. More explicitly, in the resulting figure-eight graph (triangle and four-cycle sharing a vertex) each original edge and pair of edges has the same decoder inputs as before except that the identified flags now both take their union; the decoder value is unchanged because it sees only that union. It hence has a decoder with at most three ORs. Fix the joined vertex true and select independently its four incident edges, setting the remaining vertex false. Nonempty selections agree with the original edge labels; empty rejects by monotonicity from a rejected singleton. The resulting four-input threshold requires four ORs. This contradiction proves the lower bound. An upper decoder is abc ∨ pr ∨ qs ∨ ((a∨b)∧(p∨r)) where p,r and q,s form the two opposite pairs of the square; it uses five ORs.
