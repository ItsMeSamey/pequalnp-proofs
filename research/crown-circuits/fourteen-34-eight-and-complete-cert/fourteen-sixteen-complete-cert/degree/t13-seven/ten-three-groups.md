# Three pure groups cannot realize a 24-gate, seven-AND ten-input threshold

In the regular representative of ten-minor.md, seventeen ORs and twenty raw incidences give r=p−3. At p=3 every mixed OR group has one nonraw parent and at least one raw input. Only the output AND is clean. The star bound k+max(d,2)≤4 makes every pure group a doubleton. A private pure doubleton supplies all two mandatory own-only witnesses at its dedicated AND, so that product is clean. It cannot be the sole terminal because a positive pair may lie outside the doubleton. Thus all three pure groups branch.

Every branching doubleton B has fanout two: removing its two labels costs at least four plus its fanout surplus, and C(8)=19 permits only five deletions. A direct AND reader would exhaust an additional one-sided charge, since a second operand that also dies would imply B and make the product redundant. Both B exits are ORs. Three pure fanout units leave two nonraw surplus units. The shared support bound C(m)≤24−2(10−m)−f implies m≤7 at fanout two and m≤6 at fanout three, using C(8)=19 and C(7)=16. The singleton credits from raws and branching pure groups are 20+6=26. The ten signed codes require at least 34 exclusive singleton incidences, and each of six dirty ANDs debits at least one. The two remaining units must yield fourteen credits. There are exactly two fanout-two ORs W1,W2 with singleton support seven each. A fanout-three gate gives at most twelve; a shared AND loses its own credit to its hot debit. Every dirty AND has singleton support one and every AND is single-use.

Trace each W through its unique nonraw parent repeatedly, until a pure root or an AND. The two W sites are incomparable along OR-only paths: equal size-seven supports would make every extra raw on a continuation redundant, as any raw true at an upstream singleton is dominated by that wire. The paths are disjoint after leaving their pure roots because before a W only a pure root can branch. A W whose source is pure needs at least five raw occurrences added, and a W whose source is dirty needs at least six. If m of the two W sites have a pure source, their paths cost at least 12−m mixed raw occurrences. Each of the other 6−m pure exits also needs one raw. Exactly fourteen mixed occurrences exist, giving 18−2m≤14. Hence m=2, with equality throughout. All mixed raw occurrences are already consumed on paths from pure roots. A dirty AND therefore has no outgoing OR path and must feed another AND directly. Its one hot singleton violates that operand's requirement of two exclusive singletons.

## Four pure groups

At p=4 all pure groups have size at most three; r=1 leaves at most two clean terminals. Thus at least five dirty outputs debit singleton mass. If all four pure groups branch, their initial credits plus the raw credits are at most 32. The one remaining unit must supply seven credits, since the exclusive mass must reach 34. Equality forces four branching triples of fanout two, a fifth shared OR of support seven, exactly five dirty ANDs, and one hot singleton on each dirty AND. In particular no AND branches. The collector takes the sole ordinary OR. For a branching triple, deleting its three labels charges six raw reads, the pure fanout surplus, and one further deletion per direct AND reader. A direct reader's other operand cannot become zero, because it would imply the triple and make the product redundant. Two such readers would leave at most fifteen gates on seven inputs, contradicting C(7)=16. At least four of the eight pure exits therefore enter precollector OR groups. Each of the five dirty singleton outputs must also enter an OR, since the next AND needs two exclusive singleton labels. These nine distinct source occurrences require distinct groups, each with one raw, because every precollector OR has exactly one nonraw operand. Only 20−12=8 mixed raw occurrences exist.

If three of the four pure groups branch, the credits from raw inputs and one surplus at each branching group are at most 29. Among the two remaining units, a shared AND would cancel one credit with its own hot debit and leave at most 29+7−4=32. If one unit instead augments a branching pure root, it contributes at most three: equality at the required 34 then forces 29+3+7−5=34, including three branching triples, five singleton-hot dirty ANDs and two clean terminals. The fanout-three triple cannot feed any AND, since the debit 6+2+1 exceeds the eight-gate deletion allowance. The other two triples each have at most one AND reader. At least five pure exits therefore enter distinct mixed groups and five dirty outputs do likewise. But the pure groups consume at least eleven raw slots, leaving at most nine for those ten groups. Thus in this three-branching case all ANDs are single-use and all branching pure roots have fanout two.
