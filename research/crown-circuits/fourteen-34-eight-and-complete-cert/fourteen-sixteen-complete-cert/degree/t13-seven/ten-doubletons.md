# A 24-gate, seven-AND threshold circuit on ten inputs needs a pure group of size at least three

Work in the regular clean-terminal representative of ten-minor.md. Each raw has two OR readers. Suppose all p pure groups are doubletons. Write b for branching groups and u=p−b for private groups. The branching doubleton and private-product lemmas of ten-three-groups.md give fanout two with OR-only exits for each branching group. Every private group has a dedicated clean AND with a shared partner. These partners are distinct (factor any repeated partner out of the terminal union), and nonpure (a branching doubleton only feeds ORs). The five fanout surplus units therefore imply p≤5.

Each private product vanishes on any pair outside the union of private groups. The collector has c≤p−2 products and c≥u, so b≥2, u≤3. At least two inputs lie outside all private groups. Their pair forces another terminal, hence c≥u+1 and b≥3. The case p=3 is excluded by ten-three-groups.md. If b≥4, at most one surplus unit remains outside branching groups, each such credit contributing at most seven singleton labels. The signed incidence mass is at most 20+2b+7(5−b)−(7−c)≤31, below the necessary 34.

It remains to consider b=3 and p=4 or 5. The collector now has exactly c=p−2 terminals, using all available nonraw joins. Every precollector mixed OR group has precisely one nonraw term. Let d=7−c, H be the sum of the singleton support sizes of the dirty ANDs, and m count those of support one. If a shared AND uses either of the two remaining surplus credits, its first credit cancels its own support debit. Even granting seven for the other credit and charging only d−1 additional dirty gates gives at most 26+7−(d−1)<34. Hence the two credits belong to ORs. At p=5 the two private products need distinct shared partners. At p=4 a single fanout-three OR contributes at most 12, below 8+H≥13. Thus the credits lie on distinct fanout-two ORs. Their support sizes sum to C≥8+H, and C≤14 gives H≤6.

There are M=20−2p mixed raw occurrences. Each of the six branching exits must first enter a mixed OR group, and each singleton dirty AND also needs a mixed OR before its next AND to reach the two-label exclusive-shore floor. These 6+m groups are distinct because each has just one nonraw term. For p=4, d=5 and m≥4; for p=5, d=4 and m≥2. In either case M−6−m≤2 extra raw occurrences remain.

Follow the unique nonraw predecessor through mixed OR groups back from either shared OR until reaching a branching group or a dirty AND. If the source is branching or singleton dirty, only its first mandatory raw occurrence can lie on this path among the 6+m charges. All other added raws come from the at most two extras. The support is at most 2+1+2=5. If instead the source has dirty support at least two, none of the charged occurrences is on the path, and H≤6, d≥4 give source support at most three. The same bound five applies. This counts serial shared ORs as well. Consequently C≤10, contradicting C≥8+d≥12.
