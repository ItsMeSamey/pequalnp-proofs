# The triangle requires one path center hot on a singleton

Take a collector-normalized candidate. The previous reductions give a unique triangle branch, at most one triangle flag reading an AND, no shared product of sibling leaves, and all path centers and leaves single-read. Thus at least one nonbranching triangle flag reads an OR. An OR group cannot carry two singleton-read raw flags, by the same-component identities and cross-component vertex fusion in triangle-unbranched.md. In particular this last triangle flag needs a singleton-hot OR group distinct from any charged single-read path flag group.

Charge one OR per path leaf. For an OR-read leaf use its own group. If a leaf x reads an AND x∧P, that product is false on every singleton: otherwise P is true at the singleton x, and x implies P by monotonicity on independent edge labels, making the product redundant. This is a clean terminal. No two leaf charges use the same product: across components neither leaf can detect its own local pair when the other component is empty, and the sibling-product case was excluded. A center F reading an OR costs another group. A center reading a clean AND costs a clean terminal. Clean center products on different ordinary paths are distinct by sixteen-center-comparison.md. The exceptional-center case is addressed separately below. No center product equals a dedicated leaf AND, since a same-path center absorbs its leaf, and a different center is false at the sibling singleton where the leaf reader's other operand must be true.

For the all-four-ordinary case the shared-center exclusion is direct from sixteen-center-comparison.md. Erasing the four paths erases every dedicated clean term counted above. At least one other collector term survives on the doubled triangle. Hence the final OR collector costs at least one OR per distinct charged terminal. Those ORs are distinct from the hot groups used for OR-read flags. The twelve path charges and the extra nonbranch triangle OR give thirteen, impossible.

If exactly one path is exceptional and no center is locally hot, that path necessarily has a leaf AND. Remove this leaf raw x. Its bank, its dedicated clean AND and collector OR disappear. Thus one obtains T10 of size 24 and with seven ANDs. The surviving sibling y cannot retain an AND reader in this physical minor, by ten-minor.md. A center previously reading a clean AND F∧P now becomes a further direct AND reader of y. It survived the three explicit removals (its product differs from the leaf's); any subsequent simplification would contradict the size floor. Thus the exceptional center was OR-read. All remaining clean-center charges are on ordinary paths and are distinct by the existing comparison lemma. Again all four paths cost twelve, plus the triangle OR. This proves there is exactly one locally hot center product H=F∧K. The other three paths are ordinary, so the three remaining center charges are distinct and the eight leaf charges apply. Only one of the two nonbranch triangle flags can read an OR, by the twelve-OR cap. The other has its sole AND reader.

In fact all path leaves are OR-read. Only the exceptional path could have a leaf AND, say on x with sibling y. If H is hot at y, erasing x loses its bank, its dedicated AND, its collector OR, and also H which reduces to y since K is true on the y singleton. This exceeds the three possible deletions. If H is hot at x, erase y. Its bank and direct reader simplify, and H reduces to x. If y's reader was AND, its collector also loses an OR and the size floor fails. If it was OR, the three deletions give a physical 24-gate seven-AND threshold-ten circuit with x still reading its AND, prohibited by ten-minor.md. Any additional loss would violate the size floor. Thus all leaves read ORs, and the locally hot center product must be hot on exactly one of its leaves (otherwise F implies its partner and it is redundant).
