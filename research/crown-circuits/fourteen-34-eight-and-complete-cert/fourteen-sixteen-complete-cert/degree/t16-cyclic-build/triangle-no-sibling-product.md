# A shared path-leaf product is impossible in the normalized triangle candidate

Suppose a path's raw leaves x,y are both read directly by their common AND D. Its center is F=x∨y. The other three paths are ordinary. The triangle can have one branching vertex; each such vertex has two OR readers. Every triangle bank remains physically present except upon the explicit restrictions below.

As in triangle-no-unbranched.md, set x=0. Remove its bank OR, D, and a collector OR, yielding an optimum 24-gate seven-AND threshold-ten circuit on the triangle edges, six ordinary path edges and r=y. Clean-terminal normalization survives: x and y reached all precollector wires other than D solely via F. If F had a sole reader, r does too. A sole AND is forbidden by degree/t13-seven/ten-minor.md. A sole r∨H has H false on every other singleton of this threshold, since adding r must change its outcome there; it is false also on r alone by independence. H was therefore cold on every original singleton, and fed the hot OR F∨H, contrary to the original normalization. Thus F branched, with exactly two OR readers by the path-center lemma. All ten raw labels now have exactly two physical OR readers.

Flatten the raw-incidence groups of this minimum. A branching triangle bank is a group vertex of its own: its two uses treat its output as a nonraw signal. All singly read original triangle/path flags are in distinct raw-bearing groups as in triangle-no-unbranched.md. In particular the original no-fusion restrictions survive the specialization of F to r: only D and one collector OR were removed from the preexisting decoder, leaving other precollector connections intact. Each ordinary path yields its three flag vertices, as does the triangle. A new group is allowed for either endpoint of r. The host graph is a triangle and three two-edge paths together with one additional edge r between two distinct vertices. The minimum clean-terminal regular representative has a pure group of size at least three by degree/t13-seven/ten-doubletons.md. This is possible only at an endpoint of r, and its degree is exactly three.

Write h for the order of this component and v for the order of the host. The physical relative decoder budget is v−3 ORs. If the component is not a star, no flag of that component is true on every one of its own singleton edges. Its local decoder needs at least h−2 ORs: peel inward toward the degree-three center, one OR per nonneighbor via a grandparent edge versus its union with the parent edge, then clamp the center true to obtain threshold two on three independent bits. Each other connected component on s vertices adds s ORs to this lower bound. Peel toward a retained edge at a cost of s−2, identify its two endpoints with one new independent raw, and apply comb/fourteen-construct/independent-raw-or-increment.md. The pooled base retains the absence of a universal flag after every such addition. Thus at least v−2 ORs are needed.

If the distinguished component is a star, it must be a three-star formed by attaching r as a pendant to a path center. Indeed joining two preexisting components preserves all their edges; a degree-three star with r removed has a two-edge path and one isolated vertex. The rest is the triangle and two paths. A triangle together with a three-star needs at least five ORs, with unrestricted ANDs. If each isolated triangle vertex spoils a star-edge singleton, erase the three triangle vertices successively. At each step its own witness survives all other zero pins, and a star pair remains positive, forcing a proper OR loss. Two further ORs are needed on the star (pin the center true to obtain threshold three-input, threshold value two). Otherwise clamp a harmless triangle vertex true and interpret the two remaining neighbors as independent labels. With one or both virtual bits asserted this encodes actual triangle edges. With neither asserted, all negative star singletons still reject by harmlessness and positive star pairs stay positive by monotonicity. The first independent bit adds at least one OR by essentiality and a surviving star pair. The second adds two by the independent-raw lemma, because no flag covers both the first independent singleton and the star singletons. This again gives five ORs. The two remaining paths cost three ORs apiece relative to this nonuniversal base. The required eleven exceeds the ten available at v=13.

This closes all normalized triangle cases containing a path whose two leaves feed one shared AND, regardless of the number of branching triangle vertices.
