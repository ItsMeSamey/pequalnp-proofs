# Every normalized triangle must branch

Suppose the three cycle flags are all single-read in a collector-normalized triangle-plus-four-paths decoder. By triangle-unbranched.md one path is exceptional. Its leaves x,y have a sole common reader D=x∧y in the decoder; its center F=x∨y has exactly two OR readers. The other three paths are ordinary. The cold D enters the final collector, which has another term because external positive pairs survive.

Set x=false. Remove the bank OR F, the AND D and its collector OR. The resulting physical threshold-ten circuit is minimum, with 17 ORs and seven ANDs. All ten raws already have exactly two OR readers: each triangle-edge raw goes to its two endpoint bank ORs, each ordinary path leaf to its center bank and decoder OR, and the surviving exceptional raw r=y to the two original OR readers of F. No extraneous gates remain in a 24-gate threshold-ten circuit. The collector stays normalized. In the old circuit x and y reached every precollector wire other than D through F only; hence every such wire had identical singleton evaluations at x and y. Every surviving singleton-hot wire therefore stays hot. The terminal collector simply loses D.

Flatten all single-use OR-to-OR edges. In a raw-bearing group, any two original singleton-read flag occurrences can be reassociated into a direct two-flag OR while preserving their sole uses. The same-path possibilities are absorbed or repeat the path center. Triangle-triangle is forbidden by triangle-no-pure-gate.md and cross-component flag fusions by triangle-unbranched.md. This prohibition transfers from the old normalized decoder: the specialization only replaced F by r and removed the clean term D and one collector OR, and did not merge any of the other precollector groups. Consequently the ten-edge raw incidence graph consists of a triangle and three two-edge paths plus one edge r, whose endpoints may be existing or new vertices. Distinctness of the endpoints follows from minimality (a repeated raw in one flattened OR is redundant). Every vertex away from the endpoints of r contains exactly the raws of its original flag. Away from r's component a pure vertex must therefore be an original doubleton bank read by a single AND: an OR reader would add a nonraw operand or a forbidden second flag. A singleton raw alone cannot form a pure OR group. Thus all remote pure vertices are private doubletons.

The hypotheses of degree/t13-seven/ten-doubletons.md now hold in this very representative: size 24, seven ANDs, two OR readers on every raw and a normalized collector. That lemma gives a pure group of degree at least three. Such a group must contain r, and has degree exactly three (r and one degree-two flag). Let its component have h vertices and let the whole host have v. The physical decoder relative to the incidence flags has v−3 ORs: each flag bank of degree k takes k−1 ORs, and the degrees sum to 20.

Pay for each remote component by setting its flags to zero. Every mixed vertex has a distinct explicit OR to inject its raw group flag into its nonraw operands. These disappear. Each private doubleton U feeds a dedicated clean product: the two-exclusive-singleton bound of ten-minor.md on the U shore forces both of its labels outside the other operand. The private products are distinct by the private-product lemma used in ten-doubletons.md. In particular a shared partner product with two private doubleton operands would use disjoint groups by cleanliness; deleting one group would lose its designated AND, its collector OR, its bank and its two external reader ORs, as well as the now-dead other bank, contradicting C(8)=19. These remote clean terms disappear simultaneously. A term survives because a pair of raw edges incident to the central triple must still be accepted. The collector thus loses at least one OR per disappearing term. These collector ORs are disjoint from the singleton-hot mixed injection ORs. The remote charge totals at least v−h ORs.

Peel the central component inward toward the triple, retaining the triple and its three distinct neighbors. Using distance-decreasing parents, delete each nonneighbor in descending distance: if V has parent W and grandparent Z, edge WZ alone versus WZ together with WV distinguishes V and respects all previous zero pins. A positive survives when V is zero, so a proper OR disappears. This costs at least h−4 additional ORs. Now clamp the triple true and feed its three neighbors independent raw bits: every nonempty selection corresponds to those incident edges, and zero rejects by monotonicity below a negative singleton. The three-input threshold needs two ORs. Therefore at least (v−h)+(h−4)+2=v−2 ORs are needed, exceeding the v−3 budget.

This excludes the no-branch triangle stratum of normalized candidates. Together with the branching-cycle count and the absence of triangle-only ORs, any remaining normalized candidate has exactly one branching triangle vertex.
