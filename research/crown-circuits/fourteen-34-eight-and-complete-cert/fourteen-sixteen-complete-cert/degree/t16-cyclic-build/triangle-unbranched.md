# A normalized triangle with no branching vertex needs an exceptional path

Work with the twelve-OR/eight-AND decoder for the triangle and four two-edge paths. The gate count is minimal since rebuilding seven bank ORs yields size 27 for eleven-input threshold two. The flag degrees below always refer to the normalized decoder.

A wire is cold when it rejects every edge singleton, so its value is bounded by the target on every promised input. For a nonoutput cold wire W, clamp it false in the rest of the decoder, obtaining output F₀. Then W∨F₀ suffices for the target. The new F₀ cannot vanish identically, since otherwise W already computes the target and its strictly downstream gates can be dropped. Consequently every path from W to the output contains a proper OR, and replacing W by false bypasses at least one OR. An AND reader would cost that reader and a subsequent OR. Two direct readers would also cost two gates. Either outcome contradicts minimality after reinstating W with one final OR. Thus W has a unique OR reader. When its reader also receives a singleton-hot operand, bypass that gate and append W at the output. This preserves the exact OR and AND counts and every input-bank gate, and increases the number of terminal cold gates in the final cold OR tree. Iteration gives a decoder where no cold wire enters a hot computation. Reapply the flag reader lemmas to this new representative.

Suppose all three triangle vertices are single-read and all four path centers are single-read. Every path leaf is already single-read. Two raw flags cannot form a nonredundant singleton-hot AND: distinct components have disjoint singleton support, two path leaves do too, and a center absorbs either leaf. Products of two triangle vertices are ruled out by triangle-readers.md. A same-component OR also fails: triangle-no-pure-gate.md rules out the triangle case, and within a path the OR either duplicates the existing center or is absorbed by it.

Two single-read flags of different components cannot form a common OR. Identify them at their sole gate, saving one OR; arbitrary unions of original edge labels give the new glued-vertex interface correctly. For two paths, the merged component is P5, a cubic nonstar, or a four-star. The P5-plus-triangle floor or the established respective star floors, with the full residual remote increments, exceeds eleven ORs (sixteen-center-branch.md). This fusion argument uses only that the two flags under consideration are single-read. For a triangle and a path leaf, the merged component is a triangle with a two-edge tail. Its pendant leaf is essential and costs one OR when set to zero; the cubic triangle vertex then pins true to expose three independent labels requiring two ORs. Adding the three remote paths costs nine more, since no flag of the base is true on every singleton edge. For a triangle vertex and path center the merged flag directly exposes four independent labels, requiring four ORs before adding the other paths.

Thus the first internal hot gate cannot exist: its inputs would have to be raw flags since buried cold signals are unavailable. With no internal hot gates, on the isolated triangle the decoder cannot accept the triple of flags: a first cold AND would need a pair of raw triangle flags, which was excluded, and any allowed cold AND on other flags is false there. The output collector therefore also rejects.

In a normalized candidate with nonbranching triangle there must consequently be a branching path center. By sixteen-center-branch.md it has exactly two OR readers, and its two leaves feed a common AND (both-OR leaves would make a branching center impossible). The other three paths are ordinary by sixteen-two-deletions.md. This is a classification of normalized candidates.
