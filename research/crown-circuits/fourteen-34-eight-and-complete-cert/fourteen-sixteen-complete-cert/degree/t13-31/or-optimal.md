# Exact secondary gate count at the OR minimum for threshold two

For n≥6, any monotone binary circuit for threshold two with the minimum 2n−4 OR gates uses at least n−1 AND gates. Thus its least possible total size is 3n−5. The usual prefix construction attains the numbers: maintain the nonempty-prefix predicate by one OR per additional input, and update threshold two by adding its conjunction with the new input as a new disjunct. Omit the unused final nonempty-prefix predicate.

Among circuits at the OR optimum choose a representative minimizing the number of ANDs. All cleans that remove a gate without adding ORs or increasing the total size are available; fewer ORs than the floor are impossible. If there exists x whose deletion saves at least two ORs and one AND, the OR floor at arity n−1 makes the smaller circuit OR-optimal too. Induction then bounds the original AND count by n−1. The base n=6 follows from the 13-gate size minimum at six inputs and the eight-OR minimum.

Suppose no such x exists. At most two ORs read any raw, since erasing x bypasses every OR reader. Consider a raw x read by at least one AND. Define the frontier as the first ORs reached along paths beginning with one of those ANDs and continuing through ANDs. No path to the output can consist only of such ANDs, because the output would be bounded above by x. No frontier OR can read x directly: its other operand, carrying x along ANDs, is bounded above by x. With at least one additional direct OR reader, deleting x therefore removes two ORs and an AND. With no direct OR readers, the same conclusion holds if the frontier contains at least two distinct ORs.

If x has at least two immediate AND readers, no direct OR reader, and the only frontier OR is F, temporarily substitute true for x in the AND-only cone before F. Every cone wire was x conjoined with its new value, and off-cone operands before F do not depend on x. Every path from x to output reaches F first, so all external uses after F can be left unchanged. The substitution removes all immediate AND readers of x, at least two. If one operand of F came from the cone, restore its factor x at that operand. If both did, restore x once after F by distributivity. This gives the same function with at most one new AND and no new ORs, contradicting the secondary minimization.

If the sole reader is x∧P, then P is the full disjunction of the remaining inputs. Indeed the threshold distinguishes y from x+y for each y≠x, and P is independent of x. Its last gate is an OR, as otherwise both inputs computed the same full disjunction and that AND would be redundant. Erasing x removes its reader and at least one frontier OR. On remaining inputs P dominates all wires: bypass P∧Q by Q and P∨Q by P in topological order. Since the target differs from P, P's own OR root can then be removed. This is the desired two-OR, one-AND deletion.

We may therefore assume every raw has one or two OR readers and no AND reader. If x is read only by x∨P, P rejects every other singleton because threshold two separates y from x+y. Redirect the uses of x∨P to x, then append a terminal OR with P, preserving cost and function. The other raw fanouts remain fixed because P is nonraw. If a new AND reader results, apply the preceding alternatives; otherwise repeat for this raw until it has two readers, each time advancing beyond an existing OR. The raw cannot become a direct output disjunct because its singleton is rejected.

The remaining case, every raw having two OR readers only, contradicts the regular OR-tight obstruction for n≥6 in verification/threshold-twelve/or-tight.md. That proof needs only secondary minimality at fixed OR cost: deletion contradictions use OR counts alone, OR identities preserve or reduce the OR count, and the single-frontier refactorization replaces multiple immediate ANDs by one AND without adding ORs. The overlapping private-group factorization (x∨P)∧(x∨Q)=x∨(P∧Q) saves an OR and adds no AND. Thus the unrestricted OR-optimal representative has the deletion required for the induction.
