# A single reader for the independent label is impossible

Use the collector-normalized C4+3P3+K2 residual at twelve OR and eight AND, including the opposite/adjacent raw-OR exclusions, degree's clean-square discriminator, and the mixed two-deletion bound. Write z for the independent K2 raw. A sole reader z∨P cannot occur: its partner is independent of z and must reject every other singleton (otherwise adding z there would be invisible), and hence is singleton-clean; a singleton-hot OR cannot have a singleton-clean operand in this representative.

Suppose z has the sole reader D=z∧P. Every other singleton must make P true, so P equals the full disjunction of the ten other raws. It is a physical OR, since a primary flag cannot supply that disjunction, and either input of a purported AND producer would already equal the same disjunction. Every other wire besides bare z is bounded by P: z can influence the circuit only through D≤P. Thus an OR user of P would be redundant; P ends a maximal OR group of its own. It cannot be a group charged to a sole path leaf, path center or square flag. A leaf group equal to P makes the local sibling pair indistinguishable from the sibling singleton. Delete z and drop D and one downstream OR, and bypass P to true, which preserves all nonempty answers and rejects empty by monotonicity. If the P group also used a path center as a term with no other consumer, that center's bank OR is now dead and saves a fourth gate. If P is charged to a singly used square flag, its bank OR similarly becomes dead. Either contradicts C(10)=24 after the three existing savings. For a path leaf whose bank occurrence goes only to P through the decoder, the local sibling indistinguishability above suffices. The charged P producer is therefore distinct in every case. The T2(10) circuit after the three deletions has 24 gates and seven ANDs. All P3 leaves have decoder OR readers by the regularity theorem ten-minor.md: an original direct leaf AND is neither D nor either removed OR and thus would survive; further losses violate minimality.

Let b≤1 count branching square flags, d count locally hot P3 center readers, and s count dirty square AND readers. The simultaneous cold-edge/cold-leaf deletion gives d+s≤1. Each of the six path leaves costs a distinct hot OR. Each of 3−d non-dirty path centers costs either its distinct hot OR group or a dedicated clean terminal. With OR-read leaves these path groups are ordinary. Each of 4−b−s nonbranching non-dirty square flags similarly costs a hot OR group or a dedicated clean terminal. No two singly read flag OR groups coincide (absorption, vertex fusion, or the no-square-pair OR theorem). A shared clean product of square flags is excluded even for opposite flags: pin one false and omit the other's bank, obtaining T2(9) with at most fourteen ORs and seven ANDs. A shared clean product of a square and path flag hides the square flag on local square edge-extension tests. Distinct path-center products are ensured by the ordinary center comparison. In addition P costs its extra OR and D is a distinct clean terminal. Even with the one-OR saving when collecting a list of terminals, the total is at least

    6 + (3−d) + (4−b−s) + 1 + 1 − 1 = 14−d−b−s.

Equality with twelve requires b=1, d+s=1 and exactly the charged terminals. A locally dirty path center would leave the pair of its own two path edges outside every charged terminal. Hence d=0 and s=1. Each path center likewise must feed a clean terminal. On doubled square inputs at least two square-clean flags are needed, because every individual flag is omitted by a positive triple. There are exactly two candidate clean flags after the branch and the dirty one. Opposite clean square flags are impossible; the two are adjacent. Each of their opposite vertices must be OR-only by the opposite-discriminator argument, leaving no location for the dirty one. This excludes a sole reader for z.
