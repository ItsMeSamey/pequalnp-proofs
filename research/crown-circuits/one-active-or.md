# Crown circuits with at most one true OR on each diagonal

For a wire w, let D(w) be the set of indices i where its value on the promised cell (i,i) is true. Suppose the represented n-by-n matrix is the crown and that at most one OR gate evaluates true on each diagonal. Consequently distinct OR gates have disjoint diagonal supports.

Truncate the circuit by replacing the output of every original gate with empty diagonal support by constant false. Induction over gates gives two properties. On every diagonal input the new and old wire values coincide. On every input the new value is bounded above by the old, by monotonicity. Invalid wire reads can be replaced by false throughout.

In the truncated circuit every nonempty-diagonal wire has the form M or M ∧ H, where M is a conjunction of zero or more raw literals (positive input variables), and H is the output of one OR gate. For an input this holds directly. For an AND with nonempty diagonal support, all its parents are simultaneously true on some diagonal. Any OR markers among the parents are therefore true on the same diagonal and must be the identical gate H. Multiplication combines monomials and absorbs repeated copies of H. A nullary AND has M equal to the empty conjunction. For an OR gate G with nonempty diagonal support, each nonempty-diagonal parent must be an unmarked monomial. Otherwise choose a diagonal where this parent is true, making its marker H and the later gate G simultaneously true. All other parents are false constants. Mark the result with G itself and take M to be the empty conjunction. This proves the induction and also shows that the truncated value of any OR marker is a disjunction of at most two raw monomials. Thus each truncated wire has a DNF with at most two raw monomials; false is the empty disjunction.

Assign each accepted off-diagonal cell to the earliest original gate g that is true there and has empty diagonal support. The output has these properties, so such a gate exists. For this cell, replacing earlier diagonal-empty wires by false changes nothing, by induction over the prefix. Therefore evaluating g's actual operation on its truncated inputs still accepts this cell. A true OR gate with empty diagonal support would need an earlier true diagonal-empty parent. A true unary gate has the same obstruction. A nullary OR is false, while a nullary AND has full diagonal support. A raw input has a nonempty diagonal support. The first gate g must therefore be an AND of two parents.

Now fix one such g. Evaluate its AND on its truncated parents without replacing g itself by false. Its result is a disjunction of at most four raw monomials and has empty diagonal support. Every monomial in that DNF also has empty diagonal support. A satisfiable monomial on the promised inputs mentions at most one distinct left wire and one distinct right wire. A monomial that does not constrain both sides is true somewhere on the diagonal; one that constrains both sides specifies a single ordered cell. Thus each monomial covers at most one off-diagonal cell and the fixed g receives at most four cells.

Consequently at least ceil(n(n−1)/4) AND gates are needed. When n=12 and there are 22 OR gates, the verified per-diagonal premise of at least 21 false OR gates guarantees the hypothesis. This gives ≥33 AND gates and ≥55 gates total in that case. The n=12 consequence uses the separate false-OR counting theorem. This proof is a mathematical argument and has not been kernel-checked.

## Consequence for the 40-gate shard and Lean status

The compiled Lean theorem `PEqualNP.Research.MissingCell.crown_twelve_falseOrCount` in [FalseOrCount.lean](../geometry/FalseOrCount.lean) gives, for every represented 12-crown, at least 21 OR gates returning false on each diagonal. Its hypotheses use the repository's actual circuit semantics, the monotone binary gate condition and representation on one-hot inputs. In particular at least 21 OR gates must occur in total. For any circuit with 21 or 22 OR gates the counting theorem implies the one-active-OR premise, so this argument excludes both cases from a circuit of at most 40 gates. Shard 3 requires at least 11 AND gates, hence at most 29 OR gates. Combining these facts leaves only 23 through 29 OR gates. The marker induction, four-edge bound, and resulting 33-AND lower bound are mathematical proofs in this note; the local compiled Lean result establishes the false-OR count alone. No complete refutation of shard 3 follows.

## Independent finite check

[one-active-check.cpp](one-active-check.cpp) exhaustively enumerates all fan-in-zero, one and two AND/OR gates in topological order through four gates on the 3-crown promise and through three gates on the 4-crown promise. It permits shared and repeated inputs. Commutative binary operand order is ignored. Only prefixes violating the one-active-OR hypothesis are pruned, since no extension can remove a counted true OR. This enumerator uses truth tables, independently of the symbolic marker proof. Its candidate monomials are the empty set, full grid, a row, a column, or one cell. It checks that every truncated surviving wire is the union of at most two monomials, that truncation preserves all diagonal values and can only lower other values, and that each earliest empty-diagonal gate receiving a new cell is AND and covers at most four cells after its parents are truncated. The finite tests use valid wire references. Invalid reads in the circuit model have value false and are dealt with by the mathematical argument.

With assertions enabled, compile with `g++ -std=c++20 -O2 one-active-check.cpp -o one-active-check` and run `./one-active-check`. The [recorded run](one-active-check.log) checked 18,405,773 length-four prefixes at n=3 and 864,698 length-three prefixes at n=4, as well as every shorter prefix. The largest charge was two and four respectively. Sharpness at four is witnessed by (L₀ ∨ L₁) ∧ (R₂ ∨ R₃), whose two OR gates have disjoint diagonal supports. This is a local finite check of the structural lemma, not an exhaustive search on 12 vertices.
