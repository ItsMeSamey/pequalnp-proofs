# Restrictions on adjacent small branching pure groups at size 34 with eight ANDs

The regular fourteen-input circuit has 26 ORs. As in small-branches.md, a branching pure group of raw size two or three has exactly two direct OR readers, since its deletion exhausts the total-gate allowance at arity twelve or eleven. This part of the argument uses only size, not the nine-AND hypothesis of that file.

Two branching doubletons cannot share a raw. Erasing the three labels of their union would incur at least six raw occurrences and two dark fanout surpluses, exceeding the permitted seven gate losses. Pure groups sharing two raw labels are impossible by simplicity.

Now let U,V be incident branching groups, each of size two or three, and S their union. Besides U and V, every completely erased pure group W consists of one U-exclusive and one V-exclusive label. It cannot contain the shared label (already used twice), nor two labels from either original group. There are at most min(|U|−1,|V|−1) such doubletons, since each exclusive label has at most one further raw occurrence. In the two-plus-three case every W is private, either by the exhausted dark fanout budget below or because it would neighbor the branching doubleton. In the three-plus-three case the two-plus-three exclusion below makes every W private. Two such private doubletons cannot feed the same AND: if disjoint, erasing one pair already costs five gates, and the other private root dies as an additional gate; an overlap instead permits a factoring saving.

Before there is a dark AND after zeroing S, every further dark OR group must compute the disjunction of the whole S. A private W cannot be an OR source. A group taking U through OR-only gates cannot contain a raw of U by absorption. It cannot inject a raw y from V outside U either: on the common singleton x the group and V are already positive, so adding y would be invisible at its only two occurrences. The same holds in the other direction. Combining U and V with no outside source gives precisely the disjunction of S. A second realization of that wire would itself be redundant in the original circuit. Proper internal nodes of the U,V,W trees have no external users.

There can be no initial both-dark AND. Direct uses of U and V are all ORs; two private W operands are excluded above, while any dark operand conjoined with the disjunction of S is redundant. Indeed every wire dark after the restriction is pointwise bounded by that disjunction on the full cube.

For sizes two and three, erasing the four labels leaves at most ten gate losses by C(10)=24. All ten are already debited by the eight raw occurrences and the two fanout surpluses. This forbids any one-sided dark AND, and the preceding argument excludes any initial both-dark product. All ten losses are ORs, leaving at most sixteen ORs and eight ANDs on ten inputs. The OR-floor secondary minimum requires nine ANDs. Thus these two branching group sizes cannot be incident.

For sizes three and three, the twelve already debited losses (ten raws and two fanout surpluses) allow at most one further loss by C(9)=21. If no one-sided AND occurs, no AND becomes dark. If there is a one-sided AND H, every dark nonraw wire other than U,V has fanout one, by the exhausted debit. After H, the subsequent dark nonraw outputs outside the pre-existing dark pure groups or OR(S) must lie on its single strand. A both-dark AND on this strand cannot take another strand wire (the earlier wire would fan out), or OR(S) (redundant), or U or V (only OR readers). Its other operand must be an unused private W. There are at most two W, so at most three dark ANDs are removed including H. Therefore any thirteen-loss constant propagation would give a minimum 21-gate nine-input circuit retaining at least five ANDs, contrary to the certified four-AND composition. Exactly twelve gates disappear in constant propagation, with no one-sided AND and hence with no disappearing AND. The resulting nine-input minor has fourteen ORs and eight ANDs, the optimum counts at the OR floor.
