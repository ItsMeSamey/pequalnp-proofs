# A branching flag in the five-plus-six table

In a minimal regular circuit some pure flag branches. Otherwise the earliest AND consumes two private pure groups. An overlapping pair factors. For two disjoint groups take the smaller, of size k, and erase its raws. The raw-group trees and external raw occurrences save 2k−1 ORs; the clean product and collector cost two further gates and the private partner tree becomes unused. If k≤3, even its root exceeds the permissible size reduction. If both have size four all three partner ORs disappear, exceeding the ten-gate gap. Since the groups are disjoint none of those tree ORs is already an external contraction.

Set a proposed branching pure flag to zero. At least two physical ORs simplify, including when some direct readers are ANDs: otherwise the unique first-OR frontier permits the factorization of eight-five-511.md. The surviving graph consequently has at most 15 gates and seven ORs. Every existing spoiler witness for a different vertex on the same side persists, since only that vertex and an opposite edge occur in its witness. Recomputing virtual-star spoiler requirements in this smaller decoder can add marked vertices. A connected remaining block H costs at least |H|−1 ORs to erase while opposite positives survive. It costs all |H| if two adjacent vertices are marked by choosing that edge last; marked isolated vertices also each cost one. The other side still needs its local OR floor. The same argument can run in reverse, recomputing opposite spoiler marks against any retained connected block; the cost of erasing the entire opposite shore is used only if at least two retained edges remain. Residual blocks outside the selected block are peeled separately.
