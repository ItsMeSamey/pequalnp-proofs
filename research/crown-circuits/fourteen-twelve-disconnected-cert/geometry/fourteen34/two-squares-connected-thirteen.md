# Two disjoint four-cycles require at least six OR gates

Assume a monotone decoder with at most five OR gates rejects every single edge of two disjoint four-cycles and accepts each union of two distinct edges. Among all such decoders minimize total gate count. Each vertex y is essential on its own square, as witnessed by a path y−a−b testing ab against ab∪ya. Some positive on the other square survives y=0, hence fixing y false removes at least one OR.

Every y spoils a singleton edge on the opposite square: adding y alone makes that edge positive. Otherwise erase the unique nonneighbor of y on its own square, costing one OR as above. Pin y true, use the two neighbors as two independent raw inputs, and retain the opposite square as a fixed flag bank. Old singleton edges remain negative by harmlessness; the two new singleton inputs form negative star edges; every mixed or unmixed pair contains two edges. The opposite square requires at least one OR, since a conjunction accepting all its positive triples would include no flag and accept negative edges. Its flag bank has no common flag on all singleton edges. Two applications of the independent-raw increment require at least five ORs, while only four remain.

Consequently erasing any one flag saves at most one OR. After its erasure, the other three flags on its square still have their own individual witnesses on the opposite square. Erase them successively. Each requires at least one fresh OR, since the other square still accepts a positive pair with that flag false. The opposite square itself still needs one OR. This leaves at most one of the original five available for the first erasure. By gate minimality and the unique-first-OR factorization in cycle-or-floor.md, each of the eight flags is single-read.

An earliest AND of two flags in one square cannot convey the single-spoiler witness of one while the other is false. A cross-square AND loses the local two-edge witness when all flags of the other square are false. An earliest OR joining same-square flags hides an edge extension: if xy is an edge use xz versus xz∪xy, with z the other neighbor of x; if x and y are opposite use xz versus xz∪zy at their common neighbor z. An earliest cross-square OR identifies its two single-read flags to one formal flag. The simple quotient graph on seven vertices is connected and its merged vertex has degree four. The quotient decoder works by lifting each tested set of edges to the original host; the only information its two old flags supplied is their disjunction. The connected layer bound is 7+4−5=6 ORs, while this quotient has at most four. These cases exhaust earliest gates and prove the lower bound.

For a connected thirteen-flag regular fourteen-edge host with eight ANDs, connected-large-balanced.md leaves just C4+C4+P4 and C4+C4+K1,3 after the unique cubic branching flag is erased. In either case retain the two four-cycles and peel the other four-vertex component to an independent raw, which adds four to the decoder OR lower bound by independent-raw-or-increment.md. The residual needs at least ten ORs and has at most nine. Thus the connected thirteen-flag case is excluded.

This is a source-level proof; the six-OR floor applies without any cap on AND count.
