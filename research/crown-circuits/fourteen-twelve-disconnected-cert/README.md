# All disconnected twelve-flag hosts

Under the established regular fourteen-raw, 34-gate, eight-AND incidence theorem, this excludes every disconnected host with twelve flags. The twelve-flag decoder has ten ORs. This packet is separate from the connected twelve-flag analysis.

There are fifteen possible disconnected order partitions in the component-degree ledger. In a component of order n, the presence of a connected archived h-vertex obstruction requiring h−1 ORs gives n−1 by connected peeling. A quartic also gives n−1 by the star bound. Unless a flag covers every edge, fix one independent edge per other component after peeling that component to its edge (s−2 ORs), then identify the two edge flags. The independent-raw increment costs two further ORs per edge relative to the retained task. A retained component needing n−1 thus gives at least eleven ORs in total. The exception when a quartic flag covers every edge is precisely a bare K1,4. Archived obstructors surviving the loader must be connected and satisfy the h−1 test. None is a star; the graph on h vertices then has no flag covering all edges.

The generator takes connected subcubic obstruction-free atlas graphs through order seven plus the bare K1,4. For orders eight, nine and ten, attach one new vertex of degree one, two or three to distinct vertices of smaller degree, checking the archived obstructions again. Every connected graph can lose a noncut vertex leaving a connected graph. The obstruction-free subgraph property is hereditary, so induction gives all necessary candidates. The upper cap of 13 edges is sufficient because a disconnected host has at least one further edge. Weisfeiler–Lehman hashes only bucket isomorphism tests; graphs are identified only after an isomorphism test. Component products are unordered and must total twelve vertices and fourteen edges.

The 106 retained hosts split as 9 of orders 2+10, 17 of 3+9, 56 of 4+8, 8 of 5+7, 8 of 3+4+5, 7 of 4+4+4, and 1 of 2+2+4+4. No other order partition survives. Components through order seven are given by NetworkX atlas ID. Larger components are represented by graph6.

Some pure flag w branches, by the private-first-AND argument. No retained component is quartic, as the ledger also verifies. Thus every branch is a doubleton or triple with two distinct OR readers. Set w and its t pendant neighbors false. The pendant flags are mixed; each final raw injector is distinct from the w readers by absorption. At least 2+t ORs vanish. Each surviving nontrivial component outside a chosen base can pay its full number of flags by peeling to a representative edge and applying the independent-raw increment. If a retained base of order h needs h−2 ORs and has no flag true on every singleton, this requires (11−t)−2=9−t ORs after the pin, but only 8−t remain. The marked checker uses connected graphs with a cubic flag not covering all edges, and C5 (its five length-two paths require distinct minterms, so at least three ORs). This excludes 104 of the 106 rows for every branch placement.

In the two remaining rows the only possible marks are the four vertices of K4. For K4+C8, the untouched eight-cycle needs six ORs by peeling to the six-vertex path of floor four. For K4+C4+C4, erasing the branch leaves a triangle and two squares with eight ORs. The two-square task needs six ORs by the companion proof. No individual flag covers all square edges. Peel the triangle to one edge (one OR) and identify that edge's flags as an independent raw (two more ORs). Nine ORs are required. This closes both exceptions.
