# Joint budget for the remaining five-vertex component

In each of the three smallest surviving hosts, the five-vertex component is one of the masks 255, 507 and 511 in the original pair order, with bit j(j−1)/2+i for edge i<j. Its decoder requires at least four ORs. The archived 3-OR negative graph 237 is a subgraph of 255 and 511 with its labeling unchanged; swapping labels 2 and 3 maps it to 243, a submask of 507. Satisfying every negative singleton edge and positive edge pair of a graph also satisfies those requirements on any subgraph. The independent K5 result and this subgraph result both come from the graph-cut proof ledgers in the published thirteen-input packet. The archived four-OR feasibility for all three graphs grants arbitrary conjunctions between ORs.

The full nine-flag decoder has seven ORs and eight ANDs. Pinning the four-vertex connected component false costs at least three ORs by component peeling. Consequently exactly four ORs remain and the three removed ORs exhaust the OR debit. At least one AND disappears as well. Indeed, all four erased flags are essential (for each vertex, take its incident edge and another edge at the other endpoint). Suppose the erasure removed no AND. Let P count OR gates with two zero operands and B count OR gates with exactly one. All four pinned flags and all P zero gate outputs have at least one physical user in the original live decoder. Every input slot they occupy belongs to one of these ORs, since a zero operand at an AND would also eliminate that gate. Hence 2P+B≥4+P, so P+B≥4 ORs would vanish, impossible. The residual five-vertex circuit therefore has exactly four ORs and at most seven ANDs.

For a fanout refinement, let 4+b be the total number of occurrences of the four pinned flag inputs. Let Z2 count gates with two zero operands, Z1 count ANDs with one zero operand, and B count ORs with one zero operand, evaluating all other formal inputs at one. The residual output is nonzero. Every fixed-zero gate output has a user, so 2Z2+Z1+B≥(4+b)+(Z1+Z2). The number of directly eliminated gates is at least Z2+Z1+B≥4+b+Z1. This bounds the residual size by 11−b−Z1. Its endpoint tests still distinguish the original three finite instances; they have not been jointly decided.
