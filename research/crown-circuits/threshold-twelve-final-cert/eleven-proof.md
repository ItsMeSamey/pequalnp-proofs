# Eleven-input monotone threshold two requires at least 26 gates

Suppose a minimum fan-in-two monotone AND/OR circuit on eleven inputs has at most 25 gates. The established at-most-six-AND threshold census excludes up to six ANDs at this size. The physical lower bound of 2n−4=18 ORs leaves at most seven. Hence there are exactly 25 gates, seven ANDs and eighteen ORs. Work in a minimum clean-terminal representative, as in the lower-bound packages. Lower bounds on ten, nine and eight inputs are 23, 21, and 19 respectively.

**Raw fanout.** A single false specialization saves at most two gates. Thus every raw has at most two readers. If a twice-used x enters an AND, the AND becomes zero and a further gate at its consumer vanishes. Such a consumer cannot itself read x, since combining x with x∧P would have been redundant. A once-used x entering x∧P is also impossible by the three-gate direct deletion in the direct-specialization argument of algebra/reverse/eleven-threshold-and-direct.md (lines 19–21), including elimination of the maximal OR signal: P is the disjunction of all other inputs; its signal, the AND, and a downstream simplification disappear. A once-used x entering x∨P would force P to reject every singleton, else that singleton and its pair with x are indistinguishable. A singleton-clean signal cannot enter the dirty gate x∨P in clean-terminal normal form. Therefore every raw has exactly two readers and both are ORs.

**Short pure signals.** Set a raw subset S false. Call a signal dark if it is zero when every remaining raw is true. Such signals become constant zero by monotonicity. Counting pins leaving all dark sources and subtracting pins entering dark gates shows that the number of eliminated dark gates and boundary ORs equals

    Σ_{x∈S} degree(x) + Σ_{dark gates g}(fanout(g)−1)
                          + #{dark ANDs with exactly one dark operand}.

Each surviving gate incident to a dark pin is a bypassable OR with one such input. The output is nonzero for all restrictions used below. For |S|=1,2,3 the three smaller-arity lower bounds allow saving at most 2|S|, so neither excess term can be positive. In particular a purely raw disjunction with at most three labels has fanout one. No AND can be dark. Prove that by induction on |S|. The empty case holds in a constant-free minimum. An earliest dark AND must have two dark operands. Every dark signal before it is an OR of some raw subset of S, allowing a sole raw as a singleton set. A smaller operand subset would already darken the same AND after that smaller deletion, contrary to induction. Hence the operands are both the OR of S, making the AND redundant.

**Group the OR trees.** Absorb every OR with fanout one whose sole consumer is an OR into that consumer. The resulting maximal OR trees partition the original ORs, leaving each root as an AND input, the output, or a shared signal. For a tree w let k_w count raw leaves and t_w count other operand occurrences, including shared earlier OR outputs. Its physical cost is k_w+t_w−1. In total Σk_w=22. A repeated raw within one tree could be removed by idempotence, saving a gate. Thus each raw belongs to two distinct OR groups. No two raws have the same pair of groups: after setting one raw true both groups already return true, and adding the other cannot change the output. The raw labels form eleven distinct edges in a simple graph on the groups.

A pure group has t=0 and computes the disjunction of k distinct raws. It cannot be the threshold output. If k≤3 then its output has fanout one and cannot enter an AND by the dark-gate argument. It would have been absorbed into its OR consumer. Thus every pure group contains at least four labels. Let p be their count. Summing the original OR costs gives

    18 = 22−p + Σ_{mixed groups}(t−1).

Therefore p≥4 while 4p≤22 implies p≤5. The pure vertices have total degree at least 4p. With e internal pure-pure edges their total degree is at most 11+e, since every remaining edge contributes at most one. Thus e≥4p−11. For both p=4 and p=5 their induced graph is complete apart from at most one absent edge.

**Residual function.** Keep only variables corresponding to internal pure-pure edges. Every other raw is fixed false. The pure group indicators record which vertices of that induced graph touch a selected edge. All direct raw operands at mixed groups have become zero. At p=4 all mixed groups have t=1 and so become identities. At p=5 exactly one has t=2 and all others have t=1. On the formal pure group indicators there are respectively zero or at most one residual OR gate. An AND circuit with at most one OR has a disjunctive normal form with at most two monomials even with fanout: all pre-OR signals are monomials, and the repeated OR factor in downstream products is idempotent. With zero OR there is at most one monomial.

Each internal edge alone induces its two endpoints and must be rejected; every vertex belongs to such an edge, ruling out monomials of size zero or one. Every triple of vertices contains at least two internal edges because at most one edge is missing. Picking two of these edges realizes exactly the triple of indicators and must be accepted. A monomial of size at least two covers at most p−2 triples. At p=4 one monomial covers at most two of four triples. At p=5 two monomials cover at most six of ten. These contradictions establish the 26-gate lower bound. No sharp upper bound is asserted.
