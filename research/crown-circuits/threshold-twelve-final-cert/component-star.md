# OR savings from the components surrounding a pure group

In a minimum binary monotone threshold-two circuit with two distinct OR readers per raw input, flatten any single-use OR into its OR consumer. The raw inputs form a simple graph on OR groups. Let p count the pure groups, C count the components of their induced graph, and r sum t−1 over mixed groups with t nonraw operands. Choose a pure group U of raw degree k with d pure neighbors. Let b count other pure components incident to a mixed neighbor of U. Then k ≤ r+4−p+C−b.

Use independent input signals for the pure outputs. At each mixed neighbor W of U keep only its one raw injection UW. Set all other mixed raw injections false. This costs at most r+k−d binary ORs. Leave U's formal signal independent throughout peeling. Any edge BW between a pure B and such a W can be simulated by the B pin and the injection UW: directly forcing the same OR group via either raw has the same downstream effect. The abstract pin U may stay false. Edges within the pure graph are simulated by their endpoint pins. The representation also works for unions of such edges, by induction through the remaining gates.

Setting an essential variable false eliminates at least one OR if the remaining output is nonconstant. Follow a live path from that input. Zero propagates through ANDs up to the first OR, or would force the output to zero. Subsequent eliminations charge distinct gates. A nonconstant restricted star survives every step.

In U's component, peel pure vertices farther than one from U in decreasing distance. For V whose path toward U starts V–W–Z, the singleton WZ and pair WZ,WV differ only at V. In another component touching a mixed neighbor of U, peel a spanning tree toward an endpoint B of B–W–U, using the same separating probes. B itself goes last by comparing UW and the pair UW,BW. All earlier pins have value zero in both probes. In every remaining component of size t≥2 peel to a last edge VW. Compare a retained star singleton with the same input together with VW. Pinning both V,W false saves at least one OR because at least one pin is essential in the two steps. An isolated remaining vertex contributes zero. In total these operations eliminate at least p−C−d+b ORs.

Set U true and keep its k star variables at their pure neighbor pins or mixed injections. Every nonempty star assignment agrees with the original threshold function. Monotonicity from a singleton gives rejection at zero. The residual requires at least 2k−4 ORs, proving the bound. The separate early-injection inequality k+max(d,2)≤r+4 can be used at the same time without adding overlapping savings.
