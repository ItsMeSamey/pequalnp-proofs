# Independent audit of the eight-AND threshold exclusion

The endpoint is a contradiction for a minimum, clean-terminal, binary monotone circuit computing the twelve-input threshold-two function, with at most 28 total gates and eight ANDs. The proof uses size lower bounds 26, 23, 21 and 19 at arities eleven through eight, and the physical OR lower bound 2n−4. The following checks concern the circuit argument, not a new Lean certificate.

The eleven-input floor permits only two gate savings on single-raw deletion. A raw with two readers cannot feed an AND, since the downstream simplification of that AND is distinct from its other reader by irredundancy. A sole AND reader is ruled out by the direct three-gate deletion; a sole OR reader would require a singleton-clean other operand to feed a mixed OR, contrary to the terminal-clean form. The raw degree is therefore exactly two, entirely into OR groups. Within a flattened private OR tree a repeated raw is idempotent. Two different raws with identical incident group pairs cannot be distinguished by comparing a singleton with their pair. This is a simple graph on the groups.

For a pure group U of two to four labels, deleting those variables allows 2k+1 removals and its own branching or its sole AND consumer contributes one beyond the 2k raw-pin charges. The doubleton proof uses the residual ten-input threshold at exactly 23. If the other doubleton survives with one raw, the exact lower bound on nine inputs forbids a raw-fed AND there. If both its variables survive, their second readers are mandatory because one otherwise changes nothing on the other's singleton. The eight-input floor then leaves no allowance for its one-sided dark AND. Two single-use cherries at the same AND either share a raw and factor, or are disjoint and one becomes newly unused when the other is deleted.

A branching small pure U exhausts the single surplus. No one-sided dark AND is possible. Before any earliest fully dark AND, dark OR wires have support contained in U. A first dark OR outside its private tree must combine two of U's raw labels, impossible by graph simplicity, or receive U itself, in which case it merely recomputes U. Proper private tree nodes have no outgoing occurrence outside their tree. Thus no fully dark AND can obtain two distinct dark inputs; no raw feeds an AND. Removing the counted gates requires deleting ORs only. The eight ANDs can be left in the residual circuit even if some are now redundant. The resulting OR count violates the 2n−4 floor. If every pure group is small, no shared OR can occur before the first AND: the earliest one would have a pure flattened group. The first AND would then read two pure single-use groups, prohibited by factoring on overlap or by pruning a stranded operand on disjointness.

For a large U retain just its k incident edge variables and hardwire U to true. On all nonempty assignments the hardwire makes no change. The empty assignment still rejects by monotonicity and the preserved singleton rejections. The entire private tree of U can be discarded. The d pure neighbors now output their separate variables. Each remaining variable x has just one incidence, into a distinct mixed group. Order these groups by their physical roots; inputs from other groups are always earlier, and no strict private tree node feeds any external consumer. If the group is x∨Q then Q is independent of x, and no other group receives x directly. For any y≠x, Q(y)=1 would make y and x+y indistinguishable at the only injection of x and hence at the output. Thus Q(y)=0 for every such singleton. If Q depends on at most one previously available variable, it is identically false, including when its evaluation uses hardwired constants. This saves at least one OR at each of the first max(0,2−d) injections, since mixed groups have at least one nonraw slot. The residual threshold-two circuit on k inputs consequently has at most r+k−max(d,2) ORs.

The cost identity yields r=p−4 and the star inequality gives k+max(d,2)≤p. At least one k is five or larger. Raw-degree sum 24 and at most one doubleton force p≤7; the star inequality forces p≥7. Hence p=7, k=5, d≤2. At least three incidences from this vertex end at mixed groups, but the pure degree sum is at least 22, leaving at most two mixed incidences. This is a contradiction.
