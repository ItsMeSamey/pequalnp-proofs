# Finite record space for the fourteen-input, size-34, eight-AND search

The record space describes binary monotone AND/OR circuits with free fanout. The target rejects the zero input and all fourteen singletons and accepts all 91 unordered pairs. Monotonicity then forces all higher-weight inputs positive. A positive witness is independently expanded and replayed on all 16,384 assignments.

## Physical record and inverse

A row is either a binary AND or an OR group. Contract an OR output into an OR user only when the output has precisely one use, counted in physical input slots. Treat observation of the output as a use. Repeat along all such edges. Each resulting OR group is a binary tree, with a unique root, and its terms are raw occurrences and outputs of earlier rows. A shared OR is always a root. An unshared OR feeding an AND is also a root. Put each AND in its own row. Order rows by original root index; internal OR nodes never have external consumers.

Define a record by the fourteen raw labels; the row list; for each row its type, raw-label terms and earlier-row terms; and the output row. Multiplicities are part of the initial extraction. For an AND the raw list is empty and there are exactly two earlier-row terms. An OR with d raw terms and t nonraw terms has d+t≥2 and costs d+t−1 physical ORs. With two regular OR occurrences for each raw, its two groups are distinct and different raw labels cannot have the same pair of groups: repeated terms inside a tree are idempotent, and if x,y are both in two trees, factoring one shared x∨y saves an OR. Global minimality excludes each redundancy. A correct 34-gate candidate would be globally size-minimal by C(14)≥34. The record of a physically regular candidate therefore has a simple graph G with one edge per raw and one nonisolated vertex per raw-bearing OR group. Its degree equals the number of raw terms in that group. For a non-minimal test circuit one may retain repeated terms while testing the extractor.

To invert the representation, associate each OR row as any binary tree on its terms, using exactly d+t−1 OR gates; preserve all outgoing references to its root. A mixed row can use a raw-only bank followed by an OR with its nonraw expression. Its bank root is then an internal single-use gate, not another shared hub. A raw-free OR row has at least two nonraw terms. Evaluating row by row proves functional equality to the original circuit, and summing tree costs proves equality of sizes and AND counts. Every regular candidate maps to such a record; arbitrary records can be checked by expansion. There is no ordering requirement putting the outputs of mixed rows before the ANDs: a mixed row may receive a dirty AND as a parent.

## Integer budgets

Let q be the number of raw-bearing rows, p the number of pure rows (no nonraw terms), and z the number of auxiliary raw-free OR rows. Each mixed row has t≥1; each auxiliary row has t≥2. Its contribution to the cost below is t−1. From 28 raw terms and 26 OR gates:
```
sum(t−1 over mixed and auxiliary OR rows) = p−2
sum(t over all OR rows)                  = q+z−2
2 ≤ p ≤ 14,     0 ≤ z ≤ p−2,     q+z ≤ 26.
```
There are exactly eight AND rows, hence at most 34 rows in total. Every non-output row must eventually reach the observed row. If f counts outgoing input slots (plus a single observer use at output), then sum(f−1) over all rows is exactly 7: earlier-row pins number q+z−2+16. Thus at most seven rows are physically shared. A physical OR hub is a group root, raw-bearing OR raw-free, with f≥2. A shared AND consumes surplus too. An auxiliary OR is not necessarily shared; the final collector can be auxiliary and unshared. A branching pure flag is only one type of physical hub. Receiver-graph vertices are not a substitute for the list of physical hubs. No type is inferred from graph degree alone other than that degree one cannot be pure.

## Conditional canonical graph reductions

Every putative size-34 eight-AND solution admits a regular representative and the clean-terminal rearrangement used in the existing gate-elimination arguments. It suffices for nonexistence to exhaust these representatives; structural filters asserted only after this normalization are not assertions about every ordering of every other representative. The unfiltered extraction and inverse above remain valid independently of those filters.

For canonical representatives, the maximum graph degree is four. A connected graph has maximum degree three by the receiver-layer bound q+d−5≤q−2. At least one pure flag has degree ≥3: if all are doubletons, their distinct shared private partners and branching doubletons use p≤7 surplus units. At most one doubleton branches. The c clean terminals satisfy c≤p−1; if no group branches there are at least p private clean terms, and if one branches, the remaining at most six private doubletons leave two labels outside whose pair needs another clean terminal. This contradicts c≤p−1 in either case. In particular q≤25 (at least two nonleaves, one at degree ≥3). Also q≥9: at eight vertices a connected subcubic graph has at most twelve edges, while any disconnected simple graph of degree ≤4 with positive degrees has at most thirteen by splitting 6+2, 5+3, 4+4 or refining those splits; fewer vertices give no additional edges.

Each pure group is private (f=1, directly reading into an AND) or branching (f≥2). Branching doubletons/triples have two OR readers. At most one branching doubleton exists, and it cannot be adjacent to a branching triple. Pure doubletons are clean at their private AND. A private flag of size three may be dirty, as can larger private flags: do not assign cleanliness purely from fanout. Shared mixed/auxiliary ORs and shared ANDs remain possible. Additional star inequalities or semantic constraints can be applied after their actual operands and fanouts are known.

The file ../../../fourteen-eight-cases/component_rows.py (relative to this directory) generates the coarse component-degree multisets. Its 10,584 rows are a superset before wiring: they are degree sequences, not graph isomorphism types. Within each row enumerate every realization of every component (or every labeled adjacency matrix), not only one graph having the listed degrees. Repeated component shapes can receive different role decorations. Isolated flags do not belong to the raw graph; a zero-raw physical OR is instead an auxiliary row.

## Enumeration and coverage

1. Enumerate component edge sets realizing each admitted multiset. Within each finite simple graph assign raw labels by its edges, fixing any numbering because threshold is invariant under input permutation. Enumerate every pure/mixed vertex coloring consistent with the degree and budget conditions. In an elementary implementation all labeled colorings can be tried; graph or component symmetries may only discard actual isomorphic colored realizations.
2. Choose z and the term counts for every mixed and auxiliary row from the integer budgets. Equivalently reserve one nonraw predecessor per mixed row and two per auxiliary row, then distribute the p−2−z remaining tokens among these rows in all possible ways. Include z distinguishable auxiliary ORs and eight distinguishable ANDs. Pure flags have zero nonraw parents.
3. Enumerate every topological row order. Choose the required number of prior **row outputs** for every nonraw predecessor list, in particular two for every AND. Sorting operand lists removes commutative symmetry. Repeated operands can be retained as an overgrant or rejected by idempotence at this globally minimal size. Choose an observed output; when all rows are live it is necessarily last. Compute physical usage counts before labeling any row a hub. One may discard an unshared OR feeding a sole OR because it would have been contracted in the canonical record. Every raw edge still enters both endpoint groups exactly once.
4. Evaluate all singleton and pair patterns and the zero input. For every survivor expand to physical gates, verify all 34 nodes are live, verify chronological pins and exactly eight ANDs/26 ORs, and replay all 16,384 inputs. A claimed exhaustive negative needs reproducible coverage of every component realization, coloring, cost distribution and DAG; the coarse row count alone is insufficient.

Proof of coverage: take a regular representative of any candidate, form its maximal OR groups, record every edge, row and parent from the contraction, and permute labels into the enumeration's chosen edge order. Step 1 includes its realized graph and coloring; the exact row-cost identity puts its tuple in step 2. Original chronological root order and actual physical uses supply a choice in step 3. By induction along those rows step 4 has exactly its original output. Hence eliminating all these records eliminates every canonical candidate. Any relaxation may overgrant free AND closure, omit type filters, or allow extra parent alternatives if every actual choice is retained. A relaxed positive is only a candidate for physical reconstruction; miscounting a mixed bank as a hub, suppressing auxiliary shared ORs, or identifying degree sequences as graphs would instead remove legitimate record types.

## Extractor control and dependencies

records.py extracts and expands records, verifies the algebraic ledger and performs full truth replay. On calculus/fourteen34/tap-control35.json it reproduces a 35-gate four-AND control on 16,384 inputs with eight pure raw groups and nine auxiliary OR groups. This tests extraction and inversion, not exhaustion of the 34-gate target. No large search has been run by this specification.

Dependencies are geometry/fourteen34/eight-and-high-degree.md (maximum degree), degree/t14-34/eight-and-{one-doubleton,overlapping-branches}.md (small branching flags), geometry/eleven27-seven/receiver-layers.md (connected/component cut), and the published regularization and smaller threshold minima. The star debit refinements in geometry/fourteen34/quartet-hub-debits.md are optional post-wiring filters.
