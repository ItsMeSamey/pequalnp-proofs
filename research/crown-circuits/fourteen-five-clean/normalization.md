# Reduction to the live regular representative

The single prerequisite for this reduction is the established lower bound of 32 binary monotone gates for thirteen-input threshold two. Suppose a fourteen-input circuit of at most 34 gates and at most five ANDs, all singleton-clean, computes threshold two. Choose one of minimum size within this class. Dead gates, constants, duplicate operands and gate identities may be eliminated. These changes preserve the Boolean functions of surviving gates and the singleton-clean hypothesis. Any three-gate saving after setting one input to zero would give a thirteen-input circuit of size at most 31, contradiction.

Each raw x has at most two distinct readers, since specializing x to zero eliminates every direct reader. If x has two readers and enters an AND D=x∧P, then a direct consumer of D must be distinct from both readers: D cannot be the output, and if its consumer were the other reader of x their combined gates would compute just x or D, an identity. Eliminating the x readers and simplifying at that consumer removes at least three gates. A sole AND reader D is also impossible. Here P is independent of x. Each other singleton y forces P(y)=1 since otherwise y and x+y would be indistinguishable. Thus P is the OR of all thirteen remaining raws. Setting x to zero removes D and a downstream consumer. P's own gate disappears as well: if still needed, override it to true after the restriction. Every nonempty input already makes it true, and the zero input still rejects by monotonicity below any rejecting singleton. Its defining gate is distinct from D and its consumer, even if it had already become dead.

If the only reader is H=x∨P, then P is independent of x and rejects all other singletons, since any singleton y accepted by P would be indistinguishable from x+y. Redirect H's readers to x and append one final OR of the former output with P. When P is false nothing changes; when true, at least two inputs are set and the final OR returns the required value. Each previous AND's signal can only decrease, and the new gate is an OR. Thus all ANDs remain singleton-clean, with the original size and AND count or fewer after simplification. By minimality any size-decreasing simplification is already impossible. The raw x now has the readers formerly belonging to H. The other raw fanouts are unchanged: P is a nonconstant internal wire as it rejects all singletons; if it were constant or H were the old output the circuit would have been reducible or would accept x alone.

If x is still singly used, repeat through its next OR on the former live DAG. A single AND reader has already been ruled out, and x cannot directly enter a newly appended terminal OR because the singleton would accept. This procedure strictly advances beyond an old OR on each repetition, and must end with two OR readers. Do this for every remaining singly used raw. The result is a live regular circuit covered by circuit-bridge.md. Hence, given the thirteen-input bound, every size-at-most-34 circuit with at most five ANDs computing fourteen-input threshold two must have an AND that accepts some singleton.
