# Three pair helpers, three triples, one quartet

Let l be the number of receivers of width at most two, n₃ the width-three count and a the full count. Three low receivers cannot fit since they occupy at least 24 atoms, leaving eight for eleven other receivers. Over the three pair-helper faces there are 24 atom incidences. A nonquartet full requires at least two distinct contained pair helpers: two triples based on the same pair span at most four axes. Let s be the number of full points covered by just one pair helper. Each must use the quartet, so s≤2. Every full has at least one pair-helper ancestor. Each width-four target charges at least two atoms and each width-three target four, all in one containing pair helper. Thus the high-target incidence lower bound is 2(14−l)+2n₃−s. The case l=0 is impossible.

At l=1 equality forces s=2 and n₃=0. The low target meets no helper, every doubly covered atom is full, and triple coverage is impossible. The quartet is spent on two singly covered fulls. If two overlapping helper faces shared one signed coordinate, their common four atoms would all have to be full. The third helper is absent throughout this intersection (no triple coverage). The active helpers specify just three axes between them. Generating all four choices for the two free axes needs four triples, one supplying each sign of each free axis; the available three do not suffice. Thus all double intersections are of disjoint coordinate pairs, containing two atoms each. At most two such edges exist among three pairs. Hence a≤2+4=6 and at least seven width-four targets are required. None can use two pair helpers since all double-covered atoms are already full. Each must use a triple, each of the three triples supplying at most two disjoint width-four targets. Contradiction.

Thus l=2. Both low targets have width two, since width one plus width two leave just eight atoms for twelve targets, and volume gives a≥8. If a pair helper meets a low target, their intersection has at least two atoms. This uses all available slack in 24≥24+2n₃−s; s=2, n₃=0, and exactly one helper meets one low in exactly two atoms. Thus all double-covered atoms are outside the low faces, necessarily full with no triple coverage, and both quartet fulls are singly covered. The preceding four-atom intersection argument gives a≤6, contradiction. So every helper misses both low targets.

Opposite diagonal parallel lows leave only two eligible pair helpers. Adjacent parallel lows fill one halfcube and hence all pair helpers contain the same literal. All triples then contain it too. A nonquartet full must join two triples with disjoint pairs of remaining coordinates. There are at most two such joins among three triples, because the disjoint-axis graph of two-coordinate subsets of four axes is triangle-free. Adding the quartet's two fulls still leaves fewer than eight.

For nonparallel low targets the three eligible helper faces are exactly those in remaining-banks.md: the double region is a single pair face C split into two four-atom panels, with the same two free axes d,e absent from all pair helpers. All nonquartet fulls lie in that region. They require one triple adding a sign of d and another adding a sign of e. Three triples supply at most two distinct (d,e) combinations. Each occurs at most once in each panel. Thus there are at most four nonquartet fulls and at most six fulls altogether, contradicting a≥8.
