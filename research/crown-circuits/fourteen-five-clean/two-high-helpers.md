# Five clean axes cannot use exactly two helpers of width at least three

Assume the fourteen disjoint target faces, at most seven proper helper joins, and signed-coordinate conventions of one-high-helper.md. Let a be the number of full targets and x the number of targets of width at most three. Counting atoms gives x≤2+a/2. If this bound is attained, all x targets have width exactly three and the faces tile the cube. With two higher-width helpers, there are at most five pair helpers. Their disjoint-coordinate graph is triangle-free, so at most six edges can generate ordinary width-four targets (those made solely from two pair helpers). An active helper cannot itself be a target.

A quartet cannot contribute to a distinct target of width at most four. Two quartet helpers therefore give a≤4; their two distinct generating pair edges are unavailable, leaving at most four usable edges. At least 14−4−4=6 ordinary width-four targets are required. With one triple T and one quartet Q, let a_T full targets use T and a complement pair; the remaining a_Q≤2 use Q. If b width-four targets use T, then a_T+2b≤4. Thus x+b≤4+a_Q/2 and at least 10−a−a_Q/2 ordinary width-four targets are required. Some generating pair K is contained in T. Each of the a_T distinct complement pairs H forbids using edge (K,H), whose face intersects its full target. The upper bound 6−a_T is smaller since 4>3a_Q/2.

It remains to consider two triples. Let b count the width-four targets using at least one triple. The union of the two triple faces has at most eight atoms, so a+2b≤8, x+b≤6, and at least 8−a ordinary edges are needed. Choose generating pairs K_1,K_2. Each full join of a triple and a complementary pair blocks its corresponding pair edge. A full join using the two triples themselves can account for at most one full target. If the triples have different axis sets, blocked edges from different targets can coincide only by reciprocation: the generating pair of each triple must be the complement pair of the other. At most one such edge can coincide. In that case the triples have complementary fixed pairs and share a fifth axis. If their signs on this axis coincide then their reciprocal completions are the very same target; if opposite, no full join of the two triples is consistent. In either case at least a−1 edges are blocked. The resulting 7−a edge bound is too small. On identical axis sets and different chosen generating pairs, all blocked edges are distinct. Thus only twins T_+,T_- consisting of a common signed pair K and the two signs of a third axis remain.

Call the last two coordinates D and a fixed D pattern a slice. Let d count D-pair helpers used by full targets. At most two full targets lie on a given D slice, and d edges with K are blocked. Thus a−d≥2, d≥2. In particular any d slice supporting both full targets is completely occupied over K.

At d=2, necessarily a=4, and every bound in x+b≤6 and ordinary edge count ≤6−2 is tight. There are four width-three targets, two T-based width-four targets, four ordinary width-four targets, and the cube is tiled. A T-based width-four target adds exactly one D sign to its triple. No such sign avoids two diagonally opposite occupied D slices, so the occupied slices are adjacent. The two T-based edges must therefore use the common sign selecting the unoccupied slices, one edge per twin. This fills K globally. Equality in Mantel on five vertices gives the graph K₂,₃. Since every further neighbor of K would give an unusable edge inside the now filled K, its two neighbors are exactly the two used D helpers. The other two helpers L,M are on the same side as K, and lie entirely on the first three axes since each must be adjacent to both D helpers. The four ordinary width-four targets are their products with the two D patterns. On either occupied D slice, K,L,M are disjoint two-atom bands and leave two uncovered atoms. No width-three target containing K,L or M can meet these residual atoms, and a target built with that D helper and one further sign occupies four atoms. This contradicts tiling.

At d=3, a is five or six. With D₁,D₂,D₃ and K only one further pair helper L is possible. If L is a first-three-axes pair, at most three edges are usable (its products with the D helpers). If L crosses the two axis sets, none with a D helper and at most one with K is usable. If L is the fourth D pair, only its product with K is available. For a=5 the integral bounds x≤4 and b≤1 require at least four ordinary edges. For a=6 every occupied slice is full on K. No T-based width-four target avoids these three slices, so b=0. This forces all three products D_i×L, five width-three targets, and tiling. Any width-three target meeting the missing D slice requires K or L as a helper, since the available D helpers miss it. Such a target cannot isolate the missing D slice with only one additional sign: it meets the already occupied K or L band of another slice.

At d=4 the helpers are exactly K and the four D pairs; no ordinary width-four edge is usable. Therefore a≥8, forcing a=8, b=0 and six width-three targets outside K. Each must use a D helper and a first-side literal contradictory to K. In each slice at most one such target is possible, since all literals individually contradictory to K can simultaneously hold. Four slices cannot supply six.

A width-five helper cannot properly precede another consistent target. This exhausts the two-higher-helper case.
