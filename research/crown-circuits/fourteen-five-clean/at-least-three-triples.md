# At least three triple helpers when a full receiver is necessary

In the signed 5-cube let p,t,u count pair, triple and quartet helpers (p+t+u≤7); E=floor(p²/4) bounds the number of ordinary width-four targets obtained by joining two pair helpers. Let a count full targets and x targets of width at most three. Then x≤2+a/2 by atom counting. Equality implies all x have width three and the faces tile the cube. Here stars and intersections are on deficit faces, so adding literals restricts the face.

With t=0 each quartet consumes a distinct generating pair edge and contributes at most two fulls. At least 12−3u ordinary width-four targets are required, against E−u available. For 1≤u≤3, floor((7−u)²/4)<12−2u. For u≥4 too few pair edges exist to generate u quartets. With u=0 the bank cannot make a full target.

For t=1 let q quartets have faces not contained in the triple T. Each needs a generating pair edge. Let d full targets be made directly using T with a complement pair, each blocking a distinct edge with a fixed generating pair of T. Let n be the remaining fulls (via quartets) and a_out the fulls outside T. If r edges coincide between the q generating and d blocked edges, each coincident quartet already contains one of the direct fulls. Thus n≤2u−r and a_out≤2q−r. If b width-four targets use T, then a−a_out+2b≤4, hence x+b≤4+a_out/2. The ordinary width-four count gives 10−d−n−a_out/2≤E−q−d+r, implying 10−floor((6−u)²/4)≤2u. This fails for 0≤u≤4. At u=5 at most one pair helper exists, treated below.

For t=2 not twins, use d direct triple-plus-pair fulls blocking d−c distinct edges and v additional full joins of the two triples. The reciprocal-edge argument of two-high-helpers.md gives c+v≤1. Let q quartets not contained in either triple require generating pair edges, n the other quartet-made fulls, r the coincidences between generating and blocked edges, and a_out the fulls outside both triples. Then n≤2u−r and a_out≤2q−r, as above. Counting the union of the two triple faces gives x+b≤6+a_out/2. Hence 8−E≤c+v+2u−r/2≤1+2u. This fails for u=1 (E≤4) and u=2 (E≤2); u=0 is already excluded in two-high-helpers.md.

For twins T± based on K and opposite directions of a third axis, their union is K. Let d be the number of distinct D-pair helpers used by the a_D direct fulls, where D denotes the remaining two axes. Thus d≤a_D≤2d. There are q quartets not contained in K, each formed by two pairs; these generating edges differ from the d blocked K×D edges, which lie inside K. The remaining n fulls satisfy n≤2u and a_out≤2q. The ordinary-edge and volume comparison gives 8−E≤(a_D−d)+n+a_out/2−q≤d+2u. Hence d≥2 for u=1 or 2. Whenever the pair bank consists just of K and D pairs, all width-four and full targets lie inside K: higher helpers stay inside K, and all disjoint-axis pair joins use K. At most eight such targets fit, forcing x≥6, a≥8 and all eight atoms of K to be full targets. The six remaining width-three targets must arise from D plus a literal opposite one of K's signs. At most one such target fits in any D slice, contradiction.

The only remaining twin case at u=1,2 is u=1, d=2 with a fourth pair L. Equality in 8−E≤d+2u requires E=4 attained, a_D=4, n=2 and a_out=2q. Equality in the volume bounds gives x=5, all small targets width three, and tiling. The disjoint-axis graph is K₂,₂, with the two used D helpers on the side opposite K and L. Thus L lies on two of the first three axes. Each of the two L×D products is already occupied: it is either the generating edge of the quartet (if q=1) or an ordinary width-four target, by equality in the edge bound. Both possibilities fill that product, because n=2 and a_out=2q. The two occupied D slices are both full over K. The equality a−a_out+2b=8 fills K globally, leaving twelve atoms outside K on the two missing D slices. Every remaining width-three target reaching a missing slice must use K or L as a helper; K is unavailable and L can be used only with a D sign avoiding both occupied L×D products. At most one such sign exists, supplying only four atoms. This excludes the case.

Finally p≤1 is impossible without any bound on quartets: every target of width ≥3 lies in the union of the pair-helper faces, at most eight atoms. Six other targets of width ≤2 would require at least 48 atoms. At p=2,t=2, necessarily u≤3. Excluding fulls made with quartets, at most two fulls can be generated. Indeed each triple can use at most the other pair helper as its complement, and if both triples have such a complement then the two original pairs are disjoint axes and both triples use the single remaining axis. A full join of the two triples is either inconsistent or already counted. When neither or only one has such a complement a single triple-triple join gives at most two in all. Thus a≤8. If l targets have width ≤2 then l≤2 by atom count and all other targets live in the union of the two pair faces, at most sixteen atoms. They need at least 2(14−l)−a atoms, forcing l=2 and a=8 and requiring exactly sixteen atoms. The two pair faces must therefore be disjoint, hence their signed pair patterns conflict. There can be no direct triple-plus-complement-pair fulls. Two triples based on different pairs conflict; two based on the same pair together specify at most four axes. This leaves at most six fulls made by the three quartets, contradiction.

Together with two-high-helpers.md, all cases with at most two triple helpers are exhausted under the full-receiver premise.
