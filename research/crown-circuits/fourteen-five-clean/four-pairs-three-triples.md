# The four-pair, three-triple bank

Call a full receiver direct if it can be made by joining a triple and a complementary pair helper. Let d be their number, v the other full receivers, a=d+v. A nondirect full must join two triples with disjoint axis complements: a common missing axis precludes a full union. Each complement has two axes, so their disjointness graph on three triples has at most two edges (a triangle needs six axes). Hence v≤2.

We have d≤6. If every triple has at most two complementary helpers this is immediate. Otherwise three helpers use the same axis pair D and supply three completions for some triple. The fourth pair K lies among the other three axes and generates that triple. Only twins based on K and adding opposite signs on the same fifth axis e can each have three completions. Any triple of another type has at most one direct completion, using K. Without both twins the total is at most five. With both twins their six completions already include any direct full using the third triple and K: that triple must consist of both D coordinates and e, and must have a D helper as its base. Thus at most six distinct direct fulls occur.

Let l count targets of width at most two, n of width three, o width-four targets obtainable from two pairs, and b the remaining width-four targets. Each full is contained in at least two distinct pair faces since two triples based on the same pair span at most four axes. The four pair helpers supply 32 atom incidences. Ordinary width-four targets charge at least four each, the other width-four targets two each, width-three targets four each, and fulls two each. Therefore 32≥2(14−l)+2n+2o. The three triple faces supply twelve atom incidences, so 12≥a+v+2b. Substituting the target count gives d≥16−2l−2(n+o)≥12−4l. Since d≤6, at least two low targets are needed. Three low targets plus eleven others already exceed the cube. Thus l=2, whose volume gives a≥8. Consequently both d=6 and v=2 are required.

If one triple has three complementary helpers, both twins around K are required as above. Both nondirect fulls would have to join the third triple with a twin since the twins conflict. The third triple must contain both D coordinates, so its generating pair is one of the D helpers (K and both D coordinates already span four axes). Joining it to a twin, if full, therefore gives a target already direct using that D helper. This contradicts v=2.

Otherwise each triple needs exactly two complementary helpers to reach d=6. They cannot all have the same complement axis set, since then no two triples could generate a full. The four pair helpers are therefore two patterns each on exactly two axis sets D,E. A full triple-triple join requires D and E disjoint. Any triple missing D must use a generating pair on E and add the fifth coordinate; one missing E uses a generating pair on D and adds the same fifth coordinate. A consistent full join is already obtained by the first triple and the second one's D pair. This also precludes v=2.
