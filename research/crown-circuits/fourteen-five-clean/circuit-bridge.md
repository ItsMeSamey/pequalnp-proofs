# From a monotone circuit to a clean signed union bank

Use fan-in-two AND and OR gates, unrestricted fanout and one observed output. Inputs are Boolean, and the size counts binary gates. Let a singleton denote the assignment with a single raw input true. An AND is singleton-clean if its output is false on every singleton. Threshold two accepts precisely the assignments with at least two true raw inputs.

**Regular circuit theorem.** A live fourteen-input circuit computing threshold two, with each raw used at precisely two physical gate inputs, cannot have size at most 34 if it has at most five ANDs and all of them are singleton-clean. Unused axes may be padded to five. This theorem does not require the thirteen-input bound.

Consider a live regular circuit. Give the output a formal observer wire. Assign each physical AND two distinct signed coordinates, the two signs on its own axis. Working backwards in topological order, label an outgoing wire occurrence by its destination AND-port sign if it enters an AND; if it enters an OR, by the mask already computed for that OR; for the unique observer, by the empty mask. The mask at a wire is the union of these labels over its outgoing occurrences. This records exactly the AND input ports reachable by paths whose internal gates are all ORs, including direct raw-to-AND wires. The observer contributes no sign.

A raw's mask is consistent. If it reached both ports of an AND through ORs, that AND would accept the raw singleton. Distinct raws have conflicting signs on at least one shared axis. On the positive input consisting of their pair, some AND becomes true, since otherwise acceptance would be through an all-OR path from one raw, already accepting that singleton. Consider the first true AND. All preceding ANDs are false, so each of its two true operands is reached through OR-only paths from a raw of the pair. Consistency forces opposite raws to account for the two ports. Thus the fourteen signed masks define fourteen nonempty pairwise-disjoint faces of the Boolean five-cube.

For a live circuit of size g there are g+14 wires (raw signals and gate outputs), and the sum of their physical outdegrees with the observer is 2g+1. At a wire one can form its reverse mask with at most outdegree minus one binary unions. Hence all masks cost at most g−13≤21 unions. Reserve one final union at each twice-read raw; a repeated operand or an empty contribution may be bypassed or treated as a repeated effective operand. These fourteen final unions never serve as internal operands. This leaves at most seven nonterminal helpers. Empty, inconsistent, duplicate, primitive-equal and dead helpers may be discarded. A helper equal to a desired receiver cannot contribute to any other receiver, since unions preserve inclusion and disjoint receiver faces imply incomparable masks. Replace that receiver's final two terms by the inputs of its first construction and remove unnecessary helpers. In particular a helper fixing all five components cannot be needed. Every proper helper has width two, three or four.

This gives exactly the necessary combinatorial problem excluded by the accompanying notes, with all ten singleton signs freely available. The argument permits arbitrary interconnections of ANDs; it uses only singleton cleanliness and the first true AND on each positive pair.
