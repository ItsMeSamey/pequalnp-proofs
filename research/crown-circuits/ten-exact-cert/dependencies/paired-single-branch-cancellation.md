# A dirty comparison that can be bypassed by single-branch receiver taps

Choose a minimum-size paired threshold-two circuit, secondarily minimizing its number of diagonal-active ANDs. Work in terminal normal form and assume direct receivers have been excluded. For an AND j, write U for its reverse activation mask. Every input i making j true on its singleton has U contained in its resolution mask R_i. In this representative U cannot equal R_i, by the equal-activation cancellation.

Suppose for every singleton i making j true that there is a free branch mask B_i for which R_i is contained in U union B_i and that union is proper. Replace each of these raw receivers by a single union of the existing U wire and its chosen physical branch wire. The old paid raw receiver may be removed. Distinct raw receivers are incomparable. A path from a raw receiver to a proper activation would give inclusion into that activation, so enlargement followed by equal-activation cancellation or a size reduction rules out such a path. Full activations in terminal normal form use the initial full source. Thus the old hot receiver gates are removable sinks. Distinct replaced labels cost at most one union each.

The new resolution masks enlarge the old masks, so positive pairs are still covered in all directions. Each new receiver remains proper and hence still rejects its singleton. To check cleanliness, evaluate the rebuilt forward circuit with only the chosen raw i true. Its U tap joins the outgoing contribution of gate j, downstream of that gate. The B_i tap enters exactly one parent port of the AND k that emitted that branch in the reverse circuit. If k is earlier than j or equal to j in forward order, U cannot reach k, and a signal at a single pin cannot make k true. There is no other upstream input to make j true. If k is later than j, neither injection reaches j. This applies separately to each rewired singleton. Other singleton inputs retain their old values throughout the retained gates; extra receiver taps for these distant labels contribute zero. Every previously clean terminal comparison keeps full reverse activation, so it still cannot fire on a singleton with proper receiver. Reapplying terminal normalization cannot increase the number of dirty gates. The construction therefore contradicts the choice of the representative.

In particular U cannot omit exactly two observable trace atoms. Distinct atoms have opposite branch signatures at the first comparison where their resolutions diverge: all earlier branch memberships coincide, so that comparison is active at both. There is a free branch separating either atom from the other in the desired direction. Equivalently, expand the actual receiver R_i as a union of branches: it contains one omitted atom but not the other, so at least one branch in its expansion has exactly the required orientation. Every raw hot at j strictly contains U and is proper. Its deficit is one of the two atoms and a single branch tap realizes exactly the required supplement. This argument applies at any AND count when the paired, no-direct, and terminal-normal-form hypotheses hold.

The general test for a surviving profile is that for each dirty gate there is at least one hot receiver R whose deficit is strictly inside the complement of its activation and for which no proper union of the activation and one free branch covers R. The earliest available nonfull activations can also serve as separators if their physical taps are known to be downstream of j. The single-branch result above only needs branch sources.
