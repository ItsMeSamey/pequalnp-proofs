# A 23-gate lower bound for ten-input monotone threshold two

A fan-in-two monotone AND/OR circuit for T₂¹⁰ requires at least 23 gates. The threshold-two OR lower bound of 2n−4 excludes seven or more ANDs at size 22. The finite argument below rules out size 22 with at most six ANDs.

A full direction chooses one operand of each AND. The circuit in that direction is an ordinary disjunction and dominates the original. On any rejecting singleton there is a direction preserving all wire values, obtained by choosing a false parent at each false AND. Write R_i for the directions in which the resolved output depends on input i. Each R_i is nonfull, while R_i∪R_j is full for every pair i≠j. These two conditions characterize T₂ by monotonicity. Distinct receivers are incomparable and at least ten directions are required. Transposing the graph from its full output activation copies at ORs, splits by coordinate at ANDs and joins repeated uses at each wire. Every input is essential. A g-gate circuit on n inputs gives at most g−n+1 binary unions, summing outdegrees minus one over the g+n nondead nodes with the output tap included. Size 22 gives at most thirteen unions.

Choose a counterexample minimal in size, then in the number of dirty ANDs (true on some singleton). Normalize so all clean ANDs feed the final disjunction. A nonfull activation is then dirty, and if it equaled a receiver one could move that raw tap to the activation, preserve every receiver mask, and reduce the dirty count as detailed in activation.md. Each nonfull activation distinct from the free branch sources is therefore paid and differs from every receiver.

A direct raw receiver equal to a branch can be disposed of in either of two ways. The specialization in direct-minor.md saves at least three gates and one AND upon deleting it, yielding T₂⁹ in at most nineteen gates and at most five ANDs, excluded by the separate smaller minor census. Alternatively, a family of at least three pairwise covering proper receivers has at most one direct. Two free masks cover the whole cube only if they equal opposite full halves of one switch, which would force a third receiver to be full. After specializing, split the sole direct receiver of the nine-input minor (or any receiver if none is direct). Replacing its variable by the OR of two new variables and disjoining their AND adds three gates and one clean AND and reconstructs T₂¹⁰. On the fresh axis the two replacement masks equal the old receiver unioned with the respective fresh halves. Both vary with the new coordinate, differ from the new halves, and differ from all prior branches, constant on that coordinate. Other receivers cannot equal either fresh half. Cloning duplicates singleton behavior on old gates and adds a clean gate, so the dirty count cannot increase. This gives a representative free of direct receivers at the same budget. Fewer actual switches may be padded by unused full-activation splits along fresh coordinates, with no extra joins, nonfull activations, or direct receivers.

In this representative the ten receiver sinks are paid, leaving at most three other joins. Let W be the set of nonfull activations that are not free branches. Charge all |W| joins, then grant these masks and the twelve switch branches as sources. A receiver cannot occur on an unsplit union path to another receiver, by incomparability. Hence the remaining q=3−|W| helpers must suffice to generate ten receiver masks with pairwise disjoint nonempty deficits.

profile6.cpp generates every chronological activation sequence up to switch-bit translations: each activation is full or a union of earlier branches. A fresh nonfull activation unequal to earlier branches and activations cannot equal a later branch: it is independent of the later switch bit, whereas a nonempty later branch varies along its own bit. Budget excess can therefore be detected immediately. At least ten distinct branch traces are necessary. canon6.cpp groups the profiles under signed coordinate permutations. Its preliminary colors use activation weights and coordinate influences, invariant under bit translations and equivariant under coordinate permutation. It compares all compatible permutations and all translations, retaining an actual representative of each orbit.

check64.cpp and dense64.cpp compress directions only when every branch membership agrees. They enumerate the possible extra helper masks in increasing support size, respecting generation by two prior masks. A helper missing just one atom cannot feed a different proper receiver through a union. For each library they form all candidate receiver unions, exclude free and mandatory masks, then search for a disjoint packing of deficits. Available singleton deficits can be included by replacing any overlapping larger selected member. Larger deficits containing a smaller available deficit can be dropped. The direct checker generates its next masks from the current bank; the dense checker precomputes the strict-subset union recipes. Both use the same necessary condition.

The 161,482 chronological representatives reduce to 25,601 signed-permutation profiles. Their counts at q=0,1,2,3 are 18,089, 6,400, 1,027, and 85. Each indexed profile is rejected, across 4,824,777 helper libraries. The ledger records profile ID, number of distinguishable atoms, helper budget, banks visited, and success flag. Combining this with the 16-OR lower bound gives the unrestricted 23-gate result. Splitting an input at cost three also gives lower bounds 20,17,14 for nine, eight, and seven inputs respectively.

The physical OR bound used at the first and last step is the formal theorem FreeAndNet.threshold_two_circuit_or_lower, in Research/WegenerThreshold.lean of the research repository.
