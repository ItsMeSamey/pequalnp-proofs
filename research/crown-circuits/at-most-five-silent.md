# At most five silent singleton inputs at 24 OR gates

Let a monotone fan-in-two circuit of at most 40 gates agree with the 12-crown on the one-row, one-column promise. Suppose exactly 24 gates are ORs. Then at most five of the 24 singleton inputs cause all OR gates to evaluate false.

The circuit may share wires, use nullary or unary gates, repeat an operand, or read an invalid index, whose value is false. The proof makes no assumptions on same-side pairs. It uses two previously checked physical-circuit bounds. The first says each matching diagonal has at least 21 false ORs, so it has at most three true ORs. The second says at most six raw singletons are silent, and any six are all on the same side. Their Lean endpoints are `crown_trueOrFlags_le`, `crown_twenty_four_silent_le_six` and `crown_six_silent_same_side`. A separate six-label lower bound uses the [intact-subcircuit argument and its finite checker](five-silent-certificate/six-intact.md). The assembled proof below is mathematical. An end-to-end Lean theorem for five remains pending.

## Six silent rows would require twelve comparison ANDs

Assume six silent raws occur. Exchange sides so they are row inputs. A baseline-cold wire is one that is false on the all-zero mask. Every OR is baseline-cold since a baseline-true OR would fire on each silent input. A cold wire accepting a silent row singleton computes that raw on the full input cube. Indeed its true dependency on that singleton traces only ANDs; all raw leaves in the true trace are the same row raw, and at least one is needed since the all-zero value is false. Nullary true ANDs on that trace act as constants. This statement is also the checked lemma `silent_cold_is_copy`.

Consider a silent row raw x and one of its eleven opposite column neighbors y. Each of the two singleton inputs is rejected, by containment in its respective false diagonal. The pair is accepted. The first wire accepting the pair but neither singleton must be an AND. Each parent accepts a singleton and the two parents accept opposite singletons exclusively. Both parents are baseline-cold. Thus this AND is the conjunction of a physical copy of x with a cold partner accepting y. A given useful AND of this form belongs to just one silent row: a cold parent accepting a silent raw is a copy, so an AND with two silent-row copies cannot have the required opposite column support. Its output is false on every raw singleton.

Every cold wire accepts at most eight column singletons. One proof gives each raw initial mass one and halves its weight at each OR it fires. Existing wire masses do not increase. An active OR takes the union of at most two cold parent supports; halving bounds its mass by one. A cold AND's support lies in that of a cold parent. Every column singleton fires at most three ORs because it lies below its matching diagonal, so each weighs at least one eighth. At least two distinct useful ANDs are therefore needed for each silent row.

Set the six silent rows and their matching column inputs to zero. On the intact subcube of the remaining six labels, every one of these twelve ANDs has a false operand everywhere. The six-label lower bound requires at least four ANDs without such an operand, since at most three physical ORs fire on each remaining raw singleton. Since the full circuit has at most sixteen ANDs, there are exactly twelve useful ANDs, exactly two per silent row, and four other ANDs. For each silent row i, its two partner operands' column singleton supports cover all eleven neighbors.

The output W of each useful AND has a direct OR reader. It cannot be the circuit output since it vanishes on the intact subcube. If W had no readers it could be deleted with all remaining physical OR flags preserved after reindexing; the same twelve-plus-four argument would still require sixteen ANDs, a contradiction. No other useful AND can read W since W has empty singleton support and both operands of each useful AND have a nonempty singleton support. An AND among the other four reading W would have an identically false operand on the intact subcube and leave fewer than four survivors there. Thus some OR reads W.

## Singleton weights and chronological capacity

An operand's support is the set of **all** raw singleton assignments on which it is true. Call an OR effective if both of its input slots have nonempty supports. In particular an OR reading one of the twelve singleton-empty outputs W is ineffective. For each raw x use final weight 8 times two to the power minus the number of effective ORs firing on x. Every raw weighs one, two, four or eight in these units. The column weights are written u_j and their sum T.

At every point in evaluation, every baseline-cold wire has mass at most eight under the weights obtained by charging only effective ORs so far. Initially each raw has mass eight. At an effective OR, halve the weight of every singleton it accepts; the gate's mass after halving is at most half the sum of its two parent masses. At an ineffective OR the singleton support is empty or coincides with its sole nonempty operand's support, so no halving is needed. At a cold AND use any cold parent, which exists even for degenerate gates under the evaluator's semantics. Old masses cannot rise.

For distinct effective OR roots U before V, let m(U) be the *final column mass* of U, and let m(U∩V) refer to columns firing both. At U's formation the mass of every shared column is at least twice its final weight. Consequently m(U)+m(U∩V)≤8. Two later activations multiply the corresponding lower bound by four. Every root in this argument is cold.

If i is matched to a silent row and u_i=1, then c_i alone activates three effective ORs and no other OR. Neither of the row's two useful ANDs can fire on the matching diagonal: its direct OR reader would be ineffective and would supply a fourth physical OR there. Each partner therefore rejects c_i even on the singleton. For either partner retain the columns it accepts and trace backwards through a child false on c_i at an AND, or through a nonempty-support child at an ineffective OR. All the retained columns stay accepted and c_i stays rejected. Whenever the retained mass exceeds the largest individual column weight, the trace must reach an effective OR before reaching a raw. Such a root's column support contains those retained columns and excludes i. The two roots of row i together cover exactly the column complement of i. They are distinct whenever T−1>8.

For every silent-row label i the two partner capacities imply T−u_i≤16. The smallest u_i among these six labels is at most two: if all six were at least four, any matched row would have to cover five columns of weight four and six more of weight at least one. Hence 12≤T≤18.

## Column totals from fourteen to eighteen

Call a support tight when its cold wire has final mass eight, counting raw singletons on both sides. Tight supports are equal or disjoint. At an effective OR all earlier tight supports meeting its support lose mass, so the new OR is disjoint from every surviving tight support. A tight AND shares the support of a cold tight parent by positivity. An ineffective OR of nonempty support preserves that support. Induction from the disjoint raw supports proves the assertion.

If B is tight and a cold H extends outside B, the mass of H plus the mass of its intersection with B is at most eight. Initially this holds for raw inputs. When B first becomes tight at an effective OR, the expression for an earlier H equals H's mass before halving. An older tight B that survives a new effective OR is disjoint from that OR; earlier H masses only contract. A new cold AND inherits the bound from a cold parent whose support includes H and also extends outside B. When the tight wire itself is an AND its support is inherited. An ineffective OR of nonempty support can be identified with its unique nonempty operand. This proves the overlap inequality.

At T=18, the matched columns must all weigh two and the six intact columns one, since u_i≥T−16 for each matched column. Each matched row covers a sixteen-unit complement using two eight-unit partners. Both partners are tight and cover exactly that complement, without wasted mass. Two such complements cannot differ by a singleton of weight less than eight. The difference of two unions of equal-or-disjoint tight blocks must itself be a union of whole blocks.

At T=17 there is at least one matched light column, since the six matched columns otherwise have total weight at least twelve. There can be at most one such light column by the same two-complement argument. The other five matched columns weigh two and the six intact columns weigh one. The cover for the light label i consists of two tight blocks B,C partitioning all columns except i. Pick a matched heavy column j in B, switching names if necessary. Its row requires fifteen integral units, so one partner is tight. It cannot be B, which wastes two units at j. A tight block disjoint from both B,C covers at most the single unit i and does not furnish enough of the needed fifteen. Thus the tight partner is C. The other must cover the six units of B outside j and the unit i outside B. Its mass plus its mass inside B is at least thirteen, exceeding eight.

At T=14, 15 or 16 there are respectively at least four, three or two matched light columns. Select the two rejecting effective OR roots for each such label. Each root covers at least T−1−8≥5 column units, and at least one in each pair covers at least seven. Distinct rows cannot have identical root pairs since the pairs cover different complements. If two pairs are disjoint as physical gates, their column unions overlap in at least T−2≥12 units. Their four cross-intersections have total at most ten: the two high roots share at most one unit, and each other intersection contributes at most three. If two pairs share just U, their distinct companions both cover all columns outside U except the two rows' omissions. They overlap in at least T−8−2≥4 units. The earlier companion already weighs at least five and cannot double-count four units within its capacity. This exhausts the larger totals.
## The remaining column totals of twelve and thirteen

Use the weights that skip ORs with a singleton-empty operand. Write m(X) for the weight of the column singletons accepted at wire X, in eighth-units. For each matched light column i, the dedicated-gate argument gives two effective OR roots. Both reject i and together accept every other column. Both partners reach such a root, since each must cover at least T−1−8 units, at least three at T=12 and four at T=13. An individual raw column weighs at most one or two respectively. Following a rejecting child through an AND, or the nonempty child through a skipped OR, preserves the required acceptances until an effective OR is reached.

For distinct chosen roots X before Y, the capacity of X at its formation gives m(X) + m(X ∩ Y) ≤ 8. Here each root denotes its accepted set of column singletons. Their overlap incurs at least one further halving at Y.

**Every root of weight at least seven occurs in every light row’s pair.** Let B be such a root, of weight s. Suppose both roots U,V for a matched light i differ from B. They cover at least s−1 units in B. If both precede B, adding the two capacity bounds gives 16 ≥ (T−1) + (s−1) ≥ 17. If U follows B, then m(U ∩ B) ≤ 8−s ≤ 1. Thus V must cover at least s−2 ≥ 5 units in B. If V precedes B it must pay those five units twice. If it follows B their overlap may be at most one. Both alternatives contradict capacity. Hence B occurs in i’s pair and rejects i.

**All roots weigh at most six.** Each root then weighs at least five. Pairs for distinct matched light labels i,j cannot coincide, since the first pair covers j while the second rejects j. If they share just B, the two remaining roots both cover all columns outside B except possibly i and j. Their overlap is at least T−6−2 ≥ 4. The earlier root weighs at least five and must pay the overlap again, exceeding eight. Thus the light rows’ pairs are pairwise disjoint. There are six light rows at T=12 and at least five at T=13. Each intact column activates a distinct effective OR in every pair, exceeding the limit of three.

**A root B weighs at least seven.** It rejects every matched light column. At T=12 all six matched columns are light and all columns weigh one, leaving only six units available to B. At T=13 there is one double-weight column. Let C be the columns outside B. Its mass is five or six and it contains at least five matched lights. For each such i, the companion Qᵢ of B covers C except i and rejects i. Different i give different physical roots. If C weighs six, two companions each weigh at least five and overlap in at least four, exceeding eight at the earlier root. If C weighs five, it consists of five matched light columns. Take three distinct companions in chronological order Qᵢ,Qⱼ,Qₖ. In Qᵢ, the two columns of C outside {i,j,k} each incur two later halvings at Qⱼ and Qₖ. The columns j and k each incur at least one later halving at the other column’s root. Qᵢ therefore had mass at least 4+4+2+2=12 at formation, contradicting the capacity of eight.

This excludes T=12 and T=13 for the adjusted weights. Together with the larger-total cases it rules out six silent row inputs under the preceding circuit hypotheses. Exchanging the input sides gives the same conclusion for columns.

## Verification status and references

The six-label finite case is documented in the appendix and can be replayed with standard Python. The base six-silent and same-side results, ordinary singleton Kraft capacity, cold-copy lemma, tight supports and chronological overlap inequalities have Lean proofs. The adjusted capacity follows from the gate-by-gate argument above. The complete five-silent conclusion has not yet been checked end to end by Lean. It leaves the existence of a size-40 circuit with 24 ORs open.
