# An eight-input threshold-two lower bound

Suppose a binary monotone AND/OR circuit for threshold two on eight inputs has at most 18 gates. The physical 2n−4 OR bound gives at most six AND gates. Use the normalization and direct-input specialization and resplitting in TEN.md. The resulting transposed circuit has at most eleven binary unions. Each of eight proper pairwise-covering receivers consumes a union, leaving at most three paid masks for activating splits or forming receiver operands.

Pairwise covering proper receivers are incomparable. A receiver cannot feed a different receiver through unions alone. Nor can it feed a nonfull switch activation through unions alone: in the terminal normal form such an activation is contained in the raw receiver of a singleton activating that dirty AND. Incomparability then forces equality, excluded by minimum dirty-gate normalization. Full switch activations may tap the full source. Hence all eight receiver unions may be treated as terminal. Identical receiver signals may be attached to their existing masks, pruning redundant duplicate gates. The final packing searches additionally grant the option of reusing any paid optional helper as a receiver.

Generate six-switch profiles with at least eight different directional traces and at most three nonfree activations. There are 162,128 chronological translation representatives. After quotienting by signed coordinate permutation, 25,721 profiles remain. With zero through three optional helpers the respective counts are 18,089, 6,411, 1,121 and 100. An independent adjacent-swap connectivity enumeration verifies a bijection with every retained canonical row. The relaxed helper library checker grants all branches and mandatory nonfree activations as free, pays the remaining optional helpers, and tests for eight receiver deficits that are nonempty and pairwise disjoint.

The two final checks restore the binary cost and availability order of every paid mask. The C++ dynamic search starts from the full mask and releases every switch whose activation is available, revealing its signed branches at no union cost. It may pay for one union of two available masks before repeating this closure, at most three times. Every realizable reverse network has such an order. Releasing an already enabled switch early cannot prevent later unions or other releases. The acquired helper set determines all available branches, so revisiting the same set of helpers in a different order is unnecessary. Once all switches can release, test the unions of two available operands for disjoint receiver deficits. The search permits every switch order and does not depend on the chronology of the displayed profile representative.

Independently, the Python bank-first search grants all signed branches and mandatory masks when enumerating up to the allowed number of optional helper masks. A nonredundant binary union strictly increases its unsigned bit mask over each operand. The optional masks can therefore be enumerated in ascending order without losing any bank. For each candidate bank, start from the full source and repeatedly release switches with available activation and adjoin each paid mask equal to a union of two available masks. When the least fixed point contains all required masks and activations, test packing, branching on a direction-point instead of choosing the next receiver. No physical bank can fail the release implications. Both tests check the initial relaxed-positive profile IDs individually.

The threshold-two function of three inputs costs at most four gates. Replacing one variable by a two-variable OR and adjoining their AND as an alternative output costs three gates and reconstructs threshold two at the next arity. The reusable program upper-ladder.py checks the resulting upper bound of 3n−5 gates for every assignment at arities three through eight. An eight-input lower bound of 19 propagates downwards by the same splitting construction, yielding matching exact costs at every arity from three through eight.
