# Eleven regular physical raws in the square residual

The square, three P3s and the independent single edge s give eleven raw labels. Their bank uses seven ORs. The candidate decoder of twelve ORs and eight ANDs thus attains the 27-gate minimum. Use clean-terminal normalization. The identical two flags of the isolated edge are one independent raw s.

Deleting one raw can save at most three gates. If one AND and two ORs disappear and two adjacent physical bank ORs survive, ten-forest.md excludes the resulting T10/24/A7. Any further simplification would beat the size floor.

**Independent raw.** Three direct OR readers of s would give T10 with sixteen ORs and eight ANDs, contradicting the nine-AND secondary minimum. A direct OR and an AND save both plus a first proper OR downstream of the AND, distinct by absorption. This one-AND/two-OR deletion leaves all square banks intact. A sole AND s∧P makes P nonemptiness on the other raws since adding s to each singleton changes the result. Its producer is an OR (either operand of an AND producer would already compute nonemptiness). At s=false the product and a receiving OR disappear. P's OR root can also be omitted by substituting symbolic true: all other remaining wires are bounded by P and the restricted target is not P. None of these three gates is a square bank, since P covers ten singletons. Again the forest obstruction applies.

If there are at least two AND readers and no direct OR, their AND-only cones must have exactly one first proper OR, since two initial ANDs plus two proper ORs exceed the deletion allowance. Substitute true for s in the cone and restore its factor once on the relevant operand of the frontier, or after the frontier if both operands depend on s. This reduces size. A sole OR reader s∨P requires P cold on all singletons (adding s must be visible at each other singleton, and P is independent of s), incompatible with clean-terminal normalization. Thus s has exactly two OR readers.

**Path flags.** A direct path-leaf AND x∧P is clean: otherwise P is true on the x singleton and the product is redundant. Erasing x bypasses its bank, the product, and a collector OR, keeping the square bank. This violates the forest obstruction, so every leaf reads an OR. Each center has one reader by sixteen-center-branch.md. If a center F=x∨y feeds F∧K hot at singleton x, erase y. Its bank and its direct OR reader disappear, and the product now aliases x since K is hot on x. Again all square banks survive. Thus all three paths are ordinary.

**Square flags.** The two-loss false-pin result of square-no-nonempty.md forces any vertex with an AND reader to have only this reader. If that product is hot at a singleton edge v incident to its vertex, erase its other incident edge w. The bank ORs at both endpoints of w disappear. The product now equals v by monotonicity from singleton v. The two untouched square banks have forms u∨z and z∨t on the three remaining distinct raw labels, so the forest obstruction still applies. Every direct square AND is clean.

A shared square-vertex AND is impossible. Adjacent A,B as sole inputs to their sole reader A∧B hide the distinction between CD alone and CD with BC. For opposite A,C suppose they feed only A∧C. Restrict to BC=x and CD=y with x,y independent. Set B=x,D=y; no C bank is needed since A=0 and the product is false. Bypass the product and its receiving OR, rebuilding just three outside path-center ORs. This gives T9 with at most 21 gates, leaving seven ANDs if only the stated two gates are bypassed. This contradicts the four-AND composition of size 21.

All eleven physical raws now have two OR readers: cycle edges at their two banks, path edges at their bank and their leaf OR, and the independent raw at its own two ORs.
