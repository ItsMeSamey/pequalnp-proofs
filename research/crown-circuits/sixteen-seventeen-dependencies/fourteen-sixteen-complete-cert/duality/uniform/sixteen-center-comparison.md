# Comparisons reading an ordinary path center

Assume the ordinary three-single-read-flags conclusions of sixteen-two-deletions.md and sixteen-center-branch.md. The centers of two distinct ordinary P3s cannot be the two operands of a single AND G. Their four leaves have distinct OR readers. A first OR H reached from G through only ANDs must exist, since deleting either full component sets G false while leaving positive remote pairs. Choose one of the two paths j whose two leaf OR readers do not include H. Setting j false removes those two leaf ORs and H. These distinct gates can all be eliminated even if one leaf reader occurs downstream of H, since each leaf raw is false independently and G's zero reaches H through its AND prefix.

The surviving path i's center was used only by G and is now irrelevant. Its two leaves are independent labels. The cycle plus components besides i and j need six ORs (triangle plus two P3s, or square plus one P3 and a K2). With no bank flag covering all singletons, adding the two independent labels requires four further ORs. At least ten are needed, but at most nine remain. Consequently all ordinary flags need distinct readers across components, using the earlier OR fusion and single-leaf AND arguments as well.

If an ordinary center feeds G=g_i∧W, W rejects both singleton labels of i. On each restriction keeping the cycle, the K2 if present, both labels of i, and one selected representative from every other P3, W must still be hot on some singleton. Otherwise bypass G to W: positives persist by monotonicity and negatives are unchanged because W is singleton-clean. The center g_i is now irrelevant, so i becomes two independent raws. Erasing each other P3's unselected leaf already paid one OR, by its local pair witness, after which the surviving edge is one independent raw. Relative to the fixed cycle bank, every group other than i still costs three ORs (one erasure plus the increment of two), while i now costs four instead of three. The total exceeds the original saturated cap. Thus W is hot on some cycle singleton or the K2 singleton, or on both leaves of some other P3. This imposes no injectivity between different comparisons.

For the triangle residual, define a spoiler of a triangle vertex A to be a remote singleton y accepted upon adding A alone. Pin A true and use its two neighbors as independent virtual labels. Remove all s spoiled remote labels. With p paths containing a removed label, the remaining bank needs only 4−p ORs. If a removed leaves have AND readers, the other s−a leaves have distinct OR readers. Let C count direct OR readers of A not among those leaf gates. The new T2(10−s) circuit therefore has at most 16−p−s+a−C ORs. The universal OR floor gives (s−p)+a≥C. In particular if A is single-read into OR, its reader cannot equal a spoiled leaf's reader: that OR would already be true without A at the given singleton, hiding A. Every such A thus spoils both leaves of some P3 or an AND-read leaf. If all spoilers have OR readers and lie in different paths, a branching A must spend both readers on their gates.

Every A has at least two spoilers. With none, pinning A gives T2(10) with sixteen ORs and eight ANDs, below the secondary floor. With a unique spoiler y, erase y as well. Before simplifications the ordinary T2(9) size is 24: twelve decoder ORs, eight ANDs, and four bank ORs. The affected bank loses one OR, the decoder loses y's reader, and pinning A costs another reader. If A branches, its two reader gates already provide the required total even when one reads y. A single common OR would make y harmless; a sole common AND would make A invisible on pure-triangle inputs (for example the negative edge opposite A versus the positive triangle). These are distinct gate losses, leaving at most 21 gates. Four AND erasures would be needed for the unique minimum composition; in a 21-gate restriction only three gates total were erased. Further savings would fall below the minimum.
