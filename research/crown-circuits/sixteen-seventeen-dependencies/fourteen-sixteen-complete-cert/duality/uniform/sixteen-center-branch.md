# Fanout of a P3 center in either cyclic sixteen-flag residual

The decoder plus its seven-gate raw bank is a minimum 27-gate eleven-input circuit. A P3 center g=x∨y is its physical bank OR, and the two leaf raws x,y each have one further use. Erasing both leaves eleven minus two labels, of minimum size 21. A size-21 nine-input circuit has exactly four ANDs, so a restriction losing six gates must erase exactly four of the original eight ANDs.

The standard zero-restriction dark-edge count charges four raw occurrences, every surplus outgoing occurrence of a dark internal gate and every AND with exactly one dark operand. Here dark means identically zero after x=y=0. All such debits count distinct savings under propagation; trim only afterwards. The center contributes f(g)−1 surplus units. If its fanout is three or more, at least six gates disappear and there is no room for even one one-sided dark AND. Every dark gate then depends solely on x,y: dark ORs require both operands dark, dark ANDs also have both dark. In a size minimum no duplicate or redundant pure gate occurs. Among binary monotone functions of x,y only x∧y is available as a new AND. The required four AND losses are therefore impossible.

With f(g)=2, at least five gates disappear. Six losses without any one-sided dark AND have just been ruled out. If there is one such AND, all six debits are spent and every other dark internal node has fanout one. Outside the center's two input slots, the four available dark occurrences are x's remaining use, y's remaining use, and the two g uses. The one-sided product consumes a dark occurrence and produces one; each both-dark product consumes two and produces one. Four erased ANDs thus demand three both-dark products in addition to the one-sided product. Starting with four occurrences those three joins leave exactly one occurrence for a dark-to-live exit (some exit is needed since all gates are live in the original circuit and the restricted output is nonzero). No other both-dark OR, prior exit, or branch can occur. The dark gates downstream of the bank therefore form an AND tree on the four dark inputs and the live argument W of the one-sided product. They have a single final use. Their value is x∧y∧g∧g∧W = x∧y∧W; replacing the tree by two ANDs strictly improves the original circuit. Hence f(g)=2 entails exactly five losses and no one-sided dark AND. Both readers of g are ORs (any AND of g with a pure dark function would be redundant). Any leaf AND must combine the two leaf raws; otherwise it is either redundant or one-sided. Consequently the two leaf readers are both ORs or their unique shared sibling AND.

In fact, whenever both leaves have OR readers, the center has only one reader. If the center branched, both center readers would be ORs by the preceding argument. The two leaf readers and two center readers are distinct: a joint leaf-center gate is absorbed, and a common leaf OR is the center itself. Removing this entire P3 therefore removes four decoder ORs. On the remaining interface the cycle floor and full charge for every other P3 or K2 force at least nine decoder ORs, yet at most eight remain. This is impossible.

Consequently every nonexceptional group of sixteen-two-deletions.md has all three flags single-read. An OR joining any two flags from different such groups would allow identifying the two vertices and deleting that OR. The merged five-vertex tree is P5, a cubic nonstar, or a four-star. P5 with the cycle has combined-order-minus-two floor. A cubic nonstar tree on five vertices already has floor three without a universally covering flag. The four-star with the cycle has combined-order-minus-one floor. The remaining components charge their full orders. In each case the reduced fourteen-vertex interface needs at least twelve ORs, whereas the merged decoder has at most eleven. A cross-component AND consuming a single-read leaf also loses that component's distinction of its own local positive pair from the sibling singleton when other components are zero.
