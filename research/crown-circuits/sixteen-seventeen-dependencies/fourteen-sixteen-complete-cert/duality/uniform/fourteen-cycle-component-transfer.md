# Full-order charge for any connected nonstar remote component

Let B be a fixed monotone flag bank vanishing on empty and requiring F decoder ORs for threshold two on at least two independent labels. It may expose a flag true on every singleton. Let H be a disjoint connected graph with h flags which is not a star. On its edge labels use their usual vertex incidence flags. The joint decoder needs at least F+h ORs.

At a hypothetical cap F+h−1, consider any vertex z of degree d≥2. If z spoils no singleton of B, peel the h−d−1 other vertices outside its closed neighborhood and pin z true. The d neighbors give d independent virtual star raws. The initial independent raw adds at least one OR (essentiality with a remaining positive at zero). Every subsequent one adds at least two: after adjoining the first raw no individual bank flag covers all singletons, so the fixed-bank independent-raw increment applies. The restricted decoder needs F+2d−1 and has at most F+d, impossible. Thus every nonleaf individually spoils a B singleton. Erase the leaves first. Each leaf x has an internal neighbor y with an internal neighbor z: the subgraph on nonleaves is connected and contains at least two vertices because H is not a star. The retained edge yz versus its union with xy witnesses essentiality of x, irrespective of other leaf erasures. A positive base input survives. Now erase each internal vertex using its surviving spoiler witness at a fixed B singleton. One OR contracts per vertex, leaving F−1 ORs for B.

In particular a three-edge star plus a cycle on l flags needs l+2 ORs, since the three-input star costs two ORs after its nonemptiness flag is pinned true. This is the combined order minus two. More generally K1,4 cannot coexist with any connected nonstar in a q−2 host: keep that joint base, which has no universally true flag, and charge other components at full order. The remote graph of every bare quartic is therefore a star forest.

For q=14 in the regular 14-edge eight-AND host, a degree-d branching pure flag with d≤3 and t adjacent leaves leaves N=13−t flags and 14−d edges at residual OR cap N−3. The component test in fourteen-disconnected-thirteen.md implies at most one triangle or square plus exactly d−1−t trees, each of order at most five. Consequently d=3 and t=0, with exactly two trees and one cycle. Every five-vertex tree is P5: a nonstar with a cubic vertex has the fatal h−2 floor; a four-star plus the cycle is already fatal. The orders force a triangle and two P5s, or a square, a P5 and a four-vertex tree. The latter tree cannot be a three-star by the new joint floor, so it is P4. Branches of degree four are already excluded at q=14 by fourteen-disconnected-thirteen.md. Thus only the two residuals C3+P5+P5 and C4+P4+P5 need consideration, irrespective of original connectivity. This does not resolve those two cases.
