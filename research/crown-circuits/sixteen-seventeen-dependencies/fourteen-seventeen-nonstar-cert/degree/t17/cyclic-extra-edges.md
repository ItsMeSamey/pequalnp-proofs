# The two q17 eleven-label cyclic interfaces with extra independent edges

After deleting a degree-three branch without pendant neighbors the triangular interface is C3+3P3+2K2; the square interface is C4+2P3+3K2. Each has sixteen flags, eleven independent raw edge labels, and at most thirteen OR/eight AND decoder gates, hence 27 gates after restoring its six bank ORs. Both are impossible. Also, at the (d,t)=(2,0) residual C4+4P3, setting one path leaf false loses an OR by its unchanged local singleton/pair test and a surviving outside pair. Identifying the remaining edge of that path leaves the already excluded q16 C4+3P3+K2 with at most twelve ORs and eight ANDs.

For the two eleven-label cases apply ten-forest.md to each single-raw specialization from 27 gates. Every P3 leaf is singly read: the previous leaf proof in sixteen-pendant-readers.md applies with the new independent-raw count. More explicitly erase one leaf, identifying its remaining edge with one independent raw. In the triangular case the residual needs at least twelve ORs (triangle floor zero plus two P3s at three each and three independent raws at two each); in the square case it needs twelve (square minimum one, one remaining P3 at three, four independent raws at two each). In each case the absent-universal condition holds already on the base cycle. There can be only one first OR under leaf deletion and the standard single-frontier refactorization gives a sole reader. If a leaf has an AND reader x∧P it is clean by monotonicity from the x singleton; erasing x removes its bank, the product and a collector OR. The unchanged cycle banks forbid the resulting T10/24/A7 by ten-forest.md. A path center with both leaves OR-read cannot branch: its two OR readers and the two leaf readers would be distinct, erasing four ORs while the other components (cycle and remaining P3s or independent raws) need ten ORs. The earlier path-center fanout/AND-reader proof in sixteen-center-branch.md depends only on the unchanged T11/27 and T9/21 four-AND composition. A locally hot AND at a path center is also impossible: erase its other leaf, losing the bank, the leaf OR and the now-redundant center product while preserving adjacent cycle banks. Thus all paths are ordinary. The independent raw cases of square-regularity.md apply separately to each K2 because every deletion keeps the whole cycle. Each independent raw consequently has exactly two direct OR readers.

The cycle reader arguments are unchanged with their numerical parameters checked as follows. False-pin a triangle vertex, identify the other two as one independent label and rebuild three path banks. False-pin a square vertex, rebuilding the middle bank of the remaining two-edge path and the two untouched path banks. Each restriction starts with 24 gates, sixteen ORs and eight ANDs on nine independent inputs, exactly the hypotheses of triangle-ports.md (false-pin part) and square-no-nonempty.md. For the triangle autonomous two-port lemma the unrestricted exterior contains eight independent raws with three path bank ORs, hence the unsorted or sorted cost allowances for the local encoder are unchanged (21+3 or 21+3+2 versus C10=24). Therefore no raw pair of triangle vertices can feed a gate, and at most one triangle vertex is AND-read. For the square the same composition gives the opposite- and adjacent-OR restrictions in square-no-nonempty.md and sixteen-square-adjacent-or.md. Opposite shared ANDs are excluded by the smaller bank deletion as in square-regularity.md (now only two other banks are rebuilt); adjacent shared ANDs are excluded semantically. A square AND reader hot on an incident edge resolves upon deleting its other incident edge, causing the forbidden T10/24/A7 minor with the two untouched internal bank ORs. All square AND readers are therefore clean.

Each cycle flag must spoil a remote singleton. A harmless triangle vertex pins true and exposes two independent labels, creating T10 with 13+3=16 ORs and eight ANDs, contrary to secondary optimality. For a harmless square vertex, first erase the opposite vertex (its associated local pair witness still proves an OR loss) and then pin it true to expose two independent rays. The same argument as sixteen-pendant-readers.md gives T9 at the simultaneous 14-OR/eight-AND floors (13−1+2); any extra AND or OR loss contradicts those floors, while the harmless flag retains a reader after the first erasure and its pin saves such a gate. The b branching flags each have two OR readers only, by the T11 composition and dark-edge argument of sixteen-cycle-readers.md. No group of exclusively branching cycle flags can exist because a cycle-flag pair OR is forbidden. The 2b branching uses thus charge 2b ORs; then each remaining single-read cycle flag costs an OR using its isolated spoiler. The surviving remote paths and independent edges need respectively nine ORs for the triangle case and eight for the square: one erasure per path leaves five independent raws requiring six ORs. Hence b≤1 in both cases.

All eleven original physical raws are twice-OR-read. Their flattened incidence graph with v vertices has relative decoder budget 19−(22−v)=v−3. The original cycle and paths inject as disjoint distinct vertex subgraphs. In detail, a branched bank is its own vertex and its output is a nonraw source to subsequent OR groups; single-read AND banks also remain intact. Two singleton-read formal flags in one OR group can be reassociated into their common gate then identified. A same-component pair is forbidden by the local cycle/ordinary path identities. Gluing a cycle vertex and a path flag yields a component with a cubic nonstar or quartic star center; the cubic nonstar graph has order-minus-two floor and no universal flag, and if the center has degree four its star already gives the order-minus-two bound for this glued component by peeling. Gluing two P3 flags produces P5, a cubic nonstar, or a four-star with the original cycle still present. The prior cycle-plus-P5 or cycle-plus-star and cubic nonstar bounds prohibit each case, since the new interface of fifteen flags would have budget twelve after the fusion whereas a strong base plus the full-order remaining components needs thirteen. Independent raw components in these charges add two ORs apiece. Thus the only extra edges in the physical incidence graph are the K2 raws.

Because the cycle is present, this v−3-budget graph cannot have any vertex of degree three or more (see square-final-host.md and ten-forest.md). Thus no independent edge attaches directly to the cycle or any path center. Nor can any independent edge meet a path leaf: that path's component is now a path of at least three edges, or a cycle of length at least three. For a path, keep a four-vertex subpath to form the strong cycle-plus-P4 base and peel the rest. For a cycle of length three or four use the two-cycle bound; for a longer cycle pin one vertex false and peel a path down to four vertices, again giving the strong floor. In each case at least the joint order minus two is needed, and other remotes charge full order. The cycle and original three-vertex paths thus remain separate from the independent edges.

If two independent raws enter the same group, reassociate to their OR and offer it for free as the center of a fourth triangle-side path or third square-side path. This converts the two independent labels into a two-edge path with both leaves available, reducing the decoder from thirteen to twelve ORs at the same eight-AND cap. These are exactly the q16 cyclic exclusions. Therefore each independent edge is isolated even from the others. Its two physical endpoints correspond to distinct mixed OR groups (a one-input pure group is not an actual OR), each requiring a separate hot decoder OR beyond the cycle and path groups.

For the triangle, the three ordinary paths charge nine ORs, including clean path-center products charged by collector ORs; at least one collector term survives on a doubled triangle. The independent edges charge four more. Since b≤1 and at most one triangle vertex is AND-read, at least one single-read cycle flag requires its own separate OR group. This exceeds thirteen. For the square, the two paths charge six, the three independent edges charge six, and the 4−b≥3 single-read square vertices each charge an OR group or a clean terminal. As in square-final-host.md there is an edge missed by every clean square vertex, and that edge with one independent raw forces an additional collector term separate from the charged path and square terms. Thus at least fifteen ORs are needed, exceeding thirteen.
