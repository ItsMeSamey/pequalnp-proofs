# Appending one independent raw costs two decoder ORs

Suppose at least two independent base labels are represented by a fixed monotone flag bank vanishing on empty, with no individual flag true on every base singleton. Let q be the least OR count of a monotone decoder for threshold two on the base labels. Give the decoder the same flags plus one additional independent raw x. Then threshold two on the enlarged label set requires at least q+2 decoder ORs.

Assume at most q+1 and among such decoders minimize the gate count. Setting x=0 retains the base task. Every live path from x to output has a first OR, since a positive survives at x=0. Therefore there is exactly one first OR on all paths: two distinct first ORs could both be contracted on zeroing x. The standard first-OR factorization implies x has exactly one reader. If multiple AND readers led to that OR, factor x from their entire AND prefixes and reinsert it once at the OR; a direct OR reader alongside another route gives an absorption. Both rewrites preserve or decrease OR count and reduce gates.

A sole AND reader x∧P requires P true on every base singleton, since adding x changes that singleton's result. Thus P is the base nonemptiness function on the allowed inputs. It is not an input flag by hypothesis. Its producer must be an OR: either operand of an AND producing P is also P on the permitted inputs, since both vanish on empty. The AND would therefore be redundant. On x=0 remove P's producer and supply constant true in its place; every nonempty base input already satisfied P, and empty still rejects by monotonicity below a negative singleton. Remove a first downstream OR of the AND reader by propagating its zero. This second OR is distinct from P's producer. Constant propagation gives a base decoder with at most q−1 ORs. Constants in the interim may be eliminated by identities, with nonconstant final output.

A sole OR reader x∨U has U false on every base singleton, else adding x there would be invisible. Redirect its uses to x and append a final OR with U. On U=false the circuit is unchanged; on U=true its target is already positive. The bank and the size and OR caps are preserved, so minimality and the preceding arguments force x to still have a single OR reader. Repeat. Each step bypasses an old OR on the forward route of x. No appended OR can directly read x (it would carry x unconditionally to output). The finite circuit cannot support this indefinitely. This contradiction proves the increment.

The enlarged input list of flags still has no flag true on every singleton. Iteration therefore gives an increment of two ORs per new independent raw. The counts are relative to the fixed flag interface; no claim is made about a bank in which a nonemptiness flag is free.
