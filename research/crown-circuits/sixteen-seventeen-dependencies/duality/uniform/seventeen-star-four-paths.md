# The three-star and four two-edge paths

The proposed interface has 13 ORs and eight ANDs on the edge unions of K1,3+4P3. Restoring six ORs for the bank produces T11 of size 27. All bounds below use binary monotone gates and arbitrary sharing. The local three-star requires two ORs: pin its center true to obtain T2(3). Adding the first short path costs at least two ORs by deleting one leaf and then the remaining independent raw, both essential with a surviving positive. The combined base has no flag meeting all singleton edges, so each further short path adds three and each independent raw adds two, by the independent-raw increment. In particular the cap 13 is saturated. Removing one leaf leaves K1,3+3P3+K2, of floor 12. Thus each leaf has just one first OR under erasure, and the single-frontier refactorization of sixteen-pendant-readers.md gives it a sole reader. Choose the minimum under these caps; the physical size floor prevents any saving.

As in sixteen-two-deletions.md, call a path exceptional if a leaf reads an AND or its center reads an AND true on either local singleton. At most one is exceptional by that note's two-raw specialization from T11/27 to T9/21; only the marked short banks and the composition of those two sizes enter the argument.

Suppose first there is no exceptional path. All eight short leaves read ORs. A branching short center must have two OR exits by sixteen-center-branch.md. Its two exits and both leaf ORs are distinct (a leaf already implies its center, and a common leaf OR duplicates the center), so erasing its path leaves at most nine ORs for K1,3+3P3, which needs ten. Hence each short center has one reader. Normalize clean signals to the final collector, or count them there as in degree/t17/path-four.md. Every singly read short flag in an OR costs one gate in its maximal OR group. Two flags from the same path cannot share by absorption/duplication. For flags from different paths, reassociate their shared group to identify them for a saving of one OR. The glued paths are a P5, a cubic nonstar, or a four-star. A P5 with the original three-star has the strong combined order-minus-two floor by full-order nonstar transfer. A cubic nonstar is already strong. A four-star with the three-star needs seven by peeling one leaf to the two-three-star six-OR lower. Thus the fused fifteen-flag instance needs at least thirteen, exceeding the remaining twelve. A singly read short center with an AND reader has a dedicated clean product (only its own leaves activate that center and the product is locally cold). Distinct centers cannot share one such product: choose one of the two paths so its two leaf ORs differ from a first proper OR downstream of the product, and erase it. This removes three ORs; the surviving center is unused. The three-star, the two untouched paths and the two newly independent raws require at least 2+2+3+4=11, against ten remaining. Upon erasing all short paths their charged injection ORs disappear and their dedicated clean terms vanish, leaving a positive from the isolated three-star and thus one collector-OR saving per term. Twelve distinct OR savings leave at most one for a local function requiring two.

Now take one exceptional path. For an AND-read leaf erase that leaf, bypassing its bank, its AND and a first proper downstream OR (there is a positive avoiding the path). For a locally hot center with OR-read leaves, erase the sibling of its accepted singleton, bypassing its bank, the erased leaf OR and the center AND which now equals the surviving sibling. In either case the resulting ordinary T10 has at most 24 gates. Its minimum is 24, so exactly these gates disappear: the minor has seventeen ORs, seven ANDs and the intact, disjoint banks of K1,3+3P3, as well as one independent raw. Its relative decoder has twelve ORs.

Use degree/t13-seven/ten-minor.md and degree/t16-cyclic-build/ten-forest.md on this tight minor. The displayed bank trees survive regular terminal normalization. For every raw in one of them, its bank use cannot be its only read since adding it to a sibling singleton must change output. No raw has an AND reader or three uses. Relocation of another raw does not bypass a protected bank site: these sites lie inside the restored raw-only disjunctions and have no such other raw operand. Clean-terminal normalization bypasses a gate reading a clean nonraw signal, which none of the protected bank sites does. Liveness persists by size minimality. Use the original private partial join inside the triple bank; it has no outside readers, and the preceding transformations do not duplicate its use (sole-raw relocation cannot move onto that private wire, since its only reader is the full bank gate). Hence in the simple flattened incidence graph the three triple raws share a vertex and each of the three pairs shares a vertex.

The full graph is a forest of stars by ten-forest.md and has v−3 relative ORs. No degree d≥4 is possible. A sole star costs 2d−4; for d≥4 this exceeds (d+1)−3. With other components the first remote costs at least its order minus one by peeling to and then erasing an independent raw; all remaining remotes cost full order. Thus a d-star and remotes cost at least v+d−6≥v−2. The four protected centers are distinct since coincident centers would have degree at least four. The respective 3,2,2,2 raw edges form distinct star components since any connected pair of centers of degree ≥2 would be a nonstar. The independent tenth raw cannot touch a leaf (the resulting component is a nonstar), or the triple center (degree four). Touching a double center would yield two three-stars, with joint floor six, and full-order remotes give v−2. Consequently the graph is exactly K1,3+3P3+K2.

Each of the eleven degree-one vertices (three on the triple, six on the short paths, and the two K2 endpoints) is an actual mixed OR group, because its raw has two OR readers and a real pure OR group cannot have just one raw. Each costs a separate decoder OR beyond the five bank gates. In each remaining short center group either a nonraw operand joins its two raws, requiring another OR beyond the bank, or the group is a pure doubleton. A branching pure doubleton has two OR exits by degree/t13-seven/ten-three-groups.md. Erasing its two raws then loses both exits plus the two leaf-group injection ORs in the decoder. Those four gates are distinct: an exit into its own singly-raw leaf group would absorb that raw, since the center dominates it. The rest of the bank, K1,3+2P3+K2, requires 2+2+3+2=9 ORs, versus eight left. This erasure counts relative to the reconstructed graph decoder, where singly-raw groups can be reassociated to expose one tagged injection. If a center exit lies in the same group as its own leaf, reassociation exhibits the absorbed leaf and contradicts physical minimality directly.

Hence each pure short center is private and feeds an AND. That AND is clean, since its center side must have at least two exclusive singleton labels by ten-minor.md and has exactly two labels total. Each such product can serve at most two centers. A positive consisting of two three-star raws survives with none of the short centers true, requiring a further clean collector term, so each marked product charges one collector OR. Thus the three short centers require at least two more decoder ORs, even granting a shared clean product. These are separate from the eleven singleton-bearing mixed injections. Thirteen exceed twelve, finishing the exceptional case.
