# Four diagonal-clean AND gates cannot realize the 12-crown in 40 gates

On the one-row, one-column promise, consider a monotone circuit of at most 40 binary gates with four AND gates. If all four AND outputs are false on all twelve diagonal cells, the circuit cannot compute inequality. Arbitrary sharing and raw inputs are allowed.

Discard dead gates. Each of the 24 raw inputs is essential. Erasing a row literal makes its positive cells indistinguishable from the corresponding column singleton, which must be false by monotonicity below its diagonal. The column case is symmetric. Give the output one observer edge. Associate one distinct bit with each of the eight AND operand ports. Traverse the circuit backwards. The reverse mask of a wire is the union of its outgoing edge contributions. An edge to an AND operand contributes that operand's singleton bit. An edge to an OR contributes the reverse mask of that OR's output. The observer contributes zero. The masks record exactly the AND operand ports reachable through OR gates. Each wire costs at most its outdegree minus one unions. With s physical gates, all reverse masks together need at most (2s+1)−(24+s)=s−23≤17 binary unions from the eight singleton bits. This budget holds independently of AND diagonal values.

Sever all AND outputs to form the OR shadows of their operands. A raw port appears in a shadow exactly when its reverse mask contains that operand bit. On each diagonal, every shadow agrees with its physical operand because all ANDs are false. A label therefore occupies at most one side of each AND. On a positive cross there is a first true AND. Otherwise an all-OR path from one raw indicator would accept a diagonal. Both operands of the first true AND equal their shadows on this input. Neither raw port can supply both sides since its same-label diagonal rejects the AND. Thus the row mask of one label and the column mask of the other occupy opposite sides of some coordinate, for every ordered pair of distinct labels.

Each label defines a partial assignment in four bits, fixing coordinates where either raw port has membership. The row and column masks are subsets of its signed support and their union is that support. The twelve extension subcubes are pairwise disjoint. A necessary feasibility test chooses both masks and requires opposite occupied signs for every ordered cross. When a pair of labels disagrees on just one common coordinate, both raw ports of each label are forced to use it.

The program fourbits_reps.cpp enumerates all twelve disjoint subcubes in four dimensions modulo coordinate permutations, flips and label permutations. There are 73,852 unordered packings and 279 orbits. A subcube of size eight or more cannot fit beside eleven nonempty subcubes. For each orbit, fourand_clean_all.py tests whether its masks are realizable by at most 17 reverse unions. The universe includes every nonempty subset of any possible output mask; all useful intermediate unions are in this universe. Each selected nonsingleton requires two available proper subsets with exactly that union. Proper containment orders the DAG. A cardinality constraint limits selected nonsingletons to 17. All 279 instances return UNSAT.

Compile fourbits_reps.cpp with C++20 and save stdout as fourbit-packings.txt. Run python fourand_clean_all.py 50000 with Python-SAT and CaDiCaL 1.9.5. Results appear in fourand-clean-all.out. The script imports data loading and SAT dependencies from fourand_transpose.py.
