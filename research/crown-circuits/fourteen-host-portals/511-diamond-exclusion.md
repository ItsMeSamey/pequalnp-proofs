# Mask 511 plus the diamond

Take L=K5 minus one edge, R=K4 minus edge uv, and y,z the universal R vertices. Assume at most seven ORs and fifteen binary gates. We use the certified four-OR floor on L (the 237 subgraph), C(10)=24, C(8)=19, and the established small-projection requirement that uv is accepted. The last condition determines the small function F=uv ∨ ((u∨v)yz): the five other pairs reject and all four triples accept.

Each small universal spoils at least three L edges: setting just that universal and such an edge makes the output true. Otherwise retain seven good edges. The five L vertices all remain incident since their degrees were at least three. Build their flags in 14−5=9 ORs. Pin the chosen universal y to one and let three additional raw inputs activate its three neighbors independently. When a virtual edge is selected the resulting flags are exactly those of the selected real and virtual edges. Without virtual inputs each real singleton rejects by goodness, and each real pair accepts by monotonicity; zero rejects by monotonicity. The pinned flag is essential (for example adding y to uz changes rejection to acceptance), so pinning saves at least one gate. This yields ten-input threshold two in 9+14=23 gates. Likewise each L universal spoils at least two R edges: keep four good R edges, whose four vertices remain incident since initial degree was at least two. Pin the L universal to one, adding four virtual star inputs; this gives eight-input threshold two in 4+14=18 gates. The exclusion below needs only one spoiled edge for each y and z.

Call a wire live if it evaluates to one on all L flags true and R flags false. Other wires are cold. The observed output is live. An AND is live exactly when both operands are live. Let k count ORs with exactly one cold operand (exits), m ORs with both cold. These all simplify on L, so k+m≤3, and dependence on R requires k≥1.

At each exit's cold operand and each of the two operands of each cold OR expand through cold ANDs, stopping at R raw flags, earlier cold OR outputs, or live factors. The result is a conjunction of a fixed raw R monomial and earlier stopped nonraw factors. Empty monomials count as slots too. Induction in gate order shows that, on the full input cube, all live signals and cold OR outputs depend on R only through these k+2m fixed raw monomials.

Some monomial must equal singleton y: otherwise all features agree on y alone and empty, contradicting spoiling. Likewise for z. At y=z=1 the small function is u∨v, requiring one feature containing u but not v, and another containing v but not u, by comparing the singleton choices of u,v to neither. There are at least four distinct monomials. If exactly four slots exist, the two other features are singleton u and singleton v: at y=z=0 the function is uv and deletion of either flag from the positive pair must change its unique feature.

If k=1, all live signals before the exit are pure L. At L=0 every such signal vanishes; after the sole exit the live subsystem computes only a monotone unary function of its value. Since F is nonconstant, its projection equals that exit's cold operand. The three minimal terms uv,uyz,vyz require at least two ORs, as a circuit with at most one OR is a union of at most two monomials. Therefore m=2 and both cold ORs must occur and survive in the exit's cold operand on R. No live pre-exit factor enters that cold cone. Its entrance would be a cold AND with a pure L factor; this AND becomes zero on R. Trace a path of cold gates from it to the exit operand: the first OR simplifies, contradicting the minimum, or without an intervening OR the entire operand vanishes. Consequently the exit operand is pure R and equals F globally. It is zero on every single R edge as at empty. The live exterior cannot distinguish a single L edge from that edge plus an R edge, a contradiction.

For k≥2,m=0 there are at most three slots, impossible. The only other case is k=2,m=1. The four monomials are the four distinct singleton flags. Each exit's cold operand has a distinct obligatory singleton. Unless one is y, both exit operands vanish on y alone just as on empty for every fixed L, so the live subsystem cannot detect spoiling by y. Likewise z must gate an exit. Thus the two exits have obligatory factors y,z, and when y=z=0 the entire live output is independent of u,v. This contradicts F=uv on that slice.

The proof is local to the specified host and uses the cited small-projection requirement.
