# The 507 plus K4 host cannot meet the seven-OR, fifteen-gate decoder budget

Take L to be the five-flag complete tripartite graph with parts 1,2,2, and R the four-flag complete graph. A monotone binary decoder rejects every single edge and accepts the union of every pair of distinct edges. Assume at most fifteen gates and at most seven ORs. We use the established local lower bounds of four ORs on L and seven gates on threshold two (equivalently threshold three) on four inputs. The four-OR floor for L is the inherited negative graph 237 after swapping labels 2 and 3, as documented in fourteen-eight-cases/nine-vertex-decoder-budget.md.

In the L-only restriction at least eight gates remain. Its central flag is essential: adding it to an edge between the two noncentral parts yields a positive. Fixing this flag to one leaves threshold two on the four independent flags of its four incident edges. The all-zero assignment rejects by monotonicity from a rejecting singleton. Thus the center costs at least one gate in addition to the seven-gate minor. The R-only restriction computes threshold three on four flags and costs at least seven gates.

Classify each wire by the restrictions under which it is nonzero: L-only, R-only, bilateral, or neither. For this classification evaluate the two all-ones local assignments, using monotonicity. The output is bilateral; raw flags are unilateral. A birth is an OR joining an L-only wire to an R-only wire; it is bypassed in both restrictions. Let k be their number. The first bilateral signal must be a birth, so k≥1. Since L needs four ORs, k≤3. An R injection is an OR of a bilateral wire and an R-only wire. It too bypasses on L.

Whenever local simplifications use subsets of the original physical gates, let D be the number absent in both and S the number retained in both. The sum of the local sizes is at most 15−D+S. The local lower bounds force S≥D≥k. The local simplifications below may choose which physical gates to keep, bypassing a gate if its output equals an available signal and deleting dead gates. A gate retained in both after ordinary zero propagation has two bilateral operands. Gates generating or extending unilateral signals and births cannot be shared.

If k=1, at least two R injections are required. Otherwise write h for the R projection of the sole birth. With no R injection, every bilateral R projection equals h. With just one, every bilateral R projection before that injection equals h, and afterwards every one is either h or h∨u, with u the R-only input to the injection. These possibilities form a chain closed under both operations. L-only or neither inputs to a bilateral OR contribute zero on R. All gates with two bilateral operands can therefore be bypassed on R, giving S=0, impossible.

If k=2, at least one R injection is required. Suppose none exists. Before the second birth every bilateral wire projects to the first birth h on R. Replace those copies by h everywhere, including their uses in the unilateral cone of the second birth. After the second birth, the bilateral R outputs belong to the lattice on h and the second birth j, comprising h,j,h∧j,h∨j. Unilateral wires arising after that birth cannot influence the projected bilateral output: they would need another birth or an R injection to reenter, or contribute zero from L. Therefore the final bilateral computation needs at most one doubly surviving gate. If its function is a meet or join of incomparable h,j, retain the earliest occurrence of that value, which must combine h and j; all preceding bilateral values can be bypassed or discarded when tracing that occurrence. Even if the other nontrivial lattice value was constructed earlier, combining it with h or j or with itself cannot produce the desired opposite value. Thus S≤1, contradicting S≥2.

Consequently in all three cases the births and R injections already account for at least three ORs bypassed on L: 1+2, 2+1 or 3+0. The total is exactly three. No other OR may bypass on L. In particular no OR produces an R-only wire, since both its operands would vanish on L. Call these three births and R injections the portals.

In the R restriction, the R-only operand entering each portal is a conjunction of a fixed subset of the raw R flags and earlier bilateral signals. Indeed no OR produces R-only wires; the inputs to an R-only AND are all R-live and hence either R-only or bilateral. Expand the R-only ANDs, stopping at raw flags or bilateral wires. All remaining bilateral gates combine earlier bilateral wires, or bypass on R, apart from the three portals. By induction their R projections depend only on the three fixed raw monomials, one per portal. In particular the R output factors through these three monomials.

For distinct flags i,j in R compare the positive triple missing i to the negative pair obtained by deleting j from that triple. Some monomial must distinguish the two assignments. Its fixed raw subset contains j but omits i. Thus the four raw flags have pairwise incomparable membership columns among the three monomials. The Boolean lattice on three elements has width three. The required four columns do not exist.

This excludes the 507 plus K4 host at the specified budget, independently of how the decoder interleaves its two components. It does not settle either of the other five-vertex masks.
