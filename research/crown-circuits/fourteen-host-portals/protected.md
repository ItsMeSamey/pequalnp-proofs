# Extensions of the fixed 507 decoder

The five large flag inputs feed the eight-gate circuit in ../../fourteen-eight-cases/controls/507-four-by-four.json. Its flag fanouts are (1,2,2,2,2). A proposed whole-host decoder has fifteen gates, seven ORs and eight ANDs. On the four small-component flag inputs with all large flags zero, it computes threshold three on four flags: K4's six edges are the negative pairs and every triple is an endpoint union of two edges. The minimum is seven by Boolean duality from threshold two on four inputs.

A live closed copy of the eight-gate large circuit cannot merely be combined with external gates, even if arbitrary intermediate wires are tapped. On the small restriction all eight gates vanish, as does at least one external gate accepting a large-only operand on a path to output. Thus at most six gates remain.

More generally, suppose eight distinguished original gates retain their operations and the Boolean function of EACH original input pin when all four small flags are zero. Altered signal paths and shared insertions are permitted. At least one distinguished gate must combine two incomparable restricted functions on the small slice. Otherwise each of the eight gates on that slice can be bypassed, since an AND or OR of comparable functions equals one operand. The first remaining physical gate reached from both components has a purely large and a purely small predecessor. It is external: no distinguished input pin vanishes identically on the large-only slice. At least this external gate also disappears on the small slice. This leaves at most six gates. This excludes a single small-side signature, including its duplication onto several original ports.

There is a further fanout restriction, independent of which eight gates are distinguished. Denote by F the total fanout of the five large flags. Set them zero, evaluating small inputs at all ones. A gate now zero is identically zero on the small slice. Let D count gates with two zero operands, A count ANDs with exactly one zero operand, and B count ORs with exactly one zero operand. Every zero-output gate has at least one user and the output is live and nonzero. Counting input slots gives 2D+A+B ≥ F+D+A. Removing these gates or bypassing their zero inputs therefore saves D+A+B ≥ F+A gates. It follows F+A≤8.

Since all five large inputs must be used, at least one of the four formerly twice-read flags must have a single physical reader. More precisely at least 1+A of those four flags must have a single reader, allowing additional fanout on other flags only to strengthen this requirement. In the pin-preserving class each such reader is an OR(v,S), where S vanishes identically when all small flags vanish. A sole AND cannot distinguish v from zero with all other inputs false. For a sole OR, if S were already true at any large-only assignment of the other flags, changing v could not affect any wire after that reader, contradicting retention of the exact raw-v function at the original ports. S is independent of v by acyclicity and sole use.

These arguments use full Boolean preservation of original pin functions where indicated. They do not decide unrestricted rewiring of the 507 host or the full nine-vertex instance.
