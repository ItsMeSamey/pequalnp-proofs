# Restrictions of the diamond and the nine-edge five-vertex component

Let L be K5 minus one edge and D be K4 minus one edge. Assume a monotone binary decoder with at most fifteen gates and seven ORs rejects the flag unions of single host edges and accepts the union of any two distinct edges. These conclusions concern this fixed host and decoder budget.

Let y be a universal vertex in one component, with d neighbors. Call an opposite edge harmless if its two flags together with y still reject. Choose t harmless edges spanning v nonisolated vertices. Make one independent input per selected edge, and an additional input per neighbor of y. Force y true, feed each neighbor its corresponding input, and feed each opposite vertex the disjunction of its incident selected inputs (or zero for an isolated vertex). The bank uses 2t−v ORs. An opposite singleton rejects by harmlessness. A neighbor singleton rejects as an original edge through y. Every pair of distinct inputs contains the union of two original host edges and accepts. Monotonicity proves threshold two on all t+d inputs. The all-zero input rejects by comparison to a singleton.

The restricted decoder loses at least one gate: y is essential because an edge between two of its neighbors rejects while adding y creates a pair of edges. Fixing y bypasses every direct gate reading y. Thus the new circuit uses at most 14 decoder gates, and at most seven decoder ORs.

For y in D, d=3. Seven harmless L edges span all five vertices since L has minimum degree three. Seven would give threshold two on ten inputs with 9+14=23 gates, contradicting the established exact minimum 24. Consequently y spoils at least three L edges. If precisely three are spoiled and the six harmless edges span all five vertices, the minor has nine inputs, at most 7+14=21 gates and at most 7+7=14 ORs. The established secondary bound requires at least eight ANDs at the fourteen-OR floor for nine inputs. Therefore this case is impossible. The only six-edge graph on four vertices is K4, so in the remaining three-spoiled case the spoiled edges are exactly the three edges at one endpoint of the unique missing L edge.

More generally any spanning harmless subgraph with t edges in L gives at most 2t−5+7=2(t+3)−4 ORs, the OR floor. Hence a diamond center with any direct OR reader has a nonspanning harmless graph: it spoils an entire vertex star in L. If five harmless edges span L, threshold two on eight inputs needs 19 gates, and the bank uses five. Exactly fourteen decoder gates survive. Its one reader of y must be an AND, and there are no further gate savings in this restriction.

In the opposite direction, let y be one of the three universal L vertices, so d=4. Four harmless D edges span all four vertices. The resulting threshold on eight inputs uses at most 4+14=18 gates, contradicting its minimum 19. Each such L flag spoils at least two D edges. In fact its harmless graph cannot span four vertices: for any such spanning t edges the minor on t+4 inputs uses at most 2t−4+7=2t+3 ORs, below the floor 2t+4. Thus the spoiled edges include an entire vertex star of D. If exactly two are spoiled, they are precisely the star at one of its two degree-two vertices, and the remaining three form a triangle.

Dependencies: exact minima for threshold two on eight and ten inputs, the OR floor 2n−4, and the secondary AND bound at that floor on nine inputs. No solver enumeration is used.
