# Three state minors for one dirty gate of continuation width one

Use the regular five-AND, size-34 representative in dirty-conflicts.md. Write T for the sole signed clean continuation pin, O for its opposite, and P,M for the two dirty tags. Quotient out the unobserved dirty selector on the inactive part. The trace universe is the product of three states I,+,− with the three outside Boolean axes (24 atoms). O,P,M accept respectively I,+,− and T accepts + and −. Every raw deficit is a nonempty product of a subset of states with a consistent outside face. Different raws have disjoint deficits. Fourteen raw sinks and at most seven intermediate unions suffice; T is primitive.

Write r_s for the number of receivers rejecting exclusively within state s. Delete s. On the other two states all state primitives become signs or constants on one Boolean axis. The 14−r_s remaining receivers give disjoint nonempty faces of the ordinary four-cube. Delete the r_s raw sinks. Every OR which immediately uses the vanishing primitive can be bypassed too. Such a path cannot terminate only at an erased raw, because any raw containing s accepts every word in that state, whereas an erased raw must reject some such word. There is at least one surviving use: O is used by the at least six opposite-side partners; either dirty tag is used by an irreducibly hot raw which rejects on I. Thus after removing g distinct effective direct primitive uses the remaining bank costs at most 21−r_s−g joins, g≥1.

A bank of B joins with m observations and masks of pairwise conflicting signs yields threshold two on m labels in at most B+m−1 physical gates. Remove axes with no conflict and constants introduced by restriction. For k remaining axes all 2k sign sources are live. Transposition of the OR bank uses at most B+m−2k ORs by outdegree counting, then k binary ANDs and a collector of k−1 ORs test the k opposing sign pairs. At r_s=1 this gives ≤31 gates versus C(13)=32. At r_s=0 the fourteen four-axis faces contradict the existing four-axis, 21-union bound. Hence r_s≥2 for all three states. At r_s=2 or 3 the twelve- and eleven-input bounds C(12)=29,C(11)=27 imply g≤1, so the vanishing primitive has precisely one effective direct use.

The sole primitive use must be a helper s∨X: at least two raw users need the state primitive. At s=I both active-exclusive species of receivers must use O; each also contains its respective opposite dirty tag but cannot contain the tag for its own rejecting state. Consequently X is outside-only. All O-partners reject inside X's outside face in the two active states. Since at least six labels are partners, X must be precisely one outside sign. At s=+ or −, the hot raw forbids O in X and an opposite-state-exclusive partner forbids T and the other dirty tag. X is again outside-only. With at least two opposite-state-exclusive partners in the same active panel, X has at most two distinct signs. If it has two, precisely two such partners cover the individual cells on that outside edge. A hot singleton whose outside mask is just X can be cleaned with the existing X and one reattachment T∨X.

A separate recoloring argument applies without the numerical hypothesis on r_s. At an occurrence of a dirty-tag primitive with no O-labelled descendants, replace that occurrence by T. This only enlarges acceptance on active states; every nonpartner raw affected retains its nonempty inactive face, with the identical outside signs. It introduces no dirty firing on any new singleton. If every occurrence of one dirty sign contributing to a particular hot raw lacks a partner descendant, simultaneously recolor all such occurrences, cooling that raw. Therefore in a representative also minimizing the number of hot labels, each sign at every hot raw has an occurrence whose cone also contains an opposite-side partner using that same sign.
