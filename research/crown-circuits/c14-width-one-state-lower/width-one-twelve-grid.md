# Twelve-label clean minor at width one

When r_s=2, one-dirty-width-one-state-minors.md gives at most 18 surviving joins, of which twelve are distinct receiver joins. The exact lower bound C(12)=29 forbids imposing any further savings. No primitive literal is a receiver in the resulting four-cube since a halfcube leaves at most eight atoms for eleven other labels. Thus a normal bank has at most six proper helpers and each of the twelve computed targets is a distinct last join. We may pad to six for upper bounds.

At least eight targets are full, by counting sixteen atoms. Write j for the number of triple helpers, the rest being pairs. Full targets require either a triple plus one remaining sign, or two complementary pair helpers. Thus there are at most 2j+floor((6−j)²/4) full targets (the coordinate-pair disjointness graph is triangle-free). If j=1,2,3 the bound equals eight; equality imposes a tiling with eight fulls and four width-three targets. Pair helpers must attain the maximum edge count and form one bipartition (2,3), (2,2) or (1,2) on complementary coordinate pairs. Every width≥3 target lies in a pair-helper face; the common complement of the available two sides is nonempty, contradiction. At j≥4, at most two pair-helper faces cover no more than eight atoms. At most one low target may have width ≤2 by volume, hence at least eleven must lie within those pair faces. Therefore j=0.

Six pair helpers generating at least eight fulls require all six in a single coordinate bipartition: if split between different complementary-pair types, at most floor(5²/4)=6 fulls occur (or 4+1 for 4 and 2). Only (2,4) and (3,3) can work. In (3,3), eight fulls require all other targets width three and all sixteen atoms covered, but one cell is outside the pair-helper union. Nine fulls fill the whole 3-by-3 rectangle; each available helper slice has only one atom left, so no width-three target fits inside it. The remaining three low targets would exceed volume. For (2,4) all eight full products are occupied. Each of the four additional targets must be a width-three edge in a fixed four-side slice joining the two missing two-side patterns. These patterns must therefore be adjacent, and each additional target fixes the common sign defining their half. The eight fulls occupy the opposite half; all four edges have the same free axis.

Suppose r_I=2. The unique first O helper O∨η from the state-minor argument confines all p≥6 opposite-side partners to the η-half on both active panels. Among 14−p nonpartners, at most four avoid the η-half on the inactive panel, and at most two more (the I-exclusive raws) can meet that half without consuming active atoms there. Thus 8−p nonpartner survivors and the p partners must all meet its eight active atoms. Equality forces each partner to occupy a single active atom and exactly four other nonpartners to avoid this half on I, occupying individual outside cells. In the projected twelve-label grid, the eight fulls form one coordinate half. It must be the η-half, since a different coordinate half meets it in at most four atoms and at least six partners are full. The four width-three targets outside the half have singleton outside faces; hence their free coordinate is the active-state selector, not an outside axis.

The original bank therefore has exactly its single disappearing O∨η helper and the six surviving projected helpers of the (2,4) grid. These are four pairs on the two outside axes other than η, and two pairs of η with opposite dirty tags. None can contain T or both dirty tags, as it would project to a universal signal and could not survive as a proper grid helper. Every tagged helper carries η as its only outside axis, since projecting away O does not erase any outside sign. A raw receiving both dirty tags must join two opposite single-tag inputs and so has at most this one outside sign. Reattachment to T or T∨η removes its dirty firing without changing its rejecting face. This contradicts hot-label minimality. Consequently at least three distinct raw labels reject exclusively on the inactive panel.
