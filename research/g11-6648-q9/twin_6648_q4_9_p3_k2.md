# Three starred edges forming P3 plus K2

On original G11 assume least size 21, TopForm, j=3, c=0 and 4≤q≤9. Suppose the original star graph has hub a, leaves l,r and an isolated edge de. This is impossible. The argument allows overlapping Lambda gates for the two leaves, repeated dirty suppliers, and any original sole reader at input 3.

Write W={a,l,r,d,e}, O=V minus W. Every ordinary top in the first group has one raw pin {a} and the other a nonempty subset of {l,r}. In the second group the raw pins are {d},{e}. If a physical operand or its function is shared by two different tops, regrouping the OR suffix and factoring saves a gate. Thus neither group's distinct tops reuse an operand. The original gate identity is 2t+λ=19−q. The low-false floor gives t≥3.

**Two mandatory literal junk gates are impossible.** Suppose two distinct junk ANDs imply exterior literals u,v. Set those literals false. The remaining junk gate, if it survives, is a conjunction of two raw disjunctions, since all earlier dirty alternatives vanish and TopForm excludes clean AND ancestors. At every ordinary top this one gate can OR-reach at most one original side; otherwise its original independent positive witness would pass the clean top. Thus the remaining nonstar edges are covered by one clean three-clause product (the junk's pins and the union of opposite ordinary raw guards) on the restricted graph. All its edges have a vertex cover that is a clique of size ≤3, by the three-clause argument in 6648-q9-three-matching-saturation.md. The remaining exterior vertices form an independent set, since their edges would require two positive junk suppliers at an ordinary top.

Cover G by u,v, this clique, the hub a, and either d or e. Since τ(G)=7, these must achieve equality: the clique is a triangle, contains at most one remaining exterior vertex and is disjoint from u,v,a and from both d,e (either choice gives a cover). It therefore contains l,r and an exterior vertex. Now l,r have two common neighbors, this exterior vertex and a. This contradicts absence of four-cycles. In particular at most one original junk gate directly reads an exterior literal.

**Reader and import costs.** As in 6648-two-disjoint-stars-three-junk.md, a sole exterior reader is private and cannot also read a second raw variable. A sole OR's sibling rejects every singleton: a singleton masking its literal would contradict the two-neighbor and no-four-cycle conditions. With six exterior variables, sole readers consume a private reader and every other variable consumes ≥2 slots in the remaining binary readers. There are at least six distinct direct exterior readers, including at least five paid ORs. No ordinary pin or Lambda OR can raw-read an exterior vertex.

Let h count the paid gates on OR-only traces of ordinary operands that first import a raw-empty operand into a positive W-only signal (one positive arm and one empty arm). On any exterior edge both sides of a winning top need such an import. The two imports differ or their common raw vertex would activate both pins. Hence 2≤h≤q−5≤4. Each import belongs to precisely one of the four disjoint raw-support groups: hub, leaves, d, e. Denote their counts by A,B,C,D. To win on an exterior edge either A,B≥1 or C,D≥1. Every subsequent OR on such a trace forms a union of raw labels and imported signals. A singleton group with n imports has ≤2^n pin functions and ≤2^n−1−n Lambda gates; duplicate full-cube functions violate leastness. Pure leaf traces have ≤1 Lambda; with one leaf import they have ≤2: the only nontrivial pure union is l∨r, and beyond the import itself at most one new mixed superset can be formed by adding the other leaf. These bounds allow arbitrary shared ORs and earlier common donors.

With h=2, if P3 is the joint group it has ≤2 hub pin functions, the isolated edge one, and λ≤2. If K2 is joint it has ≤2 tops, P3 one, and λ≤1. Thus 2t+λ≤8, versus the required ≥10. This settles q≤7. At h=3 all imports must be in P3. Indeed, if K2 is joint and the third import is there, t≤3, λ≤2; if in P3 at the hub, t≤4 and λ≤1; if at the leaves, t≤3 and λ≤2. If P3 is joint and the third is at K2, t≤3 and λ≤2. Every case gives 2t+λ<10.

**Feedback into the junk gates.** All six physical junk pins have nonempty exterior trace by 6648-five-endpoints-six-pin-rigidity.md, which uses the original MIS rectangle bounds and τ(G[O])≥2. Therefore a W-only raw-positive signal can influence a junk gate along an OR-only pin path only by encountering an OR with an empty-exterior inlet and a positive-exterior output. Such a transfer is paid and outside the h traced W-only imports. Every W-positive influence entering any dirty gate has a first dirty destination of this form.

If h=4, q=9 and there are exactly five exterior direct paid ORs and one exterior direct junk gate. The reader lower bound is tight. Thus all multiple-reader exterior vertices have exactly two readers and each of those gates reads two distinct multiple-reader exterior literals. A private reader OR cannot consume a W-only positive sibling. No additional paid gate remains, hence no W-only positive decoder wire feeds the junk computation. All h imports are direct since any earlier raw-empty OR would be paid in addition to the nine exhausted slots.

For h=3, the isolated component de is a bare AND. Each of d,e has another graph neighbor. It therefore must affect some dirty gate via an OR-only path from the literal, since it occurs on no other raw star and every output correction uses a dirty gate. Both require a transfer. If there are five direct exterior ORs, the same tight-reader argument prohibits a transfer at these readers. At most one other paid gate remains. One transfer cannot carry both d and e without a preceding W-only OR containing both, which would be a second additional paid gate: the three imports lie in P3, and a Lambda containing d,e cannot reach any ordinary operand with either group's allowed raw supports. With no additional transfer this case is likewise impossible.

The remaining possibility is h=3,q=9 with six direct exterior ORs. The budget is exhausted and again all imports are direct. There is no W-only OR containing d or e: a paid one would be an extra gate unless a P3 import, and a Lambda with raw singleton d or e would have to be a pure redundant singleton as neither endpoint has an import. Thus their transfers separately read the literals d and e. Among at most seven total direct exterior readers there are at most two slots for a W-only positive sibling. To see this, remove the private sole readers, none of which can be a transferring OR. If k multiple-reader exterior labels remain, their ≥2k raw occurrences occupy at most k+1 binary gates. Only two places are left over. The d,e transfers use both. In particular no signal on the P3 decoder bank is needed by the junk computation. Keep the raw d,e transfer connections intact.

**Small replacement circuits.** Retain the three original dirty gates and all paid exterior ORs, five when h=4, six in the last h=3 case. This retained computation does not depend on any deleted endpoint bank gate: its inputs other than previous retained wires are raw inputs (including the two bare d,e transfers when present). Indeed any removed W-only gate feeding a retained exterior OR would itself be a transfer, already excluded. All other pretop ORs are Lambda decoder gates. Rebuild the output from the raw endpoint labels and the already retained dirty suppliers.

In the P3 group each top multiplies a disjunction of channels from {a} plus the A participating suppliers and a disjunction from {l,r} plus the B participating suppliers. K2 likewise uses (1+C) by (1+D) channels. Repeated suppliers are allowed and can be counted separately for this upper bound. Expanding and regrouping the union of the products gives a bipartite graph function on the channel lists; dependencies among channel values do not affect the identity. With one left channel and n right channels cost ≤n gates. With two left channels and n right channels cost ≤n+2: two subset disjunctions can be built with ≤n−1 ORs by computing their shared part once (disjoint supports cost even less), then use two ANDs and one OR. A 3-by-3 group costs ≤8: three subset disjunctions of three labels cost ≤3 gates (all pairs cost 3; a full set can reuse any requested pair). A 3-by-4 group costs ≤10: three subset disjunctions of four labels cost ≤5 gates. Three triples can use two selected pair intermediates; any two triples share a pair. With two triples and one other proper requested support, first use their common pair, add the triples and at most one further pair. A full set can reuse any maximal requested proper support, or be built directly when it alone is needed. All remaining cases cost at most five. Missing or duplicate rows reduce these bounds.

At h=3 all imports are in P3, split (A,B)=(2,1) or (1,2). Its 3-by-3 or 2-by-4 decoder costs ≤8. Add the isolated edge and one final OR, giving ≤10 gates, plus ≤9 retained gates. At h=4, with all four in P3 its largest decoder is 3-by-4, 4-by-3 or 2-by-5, costing ≤10. Add two gates for K2 and collector. With all four in K2, its largest cost is ≤8 and the pure P3 and collector cost three. For splits 3+1, P3 with three imports costs ≤8 when joint, ≤6 if one-sided, while K2 with one costs two. If K2 has three its cost is ≤5 and P3 with one costs ≤4. At a 2+2 split P3 costs ≤5 and K2 ≤4. Include one collector in each mixed case. Thus h=4 needs ≤12 decoder gates plus eight retained, below 21. This contradicts original leastness. No new solver or enumeration is used.
