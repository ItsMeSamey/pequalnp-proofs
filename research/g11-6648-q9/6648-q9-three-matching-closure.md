# Three disjoint original stars at q=9,c=0,j=3 are impossible

Assume the original least 21-gate G11 graph circuit in TopForm. Denote the five exterior labels by O. Each of the three matched star edges uv has a group of ordinary tops, all with raw pins {u},{v}. Use the original singleton-label endpoint ledger of 6648-q9-three-matching-endpoint.md. Imports are paid OR gates in an endpoint's positive raw trace with a raw-empty inlet. Denote their total by m. These imports cannot receive an exterior raw pin or the raw pin of a different endpoint. Distinct traces of the six endpoints count disjoint physical gates, regardless of sharing among dirty suppliers. In a group of k tops each endpoint must provide k distinct pin functions, implying k≤2^{m_v}; λ_v≤2^{m_v}−1−m_v. An endpoint with no imports is literally its raw input. A group with no imports at either endpoint is a single bare AND on its matched pair.

The issued size equation gives 2t+λ=10, with t≥3. We use the prior source proof 6648-q9-three-matching-saturation.md for m≤5 and exclusion of t=3. Those proofs need neither a low-false 19-gate floor nor a special original readership type: the optional low-endpoint observation at the end of its t=3 ledger does not enter its exterior-edge contradiction.

At t=4 the profile is (2,1,1), with λ=2. The repeated group requires one import at each endpoint. To supply two Lambda gates needs either three imports at a single endpoint or two at each of two endpoints. Thus m≥4. At m=4 all imports are on the repeated group and both singleton groups are bare. At m=5 there is still at least one bare group: covering both singleton groups requires four nonzero endpoint ledgers counting the two repeated endpoints; with at most five imports such a distribution gives at most one Lambda gate.

At t=5 the profiles are (3,1,1) and (2,2,1), with λ=0. The first needs at least two imports at each end of the triple group; hence m≥4 and at m=4 there are two bare groups, at m=5 at least one. The second requires at least one import at each of four repeated endpoints. If both endpoints of a repeated 2-group have only one import, their pin choices are raw and augmented raw. Its two pairs must take opposite orders: pairing the smaller functions and the larger functions produces a redundant smaller term, contradicting leastness. Both terms have a raw pin and neither accepts an exterior edge. At m=4 both repeated groups are of this kind and the singleton group is bare, so no exterior edge is accepted. At m=5, if the singleton group is nonbare, it has just one import on one side, both repeated groups are again of this kind, and no term accepts an exterior edge. The exterior has five vertices, while α(G11)=4. Thus all viable profiles at m=4 have at least two bare groups and all at m=5 have at least one.

A dirty AND cannot have two direct raw pins. Equal pins are redundant. Distinct pins forming a graph edge make the AND clean; if their pair is a nonedge this AND vanishes on every graph edge input. Setting it false preserves the target on all edge inputs, can only decrease the output, and therefore preserves the entire monotone graph function. Constant folding gives a smaller circuit. Also at most two dirty gates have any direct raw pin: if all three did then the three labels hit every residual edge (take the earliest active AND on the edge, which must be dirty unless the edge is starred). One representative from each starred edge together with these three labels would cover the graph, contradicting its cover number seven.

Let r count DISTINCT exterior labels directly read by at least one dirty AND, even if the same raw label is read at several gates. Then r≤2, and at most 2−r other raw labels can have dirty readers. The other 5−r exterior labels reserve at least two physical operand slots each among the 9−m nonimport paid ORs. Indeed every direct reader of such a label is one of these ORs: an exterior label does not raw-reach any ordinary top or Lambda. If it has two or more readers reserve two of its raw pin incidences. If it has only one, reserve that OR's two slots; its other pin must be singleton-negative and in particular cannot be raw. To see this, a positive singleton u there would mask toggling v, force uv to be a nonedge, and force every neighbor of v to be adjacent to u, violating minimum degree two and four-cycle-freeness. The sole reader's other pin is independent of v by acyclicity and sole readership. All these reservations are disjoint, including when two multiple-reader labels share an OR.

Suppose there are h bare pairs. Each of their 2h endpoints needs a direct reader in addition to its bare AND, as it has another graph neighbor. This extra reader cannot be Lambda, an endpoint import, or another ordinary top: any such path carrying a bare label into an ordinary pin would belong to that endpoint's trace. It must be a dirty AND or a nonimport paid OR. Consequently at least 2h−(2−r) different bare endpoint labels need raw slots in nonimport ORs, disjoint from the exterior reservations. At (m,h)=(4,2) or (5,1), the slot bound is
    2(9−m) ≥ 2(5−r)+2h−(2−r).
It forces r=2 and exact equality. A larger h is impossible by the same bound. This also handles repeated exterior raw labels at different dirty gates: r≤1 would already contradict the count.

With r=2 no dirty gate can read a bare label. Every bare endpoint c has exactly two readers, its pure matched AND c∧d and one more OR. All nonraw reserve slots belong only to sole exterior OR readers, whose two operands comprise that exterior raw variable and a nonraw sibling. Thus c's OR must be c∨v for a distinct raw v. A multiply read exterior label may well be the partner v, and the following pointwise argument allows it.

If v≠d, the pair cv cannot be an edge, since toggling c on {v} changes neither raw reader output. For any neighbor s≠d of c, the independent candidate {s,v} and its enlargement by c likewise give identical values to both readers of c, since d is absent. Hence vs is an edge. Thus c and v have all of N(c) minus {d} as common neighbors, implying deg(c)≤2 by four-cycle-freeness. If v=d, both bare endpoints have the same extra OR c∨d and the same bare AND. They are interchangeable in the graph function and have identical external neighborhoods. Minimum degree two supplies at least one such neighbor and C4-freeness allows only one, so both have degree two. If v≠d, applying the same argument to the mate gives degree two for both as well (if its partner were c, the shared OR would already be the only extra reader for c). The degree-two vertices of G11 form an independent set, so a bare matched edge cannot exist. This excludes both remaining ledger profiles and completes the source-only result. No enumeration or numerical replay is used.
