# Four junk ANDs and two touching starred edges in the q=9 column

Let the two starred edges be ab and ac. Assume the original size-21 least TopForm circuit has j=4, c=0 and q=9. Then 2t+λ=9 for the t clean ordinary tops. Each such top has centre raw set {a} and leaf raw set {b}, {c}, or {b,c}. All t physical centre inlets are distinct and all t physical leaf inlets are distinct, since two tops sharing an operand can be factored and the final clean output aggregation rebuilt to save one gate.

Every graph edge outside {a,b,c} activates two distinct junk ANDs at opposite OR terminals of a top: raw leaves on both sides are absent, and a single shared dirty terminal would make that top accept an independent input. At most two junk functions can each imply a raw literal, since otherwise {a,b,c} and those three raw labels cover the graph, contradicting τ(G11)=7. In particular at most two dirty ANDs directly read an outsider.

For the eight outsiders the direct-reader incidence count gives at least eight distinct gates. A sole AND reader cannot read another raw literal: it would mask the addition of its sole variable at one of its at least two neighbors. A sole OR reader's other operand is singleton-negative: an activating singleton would be a nonneighbor adjacent to every neighbor of the sole variable, impossible by C4-freeness and minimum degree two. Thus sole readers are private to that label, while each multiply-read outsider needs two incidences and remaining gates provide at most two apiece. Ordinary tops and Λ ORs cannot read outsiders directly by the definition of stars. At least six different paid exterior ORs follow.

Trace the top inlets backwards through raw-positive OR pins. Call an OR with one raw-empty pin an import. Let m_a and m_L count distinct imports in the centre and leaf traces, respectively. They are paid and distinct from the exterior ORs, so m_a+m_L≤3. Both counts are positive: if either were zero every accepted edge would meet that side's one or two raw labels. Every trace signal is the union of its original raw labels with some of the imported pin functions; the count is valid for shared functions, even when later imports depend on earlier ones. Duplicates of full-cube functions violate leastness. In particular t≤2^{m_a}. A Λ gate must lie in one of these traces. There are at most 2^{m_a}−1−m_a centre Λ gates. On the leaf side, singleton-label joins on v=b,c number at most 2^{m_v}−1−m_v each, where m_v counts singleton-v imports. Double-label joins number at most 2^{m_L}, allowing every possible union of {b,c} and imports. When m_L≤2 the total leaf bound is five, and when m_L≤1 it is two. These are upper bounds even if some positive-positive centre gates are paid rather than Λ.

* At t=1, λ=7 is impossible: (m_a,m_L) is at most (1,2) or (2,1), giving at most five or three Λ gates.
* At t=2, λ=5 forces (1,2); two centre imports and one leaf import give at most three Λ, and smaller totals are insufficient.
* At t=3, t≤2^{m_a} forces (2,1).
* At t=4, again (2,1) is forced. The centre needs four distinct wires, including at least one positive-positive join in addition to raw a and the two imports. Six exterior ORs and three imports exhaust q, so that join consumes the sole λ=1. Then no positive-positive leaf join is available. Only three distinct leaf wires can remain (raw b,c and one import), too few. Larger t is excluded by the size identity.

In the t=2,3 survivors, saturation gives exactly three imports, six exterior ORs and two dirty ANDs reading exterior variables. Every multiply-read outsider has exactly two readers and all gates in that part of the incidence count have two distinct raw outsider pins. A direct AND on two distinct raw vertices is either clean (if adjacent) or false on every graph edge (if nonadjacent). In the latter case setting it false everywhere preserves every graph minterm and can only shrink the circuit, contradicting leastness. Repeated identical pins are redundant. Thus the two exterior dirty ANDs W1,W2 have private raw inputs w1,w2 with unique readers. Every pretop wire's raw OR support has size at most two: exterior ORs are private with raw-empty sibling or read a pair of raw exteriors; endpoint traces are supported within {a} or {b,c}; there is no paid gate left. Every Λ appears on those endpoint traces. In particular no raw-empty OR exists: it would be dirty with empty raw support and a paid gate outside these lists. Each imported empty pin is an actual dirty AND.

The earliest dirty AND cannot be W_i=w_i∧T. Both operands are then pure, and T is independent of w_i and has at most two raw variables. All neighbors of w_i must be in T by the sole-reader sensitivity test on each adjacent pair. Since degree is at least two, either this is impossible or T equals the two neighbors and W_i is clean. Call the earliest dirty AND F. It implies a raw disjunction of size at most two.

One side of the top interface has only one import: the centre for t=2, or the leaf side for t=3. Let S be that side's raw label set ({a} or {b,c}) and G the physical dirty AND on the import pin. Every graph edge avoiding S must activate G, since every top needs an inlet on that side and all its raw labels are absent. Consequently G cannot be W1,W2, or F: the raw support bound on any of these would give a vertex cover of size at most four after including S. G is the fourth dirty AND.

If either pin of G lacks F as a terminal in its raw OR expansion, that pin is bounded by a disjunction of at most two raw labels and W1,W2. Each W_i implies w_i. Since edges avoiding S activate G and therefore that pin, S together with the two raw labels and w1,w2 would cover the graph with at most six vertices. Thus F occurs at both inlets and F implies G everywhere. The dirty independent witness of F prevents these two donors from occurring on opposite sides of any clean top. An edge outside {a,b,c,w1,w2} would have only F,G available but requires two compatible active donors. Hence those five vertices cover G11, the final contradiction.

This is an original-circuit source argument; it uses no graph enumeration or cofactor leastness.
