# Ordered binary sums over bounded adhesions

Use a rooted tree decomposition with adhesion at most a. Let r be label dimension and b the number of colours. Put F=b+r(b−1), P=a(F+1)+b. Every bag is of bounded size or has vertex connectivity at least b+13P+25(b−1)r+2 on its actual edges. On this class a polynomial algorithm for fixed parameters decides connected ordered sums and constructs a partition, with arbitrary decomposition depth and degree.

**Boundary records.** Record interface colours, per-colour sums of interior labels (omitting the interface), a partition of each interface colour's vertices, and the subset of colours closed inside. For an interface colour all components must touch the interface, and vertices in the same recorded block must be connected. Additional connections within a colour are allowed. A colour absent on the interface either has no vertices or exactly one nonempty connected component, recorded as closed. The table size depends only on a,b,r. Ordinary finite-state joins take transitive closures of child relations, sum interior labels counting each vertex once, and reject a closed colour occurring in another region. Upon forgetting a last representative of a component, it can close only if no other component of that colour remains. For weaker lower-bound partitions such a transition might fail unnecessarily for one weak record; completeness follows by using all actually guaranteed connections, and soundness follows because extra connections never break a successful transition. At a bounded bag use the bag as the working boundary, enumerate its colourings and process children one at a time. At the root require all colours closed.

**High-connectivity step.** For each component of a child interior off its adhesion preselect one neighboring adhesion vertex. Such a neighbor exists by connectedness of the host. Its default assignment colours the whole component with that vertex's colour. Its labels can be folded into that vertex independently of future colourings. In any valid signature witness retain all children containing a closed colour; there are at most b. The deviations of the remaining children from their default ordered sums lie in the space of tuples of total zero, of dimension r(b−1). Their total is a sum of at most r(b−1) of these vectors, by choosing a basis from the vectors. Retain these selected children too, and default all others. Ordered sums and palette are preserved. Prior connectivity need not survive this operation.

Enumerate up to F exceptional children and their records. Guess the exact palette appearing on the bag and if necessary a representative vertex for each colour. Pin parent interface colours, exceptional child interface colours, and representatives. Reject incompatible colours or a closed child colour that occurs anywhere else. Colours absent from the bag can occur only closed in a single child. For bag colours, fold default children labels into the predetermined vertices and subtract retained child contributions from the target, restoring the parent-interface vertex sums omitted in the output record. Solve connected ordered sums on the actual large bag with these colour pins and adjusted labels. Every open component of a retained child touches the newly connected bag part, as does every default child component. The reconstructed colouring satisfies every weaker required parent connection. Prior bag components thus need not have been connected. Conversely every reported record is witnessed by the construction. Exceptional-set and representative enumeration takes polynomial time for fixed parameters. Tables are computed once per node.

For the pinned version of highly-connected-cographic.md remove p fixed vertices before finding the packed bases. Treat each pin as an additional prescribed exception. This adds at most p template edges and branch vertices, so adding 13p to the sufficient connectivity threshold covers the prescribed roots. Initial unrestricted assignments respect all pins. A single bag colour only requires the correct total in the connected bag.

**Certified virtual clique edges.** The argument extends to high-connectivity torsos if each child whose fill is counted supplies a rooted clique minor on its adhesion. Extend its disjoint connected branch sets to cover its region, and in the default assignment give every branch its root's colour. This realizes every monochromatic clique edge by actual edges simultaneously for any colouring. Fold its labels into the roots. Before routing keep only actual bag edges and clique edges certified by default children. At most (F+1) binom(a,2) edges can be lost from the exceptional children and omitted parent fill; add this amount to the sufficient connectivity threshold. Losing one edge decreases vertex connectivity by at most one in this range. Expand defaults after routing; interiors of different child regions are disjoint. Uncertified children may be present if their fill edges are excluded from the initial connectivity bound. Tight two-cut regions supply a rooted edge minor; the three-leaf star with leaf roots has no rooted triangle. No claim for general torso-only high connectivity follows.

**Bounded overlap of actual connected children.** Suppose each torso vertex participates in at most D credited child adhesions of size at most a, whose regions connect all their roots. In each such region use multisource growth from the roots to construct a covering family of disjoint connected branch sets. Contracting these branch sets gives a connected graph on the roots, potentially much smaller than the clique. Every quotient edge is simultaneously realized by the default branch assignment. Let T consist of actual bag edges and credited clique fills (omit parent-only fill). Let J consist of actual edges and these smaller quotient graphs. Write C=binom(a,2).

If J minus z vertices Z is disconnected, then in T−Z every edge crossing between its components belongs to a fill whose adhesion meets Z. Indeed untouched adhesions retain connected surrogates. At most Dz adhesions meet Z, so deleting at most DzC edges from T−Z disconnects it. But its edge connectivity is at least its vertex connectivity and hence at least κ(T)−z whenever z<κ(T). It follows that κ(J)≥κ(T)/(1+DC); complete or trivial remainders satisfy the needed bound directly. Deleting the surrogates for up to F exceptional children costs at most FC edges, so a torso connectivity of (1+DC)(b+13P+25(b−1)r+2+FC) suffices. This handles four-port stars and other connected pieces lacking rooted clique minors, with arbitrary decomposition depth.
