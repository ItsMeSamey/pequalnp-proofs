# A round binary quotient and an unbounded 3-connected family

Let W be the low four binary coordinates, K its low three, u=1 and w=8. Read integers as bit vectors, so addition is XOR. The following ordinary columns give a NO instance:

    20 33 53 67 91 98 120 140 145 175 176 197

Replacing only 20 by 28 gives a YES instance. The finite certificate below enumerates the spans of all 4096 subsets for each input. The NO traces on W are all 67 subspaces except the five H satisfying u∈H⊆K. The YES traces are all 67 subspaces. Consequently the pair has the terminal-gadget indistinguishability proved in the companion rank-four interface argument.

Both lists project to exactly the columns 1 through 12 after deleting their low four bits. This is a simple rank-four restriction of binary projective geometry. It is round, meaning it cannot be covered by two proper flats: the union of any two distinct three-dimensional binary subspaces of a four-dimensional space has at most 7+7−3=11 nonzero vectors. Both lists have full ordinary rank eight. Appending the same two columns 215 and 226 to both lists retains the catalogs and gives all of PG(3,2) except the single point 15 as the common quotient. The checker also exhausts these two 14-column instances.

For arbitrarily large ranks, let U be the quotient line generated by 2 and 4. The lifts 33,67,98 of its three points satisfy 33 XOR 67 = 98. They span a two-dimensional subspace L disjoint from W. Introduce an arbitrary independent binary space P disjoint from the old ambient space and adjoin all nonzero vectors of L⊕P, keeping a single copy of each shared column. Apply the same operation to both instances.

Every old trace persists. For NO, let φ be a linear functional killing K and taking 1 on w. All eligible new columns, already including the eligible common columns, span exactly ker φ intersected with L⊕P. Their span meets the old ambient on L intersected with ker φ, spanned by eligible old common columns because L has all its nonzero vectors present. Thus adding the new eligible columns cannot enlarge the span within the old ambient of the complete set of eligible old columns. The old NO instance excludes u from that span for every φ. The extension is still NO and already realizes every permissible trace. The YES extension retains its full catalog.

The new quotient is the union of the old round rank-four configuration and the projective geometry on U⊕P, meeting along U. The quotient is simple. In any partition of its columns, at least one side spans the entire old piece, by roundness. The same holds for the new piece. If the same side spans both pieces, the rank connectivity value is the rank of the other side. Otherwise the spans of the two sides intersect in a space containing U, giving connectivity at least two. Every part with at least two columns has rank at least two by simplicity, and singletons have positive connectivity. Hence the quotient is 3-connected for every choice of P. Its rank is 4+dim P. For dim P≥1, separating the two pieces outside their common line gives connectivity exactly two when the two exclusive parts span their respective pieces.

A sparse variant uses 12+4d columns at quotient rank 4+d. For each of d independent private directions p, include the four vectors p+L and no mixed-private columns. The old common line and each four-point coset form a round Fano plane in the quotient. All eligible new columns lie in ker φ inside L⊕P, so the same span-intersection bound proves preservation of the catalogs. In a partition, each Fano plane and the old round restriction is spanned by one side. If some pieces are spanned by opposite sides their shared line gives connectivity at least two. Otherwise one side spans the entire configuration. This proves 3-connectivity for the sparse extension.
