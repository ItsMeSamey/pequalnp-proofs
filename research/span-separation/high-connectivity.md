# Dense binary vertex labels on a highly connected graph

Let each vertex of a simple graph G have label γ_v in a binary space W of dimension r; repeated values are allowed. Write γ(A) for the sum on A and fix s=h+1≥2 nonempty connected parts. Assume vertex connectivity at least h+2+21hr. Under this hypothesis the following criterion decides exactly which ordered part sums (b₀,…,b_h) can be attained.

Choose a subspace D of W minimizing

    t + h dim D,     t = |{v : γ_v is outside D}|.

Call the t outside vertices exceptional. The criterion requires that the b_i sum to γ(V), and that the exceptional vertices can be assigned to the s parts so that the exceptional sum in part i equals b_i modulo D. Necessity follows by restricting any partition to its exceptional vertices. All other vertices have labels in D.

**Packing the correction vertices.** Comparison to W gives t+h dim D≤hr. Comparison to any proper U within D shows that at least h(dim D−dim U) labels in D lie outside U, counted with multiplicity. The labels in D therefore span D, and the binary column matroid on those vertices satisfies the disjoint-bases condition for h bases. Choose disjoint vertex sets B₁,…,B_h, each a basis of D. Empty bases work when D=0. They can also be found by bounded enumeration after retaining at most h copies of each nonzero label, because independent sets use each label at most once. [Matroid base packing, Theorem 4](https://math.mit.edu/~goemans/18438F09/lec13.pdf)

**Connecting the assigned labels.** Choose fresh distinct roots r₀,…,r_h outside the exceptional vertices and the bases. For every exceptional vertex assigned to i, demand a path to r_i. For every vertex v in B_i, demand a path to r_i and another to r₀. The number of distinct endpoints is at most b=h+1+t+h dim D. There are l=t+2h dim D demands. They can be realized simultaneously as paths having pairwise disjoint interiors and containing no other endpoint internally.

Indeed the connectivity hypothesis implies κ(G)≥b+10l+1, since b≤h+1+hr and l≤2hr. For l>0, choose a distinct neighbor outside the endpoint set for each of the 2l endpoint incidences. Minimum degree bounds guarantee enough unused choices even at the last step. Delete the endpoint set. The remaining graph is at least 10l-connected and hence l-linked. Link the chosen pairs of neighbors and prepend or append their corresponding endpoint edges. When l=0 the singleton roots themselves suffice. [Thomas and Wollan, An Improved Linear Edge Bound for Graph Linkages](https://iris.uniroma1.it/handle/11573/443297)

Remove all basis vertices temporarily. For each demanded path from r_i to a basis vertex place the entire path except its last vertex in part i; apply the same rule to paths from r₀. Place each exception path including its endpoints in its assigned part. The resulting disjoint connected cores contain all exceptions and their roots. The graph after removal of the basis vertices is connected by the assumed connectivity. Grow the cores across edges until they partition this remaining graph. Each vertex of B_i has a neighbor in core i and in core zero. Start all these vertices in part zero, then transfer any subset of B_i to i. Both parts remain connected. The discrepancy in part i is in D by the congruence criterion, and its basis allows precisely the required correction. Adjusting all nonzero-indexed parts leaves the root part correct by the total sum. This proves sufficiency and gives a witness.

**Application to cographic traces.** In a supplied cographic quotient take γ_v to be the boundary of the W-valued edge labels, so γ(V)=0. A target trace H of dimension h is attainable if and only if it is realized by h+1 connected parts with sums spanning H, by the connected-partition characterization. Enumerate the prescribed vectors b_i in H and test the criterion above. The zero trace has the trivial one-part witness. Vertex connectivity at least 22(r+1)² suffices to compute the full catalog. Subspace enumeration, exception assignments and possible prescribed part sums take f(r) times polynomial input size. The linkage witness can be found with the fixed-parameter disjoint paths algorithm on the distinct neighbor pairs. [Robertson and Seymour, Graph Minors XIII](https://doi.org/10.1006/jctb.1995.1006)

This covers graphs with unbounded complement degree and unbounded genus, including sufficiently large complete bipartite graphs. The explicit connectivity threshold is sufficient. The tests below exercise the parity correction and criterion on small complete graphs with enough vertices for the roots and correction bases, where the required paths can be direct edges.
