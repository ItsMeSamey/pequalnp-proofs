# Fixed union labels differ from group labels

For the eight-element commutative monoid of subsets of {A,B,C} under union, deciding a connected partition with three prescribed ordered sums is NP-complete even on graphs of uniformly bounded degree and positive uniform vertex expansion. A partition is a polynomial certificate.

Reduce from 3SAT. Replace each occurrence of a variable by a distinct copy; add cyclic binary implications from each copy to its successor (omit trivial cycles of length one). This forces consistency, preserves satisfiability, and uses each copy in at most three clauses. Use the literal ladder from [van 't Hof, Theorem 3.1](https://etheses.durham.ac.uk/id/eprint/285/1/phd_thesis_pimvanthof.pdf). A two-vertex layer per variable consists of its positive and negative literal; join consecutive layers completely. Join endpoint f1 to the first layer and f2 to the last. Each clause vertex adjoins its literals. Require f1,f2 red, all clause vertices blue; there are at least two clauses (duplicate a clause before the occurrence splitting if needed). The original degrees are at most seven: each literal has at most four consecutive-layer neighbours and three clause occurrences, while endpoint and clause degrees are smaller. A satisfying assignment colours false literals red and true literals blue, making both parts connected. Conversely a red endpoint path uses no clause and must take at least one literal per layer; blue literals thus form a consistent partial truth assignment. Every blue clause requires some blue literal neighbour since there are multiple blue clauses and no clause-clause edges. Extend this assignment arbitrarily. Thus the pinned two-part problem is equivalent to satisfiability.

Pad the ladder's vertex count to a square n=m² by appending a path to f1 whose vertices are all red-pinned. Add an explicit constant-degree expander on a second layer of n vertices, and a perfect matching between layers. For example use the degree-eight Gabber–Galil graphs on square orders (merge parallel edges and omit loops for the simple host, preserving a positive constant cut expansion as degree is bounded). [Gabber and Galil](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(81)90040-4). The combined maximum degree is at most nine. Let η>0 be a uniform lower bound on hub-layer edge expansion. For any union shore of k≤n vertices, let A_0,B_0 denote its two index sets. If |A_0△B_0|≥k/4, matching edges give a cut of that size. Otherwise |B_0| and n−|B_0| are each at least 3k/8, so hub edges alone contribute ≥3ηk/8. Dividing the cut size by nine gives uniform vertex expansion.

Give neutral old vertices label {A}, red-pinned old vertices {A,B}, blue-pinned old vertices {A,C}, and every hub vertex {B,C}. Request unions {A,B}, {A,C}, {B,C} respectively. Every old vertex carries A and cannot join the third part. A hub vertex contains both B and C and cannot join either other part. The hub layer is connected. On the old layer the first two sums are exactly the required pin constraints; both types of prescribed pins occur. Matching edges cannot supply old-layer monochromatic paths. This proves the claimed hardness for the fixed monoid. It does not give hardness for groups or binary cographic traces.
