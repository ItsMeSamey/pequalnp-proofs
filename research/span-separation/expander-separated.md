# Ordered sums from dense locally tree-like labels in a vertex expander

Assume every set X of at most n/2 vertices has at least α|X| external neighbours, 0<α≤1, and minimum degree is at least 3. For a prescribed maximum-degree-three template with m edges and no isolates put L=ceil(12(1+1/α)log₂(2n)), R=ceil(log₂(4mL)), D=R+ceil(log₂(32m/α)). Suppose its prescribed roots have tree balls of radius D+2 and pairwise distances greater than 2D+4. Then there exists a deterministically constructible subdivision with each demand path of length ≤L.

At each root select one distinct neighbour per incident demand. From each selected neighbour extend a perfect binary tree of depth D away from its root. These cones, including those of distinct arms of one root, are disjoint. Protect the first R levels of every cone, including the neighbour. For each successive demand temporarily forbid all branch vertices, earlier path interiors and all other arms' protected cores. The forbidden cardinality is at most 2m+mL+2m·2^(R+1)≤6m·2^R. Each old path avoided the current protected core. An old interior vertex spoils at most 2^(D−R) paths to leaves in the extended cone, so at least 2^(D−1) leaves remain accessible from its initial neighbour within D steps. Other protected cores are disjoint from the whole cone. This seed has size greater than twice the forbidden cardinality divided by α. Hence ordinary breadth-first balls in the surviving graph grow by a factor ≥1+α/2 until exceeding n/2. The two searches meet, giving a path from one selected neighbour to the other. Reattach their branch vertices. The length is at most 2D+2ceil(log_{1+α/2}n)+3≤L since D≤log₂n and ln(1+α/2)≥α/(2+α). The argument applies successively.

For b≥2 parts and full label span of dimension d>0, put t=(b−1)d. Select t movable vertices: for every part except the last reserve one vertex of each label from a fixed basis. For each movable vertex reserve two further roots, one for its owner and one for the last part. Connect each movable vertex to its two roots; join all roots of each part along a path. This template has 3t branch vertices and m=4t−b edges of maximum degree three. Apply the previous lemma. Assume also that deleting all movable vertices leaves a connected graph. This can be tested directly. Alternatively suppose each movable vertex has a tree ball of radius greater than t/α+1. If their deletion disconnected the graph, a component of size ≤n/2 has size ≤t/α by expansion. Each vertex has lost at most one neighbour, so this component contains a cycle. It adjoins some deleted vertex, placing the cycle within its tree ball, a contradiction.

The paths assigned to a part, excluding the movable vertices themselves, constitute its connected core; each movable vertex neighbours both eligible cores. Grow these cores to exhaust the connected residual graph. Place all movable vertices in the last part, then transfer to each other part the subset of its private basis that corrects its deficit. Every prescribed ordered tuple in the label span with the right total is attained.

For explicit root selection, increase D to at least ceil(t/α)+2. Let M be the maximum size of a radius-(2D+4) ball. If each member of a label basis appears on more than 3tM vertices with tree balls of radius D+2, pick all 3t roots greedily. Each previous choice excludes at most M candidates. In bounded maximum degree, fixed α,d,b give M polylogarithmic in n. This allows low girth elsewhere and arbitrary labels outside the selected basis. When d=0 use a spanning tree to make any b≤n connected parts. A promised expansion lower bound suffices; the actual output partition is a positive certificate.
