# Routing labelled parts through a clique minor

Use the conventions and algebraic criterion of high-connectivity.md and unbreakable-clique.md. Put B₀=h+1+hr, L₀=2hr and require c≥B₀+2L₀. Suppose every nonzero-labelled vertex has more than q+c distinct neighbors and at least B₀ vertices in total have this degree. A supplied clique model of order B₀+max(3L₀+1,q+2L₀+1) suffices for the algebraic criterion to be exact.

Choose the correction bases and exceptions as before, and choose h+1 further high-degree roots. Delete all B≤B₀ branch vertices and discard the at most B model bags they hit. For each endpoint incidence of the L≤L₀ demanded paths, add a distinct terminal clone adjacent to the surviving ordinary neighbors of its original endpoint. For L>0 invoke Robertson–Seymour, Graph Minors XIII, Theorem 5.4 (as stated in Theorem 4.1 of https://research.nii.ac.jp/~k_keniti/quaddp1.pdf). The model has order ≥3L+1. We must exclude a separation of order <2L with all terminals on the first side and a whole bag exclusively on the second.

Delete the proposed separator. All intact remaining bags are pairwise adjacent and jointly contain more than q vertices. At least one clone survives. In the original graph delete the original branch vertices other than its owner v, plus the ordinary separator vertices. At most c vertices were deleted. The component of v has more than q vertices by degree, and by unbreakability must contain the intact bags. A simple path from v to such a bag translates to a path from the clone avoiding the separator. This contradicts the proposed separation. Thus there is an undirected linkage of all pairs, in which no terminal occurs internally since every terminal is already an endpoint. Identifying clones again gives internally disjoint paths with the prescribed endpoints. If L=0 no linkage is needed.

After deleting correction vertices, each root's component has more than q vertices by degree, so all roots lie in one component. Grow their connected cores there as before. Every other component has at most q vertices, hence only zero labels by the degree bound. Attach each component to an adjacent correction vertex after the corrections.

The result also applies when the graph is partitioned into connected bags each containing at most one nonzero label and the simple quotient with summed labels satisfies these hypotheses. Such contraction preserves the unrestricted possible labelled sums. Lifting each part by the union of its bags preserves connectivity and sums.

## Arbitrary bounded-degree expanders

In any connected simple graph with a vertex v of degree d and a nonneighbor w, give v,w and the d neighbors of v distinct unit-vector labels in dimension d+2. Give other vertices zero. Request two parts, the first with exactly the two units at v,w. The unrestricted sum criterion passes, but this part would exclude every neighbor of v while containing v and w. This works on arbitrary large bounded-degree expanders at fixed label dimension. Expansion by itself therefore cannot justify the unrestricted algebraic criterion. This observation leaves the computational complexity open.

## Bounded-degree access via neutral connected bags

A local certificate replaces the degree assumptions. Supply candidate root or support bags of at most m vertices, connected, pairwise disjoint, mutually nonadjacent if desired. Each contains at most one nonzero label, all support is covered, and at least B₀ bags are available. For each bag prescribe max(1,2L₀) pairwise disjoint connected sets (arms), each of size >q, each adjacent to that bag, outside every candidate bag. Arms belonging to different bags may overlap. Require (q,mB₀+2L₀)-unbreakability and a model of order mB₀+max(3L₀+1,q+2L₀+1).

Select candidate bags just as individual branches, with distinct additional roots. Delete the B selected bags, discard all model bags hit, and give each clone all outside neighbors of its owner bag. After deleting <2L separator vertices at least one clone survives, and at least one arm of its owner is untouched. That arm has >q vertices and belongs to the same ordinary component as all intact model bags, by unbreakability after deleting the selected bag vertices and ordinary separator vertices. Thus the separation is impossible. Unselected candidate bags remain ordinary vertices in the routing graph; only selected bags need be monochromatic prior to the growth step. Contract the selected bags for the growth and correction procedure. After deleting correction bags, every surviving candidate bag lies in the unique giant via its untouched arm. Thus smaller components have only zero labels. This proves the criterion in bounded-degree graphs satisfying the certificate.

For instance in cubic graphs with tree balls, a connected radius-a ball has 3·2^a outgoing edges into distinct branches of a larger tree ball. Choose a so this is at least max(1,2L₀); the bag size is m=3·2^a−2. Extend the branches to depth d until each has >q vertices. Pairwise disjoint radius-(a+d) tree balls about all support and enough additional roots supply the arms. For cubic graphs of edge expansion ε, one may take q=ceil(3(mB₀+2L₀)/ε): deleting ≤mB₀+2L₀ vertices exposes at most 3(mB₀+2L₀) edges from the smaller exclusive side, and expansion bounds its size by q. Thus sparse separated support on a large-girth cubic expander with a supplied clique model satisfies the criterion, including examples whose maximum degree is three. The model order and expansion parameters are explicit assumptions.

For fixed required model order M, sufficiently large cubic ε-edge-expanders contain it automatically. Alon–Seymour–Thomas's excluded-minor separator theorem gives, if K_M is absent, a separator of size k≤M^(3/2)√n, both exclusive sides at most 2n/3. If k<n/6 the smaller exclusive side has size ≥n/3−k>n/6, so εn/6<3k. Thus n>max(36,324/ε²)M³ precludes exclusion. For a decision algorithm the model need not be constructed once existence follows. [Primary separator theorem](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nalon/PDFS/spr.pdf)

Tree balls are unnecessary in an arbitrary bounded-degree expander when the support is sufficiently separated. Fix maximum degree Δ≥2 and edge expansion ε>0. Put k=max(1,2L₀), take a>k+Δk/ε, let m bound the size of any radius-a ball (e.g. (Δ+1)^(a+1)), take q=ceil(Δ(mB₀+2L₀)/ε), and R=a+q+2. Require pairwise disjoint radius-R balls about candidate centers, covering all nonzero labels as centers and providing at least B₀ centers. Suppose n is large enough that every radius-R ball has fewer than n/2−k vertices. The inner radius-a ball U has at least a+1 vertices, by extending a path to distance R. There are k disjoint paths from U to vertices outside the radius-R ball. Indeed if a set S of <k vertices blocked them, the union A of components of G−S meeting U−S would be contained in the radius-R ball, with |A|≥|U|−k>Δk/ε, |A|<n/2, and all its boundary edges ending in S. Expansion contradicts ε|A|≤Δk. Truncate each path at its first exit from the radius-R ball. In that prefix, take the vertices strictly after its last visit to U and strictly before its exit. These form disjoint arms of size ≥R−a>q adjacent to U. Disjoint outer balls avoid all other candidate bags. This verifies the bag certificate without any girth condition. For general Δ, the same balanced separator argument guarantees K_M once n>max(36,36Δ²/ε²)M³.
