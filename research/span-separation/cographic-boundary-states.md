# Boundary states for ordered connected partitions

Consider binary vertex labels in a space of dimension $r$ and an ordered palette of $b$ colors. A partial graph has boundary $A$ and may meet the exterior only through $A$. A state records the colors on $A$, a partition of $A$ into the intersections with monochromatic connected components, a mask of closed colors, and the XOR of the labels on vertices outside $A$ for each color. A closed color has exactly one component in the partial graph and that component misses $A$. Any color with a component missing $A$ and a second component is rejected, since the first component can never be joined to the second through the exterior. A color absent from the boundary and from the closed mask has no vertices in the partial graph.

For $|A|\le a$, there are at most $b^a \operatorname{Bell}(a)2^b2^{rb}$ states. The empty graph has the empty coloring and partition, no closed colors and zero part sums. The following updates on a nice tree decomposition give exactly the realizable states.

- Introducing an isolated boundary vertex adds a singleton block with any color outside the closed mask. Its label is still excluded from the sums.
- Introducing an actual edge between boundary vertices merges their blocks precisely when their colors agree.
- Forgetting a boundary vertex adds its label to its color's sum and removes it from its block. If it was the last member of the block, reject if another block has that color. Otherwise close the color.
- Joining graphs with equal boundaries requires equal boundary colorings. Reject if a closed color in one graph occurs in the other graph. Take the union of the masks, the componentwise XOR of the sums, and the transitive closure of the two partitions on the common boundary.

The intersection condition ensures that monochromatic paths crossing the two sides pass through common boundary vertices. Thus the join closure is exact, and the two sets of counted vertices are disjoint. Conversely, restricting an extendible coloring to either side gives admissible states. With empty final boundary a full connected partition requires all $b$ colors closed. Decisions can retain one witness per state.

On variable adhesions, temporarily retain the union of the interface vertices needed by the current join. Add any missing bag vertices as isolated boundary vertices, and forget vertices with no future incidence only after all their incident edges are processed. Equivalently, convert the supplied decomposition to a nice one. A completed edge set on an adhesion may be used only when these edges are present in the original graph, or its actual connectivity is supplied by a child state.

## A high-connectivity core and clique attachments

Suppose each fringe meets its core in a nonempty clique of actual edges, and different fringes have disjoint interiors with no edges between them. Each interior component must touch its adhesion. Fix a neighboring adhesion vertex for each such component. The default puts that whole component in the neighboring vertex's color, which connects it to the core. Allow a parent adhesion in the core that is also a clique of actual edges.

For any admissible coloring of the whole region, reserve every fringe that has a closed color. Such a color appears in no other fringe or in the core, so there are at most $b$ reserved fringes. Compare each other fringe's ordered interior label sums with its defaults for the current core coloring. Their difference belongs to the space of $b$-tuples with total XOR zero, of dimension $r(b-1)$. Choose a basis from these differences and keep just the original fringes representing their total difference in that basis. Default every remaining fringe. The ordered sums are unchanged. A nonclosed color present in a modified fringe also occurs in the adhesion. The actual clique keeps all its boundary vertices of that color connected, and each new monochromatic component attaches to its preselected neighbor. No color disappears and no monochromatic core connection is lost.

One may enumerate the at most $b+r(b-1)$ exceptional fringes and their table states. Pin their boundary vertex colors and the parent boundary coloring. Each default fringe contributes fixed label adjustments to its preselected core vertices. The pin-aware core theorem then applies to the adjusted labels. The parent adhesion is a real clique, so each boundary color has a single boundary block; after repair the parent recovers the same exact signature, omitting its own boundary labels from the sums. If the parent boundary is not a clique, a repair that connects all occurrences of a color need not preserve the original boundary partition. For fixed parameters and polynomial-size fringe state tables, the enumeration is polynomial; its exponent can depend on the parameters.

The checkers cographic_boundary_check.py and clique_fringe_check.py compare the transitions and the restricted fringe repairs against exhaustive colorings on small graphs. The former performs 638 comparisons of complete state tables. The latter checks 6,594 admissible colorings on 48 graphs, including 520 repairs that change the coloring and preserve the parent signature.
