# Connected part sums after adding a bounded number of vertices

Suppose r-bit vertex labels, s ordered target sums and p prescribed vertex-to-part assignments are given on a connected graph G. Let Z be a supplied set of z vertices such that each component of G−Z belongs to a class admitting an algorithm for any fixed number of connected parts, prescribed exact binary sums and a fixed number of pins. For fixed r,s,p,z there is a polynomial-time algorithm with witness on G. In particular the result covers adding boundedly many vertices of unrestricted degrees to bounded-genus graphs.

Guess the colors of the apices Z; “color” denotes the part index. First consider connected B=G−Z. Among feasible partitions minimize the number of components of the monochromatic induced subgraphs of B, called islands here. For a color having no apex its only island is kept. For a color i with z_i positive apices, the connected incidence graph of its apices and its islands has edges for apex adjacency and apex-island adjacency. Preserve a tree connecting the apices, omitting all non-apex leaves. No two island nodes in this tree are adjacent and every retained island has degree at least two, so at most z_i−1 islands are retained. Every other island of this color touches an apex of the color. Also protect the islands containing pinned vertices. At most z+s+p islands are protected in total, and simultaneously removing any collection of unprotected islands preserves a nonempty connected remainder of each color. If B itself consists of just one island the following bound holds directly.

Every island has a neighbor of a different color in B since B is connected and has more than one island. Independently mark each island with probability one half. Each unmarked, unprotected island with a marked neighbor is a possible donor. The expected number of donors is at least one fourth of the unprotected islands, by fixing one neighbor of each in advance. For each donor fix a marked neighboring recipient and record in W^s the change in the vector of part sums on transferring the whole donor to the recipient's color. If there are more than rs donors, some nonempty subset of their transfer vectors sums to zero. Perform those transfers simultaneously. The protected interiors still connect all apices of their colors. All unmoved unprotected islands still attach to an apex of their color; donors connect to recipients that stayed put. Pins and sums are preserved. In B each transferred island merges with its recipient, creating no new components of its old color because it was an entire component. This contradicts minimality. Thus there are at most z+s+p+4rs islands.

Enumerate that bounded number of connected base parts, their sums, their colors, their existing pins, and at most z witnesses of necessary apex-island adjacencies per island. For each adjacency guess an actual base vertex joined to that apex and pin it to the relevant island. Require the abstract graph of islands and apices within each demanded original color to be connected using the guessed adjacencies and actual apex-apex edges. Each color without an apex needs only one island. No prohibition of extra adjacency is required. Solve the resulting connected labelled-partition problem on B. An actual witness yields a successful guess by the preceding bound, and every successful guess gives a connected partition of G with the prescribed sums.

If G−Z has many components and Z is nonempty, choose for each component a baseline assigning its entirety to the color of one fixed adjacent apex. Preserve the components that supply islands needed to connect same-color apices, all components containing an apex-free color, and all pinned components, using at most z+s+p. For each remaining component the difference of its actual and baseline contributions is a vector in W^s. Preserve at most rs additional components generating the sum of these differences, resetting the others to baseline. The new partition still connects every color: all apex connections were preserved, all surviving islands of apex-containing colors touch one of those apices, and the apex-free colors stayed within their preserved components. In each exceptional component the island reduction now applies with the same global protected islands, restricted to that connected component. Transfers preserving the total vector leave all other components untouched. Enumerate the at most z+s+p+rs exceptional components and solve their bounded island problems with guessed apex contacts and aggregate sums. If B is empty or Z is empty, direct checking or the base algorithm suffices.

## Exact pinned sums from bounded-genus trace catalogs

For a connected bounded-genus base with total label γ(V), guess a distinct root r_i in each of its s parts. Adjust the label at r₀ and its required part sum by γ(V) to make the total zero. Any such vertex labeling is the boundary of some edge labeling, obtained by eliminating leaves of a spanning tree. For h=s−1≥1, add h binary coordinates: place e_i on r_i for i≥1 and their sum on r₀. For every pinned vertex v add one more coordinate supported on v and its prescribed root; if they coincide this coordinate is zero. The target trace is the h-dimensional subspace spanned by the triples (e_i,b_i,0), i≥1, with b_i the adjusted required sums. Tuple feasibility implies existence of this trace. Conversely an attainable trace has a realizing connected partition into exactly h+1 parts. The marker projection has rank h. The s tagged root vectors have their total sum as their only dependence, so this rank forces every part to contain exactly one root: if fewer than s parts contained roots then their part tags, summing to zero, could span at most h−1 dimensions. Now the prescribed graph of the linear map e_i↦b_i forces all part sums, and the zero pin coordinates put each pin with its root. Invoke the bounded-genus auxiliary graphic algorithm. The s=1 case is direct.

For example, if the base is planar and Z is a single universal apex then the construction permits the base labels to be arbitrary and places no bound on the genus or maximum degree of the entire graph. The general closure takes n^{f(r,s,p,z)} time on a base class with a polynomial algorithm for each fixed parameter tuple.
