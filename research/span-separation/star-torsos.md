# Three-port attachments and repeated decomposition

Suppose a bag of central vertices has physical edges, plus pieces with pairwise disjoint connected interiors. Each interior has one to three neighboring ports in the bag and touches each of its ports. Use a star torso consisting of the bag, its physical edges, and one new center for each interior, adjacent to all its ports. Direct port-to-port edges of a piece are kept as physical edges when present. If fixed-parameter-tuple connected partition sums with pins can be solved on the star torso and its minors, the same type of problem can be solved on the original graph in polynomial time for fixed interface dimension r, part count s and input pin count, provided the pieces have accessible local signatures. The core solver need only handle the minors specified below, with new vertex weights and bounded pins.

In a given partition, at most s interiors contain a color absent from the bag. Mark these pieces. Also mark pieces containing input pins not in the bag. Every monochromatic component in any other interior reaches a same-color port. Assigning the whole interior the color of any one port keeps it connected. If some color appears at two or more ports, choose that color. There is at most one such color, since there are at most three ports. This normalized assignment preserves every connection the piece previously supplied between same-color ports: such a connection requires two ports of that color. If all ports have distinct colors there was no such pair to connect. A difference in sums is a vector in W^s, so it suffices to preserve at most rs of the unmarked pieces as parity exceptions, by taking a basis representation of the aggregate difference.

Enumerate the bounded set of exceptional pieces, their port colors, connected-component signatures and interior contributions. Require all whole peripheral parts to be connected within their own exception. For each remaining piece put its full interior label sum on its star center, whose color is a variable. In an exception delete its center if there is no same-color port pair connected by the signature. If there is a connected pair, retain the star restricted to the ports in that component (two or three), give its center weight zero, and pin the center and these ports to that component's color. All other ports of an exception are also pinned to their prescribed colors. This uses at most one center in each exception and produces a subgraph of the star torso. Subtract all fixed exceptional interior contributions from the requested sums. If the modified torso disconnects, allocate disjoint nonempty sets of colors to its connected components and distribute the corresponding sums and pins. At most s components are possible in a feasible case after removing any closed peripheral parts.

A normalized witness supplies a torso partition. Conversely, a star center of an ordinary piece can be expanded to the entire connected interior, preserving its sums and all monochromatic connections. A center alone can represent a whole part. Expand the exceptions according to their signatures. Local components of central colors touch their assigned ports; the retained pinned centers represent the genuine port connections in these pieces. The full parts are connected. The construction depends on the star torso, which may have bounded genus even when the input contains arbitrarily many nonplanar interiors. Four ports can connect two different color pairs simultaneously, which the single-center normalization does not preserve.

## Arbitrarily many levels

For a rooted tree decomposition with adhesion at most a=3 and the required star-torso property at each bag, keep at each subtree a table of partitions into at most s+a−1 abstract connected parts. Record their interior label sums and which of the at most a parent ports they contain. The labels of parent ports are excluded from the subtree sums to avoid duplicate counting. Apply each piece's union-of-colors choice by mapping its connected-part entries to parent colors. Parts without ports can occur only as whole peripheral colors. Each retained port component gives a local connection between all its ports; with ≤3 ports at most one component uses two or more. Internal child pins can be tracked in table entries or by treating their locations as named marks. Table rows can also represent finer connectivity than the final coloring when neighboring child components are eventually assigned the same parent color: extra adjacencies only help a claimed connection.

At a bag, test each candidate row by considering its abstract parts as distinct colors, pinning its parent ports, and using the child tables as signatures for the bounded exceptions in the star-torso algorithm. Nonexceptional interior connectivity and total weight can be determined directly for normalization. Store reconstructed witnesses for successful rows. Only physical edges and child pieces are used at the current bag; any artificial connections to the parent must be excluded when forming its testing torso. Assume the interior of each child piece is connected and touches each of its ports, or use a decomposition that supplies such pieces.

Soundness follows by expansion of stored actual partitions. To see sufficiency, fix a globally feasible connected s-part partition. On a subtree, a global color that also occurs outside has each of its local components touching a parent port. If k distinct colors meet the boundary, their total number of local components is at most a and contributes at most a−k excess over one per color. Colors invisible outside the subtree each contribute just one component. Thus at most s+a−1 components occur on the subtree whenever some part crosses its boundary, and at most s otherwise. Inductively the needed signatures of the original partition on the exceptional children are available, and normalization supplies the others. This completeness argument is relative to globally feasible partitions; it does not require a new larger table bound at each depth. The number of rows depends only on r,s,a and the number of pins. Each bag performs polynomially many choices of exceptions at fixed parameters, so the total exponent is independent of tree depth.

The two-port case can instead use an ordinary virtual edge in the torso and a fixed two-region baseline to absorb contributions into its endpoint weights. The three-port argument requires solving the star graph with potentially nonzero labels on its center vertices.
