# Fixed-dimensional binary lifts of graphic columns

Let each edge of a supplied undirected multigraph have column (∂e,σ_e), with σ_e in F₂^d. Let the supplied k-dimensional terminal space K contain the entire lift space 0×F₂^d, and let u=(0,b) with b nonzero. For arbitrary w, deciding whether u is in span X and w is outside K+span X, and returning X, takes deterministic time f(k,d)·N^{O(1+k+d)}, where N is the explicit input size. The exponent here depends on the parameters. In particular this extends the polynomial-time result for fixed parameters to higher-dimensional labels and pure targets that can require several cycles.

A separating functional annihilates the entire lift. Write its remaining vertex coordinates as a coloring. Allowed edges join equal colors. The k−d remaining equations on K and the equation taking value one on w give h=k−d+1 binary parity equations on the colors. Denote their vertex columns by γ_v and the right-hand side by s. Subdivide each original edge into a path of length three, using independent auxiliary vertices, label one segment by σ_e and the other segments by zero, and give the auxiliary vertices zero parity labels. Even edge sets use an entire subdivided path or none. Thus the transformed graph is simple and the decision is unchanged, also for loops and parallel edges.

In a yes instance choose an inclusion-minimal representation of u as a sum of allowed columns. Its columns are independent, since a nonempty zero-sum subfamily could be removed. Its selected graph is Eulerian with total label b. The map from its binary cycle space to the lift space is injective by independence. Its cycle rank β is therefore at most d. In the converse direction any Eulerian selected graph with total label b and components monochromatic under a feasible coloring is a witness.

Let c be its number of nontrivial components. The sum of deg(v)−2 over used vertices is 2(β−c), so at most β−c vertices have degree at least four and their degree sum is at most 4(β−c). Mark these as anchors, together with one vertex from each component consisting of a simple cycle. The graph is a union of O(d) segments from anchor to anchor whose interiors are vertex-disjoint, allowing a segment to return to its starting anchor. After fixing the component colors, start with color zero off this graph. The remaining syndrome is a sum of some γ_v outside the graph and can be realized by at most h of them, by choosing a basis.

Enumerate the O(d)-size abstract pattern, its anchors, their images, component colors, and the at most h exceptional exterior vertices. Delete the exceptions. For each segment guess its first and last edge. Establish segments of length one or two directly. For a longer segment these guesses specify two distinct interior ports. Reject collisions incompatible with the pattern, and remove anchors and all used internal vertices of the short segments. The remaining long segments require vertex-disjoint paths between distinct pairs of ports. Component colors, the exceptions, and all guessed anchor and port contributions are now fixed.

Give each edge ij its original lift label in F₂^d and, for each parity bit t, the integer γ_i[t]+γ_j[t] modulo four in an additional coordinate. Along a port-to-port path, this last sum modulo four equals the two port weights plus twice the sum of the internal weights. Thus the path lift and all parities of its internal vertices can be prescribed by an exact label in F₂^d×(Z/4)^h. Enumerate the labels for the O(d) paths; keep only those producing aggregate lift b and total coloring syndrome s, counting each chosen vertex once. Importantly, only paths in color-one components contribute internal vertex weights to that syndrome. Find the paths in each remaining case using Liu and Yoo's deterministic disjoint-paths theorem, forbidding the complement of the required value in each coordinate. There are at most three forbidden elements per coordinate and all parameters of that theorem are fixed when k,d are fixed. A returned collection gives an actual edge witness. All enumerations of vertices and edges have exponent O(k+d). [Liu and Yoo, Theorem 1.3](https://people.tamu.edu/~yyoo/stoc25.pdf#page=3)

The finite checker compares direct color enumeration and linear span with the bounded-cycle-rank characterization and the exterior exception bound on 500 multigraphs, and checks the modulo-four path identity. An additional two-loop example needs both cycles in different colors to realize its target. The checker does not implement the general disjoint-paths theorem.
