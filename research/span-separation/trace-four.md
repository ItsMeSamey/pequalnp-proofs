# A four-dimensional interface invisible to triple-circuit queries

Work over the binary field. Let W=K+⟨w⟩ have dimension r≥4, with K of dimension r−1 and u nonzero in K. The trace of ordinary columns X is span(X)∩W. We construct a NO instance realizing every subspace of W except those H with u∈H⊆K, and a YES instance realizing every subspace. This gives an obstruction to adaptive terminal-only oracle queries on the unchanged ordinary instance.

Write coordinates in K as (a,z), where u=(1,0) and z has r−2 coordinates. Introduce a disjoint exterior copy of the z-space. Include every column in W outside K. For every z≠0 and bit a include two further columns. Their common exterior component is z. Their W components are aw and aw+(a,z), respectively. These are the NO columns. Adjoin u for YES.

Every subspace H crossing K has a basis entirely outside K, so occurs as a trace. For H⊆K avoiding u, the projection (a,z)↦z is injective on H. Select the two columns for each basis vector (a,z) of H. In a sum landing in W, independence of their exterior z coordinates forces both columns or neither for each basis vector. The trace is exactly H.

Conversely, if a trace lies in K then there is a functional vanishing on the chosen columns and K and taking value one on w. Write x for its exterior coordinates. None of the ordinary W columns can be chosen. Each eligible paired column has a=x·z. A combination landing in W has total exterior z zero, so its aw terms sum to zero. The remaining contributions (a,z) all lie in the kernel of (a,z)↦a+x·z, which excludes u. This proves the NO catalog. In YES, realize each missing H by selecting u along with columns realizing any complement to u in H. Both instances can have equal numbers of simple ordinary columns, all outside W. Add a duplicate w to NO instead of u, then replace each ordinary column e on both sides by p_e and p_e+e with its own new exterior direction. These replacements preserve all W traces.

Every rank-(r−1) linear quotient of W shows every possible trace in both instances. Given the target trace, its inverse image P is available unless u∈P⊆K. In the latter case take a complement in P to the quotient kernel ⟨q⟩ that avoids u. Such a complement is the kernel of a functional on P taking one on both q and u. Every restriction to a hyperplane T also shows every trace. For each missing D⊆T, extend D by one vector outside both T and K. The new subspace meets T in exactly D and occurs in NO.

Even arbitrary appended terminal-side vector gadgets cannot separate the instances by probing circuits through at least one and at most r−1 designated gadget elements. The gadget may have private exterior dimensions, but its span meets the ordinary side only through W. Consider a witnessing YES circuit. If it lies wholly in the gadget, it persists. Otherwise let G be its gadget part, which is independent, let v be its sum, and let P=span(G)∩W. The vector v is nonzero and in W. Whenever the NO side realizes H with H∩P=⟨v⟩, choose a minimal ordinary representation of v from the columns realizing H. Its columns joined to G form a circuit, since a proper mixed dependence would represent v on a proper subset of an independent representation. If v≠u take H=⟨v⟩. If v=u and P is proper, extend ⟨u⟩ by a vector outside both P and K.

In the remaining case v=u and P=W. On W consider the coordinates of its vectors in the independent G basis at the designated roots. There are fewer than r such coordinates, so their common kernel contains a nonzero z. Since u has coefficient one at every designated root, u+z is nonzero and distinct from u. The subfamily of G representing u+z contains all designated roots. The NO side realizes its exact line, giving the required circuit as before. Adjoining an ordinary column preserves existing circuits, giving the converse. More generally that converse only needs trace catalog inclusion: the same gadget half can be completed using any ordinary set with the same trace as the original circuit's ordinary half.

The dual statement follows from the boundary calculation below. This proves indistinguishability for any sequence of these Boolean queries, including an oracle asking for any three out of a larger designated set. The argument assumes gadget-side roots and a fixed ordinary side. Algorithms may instead inspect or modify the ordinary columns, mark ordinary roots, or use gates other than these predicates.

## The dual interface

Let B denote one side, with coordinates indexed by its ground set I, and let V_B be the span of its columns. Glue two sides by identifying their shared space J and keeping remaining directions independent. The ordinary V_B contains W. If the gadget meets W in a proper J, both ordinary instances have every trace on J by the hyperplane-section result. For J=W the full catalogs above apply.

For each side define its dual piece in the ambient quotient

    D_B = F₂^I / { Bᵀf : f∈V_B*, f|J=0 }.

The columns are the coordinate units modulo this subspace. Embed J* into D_B by extending λ∈J* to f∈V_B* and taking the class of Bᵀf. The class is independent of the extension and determines λ since the columns span V_B. Glue the dual pieces along J*. A relation between their coordinate columns is precisely a pair (Bᵀf,Cᵀg) with f and g agreeing on J. Such pairs are exactly evaluations of a single functional on the primal glued space. Thus the relation space of the glued representation is the primal row space, as required for binary matroid duality.

For a subset S of one side the dual trace is the annihilator in J* of the primal trace on its complement. Indeed λ belongs to that dual trace if and only if it extends to f whose evaluations Bᵀf vanish outside S. Existence of this extension is equivalent to λ vanishing on J∩span(B restricted to I minus S). Thus the full-interface NO dual catalog omits precisely the interval between the line annihilating K and the hyperplane annihilating u. The YES dual catalog is full. The argument for rooted circuits uses only these catalogs and applies in the dual. For a proper J both dual catalogs are full.

For r=4 the adjacent checker enumerates 62 of 67 subspaces in NO and all 67 in YES, verifies identical catalogs after every line quotient and hyperplane section, and checks the primal and dual any-three answers for all 840 unordered interface bases.
