# Shared computation on disjoint input blocks

Work over fan-in-two AND/OR and complemented wires. For every sufficiently large m there is a Boolean function f on m inputs with f(0)=0 and binary gate complexity C>10m. There are at most 2^{O(m log m)} distinct circuits of at most 10m gates, versus 2^{2^m-1} tables. Choose the lexicographically first table absent from the circuit list. One can construct it by sorting the generated tables and finding the first gap, in time 2^{O(m log m)}, including 2^m evaluations per circuit. This avoids a double-exponential search through all tables.

Define a partial function on d blocks of m bits under the promise that at most one block is nonzero. Its value is the OR of f evaluated on all blocks. Each single-block restriction is a full copy of f, yet coordinatewise OR followed by a single optimal circuit gives total size at most (d−1)m+C, strictly below dC when d>1. After restriction the same final C gates can supply every copy. Even a uniform version can allow m to increase: choose the largest m≥1 with m^m≤d, for d≥2, and expose the block parameter d in a length-charged input header. The table construction then costs d^{O(1)}. Table evaluation and pooling are polynomial in the full input length dm plus its header. Polynomial uniformity of the truth table algorithm makes no claim of efficient synthesis of the size-optimal circuit. The same example works with fixed m, hence also without length-parameter ambiguities. This falsifies a direct-sum deduction from single-block restrictions alone, and has no implication for products on independent unrestricted blocks.

On valid complementary rails, simulating an arbitrary AND/OR/complemented-wire DAG by computing the pair of rails per binary gate costs at most twice its size and preserves all fanouts. A monotone circuit of size M on valid rails converts back with M binary gates and at most m input inverters for m raw inputs. For unrestricted two-input truth-table primitives the forward factor six follows from simultaneous positive and negative minterm DNFs (handle constants and literals separately). Thus a monotone lower bound *on the valid-rail promise* translates directly. A bound for a specified total extension need not hold under the promise. On independent product copies a fixed k-ary composition of depth t has k^t input blocks and a circuit of at most k times the preceding size plus the constant outer-gate cost at each level. Any lower recurrence only multiplying by a fixed factor yields at most a fixed polynomial in the expanded address length when k≥2.

If uniform synthesis of the optimal base circuit is desired, further slow m to at most half log₂ log₂ d for sufficiently large d. DNF gives size O(m2^m), and enumeration with truth-table checks through this size takes 2^{O(m²2^m)}≤d^{O(1)}. Both the exact-size pooled circuit construction and evaluation now run in polynomial time.

For a different uniform promise family, alternate independent read-once XOR₂ and MAJ₃ levels on valid rails. Two levels multiply input count by six and use only constant outer cost above six shareable child circuits. Each leaf is essential by setting majority siblings to opposite bits and XOR siblings to zero. A connected binary-gate output cone involving N distinct input groups needs at least N−1 gates by edge counting. Thus this example has Θ(N) gate complexity including all shared intermediates.
