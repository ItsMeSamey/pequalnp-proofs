# Routing supported labels through a clique in an unbreakable graph

Use the convention that a graph is (q,c)-unbreakable if a vertex separation of order at most c cannot have more than q vertices on each exclusive side. Suppose G is connected and contains a supplied clique Q of at least q+c+2 vertices. Suppose every vertex with a nonzero binary label has degree greater than q+c. For s=h+1 connected parts, h≥1, and r-bit labels, assume c≥h+1+5hr. Under these assumptions the algebraic part-sum criterion in the high-connectivity note is exact. Actual vertex connectivity may be low.

Choose the exceptional vertices and the h disjoint correction bases as in that note. They are all nonzero-labelled (a linear basis never uses the zero vector). Select s additional distinct roots in Q away from those vertices. The branch vertices consist of the roots, exceptions, and correction vertices. Their number B is at most h+1+hr. There are L≤2hr required connections: one from each exception to its assigned root, and two from each correction vertex in the basis for i, one to root i and one to root zero. Thus B+2L≤c. If L=0 there are no paths to construct.

Delete the branch vertices from a routing copy of G, and introduce one source copy for each of the 2L required endpoint incidences. Its outgoing neighbors are the old neighbors of its underlying vertex other than branch vertices. Use the clique vertices Q outside the branch set as possible distinct destinations. There are 2L mutually vertex-disjoint access paths from the sources to this destination set. To see this, forbid source copies from being internal vertices (or take them as source-only vertices in a directed vertex-capacitated flow). Any separation of cardinality less than 2L leaves some source copy at a vertex v. In the original graph delete the ordinary separator vertices and the other branch vertices, at most c vertices in total. The component of v has more than q vertices because v has degree greater than q+c, including when v is a root in Q. The surviving clique outside the branch set also has more than q vertices. By unbreakability these lie in the same component, giving an access route from the surviving source copy to an available destination. If v belongs to Q this route can start with the direct edge to another surviving clique member. The vertex-disjoint access paths follow by integral maximum flow. Trim each path at its first destination, then pair their distinct tips by clique edges. Restoring the branch vertices gives all required paths with disjoint interiors.

Remove the correction vertices for the moment. The surviving clique lies in one component with more than q vertices and includes every root. All constructed connected cores lie there. Grow them to partition that component and use the bases to adjust their sums. Any other component has at most q vertices by unbreakability. Its vertices have degree at most q−1 plus the number of deleted correction vertices, hence label zero. It has a neighboring correction vertex since G is connected. Assign the whole component to the color of one such neighbor after the corrections. Connectivity and sums follow. Necessity of the part-sum criterion holds on every graph.

The clique can be supplied as a certificate. The proof addresses a subclass of unbreakable graphs. For comparison, an n×n grid with n>c is (c²,c)-unbreakable: intact rows and columns after a deletion meet in one component, and all other vertices are in the at most c damaged rows and c damaged columns. It has vertex connectivity two. Cubic edge expanders likewise have (O(c),c)-unbreakability but bounded degrees and can have arbitrarily large genus. Thus unbreakability alone does not supply this routing certificate.
