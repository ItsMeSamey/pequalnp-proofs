# Balanced cuts and absolute parse mass for the permanent

Let n ≥ 2. Use variables x_{ij} with rows and columns indexed by [n]. A real syntactically set-multilinear arithmetic DAG has a row set R(g) at every gate. Variable leaves have their singleton row, scalar leaves have the empty set, weighted sums preserve the common row set, and binary products join disjoint row sets. The output has all rows and computes the permanent exactly. Count gates including input gates. Let M be the output evaluated after replacing all scalar constants and sum-edge weights by their absolute values and setting every variable to one. This equals the total absolute weight of the expanded parse trees, with edge choices distinguished even at shared gates.

**Theorem.** If s is the gate count and B is the binomial coefficient n choose ceil(n/3), then s M ≥ B n!.

Start at the root of a parse tree. At every sum follow its chosen edge. At every product follow the child with the larger row set, breaking ties by a fixed input order. Stop as soon as the current gate has at most 2n/3 rows. The starting size exceeds that threshold, sums preserve size, and a product crossing the threshold retains more than n/3 rows. Leaves have size at most one, so each tree stops at one occurrence of a balanced gate. There are at most s distinct endpoint gates.

For a fixed endpoint gate g, remove the entire subtree at the selected occurrence and record a context with one hole. The routing decisions above the hole depend only on sum choices there and fixed row sizes. Every parse tree rooted at g therefore plugs into each such context, bijectively recovering the original trees in this class. This holds even with DAG sharing because a parse tree unfolds occurrences independently. Let a_g be the polynomial at g and b_g the sum of weighted context monomials, with the hole contributing one. Every context monomial uses exactly the complementary rows, once each. Grouping by endpoint gives permanent = Σ_g a_g b_g.

Use the coefficient ℓ₁ norm. Within an endpoint class the absolute parse weight factors as the product of the total absolute subtree weight and total absolute context weight. Collecting like monomials can decrease either factor's norm. Consequently Σ_g ||a_g||₁ ||b_g||₁ ≤ M. The final output norm is n! and is a lower bound on this cut sum. An independent bound on the output norm alone therefore does not imply the required upper bound on M. For example a circuit can add K times a monomial and its negative using full-row sums, increasing M by 2|K| with no effect on its output. The resulting cut budget can itself exceed n! if the two added monomials route to distinct copies of balanced gates.

For completeness, index full-row monomials by assigning a column to every row. On the n! permutations the output coefficient is one. For a nonzero cut term with k left rows put D = binom(n,k) and t = ||a||₁ ||b||₁/n!. Normalize the absolute coefficient arrays of a and b separately to probabilities. Write p_A for the probability that the left assignment is injective and has column set A, and q_{Aᶜ} for the complementary event on the right. Under uniform permutations the event of left column set A has probability 1/D. The contribution of that event to the expectation of min(1,|ab|), where ab denotes the coefficient at the random permutation, is at most min(1/D,t p_A q_{Aᶜ}). Summing the geometric-mean bound and using Cauchy–Schwarz gives an expectation at most sqrt(t/D), since the two marginal lists each sum to at most one. At each permutation the sum of the clipped absolute coefficients is at least one. Hence

    1 ≤ Σ_g sqrt(t_g/D_g) ≤ sqrt((M/n!) Σ_g 1/D_g) ≤ sqrt(M s/(n! B)).

For an absolute parse mass of at most n! times C, the conclusion is s ≥ B/C. The proof imposes no depth or fan-out restriction. A bound on binary product fan-in and the syntactic row-set discipline are part of this model. The conclusion concerns permanent circuits with the specified mass control. It gives no unrestricted IPS lower bound.

## A witness to cancellation inside the circuit

In a circuit with identically zero gates pruned, put κ(g)=Σ_i |w_i| ||P_i||₁ / ||P_g||₁ at a weighted sum g. Choose a parse tree by following one sum edge with probability proportional to |w_i| ||P_i||₁, and independently expanding both children at a product. The expected product of κ(g) over all sum occurrences of the parse tree is M/||P_out||₁. This follows by induction: at products the coefficient norm factors exactly because the row sets are disjoint, and at sums the local multiplier times the expected suffix gives the absolute-parse recurrence after normalization. Hence every size-s reduced circuit for the permanent has a parse tree whose sum of log₂ κ(g) over sum occurrences is at least log₂ binom(n,ceil(n/3))−log₂ s. This is a lower bound on the total local cancellation in one parse tree, not necessarily along a single root-to-leaf path. A zero polynomial gate can be replaced by zero and its dead inputs removed before defining these ratios.

Only modulus-one coefficients on permutation tuples enter the clipping argument, so the determinant satisfies the same bound in this model. A decision-function instance follows by feeding exactly one active column bit per row and asking if the selected columns are all distinct. In a homogeneous row-set-multilinear output each one-hot input selects exactly one coefficient. Thus computing this P predicate on the promise already fixes the formal output to the permanent and gives the same lower. Small unrestricted decisions use pairwise comparisons.

For strict row typing, prune identically zero gates by propagating zero products, deleting their contributions at nonzero sums, then discarding unreachable gates; no untyped zero replacement is needed. Edges are occurrence-distinguished, including parallel sum edges.
