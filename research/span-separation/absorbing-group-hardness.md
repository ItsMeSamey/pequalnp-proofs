# Cyclic group with one absorbing element

For the four-element commutative monoid consisting of Z/3 with an absorbing element infinity, connected partitions with fixed two targets 2 and infinity are NP-complete on biconnected bounded-degree vertex expanders. The idempotents form a chain.

Use the occurrence-copy SAT reduction and square-order expanders from two-colour-monoid-hardness.md. Pad to n variables with n+1 square. Make anchor pairs r_i,b_i for i=0 through n, join each pair and give all b_i the absorbing label. Give r_0,r_n the only unit weights and all other low vertices group weight zero. At each variable i make two neutral literals, each adjacent to both anchor pairs i-1 and i. Clause vertices have the absorbing label and see their own literals. Overlay edges r_i b_j and r_j b_i for each edge ij of the index expander.

The part with target 2 excludes every absorbing vertex and needs both unit endpoints. The graph of low vertices is a sequence of diamonds, so a same-colour endpoint path visits all r_i and uses at least one literal per variable. Remaining blue literals are consistent, and every blue clause needs one of them. Conversely, in a satisfying assignment false literals join the red chain of r_i and true literals join the blue chain of b_i, admitting every clause. Both targets hold. The same construction works for a six-element product of binary maximum with Z/3, using high labels (1,0) and targets (0,2) and (1,0).

The paired anchor and literal layers retain consecutive links under any single vertex deletion, and every clause has at least two distinct neighbours, proving biconnectivity. Cluster anchor pairs with their own literals and adjacent assigned clauses, the initial cluster being connected by its anchor edge. These connected clusters have bounded size and degree. Their quotient contains the explicit expander, so the mixed-fibre proof in two-colour-monoid-hardness.md establishes uniform vertex expansion.

## Exact threshold for two parts

Hardness applies to every finite abelian group G of order at least three with an absorber adjoined. Pick nonzero a,b with a+b different from 0,a,b: use the same element of order ≥3 twice, or two distinct nonidentity elements if all have order two. Requiring a+b in the low part forces both endpoints.

For |G|≤2 and no external pins the two-part problem is polynomial on all connected graphs. The crossing edges of any partition into two connected parts belong to one block: two such edges lie on a single cycle via paths in each shore (allow a path of length zero for a shared endpoint); a sole crossing edge is a bridge. Enumerate its possible block and fold every pendant component into its attachment by monoid addition. Both colours must occur in the chosen block. Check bridge blocks directly.

On a biconnected block, two absorbing targets require two absorber vertices, sufficient by connected growth. If both targets are finite, every label must be finite and conservation must hold. If just one target is absorbing, at least one absorber is needed and every finite shore automatically avoids it. Test each finite singleton and each adjacent finite pair with nonempty connected complement; try either designated finite target, verifying both sums. For the trivial group a singleton suffices. In Z/2 a feasible finite shore of sum 1 contains a label 1, usable as a singleton. For sum 0 a finite label 0 gives a singleton whenever present. Otherwise a feasible finite shore consists of a positive even number of ones. Any connected bipartition of a biconnected graph occurs as a prefix/suffix of an st-order, so the first two vertices of that shore form an adjacent pair leaving a connected complement. Their sum is 0. The other target persists by conservation or the remaining absorber. Thus |G|=3 is the sharp group-order threshold for fixed two-part instances.
