# Vortices with consecutive adhesion at most one

Suppose the host is supplied as an embedded graph of bounded Euler genus with interiors of disjoint face discs reserved for vortices. In each disc the society w₁,…,wₙ consists of distinct boundary vertices in cyclic order, and a supplied (linear) path decomposition B₁,…,Bₙ of the vortex has wᵢ in Bᵢ, bounded bag size k and |Bᵢ∩Bᵢ₊₁|≤1. Each vortex intersects the embedded graph exactly in its society and the vortices have disjoint interiors. The following reduction handles arbitrarily long societies, arbitrarily many such discs, and arbitrary binary vertex labels. For fixed genus, k, part count s, number of pins p, and label dimension r, it solves prescribed ordered connected-part sums deterministically in polynomial time. It also yields the corresponding cographic trace catalog by the standard part-sum formulation.

Keep the embedded host graph, all society vertices and all vertices occurring on an adhesion. Each bag has at most three distinct retained vertices: its society position and its two neighboring adhesion vertices if present. The interval property gives this statement even when other society positions occur in the bag. Every remaining vertex belongs privately to exactly one bag, and its neighbors are private vertices of that same bag or retained vertices of that bag. Keep all original edges between retained vertices. In each bag partition private vertices into private connected components and group components with identical sets of neighboring retained vertices (their ports). On a connected nontrivial host each such component has a port unless the whole host consists of that component, a separate bounded-size case.

Replace each group by one vertex adjacent to its ports, with label the sum of all private labels in the group. This graph embeds in the same surface. To see this, line up triangular cells in the disc along the boundary order. The outer corner of cell i is wᵢ, and its other corners are at the gaps immediately before and after position i, where adjacent cells touch at their common corner. Unused gap corners are omitted. Locally draw copies of the ≤3 ports, identifying corners denoting equal vertices by contracting auxiliary triangle-side edges before inserting the local graph. Equivalently these identifications can be performed after drawing a triangle with branches mapped to one representative per distinct port. All occurrences of any retained vertex are joined in its interval of bags, and the contractions produce exactly its original identity. The three direct port-pair edges run along the sides, a single possible triple-port star lies inside, one double-port star for each pair can run alongside its side, and singleton leaves fit close to corners. This drawing is planar inside each cell and introduces no crossings with the embedded graph. The same argument permits any subset of the port edges, even if a side is needed to contract equal local corner occurrences. Loops and duplicate port edges are harmless.

Enumerate s distinct original vertices, one representative per color; treat these as extra pins. Mark groups containing a pinned private vertex exceptional, at most s+p groups. In a genuine solution and any other group, every monochromatic private component has same-color contact with a port: otherwise the whole color would lie there together with its representative. To normalize, choose a repeated port color if present, otherwise any port color, and color the entire group with it. Each private component has a neighbor at every port. All old connectivity between distinct ports of the same color is preserved since at most one color can repeat among three ports. The normalization therefore preserves connectedness and representatives, making that group equivalent to its single weighted star. Its change to the ordered sums is an element of dimension rs. Normalize all unmarked groups and keep exceptional a further subset of at most rs whose changes span and sum to the total change (choose a basis of the change vectors and express the total). Leaving these groups unnormalized restores the original sums, with connectivity preserved by every individual normalization. Thus it suffices to enumerate at most s+p+rs exceptional groups.

Guess private and port colors at each exceptional group, respecting all repeated vertices and pins. Each private connected monochromatic component is adjacent to some same-color ports or is a whole completed color. In the former case connect its incident same-color ports into a clique for the residual solver. These added edges are among the same ≤3 cell corners and hence preserve the embedding. In the latter case require a unique such component for its color, containing its representative, and prohibit the color everywhere else. Subtract the guessed private sums, check completed-color targets, and drop completed colors. Preserve surviving private pins by the guessed local assignment. If their representative survives through a port connection, its color is fixed on such a port. Every active color has its representative either at a retained vertex or in a guessed private component reaching a prescribed same-color port. After deleting the exceptional stars, the remaining stars in any connected solution must therefore have the color of some incident port. Restore each by coloring every private vertex of the group with that color. Every restored component touches every port, so this realizes the star's connections. Replace exceptional port edges by their represented private monochromatic connections, recovering original colors, connectivity, pins and sums. The residual solver is the bounded-genus prescribed part-sum solver with pins. If the residual graph splits, enumerate assignments of active colors to its components (at most s in any feasible solution) and solve them separately, including the empty case.

This proof depends on the adhesion bound. At two adhesion vertices per side a cell may have five distinct ports, and one group may simultaneously link two different repeated colors. The present normalization need not preserve those two connections.

## Unbounded bag size with bounded local treewidth

The argument only uses the bag-size bound to enumerate exceptional local colorings. It applies verbatim if each bag instead induces a graph of bounded treewidth with a supplied decomposition. For each exceptional group include its ports and use the induced local graph with its private-to-port and private edges; port-to-port edges may be left in the scaffold. Add all ≤3 ports to every tree-decomposition bag. Guess port colors. A standard coloring dynamic program on a nice tree decomposition records the color on each current vertex, the partition of same-color bag vertices connected by processed vertices, the sum of processed forgotten private labels per color, and the count of closed monochromatic components per color, truncated at two. Pins restrict the permitted color at their vertex. Edge introduction merges the endpoint blocks when their colors agree, forgetting a last block member counts one closed component, and a join takes transitive closure of both partitions and adds the two disjoint forgotten contributions and closed counts. Keep ports through the final bag. At completion every surviving partition block contains a port. For each closed component its color may appear in no other component of the group or elsewhere, and must contain its enumerated representative. Representative membership of a closed component follows by guessing which colors complete here, checking their representatives lie privately in this group and forbidding those colors elsewhere. If a completed color has a port here that local record is invalid. The final port partition, private sums and completed-color set give exactly the local information used in the global proof. Reconstruction recovers an original coloring. With no bound on bag cardinality this further allows arbitrarily large nonplanar pieces of bounded treewidth along a single disc.

For a concrete family, take the rim of an arbitrarily large plane grid as society. In each internal cell introduce three private vertices, each adjacent to its left adhesion, its distinct right adhesion and its society vertex. Consecutive cells share their one adhesion; nonconsecutive cells are disjoint. Every other cell contains a vertex-disjoint K₃,₃. Thus no constant number of apex deletions makes these graphs planar, while the center retains unbounded treewidth. The merged ordinary cell has one triple-port star.

The normalization argument also works for a fixed finite abelian group Γ when a bounded-genus residual solver for Γ is supplied. To preserve the total deviation in Γˢ, retain a shortest sublist with that sum. Its partial sums, including zero, are distinct, so its length is at most |Γ|ˢ−1. This gives the exception bound s+p+|Γ|ˢ−1. The binary cographic statement uses the sharper rs bound and the established binary residual solver.
