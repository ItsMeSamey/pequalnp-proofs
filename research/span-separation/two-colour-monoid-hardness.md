# Two bitwise-OR targets are NP-complete on biconnected expanders

Use targets R={red} and B={blue} in the four-element union monoid, or two incomparable idempotents in any fixed finite commutative monoid (order e≤f iff ef=f). An idempotent product equal to R cannot contain an incomparable factor. Labelled R and B vertices are thus forced to their respective parts; identity labels are free. Both types occur. Checking a colouring is polynomial.

Start with 3SAT with three distinct variables per clause. Use a variable copy for each occurrence, adding cyclic binary implications among the copies of each original variable (skip singleton cycles). Each copy occurs in at most three clauses and all clauses have at least two distinct variables. Pad to a square number n=m²≥4 of variables with unused dummy variables. Each variable has two identity-labelled literal vertices and two pins R_i,B_i, each adjacent to both literals. Join consecutive literal pairs completely. A B-labelled clause vertex is adjacent to its own literals. Take a simple constant-degree expander E on these n indices. For every edge ij add R_i B_j and R_j B_i. These added edges are always bichromatic. Square orders permit the standard degree-eight [Gabber–Galil expander](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(81)90040-4), simplifying loops and multiplicities without losing a constant expansion bound.

A satisfying assignment colours true literals blue and false literals red. Consecutive links connect each chain, every pin reaches its chain and every clause has a blue neighbour. Conversely R_i and B_i must each reach a literal of their own colour on their own rung: all their other neighbours are forced to the other colour and each colour contains many pins. Thus exactly one literal per rung is blue, and every clause needs such a literal, giving satisfaction.

Maximum degree is at most ten. Each K_(2,2) rung stays connected after deleting any one vertex, and each consecutive pair retains a surviving literal edge. Every clause has at least two distinct neighbours. Thus the graph is biconnected.

Assign each clause to one of its incident rungs. The resulting disjoint connected clusters have size at most F=7; their quotient contains E. Consider a shore of size k≤|G|/2. At least k/(2F) mixed clusters yield that many distinct external neighbours. Otherwise each shore contains at least k/2 vertices in pure clusters. Choose one root per cluster and colour the indices by root shore. Both index shores then contain at least k/(2F) roots, so E has linearly many crossing edges. Each crosses between physical clusters and is realized by a path of length at most 2F+1 between roots. Bounded physical and index degrees bound the number charging any given exterior neighbour, proving constant vertex expansion. This is hardness under bitwise OR; fixed-dimensional binary XOR on this host class has a polynomial algorithm.
