# Exact real linear combinations of small ordinary circuits

Assume the October fixed-density exact 3-CNF algorithm with time V^b 2^(A V/sqrt(log₂ V)) on at most 5V clauses. The October quantitative appendix supplies a unary H in NTIME(2^n) outside NTIME(2^(n/2)), a locally decoded 3-CNF satisfiable exactly on 1^n in H, and a single W in E^NP giving the first satisfying assignment's bits when there is one. With q=n+O(log n) clause-address bits, one decoder of O(n) gates produces three variable labels of t=dn bits, and their signs, for fixed d. An NP oracle constructs W by testing prefixes of the explicit exponential-length formula; all queries and their number fit 2^O(n). October's source obtains W's size lower c t sqrt(log t) infinitely often by checking three copies of a guessed witness circuit with the decoder.

**Stronger representation conclusion.** For some constants alpha,eta>0 this same W, infinitely often, has no exact real linear combination of at most 2^(alpha t) ordinary fan-in-two Boolean circuits each of at most eta t sqrt(log₂ t) noninput gates. All sufficiently large n under the contrary assumption admit such a guess. Select independent Boolean truth-table columns and pivot rows. Cramer's rule supplies a common positive denominator D and integer numerators a_i with O(r log(r+1)) bits for r summands. Guess the atoms and these bounded coefficients. Rational alpha and eta can be rounded down; enforce the sparsity by 2^floor(alpha t) and gate budget by a conservative integer-square-root cutoff, reducing the ultimate claimed constants if necessary.

Let A_j(i)=sum a_u f_u(label_j(i)) for each decoded occurrence. Test exactly whether the sum over all q-bit addresses and j of A_j²(A_j−D)² is zero. Nonnegativity forces Booleanity of A_j/D at every *occurring* label. Unused labels need never be evaluated on a t-bit full cube. The same guessed sum is used in each occurrence, so repeated labels agree. Next sum the product of the three false-literal indicators over addresses. Split by the eight possible sign patterns from the decoder, and for each j use either A_j or D−A_j with D³ cleared. This counts violations with a nonnegative common factor. Accept exactly when both checks vanish; the induced Boolean assignment extends arbitrarily to all unused labels. Completeness follows by guessing W's representation whenever the CNF is satisfiable.

The first test expands into O((r+1)^4) signed integer-weighted counts of conjunctions of at most four ordinary circuits; the second needs O((r+1)^3) with at most three and a constant-size sign test fed by the shared decoder. For each count there are q primary input bits, O(n) decoder gates, at most 4s bottom gates and O(1) terminal gates, s=eta t sqrt(log₂ t). The parsimonious truth-table encoding has V=q+g variables and at most 4g+1 clauses for g total gates. Thus V≤4s+O(n) and M≤5V. Each count is at most 2^q. Signed integer expansion and accumulation require polynomial time in r,n: the largest terms have O(q+r log(r+1)) bits and there are polynomially many of them in r. Guess lists and tape overhead also cost polynomial in r,n. Hence the total time is (r+1)^K poly(n) 2^((4A eta d+o(1))n) for a fixed K. Choose 4A eta d<1/8 and K alpha d<1/8. Eventual representations would decide H nondeterministically in time below 2^(n/2), contradiction. This argument uses the exact counting guarantee for signed expansions; ordinary decision suffices for the paper's single-atom consequence.

**Limits.** One unrestricted polynomial-size ordinary circuit may exceed the bottom cap. Williams's 2010 NEXP versus P/poly transfer requires a saving at every fixed polynomial gate exponent. Murray and Williams's fixed-polynomial NP lower theorem requires nondeterministic gap-UNSAT on circuits of size 2^(epsilon n) in O(2^((1−epsilon)n)) time. Neither premise follows when direct Tseitin has V=n+s and s=n². In the 2018 SUM proof, specializing Circuit Evaluation on encodings of size roughly n+s by fixing the circuit description does not force its atom gate count down to the budget for n remaining primary inputs; no NP near-linear-atom conclusion is inferred here.

**Structural option avoiding per-gate auxiliary cost.** In the graph joining each gate and both parents into a clique, take a supplied separator S. Guess its primary inputs and each separator gate with any parent outside S, b bits in all. Compute remaining separator gate values in topological order. For each residual component enumerate its at most m primary inputs, compute all its gates, and test its gate equations, including those for guessed separator gates touching this component. The clique condition ensures any equation's residual variables occur together. Include the output condition. Multiply component counts for each feasible separator guess then sum: uniqueness of gate extensions prevents overcounting. Time poly(n+s) 2^(b+m); a saving requires b+m below n by enough to cover overhead. The circuit that ANDs every pair of primary bits and ORs all results induces a primary clique, forcing b+m≥n for every separator. This falsifies universality for the supplied syntax.
