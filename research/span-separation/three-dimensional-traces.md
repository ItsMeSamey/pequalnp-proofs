# Exact span traces on a three-dimensional binary interface

Let $E$ be an indexed family of columns over $\mathbb F_2$ and let $T$ be a specified three-dimensional subspace of their ambient space. For $X\subseteq E$, its trace is $T\cap\operatorname{span}X$. Every prescribed trace can be decided in deterministic polynomial time. A line or plane requires one query for a circuit through three designated elements of a represented binary matroid. The zero trace is witnessed by the empty set. The full trace is possible exactly when $T\subseteq\operatorname{span}E$.

**Terminal minors.** Append any ordered basis $(t_1,t_2,t_3)$ of $T$ to the input matrix as three fresh indexed elements $R$. Write $M$ for the resulting column matroid. Contracting $X$ and deleting $E\setminus X$ gives a matroid on $R$ represented by the images of the $t_i$ modulo $\operatorname{span}X$. Its dependency space consists of exactly those triples of coefficients whose linear combination of the $t_i$ lies in the trace.

A matroid has a minor equal to the labelled triangle $U_{2,3}$ on $R$ exactly when it has a circuit containing $R$. In one direction, contract the other members of such a circuit and delete the remaining elements outside $R$. In the other direction, the circuit $R$ in a contraction lifts to an original circuit contained in $R$ together with contracted elements and containing every member of $R$. For binary matrices this follows directly by taking a basis for the contracted columns and expressing the terminal sum in it. The resulting dependence cannot contain a proper circuit, since any such circuit meeting fewer terminals would give a dependence of a proper subset of $R$ in the minor.

**A prescribed line.** Write the line as $\langle u\rangle$ and extend $u$ to a basis $(u,a,b)$ of $T$. Append terminals $(a,b,u+a+b)$. They are independent and sum to $u$. Their quotient matroid is $U_{2,3}$ exactly when its only nonzero dependency has coefficients $(1,1,1)$, equivalently when the trace is exactly $\langle u\rangle$. Apply the three-element circuit test to $M$.

**A prescribed plane.** Write the plane as $H=\langle a,b\rangle$ and choose $c\in T\setminus H$. Append terminals $(c,c+a,c+b)$. They form a basis of $T$. Their images modulo $\operatorname{span}X$ are equal and nonzero precisely when $a,b\in\operatorname{span}X$ and $c\notin\operatorname{span}X$. This is equivalent to trace $H$, and also to the quotient matroid on the terminals being $U_{1,3}$. Duality interchanges deletion and contraction and takes $U_{1,3}$ to $U_{2,3}$. Thus apply the three-element circuit test to $M^*$ with the same element labels. A binary representation of $M^*$ is computed by taking a row basis of the nullspace of the original matrix.

**Witnesses and complexity.** Seymour characterized triples contained in a circuit of a binary matroid. Geelen, Gerards and Whittle describe the efficient algorithm for binary matrix input immediately after their Theorem 2.1. A decision oracle recovers a circuit by deleting optional elements one at a time whenever the answer stays positive. At termination the surviving optional elements together with $R$ form a circuit. In the line case contract the surviving optional elements in the original matroid. In the plane case contract their complement in $E$, because the circuit was obtained in the dual. Both recovery procedures take $O(|E|)$ decision queries. [Seymour, *Triples in Matroid Circuits*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0195-6698(86)80044-0). [Geelen, Gerards and Whittle, section 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.20960v1#S2).

**Application to span separation.** Let $K$ be a plane, $0\ne u\in K$, and $w\notin K$. Take $T=K+\langle w\rangle$. A subset satisfies $u\in\operatorname{span}X$ and $w\notin K+\operatorname{span}X$ precisely when its trace contains $u$ and is contained in $K$. Indeed, the vectors in $T\setminus K$ are exactly $w+K$. The two candidate traces are $\langle u\rangle$ and $K$, giving one primal and one dual circuit query. When $w\in K$ no witness exists. When $u=0$ and $w\notin K$ the empty set works.

**Verification.** The checker rank_two_check.py compares the reduction against enumerated subsets of ground columns, with dual circuits calculated from the dual rank formula. It checks each separation branch independently on all 128 subsets of the nonzero columns in dimension three with one fixed terminal basis, plus 600 seeded cases in dimensions three through eight with possible zero or repeated columns. It also compares the entire family of 16 candidate traces on the 128 exhaustive cases and 50 of the seeded cases. The checker enumerates circuits for these small tests. The polynomial decision bound uses the referenced binary matroid algorithm.
