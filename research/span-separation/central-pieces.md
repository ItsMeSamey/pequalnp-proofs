# A highly connected centre with attached pieces

Consider exact ordered connected-part sums over a binary space W of fixed dimension r, for s≥1 parts. Suppose the supplied graph consists of a highly vertex-connected central graph B and an arbitrary number of pieces of bounded size. Each piece intersects B in between one and a specified constant a of its vertices, called ports. Piece interiors are pairwise disjoint, have no other neighbors, and every component of the piece contains a port. Edges of B are present in the original graph. For fixed r,s,a and piece-size bound, exact ordered part sums and witnesses can be computed in polynomial time if B has sufficiently large connectivity depending only on r,s,a.

Choose a partition of each piece into connected regions, one for each port and containing that port, for example by simultaneous forest growth from the ports. In the baseline assignment the interior of each region receives its port's color. This always attaches every interior vertex to the central graph. Add the W-valued sum of each region's interior to its port. With these augmented labels the central coloring alone computes all baseline sums.

In any feasible global partition, some part colors may be absent from B. Each such peripheral part is confined to one piece: a path leaving an interior must enter B. Mark every piece containing one of those colors, at most s pieces. Keep their original assignments. On each other piece the difference between its actual interior contributions and its baseline contributions gives a vector in W^s. The sum of all these vectors is generated by at most rs of them, by taking an independent representation of this sum. Retain those pieces' actual assignments and use baseline contributions on the others. This argument preserves the required additive contributions for the given central coloring. Connectivity of the central colors will be established independently by the central solver.

Enumerate at most s+rs exceptional pieces, the set of colors appearing in B, and assignments on the exceptional pieces, including their ports. Reject incompatible port assignments. Every globally peripheral color must form a nonempty connected part inside just one piece. For each central color in an exceptional piece, require each monochromatic component to meet a port of its color. This is necessary whenever the full color is connected and meets B. The baseline contribution of an exceptional piece is replaced by its fixed interior contribution; this can be done by omitting that piece's baseline labels from the centre and subtracting the known contribution from the target tuple. The at most a(s+rs) prescribed port colors are now pins.

Apply the high-connectivity part-sum test on B to its central colors. Handle the pins as additional exceptional vertices with mandatory color: first set them aside, minimize the packing potential on the remaining vertices, and assign each pinned original label to its prescribed part. The criterion is necessary for any assignment on B, even a disconnected one, and sufficiently high connectivity realizes it by connected parts. With h equal to the number of central colors minus one, adding p pins increases the branch count by p and the number of demanded paths by p, so connectivity h+2+21hr+11p suffices. With just one central color its assignment is forced and checked directly. Reattaching the local assignments joins each central-colored component to its connected central part. Every peripheral part has been verified inside its piece. Conversely every feasible original partition supplies an enumerated case by the span argument above.

There are polynomially many choices of exceptions for fixed r,s; dependence on their locations is n^{O(rs+s)}. The argument extends to pieces of unbounded size supplied with algorithms returning their possible port-color, interior-sum, and component-connectivity signatures and witnesses. Physical edges of the central graph ensure that routing there is independent of the pieces.

## Virtual edges with at most two ports

An additional case allows the central graph used in the connectivity assumption to include one virtual edge between the two ports of each connected two-port piece. A normalized baseline inside such a piece realizes this edge whenever its ports have the same color: every vertex then has that color. For unequal-colored endpoints that edge cannot be used by either monochromatic central part. A one-port piece provides no virtual edge.

For each exceptional piece, retain its virtual edge precisely when its two ports have the same prescribed color and the fixed local assignment connects them in that color. Otherwise delete its virtual edge; at most s+rs edges are removed. Parallel physical or other viable virtual edges remain usable. Deleting these edges lowers vertex connectivity by at most s+rs, so adding s+rs to the previous sufficient threshold handles the two-port case. One proof of the connectivity bound uses vertex-disjoint paths between any vertex pair: a deleted edge destroys at most one path of such a family. Monochromatic paths in the central output expand piece by piece in the original graph. The polynomial test thus also covers arbitrarily many two-terminal edge replacements even when there is no highly connected physical central subgraph.

A general unbreakable graph need not admit this presentation. In particular large planar grids have bounded vertex connectivity and no highly connected physical centre, even though removing a fixed number of vertices cuts off only bounded-size non-giant regions. A reduction to unbreakable graphs alone does not supply the central routing hypothesis.
