# Fifteen OR gates for Fano incidence with free AND and arbitrary sharing

In a live simplified AO DAG, pinning any essential input x low folds at least one OR whenever the residual is nonzero. Follow a path from x through ANDs to the first OR. An AND-only path to output would force its residual to zero. Deleting vertices from a connected graph in reverse spanning-tree order down to an edge therefore proves the bound |V|−2 on OR gates.

Suppose every vertex loses only one OR on its own zero restriction. All paths from x must enter their first OR at the same physical pin. Two different first ORs both fold, while two zero pins at one OR propagate zero to a second OR or force zero output. The globally earliest OR has nonempty conjunctions of raw labels as its inputs. If an operand contains distinct x,y, all influence of x and y passes this pin, so x cannot be essential at y=0, nor y at x=0. For an edge predicate this forces the isolated edge xy: any other neighbor supplies a counterexample by its singleton. If both operands are distinct singleton raws, their sole route is their OR, requiring them to be nonadjacent and have identical neighborhoods. Equal singleton operands are a removable alias. Consequently, with all vertices essential, no isolated edge or false twins, and every single deletion leaving a nonzero predicate, some vertex deletes at least two distinct OR gates. Simplify before reapplying this lemma.

In Fano point-line incidence, after at most two vertex deletions every remaining vertex is essential. Distinct same-shore neighborhoods originally have symmetric difference four; opposite-shore essential neighborhoods differ. An isolated edge requires removing the other four neighbors of its endpoints. Thus three successive applications delete at least six ORs.

After three deletions the residual requires at least nine ORs. If three noncollinear points are removed, every line still meets a point and any surviving point pair is joined by its line; hence the eleven remaining vertices are connected. The dual applies to lines. With two points and one line removed all surviving lines touch a surviving point. If the joining line of two points was removed, take an intermediate surviving point outside that line; all other joining lines survive. The dual applies to one point and two lines. The connected bound gives nine.

Three collinear deleted points isolate their line. The other six lines realize all six pairs of the four surviving points, giving the once-subdivided K4 on ten essential vertices. This has no isolated edge or false twins, and any vertex deletion leaves nine connected vertices: deleting a midpoint leaves the other K4 connections, and deleting a branch vertex leaves a triangle of branch vertices with each affected midpoint retaining a neighbor. Thus a further double refund and the connected bound give 2+7=9 ORs. The dual is identical.

In every case the original DAG requires at least 6+9=15 OR gates. The seven-star implementation has twenty; the unrestricted m−1 claim remains open here.
