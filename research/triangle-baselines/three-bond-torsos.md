# Splitting cographic three-bonds without a global cographicity assumption

Let G be a connected graph, let its cut space be the primal code, and let its even-edge space be the dual code. Assign arbitrary fixed-dimensional binary marker labels to real edge memberships on both sides. Seek a primal/dual pair disjoint on real edges with specified total marker. Virtual triples used for code gluing have no disjointness requirement. Here gluing means equality of the two copies of the coordinates followed by their elimination, for the primal and dual codes separately. Take a supplied family of edge-disjoint three-edge bonds with laminar chosen shores. The two shore graphs of each bond are connected. The construction below applies to ordinary real bond triples and to triples already used as child interfaces. We distinguish the exact code identities, which need only these assumptions, from the high-degree dynamic program, which uses rooted baselines.

## One cut, retaining the original three elements

Write the bond as l_i r_i, i=1,2,3. In the left shore add a virtual apex connected by three edges U_i to l_i. In the right shore similarly attach V_i to r_i. Repetitions of endpoints cause no difficulty for the code identity. Join these two cographic pieces to a middle **graphic** piece: three vertices forming a triangle, with three copies of each side. The copies opposite vertex i are named U_i,V_i,W_i. The original real edge l_i r_i is represented by W_i, carrying exactly its original labels; if the bond was an interface to another piece, W is that interface instead.

The middle cycle code consists of u,v,w with u+v+w constant on the three indices: the three multiplicity parities on the triangle's sides must coincide. Its cut code consists of (s,s,s), s of even weight. For a shore cut, u_i is the color of l_i plus the left apex color, and likewise for v_i. Thus w_i is the desired original edge cut up to a common bit. This bit is free: flip all vertex colors on one shore, including its apex, to match the required absolute offset, preserving all shore traces. Conversely any original coloring extends to shore cuts and a middle cycle. On the dual side a selected original bond set s has even weight, by parity of degrees on either shore. Replacing each selected bond edge by the appropriate virtual spoke gives an even set on each torso. The converse glues immediately from equality of the three s traces. These constructions respect arbitrary ordinary-edge labels and disjointness, since W is the unique real copy. In particular the middle piece is not a single six-edge K4: its nine elements distinguish three independent ports.

A K4 completion of W replaces its triangle by the other three edges of a graphic K4 by cycle-code gluing. This is the ordinary graphic ΔY on W, adding a center and leaving U,V intact. Consequently the completed middle piece remains graphic, even if the original global matroid after the same exchange has a noncographic minor.

## A laminar family

Use the tree of chosen shores ordered by inclusion, with G as root; an atom is the set difference between a tree node and its children. In each atom replace every neighboring exterior region by its own apex, keeping real internal edges and the corresponding three incident virtual spokes per apex. An edge crossing one selected separation cannot cross another, since the bond edge sets are disjoint. Thus all its endpoints belong to the two neighboring atoms and no two apices of an atom are adjacent. Place the nine-element connector above on each tree edge. Iterating the one-cut identity yields the exact original labeled pair of codes. Each piece and the sum of their sizes are polynomial in the input. If each atom has only a bounded number of apices, the ordinary tree interface DP with at most 2^6 pair states per triple and 2^t marker totals already applies using the fixed-exception graphic/cographic piece oracle.

For simple 3-connected G, repeatedly splitting on nonstar three-bonds keeps the cographic atom graphs simple and 3-connected. In a nontrivial shore at least three distinct vertices are used as attachment points: a two-vertex shore cannot have boundary three by the minimum degree and simplicity, and at most two attachment vertices of a larger shore would separate its interior. Each nontrivial shore remains connected on deletion of one vertex x: otherwise each component of the shore minus x would require at least two outside edges, since deleting x and the sole outside neighbor would separate such a component. Contracting the opposite shore to a single apex therefore preserves connectivity after any two vertex deletions, including deletion of the apex. Other three-bonds retain their shores after contraction: the contracted region was entirely on one side, and no surviving bond edge was contracted. If a cut is already a star, it requires no splitting to use the local baseline argument below. Its center can be treated as another designated degree-three apex; edge-disjoint stars have nonadjacent centers.

## High-degree atom with rooted K4 baselines

For clarity, a rooted K4 baseline at an outgoing matroid triangle T is a K4 minor retaining T as its triangle, with inherited labels on the other three real elements via fixed linear lift maps. Under minor contraction/deletion, such maps always exist: lift minor cycles using only contracted coordinates beyond their retained values, and lift minor cocycles using only deleted coordinates beyond their retained values. Sections can be chosen linear by elimination. Added supports are disjoint, so disjointness of ordinary real elements survives. Push the original two marker maps through these sections; this may also produce linear contributions on the virtual trace T. These are retained as boundary labels; they can equivalently be transported to the adjacent expanded piece. The statement requires the hypotheses of a genuine rooted minor, or equivalent sections with the stated support properties.

A triangle T in a graphic K4 has ordinary outside spokes. With P the 3-by-3 matrix having zero diagonal and ones elsewhere, prescribed triangle primal and dual traces t,s extend with real spokes p=Pt and d satisfying Pd=s. Here s is even. The spokes can be made disjoint precisely if t·s=0. Indeed p has size zero or two; p·d=t·s. With p of size two, orthogonality makes d agree on both its coordinates, and complementing d on all three lets us set both to zero. The necessity follows also from code orthogonality. Every actual child solution disjoint on real coordinates has t·s=0. Thus each such solution can be replaced at the same boundary by some baseline solution, although their marker totals may differ.

Given a successful set of child states at an atom, choose one baseline completion for each at unchanged boundary. Their marker discrepancies lie in F₂^t; their total can be expressed as the sum of at most t of those discrepancies, using a basis of their span. Retain the successful true child choices for that subset and use the baseline elsewhere. The total is unchanged. Conversely all baseline solutions lift to child solutions. Enumerate the O((1+d)^t) candidate exceptional child subsets at an atom of degree d, their fixed-size boundary states and their table marker values. Replace the other children by their labeled K4 baselines. A replacement on a degree-three apex of a cographic atom is graph YΔ: remove the apex and replace its three spokes by the triangle joining its neighbors, indexed by opposite spoke. A direct check uses cut values Pt on the new triangle when t was the old spoke cut vector, and all cycle routings d with Pd=s when s was the old even spoke set. Distinct apex centers are nonadjacent, so any number of such operations can be performed simultaneously. The resulting piece is cographic with at most t exceptional child triples, plus its parent triple. Apply the fixed-exception marked graphic/cographic oracle to this piece. Labels previously sitting on the eliminated virtual coordinates require care, as detailed below. A bounded middle connector and its incident baselines can instead be exhaustively tabulated. Store backpointers and mark only real edges in reconstructing the pair. For fixed t and valid baseline representations this is polynomial independently of node degree.

**Virtual-label bookkeeping.** An arbitrary linear section's baseline labels on an eliminated virtual triangle cannot simply be discarded. For the primal section, choose the lift of the all-one triangle cycle with no new real coordinates (the actual child contains this triangle cycle); extend that choice to a basis. The virtual triangle has no real marker itself. The resulting primal boundary-label map kills 111, hence factors as a linear map of Pt, the retained new spoke set. For the dual section the boundary s always equals Pd for the retained dual spokes, so any dual boundary labels factor through d. Equivalently choose a basis for the full K4 cycle and cut spaces and evaluate the two real marker functions by lifting; both marker functions of real edges of the child are representable entirely on the three K4 spokes by the same argument. Thus YΔ replaces ordinary real edges with ordinary labeled real edges without a separate charge on eliminated interface values.

**Finding baselines recursively in simple 3-connected atom trees.** At a cographic atom, complete all child stars by their rooted K4 baselines. The resulting ordinary graph is still connected after deletion of up to two surviving vertices: any segment passing through a removed degree-three apex can be shortcut on its neighbor triangle. The parent apex and its three distinct neighbors survive because the apices were nonadjacent. The graph minus the parent apex is 2-connected, possibly with parallel edges, and has three specified distinct neighbors. A 2-connected graph has a rooted triangle minor on any three vertices: taking a cycle through the first two and a two-fan from the third yields either a cycle through all three or a theta with the three roots on distinct branches, which contracts to a rooted triangle. Thus the parent apex and its three star edges extend to a rooted K4 minor of the graph. Under planar K4 self-duality the parent star is the distinguished triangle in the cographic matroid. At a middle connector rooted at one of its three triangles, the parent triangle and the center obtained by ΔY baseline completion on either of the other two triangles already give a rooted graphic K4. Rooting the refinement at an atom ensures each middle connector has a child shore available for this completion, even if W is kept real. These local minor constructions and composed linear sections build the required rooted baselines given compatible rooted baselines for any externally attached subtrees. The decomposition alone imposes no such existence assumption on arbitrary external subtrees. The tree algorithm need not assert that the matroid represented by its entire merged graph remains globally graphic or cographic.

**Laminarity is automatic for minimum cuts of odd size.** In a 3-edge-connected graph two distinct size-three cuts cannot have crossing shores in the bipartition sense (all four corners nonempty). Indeed write P=A∩B, Q=A−B, R=B−A, S=V−(A∪B). Each corner has boundary at least three. Subtracting |δ(P)|+|δ(S)| from |δ(A)|+|δ(B)| shows that twice the number of Q–R edges is at most zero. Applying the same argument with B complemented eliminates P–S edges. Both inequalities are equalities, so all four corner degrees equal three. The remaining edge counts around the four-cycle of corners P,Q,S,R alternate a,3−a,a,3−a. The boundary of A then has even size, a contradiction. Orienting all chosen shores away from a fixed root vertex therefore gives a laminar family. Simple 3-connected G satisfies 3-edge-connectivity, so no separate laminarity promise is needed for a supplied edge-disjoint list of size-three bonds in that case.
