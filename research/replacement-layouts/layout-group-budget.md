# A group-support obstruction independent of the number of evaluation points

At t write q_t for the number of source groups with positive measurement rank, c_t for the sum of group measurement ranks, and s for maximum source-group size. Let f_v be one selected incident factor per included variable, with mismatch count h_v between its target schedule and v's source schedule. Every layout over a field with Q distinct nonzero points satisfies

    sum_v h_v ≤ nR + s sum_t q_t ≤ nR + s sum_t c_t.

At a mismatch with zero measurement rank on v's source group, the kernel condition makes the target row vanish on the whole group. Its coefficient at v is zero. The diagonal coefficient is a polynomial equal to one at zero, so each selected variable suffers at most R such mismatches. All remaining mismatches at t belong to the union of measured source groups, containing at most s q_t variables. Sum over t. With integer rows of absolute row sum at most D one can use min(s,D)c_t instead of s c_t: every nonzero column is hit by some member of a row basis chosen from the supplied rows.

The expander scopes in layout-determinant.md have a selection with sum_v h_v ≥ γ nQ. Hence R/Q + s average(c_t)/n ≥ γ. When s≤2n/r, this implies R/Q + 2 average(c_t)/r ≥ γ. In particular if R/Q≤γ/2, average c_t≥γr/4.

**One-point streaming obstruction.** If R/Q≤γ/2, at least γnQ/2 mismatches must land in groups whose measurement map has positive rank. One point t therefore has at least γn/2 mismatching variables covered. With a_t active groups of maximum size s_t, a_t s_t≥γn/2. At least 2^{a_t} global measurement tuples occur: every active group can independently pick zero or a nonzero Boolean column. More sharply the number of tuples is at least 2^{c_t}, because distinct Boolean vectors on c_t independent columns have distinct measured outputs. At E=U=empty the loop visits all these tuples. The literal full scan of the largest group takes 2^{s_t} iterations per retained tuple. Thus even after pruning every unattainable tuple the loop takes at least 2^{s_t+c_t} ≥ 2^{2 sqrt(γn/2)} iterations at this one point. If one visits only assignments in each fiber, every local assignment still occurs in some attainable tuple; the weaker max(2^{a_t},2^{s_t}) bound remains. Over finite fields ranks and the kernel assumption must both hold after reduction, with Q distinct nonzero points. The published routine uses rational rows and integer measurements.

The alternate case is verified for the direct product of edge factors 1−Y_{e,u}Y_{e,v} on a bounded-degree expanding graph. The manuscript defines the budget d in separate factor-incidence indeterminates, so d=2|E|=Θ(n), d_H=dR and q=floor(dR/Q). Its correction loop per point visits exactly sum_{j=0}^{min(q,n)}2^j binom(n,j) pairs. If R/Q≥γ/2 this is 2^{Ω(n)}. For r<4 a group has size Ω(n) already. Hence every allowed layout incurs at least 2^{Ω(√n)} iterations for this literal direct-product algorithm. This does not lower bound the whole October SAT algorithm, whose preprocessing can change factor scopes, or routines aggregating realizable tuples, local assignments or correction pairs. Even existential layouts with s=O(log n), c_t=O(log n) at every point, R/Q=o(1) fail on the expander scopes, independently of Q.

For the exact ambient tuple box in the rational manuscript there is an additional factor. At the same witness point, let k_t be the sum of measurement ranks. Measured columns belong to the supports of basis rows, giving k_t min(s_t,D)≥γn/2 when the counting of active vertices is refined to those with a nonzero diagonal entry (mismatches at diagonal roots have already been charged globally). The full integer box has at least (2D+1)^{k_t} elements for D≥1. If D≥n^{1/4}, the larger of s_t and k_t log₂(2D+1) is Ω(√(n log n)); if D<n^{1/4}, then k_t=Ω(n^{3/4}). Together with the scan cost this gives 2^{Ω(√(n log n))} iterations for the literal ambient-box implementation. The bound surviving removal of all unattainable tuples is 2^{Ω(√n)}.
