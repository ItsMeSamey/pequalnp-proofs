# Swap closure and the size of a coded layout

The auxiliary layout in the supplied manuscript uses labels (a,d), with each word having Q coordinates. Its commuting permutations exchange the letters of a and d at one coordinate. The Fourier interpolation uses these permutations separately. The following bounds concern layouts retaining those particular permutations.

**Product codebooks.** Suppose variable and factor labels can be chosen independently from the same nonempty code K contained in [r]^Q, and the new layout is K × K. It is closed under every coordinate swap if and only if K is the product of its one-coordinate projections. For the forward implication choose, for each desired coordinate, a word carrying that letter, and splice it into a running word by one swap. Closure keeps each running word in K. The reverse implication is immediate. Consequently if all r groups appear at every point, this Cartesian codebook has exactly r^Q words and its layout still has r^(2Q) labels. Even allowing two different codebooks A and B gives the same result: closure under the product of all swaps sends A × B to B × A, which forces A=B. This rules out replacing the full Cartesian label set by an error-correcting Cartesian codebook with the published independent-swap proof unchanged.

**Layouts permitting correlated pairs.** Every closed layout is a union of swap orbits. An orbit is specified by its unordered pair of letters at each coordinate. If the two words differ in h coordinates, exactly 2^h orientations occur, all distinct. Thus a closed layout containing just one pair at distance h has at least 2^h positions. On this orbit the active swaps act as translations of the binary h-cube and the inactive swaps are identities. In the manuscript’s rational Fourier construction, exactly binom(h,j) character vectors have swap weight j. The fraction of exceptional rows in the manuscript's construction on a union of orbits is therefore the orbit-size-weighted average of the respective Bin(h,1/2) tail probabilities for its chosen interval. The orbit supports are disjoint, so different intervals of length R can also be chosen independently without changing the support proof or degree bound. Any explicit polynomial-size closed layout consequently has h=O(log n) on every incidence pair. Shortening the alphabet overhead is possible for individual orbits; assigning compatible first and second labels across an entire factor graph while keeping measurement cost and group sizes small remains a separate requirement.

**Balanced labels on expanding incidences.** For a concrete obstruction even to correlated codebooks, take the factors to be the edges of a graph on n variables with maximum degree Δ and edge expansion α>0 for sets of at most n/2 vertices. Suppose r≥4 and every variable group at each point has size at most 2n/r. Summing expansion over groups counts every crossing edge twice, so at every point at least αn/2 factors have endpoints in different groups. Each such factor receives a single group and hence has at least one mismatched incidence. Summing over all Q points and averaging over at most Δn incidences, some incidence pair differs on at least αQ/(2Δ) coordinates. Its full swap orbit alone needs at least 2^(αQ/(2Δ)) positions. In particular a polynomial explicit layout retaining the individual swaps requires Q=O(log n) on bounded-degree expander families. This counting argument leaves open implicit matrix operations, alternate permutations, and methods that directly satisfy the polynomial-row conditions.
