# Fixed-marker disjoint cycles and cocycles of regular binary matroids

**Theorem.** Fix t. Given an explicit binary representation H of a regular matroid, vectors a_e,b_e in F₂^t on its labelled elements, and a target h, a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm finds c in ker H and d in row H with disjoint support and ∑a_e c_e+∑b_e d_e=h, or determines that none exist. For variable t the bound given here is XP. The method gives witnesses but does not count them. In the fundamental representation [I|B], the primal and dual vectors are (Bq,q) and (p,Bᵀp), recovering the bipartite guarded-matrix problem with linear markers on (p,q).

## Decomposition and table semantics

Use the polynomial conflict-tree decomposition of Fomin et al., Theorem 3 and Corollary 1. Its basic nodes are graphic, cographic or parallel extensions of rank-five R10. Graph models are supplied for the first two. Neighboring nodes share zero, one or three labelled elements; the triple is a circuit in both incident pieces. The sums may be performed in any order. Consequently, on merging the whole side of any triangle interface first, its three columns still form a genuine triangle in that assembled side. Distinct interfaces at a node are disjoint: a label in three basic pieces would form a cycle in the conflict graph. Extended singleton joins may use loops or coloops; triples need not be coindependent. The proof below handles these cases. [Fomin et al., Section 4.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.02300)

Binary code gluing means matching the primal values on the two copies of an interface and projecting them away. Dual gluing has the same rule: take the orthogonal of the intersection of the direct sum of side codes with the equality subspace. It is the sum of their orthogonals, and the orthogonal of equality consists of equal masks supported just on the two interface copies. Iterate over the tree. Thus genuine global primal and dual vectors can be recovered from compatible local vectors.

Root the tree arbitrarily. Each subtree stores all possible triples of outgoing primal mask, outgoing dual mask and score, requiring disjointness on genuine real coordinates. Only real labels count. Summing local inner products on one side of an interface gives zero cross-mask dot product, since internal interface terms cancel in equal pairs. At a triangle T add its supported primal 111 cycle simultaneously to both sides to choose the unique mask of weight at most one; the dual mask is even, and orthogonality then implies actual disjointness. Operations on different slots commute and leave scores unchanged. At a singleton the masks must already be disjoint. Thus raw virtual masks can always be retrieved from the canonical table by the triangle toggle. The root table has empty boundary. Tables have O(2^t) states per node with a constant number of boundary bits. Compute a binary matrix for any *complete side*, with the outgoing boundary kept, by Gaussian elimination in the stacked side-code and matching equations, ignoring all guards. This is a polynomial-size preprocessing independent of the dynamic program.

## Covering every boundary state with small labelled minors

Consider an assembled child with an outgoing triangle. The following construction preserves all unlabelled boundary states and supplies a lift for every labelled minor witness. In general its labelled table is only a subset of the actual child table.
Fix a two-dimensional subspace W of a binary representation. Keep its three nonzero elements as distinguished boundary columns. A boundary state (s,ℓ), with s in W and ℓ in its dual, is attainable if some subset X of outside columns sums to s and a functional f on the ambient space extends ℓ and vanishes on X. Thus primal and dual supports are disjoint outside the boundary. A primal trace on the triangle may be chosen to sum to s. Since ℓ(s)=0, one of its two representatives avoids the support of the dual triangle trace.

**Representative theorem.** A deterministically constructible minor retains the boundary and at most three outside columns while preserving exactly the attainable boundary states. Either the outside columns are distinct nonzero vectors of W, or the six columns form M(K₄) with the boundary as a triangle.

Choose outside columns C that project to a basis modulo W. In coordinates W⊕span(C), write columns as (pₑ,qₑ), with pₑ in W and qₑ in the C-basis. Contracting C leaves the columns pₑ. If all three nonzero types occur, retain one copy of each. This realizes all pairs satisfying ℓ(s)=0 by using the copy of s when s is nonzero. If zero or one type occurs, retain one copy per nonzero type. Every attainable s lies in their span and satisfies ℓ(s)=0. These copies realize exactly those possibilities.

Suppose two nonzero types a and b occur. Form the bipartite fundamental graph joining each nonbasis column e to each basis element x on which qₑ is one. If the types lie in different components, each component carries at most one nonzero type. Given a realized state with support X, its qₑ sum to zero separately within every component because distinct components use disjoint basis coordinates. The sum of the full columns of X in a component therefore lies in W, on its single line, and is annihilated by ℓ because f vanishes on X. Thus s belongs to the span of the types in {a,b} annihilated by ℓ. Contract C and retain one copy of each type to realize every such pair.

Otherwise take a shortest path from any a-column to any b-column. All internal nonbasis columns have type zero. If it begins e–x–z–x′ and continues, exchange basis element x for nonbasis element z. This preserves span(C) because p_z=0, so every column type stays fixed. For any nonbasis column with x-coordinate one, the exchange toggles its x′-coordinate, since z has both coordinates. In particular e gains adjacency to x′. Every nonbasis column on the remaining path after x′ has x-coordinate zero by shortestness and its remaining adjacencies persist. This reduces the distance between types by at least two. Repeat until there is a length-two path e–x–f. Contract C minus x. In W⊕⟨x⟩ the three columns x,e,f now equal (0,1),(a,1),(b,1). Retain them and delete the other outside elements. Together with the boundary they represent the spokes and rim of K₄.

In this minor the zero syndrome has the empty support. For any nonzero s with ℓ(s)=0, exactly two spokes sum to s. Choose the dual value on x to annihilate the first of these spokes. It also annihilates the second, realizing the state. Conversely any minor witness lifts. Correct its primal sum using contracted columns and extend its dual functional with zero on the contraction space. Deleted columns may carry dual support and no primal support.

For transferring markers, the contracted columns in this construction are independent, so the required primal correction is a unique linear function of the retained primal coordinates. On the dual side choose a linear section extending minor functionals, choosing a section of the evaluation map as well if only the minor cocycle is specified. The retained triangle ensures the same ℓ. Hence every lifted marker is a linear function of minor cycle and cocycle coordinates. The pure virtual triangle has zero outside coordinates and requires no primal correction, so toggling it preserves every lifted real marker. For the parallel templates the retained types span all the original types. Thus a boundary functional vanishing on all retained copies extends by zero on span(C) and vanishes on all outside columns. Choose the dual section to do this on that subspace and extend linearly. Its lifted real marker then factors through only the outside gadget coordinates even if fewer than two types occur. For the star template the outside spokes span its entire ambient space, giving the same conclusion immediately.


For a singleton e which is neither a loop nor a coloop of the assembled child, all three states 00,10,01 are feasible by using zero, a primal circuit through e alone on the primal side, or a dual cocircuit through e alone on the dual side. Their labelled responses give a baseline using one weight for each bit and zero for 00. If e is a loop the dual bit is forced zero, the primal bit is free, and the empty real response realizes either legal state with score zero. Contract this unweighted parent element, lifting a primal coordinate from the contraction and a dual coordinate fixed at zero. If e is a coloop use deletion dually. An empty interface has the zero baseline. This construction applies to complete children and does not require the basic node adjacent to its parent to be an adequate representative by itself.

## Sparse exceptions at an arbitrary-degree node

Fix any successful choice with a prescribed outgoing state. For every child choose a baseline with its same boundary and let its discrepancy be the actual real score minus the chosen baseline score. Their sum in F₂^t equals the sum of at most t of the original discrepancies by selecting a basis. Keep those exceptional children and use the corresponding minor realizations instead of all other child witnesses. This preserves both the target and the parent vectors. Conversely every labelled baseline response lifts. Enumerate subsets of at most t exceptional children, and for each their feasible boundary states and scores from the full tables. Retain their parent virtual slots with fixed masks and no labels, adding their scores separately. Keep the parent-facing slot similarly when filling a table entry. The other child interfaces use the following physical substitutions or the equivalent local tests. The discrepancy argument is existential and relies on retaining the complete child tables for exceptions.

For a graphic parent, K4 completion removes the rim T and adds a fresh star on the three rim vertices. If s selects a rim edge then its endpoint spokes are selected by the primal vector; otherwise none are selected. The center cut color can be chosen consistently because these endpoints have equal colors. Conversely the even spokes recover the unique canonical s, and pairwise XORs of the cut spokes recover the rim cut. Distinct new centers are independent even for triangles sharing vertices. The primal and dual weights transported by the linear sections go directly on the three spokes.

A parallel triangle representative has one real copy for each retained type and no copy for each missing type. Its primal trace modulo 111 is the vector of the three real-copy selection bits (put zero at missing types), and its real dual bit on each copy equals the old dual bit of that type. Use the freedom to toggle the virtual triangle to agree with these primal bits. In the parent replace each retained virtual edge by its labelled copy and delete missing elements as matroid elements. A cocycle in this restriction extends to the old representation by extending its functional; the missing dual entries do not affect any retained score. These rules prove equality after projection, not merely containment. On a cographic graph these deletions correspond to graph contractions. Ordinary singleton baseline weights simply add to the weights on their parent edge. Perform K4 graph replacements or record the signed tests below before destructive minor operations on other slots, so the original slot identifications remain available. Equivalently keep the graph unchanged and implement missing parallel elements or degenerate singleton elements with the indicated forced zero primal/dual masks; the relevant minor operations and linear lifts are performed together as described below.

A nonplanar cographic basic graph from the general conflict tree need not have star ports. The next subsection handles arbitrary possibly crossing three-edge bonds and avoids imposing such a promise. There is also an independent geometric route using the refined decomposition of Aprile and Fiorini on three-connected torsos: their nonplanar cographic slots are degree-three vertex stars, a K4 completion replaces a star by the triangle of its outside neighbors, and planar duals supply graphic nodes. The proof here uses the unsigned Z/4 route and does not require this refinement. [Aprile and Fiorini, Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08539) · [Bérczi et al., Theorem 2.5 and end of Section 2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jctb.2025.10.009)

## The cographic node and its signed correction
Let G represent the cographic parent. Its c is a graph cut and its d is a graph even subgraph. The named triples are edge-disjoint three-edge bonds. On each virtual triple let p be c restricted there modulo 111, and q be d restricted there. Child feasibility requires q even and p·q=0. Labels are t-bit vectors. The conclusion below is a deterministic XP algorithm for t markers and O(t) extra prescribed boundary states.

A representative c disjoint from d exists on every named triple simultaneously. At weight-two q the two corresponding c bits are equal, so complement the triple if needed. At q=0 any representative works. Complementing the three bits of one bond adds a graph cut with precisely that support, altering neither real coordinates nor any other boundary class. Thus no assumption about laminarity is needed.

Number the three K4 spokes opposite their corresponding triangle edges. Given p,q their selected c bits on the spokes are uniquely the vector p+(sum p_i)111 by parity at the boundary vertices. A canonical d selection on the spokes is zero at q=0 and 111+q otherwise. This realizes the correct boundary differences. For nonzero p, the c-spoke support consists of the two coordinates where the canonical d vanishes (or d is identically zero); this follows from p·q=0. The only other d choice complements all three bits and is legal exactly if p=0. Thus each optional child choice, at eligible triples, adds the fixed label W=sum_i w_i for its d-spoke weights w_i. In a feasible witness any sum of such W can be realized by at most t originally eligible triples. Enumerate their locations and impose p=0 there. The c-spoke labels are GF2-linear in parent cut bits and gauge invariant. The canonical d-spoke label is

    sum_i q_i w_i + ((integer weight(q)/2) mod 2) W.

The first term is a sum of GF2 parent even-edge labels. For the second add, on each of the three virtual edges, the vector W lifted in {0,1}^t to unsigned (Z/4)^t edge labels. In every full even edge set the resulting residue in each coordinate is even: each triple contributes zero or twice W coordinatewise. Divide by two to read its bit contribution. Across all triples residue addition automatically sums their high bits. Exceptional triple states constrain per-edge coordinates: enumerate a disjoint cut representative for each prescribed p with its q and impose the requisite O(t) single-coordinate GF2 markers. Labels at exceptional triples can be omitted and replaced by the prescribed contribution. This enumerates all disjoint representatives, including q=0 when both are possible.

For any fixed cut D, take a fundamental cycle basis of the edges outside D. The indicator that an even set attains the required target is a Boolean polynomial of degree O(t) in the basis coefficients: each GF2 sum is linear, and the high bit of an unsigned Z/4 sum of selected labels equals the XOR of the individual high contributions and of all pairs of low-bit contributions. Thus each target-bit test has degree at most two. Each extra prescribed ordinary bit adds one to the degree; a conjunction is their product. Composition with linear basis coordinates does not increase degree after Boolean reduction. A nonzero degree-r multilinear polynomial on a Boolean cube takes value one at a point of weight at most r by the Boolean Möbius transform. Therefore any successful cut admits a successful even set in the union of a forest and O(t) chords. Cut markers are ordinary GF2, so the root-shore contribution outside this bounded-cyclomatic support can be reduced to O(t) extra positive vertices using a basis of their weights. This second compression preserves all designated cut pins by including them as ordinary markers.

Apply the bounded-topology subdivision enumeration below with the expanded O(t) cyclomatic bound. Match each topology segment by its unsigned Z4 edge sum in addition to the existing GF2 edge labels and vertex-label endpoint encoding. These are precisely the finite abelian unsigned groups handled by the cited Liu–Yoo linkage theorem. Combine the guessed segment residues globally before dividing any even coordinates by two. This gives fixed-t deterministic polynomial decision and witness recovery; it uses neither a 3-connectivity hypothesis nor a laminar decomposition.

For decision only it is sufficient to keep the canonical dual-spoke choice for every nonexceptional K4; it already covers every boundary state, and a noncanonical real score is accounted for by the general discrepancy argument. Optional complementary choices in the preceding calculation can alternatively be included with the stated extra guesses. If p=0 is imposed, this denotes zero boundary class, i.e. a constant three-bit representative; enumerate the permitted raw representatives.

In the cographic calculation, keep the K4 slots as the original three graph edges. First put the invariant cut weights and the even-edge low-bit and mod-four weights on their slots. Other slots that require a zero bit have no removed-element label. Restrict by the corresponding matroid minor (zero primal gives deletion; zero dual gives contraction). The corresponding graphical deletions/contractions occur outside every K4 triple. A cut supported on exactly such a triple remains a cut after graph-edge deletion or contraction elsewhere: an edge outside this cut has both ends on the same shore. Thus the even-trace and independent bond-toggle identities survive even if the cut ceases to be minimal. Retained real copy labels remain ordinary. Simultaneous lift in reverse order restores all omitted masks, and the removed zero-side coordinates do not introduce actual conflicts. Exception and outgoing boundary slots are kept, so their prescribed bits survive the projection. When using cropped parallel templates, the parent masks originally used for their copies are restored by the functional-extension and independent triangle-toggle rules.

## Graphic fixed-exception oracle

In ordinary graph notation let C be the even support, D=δ(S) its disjoint cut. Each cut edge label b_e becomes a vertex label w_v by XOR over incident ends (a loop counts twice); its total is the XOR of weights of S. Up to O(t+1) specified exceptional edges may prescribe either or both C,D bits even when both are one: remove these edges, record contributions, and prescribe the colors of their endpoints compatible with their cut bits. The residual even-side support is a join with fixed odd-degree set U, the mod-two boundary of the removed selected edges. For the Z/4 method one may instead prescribe disjoint canonical representatives as ordinary single-coordinate score tests. When raw overlaps on the outgoing slots are needed, separately remove those edges, retaining their fixed contributions including to the indicated Z/4 residues; pins and final residues are thus interpreted in the original full graph.

Take a hypothetical successful coloring and a spanning forest of the remaining monochromatic graph. It has a unique forest U-join P, and every allowed join is P plus a combination of fundamental cycles. In the purely binary case at most t of these additional cycles suffice to preserve the even-edge label. More generally the indicator of the required scores and pins, after fixing the shore, is a multilinear Boolean function of those coefficients of degree O(t+1). For an unsigned mod-four sum, the low bit is linear and the high bit is linear in the high coefficients plus all pair products of low coefficients. Testing a specified final low and high bit therefore costs degree at most three per coordinate. This remains true after the affine substitution from fundamental-cycle coefficients and a fixed join. A nonzero multilinear polynomial of degree r has a nonzero value on some vector of Hamming weight at most r, by the Boolean Möbius transform. A successful support can thus be chosen in a forest plus O(t+1) chords. This argument also preserves fixed edge pins when they are encoded as extra marker bits.

Now fix the resulting support and keep its component colors, together with any prescribed endpoints. Outside them the original positive shore contribution can be realized by O(t+1) extra positively colored vertices by taking a basis of their binary weights. This step works because the cut scores are binary *linear* in shore variables, and the mod-four corrections fall on the even-edge side. The support has bounded cyclomatic number β=O(t+1). It has at most |U| leaves, at most 2β+|U| vertices of degree at least three, and O(β+|U|) nonempty components. Keep the branch vertices, all odd or prescribed vertices on the support, and one anchor in every remaining pure cycle. Suppress unmarked degree-two vertices. To allow a simple pattern with distinct endpoints and no parallel links, first stretch every original edge to a three-edge path (loops become triangles), assigning its even-edge weights to one edge and zero to the other two. Only original vertices need nonzero shore weights. Any even or prescribed-U support uses a whole chain or none. Add two anchor vertices to each core segment; they exist by stretching. The resulting simple topology has O(t+1) vertices and edges.

Enumerate the topology, positions of its anchors, component colors, off-support prescribed vertices and extra positive vertices. Exclude the latter vertices from paths. For each topology edge enumerate the required sums of its ordinary F₂ edge labels, any unsigned Z/4 even-edge labels, and the XOR of the binary weights of its strict internal vertices. Add all edge scores and all positive component/extra shore scores, counting anchors only once. Form mod-four totals *before* extracting the final bit. The interior XOR bit k on a path from x to y is imposed using unsigned Z/4 edge weights w_u+w_v on each uv, with residue w_x+w_y+2k at the endpoints: interiors are counted exactly twice. Thus every segment constraint is an exact residue in a fixed product of finite abelian groups.

For a fixed placement, clone each anchored host vertex into one port per incident segment. Test vertex-disjoint paths between the assigned port pairs, copying host edges and labels to the clones. All clones are path terminals, and no path can traverse another. A lifted path projects to a simple segment; shared underlying host edges cannot occur since internal vertices are distinct and a direct branch-to-branch link can belong to only one edge of the simple pattern. This realizes precisely the required subdivisions. Treat a component consisting solely of an isolated pinned vertex separately as an outside endpoint; a single-edge graph and the empty support are also included in the enumeration. Liu and Yoo's deterministic Theorem 1.3 permits fixed terminal pairs, unsigned group labels and a bounded forbidden set per coordinate. A prescribed Z/4 residue forbids the other three values. For fixed parameters the time after placement is O(N^8+M), with N vertices and M edges. It returns the paths. Enumeration therefore takes N^{O(t+1)} for this fixed-exception oracle. [Liu and Yoo, STOC 2025, Theorem 1.3](https://people.tamu.edu/~yyoo/stoc25.pdf#page=3)

## Bounded-rank nodes, runtime and witness recovery

For an r-row basic matrix H enumerate each functional y in F₂^r and set d=yH. Divide the columns into single real coordinates and interface blocks with at most three coordinates. Each block offers a list of possible primal masks at this fixed d, a partial column sum and score, using its actual child table, ordinary disjointness on genuine elements, or its specified outgoing trace. Accumulate reachable pairs (total selected-column sum, total score) by XOR over the blocks. Keep total column sum zero. The cost is linear in the number of blocks times 2^{2r+O(t)} up to polynomial factors. R10 and all its parallel extensions have rank five. Direct-sum contributions are combined by score XOR independently.

The decomposition and the stacked linear-algebra computations are polynomial in the original encoding. A node of degree m has at most (m+1)^{O(t+1)} exception choices, with parameter-dependent boundary and score choices per exception. Each resulting basic test is polynomial at fixed t, and complete tables are computed once in postorder, not by expanding the search over full subtrees anew. Thus the total time is input-length^{O(t+1)} times a factor depending on the linkage theorem and t, plus fixed polynomial preprocessing; in particular polynomial for every fixed t. Save each successful local witness, exception table choices and their labels. Read back real graph-edge sets, shore colors, and each compressed child response. Lift through the recorded minor operations or consult the stored true child backpointer for its attained state and score. Expand exceptions from their actual tables. The assembled real masks solve the original equation. Finally verify disjointness, kernel membership, row-space membership and the score directly by binary elimination. No counting conclusion follows: several lifts, baseline decisions or exception sets may yield the same real pair.

## Verification scope

The supplied tree_check.py compares a raw global primal projection, its independently projected dual and a canonical-state tree DP with direct global enumeration. It passes 150 seeded small instances and a graphic tree using all three sum sizes, whose 945 distinct disjoint primal–dual pairs are preserved. Component checks separately cover rooted minor templates, small graphic supports, crossing three-bonds, and labelled star substitutions. These are finite checks of the formulas. The decomposition routine and the Liu–Yoo disjoint-path routine are invoked by published constructive theorems; this package does not implement an end-to-end production solver or independently execute those subroutines on large inputs.

The [fixed-support affine extension](mandatory-ports.md) retains raw overlapping virtual-port states for mandatory children. Its graphic endpoint correction passed all 1,912 projected pin configurations in `mandatory_check.py`. The homogeneous normalization above applies to the ordinary unshifted tree tables.
