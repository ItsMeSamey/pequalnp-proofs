# Mandatory overlaps in the fixed-label regular-matroid reduction

Work throughout with one homogeneous cycle word c and one homogeneous cocycle word d. Their pair must be disjoint on every ordinary element except a specified set. At each specified element use its given acceptance table on the raw pair. For a 1-sum there are no interface bits. A proper 2-sum identifies one cycle bit and one cocycle bit on its virtual element in the two child witnesses and discards that element. A proper binary 3-sum on a triangle identifies the cycle trace p modulo 111 and the exact even cocycle trace q before discarding all three elements. For raw cycle gluing, adding the pure triangle to either cycle chooses the desired representative of p without changing any real element. There is no independent constraint on the overlap of the two words on the removed virtual elements. These statements follow by symmetric difference of matching-trace cycles and orthogonality for cocycles.

For a piece with a single boundary, its raw overlap parity on that boundary equals its total real overlap parity, since c·d=0. A subtree containing a designated element may therefore export an odd-overlap singleton or triangle, including p·q=1. A genuine table entry, including its real label, is then mandatory; the homogeneous K4 template is not sufficient. Keep the original virtual edges for each mandatory child and the parent slot. In a query enumerate their table entries and pin both raw words there, allowing overlap. On a triangle use any representative of p, toggling the pure triangle if necessary. These gauges are independent on disjoint slots. Labels on virtual slots are zero. Labels assigned to actual exceptional elements are charged only by the piece owning them. The linear-label replacement argument still limits the number of additional exceptional, originally homogeneous children to the dimension of the original target. Augmented coordinates used to pin a local torso's slots do not increase the outgoing message dimension. These rules concern genuine proper sums; singleton loop/coloop degeneracies require their own contraction/deletion conventions.

## Removing arbitrary pinned edges before a graphic solver

Take any undirected multigraph. In this paragraph c is an Eulerian edge set and d is the cut of a binary vertex coloring. Let F contain h edges with arbitrary specified pairs, including 11. Delete F, subtract its fixed label contribution from the target, and retain the old labels on ordinary edges. Guess colors on all endpoints of F, at most 2h vertices, rejecting guesses that disagree with the specified cut bits (a loop always has cut bit zero). Let T be the odd-degree vertices of the subgraph of deleted edges with c=1, counting loops twice. In an original witness the undeleted selected edges have exactly these odd vertices. They are monochromatic, so each color contains an even number of T vertices by the handshaking lemma. Reject if this necessary condition fails.

Pair the T vertices *arbitrarily* within each of the two colors. Add an independently labelled edge per pair, even when the endpoints were already adjacent, and pin it to (1,0). Add a fresh anchor vertex and one edge to each endpoint of F, pinned to (0,guessed color). These new edges have zero original label. Every new pin is compatible with disjointness; enforce all using two fresh syndrome bits per edge for reading its two raw bits. A witness to the original problem extends: choose the anchor color zero, preserve the coloring and all original unpinned edge selections, add all pairing edges, and leave anchor edges unselected. Degrees are then even, and the graph cut on the pairing edges vanishes. Conversely orient the global two-coloring relative to the anchor, remove the new edges and restore the pinned ones. Vertex parities change twice on T and nowhere else, and all restored cut bits are as prescribed. The two constructions preserve the complete pair of words on all undeleted edges, hence every choice of linear labels. At most 3h edges and 6h fresh bits are required per branch. A cographic solver interchanges the two raw coordinates and their linear labels first. This reduction uses only the homogeneous graph oracle even for 11 pins; it does not assume a separate linkage theorem for translated Eulerian spaces.

## Offset scope

For an affine cycle/cocycle problem with raw words c+alpha,d+beta, one may first change the coset representatives by any homogeneous cycle and cocycle. On the union F of their residual supports use the table ((c_e+alpha_e)(d_e+beta_e)=0) and retain disjointness elsewhere. A label on translated words acquires precisely its value on (alpha,beta) as a constant offset. To find representatives of combined support size at most k, enumerate candidate F and solve the two independent linear-space membership problems outside F. Thus fixed k and fixed label dimension give the extension on the underlying regular instance. More generally a supplied decomposition can bound the number of mandatory children plus locally exceptional real elements per node. This local bound, including the outward port, determines the graph-gadget budget; it need not bound the total number of exceptional elements along a long chain. Dense offsets on an arbitrary decomposition are not addressed.

For example, consider a triangle graph with all three edges cycle-selected and a cut consisting of two of them. Allow 11 on the first overlap as a genuine exception and expose the other as a virtual port: the boundary raw 11 is unavoidable for this witness. An even-total-overlap condition does not remove either of the two local overlaps.
