# Polynomial marker search on bipartite circle graphs

Given a chord diagram whose interlacement graph is bipartite, the alternating guard model with any t parity equations admits a deterministic O(2^t poly(n,t)) decision and witness algorithm. Columns of its t-row marker matrix may be arbitrary. Two chords are adjacent when their endpoints alternate around the circle. A circle representation can also be found with a circle graph recognition algorithm. [Gabor, Supowit and Hsu](https://doi.org/10.1145/65950.65951)

Read the chord endpoints clockwise to obtain a cyclic double-occurrence word, with one symbol per chord. Make its symbols vertices of a directed tour graph; each consecutive pair of positions gives an individual directed arc. Every vertex has two incoming and two outgoing arc incidences, even if an arc is a loop. Assign a binary bit to each arc. A circulation selects as many incoming as outgoing arcs at every vertex. At a vertex's two visits, this says precisely that either both transitions keep their bit or both toggle their bit with opposite entering values. Write x_v for the common toggle bit. The toggle events encountered between the two visits of v sum modulo two to the parity of its selected crossing neighbors, since chords internal to that interval contribute twice. When v is selected, the condition that the entering values at its two visits be opposite is precisely (Ax)_v=0: their difference is the toggle on leaving the first visit plus the sum on the open interval. Thus every circulation gives an even support. Conversely, any even support tells the tour at which visits to toggle; choose one initial arc bit arbitrarily and propagate around the tour. There are exactly two complementary lifts when the word is nonempty.

For each v choose its first visit and add its t-bit marker column L_v to the labels of that visit's incoming and outgoing arcs using XOR. The total label of a circulation is Lx. Summing all arc labels gives zero, so selected and omitted arcs have the same label.

Replace each tour vertex by incoming and outgoing incidence ports, two of each, with all four edges between the two types. Add a wire per tour arc connecting its outgoing incidence to its incoming incidence. In a perfect matching the arcs whose wires are omitted have the same number of incoming and outgoing incidences at each vertex, since the unused ports must be paired by local edges. Conversely every circulation extends to a matching by locally matching its used incoming and outgoing incidences. If both incidences of each type are used there are two local completions. Positive multiplicity suffices for existence. Wire arcs carry the marker; local edges carry zero.

Draw the word as a directed cycle on a sphere. Chords belonging to one side of the bipartition can be drawn without crossings inside the circle, and chords in the other side without crossings outside it. Contract the chord edges. At both endpoints of any individual chord, the two directed circle half-edges have the same cyclic order relative to that chord half-edge, because the chord lies on the same side of the oriented circle at its two endpoints. Contraction concatenates those two half-edge lists, giving the alternating cyclic order in,out,in,out at the resulting tour vertex. The local K₂,₂ can therefore be embedded as a four-cycle preserving the incidence order. Wires may be parallel to local edges when a tour arc is a loop; keep separate edges, or replace each wire by a path of length three. Such a path matches its two ports outward together when used, or pairs its two internal vertices when unused. Only one end-edge of the path needs the original wire weight.

A planar Pfaffian signing works simultaneously for all edge weights. For each character of the marker group give each wire its character sign and each local edge weight one. Signed matching sums are obtained by a Pfaffian or, for this bipartite graph, by a signed determinant with fixed sign convention. Calibrate the common sign by evaluating all-positive weights, since the all-wire perfect matching exists. Inverting the Walsh transform over the integers gives the nonnegative number of perfect matchings of each label. Testing positivity decides feasibility. For witness recovery, fix edges successively, retaining a choice whose target coefficient is positive; equivalently delete candidate edges whenever feasible and finish with an inclusion-minimal feasible edge set. The remaining perfect matching gives the support by the toggles. This is polynomial per character. It does not claim matching counts equal model counts. [Kasteleyn's planar matching method](https://doi.org/10.1016/0031-8914(61)90063-5)

For a single class of occurrences covering no input vertices handle the empty graph directly. The derivation of the tour circulation itself holds for any chord diagram, regardless of its interlacement graph's bipartiteness; planarity of the local perfect matching graph is the extra step established here.
