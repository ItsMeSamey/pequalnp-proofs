# Fixed-marker decision for graphic fundamental matrices

Let G be a finite undirected multigraph, including possible loops. Give each edge e two vector labels a_e,b_e in F₂^t and prescribe h in F₂^t. Seek an even edge set C and a cut D disjoint from C with the sum of the a-labels on C and b-labels on D equal to h. For every fixed t this problem has a deterministic polynomial-time decision and witness algorithm. The running-time bound below is XP as t varies. It does not count witnesses.

For a spanning forest with fundamental-cycle matrix B (rows are forest edges, columns are other edges), all cycles have form (Bq,q) and all cuts have form (p,Bᵀp). Their edgewise disjointness is exactly p_i(Bq)_i=0 at every forest edge and q_j(Bᵀp)_j=0 at every other edge. Consequently this solves the guarded bipartite matrix with blocks 0,B and Bᵀ,0. A linear marker on the actual state (p,q) is obtained by placing its forest-edge coefficients on b and its other-edge coefficients on a. Conversely a successful cycle/cut yields the unique vector (p,q); different choices of cut shore can represent the same event. If only B is supplied, graph realization seeks a tree whose edge-path incidence vectors are the columns of B. Add a chord between the endpoints of each path and a loop for each zero column. Recognition and construction are polynomial. [Bixby and Wagner](https://doi.org/10.1287/moor.13.1.99)

## Small witness structure

Write D=δ(S). Define w_v to be the sum of b_e over all incident edge ends at v; a loop contributes twice. Then the cut label is the sum of w_v over S. Disjointness says every edge of C has its endpoints on the same side.

Fix any successful pair temporarily. Work in the graph of monochromatic edges for this S and take a spanning forest. The label of C belongs to the span of the labels of the fundamental cycles. It is a sum of at most t such labels, by taking a basis of the image over F₂. Let C′ be the symmetric difference of the corresponding fundamental cycles. It has the same label and is still monochromatic. It is contained in the chosen forest plus at most t non-forest edges, so the cyclomatic number β of its support is at most t. This uses a spanning forest adapted to this *existence proof*, which need not be found by the algorithm.

Retain the old color on the vertices incident with C′. The contribution from the positive vertices outside that support is the sum of at most t of their weights by the same basis argument. Keep only those extra positive vertices and color all remaining vertices negative. This changes the cut but preserves its label and disjointness.

To handle loops and parallel edges uniformly, the working graph can first replace each original edge by a chain of length three with two fresh internal vertices (a loop becomes a triangle). Put its a-label on one of these edges and zero on the other two, and retain w only on old vertices. Work henceforth with w as vertex labels. Even edge sets use an entire chain or none. Consistent colors on used chains are exactly the monochromatic requirement on their projection. Zero-weight added vertices outside the support never need to be selected as extra positives. The expansion preserves every attainable target in both directions.

In the support of C′, all nonisolated vertices have even degree at least two. For a connected component of rank b, the sum of degree minus two is 2(b−1). Hence at most b−1 vertices have degree at least four. Suppress its degree-two vertices; in a pure cycle keep a single designated anchor, obtaining a loop. The resulting core has at most β vertices and at most 2β edges over all components. Choose all branch vertices and pure-cycle anchors on the original graph, before stretching edges. On the stretched graph each core segment then has length at least three. Designate two distinct interior vertices on each segment, replacing it by three pattern edges. This yields a simple pattern H, with distinct endpoint pairs and no loops, having at most 5t vertices and 6t edges. Its subdivisions cover every necessary witness. All core vertices have even degree, and the two new vertices per segment have degree two.

## Enumerating subdivisions with prescribed labels

Enumerate these finitely many even patterns, the color of each component, an injective placement of their branch and anchor vertices (including the designated interior vertices), and at most t distinct extra positive vertices outside that placement. Delete those extras from the linkage instance so the subdivision avoids them. The empty pattern is handled by the extra-vertex test alone. Let the component colors determine which anchored vertices are positive.

For each pattern edge enumerate a vector g giving its sum of a-labels and a vector k giving the XOR of the w-labels of its strict interior vertices. Check that the sum of all g, the w on positive anchored vertices and extras, and all k on edges of positive components gives h. There are only a parameter-dependent number of label guesses. Soundness holds because subdivision paths have disjoint interiors and the complete subdivision is even. Its positive vertices together with the extras define the required cut shore. Completeness follows from the small witness structure.

To test a fixed rooted placement, replace each placed vertex by one port clone for each incident pattern edge, copying host edges to the corresponding clones. Require a vertex-disjoint path between the two assigned clones for each pattern edge. Other clones cannot be internal because all are terminals. Projecting the paths preserves disjoint interiors. A projected host edge incident with an internal vertex cannot be reused, by vertex-disjointness. An edge directly joining two branch images could be reused only by two links with the same end pair, excluded by simplicity of H. Thus the paths project to a subdivision.

Label each host edge uv by its a-label and, for each bit of w, by the sum of its two endpoint bits as an element of Z/4; copy labels to its clone edges. For a path with endpoints x,y the latter coordinate equals w_x+w_y+2 times the sum of its strict interior bits modulo four. Therefore k is imposed by an exact residue, and g is imposed in F₂. In the Liu–Yoo theorem an exact residue is expressed by forbidding all other group elements in that coordinate (one forbidden element in F₂ or three in Z/4). The number of path pairs, coordinates and forbidden residues per coordinate are fixed for fixed t. Its deterministic algorithm constructs the linkage or certifies failure in O_t(N^8+M) time on a graph of N vertices and M edges. Cloning changes sizes by at most parameter-dependent factors. [Liu and Yoo, STOC 2025, Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 1.4](https://people.tamu.edu/~yyoo/stoc25.pdf#page=3)

There are O_t(N^{6t}) possible placements including extras. In the stretched host N,M=O(|V(G)|+|E(G)|), so the overall bound is O_t((|V(G)|+|E(G)|)^{6t+8}). Store the linkage at the successful test and project the subdivision and its coloring to recover the original cycle and cut. For t=0 the empty witness suffices.

## Finite checks

Run `python check.py`. On 300 seeded multigraphs including parallel edges, loops and disconnected cases it compares all fundamental-matrix guard labels, all cycle/cut labels, and all labels having a compressed support and few extra positives. The Z/4 path encoding is independently checked on all 508 binary-labelled paths of two through eight vertices. The large fixed-parameter linkage subroutine is supplied by the cited theorem and was not implemented in this checker.
