# Triangle normalization and star replacement

All scores use only real elements. Sum the local primal-dual inner products on one side of a tree interface. Matched virtual contributions occur twice, and real contributions vanish. Thus the exposed masks s,d have s·d=0. A triangle interface supports primal 111 on both incident pieces. Add it to both lifts when needed to leave the unique representative s of weight at most one. The even dual mask d is unchanged; the two are now coordinate-disjoint. These operations commute on disjoint interfaces and preserve real scores. For a triad interchange primal and dual, and for a singleton orthogonality suffices.

The ten canonical triangle states need not have a linear feasible baseline. In K4 with boundary triangle abc and center o, mark only the cut bit of oa. Each singleton s fixes the two cycle spokes to its endpoints. For d=0, their center lies on the common triangle side and the score is zero. For d the complementary pair, the center is on the side of the endpoints of s, and the sole cut spoke goes to the opposite vertex. The three mixed scores are thus 1,0,0. The pure singleton states force all primal coefficients zero, while the three nonzero even d vectors sum to zero. This rules out a linear baseline, even without quotient-invariance.

Suppose the child has a supplied triangle-rooted K4 minor. Lift minor cycles linearly, adding primal support only on contracted elements and zero on deleted elements. Lift minor cuts dually, adding dual support only on deleted elements and zero on contracted elements. The two sections preserve disjointness. Choose the primal section to send the T-only 111 cycle to the same cycle in the child; it extends that prescription by linearity. The real primal score vanishes on the kernel of restricting a K4 cycle to its spokes, hence becomes a linear weight on the even spoke plane. Extend to three individual spoke weights. K4 cuts are determined by their three spoke bits, so the real dual score likewise becomes linear spoke weights. All sections and weights are computable by elimination.

Replace each nonexceptional literal triangle in the graphic parent by three labelled spokes to a fresh center. Every compatible canonical s,d has a disjoint K4 lift: for a nonempty s use the two spokes of its endpoints and choose the center on their common side; for zero s use no cycle spokes and choose any side. Fix one such baseline per state. The resulting cycles and cuts glue to a cycle and cut of the augmented parent. Different edge-disjoint triangles may share vertices, but their centers are chosen independently.

For an actual successful witness with fixed boundary masks, subtract each child's baseline from its actual real contribution. The sum of all discrepancies can be expressed using at most t of those vectors over F₂. Keep just those children exceptional. Enumerate up to t exceptional children and their attainable fixed masks/scores, using their full precomputed tables. Retain their parent virtual triangle edges with prescribed bits and zero real weights; use the graphic exceptional-edge solver to handle them, together with any prescribed external boundary. Use physical stars for the others. Any solution of the augmented graph has zero or two primal spokes per center. This recovers the canonical s, and the pairwise XORs of the spoke cut bits recover d. Replacing each center by its original triangle preserves the parent cycle and cut. Each nonexceptional child lifts with exactly the charged weight, and each exceptional table supplies its chosen response. This proves arbitrary-degree compression under the stated minor/coverage hypotheses. It does not establish an unconditional algorithm for all regular matroids.
