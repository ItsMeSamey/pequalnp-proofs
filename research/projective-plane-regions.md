# First-fork lower bounds for projective incidence

Let d≥2 be the order of a finite projective plane, N=d²+d+1 its number of points and of lines, and g the gate count of a least binary positive AND/OR DAG for the full point-line incidence predicate. Each vertex has d+1 neighbors. Regularity supplies a perfect matching by Hall, and two distinct lines meet once, with the dual property for points. The point-centered implementation costs (d+2)N−1. Below are unconditional lower bounds; no assumptions on dirty gates, depth, or sharing.

Use the ordered first-fork emitter lemma in small-excess-proof.md. Every raw is essential, all gates are live, the output is computed, and the pin surplus is E=g−2N+1. If f is the number of forks and e the number of emitter raws, then N≤e≤f≤E. Emitters cover every incidence; each owns a distinct fork region with one fresh raw. The remaining independent 2N−e nonemitters occupy at most f−e+1 regions (including the terminal). A region is a tree on fresh raws and earlier fork-pin occurrences. All influence of a fresh raw on the suffix goes through the region root. Counting consumer pins allows repeated operands.

For a nonempty set A of a points write r_l=|A∩l|. Summing incidences and ordered pairs gives Σr_l=(d+1)a and Σr_l²=a²+da. Cauchy therefore gives |Γ(A)|≥(d+1)²a/(a+d). If some line misses A then 1≤a≤d². The inequality (a−1)(d²−a)≥0 implies |Γ(A)|≥a+d. Hence every independent set occupying both shores has size at most N−d. More generally, if an independent set has shore counts a,b then (d+1)²ab≤d(N−a)(N−b), by the same bound and b≤N−|Γ(A)| (trivial if either is zero). Thus its smaller shore has size at most U=floor(N sqrt(d)/(d+1+sqrt(d))).

Choose one shore of nonemitters, called lines by duality. A region with k≥2 fresh such lines costs at least (d+1)k gates that can be counted across regions. To prove this, fix line raws outside the k to zero; fix all nonemitter points to zero. All neighbors of the k lines remain available since nonemitters are independent. For every enabled point set meeting some fresh line, the answer as a function of the k bits is a nonconstant disjunction. For this fixed exterior assignment the suffix is a monotone unary function of the region root, hence identity. With all available points high, the root is OR of all k. Every join of two fresh-bearing branches must be OR: each branch carries pivotal fresh inputs; on the all-zero fresh assignment it must be zero (otherwise that branch is constant on this face). A singleton from either branch must pass the join. The two branches have disjoint fresh occurrences.

For each fresh line l take as background all available points not on l. Another fresh line has at least one available neighbor there, so the root section is nonconstant. When l alone is high on that background the root is false. On the unique upward l path some AND with a fresh-free operand must block it; two fresh-bearing joins are OR. For each singleton point on l, toggling l is pivotal, forcing this same guard operand high on that singleton. These tests on the operand are valid with *all* lines zero since the fresh-free operand has no dependency on the fresh lines: earlier fork values cannot depend on a raw whose first fork is this region. Tracing backwards from the guard, at an AND use a fixed background-false child; at an OR route each singleton to an accepting child. All traced wires reject the background. Paths end at the distinct d+1 named raws; their connected union has at least d distinct two-child splits by edge counting, even if paths merge. These splits are ORs accepting two distinct singleton points. The singleton tests and available points are common to all regions, and a gate rejecting the l background cannot accept any available singleton off l. Thus two lines cannot charge the same OR. Guard ANDs in different regions are distinct; within a region a common guard would accept a singleton off l belonging to the other line. This proves the charge; no regional merge gates are added.

If E≤N+d−1 the nonemitters number at least N−d+1, hence all are on one shore. Some region has at least two of them since their number exceeds f−e+1. At most f−e singleton regions remain. At least 2N−f≥N−d+1 lines are charged. For d≥3, (d+1)(N−d+1)=(d+1)(d²+2)>3N+d−2≥g, a contradiction. Thus g≥3N+d−1.

Without any restriction on E, choose the larger nonemitter shore. It contains at least 2N−e−U lines. If there is a region with at least two such lines, at most f−e remaining singleton groups give at least 2N−f−U charged lines. If all groups have at most one, 2N−f−U≤1 and g≥d+1 already by raw essentiality. Thus g≥(d+1)(2N−E−U), giving g≥ceil((d+1)(4N−1−U)/(d+2)). The separate Fano argument in /dev/shm/8573-fano22-09.md gives 22 at d=2 by additionally counting terminal joins. These estimates leave the general issued benchmark open.
