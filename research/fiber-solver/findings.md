# Permutation quotients of binary constraints

For an explicit finite binary CSP, `fibers.solve` enforces arc consistency and handles diagonal self-constraints. On each remaining table, collect left values that have identical right neighborhoods. The resulting distinct neighborhoods cover the right domain. Their total size equals the right-domain size exactly when they are pairwise disjoint. In that case the relation is a union of complete rectangles, and its left and right block partitions correspond bijectively. A single rectangle is universal on the surviving domains and adds no restriction.

If every nonuniversal incident edge induces the same partition at each vertex, contract to these blocks. Fixing one root block in a connected component forces a unique block at every other vertex along any spanning tree. Check every edge for agreement and try each root block until a trial succeeds. Every satisfying assignment must correspond to one trial, and every consistent trial lifts by independently choosing one surviving representative per block. Isolated vertices take any surviving value. An unsupported result means that the recognizer declined the instance. All stages run in polynomial time in the explicit domain and table size. With hash tables, the propagation stage uses O(k(m+n)) expected dictionary operations per component with k root blocks, m edges and n vertices.

## CNF incidence reconstruction

Assume each input clause forbids one full assignment to the union of at most two disjoint original blocks. Group bits by equality of their sets of incident distinct clause supports. All bits of one original block have the same signature. Two distinct nonempty-signature original blocks can coincide in signature only when their union is the only observed support incident to either: any support containing both exhausts its two allowed endpoints. Their union can therefore be decoded as a single unary table. Empty-signature variables are free. This reasoning recovers an equivalent decomposition, while individual endpoints in a single-scope component can be unidentifiable. Clause shortening, auxiliary encodings, and missing bit occurrences within a forbidden tuple invalidate the premise. Explicit expansion costs up to exponential time in support width. For bounded support width it is polynomial.

## Incompatible partitions

For a 3-CNF with three distinct variables per clause, give each Boolean variable a two-element domain. Give each clause a local domain of seven triples of underlying Boolean values satisfying the signed clause. Join each clause vertex to its three variable vertices by equality with the corresponding coordinate. Each edge is a union of two disjoint complete rectangles and induces a bijection on its two blocks. Local clause partitions differ by coordinate. A CSP witness exists exactly when the original formula is satisfiable. This proves NP-completeness for the relaxation that retains per-edge rectangularity but drops the common-partition condition, even with maximum domain size seven.

The same reduction stays NP-hard with at most two distinct incident partitions per vertex, nested under refinement. Replace each incidence edge (u,v) by three edges u--p--q--v. Give ports p and q the respective endpoint domains, put discrete equality on the two outer edges and copy the original table to the middle edge. Every old vertex now sees only its discrete partition. Each port sees the discrete partition and one coarser partition. The bipartite graph joining blocks of the two partitions when they intersect is a disjoint union of stars, hence a forest. Equality forces the ports to copy the old values, preserving witnesses in both directions. The reduction bounds domains by seven and uses polynomially many ports. This distinguishes local acyclicity of partition overlaps from acyclicity of the global binary constraint graph.
