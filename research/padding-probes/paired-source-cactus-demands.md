# Cactus demand counts on the coupled physical source

Let the simple cubic base B have b vertices. Its triangle replacement G has n=3b port vertices and m=3n/2 edges. Use the strict source clauses and equality/OR DFA of stage6_coupled_source.py. Both clauses on edge uv hold exactly when x_u x_v=0, independently of pad z. On a positive trace enable is one, the equality triples uniquely fill the occurrence and link names from the source bits, and each tautological padding triple (d,-d,f) leaves its names unrestricted. Across the 2c padding triples for c=2m strict clauses there are 2c names d and c names f, since consecutive pairs reuse f. Thus every independent set lifts to exactly 2^(1+3c) physical positive keys if pad and dummies are free. Read source and dummy values from the physical key using signed aliases. Conversely repeat the source values on their linked names, insert the chosen dummy and pad values, set enable, and use the original canonical DIMACS encoder. The carrier formula is unsatisfiable on every key. The cactus computes the fixed predictor's positive count. Canonical pins remove the corresponding free choices.

## Direct cactus preprocessing

Apply the ordered-ring-planar-phase-boundary construction to the literal source. Each variable has an even cycle with a private four-edge block for each of its occurrences. Each clause contributes a positive demand on three designated edges. All designated edges are pairwise vertex-disjoint and none is reused across clauses. Every one of the d=2m=3n demands survives duplicate or same-root-star filtering. The n+1 variables have 6m literal occurrences in total and all occur, giving 24m root vertices and 24m+n root edges before separate dummy factors. Resolving complementary pad literals recovers one negative binary clause per edge and leaves pad z free. Applying the same map to this reduced source gives d=m.

## A smaller planar realization

In each triangle a positive assignment selects none or exactly one of its three ports. Choose a bijection from these four states to the four words on two cycle phases. Use an even cycle for each phase, attaching cycles in a path with bridges as in the ordered-ring construction. Each cycle has three private four-edge occurrence blocks, one for each external edge incident to its triangle. The coupled root has 2b cycles, 24b vertices and 26b-1 edges. A bridge cuts off an even number of vertices and cannot participate in a perfect matching. Each matching is therefore precisely a choice of all 2b phases. Append one four-cycle per free pad or dummy for a literal matching-to-physical-key bijection. Each creates no demand.

For external edge e=uv, let the local phase codes of its ports be a and b'. In each of their four phase cycles mark the private edge selected when the phase bit differs from its target in (a,b'). Demand a hit on these four edges. Failure selects exactly the two forbidden ports. The resulting perfect matchings are in bijection with all independent sets of G, or positive physical words with the free cycles included. This uses exactly d=|E(B)|=3b/2=n/2 supplemental demands. The designated edges of different demands are disjoint. Planarity concerns the root and does not require a planar base.

This demand count is optimal for independent per-triangle phase blocks and positive edge demands, even with unbounded demand size or another local bijection. For each external edge e, select only its two endpoint ports, with all other triangles in the default empty state. This test word must fail a demand. Failure of a positive demand fixes independent phase literals and defines a subcube or the empty set; bridges impose no restriction. If one demand excluded test words for two distinct edges, choose whole block values from either test independently. Select the default outside the intersection of the edges. A simple graph has at most one common endpoint; choose either test value there. The hybrid still fails the demand yet selects at most one triangle, which is legal. Hold pad and dummy phases fixed throughout. Distinct test words therefore need different demands. This lower bound does not extend to arbitrary matching gadgets.

## Exact count

A subset of demands can fail jointly exactly when the corresponding edges form a matching M in B. Incident edges would require different port choices at their shared triangle. A matching fixes 2|M| triangles, leaving 4^(b-2|M|) choices. Thus the coupled positive count F(B) is the matching polynomial of B evaluated at 4:

    F(B) = sum over matchings M of (-1)^|M| 4^(b-2|M|).

Multiply by the free phases. The supplied K4 base gives 256-6·16+3=163 and full count 163·2^109. The direct, pad-resolved and triangle-recoded demand counts are respectively 36,18,6. Generic inclusion-exclusion on the last root costs up to 2^(n/2) planar Pfaffian evaluations. Here its matching counts have the explicit form above. Group matchings by whether they cover vertex v to obtain F(J)=4F(J-v)-sum over u adjacent to v of F(J-{u,v}), with F(empty)=1. On maximum-degree-three induced subgraphs this takes O(((1+sqrt(13))/2)^b poly(b)) bit operations and polynomial space. Intermediate magnitudes have O(b) bits: the unsigned sum satisfies the plus-sign recurrence with bounded degree. Input recovery, cactus construction and physical witness printing retain their separate polynomial costs. The empty independent set already gives a positive witness.
