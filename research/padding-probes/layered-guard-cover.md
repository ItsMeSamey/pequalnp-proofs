# A harmonic gap for the signed-pair compiler

Let k=lcm(1,…,D). Partition k left vertices into groups of size j separately for every j=1,…,D, and give each group a new right vertex adjacent to its members. Each graph edge ab contributes the strict positive clause (u,a,b). Assign positive IDs in order hub u, all right vertices, all left vertices.

At the start of remaining layer j in descending order every left degree is j and the maximum right degree is j. After any size-j group is covered, the other groups in that layer still have degree j, and no left degree increases. Lexicographic ties of maximum frequency therefore select hub/right pairs, even at j=1. The compiler selects q=k H_D pairs. A left spoke per left vertex covers all clauses. The k layer-1 clauses have disjoint sets of literal pairs, certifying optimum k. The old pair_frequency_visits is 3[Dk²−k²H_D/2+Dk/2]. Recomputing all frequencies for prescribed left choices would cost 3Dk(k+1)/2.

On the bare positive clauses both spoke covers have b=0. Put the hub inverter alone and each selected leaf inverter and its pair NAND in one interval. The leaf raw bit is private, absent from exterior clauses, and only read inside its own interval. Its projection has at most two ports. Pure-literal elimination decides this input directly.

To expose each gate, adjoin a fresh twin v′ to every original v, constrained by two native binary clauses for v=v′. Each raw coordinate is now observed. An interval with two spoke gates needs three different incoming origins even if it includes inverters. For each single spoke its observable projection includes the pair output and the raw or complemented input from each origin. Internal complemented entries are ports, and raw entries are observed. These three bits realize the signed OR graph, which fails majority closure. Singleton gate partitions attain the reverse bound. Hence b=q or b=k for these respective covers.

Old signed greedy continues selecting the same q layered spokes and at most four guard spokes: choices in disjoint components do not alter relative frequencies or lexicographic priorities within a component. Each component's gate inputs were created in its own steps; any interleaving still leaves q separate bad intervals. Thus q≤b≤q+4.

After finding a cover, selecting one endpoint per cover pair and branching on its value reduces all original clauses to width two, in at most 2^(k+1) subproblems.
