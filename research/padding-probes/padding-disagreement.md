# Disagreement on a prescribed SAT padding family

Use an explicit text encoding of ordered CNF clauses. Every literal has a one-character sign and a fixed-width binary variable identifier within the instance. Strict triples use three distinct variables. For an input strict 3-CNF Φ with m≥1 declared variables, keep its literal and clause block byte-for-byte in all queries. Append all eight sign patterns of one triple on three fresh variables. Every assignment violates one of these clauses. Append one marker triple per bit z_j, using three further fresh variables for each and fixing the signs of the last two. The first literal's sign encodes z_j. An optional extra marker in precisely the same format encodes an enable bit e. Use deterministic identifier widths, order and delimiters, with a header specifying the immutable block boundary. Thus for fixed Φ the physical encodings P(Φ,e,z) all have exactly the same length L, represent UNSAT, and differ only at prescribed sign positions. Their length is polynomial in the source encoding, using explicit declarations of the m input variables. An immutable source already using variable-width identifiers may retain those identifiers and use common-width names for the fresh variables. SAT on strict triples remains NP-complete: remove repeated literals and tautological clauses, expand a binary clause D into D∨u and D∨¬u, and expand a unit clause analogously with two fresh variables and four sign choices. A source with an empty clause can be replaced by the fixed eight-clause contradiction.

## Worst-case decision and search

Fix a single polynomial-time predictor A. On a well-formed member of the construction it outputs one exactly when e=1 and z satisfies the parsed immutable Φ. On malformed encodings it returns zero. The rules are checked and evaluated by scanning the input and looking up the assigned bits, in polynomial time. The designated point e=0,z=0 has prediction zero regardless of Φ. The designated family contains a disagreement if and only if Φ is satisfiable. Every disagreement pair contains an enabled satisfying assignment on its accepting side. Conversely every satisfying assignment supplies a disagreement with the designated point. Existence belongs to NP by guessing two sign vectors and evaluating A. These witness maps give FNP-completeness of finding a pair under polynomial-time search reductions from SAT witness search (the associated relation is polynomially verifiable and polynomially balanced). Source strictification also preserves search via projection and polynomial-time lifting. This is a fixed predictor and a prescribed fiber for each input Φ. A single finite fiber frozen independently of the input cannot encode asymptotic hardness. All formulas on a given fiber have the same SAT truth bit. Opposite predictions witness an error somewhere in the pair; for this UNSAT carrier its accepting member is explicitly erroneous. Constant prediction on a fiber provides no guarantee of correctness there or elsewhere.

## Exact disagreement detection under a Walsh sparsity bound

For a fixed physical family with d free sign bits (including the enable coordinate if used), write f(v)=(-1)^{A(P(v))} on the binary d-cube. Its normalized Walsh expansion is f(v)=Σ_u c_u (-1)^{u·v}. Suppose at most s coefficients are nonzero, where s≥1 is a supplied bound. For d≥1 choose a binary extension field of order q≥2, a power of two strictly larger than s(d−1); the least such q is O(1+s(d−1)). An irreducible defining polynomial can be found deterministically by enumerating monic degree log₂q polynomials and testing irreducibility, polynomial in q. For α,β in the field form the d-bit key K(α,β)_j=Tr(β α^j), j=0,…,d−1, with α^0=1. Evaluate the actual predictor on every physical formula P(K(α,β)). This uses at most q² predictor calls, plus encoding and key generation per call. If two outputs differ return their physical formulas, otherwise report constant under the promise. For d=0 there is only one point.

Indeed write h_u(X)=Σ_j u_j X^j over the extension field. For a nonconstant f take any nonzero supported u. Against each other supported v the equality h_u(α)=h_v(α) excludes at most d−1 field points. Also exclude roots of h_u itself, at most d−1 points, to ensure a nonzero projected frequency. The total excluded is at most s(d−1). At a remaining α the restriction β↦f(K(α,β)) has the nonzero frequency h_u(α) with coefficient exactly c_u: the field trace pairing is nondegenerate and β↦(-1)^{Tr(βh)} gives distinct Walsh characters for distinct field elements h. This restriction is nonconstant, so two sampled predictions disagree. Conversely an observed disagreement is valid without any sparsity assumption. Under the promise the β=0 row gives a common baseline and each unequal sample can be returned with that baseline. The scan detects constancy; it need not reconstruct the full spectrum. Counting field construction and deterministic generation, bit time is q² times the cost of one length-L predictor evaluation plus polynomial overhead in q,d,L. If the promise is unavailable, exhaustive enumeration of the 2^d physical inputs is an unconditional fallback.

The construction gives no general sparse-spectrum guarantee. For a uniquely satisfiable Φ there is exactly one accepting vector on the enabled fiber. At dimension d=m+1≥2, f is the constant +1 except at one point v*. Its zero-frequency coefficient is 1−2^{1−d}, and each nonzero-frequency coefficient is −2^{1−d}(-1)^{u·v*}; all 2^d are nonzero. The algorithm is polynomial for polynomially bounded s and physical evaluation cost, without implying an efficient general SAT-error search.
