# Discovering two-input banks on source clauses

Normalize original pins, tautologies and repeated literals; take one unsigned variable triple per distinct three-variable scope. Choose unordered pairs hitting all the triples. Signs and duplicates are removed only from this optimization. Every original clause is independently encoded and checked with the true signs. Source unary and binary predicates stay native.

Three-way branching on an uncovered triple is the ordinary rank-three Hitting Set algorithm. With remaining budget q, any pair in more than q distinct scopes is forced: another pair can cover at most one such scope because the common triple would be the union of the two pairs. After applying forced choices and decreasing q, if every pair occurs at most q times and more than q² triples remain, this budget fails. Otherwise the kernel has at most q² triples and 3q² candidate pairs. Greedy supplies a feasible upper bound; candidate-disjoint scope packing supplies a lower bound. A cutoff does not certify optimality unless the bounds already coincide, and failing a cover budget proves nothing about source satisfiability.

Compile each selected pair as one contiguous bank with two raw inputs, private sign-inverting gates, and at most four signed OR outputs. Every assigned original ternary becomes a binary predicate on its correct signed OR output and its remaining literal. Thus every original assignment uniquely evaluates the network, and each accepted network witness recovers an original model. At most one bad block per used bank is an explicit certificate; the separate interval optimizer might find fewer. A budget-truncated discovery certifies this bound only using its incumbent p, not the unknown minimum k*. If discovery completes, b≤k*.

Complete discovery costs O(3^k* poly(L)) and the chosen network solves in O(2^p poly(L)). The exponential solve is applied only once. Costs include normalization, all greedy scans, forcing iterations, physical gate and binary predicate storage, optional exact interval optimization, verification of the recovered source assignment and original physical byte replay. Node caps are not caps on polynomial preprocessing.

The adaptive launcher first invokes the earlier bounded source stages with an empty cover-search budget. On UNKNOWN it computes the prior complete search ceiling H, budgets at most H discovery nodes, and dispatches a feasible bank only if its branch ceiling also fits H. Otherwise it can use the original complete covering fallback. Thus the default whole clock remains poly(L)(1.5+o(1))^v. If the full discovery tree for a small k* fits H, the effective extra cost is O(3^k* poly(L)); constant-factor comparisons refer to charged units, not measured wall time. A user can restrict both discovery and final search.

The lower-level algorithm is a special case of rank-three Hitting Set. Choosing one endpoint from every selected pair already gives an original strong backdoor to 2-CNF of size ≤p. The distinct contribution is an executable source search with a certified cost for the emitted two-input banks, not a new general SAT exponent. See Fernau (https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2004/073/download/), Williams, Gomes and Selman (https://www.cs.cornell.edu/selman/papers/pdf/03.ijcai.backdoors.pdf), and Manthey, Heule and Biere (https://cca.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/MantheyHeuleBiere-HVC12.pdf).
