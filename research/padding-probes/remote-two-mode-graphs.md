# Connected remote-pivot systems with arbitrary signed binary residual graphs

Orient each edge e=uv of a connected graph G. Choose signed literals U0(e),U1(e) on x_u and V(e) on x_v. Use private bits a_e,b_e, a single remote bit q, and NAE(a_e,b_e,V(e)) as two complementary ternaries. Add (q∨¬a_e),(¬q∨¬b_e),(¬a_e∨U1(e)),(¬b_e∨U0(e)) for each edge.

At q=0 only b_e may be true, and at q=1 only a_e may be true. The negative ternary always holds. The projection onto the x coordinates is exactly the conjunction of (Us(e)∨V(e)) at q=s. Given a model set every enabled private bit to the truth of its corresponding Us(e), and all disabled bits to zero. Conversely the positive ternary and its binary implications force the displayed edge condition. Simultaneous lifting works even with shared x coordinates.

No Horn renaming of the written source exists: a complementary strict ternary pair includes a clause with at least two positive literals in every renaming. In a bounded-degree graph with maximum occurrence degree Δ, each variable hits at most max(Δ,1) positive ternaries, and q hits none; any variable hit of all those scopes has size at least |E|/max(Δ,1). Nevertheless q resolves every row. The connected raw primal graph has G as a minor, with internally disjoint paths x_u,a_e,x_v for the original edges. Thus bounded-degree graphs of unbounded treewidth give growing raw width and growing syntactic backdoor on these same inputs. Solving by another decomposition may remain easy.

For a nonbijunctive, nonaffine SAT instance, use a simple graph and all positive endpoint signs. On an edge uv take x_u=1, x_v=0 and every other endpoint one. Across the two q settings the positive ternary can realize a_e alone and b_e alone in full models. A third model sets every endpoint one and both private bits of e zero. The majority violates that ternary. At fixed q the coordinate projection on x_u,x_v is exactly {01,10,11}: all other endpoints may be one. It cannot be a projection of an affine relation, since nonempty affine relations have power-of-two size. Other existential or global simplifications may indeed solve these inputs.

For NO instances take two parallel rows per edge encoding both signed binary clauses of inequality in both modes. A nonbipartite connected host is impossible. Count multiplicity in occurrence degree and use fresh private bits per row. Recognizing the projected parity equations also decides this particular NO family. These results concern representation and parameter bounds, not general hardness.
