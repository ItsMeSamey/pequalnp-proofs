# Computing the original contraction without the hard Cauchy evaluations

At the origin Appendix D gives W(0)=I. On a loopless graph with m binary edges its scalar is the usual sum over shared edge bits of the product of local tables. First contract attached trees directly if the tables are explicit or the required local multilinear contractions are efficient. This handles the star graphs underlying the programmed NP-hard intermediate evaluation. The cyclic core below can have unbounded treewidth.

**One changed leg per vertex.** The structural input is a supplied representation of each original local tensor
f_v(x)=b_v∏_e u_ve(x_e)+Σ_{e incident v} v_ve(x_e)∏_{j≠e}u_vj(x_j).
Here the similarly named v_ve denote changed two-entry leg vectors. Equality of such a decomposition with an explicit truth table can be checked entrywise. This includes genuinely non-affine Boolean tensors: choosing u=(1,0), changed vector (0,1), and b=0 gives exactly one selected incident bit. Absorbing a unary leaf into leg j preserves the form: if its pairings with u_j and v_j are α,β, the new b is αb+β and all remaining changed vectors are multiplied by α. Inherited witnesses for arbitrary implicit tensors require a separate correctness argument.

Expand by having every vertex choose no leg with coefficient b_v or select one of its incident legs. Along each edge e=uv put the two-by-two mode table [[A_e,C_e],[B_e,D_e]], formed by summing products of the leg vectors over the two values of the shared bit. To compute the sum Z(0), introduce a *different* interpolation polynomial Z(t): add t only to A_e on every edge. Every summand has degree at most m in t. At any point where all a_e=A_e+t are invertible, the normalized edge table equals the outer product (1,B_e/a_e) times (1,C_e/a_e), plus h_e in its (1,1) entry, where h_e=(a_eD_e−B_eC_e)/a_e². Edges choosing that extra term must form a matching. All remaining selected-leg possibilities sum locally. Thus
Z(t)=(∏a_e)Σ_D ∏_{e∈D}h_e ∏_{v uncovered}d_v, summing over matchings, with d_v=b_v+Σ incident endpoint (B_e or C_e)/a_e.

**Fixed precision, including zero pivots.** Work over the source base ring (Z/2^k)[V]/V^ℓ. Extend its coefficient ring by a monic lift of an irreducible binary polynomial to obtain residue-field size ≥m+3. Choose m+1 residue-distinct lifted t outside {0,1}. Then every a_e is a unit by its residue modulo the maximal ideal (2,V), and the node differences are also units. The original value follows by Lagrange interpolation at zero. This does not query any of the prescribed Cauchy-deformation points.

Evaluate the weighted matching polynomial by doubling the vertices. The original-original dimer edges have weights Xh_e, each vertex has a spoke of weight d_v to its own twin, and the twins form a clique of unit weights. A j-dimer matching in the original graph leaves 2j twins to pair in (2j−1)!! ways. Therefore the degree-j coefficient of the doubled weighted perfect matching sum divided by this odd integer gives the original j-dimer contribution; use (−1)!!=1. A weighted perfect matching sum is a hafnian. Hirai and Namba's Theorem 2.1 computes a univariate polynomial hafnian modulo 2^k in poly(size,degree)^k time. To cover the extension variable, V, the dimer marker X and an optional filtration marker below, use bounded-degree polynomial representatives, then Kronecker substitution with radices greater than the *full product* degrees. Decode before reducing the extension and truncation relations. There are only a fixed number of variables and all upper degrees are polynomial in the input parameters, so the cost is polynomial at fixed k. Parallel edges can be combined by adding their appropriate weights. [Hirai and Namba](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.08073)

**Large declared precision with a computable small remaining precision.** For target precision p define ν(s) as the minimum 2-adic valuation of the coordinates of its canonical representative (cap at p and assign zero cost p in the sense ν(0)=p). Find the capped minimum h over all mode selections of the sum of ν for each unselected vertex b_v and each selected edge-state entry W_e(i,j). For scalar integer factors this is min(p,minimum summand valuation). In the nilpotent ring products may vanish sooner, so h is only a lower bound for actual summands.

This minimization is polynomial. Add an auxiliary vertex z_e on each edge, retaining u−v and adding u−z_e and v−z_e. A matching uses none of this triangle, or exactly one of its edges denoting state 10, 01, or 11. Conflicts at original vertices enforce the one-leg rule bijectively. Start with constant base cost Σν(b_v)+Σν(A_e). The u−z_e cost is ν(B_e)−ν(A_e)−ν(b_u), the v−z_e cost analogously, and u−v costs ν(D_e)−ν(A_e)−ν(b_u)−ν(b_v). Add the minimum weight of an unrestricted matching and cap at p. Ordinary minimum-weight matching permits negative edge weights. If h=p, return zero.

Otherwise substitute for each entry s its coefficientwise primitive part (s/2^{ν(s)}) times z^{ν(s)} in the truncated ring z^p=0, taking s=0 to zero. The result has no terms of degree below h. Compute it with only k=p−h bits by the same interpolation and hafnian routine; z is nilpotent, so the unit tests still use the scalar residue field. To recover the target, lift the coefficients for degrees h through p−1 and sum 2^j times the jth coefficient modulo 2^p. Each lift ambiguity is then killed. This is coefficientwise lifting, not a homomorphism mod 2^k→mod 2^p. Runtime is poly(input size,p,ℓ)^{O(k)}. In particular the original precision may grow linearly while the measured gap k stays fixed. This method cannot recover arbitrary permanent counts in polynomial time: exactly-one tensors contain that problem when no bound on effective precision is imposed.

**Affine example.** For f_v=c_v+Σ_e a_ve x_e, let d_v=2c_v+Σa_ve. Centering the independent bits as signs in 2f_v shows directly that the scalar equals 2^{m−|V|} times the matching sum with monomers d_v and dimers a_ue a_ve. Odd sign occurrences cancel. For components with fewer edges than vertices (trees), contract directly. For arbitrary graph families with m≥|V| and p−(m−|V|) fixed this gives a polynomial computation. For arbitrary numerical p, a connected component of cycle rank κ either vanishes when κ>p or can be evaluated by fixing at most p non-tree edge bits and running tree contraction, costing O*(2^p).

The forty tests in check.py compare explicit edge-bit contraction, mode expansion, translated-node evaluations in the unramified degree-four extension, doubled-graph hafnians, interpolation, and additive-valuation floors. Small examples use exhaustive matchings and hafnians as independent identity checks. They do not benchmark the published modular hafnian algorithm. No uniform polynomial bound follows for arbitrary tensor networks or general SAT.
