# Two changed legs: a constructive direct projection and a sharp parity barrier

Take a loopless binary tensor network. At vertex v with d incidences supply a reference leg u_i, a changed leg v_i, and coefficients a_v(S) for subsets of size ≤2. Its tensor is the sum of a_v(S) times the product of the changed legs on S and reference legs elsewhere. Contracting each original physical bit gives a two-by-two edge mode table W_e of its four endpoint dot products. The resulting sum is over local port subsets S_v with weight ∏a_v(S_v) times the product of the edge table entries selected by those subsets. This is the original scalar F(0), regardless of the Cauchy packing. Below the main parity criterion is for scalar integer coefficients; the higher-bit caveat is given explicitly.

## Polynomial at small effective precision

Form a (d+1)-square alternating matrix over F₂. Its entry P₀ᵢ is the parity of the singleton coefficient at port i, and Pᵢⱼ for i,j>0 is the parity of the coefficient on the unordered pair. If its rank is two, obtain α,β with Pᵢⱼ=αᵢβⱼ+αⱼβᵢ. For example select P_ab=1, take its rows a and b, and verify this formula entrywise. A zero matrix has the zero decomposition.

Two selector hubs, each allowed at most one chosen incident edge, generate the following exact integer local table. The first hub's idle weight is α₀ and selection weight at i is αᵢ; the second uses β. At each port a ternary gate permits (hub1,hub2,external) only in {000,101,011}; flipping the external argument makes it the exactly-one gate. Thus two hubs cannot select the same port. A separate weighted edge between the hubs, of weight a_empty−α₀β₀, lets them match to each other instead of either selecting a port. The resulting empty coefficient is exactly a_empty; singleton and pair coefficients are respectively αᵢβ₀+βᵢα₀ and αᵢβⱼ+αⱼβᵢ over the integers. All larger subsets have coefficient zero. Each constituent tensor has a supplied one-changed-leg representation, including the exactly-one gate, the hubs with their idle terms, and every binary edge table. In particular the ternary gate is exclusive partial addition, not ordinary XOR. The original table f and the gadget G agree modulo two, and D=f−G is even and supported only on the O(d²) listed subsets.

At target precision k, expand the product of G+D over vertices and discard terms with ≥k defective vertices. More generally a vertex with minimum coordinatewise D valuation η contributes η units of precision, so terms with total η≥k vanish. At each retained defective vertex enumerate its O(d²) possible coefficient states, multiply by the chosen coefficient and pin *all* its external leg modes, including the unselected ones. The remaining network is one-changed-leg throughout, and the shifted monomer–dimer/hafnian method in ../tensor-project/projection.md evaluates it in poly(input)^O(k). This proves polynomial time for fixed k on arbitrary graphs, without materializing exponential truth tables. If f already equals the supplied gadget over the full coefficient ring, no error expansion is needed.

For an augmented rank 2r_v, binary symplectic elimination gives a sum of r_v wedges. Use the sum of this many two-hub gadgets as the leading table, assigning the desired constant to one summand. A zero matrix uses one constant gadget. The remaining D is again even and sparse. Branching over intact gadget choices gives the sufficient bound ∏_v max(1,r_v) times poly(input)^O(k). This permits a small total logarithmic rank cost rather than requiring rank two everywhere. For coefficient polynomials modulo V^ℓ, equality modulo two must hold *in all coefficients* in the whole truncated ring; checking only V=0 does not certify divisibility. A supplied two-channel decomposition in that ring suffices.

## Valuation shifts

Integer lifts may be rescaled before the parity test. Choose port integers λ_vi and local integers g_v,g_e such that a'_v(S)=2^{−g_v−Σ_{i∈S}λ_vi} a_v(S) and W'_e(b,c)=2^{−g_e+bλ_ui+cλ_vi}W_e(b,c) are integral. The original scalar is 2^H times the rescaled one, H=Σ_v g_v+Σ_e g_e, by termwise cancellation of port exponents. Check divisibility on actual lifted coefficients, skip an unhelpful H≤0, and return zero if H≥p. Otherwise test the new local signatures at k=p−H.

In particular let m₀ edges have coefficientwise even 00 entry. Choose every λ=1, g_v=−2 and g_e=1 on these edges, zero elsewhere. H=m₀−2|V| may be linear. Now a'_v(S)=2^{2−|S|}a_v(S), so only pair parities must have rank ≤2; arbitrary singleton and constant weights are allowed. To see sharpness, take a five-regular bipartite graph with a proper edge coloring by five colors. At left make only the pair of colors 1,2 odd, and at right only 3,4 odd, with arbitrary even corrections on all other pairs. Use W=[[2,1],[1,1]], realized by binary leg dot products, on each edge. In the scaled parity model exactly one assignment of selected pairs survives, with no double-selected edge. Hence the scalar has valuation exactly H=m−2|V|. Singleton coefficients on color five may be odd, making the unscaled augmented parity test fail. This example can be used on high-expansion graphs. The five-parallel-edge instance gives a small direct replay.

## Homogeneous rank-four hardness

Already simple 4-regular bipartite networks with only the two changed pairs {1,2} and {3,4} at every vertex encode #3SAT parsimoniously. Pad each clause to four by repeating a literal, and repeat every clause four times so every used variable occurs a multiple of four times. A clause is ∏1−∏δ_false across its four ports. Put δ_false on the first two reference legs and 1 on the other two; use changed legs 1,1,−δ_false,δ_false. Its two listed products sum to the clause indicator. A four-ary equality tensor similarly uses δ₀,δ₀,δ₁,δ₁ as references and the opposite point indicators as changes. All reference/changed pairs are invertible local bases over F₂, and all physical tensor entries are zero or one. Both mode coefficients may be one, with the minus sign in the clause leg. The alternating pair matrix consists of two disjoint symplectic blocks and has rank four at every vertex.

For d=4b occurrences of a variable, use b copies of K₃,₄ filled with equality-4 vertices. Their four right vertices have one external socket each. Join consecutive blocks by removing one internal edge per block and switching the endpoints across the two blocks, replacing the removed edges. Use distinct edges for two switches at an interior block. K₃,₄ stays connected after two deletions, so every external socket and every internal edge must carry the same bit. The resulting gadget stays simple and bipartite. Join each left clause tensor to distinct exposed right sockets. Each assignment to used variables has a unique extension, and the contraction is the number of satisfying assignments. Unused declared variables can be handled separately. Thus arbitrary two-change networks are #P-hard exactly and ⊕P-hard already at precision one. The rank-two polynomial theorem does not extend indiscriminately to homogeneous rank four. This is a counting hardness assertion rather than a superpolynomial lower bound.

## Two arbitrary product summands per vertex

A supplied two-product decomposition gives one binary spin per vertex. Summing each physical edge leaves a two-by-two spin weight Q_e, and summing spins gives the original scalar. For scalar weights whose odd support on every edge is affine or empty, write Q_e as its 0/1 odd-support indicator plus an even correction. These are exactly the supports of size 0,1,2,4. Unary odd supports are always affine or empty. Expand fewer than k even defects modulo 2^k. Pin the endpoints of each defective edge in its four ways, and count the surviving affine system by binary Gaussian elimination, including any remaining empty supports. Hence fixed precision has polynomial cost independent of the graph. A few unrestricted exceptional edges may be pinned first. More product terms can similarly be indexed by short binary vectors if the odd local and edge relations are certified affine on those vectors; nonpower-of-two domain padding must be enforced.

A homogeneous three-entry relation can already encode ⊕SAT on bipartite graphs. With the edge indicator equal to one unless both endpoint spins are one, the count is the number of independent sets. Alternatively summing the right spins first shows its parity is the number of left subsets hitting every right neighborhood. For each variable use three left vertices l, lbar, y with right neighborhoods {l,lbar},{l,y},{lbar,y}. Choosing exactly one of l,lbar forces y; choosing both leaves y free and contributes zero modulo two. Add a right neighborhood for every clause containing its satisfying literal vertices. Independent set parity equals the parity of the original SAT count. Such an edge matrix is implemented with delta-spin vectors on the left and its two columns on the right. Relabeling the spins on each bipartition side moves any one missing entry to the forbidden (1,1). No hardness claim is made for every individual instance containing an exceptional edge.

## A general explicit-table parity lift

For an explicit Boolean parity signature f on d mode bits, twist a feasible bit pattern to the empty set; the all-zero signature is handled separately. Set a symmetric GF₂ matrix with diagonal f({i}) and off-diagonals f({i,j})+f({i})f({j}). Check f(S)=det(M[S,S]) over GF₂ for every subset S, with the empty determinant equal to one. This checks a binary delta-matroid representation in time polynomial in the supplied full table size. At each active port place a vertex allowing either a monomer of weight M_ii or exactly one internal edge M_ij; a gate using the complementary external bit disables such a port when inactive, leaving its contribution one. The resulting boundary weight is the matching sum on the active induced subgraph. Over GF₂ its value is the symmetric determinant: cycles of length ≥3 cancel against reversed orientations, and a transposition supplies M_ij²=M_ij. The realization uses only one-change gates. Twist back, and lift arbitrary higher coefficient bits through the same even-defect enumeration, now allowing up to the explicit table size per defective boundary. This test is a sufficient, checkable route and does not claim to classify all tractable signatures.
