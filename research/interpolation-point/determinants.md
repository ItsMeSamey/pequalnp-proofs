# Two determinant methods for Appendix D packet evaluations

## Aligned Cauchy matrices

Use the m-packet exactly-one wiring of program.md with every (i,j) slot present, distinct live row positions x_i by packet, and distinct column positions y_j aligned across packets. A nonzero product requires precisely one active column port per packet. The scalar is therefore per(W_{x_i,y_j}(T)). Let C_ij(T)=1/(T+x_i−y_j), N(T)=C_grid(T)/C_grid′(0). Over Q(T), Borchardt's identity gives

    F(T) = N(T)^m det(C_ij(T)^2)/det(C_ij(T)).

The denominator is a nonzero rational function. The left side is a polynomial of degree ≤m(2L−1). Before specializing to the source's zero-divisor ring, recover the polynomial over Q by evaluation at degree-bound-plus-one ordinary integers larger than 2L. The Cauchy determinant there is nonzero. Rational determinants, denominator arithmetic and polynomial interpolation cost polynomially many bits and operations in m,L: the degree and the sizes of products appearing after clearing denominators are polynomial, with log(m!) extra bits for summation. Evaluate at the lifted source node over its exact algebraic number field, then reduce using its integrality guarantee. Equivalently clear all polynomial coefficient denominators, using their 2-adic valuation as extra precision. This covers singular specializations without inverting any nonunit after specialization. Separable row/column scalars multiply the answer. With d exceptional rows, expand them on injectively chosen columns, using at most m^d Cauchy minors; fixed d remains polynomial. The compatible full-packet layout is supplied, with m≤L≤Δ.

## A low-rank certificate for weighted matching signs

Alternatively use one triangular single-live-bit packet on each logical edge of a balanced bipartite graph. Require exactly one incident selected bit at each vertex, on the Y side at left and X at right. Equal selected totals and K(1,0)=0 force identical edge selections. Hence the scalar is the weighted perfect-matching sum with actual heterogeneous g_e=P_e W_{x_e,y_e}(T). Canonical endpoint entries depending on which single edge is chosen multiply its weight. A supplied Pfaffian signing gives the familiar one-determinant case.

Order the vertex classes. Let q(x) sum binary pair products x_e x_f over edges crossing between these two orders. On matchings this equals the permutation-inversion exponent. Add any supplied combination of x_e x_f for distinct edges incident to one vertex, which vanish on matchings. Restrict to the affine degree-parity system Bx=1 and parameterize x=x₀+Dt over F₂. If it is inconsistent no matching exists. Let r be the polar rank of this restricted quadratic. Splitting the alternating form into r/2 hyperbolic pairs and the radical shows its sign (−1)^q has exactly 2^r nonzero normalized Walsh coefficients, all ±2^(−r/2). The support and signs take polynomial work per term by binary elimination. A character on t gives an edge sign vector s by solving Dᵀs=ell and adjusting its constant using s·x₀.

A determinant includes the permutation sign already. Evaluate the signed determinants for these edge twists and combine them with the normalized Walsh coefficients to cancel the sign on each matching. This costs 2^r times a polynomial. For modulus 2^p, lift weights arbitrarily to p+r/2 bits, use division-free determinant arithmetic, combine the signed results, divide their coordinates by 2^{r/2} and reduce modulo 2^p. After clearing denominators the identity is an integer polynomial in edge weights, and thus works over the truncated source ring even with zero divisors. Edges on no perfect matching may be removed and components treated separately. No optimizer for the conflict corrections is assumed. A rank-zero correction can be found by linear feasibility in its coefficients; annihilation of a supplied radical space is likewise a linear test.

sign_check.py verifies minimum rank two on K₃,₃ for this conflict family, and its four signed determinants recovered the permanent for 100 modulo-32 weight choices with one guard bit. gadgets.py checks five Borchardt identities against direct permanents and fifty masked scalings. No priority claim. Reference: [Singer, A Bijective Proof of Borchardt's Identity](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v11i1r48).
