# Signed rational interactions

For a nonzero rational table Q choose the principal x=sqrt(Q10 Q01/(Q00 Q11)), t=Q10/(Q00 x), u=Q01/(Q00 x). Check all four entries of Q(a,b)=Q00 t^a u^b x^[a≠b]. Multiply the phases of t,u and the unary h(1)/h(0) in {1,i,-1,-i} to obtain the phase of each vertex field H. Its squared magnitude is rational. The set T={v:H_v²≠1} is invariant under root sign changes. Every other H is 1 or −1. Pair minus fields by paths and change signs on the disagreement weight of each edge used oddly. For odd parity end the leftover path at a genuine field and negate that H. Without a genuine field global spin flip gives cancellation. Work per connected component.

For total rational input bit length B each original assignment weight has absolute value ≤2^B. D, the product of denominators of all entries, is a common denominator and ≤2^B. Round positive magnitudes to relative error δ=2^(-4(B+n+ceil(log2(m+n+2))+2)), keeping phases and the common rational prefactor exact. A term changes by at most 2^B ((1+δ)^(m+n)-1), so total error is below 1/(2D). Compute using exact Gaussian dyadics in the cut Pfaffians; round the real part after multiplying by D. The bit complexity is polynomial outside the established cover/genus factor. This rational reconstruction handles cancellation and avoids constructing an exponential-degree field.

Singletons, zero rows/columns and zero unaries can imply pins. Bijective support on an edge enforces XOR, permits identifying spins after a parity relabeling and moving allowed weights to a unary. Inconsistent constraints yield zero. Aggregate any loops into unaries. A signed symmetric table with zero entries can also be handled directly by the integral Pfaffian route.

A symbolic certificate for further zeros replaces each zero by c z^w, with nonzero signed rational c and positive integer w. Let L be the sum over input factors of their largest exponent. The partition polynomial has degree ≤L. For z>0 each squared field is C_v z^d_v for computable signed rational C_v and integer d_v. If d_v=0 and C_v=1 outside a small face cover, evaluate at L+1 positive distinct integers and interpolate at zero. This generalizes to free embeddings using the original graph. The runtime includes L and the sample bit lengths; large binary-encoded exponents need not be efficient. For example Q=[[1,z],[1,1]] on a directed graph with equal indegree and outdegree everywhere gives t²=1/z and u²=z, hence complete cancellation. There is no general guarantee that zero completions preserve low cover.
