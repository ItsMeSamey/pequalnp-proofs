# Unpadded one-hot colouring against specified efficient gates

Use variables x_vc for three colours per graph vertex, a positive totality clause per vertex, the three internal negative pair exclusions, and one negative exclusion for each graph edge and colour. Satisfiability means proper three-colourability.

**Hosts.** Fix 0<ε<1/2. For sufficiently large fixed d (e.g. d=200) a simple d-regular configuration-model graph on N vertices can be chosen with independence number less than 0.32N, at most log N triangles, and e(S)≤(1+ε)|S| for all |S|≤γN (γ>0 sufficiently small). For the independence bound let s=⌈0.31N⌉ and expose pairs from ds stubs of a specified s-set. On avoidance of internal pairs, the first floor(ds/2) exposures each have conditional probability at least (ds/2−1)/(dN) of hitting another of its stubs. Multiply the avoidance bounds and union bound by 2^N; d=200 gives exponential decay. A fixed triple forms a triangle with probability O_d(N^-3), giving the triangle bound by Markov. For local sparsity take t=⌊(1+ε)s⌋+1. Specifying t disjoint internal pairs among ds stubs costs at most (ds)^(2t)/(2^t t!), and each specified set has probability at most (dN−2t)^(-t). After union over vertex sets this is at most [C_d(s/N)^ε]^s for s≤γN. Sum separately at s≤√N and above; events requiring more available stubs are impossible. Conditioning on simplicity preserves high probability because simplicity has probability bounded away from zero at fixed degree. [Janson's configuration-model theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0609802).

Delete one vertex per triangle (or fewer), obtaining a triangle-free bounded-degree graph G. Its independence number is below |V(G)|/3 for large N, so χ(G)>3. Local sparsity persists. The original graph has linear treewidth: recursively remove O_γ(1) centroid bags to leave components of size ≤γN. These collectively contain at most (1+ε)N edges, forcing a linear separator union because the graph had dN/2 edges. Deleting O(log N) vertices reduces treewidth by at most that many. This is a probabilistic existence proof; exhaustive lexicographic selection gives a specified family, without a claim of polynomial-time deterministic construction. Every induced subgraph on ≤γN vertices is 2-degenerate and hence three-colourable, so the contradiction cannot be witnessed on a fixed-size vertex subset.

**Native resources and pinned pairs.** The conflict graph on the positive events has vertex-colour triangles and, separately for each colour, the host edges. A clique mixing different graph vertices and different colours cannot occur: different colours can conflict only within the same vertex and no event outside that vertex can conflict with both. Every clique therefore has size ≤3. Negative events have only their optional complement edge. Setting all positive literal marginals to 1/3 and complements to 2/3 satisfies every native clause cover and all clique capacities, ruling out any rational Farkas refutation using these rows.

Even pinning any two original literal events and taking the GF(2) row span of the restricted explicit violation ANFs adds no conflicts. At every untouched vertex the totality row retains its unique cubic, so that row cannot occur in a combination yielding 1. Given a nonconflicting pair, properly colour its at most two touched vertices respecting its pins, and set all other colour bits to zero. This satisfies every exclusion and every touched totality. All rows eligible for the alleged dependence now vanish. Complement conflicts and native positive conflicts are the only exceptions. Without pins the same cubic and distinct quadratic pivots preclude every nonzero affine row. No claim follows for multiplied rows or independently strengthened affine charts.

**Supported integer tables.** The uniform local one-hot distribution on each vertex, and on each same-colour edge pair the distribution with mass 1/3 each on 00,10,01, have consistent marginals. The same vertex table marginalizes to all internal pair constraints and the vertex capacity/totality clauses. For the signed integer consistency test, choose any one colour at each vertex, with coordinate marginals u,v in {0,1} for each edge pair. Give 10,01,00 masses u,v,1−u−v, respectively. All masses sum to one, are supported by the rational table, and agree on shared coordinates. Triangle-freeness ensures there are no nontrivial cross-vertex conflict cliques beyond individual edges. If edge relations are first aggregated into full unequal-colour tables, the uniform distribution on six allowed pairs works rationally; a signed integer table realizing prescribed unit row and column marginals exists because the bipartite support graph K3,3 minus its diagonal is connected (route the demands on a spanning tree). This addresses these explicit local scopes.

**Horn and matching.** Under any variable polarity renaming, at every vertex either at most one colour bit flips and the totality still has at least two positive literals, or at least two flip and their internal exclusion now has two positive literals. Deleting clauses to make a renamed Horn instance costs at least |V(G)| exceptions. This lower bound concerns the exception parameter. On the original all-negative exclusion Horn core, an exception totality has truth indicator equal, on Horn models, to the sum over GF(2) of its three singleton colour events. The product for a processed set S has precisely one distinct noncancelling closure monomial per proper colouring of G[S]: Horn closures are the selected bits themselves unless a pair conflicts. Under local sparsity every S up to γN admits a degree-at-most-two elimination order. If l vertices have at most one as-yet-unremoved neighbour, 2(|S|−l)≤e(S)≤(1+ε)|S|, hence l≥(1−ε)|S|/2. Reverse greedy colouring yields at least 2^l colourings. Thus the native explicit sparse closure dictionary is exponential at the first fixed small linear number of processed totalities, in every vertex order. This does not measure sparse expansions of every possible renamed Horn core.

For the designated long covers, monochrome cross-conflict components are precisely host components. Each component contains at most one event from each cover, so the matching parser requires it to be complete. At least one component fails this requirement because the host is triangle-free and non-three-colourable. The validated choice-matching promise is therefore false.

**Width and scope.** The mixed native/spectral proof in fphp-native-spectral-portfolio.md applies verbatim: a touched variable's singleton Walsh coefficient is 1/8 minus a multiple of 1/4 when totality is present and otherwise a strictly negative multiple of 1/4. Pair Walsh coefficients and all-zero pair mixed differences are positive for every generating clause. The primal graph contains a host copy on each single colour. Thus that portfolio costs w+λ+log₂(2H)=Ω(N) for every grouping and tree. This family simultaneously gives a negative result for the enumerated efficient tests and parameter bounds. Direct decision procedures, additional learned clauses, further projection, arbitrary core choices and exhaustive fallbacks have not been lower-bounded by this analysis.

## Strengthening to degree six and all signed Horn closure dictionaries

Use a uniform simple 6-regular graph. Fixed-degree short-cycle asymptotics give a limiting probability exp(−125/6)>0 for zero triangles. Hence triangle-freeness intersects every finite list of asymptotically almost sure properties below; no vertices need be deleted. For d=6 use the established non-3-colourability statement rather than the large-d independence estimate. Corollary 6.2 in the full 2025 paper states that for d≥2 with 6d³≤log n and k≥3, a.a.s. every polynomial calculus refutation of Col(G,k), over any field, has degree at least d^(−Cd)n. Its §2.1 gives the exponential size implication also for polynomial calculus resolution (PCR); its discussion of Lemma 2.8 records a.a.s. non-3-colourability for d≥6. Its local sparsity lemma supplies a fixed small positive ε sufficient for the previous width and closure arguments. This specializes to fixed d=6. The conference version's body proves k≥4 and defers the k=3 case; the cited full paper supplies it. [Full paper, Corollary 6.2 and §§2.1–2.2](https://jakobnordstrom.se/docs/publications/GraphColouringAverage-casePC_ECCC.pdf). [Wormald, short cycles in random regular graphs](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0095895681800226).

The Col axioms include the linear equation x_1+x_2+x_3−1=0 at each vertex, the negative pair exclusion monomials, and Boolean axioms. Our positive ternary clause's violation is (1−x_1)(1−x_2)(1−x_3). Multiply the linear equation by this constant-width product; each x_i term vanishes by idempotence, leaving the desired violation equation up to sign. In PCR use the complement axioms to replace each 1−x_i by its formal complement. Thus a small PCR refutation from our clause violations would give one from Col. The lower bound holds over GF(2).

To cover arbitrary polarity switches and arbitrary Horn-core choices, fix the *explicit sparse closure dictionary multiplication* procedure: start with the constant 1 on Horn models, multiply by the satisfaction indicator for each remaining original clause, replace each monomial by its least Horn closure (or zero on a conflict), and combine equal monomials modulo two. Let D be its largest retained dictionary. There is a PCR refutation of size polynomial in N and D whenever this process finishes with zero. A toggle variable is either an original formal positive or negative literal variable; PCR treats both as individual symbols. For a toggle set T denote its positive toggle monomial by m_T. If an implication A⇒b fires on the current set, multiply its Horn violation axiom by m_T to obtain m_T(1−b)=0 using Boolean idempotence. The complement axiom yields m_T=m_{T∪{b}}. If a negative constraint fires instead, multiplying its axiom by m_T proves m_T=0. At most 3N successful bit insertions occur in one normalization, and these derivations use only polynomially many monomials. This represents an isolated normalization without expanding complements of all T.

For a dictionary polynomial P known to equal 1 on the full instance, and next clause C with violation p_C, the input axiom p_C=0 gives Pp_C=0 by multiplying by each dictionary monomial and adding with its coefficient. Thus P(1−p_C)=1. Its termwise expansion in positive toggle variables costs at most eight monomials per old term because the clause width is at most three, with each bounded expansion certified using the complement axioms. Apply the normalization proof to each term, add the identities, and collect terms. This derives P_next−1=0. Intermediate sums of at most O(D) monomials can be added even by quadratic total size. At the last stage a zero dictionary derives 1=0. Indeed a polynomial expressed in distinct feasible closure monomials evaluates to identically zero on Horn models only when its coefficients are zero, by triangular evaluation ordered by inclusion of the closures. These derivations apply to any chosen order and any variable renaming. The exp(Ω(N)) PCR size lower bound forces exponential D for this dictionary procedure across all those choices, strengthening the native-only combinatorial lower bound above. This argument addresses a specified arithmetic computation with explicit closure terms; stronger learned lemmas, differently encoded semantic shortcuts or generic polynomial-time checkers do not automatically translate into small PCR proofs.
