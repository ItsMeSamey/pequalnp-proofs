# An exact two-residue spectrum on a growing matching-covered brick

Index three groups by i in F3. Group i has m+2 principal vertices A_i and m reserve vertices B_i, with m≥1. Make all principals a single clique. Each reserve sees precisely the principals in its own group. A principal edge across types i,j has label ij, including i² within a group. Reserve edges have label zero. The target is zero.

Each matching pairs the m reserves in every group with distinct principals there, leaving two named principal slots per group. A matching on the six slots projects to a degree-two multigraph on the three types. Three loops have gain 0²+1²+2²=2. A triangle has gain 0·1+1·2+2·0=2. A loop at i with two parallel edges between the other indices j,k has gain i²+2jk=1 for every i. On fixed named slots there is one all-loop matching, eight triangles and two matchings for each choice of the unique loop type. The exact profile in order 0,1,2 is therefore

    (0,6,9) · [binom(m+2,2) m!]^3.

The graph is 3-connected because deleting two vertices leaves a nonempty principal clique with a neighbor for every surviving reserve. To prove bicriticality after deleting any two vertices, observe that the remaining surplus of local principals over local reserves is nonnegative in every group. Match all remaining reserves locally and pair the even number of leftover principals arbitrarily in the clique. Every edge is allowed by the six-slot construction. By the 3-connected bicritical characterization this is a brick. Its clique has order 3(m+2), so its treewidth grows linearly.

Forcing an individual principal edge within one group permits either three loops or only this loop. Forcing an intergroup principal edge permits a triangle or a double edge. A forced reserve edge can be extended to either gain after the principal slots are chosen to exclude its endpoint. To forbid a principal edge, exclude one endpoint from the slots. To forbid a reserve edge, put its principal endpoint into the slots. Thus all single-edge in/out affine hulls are full. The K3,3 of cross edges between two labelled palettes of three fixed slots is a conformal subgraph (choose the other local pairs). Its six permutation matchings have gain 2a for π(i)=ai+c. Their three incidence vectors of each gain sum identically, so no rational affine edge functional takes distinct indicator values on the two residue classes. The same conformal K3,3 excludes a Pfaffian orientation, by multiplying all six putative equal-sign bipartite determinant monomials.

Partition A_0 and A_1 into disjoint pairs. One pair from each forms a rectangle in cyclic order 0,0,1,1 with alternating gain 1. This yields floor((m+2)/2) disjoint nonzero rectangles. Any vertex modulator making all residual labels vertex potentials must hit each rectangle. In any potential-and-owner representation, subtract the reports of constant-report vertices into their potentials. If no variable-report vertex occurs on a rectangle, all four edge labels there are potential sums, contradiction. Thus optimized scalar affine owner nullity κ is at least floor((m+2)/2)−1 over all gauges and owners.

Start with three loops on fixed slots, writing the loops at 0 and 1 as ab and cd. For every local unselected principal u in A_0 with matched reserve v, the alternating six-cycle a,b,c,d,u,v,a replaces the two loops by principal edges bc,du and a reserve edge va. It changes gain from 2 to 1. Thus even one base matching has m distinct short wrong-residue exchanges, while no sequence of exchanges attains zero. Fleeting single-edge support, small tight-cut torsos, small gauges and sparse exchange lists all fail as universal explanations. The six-slot count is a direct polynomial decision rule for this family, and conditioning several edges simultaneously may already fix the class.
