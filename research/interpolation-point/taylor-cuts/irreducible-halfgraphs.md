# Unbounded NO instances after neutral cut-rank-two reduction

For n≥2 take pins p,q,r inducing a path. Neutral vertices a_i,b_i for 1≤i≤n have edges a_i b_j iff i≤j. Join b_j to q, odd a_i to p and even a_i to r. No other edges. Every selected neutral needs odd selected neutral degree. A nonempty choice includes both classes, and the least selected a sees every selected b since each selected b must have a neighbor. Thus there are oddly many selected b, contrary to the requirement at q. Empty selection fails at p,r.

Every terminal-free shore S with ≥4 vertices has cut-rank≥3. All three terminal neighborhood types give independent rows. Within just one half-graph shore the opposite-side staircase rows are independent. Otherwise rank≤2 requires both shores but just one parity of a, making restriction of the cut row span to the two independent terminal types injective. All rows of each type would agree on the exterior. Two selected a_i,a_k with i<k have k≥i+2 and force b_i,b_{i+1} inside S. Their rows differ on excluded a_{i+1}. With just one a and ≥3 b, the extreme b rows differ on ≥2 intervening a vertices, at least one outside S. Thus rank≤2 is impossible. The entire neutral shore has rank three, yielding κ(X,T)=3 for every neutral four-set X.

A chain-graph interior with arbitrary local marks and h parity checks admits deterministic witness recovery: interleave the two shores so adjacency a–b means that a precedes b (sort nested neighborhoods, insert opposite vertices at their thresholds). Guess parity of total selected B; maintain prefix selection parity on each shore and current h-bit syndrome. A selected a has degree parity total B plus prefix B, and selected b has degree parity prefix A. Check marks when selecting, update states, check terminal syndrome and initial guess at end, and retain predecessors. There are O(n·2^h) transitions after ordering for fixed h. The family demonstrates unbounded core size, not a running-time lower bound.
