# Homogeneous short consequences over GF(3)

For the combined row span R, echelon reduction defines a linear quotient map with kernel R. Write gᵢ for its value on unit vector i. A singleton consequence occurs precisely when gᵢ=0 and contradicts full support. Normalize a two- or three-column candidate to have first coefficient 1. The second signature must be −c gᵢ for a pair, and the third must be −d(gᵢ+c gⱼ) for a triple with coefficients 1,c,d. Both c and d are units and equal their own inverse. Exact tuple lookup against the submitted columns enumerates all candidates without enumerating the image of the quotient map.

A two-term zero sum requires opposite signed terms. A three-term zero sum among nonzero trits requires three identical signed terms. Hence the resulting signed relations are logically equivalent to these short equations on the full-support domain. A negative cycle in the signed union structure contradicts full support. Otherwise replacing each variable by its signed representative yields a bijection of feasible assignments. Recompute consequences on the quotient, since the new equalities can create more short consequences. Each continuing pass strictly decreases the dimension. At termination retain independent original local rows first, extending their span by independent global rows. Either a submitted global row or its echelon residual gives the same constraint modulo earlier rows. The implementation selects the one with fewer nonzeros, preferring the submitted row in a tie. Local rows never become wider.

At most n+1 passes suffice. Each pass performs polynomial-size elimination and examines O(n²) signed prefixes and at most O(n³) matching triples. Even the uncompressed signed union implementation uses polynomial time and space. This gives a deterministic polynomial preprocessor, with no general completeness claim for full-support feasibility. The existing four-row solver uses it automatically on homogeneous input and preserves original-coordinate witness verification.
