# Complete joins turn matching exchanges into quadratic-gain oracles

Let a finite simple underlying graph have vertex partition A,B with every cross edge present. Original parallel edges may be distinguished by edge IDs, allowing identity swaps between parallel edges. Write a=|A|≥b=|B|, assume even total order, and set k=(a−b)/2. A perfect matching exists iff ν(G[A])≥k. If k=0, or if k<ν(G[A]) and either B contains an edge or the line graph of G[A] is connected, all original perfect matchings are connected by four-vertex flips and parallel-ID swaps.

Increase the cross-edge count by two whenever each side still has a matched internal edge: pair their four endpoints across the cut. In the final form all B vertices participate in the cross matching. These states consist precisely of a k-matching of A and an arbitrary bijection from B to its exposed vertices. Bijections are connected by two-cross-edge flips. When k=0 this proves the result, including edgeless interiors.

For k<ν(G[A]), k-matchings of A are connected by exchanges that replace one edge by another. Fix a maximum matching N. From any k-matching M with some off-N edge, at least one edge of N outside M conflicts with at most one edge of M outside N. This follows by averaging the conflict counts, since each off-N edge touches at most two N edges and |N−M|>|M−N|. Replace its unique blocker, or any off-N edge if there is no blocker. This increases overlap. All k-subsets of N are mutually reachable by exchanges.

To realize a shared-endpoint replacement, flip the old internal edge and the cross edge at the newly covered endpoint. For a disjoint replacement, choose a real edge st of B. Use cross-bijection transpositions to assign s,t to the endpoints of the desired new edge f. Flip their two cross edges to st and f, then flip st with the old internal edge e to two cross edges. This realizes the change without imposing any adjacency inside A beyond the two given edges. Alternatively, when the line graph of G[A] is connected, Corollary 4 of Bonsma, Kamiński and Wrochna (SWAT 2014, Reconfiguring Independent Sets in Claw-Free Graphs) replaces every edge jump by a sequence of shared-endpoint slides on the connected claw-free line graph, so no edge in B is needed. All cycle orientations are physical. For parallel edges choose one representative for structural transformations and change the ID before or after via a two-vertex swap.

A catalogue of all four-vertex flips and parallel-ID changes is polynomial in the explicit input. For quadratic Q on edge incidence or any additive gain features, Q(R+A')−Q(R+B') is affine in the residual incidence R. Scan every flip, testing this affine form on all residual perfect matchings by Lovász's finite-field linear matching-span algorithm over F₃. A nonzero difference provides actual endpoint witnesses by deletion self-reduction on the affine test. If all differences vanish, Q is constant over all original matchings and a baseline decides nonvanishing. Iterating on the annihilator of witnessed evaluation rows returns the entire quadratic evaluation span in deterministic polynomial time. The scalar target polynomial is 1−(g−t)². A requested NO covers every perfect matching. The result concerns the joined instance, and does not restrict a target witness to any desired internal port configuration.

In particular, adding two adjacent vertices complete to an arbitrary matchable graph H satisfies the hypotheses (when |H|≥2). The two added vertices may be nonadjacent when the edge support of H is connected. A universal clique U joined to H has the property whenever |U|−def(H)≥2 and the total order is even. If H is the larger side this follows from the preceding argument (small |U| cases are immediate). If the clique is the larger side its edge-support is connected and, unless H has at most one vertex, its matching capacity exceeds the normalized k; the exceptional complete graph cases are direct. Deleting r vertices worsens the indicated slack by at most r. For a degree-d test, d≥2, a slack of 4(d−2)+2 guarantees connectivity until the last quadratic node, since a derivative deletes at most four vertices and the base linear tests need no connectivity. Test complexity m^{O(d)} with explicit-polynomial arithmetic for fixed d. For a specified F₃^q target use degree 2q. At zero slack add b isolated core ports to an arbitrary matchable H₀, then join b hubs. Every hub is forced onto one isolated core port, leaving the original matching problem on H₀ intact. Taking H₀=C₆ leaves two square-flip components.

For a supplied join, recognition uses adjacency tests and ordinary maximum matching. Buffered connectivity itself is not a reduction from an arbitrary labelled core to an equivalent target in the augmented graph. The scalar algorithm permits arbitrary cross-edge labels.
