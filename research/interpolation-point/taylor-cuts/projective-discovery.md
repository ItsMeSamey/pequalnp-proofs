# Discovering translation certificates from bounded-scope tables

Given a product F(h,y) of explicit local tables, h denotes hidden bits and y observed bits. For each nonzero local table, a binary shift v is a projective period when shifting its input multiplies the entire table by a fixed sign ±1. Its Walsh spectrum yields linear tests: choose one nonzero frequency a and require (s+a)·v=0 for all nonzero frequencies s. The sign is (−1)^(a·v). An identically zero factor kills the product. Lift the local equations to shared variable coordinates and intersect them by Gaussian elimination. This gives certified translations of the global product, including arbitrary overlapping factors. One hidden-only shift with negative total sign annihilates all hidden sums. Otherwise the sign descends to the projected visible subspace K. With a computed linear lift for each visible basis direction, S(y)=sum_h F(h,y) is determined from one representative per coset of K, with known signs. Terminal observers can be arbitrary; their signed sums on the cosets and the anchor fiber evaluations must be charged. This applies coefficientwise to explicit rational polynomial tables.

For nowhere-zero tables the complete global projective period subspace (before summing h) is itself efficiently recoverable. First use the magnitudes. In an additive log-magnitude representation the global Walsh spectrum is the sum of the local spectra extended by zero outside each local scope. Its potentially nonzero frequencies are therefore enumerable, even when the product spans all bits. For each such frequency test the vanishing of its Walsh coefficient exactly by multiplying local squared values with signed integer exponents and comparing with one, clearing the common power-of-two denominator of the local Walsh transforms. Squaring avoids signs. No logs or integer factorization are required. The allowed shifts are orthogonal to all surviving nonzero frequencies. Over Q[T] these are rational-function identities: compare numerator polynomials after denominator clearing. The real-Walsh argument applies for all sufficiently large generic real T; eventual signs are the signs of leading coefficients. Individual entries must not be identically zero, but may have isolated roots. With explicit tables, bounded arity k and polynomial-degree coefficient lists, cross-products contain polynomially many factors counting multiplicities and have polynomial bit length.

The product sign has form (−1)^φ, with Boolean ANF φ the XOR of bounded-scope local sign ANFs. Its degree is at most k. On the remaining shift space, compute for each basis shift v its derivative φ(z+v)+φ(z). These start with degree at most k−1. If every derivative on the current space has degree at most d, the map from v to its degree-d homogeneous coefficient vector is linear: composing shifts adds the derivatives, and translating the argument of a degree-d polynomial preserves that top homogeneous component. Restrict to the kernel, descending d from k−1 to 1. The surviving shifts are exactly those with constant sign ratio, whose character is obtained at z=0. ANF arithmetic takes n^O(k) time. Example of strict recovery beyond factorwise testing: g(x⊕y)h(x) multiplied by 6/h(x), with h(0)=2 and h(1)=3. Their product has simultaneous x,y shift symmetry even though neither individual table admits that shift up to sign.

Some zero entries are also handled completely. Intersect the lifted affine hulls of each table's nonzero support to obtain an affine space A. If empty the product is zero. If every local projection of A lies entirely in that table's support, then A equals the global support. Parametrize A by free binary coordinates. Each local table is now a nowhere-zero function of at most k affine linear forms in those coordinates. Compute its Walsh support in a basis for the independent local forms and map the frequencies into the free-coordinate dual space. Sign ANF substitutions still have degree at most k. Every projective global translation necessarily preserves A, so this computes all shifts. Failure of this affine-hull check is inconclusive.

An observed family uses hidden cyclic x_i, with n not divisible by three, and edge observations y_e of a cubic expander on these vertices. Put u_i=x_i+x_{i+1}+x_{i+2}+sum_{e incident to i}y_e mod two. Forbid adjacent occupied u's around the cycle. B=I+shift+shift² is invertible because the polynomial 1+z+z² divides z^n−1 over F₂ precisely when 3 divides n. All visible shifts lift by B^{-1} times the edge-incidence map, keeping every local predicate unchanged. Each observation fiber has exactly the number of independent sets of an n-cycle, F_{n−1}+F_{n+1}. At y=0 its direct bounded-scope predicates fit a constant-width cyclic layout. The direct truth-table CNF contains the expander's subdivision in its primal graph since x_i occurs with every incident y_e. Grouping clauses by scope yields the input tables without identifying computational gates. An alternative width-three XOR encoding retains the lifts through the auxiliary XOR bits.

The goal is not complete recognition of equal hidden sums: even (y OR NOT h1) AND (NOT y OR NOT h2) has two size-two fibers whose different line directions prevent a hidden translation between them. Determining whether the two hidden counts agree for a single observation bit is C_=P-complete under polynomial reductions: condition a 3CNF F0 on y=0, another F1 on y=1; resulting clauses have width at most four. The recognition theorem applies to the unsummed global product's projective periods under the stated nonzero/affine-support conditions.
