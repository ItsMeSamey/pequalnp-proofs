# Native and sparse-spectral cost portfolios on unpadded graph FPHP

Take a simple bipartite graph G on n pigeons and fewer holes, with constant pigeon degree d≥3, bounded hole degree at least two, and boundary expansion δ|S| for every pigeon set |S|≤γn. Use one Boolean variable per incidence edge, one positive totality clause per pigeon, and negative binary exclusions for every pair sharing a pigeon or a hole. These are precisely the graph functional pigeonhole clauses. They assert an injection into fewer holes. Their primal graph P=L(G) has bounded degree D.

**Existence.** Take m holes divisible by seven and n=2m. Partition holes into seven equal bins. In each independently, assign the n pigeons uniformly to blocks of fourteen, one block per hole. Every pigeon has seven distinct neighbors and every hole has fourteen. For fixed S,T of sizes s,t the probability that all neighbors are in T is at most the product over bins of (14|T_i|/n)^s, at most (t/m)^(7s) by AM–GM. Fewer than s unique neighbors implies |N(S)|≤4s by counting the 7s incidences. For s≤γn with 4s≤m, a union bound over S and hole sets of size 4s gives at most [C(s/n)^2]^s at each s, for an absolute C. Sum separately up to √n and above √n, choosing γ with Cγ²<1. The failure probability tends to zero.

**Gaussian and Walsh facts.** The raw GF(2) totality violation has a unique degree-d monomial and every exclusion is a distinct quadratic monomial. A linear combination of degree at most one must be zero. Every single clause has full affine hull. In spins σ_e=(−1)^x_e a totality violation is 2^(−d) times the product of (1+σ_e), and an exclusion contributes (1−σ_e)(1−σ_f)/4. In any clause group, the singleton coefficient at a touched e is either a negative multiple of 1/4, or 2^(−d) minus a nonnegative multiple of 1/4. It never vanishes. All contributed pair coefficients are positive. Thus each original cost group's minimal linear measurement space equals the coordinate span on its touched variables. The cut interface W is the coordinate space on shared variables; denote its maximum dimension by w. This concerns original cost spaces before child minimization.

**Native leaves.** Pin shared variables to zero in their full interface table. Every private pair appearing together in a group clause has strictly positive second mixed difference at the all-zero assignment: every generating totality or exclusion contributes +1 and every other clause contributes zero. At a native cut with E crossing private primal edges select suffix vertices with a crossing neighbor, greedily excluding distance at most two between selections. This retains k≥E/[D(1+D²)]. The prefix neighbor sets are disjoint. For each chosen v project a residual g to g(e_v)−g(0) on its suffix cube. This linear functional depends only on prefix variables sharing a group clause with v. Its value changes when a crossing partner alone flips from the zero prefix, by the strictly positive mixed difference. The joint image of all prefixes is the Cartesian product of the k individual finite images. Its hull is a box with 2^k vertices. Restricting to two values per coordinate gives 2^k corners, at most two per invertible affine-output class because the shift cancels and the scaled coordinate vectors lie on one line. Consequently E≤D(1+D²)log₂(2H) for peak native hull or class count H. This handles the nonlinear totality clauses.

**Treewidth.** Repeatedly remove centroid bags of a decomposition of G to balance pigeon weights, for at most ⌈log₂(1/γ)⌉+1 rounds and only in pieces containing more than γn pigeons. At most 1/γ bags are removed per round. Their union X has size O_γ(tw(G)+1). Each remaining component has at most γn pigeons. For its pigeon set S a unique-neighbor hole is either in X or adjacent to a deleted pigeon, since every original hole has at least two neighbors. A deleted hole counts in at most its degree many component sets; an undeleted hole adjacent to a deleted pigeon counts in only its own component. Thus δ(n−|X|)≤O(Δ_G|X|) and tw(G)=Ω(n). Replace the incidence edges in each line-graph decomposition bag by their original endpoints. For any original vertex, the subtrees of its incident edges meet pairwise as they form a line-graph clique; their union is connected. It follows that tw(P)≥(tw(G)+1)/2−1=Ω(n).

For an entirely native tree, each group's private vertex order yields a path decomposition with bag consisting of the current vertex and earlier vertices having a neighbor at or beyond the current position. Add its physical middle set to each leaf-path bag, and at an internal node use the union of its three incident middle sets. Each vertex connects all leaves using it along their minimal subtree. Thus tw(P)≤max{3w,w+1+D(1+D²)log₂(2H)} up to an additive one.

**Mixed leaves.** Assign each primal edge uv to one leaf containing a generating clause. Its column e_u+e_v is present in this group's Fourier support, and these assigned columns represent the binary cycle matroid of P. On cuts of the group tree the intersection of the two spans has dimension at most w.

At a spectral group let P_0,Q_0 be prefix and suffix spans for its original frequency order. Global connectivity of an assigned edge prefix, measured as the intersection of that prefix span with the span of every remaining assigned column globally, is at most dim(P_0∩(Q_0+W)): the contribution from outside to such an equality belongs both to the local original span and the outside span. Assigned columns are a subset of the recorded frequencies, so suppressing unassigned positions does not increase the bound. This directly applies when the terminal trellis width λ bounds this intersection.

If instead λ is measured only after the zero interface pin, first set aside the at most Dw assigned primal edges having a shared endpoint. All remaining assigned pair frequencies have a positive coefficient on the zero fiber: every generating residual totality contributes a positive pair coefficient, and each generating private exclusion contributes 1/4. No other term contributes a negative pair coefficient. Order the exceptional edges first, then the private assigned edges in their pinned spectral order. Outside-leaf vectors vanish on the private coordinates. At a cut after the exceptional prefix, project onto private coordinates: any vector in the global intersection on that projection lies in (prefix-private span plus exceptional projections) intersected with the suffix-private span. Its dimension is at most λ+Dw. The kernel on the prefix side has dimension at most Dw. Earlier cuts have prefix rank at most Dw. This gives global connectivity at most λ+2Dw. An invertible reparametrization of the private spectral coordinates preserves these dimensions when applied consistently.

At a native group again prepend the ≤Dw exceptional edges. Sort remaining assigned edges by their later endpoint in the native vertex order. Just before a block the private vertices supporting both processed and unprocessed edges lie among earlier vertices incident to a crossing edge, bounded by D(1+D²)log₂(2H). Within a block add at most D+1 endpoints. Including the exceptional prefix adds at most O(Dw) dimensions as before.

Replace every group leaf by a caterpillar for its assigned edge order, attaching it so the exposed cuts are prefixes versus their complements. Prune empty leaves and suppress degree-two nodes. This gives a branch decomposition of P's cycle matroid, of width O_D(w+λ+log₂(2H)), including the customary additive one for matroid connectivity. For any graph with a cycle its graph branchwidth equals its cycle-matroid branchwidth. A graph branch tree of width b gives a tree decomposition with internal bags the union of at most three incident middle sets; add endpoint bags at edge leaves. Its treewidth is O(b+1). Thus w+λ+log₂(2H)=Ω(n) even for a mixture of these leaf types. This concerns the specified frequency trellis and native residual representations, not semantic minimization or arbitrary decision algorithms.
