# Private normal directions in forbidden-flat counting

Parameterize the ambient affine space by F₂^d and absorb affine offsets into the forbidden values. A constraint on independent linear forms spanning P is a table f on P*, initially the Boolean indicator that the evaluation is different from one specified point. The joint normal span V controls every constraint. Enumerating V* instead of the original space leaves the outer multiplier 2^(d−dim V), since every evaluation on V has that many preimages.

For one live factor on P let W be the span of all other factor scopes, L=P∩W, and t=dim P−dim L. If t>0, replace its table f by

    g(l) = sum_{z in P*, z restricted to L = l} f(z).

Every pair of evaluations on P and W agreeing on L glues to a unique evaluation on P+W. Each other factor is constant on those private extensions. The replacement therefore preserves the count with the live assignment space now W; no extra multiplier is needed for these t dimensions. For a single original forbidden point a the replacement is 2^t−1[l=a restricted to L], always positive. For decision the factor can simply be dropped, and a witness lifted by choosing any nonforbidden extension. General nonnegative weights use g(l)>0 for feasibility; one maximizing or positive extension may be stored for witness recovery. For rank at most two a genuine shrink produces a unary table or a scalar. Merge equal-scope tables by pointwise multiplication. A constant factor on a positive-dimensional scope can either be left there or explicitly remove its unused dimensions while restoring their fiber multiplicity.

Gaussian elimination computes P,L,W and extension maps. Repeatedly scan factors for a private direction, merge equal scopes, and remove scalar factors while keeping their multipliers. Every shrink cuts the live rank by at least one. When no more shrink applies, each remaining P is contained in the span of the others; its residual assignment rank r gives a 2^r times polynomial exact algorithm, including elimination, for fixed maximum initial rank. Feasible multiplicities are counts of assignments to independent eliminated dimensions and have polynomial bit length. If optional reductions drop zero-dependence coordinates, update their multiplicity as above. Ignoring an accumulated unary weight merely because its original private gate was nonblocking would corrupt counts.

One can also compute the finest direct-sum decomposition that keeps every scope together. Choose a basis B from the union of the input scope bases. In a graph on basis elements, for every listed normal connect all members of its expansion support in B. For each factor also connect the supports of all its basis normals. Each connected component spans a subspace independent of the others, and all forms within a gate lie in one such component. Conversely, any direct-sum grouping of the listed gate scopes assigns each member of B to its own group's span. Uniqueness of basis coefficients forces all basis elements joined by the preceding rules to lie in that same group. The components therefore give the finest such grouping. Multiply the component counts; isolated free directions were accounted for by the ambient multiplier. These computations are deterministic polynomial time. Decomposition may be repeated after partial summation.

A connected family shows the reduction has reach beyond finding shared normals. For q≥2, take q core bits y and, for every nonzero linear form l on them, a fresh bit z_l. Forbid one value pair of (l(y),z_l), independently selectable for each gate. The m=2^q−1 rank-two normal planes intersect pairwise only at zero. Their finest gate-respecting direct sum has one component: for distinct nonzero l,l', the dependency l+l'+(l+l')=0 across the three differently labelled forms joins these core normals, and each private normal joins its core normal through its gate. Peeling the m private bits leaves only q dimensions, so exact counting takes polynomial time in m even with independently chosen bad tuples. If every bad tuple is (0,0), the count is 1+(2^q−1) 2^(2^(q−1)): at zero every private bit is forced; at nonzero y exactly 2^(q−1) private bits are free. The remaining irreducible kernels can still encode the hard graph-coloring family in affine-flat-branching.md, so this identifies a tractable parameter rather than settling the unrestricted case.
