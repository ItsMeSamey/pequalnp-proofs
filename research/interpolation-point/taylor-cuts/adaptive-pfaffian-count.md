# Adaptive exact matching counts using certified signs

In a fixed vertex order, the Pfaffian sign of a perfect matching is (−1)^q, where q counts crossing pairs of selected edges modulo two. Compute certified quadratic identities over F₂ from linear matching identities times affine forms, both edge-literal conditional affine annihilators times their respective literals, incompatible pair monomials, and Boolean relations. If a linear solve expresses q as affine modulo this dictionary, the coefficients give a sign-correct reorientation. This suffices for a count leaf. Additional leaves include planar orientations and polynomial bipartite Pfaffian recognition by Robertson, Seymour and Thomas.

The previously known Walsh leaf restricts q to the F₂ affine hull of actual matching indicators. For polar rank h its sign has 2^h supported linear characters with absolute coefficient 2^(−h/2). Extend these characters to signs of physical edges. The signed combination of Pfaffians gives an unsigned gain histogram. For F₃^b use 3^b characters, evaluating Pfaffians over Q(ω), then invert exactly. Do not pivot on arbitrary elements of the group ring. This exponential output dimension is constant only for fixed b.

Every leaf predicate above is hereditary. Specialize fixed edge indicators to one and deleted or competing edges to zero. In inherited vertex order the residual crossing quadratic differs from the old restriction by only affine terms. The actual residual affine hull embeds into the old affine hull; polar rank cannot increase. Proven sign orientations restrict, and conditional identity certificates restrict as in adaptive-selector-rank.md. Apply its greedy Strahler recognition to the union of these leaf predicates with a fixed raw-rank cap r. Counts of both successful branches add, shifting the forced branch by its original gain. UNKNOWN never contributes zero. The clock at rank s is (m+1)^{O(s)}2^r 3^b poly(input) with classical matching-span and Pfaffian routines. Positive counts yield an original matching by conditioned queries and final verification. For example a planar graph with one added apex has a rank-one list scanning the apex's possible mates.

With Pfaffian-only leaves the minimum edge-decision Strahler rank of K_{n,n} is n−2 for n≥2. K₃,₃ is not Pfaffian: multiplying all six permutation sign constraints would equate the product of squared edge signs to −1. Pfaffian orientations restrict, so K_{n,n} is non-Pfaffian for n≥3. The first edge-present branch is K_{n−1,n−1}, and the absent branch contains such a face by forcing another mate for that row. Restrict any putative tree to that face to lower bound both child ranks. Induction raises necessary rank by one. Sequential row-partner lists achieve this bound recursively.

Any such tree partitions all n! original matchings. A Pfaffian leaf on N≤n free rows has at most N^{N/2} matchings by Hadamard's determinant bound (empty determinant one). Thus there are at least n!/n^{n/2} leaves. When each leaf admits a signed sum of ±1 determinant evaluations of coefficient ℓ1 norm ≤2^{r/2}, as for raw-rank Walsh leaves, divide this lower bound by 2^{r/2}. This concerns explicit trees, not algorithms sharing repeated subinstances or target-specific decision tests. The host is a connected brace for n≥3.
