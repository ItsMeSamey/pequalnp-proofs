# Quadratic matching gains via matroid bases and independent slots

For x in GF(3)^b write Φ(x) for its constant, linear and degree-two monomial evaluations. Let D=1+b+b(b+1)/2. A deterministic oracle finding an actual basis or matching where an input quadratic is nonzero gives the complete evaluation span with witnesses. Maintain the row span of Φ on returned gains, test a basis of its annihilator by oracle, and add a row if possible. At most D augmentations and O(D²) oracle calls suffice.

## One matroid supplied by independence oracles

For an arbitrary matroid M, associate an additive vector gain w_e to each element. Find any basis and evaluate the queried polynomial Q. If that value vanishes but some other basis has nonzero value, connectivity of the basis exchange graph supplies adjacent bases I+e,I+f on which evaluations differ. Enumerate all distinct nonloop e,f. On E minus {e,f}, take the two matroids (M/e) deleted at f and (M/f) deleted at e; both must retain rank r(M)−1. Their common bases are exactly the eligible residual sets I. The difference Q(g(I)+w_e)−Q(g(I)+w_f) is affine in g(I). Convert this to scalar additive labels and add a compulsory coloop, on both matroids, carrying the affine constant. The [nonzero common-basis algorithm of Hörsch et al., Theorem 3.7](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2024.86) works over GF(3), which has no element of order two. A common basis with nonzero difference lifts to two genuine bases of M, one evaluating nonzero. Apply the preceding Gaussian oracle procedure to recover the complete span in deterministic polynomial time in b and the ground-set size. This inference only uses the linear nonzero-common-basis theorem at the leaves; arbitrary quadratic tests on *two* simultaneous matroids are outside it.

For a matching application take a bipartite graph with left L∪D and right E, where D is a complete reservoir, and the edges between L and E are arbitrary. The side sizes balance. Labels on core edges have the form p_l+q_e; labels on reservoir edges have the form r_d+s_e. Once L is saturable, the sets I of right vertices matched to L are exactly the bases of the associated transversal matroid. Every complement extends to the complete reservoir and the total gain is a global constant plus Σ_{e∈I}(q_e−s_e). Thus the method returns the quadratic evaluation span on actual original perfect matchings, by ordinary bipartite matching reconstruction on a selected basis and its complement. The core graph may have large width. Parallel edge variants of equal label may retain any original representative.

## Exact interpolation for slotted assignment boundaries

For each optional assignment edge or compulsory slot e select a concrete baseline gain h_e. For a feasible outer assignment A (including compulsory slots), H(A)=Σ_{e∈A}h_e. Its independent slot changes v_e belong to S_e−h_e. Assume a quadratic evaluation span with actual witnesses for each local profile S_e and for the baseline-only assignment family. A particularly simple case permits an arbitrary bipartite assignment graph and a constant total H across all its perfect matchings; this property is certifiable by ordinary finite-field matching-span computation.

Recover the complete evaluation span by uniting the following rows, or the individual evaluation witnesses from which their differences are formed.

* Include a basis of Φ(H(A)) over bare assignments.
* For each slot e, get an affine basis of H(A) over assignments containing e, with concrete assignments. This is ordinary linear matching span with e pinned for an optional assignment edge; obtain actual representatives by affine-subspace avoidance and self-reduction. For each such assignment and each witness in a local quadratic-span basis, include Φ(H(A)+v)−Φ(H(A)). This difference is affine in H and polynomial of degree at most two in v.
* For each two distinct jointly selectable slots e,f, find any extending assignment A by ordinary matching. Using affine spanning difference representatives v,w at the two slots, include Φ(H+v+w)−Φ(H+v)−Φ(H+w)+Φ(H). This term is independent of H and bilinear in (v,w). All four evaluations use the same assignment and disjoint original slot realizations.

The expansion of Φ(H+Σv_e) consists exactly of the baseline, single and pair terms above. Each generated difference belongs to the true span because all its contributing witnesses are realizable. Conversely, affine and bilinear interpolation spans every term of the expansion, providing the reverse inclusion. To return solely genuine matching rows, store the underlying baseline/single/pair witnesses and reduce their evaluations, rather than treating a difference vector as a matching. More than one change per slot is never needed. If the input bare-assignment span and local oracles are polynomial, this lifting step is polynomial in slot count, b, graph size and the supplied oracle costs.
