# Keeping actual vertices of a signed affine-cell residual hull

Let f(x) be a sum of explicitly encoded rational weights times indicators of binary affine cells. A cell is specified by any list of binary linear equations and may be empty, full, or have free coordinates. Fix a variable order. At a cut, each assigned prefix defines a real-valued residual function on the entire remaining Boolean cube. The objective here is the maximum (or minimum) value and a genuine optimizing assignment.

Store only the distinct vertices of the convex hull of residuals, each with an actual prefix producing it. Initially there is one prefix. Restriction of a function to either value of the next bit is linear, even when applied to signed residuals. Hence the hull after the next split is precisely the hull of the two restrictions of each retained vertex. Discard interior points and keep a concrete originating prefix for every surviving vertex. At the empty suffix these residuals are scalars and an endpoint gives the optimum and its witness. No combination of prefix witnesses is needed. This argument also works for fixed linear payoffs on a terminal vector. Intermediate scalar values, counts of assignments and exact-level feasibility need not survive hull pruning.

## Exact rational LP test

Suppose r candidate residuals g_1,...,g_r have been generated. View them as vectors indexed by all suffix assignments, with the ordinary real sum inner product. Compute their Gram matrix G without listing the cube. A pair of signed cell sums has inner product equal to the sum of products of weights times sizes of pairwise cell intersections. Combine the two sets of affine equations; Gaussian elimination gives zero on inconsistency and otherwise 2^(remaining dimension minus rank). A prefix fixes the existing cell descriptions rather than introducing new terms. In particular all counts, rational Gram entries and row encodings have polynomial bit length in the original explicit input length and ambient dimension.

The candidate g_i is in the convex hull of the others precisely when there is a nonnegative rational vector λ of length r with λ_i=0, sum λ=1, and G(e_i−λ)=0. For the proof, with column matrix A of actual residual values, G=AᵀA. Its real kernel equals that of A, so the displayed equation is equality of functions. Rational LP feasibility agrees with real feasibility for rational data. One may deduplicate first using G_ii+G_jj−2G_ij=0 and then test each distinct candidate against the original other distinct candidates. Alternatively delete redundant candidates sequentially; simultaneous testing without deduplication can incorrectly discard two identical vertices. A singleton is retained. No signed-cell independence or strict positivity of the original objective is assumed.

A redundant-point certificate is the barycentric vector λ itself. For a distinct retained vertex, strict finite-dimensional separation gives a linear functional exceeding its value at every other candidate by a positive gap. Project the functional to the span of the candidates and rescale its gap to one. Consequently a rational certificate can be taken as coefficients α with (Gα)_i ≥ (Gα)_j+1 for every j other than i. This rational linear system has polynomial-bit feasible witnesses under the usual LP bit bounds. A singleton needs no separating inequalities. Both types of certificate can be checked from the same Gram matrix.

Polynomial-time rational LP therefore makes each layer polynomial in the input and maximum retained vertex count H: there are at most 2H candidates per split, and their originating prefixes can be replayed and their signed cells recomputed without storing arbitrary LP mixtures. The total space is polynomial in H and the explicit input. An optional H cap can abort during a layer once its vertices are certified to exceed the cap. Small affine rank alone does not imply a small hull.

For a strict separation from caching distinct (even affinely normalized) residuals, use bits z,y,x_0,...,x_(n−1) and the objective

    f = z + y sum_i 2^i x_i.

It has one unary cell and n two-equation cells, with polynomial binary weight lengths. Process all x_i before y,z. At each x-prefix only the scalar multiplying y varies, so the residuals lie on a segment whose two endpoints are genuine prefixes. Subsequent restriction to y gives points z plus a scalar and the last layer contains scalars; every hull has at most two vertices. After assigning the n x bits, however, all 2^n residuals z+ay are distinct. All have common values 0 and 1 at (y,z)=(0,0) and (0,1); therefore affine rescaling and translation of function values cannot identify two of them. This is a separation between these representation sizes for the displayed order.

## Exponential vertex obstruction in every order

Use any infinite family of constant-degree undirected simple edge expanders of even size n with a fixed positive edge-expansion lower bound. On each edge give weight one to the affine cell requiring its endpoints' parity to equal a selected bit. Sign labels may be arbitrary. In spin coordinates σ_u∈{−1,1}, the cell indicator is (1+ε_uv σ_u σ_v)/2. For any variable order cut after n/2 vertices, with prefix A and suffix B. Project the entire residual hull to the singleton Walsh coefficients on B. Prefix-internal terms contribute constants, suffix-internal terms have no singleton coefficient, and cross terms give precisely (1/2)Mᵀσ_A where M is the signed crossing adjacency. This projection is the zonotope generated by the rows of M (symmetric intervals with an inessential common factor).

There are Ω(n) crossing edges. Greedily choosing an edge and excluding edges that conflict with a diagonal induced matching discards at most 2Δ² edges per choice for maximum degree Δ. The resulting signed submatrix on its matching endpoints is diagonal with nonzero diagonal. Consequently the real crossing rank r is Ω(n), uniformly in edge signs and in the variable order. Select r independent generators. Linear functionals on their span realize all 2^r strict sign patterns on these generators. Perturb each within its open sign cone so that it is nonorthogonal to every nonzero generator. Its maximizer on the zonotope takes each segment at its unique maximizing endpoint. Distinct sign patterns give different maximizers: evaluating a purported common endpoint using the first functional shows a strict loss on a flipped generator and no gains anywhere else. There are at least 2^r exposed projected vertices. A linear image of a finite polytope cannot have more vertices than its source. This proves a 2^Ω(n) obstruction for the entire residual hull in every variable order, despite the affine-cell input being linear in size.

The same proof works for the *additive* count of violated ordinary two-literal clauses on these edges, with any choice of literal polarities. Each violation indicator is a single affine cell, with nonzero mixed spin coefficient ±1/4. Its additional constant and singleton terms translate the projection by a prefix-independent vector or contribute only to the discarded constant. It does not prove the same formula for the conjunction indicator, nor a lower bound for optimizing by another algorithm. For example the all-equality parity instance has a known maximizing assignment.
