# Extended resolution refutations in the ANF decision-edge calculus

Every extended-resolution refutation of an explicitly given 3-CNF translates in polynomial time into a chain of the two-direction ANF edges of anf-decision-edge.md, ending in the equation 1=0. Use ordinary resolution with fresh fan-in-two extension definitions over earlier literals; retain all proof lines even when an ER presentation deletes some. The simulation concerns chains that may introduce fresh coordinates, retain proved equations, and use a terminal contradiction. The standard ER convention is described in [Buss and Thapen](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/ResearchWeb/DRAT_PR/SATpaper.pdf).

**Primitive edge realizations.** All equations set their left-hand polynomial to zero in the Boolean quotient over F₂. To append a fresh wire e=g, the forward map fixes old coordinates and gives e the image g. Under this map the new generator e+g pulls back to zero (empty multiplier list). The reverse map projects, proving every retained generator by itself. To append a consequence d=Σ h_j g_j, use identity maps in both directions, that multiplier list for d, and unit single-row certificates for retained equations. All operations below use these two realizations.

**Clause wires.** Introduce the ER extension variables in dependency order by the first edge operation. Their gate polynomials have constant size. Fix one order of all original and ER variables, excluding subsequent helper coordinates. On the ith variable, the falsity factor z_i(C) is 1 for absence, 1+x_i for a positive literal, x_i for a negative literal, and 0 for both. Repeated literals have no effect. Introduce variables p_i(C) with defining equations h_i(C)=p_i(C)+p_{i-1}(C)z_i(C), taking p_0(C)=1. Write p_C for the final wire (or constant 1 when the order is empty). Prepare wires for all proof clauses, resolution remainders and singleton pivots needed below, and any operands used for weakening. No wide falsity polynomial needs to be expanded.

For an input clause of width at most three, also let f_i be its ordinary falsity product through position i, expanded in the Boolean quotient. It has at most eight terms. Obtain p_i+f_i from h_i+z_i(p_{i-1}+f_{i-1}), starting from zero. The input equation f_C=0 now gives p_C=0. For an extension axiom, its constant-width falsity polynomial f(e,u,v) vanishes identically at e=g(u,v). Its row follows from the gate row by the identity f(e,u,v)=(f(0,u,v)+f(1,u,v))(e+g(u,v)), obtained by separating the affine coefficient in e. The same short-prefix calculation then yields its clause wire row. This also permits constants and repeated or complementary literal inputs.

**Union identity.** For U=C∪D, successively append d_i=p_i(U)+p_i(C)p_i(D). At the initial cut d_0=0. Write s,p,q for the three previous wires and S,P,Q for the next wires. Write z_s,z_p,z_q for their local falsity factors, with z_s=z_p z_q exactly in the Boolean quotient. Then

    S+P Q = h_s + z_s d_prev + Q h_p + (p z_p) h_q.

Expanding cancels the terms containing s and Qp z_p; the remaining product pq(z_s+z_p z_q) vanishes. Thus each row is an explicit bounded-size consequence even with overlapping clauses, repeated literals or contradictory signs. For singleton literal wires the short-prefix proof above yields their final wire plus the literal's falsity indicator without assuming the singleton holds. A union certificate with a singleton therefore gives the literal insertion identity by adding p_C times this latter row.

**Resolution.** For parents L=C∪{x} and H=D∪{¬x}, let A=p_C, B=p_D, R=p_(C∪D), and abbreviate the full parent wires by L and H in the following identity. The union and literal-insertion calculations give rows d_s=R+AB, d_l=L+A(1+x), d_h=H+Bx. The previously proved parent rows are L and H. Append R via

    R = d_s + B d_l + A d_h + B L + A H.

This remains an identity if a clause is tautological, if a remainder already contains the pivot's other sign, or if the derived clause is wide. Weakening C to C∪D follows by adding p_D times the parent row p_C to the corresponding final union row. A tautological axiom of arbitrary width can be introduced independently: at its first opposite-sign position z_i=0, its gate row is p_i; propagate this zero through each later position by adding z_i times the preceding zero row to h_i. The empty clause has all falsity factors one. The short-prefix proof gives p_empty+1; together with its derived row p_empty this appends the terminal row 1.

**Size and scope.** Let L be the explicit input plus refutation length, using only the variables that occur, after relabeling if necessary. There are O(L) base variables, helper clause descriptors, and inference triples; each uses O(L) prefix positions. There are O(L²) coordinates, generators and successive edges. Every appended relation and all nontrivial multipliers have constant ANF support and bounded degree. Every forward map introduces at most one constant-size nonidentity coordinate, and old generators remain unchanged. Thus naive copying of all polynomial-size maps, systems and old-row identities at every edge stays polynomial, including the O(L²)-bit monomial masks if using dense variable masks. Direct squarefree verification of each substitution and multiplier product is also polynomial: no map composition across multiple edges is requested, and no transient expansion of a wide raw clause occurs. The translation is a refutation p-simulation; it supplies neither an efficient ER proof search nor a converse simulation of arbitrary semantic maps. A frozen subsystem that forbids these edges is outside the theorem.

The independent local checker verified the union formula on all 2,048 combinations of literal patterns and independent wire values, the resolution formula on all 64 input combinations, and the extension multiplier identities on all 24 AND-axiom cases. These checks accompany the algebraic derivation; they do not constitute a full ER parser or proof translator.
