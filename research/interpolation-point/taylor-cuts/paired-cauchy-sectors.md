# Exact aggregation of subdivision-equality choices at an October parent

In the fixed-density encoding, place the original vertices on the row side and binary subdivision equalities on the column side. Summing row bits multilinearly extends each source tensor at its kernel row forms. The column equalities identify the two column bits associated with each pre-subdivision edge. Consider a term with m row forms and m selected distinct logical column bits, and the sector in which every selected bit occurs. Its coefficient is the permanent of the m-by-m effective matrix, whose column j sums the kernel columns of its two subdivision ports (zero outside each port's packet). This sector is one part of the full Boolean moment. A map from row forms to columns contributes with multiplier 2^{m−|image|} in a moment over m free bits. In particular the unnormalized square moment equals this permanent modulo two. At higher precision the collision sectors generally survive.

Build a packet multigraph with an edge for every selected logical column, connecting its two packet endpoints; a same-packet pair gives a loop with two distinguished choices. Give packet v demand equal to its number of selected rows. By multilinearity of the permanent choose one endpoint of each column; a nonzero term requires exactly the local demand columns at each packet. Such terms split into products of single-packet Cauchy permanents. Fix the choices of all edges outside a spanning forest, including loops. Peeling leaves forces all remaining endpoint choices; reject infeasible local demands. Thus at most 2^{m−v+c} assignments require evaluation for v incident packets and c components, compared to 2^m independent choices. Each accepted assignment is evaluated via the Cauchy permanent identity. Verify isolated row packets with no incident columns separately.

There is also a dense case requiring no orientation enumeration at all. Suppose a component consists of exactly two packets and every column spans the pair. Number its column slots by y_j in the first and Ay_j+B in the second, with a fixed rational A≠0, B. A row from the first packet has entries c/(T+x−y_j); a row x from the second has entries (c/A)/((T+x−B)/A−y_j). All rows are therefore scalar multiples of one stacked Cauchy matrix, including arbitrary permutations of rows and columns. Its permanent follows from Borchardt's identity per(C)=det(C entrywise squared)/det(C). The equality here is an identity over the characteristic-zero coefficient field. With m/2 rows in either packet a single computation aggregates binom(m,m/2) balanced endpoint choices.

Use exact arithmetic before residue reduction; inverse determinants need not be units at the child precision. For numeric T with no kernel denominators zero, coincident transformed row nodes can be distinguished by adding i·z to row node i and taking the removable value at z=0. This requires polynomial bit complexity: each determinant after clearing its entry denominators is a univariate polynomial of degree O(m²); at integer evaluation points outside its poles it is computed by rational/number-field Gaussian elimination, then interpolated and divided by its common leading power of z at zero. Coefficients have polynomial bit length by determinant and denominator product bounds; the input extension is the explicit degree-O(log s) number field in the manuscript. At an original Cauchy-kernel pole in T, use the original polynomial C(T)/(C'(0)(T−y+x)) and interpolate the full scalar sector at generic T values, degree ≤m(2L−1). Reduce the resulting integral scalar at the actual node after evaluating. The affine A,B may be derived from two distinct slot pairs (with at most O(log L) bit numerators and denominators) and validated on all columns. This applies to original crossing kernels or full W factors, with exact creation-time inputs, and does not replace subsequently canonicalized child tensor entries by algebraic lifts.

For a genuine scalar control take two positive unit clauses on distinct single-occurrence variables. The degree-one variable tensors are unrestricted and disappear after summing their row bits. There remain two row forms and two logical subdivision bits. The deformed scalar is per(C)+2C11 C21+2C12 C22. This calculation isolates the precise repeated-column obstruction to applying the permanent alone to arbitrary parent values. For example if both column pairs lie wholly in one packet, with real slots 0,1 for the first and 2,3 for the second and clause rows at 0,2, the difference has nonzero leading coefficient as a polynomial of T because every effective entry has leading coefficient 2/C'(0). This local example need not be the packing chosen by the balancing algorithm. More generally the omission multiplier gives at most m powers of two per term, whereas at t=−1 the original working precision is at least 24ML/u with m≤M. Subtracting M from the weight budget alone leaves the existing Cauchy minor bound unchanged.

The results concern a coefficient sector. Obtaining all sectors or proving a favorable affine packet geometry for arbitrary source instances is a separate obligation.

A singleton bundle admits any nonzero A with B chosen to match that slot. For a fixed occupancy vector ν with |ν|=d equal to the number of chosen rows, repeat column j exactly ν_j times. Affine alignment is preserved, so a confluent Cauchy permanent divided by ∏ν_j! computes the coefficient over the exact field without any endpoint enumeration. Perturb both repeated column nodes and coincident row nodes by distinct multiples of one auxiliary z within each shore, taking the removable value at zero as before. For a product of centered linear forms in m independent signs with the full Walsh character inserted, summation selects exponents odd on all m columns and multiplies their coefficient sum by 2^m. When d=m+2h only binom(m+h−1,h) occupancy vectors survive. This statement concerns centered homogeneous linear factors. Constants introduced when centering a general deformed factor must be accounted for separately.

## A source layout obstruction to filling two-packet bundles

Let old source vertices be the two shores of a cubic bipartite edge expander on N vertices with expansion κ>0 for sets of size ≤N/2. Use one positive 3-clause per vertex on the clause shore, and one Boolean formula variable per vertex on the other. Its degree-three equality implementation is a single trivalent vertex. After source subdivision, M=3N real ports occur on the old row shore, and m=M/2 original edges become logical columns. For each packet coordinate the balance guarantee bounds each symbol class of old vertices by (3M/r)/3=3N/r<N/2. Summing edge expansion over these classes and dividing by two gives at least κN/2 old edges with differently labeled endpoints at every coordinate. Thus over all a coordinates the average number h of old-endpoint disagreements per logical column is at least κa/3. Since h≤a, at least κm/6 columns have h≥κa/6.

Fix such a column spanning packets P,Q. At a disagreeing coordinate the two unordered packet signatures have the common subdivision-column symbol as their unique intersection, because their old endpoint symbols differ. At least one signature has two distinct symbols. In each packet with a distinct pair this fixes the slot bit of all columns connected from P to Q with these signatures. Therefore at least h/2 slot bits are fixed on one shore. Distinct logical columns use distinct slots there, so the entire oriented P-to-Q bundle has capacity ≤L·2^{−h/2}. For unordered packet endpoints use a single consistent orientation from the named packet P to Q, which does not change this count. A disagreeing column cannot have both ends in the same packet because its common column symbol determines the complementary row symbol. Hence a linear fraction of logical columns of this genuine source family lies in packet pairs with at most L^{1−κ/12} columns, independent of dummy placement and of within-packet permutations. This does not exclude more general aggregation across many pairs or smaller aligned components. The monotone control CNFs are easy to satisfy.
