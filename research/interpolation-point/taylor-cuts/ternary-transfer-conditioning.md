# Conditioning a ternary full-support decision on a small column interface

Let v_1,...,v_m be explicit columns over F3. Seek signs s_j∈{1,2} such that Σs_j v_j=0. Both methods use Gaussian elimination to select basis columns and express each dependent column in them. Connect a dependent column to the basis indices with nonzero coefficients. Distinct connected blocks use disjoint basis indices; their spans form a direct sum. A zero column is a singleton block of nullity one. In a block C, once the ν_C nonbasis signs are chosen, the entire basis coefficient vector needed for a specified block target is forced. Reject assignments with any forced zero; otherwise this is an actual full-support word. There are 2^ν_C choices, regardless of block rank.

**Pin a column set T.** Delete T and compute the above blocks on the remaining original columns. For each of the 2^|T| sign words on T, demand the negative of its contribution from the remaining columns. Reject this word if the target is outside their span. Otherwise the expansion on the remaining basis splits it into one specified target per block. Each block independently finds its required signs, so successful local words and the pinned signs give a global witness. The running time is polynomial in the matrix dimensions times 2^|T| Σ_C 2^ν_C (including polynomial work when no columns remain).

**Contract a column span W.** Let W be spanned by a supplied set T, with dim W=t. Project *all* columns modulo W, retaining columns in W as quotient loops with their signs still to choose. Compute blocks in the quotient. In any solution each block's quotient target must be zero. Enumerate the at most 2^ν_C locally admissible words per block as above, retain each word's actual contribution in W, and keep one witness per distinct contribution. Join blocks by addition in W, with at most 3^t retained states. At the end accept the zero contribution and recover the stored original signs. A quotient coloop immediately rules out full support. A direct bound is polynomial in the input times Σ_C (2^ν_C+3^(2t)), using the elementary pairwise join. The methods apply to arbitrary explicitly represented F3 matrices without a graphic, cographic or regularity test. They are distinct from the study of binary basis-transfer matrices.

For a supplied interface these are fixed-parameter bounds in interface size (or contracted rank) plus maximum component nullity. With a hidden interface, try every subset of at most b columns and choose the smallest predicted bound among the two modes. This is polynomial recognition at fixed b (in particular b=1), and costs m^O(b) rather than a claimed uniform FPT search in b. A contraction space generated by some columns and of rank b has a generating subset of b columns. The solver cap bounds max(3^t,2^maxν) for contraction and 2^(|T|+maxν) for pinning; polynomial and join overhead are separate. A cap miss reports UNKNOWN.

The frame projector's transfer_rank option runs this solver on original-indexed columns after its existing projective peeling. The original reconstruction restores peeled signs, expands signed equivalences, solves for translations, and tests every graph edge. With transfer_rank unset the old solver remains the default. The chosen plan may use the empty interface.

For a connected matrix family take e_0 and all pairs e_i, e_i+e_0, i=1,...,r. Before conditioning, fundamental circuits connect all columns, and both rank and nullity grow. Contracting e_0 leaves parallel pairs and one quotient loop: every block has nullity one and the transfer dimension is one.

There is also an unbounded native source family. Take a hub triangle and m≥2 disjoint satellites, each a triangle joined to the hub by an identity three-edge matching. Use frame ordering by matching sites. For hub and satellite slopes h and s and the three nonzero oriented matching-edge differences d_0,d_1,d_2, translation elimination gives d_1−d_0=s−h and d_2−d_0=2(s−h). These are two independent rows per satellite; the columns for s,d_0,d_1,d_2 are four distinct projective points of F3². The hub column has support across every satellite plane and no pair can be peeled initially. Reordering sites or choosing opposite edge signs only reparameterizes these rows and scales columns. Pinning the hub slope makes the satellite spans independent and their nullities two. Thus one hidden sign suffices with pinning bound eight while the original rank grows as 2m. The 27-vertex instance returns a verified coloring at cap eight with transfer_rank=1, where the original capped rank search returns UNKNOWN. The example is a separation of these parameter bounds; it is also easy to color directly.

The checker covers 480 random matrix-mode comparisons and 100 graph decisions against exhaustive enumeration, plus the rank-16 abstract contraction and the native hub example.
