# Original matching width forces raw restoration rank

Let F be a CNF with primal graph G. A binary group tree partitions variables into disjoint groups of at most b bits. At an evaluation point every clause has an arbitrary owner group, including groups not containing its variables. For each occurrence of variable v in clause e assume the degree-at-most-R vector polynomial P_{e,v} has P_{e,v}(0)=e_v. In each direction across a cut, β bounds the rank of remote baseline rows, and ρ bounds the collective rank of retained remote Hasse coefficient rows of positive order. Use the same field for all rows, with well-defined coefficients.

Take any matching crossing a whole-group cut. For each edge select a clause witnessing its adjacency. Its owner is on one side, so the opposite matched variable v is remote. If orders through R are retained, the polynomial identity P(0)=sum_j (−t)^j P^[j](t) puts its remote coordinate row in the baseline-plus-correction span. Chosen matching edges have distinct endpoints, so the associated units in each direction are independent. Hence the cut matching size is at most the sum of the two directional baseline-plus-correction ranks, in particular at most 2(β+ρ).

Refine each leaf group by a binary tree on its own variables. Each new cut isolates at most b vertices. This yields a vertex branch decomposition of maximum matching width at most max(b,2(β+ρ)). For graphs with an edge, the known relation tw(G)+1≤3 mmw(G) gives a treewidth bound of three times this maximum. Thus the literal small-leaf, small-full-jet-rank compiler with b=O(log n), β+ρ=O(log n) cannot certify a raw primal graph of superlogarithmic treewidth. This is a statement about raw occurrence interfaces and does not constrain nonlinear preprocessing, efficient integration of large leaves, or other scalar representations. Source for maximum matching width: https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.02384 .

Explicit obstruction: take all negative two-variable clauses on pairs of a complete graph. For any layout with leaves of size at most 2n/3, a balanced whole-group cut has matching size at least n/3; one directional rank sum is at least n/6. For a bounded-occurrence explicit example take an m by m square grid. A balanced cut has at least m/6 pairwise disjoint crossing edges: if at least m/6 rows contain both sides, take one crossing horizontal edge per such row. Otherwise both sides have an entire row (a side occupying only mixed rows would have size less than m²/6). Each of the m columns joins these two rows and has a crossing edge. Thus rank must grow as Ω(sqrt n) on this sparse width-two CNF irrespective of clause ownership and polynomial replacement choices. The lower bound is about representations even when SAT decision on the family is immediate.

For partial jets allow changing owners at distinct t but require the same variable cut there. For each variable side form W as the sum over t of its remote-demand spans including baseline and all retained orders less than J_t. For each crossing matching edge and fixed witnessing clause, assign each t to the endpoint remote from its owner. The endpoint's row polynomial, restricted to its side and considered modulo that side's W, has a root of multiplicity at least J_t at each assigned t. If sum_t J_t>2R, one endpoint receives more than R roots counting multiplicity. Every component of its vector polynomial in the quotient vanishes identically. Its original unit row therefore lies in W. Independence on each side yields |matching|≤dim W_left +dim W_right≤2 sum_t(β_t+ρ_t). Distinct nodes in the field and restoration identities are required. The extension has no force on unrelated cuts at different sample points.

A different partial-jet argument permits unrelated trees and ownership at every node. Let D_occ be the sum of replacement degrees over literal occurrences. For multilinear input clause factors, D_occ is a sufficient (potentially loose) degree bound on their product and hence on H. If the sum of Q node precisions exceeds D_occ, choose t with J_t>D_occ/Q. Fewer than Q rows can have degree at least J_t. Delete every original variable named by such a row. For a crossing matching in the primal graph induced on remaining variables, both endpoints have all their occurrence rows fully retained; the one-point matching argument applies. Pruning deleted variable leaves of the branch tree cannot increase its maximum cut bound. Restore the fewer than Q vertices by adding them to each bag of a decomposition of the remaining graph. Thus tw(G)+1≤(Q−1)+max(1,3 max(b,2(β_t+ρ_t))). The assumptions use the sum-of-occurrence-degrees certificate. A better interpolation degree bound obtained by cancellation need not satisfy this condition.

To meet the October overview's at-most-three-occurrences assumption use two vertices H_v,V_v per grid vertex, joined by an edge; attach horizontal neighbors only to H_v and vertical ones only to V_v. The resulting graph has maximum degree three, 2m² vertices, and the entire m-grid as a minor by contracting the joining edges. Its treewidth is Ω(m). Use one negative binary clause per edge to obtain the exact source occurrence promise. The source overview (official TeX lines 3096–3102, 3191, 3585–3607) uses the 3nR upper bound on its occurrence-degree budget and QJ>3nR, so both the full-jet result when q≥R and the aggregate-budget variant have meaningful native hypotheses here.

A direct constructive proof recovers bags with no treewidth approximation. For every retained variable v take its minimal subtree T_v spanning its home and the owners of all clauses mentioning v. For each tree edge traversed by T_v some owner is across that edge from the home, hence a distinct restored e_v belongs to the remote rank space in the appropriate direction. At most 2(β+ρ) such subtrees cross any edge. Place v at all nodes in T_v. This gives connected bags covering every clause at its owner. An internal node charges its bag to its at most three incident edges; a leaf additionally has at most b private home variables. Add outlier variables everywhere. This gives the same width order constructively. The proof assumes owners are at group leaves. The most general October algebra-valued interpolation degree certificate may instead exploit a lower degree of the whole product, so the occurrence-degree argument must not be applied to it automatically.
