# Deterministic subexponential search for a prescribed ternary matching residue

On arbitrary bipartite graphs with n base pairs and labels in GF(3), a target matching or a proof of infeasibility can be obtained in n^{O(sqrt(n log(2n) loglog(4n)))} poly(input) time. This argument is independent of claimed polynomial algorithms for bipartite exact matching.

Find a base perfect matching or report infeasibility, then contract its pairs. A nonbase edge is an arc from its left endpoint's pair to its right endpoint's pair with its label minus the base label at the tail. Disjoint directed cycles specify a matching. Parallel alternatives give loops. Deduplicate equal (tail,head,gain) triples, retaining original representatives; at most 3n² arcs remain. Return the base if its label is right.

Find a shortest nonzero-gain closed walk C by shortest paths from (v,0) to (v,1) or (v,2) in the three-sheet gain lift, minimized over v. It is a simple cycle, since splitting a closed walk at a repeated internal vertex yields a shorter nonzero part. If none exists, report infeasibility. Write k for its length. If its gain equals the target difference, return its flip. If D−V(C) has another nonzero cycle, either that cycle or its union with C realizes the target difference. Otherwise X=V(C) is a portal set of size k: every exterior SCC is neutral (all its directed cycles have zero gain).

A second portal set follows from symmetric directed multicut. Split each site of the three-sheet lift into in and out vertices linked by an internal arc; transitions go from out to in. For each v require (v,0)_in and (v,1)_in in different strongly connected components. Assign length 1/k to every internal arc and zero to each transition. Each path connecting the pair in either direction projects to a nonzero closed walk and traverses at least k internal arcs. Thus LP (4) of Iwata and Yoshida is feasible at cost 3n/k. Theorem 6 deterministically rounds it to a symmetric edge multicut of cardinality O(n log(2n)loglog(4n)/k). Project each cut split arc to its original vertex and each cut transition to its original tail. A surviving nonzero cycle through v would, by repeated lifting, visit all three copies of v cyclically without using a cut arc. This contradicts separation. Use the smaller of the two valid portal sets.

For x portals enumerate participating portals and an incoming and outgoing arc at each participant, enforcing endpoint consistency. Exterior heads reached from portals are sources; exterior tails feeding portals are sinks. Drop neutral exterior cycles. Find disjoint paths from the sources to the sinks in any pairing. Process the exterior SCCs topologically. Maintain at most x pending arrivals, initially the sources, and a ternary gain. In each SCC route its pending arrivals to its fixed sinks plus chosen tails of at most x outgoing intercomponent arcs. Require equal input/output counts, distinct terminals on each side and no repeated pending head. Shared arrival/departure vertices are forced length-zero paths. Route all other terminals by unit-vertex-capacity integral flow with free pairing. Neutrality makes the internal gain a sum of terminal potentials, independent of the pairing. Add it and the chosen intercomponent gains and update arrivals. Drop neutral flow circulations. Retaining local paths and state predecessors reconstructs original edge IDs. Enumerating portal incidences, states and transitions and solving polynomial local flows takes n^{O(x+1)} time. Full routing details are in bidirected-ternary-matching.md.

Now x≤min(k,O(n log(2n)loglog(4n)/k)), proving the bound. The construction is for bipartite hosts. In general graphs a directed port walk can use both orientations of one matched pair.

Primary multicut source: Y. Iwata and Y. Yoshida, Theorem 6, https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2013.127 . Du's preprint https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01571v3 claims deterministic polynomial bipartite exact matching. If correct, that claim would imply polynomial prescribed residue search via the three-edge reduction and a polynomial number of exact red-count queries. We do not rely on it or claim priority for the present bound.
