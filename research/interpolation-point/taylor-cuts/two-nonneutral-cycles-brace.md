# Two gain-changing alternating cycles are hard even when every residue is explicit

Input: a simple non-Pfaffian bipartite brace with a specified perfect matching M and scalar F₃ edge gains. Deciding whether two vertex-disjoint M-alternating cycles each have nonzero gain change is NP-complete even with only two nonzero edges, both outside M. The theorem concerns cycles relative to the supplied M.

Reduce directed vertex-disjoint paths for two given ordered pairs (s₁,t₁),(s₂,t₂), with four distinct terminals, the NP-complete fixed directed two-linkage problem of Fortune, Hopcroft and Wyllie, https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(80)90009-2 . Start with the input loopless digraph and three new vertices a,b,c forming a bidirected triangle, initially isolated from the input. Add all nonloop arcs from every vertex to each s_i and from each t_i to every vertex. Add s₁→t₂ and s₂→t₁. Mark exactly t₁→s₁ and t₂→s₂ with weight one; these arcs exist by construction. Keep a single copy per ordered pair and set all other weights to zero.

Two vertex-disjoint nonzero directed cycles must each use exactly one marked arc. Removing them gives disjoint prescribed simple paths. No path can use an added arc into a source or out of a sink: both paths use their own terminals as endpoints and cannot visit the other path's terminals. Neither can use a crossed source-to-sink arc for the same reason. Dummy vertices are inaccessible before a terminal sink and cannot furnish any detour. The resulting paths thus use original input arcs only. Conversely two input paths close by the marked returns to produce two disjoint nonzero cycles.

After deleting at most one vertex, at least one of (s₁,t₂) and (s₂,t₁) stays intact. Its crossed arc s_i→t_j gives routes from every surviving vertex into s_i and thence from t_j to every surviving vertex (omit trivial first/last steps). Thus the augmented digraph is strongly two-vertex-connected. Replace each vertex v by the matched diagonal L_v R_v of weight zero and each arc u→v by the edge L_u R_v of the indicated weight. Directed simple cycles correspond to physical M-alternating cycles; disjointness and gain increments are preserved. For every nonempty set S of at most n−2 indices, at least two external indices receive arcs from S: otherwise deleting its sole external out-neighbor (or none if empty) would leave nonempty exterior unreachable. Hence |N_G(L_S)|≥|S|+2. For n−1 left indices the neighborhood is all n columns since each right vertex has degree at least three by two-strong connectivity. Hall's theorem now extends any two disjoint edges to a perfect matching by deleting their four endpoints. The graph is connected and is a bipartite brace (standard 2-extendibility characterization). On the three dummy row/column pairs one has all of K₃,₃, and the other diagonal edges give a perfect matching of its complement. A Pfaffian orientation would restrict to one of this conformal K₃,₃, impossible because the six permutation-sign equations multiply to contradictory signs. Membership in NP follows by direct cycle replay. The construction is polynomial and simple.

Nevertheless every reduced instance has explicit matching witnesses for all three scalar residues. M attains zero. The directed triangle t₁→s₁→t₂→t₁ has gain one, and t₁→s₁→t₂→s₂→t₁ has gain two. Flip the corresponding single physical cycle in each case. In an arbitrary instance with just two marked edges and zero baseline, requiring total two merely requires the ordinary matching to contain both, regardless of its cycle decomposition. The reduction therefore supplies no hardness result for prescribed-residue matching.

Absence of the two-cycle certificate does not imply a single vertex meeting all gain-changing cycles. On K_{n,n}, n≥4, with its diagonal matching, mark exactly all six off-diagonal arcs among three specified indices. Every changing directed cycle contains at least two of these three indices, precluding a disjoint changing pair. The three directed 2-cycles on their pairs all have gain two and have empty common vertex intersection. The identity, a cycle using one marked arc and one outside index, and an internal 2-cycle give residues zero, one, two respectively.
