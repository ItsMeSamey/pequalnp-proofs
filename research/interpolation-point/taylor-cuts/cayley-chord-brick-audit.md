# Bounded-degree bricks with long chord exchanges

Fix an odd prime p, a fixed positive integer C and k=4p^C. In Z_k times SL2(F_p) use s=(1,A), t=(1,B), for upper and lower elementary unipotents with off-diagonal entry 2, respectively. Let Γ be the subgroup they generate, of order n. For each g create L_g,R_g and join L_g to R_g,R_gs,R_gt. This is simple cubic bipartite. Its layer is the Z_k coordinate, surjective on Γ. Add one left-left edge with endpoint layers 0,k/4 and one right-right edge with endpoint layers k/2,3k/4.

**Elementary expansion.** In the Cayley digraph generated positively by s,t, every nonempty set S of size at most n−2 has |S∪Ss∪St|≥|S|+2. Suppose the contrary. Strong connectivity follows from finite generation and finiteness. Thus the external boundary of a violating set has size one. Its nonempty exterior has boundary one in the reverse digraph. Choose a minimum cardinality one-boundary set H with nonempty exterior over both orientations; h=|H|≤(n−1)/2. In its chosen orientation, f(X)=|N[X]|−|X| is submodular (a union-of-neighborhoods size minus a modular function). For two intersecting distinct left translates H,J, their union has size at most 2h−1≤n−2. Both union and intersection have f≥1 by strong connectivity, so submodularity forces both to have f=1. Their intersection has nonempty exterior and is strictly smaller, contradiction. Translate H to contain the identity. Each h' in H now has h'H=H by intersection, so H is a subgroup. Its closed neighborhood is a union of right cosets of H, so boundary size one forces |H|=1. A singleton has two distinct external neighbors in either orientation. This establishes the bound, also for the reverse orientation.

**Brick.** The same expansion is the full left-to-right bipartite neighborhood bound, with its transpose on the right. Deleting two vertices preserves connectivity. Orient the shore names so at least one deletion is on the left. Each component still meets both shores since the original graph is cubic. With two or more components, its left shore S has size at most n−2. Each component's right shore has size at least |S|+2−d_R, where d_R≤1 counts right deletions. Summing over components contradicts the total shore sizes. This proves 3-connectivity of the initial connected graph, and adding chords preserves it. Deleting j vertices from each shore, j=1 or 2, leaves a perfect matching by Hall: for 1≤|S|≤n−2 expansion suffices, and for |S|=n−1 all n right vertices have a neighbor by cubicity. After deleting two vertices of the same shore, use the chord on the other shore and apply the j=2 result. Thus the augmented graph is bicritical and 3-connected, hence a brick of maximum degree four.

**Girth.** A simple cycle in the original bipartite graph produces a closed word with exponents alternating between plus and minus, where identity edges contribute no letters. Two identity edges cannot be consecutive. Consecutive retained letters cannot cancel: either they were consecutive physical edges and cancellation would immediately backtrack, or they flank one erased identity edge and have the same exponent sign. A nonempty reduced word survives and its evaluation modulo p is the identity. Over the integers any nonempty reduced word in A,B is nontrivial. Indeed, act on the complex vector (1,i). Its rightmost nonzero power of A or B makes the corresponding coordinate strictly larger in modulus. Each successive syllable makes the opposite coordinate dominant by |2m v+w|≥2|v|−|w|>|v|. An identity matrix would preserve equal coordinate moduli. The maximum row-sum norm of each letter is 3, so a length-ℓ relation modulo p requires p≤3^ℓ+1. Therefore ℓ≥log_3(p−1).

Any simple cycle containing a chord has a chord-free segment between distinct chord endpoints. Their layers have cyclic distance at least k/4, and every original edge changes layer by at most one. Thus the cycle length is at least k/4+1. The augmented order N=2n≤2k p(p²−1), giving girth Ω(log N) and a chord-containing cycle length lower bound Ω(N^{C/(C+3)}). Both chords can occur simultaneously in a matching by the balanced two-deletion result. With the identity base matching, their symmetric difference with this matching has an alternating cycle containing both, since each separately flipped cycle preserves balance of the numbers of same-side selected edges.
