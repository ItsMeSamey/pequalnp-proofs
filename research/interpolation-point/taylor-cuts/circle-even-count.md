# Exact counting on circle graphs already captures Eulerian orientations

Given an undirected 4-regular graph D, compute an Euler tour separately in each nonempty component. Its cyclic vertex word has two occurrences per original vertex, hence defines a circle graph. For a component with zero marks and no syndrome requirements, give each arc the reference direction of the tour. Reverse an arc iff its binary variable f is one. The new orientation is Eulerian exactly when the reversed arcs balance at every vertex, precisely the unmarked integer balance condition in circle-graph-parity-matching.md. There are two complementary f assignments for each induced all-even set in a nonempty component. Thus the number of Eulerian orientations of D is 2^c times the number of induced all-even vertex sets in the disjoint union of its component circle graphs, where c is the number of nonempty components. Concatenate the component words to present that disjoint union. Parallel edges of D produce distinct arcs, and the chord intersection graph is simple.

Huang and Lu prove that counting Eulerian orientations of 4-regular graphs is #P-hard (Theorem C.6 in the linked author version). Hence exact counting of induced all-even vertex sets on circle graphs is #P-hard under polynomial-time counting reductions even when the chord word is supplied. The predicate on a specified vertex subset is polynomial-time verifiable. No parity rows, dense side conditions or marked vertices are needed for this counting statement. [Huang and Lu, A Dichotomy for Real Weighted Holant Problems, Appendix C](https://www.csc.kth.se/~sangxia/papers/2012-ccc.pdf)
