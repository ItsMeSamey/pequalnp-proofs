# Decision by affine matching-gain orbits

Let G be a loopless multigraph with gains in V=F3^b and original edge IDs. Suppose a vertex permutation σ maps each endpoint-pair multiplicity table to its translate by p_u+p_v. Map the kth original edge in each gain/endpoints cell to the kth edge in its image. Every matching gain changes by t=sum p_u. In this note matching means perfect matching. Composition adds these shifts. Compute the span T of generator shifts. A quotient witness at the requested coset is repaired using each selected generator 0,1,2 times to realize the exact gain difference.

For supplied σ test the support and on each pair compare translated tables by trying image gains of one fixed source gain. Feasible translations form an empty set or a coset of the local period subspace. Solve all resulting affine equations on p_u+p_v, then project by summation. This works in polynomial time in explicit input, including growing b. The identity yields T_id. A vertex permutation of order prime to 3 contributes nothing outside T_id by powering.

For complete switching discovery distinguish centers and states (u,a), join each center to its fibre, and add directed coordinate-coloured arcs (u,a)→(u,a+e_i). Across fibres add one link for states (u,a),(v,c) with colour equal to the positive multiplicity of original gain a+c. The marked Cayley arcs force all automorphisms to be translations inside each permuted fibre; cross links impose exactly the table condition. Graph-isomorphism self-reduction computes generators by pinning a stabilizer chain and recovering possible point images. The expanded size is polynomial in explicit input and 3^b. Directed links and colours can be encoded with distinguished local incidence roles. Babai gives a quasipolynomial clock in expanded size; Luks gives polynomial at fixed b and bounded underlying degree. The native switching solver instead accepts supplied vertex permutations. See https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03547 and https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(82)90009-5.

More generally use invertible affine actions g(σM)=A g(M)+t, with original graph-automorphism witnesses. The edgewise switching test compares A times source gains with image gains. For fixed b enumerate GL(b,3) and find one switching isomorphism per viable A between the A-relabelled and original coloured graphs; add generators for the identity linear part. Alternatively a supplied edge bijection already proved to preserve matchings can be checked semantically by computing the affine span of the paired matching gains (g(M),g(σM)) and checking containment in the proposed affine graph. The finite-field matching-incidence span, homogenized by a vertex star or translated by a witnessed baseline matching, suffices. First test ordinary matching existence and treat empty graph directly.

For target t0 compute its orbit O under the certified group, with edge-permutation words for traceback. If c+U is ANY affine flat contained in O, original feasibility at t0 equals matching feasibility with gain in c+U. A witness at t0 can be transported to c; any witness in the flat can be transported back to t0. This costs one rank b−dim U query, with no global period required. One may enumerate flats through t0 since every flat contained in O can be transported to one through t0. Enumeration costs 3^{O(b²)} outside the matching and automorphism clocks. For scalar b=1 a two-element target orbit permits a stronger polynomial algorithm: use matching-span avoidance to find a matching whose gain differs from the third element, or prove none exists. For any b, if H=affspan(V minus O) is proper, any witness outside H solves the target; if none exists then all gains lie in H, giving a lower-dimensional problem if t0 lies there and immediate rejection otherwise. Empty complement merely requires an ordinary matching.

Strict brick control: K24 has one hub and 23 outer vertices indexed by F3³ minus the line (x,1,0) and singleton 000. Spoke gains are the indices, other gains zero. Every spoke extends to a matching, giving exactly these 23 gains. All scalar projections are full and no nonzero translation preserves the profile (23 is not divisible by 3). The shear (x,y,z)→(x+y,y,z) permutes spokes and matching gains; target (0,1,0) has the excluded line as its orbit, reducing the genuine missing-target query from rank three to rank two. All nonzero alternating exchanges must contain the hub and its matched edge. None of these criteria promises a rank drop on every ordinary graph.
