# A treewidth obstruction for native-coordinate grouped cost leaves

Fix a simple bounded-degree N-vertex edge expander G of degree at most Δ. Give each edge a positive clause x_u∨x_v. On two fresh variables y,z include the four binary clauses forbidding their four assignments and attach the positive bridge x_r∨y at one expander vertex r. The entire CNF is connected, has bounded occurrence and is unsatisfiable. Scores are ordinary sums of violated-clause indicators. The constant-size contradictory gadget deliberately separates the width obstruction from global decision complexity.

Partition the original clause multiset into disjoint groups and put those groups at the leaves of any subcubic tree. For each tree edge take the intersection W of the dual spaces spanned on its two shores by the original cost groups' semantic GF(2) linear interfaces. Let w be the largest such dimension. The conclusion also holds when each shore's interface is recomputed directly from the additive sum of its original scores. It concerns interfaces computed before private minimization.

**Physical shared vertices.** Put σ_v=(−1)^x_v. A positive clause contributes (1+σ_u)(1+σ_v)/4 to the score. In every group and every shore, an expander vertex touched by an edge there has a strictly positive singleton Walsh coefficient; the bridge has the same sign, and no gadget clause touches an expander vertex. A frequency with nonzero Walsh coefficient must belong to every dual linear space determining the function, since all periods annihilate it. Thus each expander vertex incident to expander edges on both shores contributes its unit coordinate independently to W. The physical middle set at each cut has size at most w. A private expander vertex appears nowhere on the opposite shore except possibly r through the bridge; dropping r from the local analysis and adding it to every final bag costs one.

**Leaf cut bound.** For a group, pin its shared expander coordinates and consider the private expander graph formed by its edges on remaining private vertices other than r. Such vertices do not occur in outside costs. No interface pin with the outside can involve them, since every determining frequency on the outside has zero coefficients there when using its minimal space. The bridge and all gadget variables can be separated from the private expander coordinates by dropping r. Any remaining affine pin on the gadget leaves contributes no interaction with the private graph. Use the order of private expander coordinates induced by the native leaf order.

At any such cut with E crossing private edges select an induced crossing matching of size k≥E/[2Δ(Δ+1)] by greedy deletion. A cut in the full leaf order realizing the projected cut always exists. Project the residual functions onto the singleton Walsh coefficients of the matched suffix coordinates. Terms with both endpoints in the suffix contribute a constant translation to this projection. The varying part is the sum of the crossing adjacency rows, scaled by the independent prefix spins and 1/4. On the k chosen prefix rows and suffix columns the matrix is diagonal with nonzero diagonal. More generally all other prefix spins affecting this projection are independent native bits or pinned constants; dropping r eliminates possible correlations with a gadget chart, and shared expander vertices are pinned individually. The projected hull is a translated zonotope of dimension at least k. A zonotope with k independent generators has at least 2^k exposed vertices: choose each strict sign pattern on the independent generators, perturb off all remaining nonzero generator hyperplanes, and compare maximizing endpoints. A common maximizing sum cannot change a chosen generator's sign, since in that representation each term must maximize separately. The full residual hull has at least as many vertices as its projection. For exact residual or affine-output classes, holding the other prefix spins fixed yields all 2^k distinct projected box corners, at most two per affine-output class. Thus for either leaf budget H one has E≤2Δ(Δ+1)log₂(2H). Here H is the actual peak over all native-order cuts for the method used by that leaf, on the relevant pinned fiber. An additive objective depending on disjoint non-expander variables shifts only the discarded constant coefficient.

**Tree decomposition.** From the native order v_1,...,v_m of the private graph form a path bag at i consisting of v_i and earlier vertices with a neighbor at or after i. The size is at most one plus the crossing-edge bound at cut i−1. Every private edge is covered and each private vertex has consecutive occurrences. Add the group leaf's physical middle set to every bag (use a single bag when there are no private vertices). This covers private-to-shared and shared-to-shared edges assigned to that group. Treat r as removed for now. At every internal tree node install a bag equal to the union of the at most three incident physical middle sets, of size at most 3w. Attach the leaf path to its neighboring tree node through any one of its bags. For a vertex appearing at several group leaves, the cuts on which it is in the middle set are exactly the edges of the minimal subtree connecting those leaves. Its internal occurrences and its full paths at incident leaves are therefore connected. Now add r to every bag to cover all incidences with r; its neighbors already occur in bags. Isolated vertices can be given singleton bags.

It follows that tw(G)≤max{3w,w+1+2Δ(Δ+1)log₂(2H)}+1 with ample slack. Bounded-degree expanders have linear treewidth: a centroid bag of a tree decomposition balances unit vertex weights, so removing the bag leaves components of size at most N/2. Unless the bag has size at least N/4, one can union components to a set of size between N/4 and N/2 (take any component in that range or greedily combine smaller ones). All crossing edges meet the bag, so expansion forces its size to be Ω(N). Consequently w+log₂(2H)=Ω(N) for this fixed-degree family, however clauses are grouped and the tree and native orders are chosen.

Arbitrary invertible leaf charts, projected widths of previously minimized child messages, assignment-wise one-sided dominance rules, removal of the contradiction before grouping, and global implication solving are outside this result. In particular the attached gadget itself has a constant-size refutation. The lower bound is for the stated raw-cost portfolio.

For precision about r in the leaf cut argument, removing r from the graph in the decomposition does not erase the original score terms incident to it. If the score on each shore touches r, positivity of its singleton on both shores puts e_r in W, individually fixing it. If only the current leaf touches it, the opposite minimal dual space has no r component, so r remains an ordinary unrestricted native bit. Thus its effect on a private suffix singleton is either a fixed translation (pinned r), an extra independent generator (free prefix r), or zero from the mixed term (free suffix r). Gadget-only terms never use a selected private suffix variable. These facts justify the zonotope projection without supposing that an arbitrary prefix restriction preserves the original hull vertex count.
