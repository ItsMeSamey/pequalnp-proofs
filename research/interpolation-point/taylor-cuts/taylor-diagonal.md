# Batching Taylor corrections by exact linear-statistic moments

In the October paper's overview, fix an evaluation point t, J=q+1, and one consistent ordinary measurement tuple. Each correction to a substituted form is a positive-z-order vector polynomial dotted with the formal input. Collect its coefficient rows for orders 1 through q across all forms. By Gaussian elimination choose independent rows in increasing order, naming their scalar linear statistics ℓ_j(x) and birth orders e_j. An order-a coefficient row lies in the span of statistics born at orders at most a. Thus replacing ℓ_j(x) by formal y_j gives entries whose y_j coefficient is divisible by z^{e_j}. All arithmetic here is over Q[z]/(z^J).

Keeping the y formal in the paper's separated identity, denote the local factors by F_g(x_g;y,z). The globally substituted factor is their product after setting y=ℓ(x). Each monomial y^α of weighted degree e·α≥J has zero coefficient. Let B contain the α of weighted degree <J. On each group form S_g=Σ_{allowed x_g} F_g(x_g;y,z) exp(u·ℓ_g(x_g)), where ℓ_g denotes that group's contribution to the global linear statistics. Truncate each of the y and u monomials independently to B. This is quotient-ring multiplication since the excluded sets are upward closed. Multiply the S_g, and return the sum over α in B of (product_j α_j!) times the coefficient of y^α u^α. Upon expanding over x, the exponentials multiply to exp(u·ℓ(x)); its u^α coefficient is ℓ(x)^α divided by the same multifactorial. The extracted polynomial therefore equals the desired Taylor contribution after actual substitution. Sum over the original measurement tuples and perform the original interpolation. This computes all Taylor orders simultaneously and avoids the two subset enumerations in the paper's overview Taylor recovery identity.

The ring needs at most J|B|² scalar entries. All coefficients have polynomial bit length for the paper's polynomial-bit inputs and polynomial parameters: elimination uses determinant-size rational coordinates, factorial denominators are polynomial in bit length, and the degree, number of local factors and input bit sizes are polynomial. Original measurement and group enumeration costs remain. Exact station/group evaluation, the fixed baseline fibers, and the birth divisibility are required. A single binary-weighted statistic can have exponentially many distinct values while B has J members.

All birth orders are at most the actual replacement degree R. Consequently |B|≥binom(d+floor(q/R),d), where d is joint correction rank. In the overview parameter regime q=floor(3nR/Q) and Q of order log n/loglog n, so q/R=n^{1−o(1)}. A polynomial full-basis bound in this regime requires bounded d. As a separate universal basis limitation, use d disjoint sets of s bits and their population counts. They realize {0,…,s}^d, on which polynomials of degree at most s form a function space of dimension binom(d+s,d), by univariate interpolation in each coordinate. This applies when ds≤n and concerns representation of arbitrary moment functions, not arbitrary algorithms for the original scalar.
