# Signed-CNF isomorphism in the matching-first flow family

Use the mod-three two-cycle construction of decision-cache-mod3.md with n≥5, all top charges zero, and bottom charges summing to one. Keep each forbidden corner clause on its entire local scope. After a partial matching assignment the local scope is four bits if the matching bit is assigned and five otherwise. Each scope still occurs, and the remaining clauses on it are exactly the forbidden local corners.

**Scope reconstruction.** Treat distinct clause supports as vertices. Group variables by the collection of supports in which they occur. The two bits of each trit have identical collections consisting of the two incident horizontal scopes. No other variable has that same collection. Each remaining matching bit has a singleton variable class, with its two incident vertical scopes. This follows from simplicity of the prism and from the complete forbidden-corner encoding at every scope. Consequently every signed literal isomorphism sends remaining matching bits to remaining matching bits, and trit blocks to trit blocks. At a full matching assignment the horizontal adjacency graph consists of two n-cycles, recoverable from these incidences alone.

**Distinguished code.** Read the relation on each scope by complementing its list of forbidden corners. Projecting the relation onto either of its trit blocks yields exactly {00,01,10}. The unique excluded word 11 must be mapped to 11 in the target block. A signed coordinate permutation preserves bitwise complement, so it maps 00 to 00 as well. At an endpoint mark every vertex whose local relation contains the pair (00,00). An isomorphism preserves these marks. On the top cycle the marks recover exactly the zero entries of the matching assignment since the top charge is zero. Given any endpoint type, its two marked cycles permit at most 2·2n choices of a cycle, orientation, and starting position to read the labeled top pattern. Thus at most 4n assignments realize any endpoint type.

**Early sharing.** For an intermediate state let E denote the set of signed-isomorphism types obtained after assigning every remaining matching bit. An isomorphism maps all such completions bijectively and preserves E, including when the isomorphism flips signs of some remaining matching bits. E is the union of the corresponding sets for the two children for any choice of a remaining matching bit. Induction on the number of remaining bits shows that a completed exhaustive staged decision search, with sharing by signed-CNF isomorphism, reaches representatives of every member of E. This works even if representatives choose different orders within the matching stage. All branches are unsatisfiable, and the specified search has no earlier contradiction under ordinary empty-clause, unit, or exact GF(2) row-space constant tests, as proved in the companion note. There are at least 2^n/(4n) distinct reachable endpoint keys. If identical answers at trivial leaves are deliberately left uncached, the same count bounds endpoint visits, while their binary predecessors number at least half as many.

The signed key concerns the explicitly restricted CNF before any presentation-changing algebraic row transformations. The GF(2) constant test can be run separately. Semantic equivalence, canonicalization under general permutations of the three trit symbols, and modular-flow solving have different capabilities.

**Finite check.** With bottom charge one at vertex 0 and zero elsewhere, orient top-to-bottom matching and use outgoing-minus-incoming balance. On the bottom at position i the horizontal shift is charge_i+a_i. For n=5 through 10 the numbers of distinct unordered pairs of dihedral binary mark patterns were respectively 17, 32, 53, 101, 180, and 349. Maximum numbers of assignments per such invariant were respectively 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, and 14. This checks the mark invariant, which can identify more patterns than signed CNF isomorphism does.

## Extension to signed isomorphism of the GF(2) row space

Use the equivalence that maps entire ANF row spaces by permuting bit variables and independently substituting x+1 for x. The endpoint lower bound above extends to this equivalence using the following reconstruction, which does not depend on the appearance of an echelon basis.

If any matching bits remain, project the space onto its homogeneous degree-five part. It is exactly the coordinate span of monomials of the remaining five-bit vertex scopes: each width-five corner indicator has just its scope monomial in degree five, and each such scope has a forbidden corner. Under signed permutations this top-degree projection is carried to its coordinate permutation. The support-incidence signatures for just these scopes intrinsically identify matching variables as the classes consisting of one bit. Each remaining matching bit lies in its distinct pair of vertical scopes; every horizontal bit still has its partner bit with the same signature, even if both signatures are empty. A horizontal signature cannot equal a vertical pair since a horizontal edge stays in one cycle. Thus isomorphisms of intermediate spaces preserve the unassigned matching set. Nonterminal matching stages also have a nonzero degree-five projection, distinguishing them from endpoints.

At an endpoint, use the homogeneous degree-four projection to recover all four-bit vertex scopes by the same argument. Within each recovered scope every forbidden corner indicator belongs to the row space. For a legal corner indicator the local witness L_a from the companion note takes value 1, proving nonmembership. Thus the row space intrinsically recovers the legal relation at every scope. Signed-variable isomorphisms carry corner indicators to corner indicators, hence induce signed-CNF isomorphisms at endpoints. Restriction commutes with the isomorphisms when matched variables are fixed to corresponding, possibly complemented, values. The endpoint coverage induction therefore applies unchanged, giving at least 2^n/(4n) endpoint types for signed-row-space sharing under the stated stopping policy.
