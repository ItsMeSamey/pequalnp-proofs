# Walsh violation energy and a regular augmented matrix

Expand the total number of violated clauses as E(x)=a0+Σ a_i (−1)^(v_i·x). Group equal characters, remove zero coefficients and separate the constant. For width k the expansion takes O(m 2^k poly(input)) time; scaling by 2^k gives integers with O(k+log m) bits for unit clauses. With V consisting of rows v_i, t_i=[a_i>0], w_i=|a_i|, we have E(x)=a0−Σw_i+2Σw_i d_i where d=Vx+t over F₂. Compute H with ker H=im V. Attainable deviations are exactly Hd=Ht.

Supply p marked columns U satisfying U1=Ht and regularity of [H|U]. Check this in polynomial time, obtaining a labeled TU representation [D|J] of the same matroid (Seymour, https://web.math.princeton.edu/~pds/papers/regular/paper.pdf). Modulo two its nullspace agrees with that of [H|U] since the circuit supports span the binary cycle space. Taking U=[Ht] gives an intrinsic test. For p≥1 enumerate the 2^(p−1) sign vectors epsilon with first entry +1. Minimize w·(u+v) subject to D(u−v)=−J epsilon, 0≤u,v≤1. TU gives an integral optimal vertex when feasible. Reduction modulo two gives a feasible d of no greater cost. Conversely (d,1) for any feasible d is a disjoint union of circuits, each of which has an integer dependence with nonzero entries ±1 by Cramer's rule and total unimodularity. Sum the disjoint lifts and choose global sign, obtaining the same objective in one enumerated LP. Thus an optimal reachable deviation is recovered. For p=1 this is a classical minimum-weight circuit through one element, loops allowed. For p>1 an optimal circuit union may be required. For p=0 the syndrome is zero. All LP right-hand sides are bounded in magnitude by p and exact LP bit costs are polynomial. For minimum circuit LP precedent see https://www.research.unipd.it/bitstream/11577/3421196/7/Submitted_RegularMatroids.pdf .

Apply the displayed offset and factor two to the optimal cost. Zero is equivalent to satisfiability. Solve Vx=d+t and check the original formula to recover a witness. This computes a minimum, not a model count. The recognizable certificate gives polynomial time for supplied p=O(log input). A regular homogeneous matroid alone is insufficient: nearest word to all ones in a graph cut space yields |E|−MaxCut. One ternary clause has all seven nonzero characters on its 3-plane, giving Fourier column matroid F7 and a nonregular dual F7* parity-check matroid. Adding marked columns cannot repair a nonregular restriction. Therefore an unrestricted algorithm requires a different representation or an independently justified restriction scheme.

## A nonaffine control

Take four bits specifying the northwest 2-by-2 entries of a 3-by-3 binary array z. Fill the other five entries uniquely so all six row and column parities are odd. For each row and column prohibit all three entries being one. Encode each prohibition by listing its forbidden four-bit seed assignments as ordinary width-four clauses, retaining clause occurrences across prohibitions when computing energy. The total number of violations is |z|−3, its nine Fourier deviations are the nine entries z, and its six satisfying words encode the perfect matchings of K3,3. H is the incidence matrix of K3,3 with one row removed; the single marked column is all ones. This extension is the regular R10. In its standard representation [I5|C], C has diagonal −1 and cyclic-neighbor entries +1. An explicit binary isomorphism sends basis columns to masks (1,2,9,20,17) under the vertex numbering in check_walsh_regular.py. That checker tests all square minors of C, the isomorphism, all 16 energies and 120 small marked LP optima against brute enumeration. The LP implementation in the checker uses numerical optimization for these fixtures; the general theorem uses exact rational LP. Matching itself already has a polynomial algorithm.
