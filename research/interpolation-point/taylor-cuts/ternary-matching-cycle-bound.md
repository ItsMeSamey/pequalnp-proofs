# A sharp bound on switches from a specified perfect matching

Fix an undirected loopless multigraph, a perfect matching M, and additive labels in G=F₃^b. The gain of an M-alternating cycle is the sum of its non-M labels minus its M labels. Distinct parallel options may form a length-two cycle. If a prescribed label sum is feasible then it is realized by switching at most 2b vertex-disjoint M-alternating cycles. Indeed choose a feasible matching minimizing its symmetric-difference edge count from M. Its difference is a disjoint union of alternating cycles. Undoing any nonempty zero-sum subset would retain feasibility and shorten the difference. The sequence of cycle gains is therefore zero-sum-free, including singletons.

For a self-contained form of the Davenport bound, in F₃[G] the augmentation ideal is generated by y_i=[e_i]−1, with y_i³=0. Every product of 2b+1 such ideal elements vanishes by total degree. If g_1,...,g_{2b+1} had no nonempty zero-sum subsequence, the identity coefficient of the vanishing product Π_j(1−[g_j]) would instead be one, from its empty choice. The sequence listing each basis vector twice is zero-sum-free, so this length bound is exact. The proof also applies with b replaced by the dimension of any known subspace containing all gains. [Olson, A combinatorial problem on finite Abelian groups, I](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0022314X69900213).

For sharpness take two disjoint four-cycles per coordinate and the alternating baseline matching on each. Label just one nonbaseline edge in each of the two coordinate-i gadgets by e_i. The target (2,...,2) requires both switches in every coordinate, including two switches for rank one. If desired join the gadgets in a chain by single bridges: each bridge separates an even union of complete four-cycles from its complement, so no perfect matching uses it. The connected instances have the same certificate lower bound. The result bounds the number of switches, not their lengths: a single even cycle can be arbitrarily long.

Neither unrestricted sums nor linear spans of available cycle gains describe the feasible sums. In a bipartite three-by-three instance include the identity matching and the four off-diagonal edges for swaps 12 and 13 only. Its matchings are the identity and the two swaps. Give each swap gain one in F₃ (e.g. label (1,2) and (1,3) by one and every other edge by zero). Two different elementary cycles supply one each, but both use the baseline edge (1,1); gain two is infeasible. Even the single-cycle example prevents reusing a gain an arbitrary scalar number of times.

**Discovery.** The existing ternary_labelled_matching.py evaluates the characteristic-two matching Pfaffian over the 3^b characters. Independent field variables for the edges prevent cancellation between different matching monomials. Fourier projection selects the target. A nonzero evaluation permits vertex-by-vertex witness extraction using the same random weights, with no extra failure probability: the nonzero target sum has a nonzero summand among choices for any fixed vertex. The zero outcome is not an impossibility certificate. With field size q≥n on n vertices the polynomial degree is n/2, so an existing target yields a nonzero test with probability at least 1/2 per independent evaluation. Running time is 3^b polynomial(n,|E|,b) per trial, including extraction, and the final witness is directly validated. The equivalent symmetric Tutte determinant has doubled gains and degree n, requiring projection onto twice the target and a suitably larger field for the same failure bound.

Given any target witness, ternary_matching_cycles.compress computes its alternating components, discards zero gains and repeatedly finds a nonempty zero-sum subset among the first 2b+1 remaining gains by a 3^b-state subset dynamic program. Undoing that subset preserves the witness's target. This gives a deterministic fixed-parameter postprocessor for the promised short switch certificate without searching all simple cycles. The Davenport bound alone provides no deterministic fixed-parameter method for finding a target witness from scratch. A graph that admits a supplied Pfaffian orientation allows deterministic exact Fourier counting by summing same-sign matching monomials via Pfaffians over the Eisenstein integers and dividing the inverse transform by 3^b. This extra orientation hypothesis is essential to that counting argument.
