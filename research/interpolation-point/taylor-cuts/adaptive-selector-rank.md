# Recognizing negative selector trees without a depth bound

Use as base NO tests ordinary infeasibility, the rational two-face binary-gain certificate and zero verdicts from the anchored near-perfect quadratic correction. All use polynomial time with finite-field linear matching span. These certificates are hereditary under both physical edge literals. An accepted two-face test furnishes a real affine lift on every perfect matching; this specializes to any restriction, where the base solver succeeds including the constant case. For the correction, edge deletion restricts both perfect and near-perfect families. For forcing an edge, delete its endpoints, insert its fixed gain and set competing edge indicators to zero. Every residual near-perfect matching extends to an old near-perfect matching by the pinned edge. Global and literal-conditioned affine identities specialize to valid identities of the corresponding smaller families; a literal fixed by the pins gives either zero or a global identity. Nonextendible pairs specialize to nonextendible pairs, excluded literals, or zero. Thus a successful target-free correction remains successful. Empty graphs are direct.

Use physical edge decisions, rank-zero certified leaves, and Horton–Strahler rank: unequal child ranks give their maximum, equal ranks increase by one. Let C_s be the instances with such a NO tree of rank ≤s. Restricting a tree specializes its leaves, suppresses resolved decisions, and cannot increase rank. For s≥1 any nonleaf of a rank-≤s tree has at least one child of rank ≤s−1.

Try the base test. At rank zero return its verdict. Otherwise scan unassigned edges and both literals for a child admitting a C_{s−1} certificate by this recursive algorithm. If no child succeeds return UNKNOWN. Keep the child's certificate, continue on the other branch at rank s, and repeat until closure or failure. This builds a replayable tree of rank ≤s. Conversely, if the current state is C_s and has no base certificate, a qualifying child must exist. Any choice leaves a sibling still in C_s by heredity. Induction on rank and residual variable count proves completeness. At most m peels each scan at most 2m rank-(s−1) subcalls, giving (m+1)^{O(s)}poly time, inclusive of certificate construction. Rank one recognizes arbitrarily long decision lists without knowing their variable order. Polynomial-size trees in general can have logarithmic rank, yielding only a quasipolynomial recognition bound here.

Discarding a certified negative child is sound on arbitrary inputs. Recognition failure does not certify that a target matching exists. Under a promise that all target-free restrictions belong to C_s, ordinary edge-deletion self-reduction yields an original witness. Outside that promise check the candidate and return UNKNOWN if it fails.

For the four-clique selector port-replica families, each physical perfect matching projects to one external port per original vertex. Scan every replica of the macro selector edge. Its first selected copy implies marked-edge count zero or four. If all are absent the count is one or three. Both branches give real affine lifts of their normalized gain profile. This gives rank one even for arbitrarily large mandatory physical depth. Depth alone is insufficient for a tree size or time lower bound.
