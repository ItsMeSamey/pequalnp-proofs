# Inflated three-pool brick with automatic near-barrier

Use three groups indexed by i in F3. Each consists of m+2 principals and m disjoint triangles, m≥1. All principals form a clique. Join each triangle vertex to every local principal. Internal triangle edges have gain zero, external triangle edges have gain c_i=2i² and principal edges between groups i,j have gain ij, allowing i=j.

A triangle uses one or three external ports. A group has only two surplus principals, so at most one triangle can use three ports. With all triangle demands charged one ordinary port each, subtract the baseline mΣc_i=m modulo three. A triple-port costs an additional 2c_i=i² and consumes both surplus slots. It is a virtual loop on i. The remaining slots form a matching by principal edges. Counting virtual loops restores exactly two slots per type. A triangle on types or three loops has gain two; one loop and two parallel edges have gain one. Both are attainable with ordinary ports alone. Thus the original missing target is m modulo three.

Define W=binom(m+2,2)m!3^m and U=m binom(m+2,3)·6(m−1)!3^{m−1}. W counts ordinary local choices, leaving two named slots, and U counts local choices with one triple-port triangle. When precisely one virtual loop is present, the four remaining slots admit two gains of one and one gain of two. Two or three virtual loops force gain two. Hence the shifted profile is

    (0, 6W³+6UW², 9W³+3UW²+3U²W+U³).

Add m to the residue indices to obtain the original counts. At m=1 the original profile is (9207,0,7290). Independent full subset DP checked these counts and all 144 inclusion/exclusion conditions on the 72 edges. The one-port branch proves that both gains survive every pin for all m. Within one group a forced principal edge permits either a single loop or three loops; a forced crossing principal edge permits either a triangle or double edges. To avoid a principal edge select principal slots excluding one endpoint. To include an external triangle edge reserve its principal for the indicated triangle; to exclude it, keep that principal in the slots. An internal triangle edge is included or excluded by choosing the single outside port appropriately.

The order is 12m+6. The independent-set maximum is 3m by taking one vertex per triangle. An independent set touching a principal has at most 1+2m vertices. The König deficiency is therefore 3m+3. The clique stays connected after two deletions and each triangle fragment has a surviving principal neighbor. After deleting any two vertices, each local odd triangle fragment can take one distinct principal because at least m remain. Internally pair all remaining triangle vertices and pair the even surplus of principals in the clique. Thus the host is 3-connected and bicritical.

Seed the Gallai–Edmonds search with three principals from every group. There are now 3m odd triangles and 3(m−1) principals. A matching using t external edges leaves at least 3m−t vertices exposed on triangles. Equality at deficiency three forces every remaining principal to serve a distinct triangle. Each triangle vertex can be exposed by designating its triangle as its group's exceptional triangle, serving every other triangle locally. Thus D is exactly the triangle vertices and A exactly the remaining principals. The recovered barrier is all original principals, with slack six and component size three.
