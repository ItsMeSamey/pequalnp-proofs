# Euler witnesses for homogeneous ternary four-port rows

Let every GF(3) unknown be required nonzero, so it is a sign ±1. Expand each residual row into four signed occurrences c_p x_{e(p)}, with c_p=±1. The sum of four signs lies in {−4,−2,0,2,4}, hence vanishes modulo three exactly when two signs are positive. Count occurrences with multiplicity and assume each variable occurs in at most two ports. Unused variables are freely assignable. The conclusions also apply directly to exact-two signed Boolean rows.

Treat rows as degree-four nodes. A twice-used variable is an undirected edge with port-sign ratio ρ=c_p c_q at its two ends; loops and parallel edges are permitted. A singleton occurrence gives a half-edge. For a closed connected component, choose an Euler circuit and pair its incoming and outgoing ports at each visit. Require opposite signs in every such pair. Propagate signs around the circuit using ratio ρ across edges and −1 across row visits. This is consistent exactly when (−1)^|E| times the product of edge ratios is +1. In a closed component |E|=2|V|, so the condition simplifies to product of ratios +1. It is necessary for *any* balanced assignment: the product of the four signs in every row equals +1, and multiplying all rows counts each edge by its ratio. Once port signs are assigned, recover each variable as its port sign times that port's coefficient.

In a component with half-edges, their count is positive and even by the degree sum. Join them to one auxiliary hub and start an Euler tour there. Split at every visit to the hub. On each resulting trail choose an arbitrary first real port sign, enforce opposite signs at every paired real-row visit, and propagate signed edges. A trail has no end constraint, and the distinct trails cover all edges and ports. Choosing one sign for a closed circuit or each open trail is sufficient for a witness, but this does not enumerate all balanced assignments. Each connected component is processed in linear time in its ports and linkage information.

A Boolean parity relaxation requiring an even number of negatives at every four-port row already detects every inconsistent closed-component product by Gaussian elimination. It need not give an exact-two witness: four equal signs can also satisfy parity. The Euler procedure proves sufficiency and constructs an actual ternary full-support witness. Homogeneous GF(3) elimination on the original rows instead always allows the all-zero assignment if the full-support condition is forgotten.

## High structural widths without short ternary consequences

Let H be a simple bridgeless cubic expander with n vertices, and G its Cartesian product with K₂. Then G is simple and 4-regular, contains H as a layer, and has linear treewidth. G is 4-edge-connected: for a cut with both layer slices nonempty and proper, each H slice has at least two crossing edges. With a slice empty or full, use symmetry to assume it empty; a one-vertex other slice gives three horizontal crossings and one vertical crossing, and a proper slice of size at least two gives at least two of each. The cut of an entire layer crosses n vertical edges. Such H can be taken from fixed-degree expanders with sufficiently large fixed girth, excluding a bridge by expansion on large sides and by the forest edge count on small sides.

Use the oriented incidence matrix of G over GF(3), the variable on each edge having opposite signs at its endpoints. This is a satisfiable full-support instance since every 4-regular graph admits an Euler orientation. The row code contains no nonzero word of weight below four: a row combination with a proper nonempty multiplier support is nonzero on the associated edge cut. With full multiplier support, the multipliers are ±1, so the nonzero output is again a cut. Hence arbitrary linear combinations do not expose a unary, binary, or ternary row.

For an unsatisfiable control choose five pairwise vertex-disjoint edges of H and their vertical squares in G. Flip exactly one incidence sign on one edge of each square. The full edge-ratio product changes by (−1)^5, certifying infeasibility. Every nonzero proper-support vertex multiplier still leaves its entire cut nonzero. With full multiplier support, each twisted square forces some nonzero output coordinate on that square, since cancellation on all four would give inconsistent sign transport around it. The squares are edge-disjoint, so the output weight is at least five in this case. Again no nonzero short ternary consequence exists. A single twist would fail this control, since summing the original oriented rows would expose just its one modified column.

The original column matroid is graphic, whose branchwidth agrees with graph branchwidth on graphs with cycles. The latter is linear here by the linear treewidth of H. Changing five columns changes ranks of arbitrary column subsets by at most five, so the connectivity r(A)+r(B)−r(E) changes by at most fifteen simultaneously on all partitions. Thus the SAT and UNSAT controls have linear column-matroid branchwidth and every fixed-order trellis cut sequence has linear peak connectivity. The examples test the independent structural branches, not the absolute difficulty of deciding the instances; the signed Euler test decides both.
