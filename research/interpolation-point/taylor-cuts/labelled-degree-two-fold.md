# Folding degree-two vertices in group-labelled perfect matching

For a loopless multigraph with GF(3)^b edge labels, degrees here count distinct neighbors. At every stage keep one variant per unordered endpoint pair and label, with a representative set of original edge IDs. A perfect matching of each uncontracted vertex set lifts through these choices. Equal-label parallel edges need only one representative for decision and reconstruction.

Suppose v has exactly two distinct neighbors u,w. Replace u,v,w by a fresh vertex z. Keep edges disjoint from the triple. For each outside neighbor k and each old edge yk, with y either u or w, create a variant zk for every edge between v and the *other* neighbor. Its label is the sum of the labels on these two old edges, and its history is the union of their original-edge histories. Discard the old edges inside the triple, including any uw edge. Indeed every perfect matching pairs v to one of u,w, and the other must be matched outside. Thus the selected outside edge and v's matched edge contract to one variant at z. Conversely a recovered matching selects exactly one edge incident to z, whose stored two-edge expansion covers the triple and the external partner. Merging equal endpoint-and-label variants preserves at least one expansion for every possible total. Repeatedly, supervertices are disjoint clusters of original vertices; each superedge history is a perfect matching on the union of its two endpoint clusters. Consequently expansions from selected disjoint superedges cannot collide.

Any isolated live vertex proves UNSAT. At distinct-neighbor degree one, the endpoint pair is forced but its parallel labels may differ. Remove the pair and combine its alternatives with an independent table of accumulated offsets, retaining one original-edge witness for each sum. There are at most 3^b offsets. At termination the core is empty or has minimum distinct-neighbor degree at least three. For each offset h call the residual solver with target t−h and check the decoded original matching and sum. A negative result requires all residual targets to be impossible; an unresolved query can give UNKNOWN. If the core is empty one dictionary lookup at t suffices.

There are at most n/2 successful steps, each reducing the live order by two. At most 3^b variants are stored per pair. The number of local pair combinations is bounded by O(9^b) per exterior incidence; identical labels are merged at insertion. Offset convolution similarly costs at most O(9^b) per forced pair, times label and witness manipulation. Thus preprocessing takes 9^b times a polynomial in explicit sizes, and the core needs at most 3^b target queries. A polynomial-bit mask of original edge IDs per retained variant suffices. This is not a counting kernel, since equal-label histories may represent multiple distinct matchings or expansions.

The executable dense_search composes the fold with the dense moved-pair matching solver. It obtains an ordinary perfect matching on the core once, detects whether a bipartition applies, and reuses this base for every target. With unbounded support search it returns a decision and original edge witness; with a cap below the dense guarantee an unresolved query is propagated. The generic search interface also accepts any compatible backend.

A 160-cycle folds to an empty core with its two attainable offsets. In mixed three- and five-edge path replacements of K4, K6 and K8, the routine recovers a complete core of the original order. For each path, a perfect matching of the original complete graph uniquely chooses the alternating edges: the even-indexed edges of the path when it is selected and the odd-indexed edges when it is not (indices start at zero). These independently expanded controls matched the folded target spectra and recovered edge IDs for every two-coordinate ternary target, also through the actual dense backend. The independent checker additionally compared 1,036 small multigraph target decisions against original edge enumeration. These examples concern odd path lengths; changing the parity of a replacement changes the relation at its endpoints.
