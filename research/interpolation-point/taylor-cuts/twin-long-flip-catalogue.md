# Long true-matching flips from a twin quotient

Compute twins on the simple unlabelled support by checking vertex transpositions. Transitivity follows by conjugation. Classes induce cliques or independent sets; every between-class edge relation is all or nothing. Give each nonsingleton clique type a loop in the type graph and hence a diagonal link in its bipartite double cover. A primitive even cycle (no odd-distance chord) of length ≥6 never repeats a type in the SAME parity: the two occurrences see identical neighbors in the opposite parity and their two cyclic neighbor pairs cannot coincide. Its type sequence therefore lifts to a simple induced cycle of the type DOUBLE cover. The projection to the undoubled type graph may repeat types in opposite parities; keep these and use two distinct physical representatives for that type.

Store one canonical physical realization per currently feasible type-cover cycle and include all four-switches on physical vertices and physical same-pair parallel swaps. Canonical edge IDs suffice on larger cycles. Each permutation of vertices within a type is generated by transpositions, and a transposition of two separately matched twins exchanges their partners by one actual four-switch. Twins already matched together need no move. Hence normalize parallel IDs, transport an arbitrary primitive cycle to the chosen representatives by twin permutations, perform its canonical flip, undo those permutations and restore target IDs. Gain invariance of the permutations is unnecessary because these are physical reconfiguration paths. Odd-distance chord splitting reduces every other alternating cycle to primitive ones. Under induced restriction keep the root type classes and RESELECT surviving representatives per occurrence; any surviving physical primitive cycle was primitive before deletion, so the root catalogue suffices. Realizability requires two surviving representatives for a repeated type. Thus fixed-degree finite-difference nonvanishing takes (n^{O(1)}+L_Q+O(m²))^{O(d)} times polynomial evaluation overhead, where L_Q charges all enumerated induced type-double-cover cycles. These assumptions concern support only; edge gains/IDs are arbitrary.

Strict brick. Start with a 4k-cycle, even k≥4, and two chords (0,2k) and (k+1,3k+1). There are three base matchings collectively covering every edge: both cycle choices and a matching using both chords since deleted chord endpoints alternate parity. Replace every vertex by six independent clones and each edge by a complete bipartite block. The new graph is nonbipartite and remains connected after any two deletions. Twice the sum of the three matchings gives nonnegative integral base edge counts totaling six per type and at least two on each edge. To remove clones of types i,j, take an odd i,j walk by a shortest path in the connected base bipartite double cover (works for i=j). Each base edge is used at most twice. Alternately subtract and add one starting and ending by subtraction. The updated counts are nonnegative and have exactly the desired two defects. They are realized by allocating individual clones to incident blocks and pairing within each complete block. This proves bicriticality, hence the blow-up is a brick.

At least 6^{4k} physical primitive cycles arise from one clone per type around the original cycle; neither chord joins opposite parity. The supplied 4k types give a connected double cover of cycle-space dimension five (the base has 4k+2 edges), hence ≤31 induced cycles. Assign gain one to the even-chord block and zero elsewhere. Both chord counts agree in every matching by parity-shore balance on the base. Counts zero and one occur by lifting the six base copies. Any gain-changing flip uses both chord blocks, hence includes a segment on base-cycle edges joining their endpoint-type sets, of length ≥k−1. The primitive catalogue is exponentially compressed while genuinely long boundary flips are necessary. This twin-brick control is separate from the logarithmic-girth Cayley family in cayley-chord-brick-audit.md.
