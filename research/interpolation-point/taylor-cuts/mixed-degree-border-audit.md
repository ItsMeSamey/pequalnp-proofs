# Mixed-degree Boolean border audit

Use an explicit list of squarefree ANF equations over F₂, with total expanded encoding length L and D variables. Sort columns by a graded multiplicative monomial order on ordinary monomials (with Boolean reduction only after multiplying), then eliminate over F₂. Each nonzero pivot support p yields a valid rule x_p=f_p with all tails strictly below x_p. Distinct pivot sizes can occur. A pivot with empty support proves the system inconsistent. Otherwise define faces as subsets not containing any pivot support. This is a downward-closed family containing the empty face. Call its size C. It is an outer approximation to the true quotient's standard monomials: the pivot rules need not form a Gröbner basis.

Enumerate faces by extending subsets with later coordinates unless a pivot is contained. On their formal span tabulate representatives of all border products x_i b, b a face. Order border products by the ordinary graded monomial order, retaining multiplicities for comparisons. Squares reduce to the known face b when i belongs to b. Face products represent themselves. In the remaining case x_i b is squarefree and contains a pivot support p that necessarily contains i. Then q=(x_i b)/x_p is a divisor of b and is a face. Replace the product by q f_p. For each tail monomial t multiply successively by the variables of t, starting from q and expanding the known representatives after each multiplication. Every intermediate lookup is an earlier border product: proper prefixes have total degree less than deg(x_i b), and at the last step multiplicativity bounds every queried monomial by qt, strictly less than qx_p. Boolean reductions can only lower the monomial. This works for constant tails, singleton pivots and any mixture of degrees. The procedure never recursively expands an exponentially large expression: each intermediate polynomial is a length-C coefficient vector, with at most D multiplication steps per explicit tail term. Echelon rows have polynomially many monomials in the explicit input length, since elimination introduces no new columns. Thus table construction is polynomial in D,L,C even without a fixed-degree promise on explicit inputs.

Take the corresponding D multiplication templates M_i and split the full covector space successively into its nonzero intersections with the two left eigenspaces at 0 and 1 for each i. Every true evaluation survives. The sum of child dimensions is at most the parent dimension, so at most C candidates survive, with polynomial work per level in D,L,C. For a full eigenvalue record λ, any surviving w satisfies w_b=(product of λ_i over i∈b) w_empty for every face b: peel off one variable and use its exact standard border column. Hence w_empty=1 for every nonzero w. This argument does not require that singleton {i} be a face for every i. For excluded singletons the candidate bit λ_i is the eigenvalue recorded at that split; the first column of M_i may be a linear combination instead of a singleton. Check original equations on all candidates. No raw-template commutativity assumption is needed. This proves the exact model enumeration and count bounds, including a witness when one exists.

The cardinality upper bound is a finite-grid footprint bound. The connection between solutions and evaluation covectors of multiplication matrices is standard finite-algebra methodology. The algorithm explicitly tracks an outer footprint rather than claiming an arbitrary partial echelon presentation is a Gröbner basis. [Geil on finite-grid footprint bounds](https://doi.org/10.1142/S021949882150136X) · [Mourrain on multiplication and evaluation](https://www-sop.inria.fr/saga/mourrain/ALP/html/MPolyEigenSolve.html).

For m≤cD 3-CNF clauses on distinct sets of three variables, each ANF row has its own distinct cubic leading term. These terms do not occur in any other row, so the input row-span echelon pivots are exactly those m triples. Their standard faces include every subset of an independent set in this triple hypergraph. Sampling each variable with probability p=min(1/2,(2c)^{-1/2}) and deleting at most one variable for each contained triple yields an independent set of size at least pD−mp³≥pD/2 (for c>0). Therefore C≥2^{pD/2} for every signing. For all sufficiently large D one can use 6D distinct triples and choose signs independently. Each assignment satisfies all signed clauses with probability (7/8)^{6D}, so the expected number of models is 2^D(7/8)^{6D}<1. Some such formula is unsatisfiable, despite its exponentially large input-row footprint. This obstructs explicit border-face enumeration on the given generators and coordinate system; deriving additional equations or other solution strategies remains possible.
