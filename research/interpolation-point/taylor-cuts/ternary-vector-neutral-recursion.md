# Reducing vector label dimension inside neutral components

On bipartite graphs with n pairs, prescribed perfect matching over GF(3)^b admits deterministic search in n^{O(b² sqrt(nF(n)))}·3^{O(b²)}poly(input), where F(n)=max(1,log(2n)loglog(4n)). In particular the bound is subexponential in n when b=O(log n). Use ordinary matching at dimension zero. Recompute and subtract a base perfect matching at every recursive call; contract it to a directed graph as in ternary-subexponential-bipartite.md. Retain original representatives of identical endpoint/label triples.

Let L≥1 and s=ceil(b log₂3)+1. Greedily form disjoint bases from directed simple cycles of length at most L. For each partial basis of span H, search for a cycle whose gain is outside H by testing a basis of H's scalar annihilator using three-sheet shortest nonzero-cycle searches. Select a qualifying cycle, record its full gain and arcs, and delete its vertices. Reset H to zero after each complete basis. If s bases form, flip their cycles independently with probability one half. Each nontrivial character has a nonzero pairing with some vector per basis, and the corresponding normalized Fourier factor has absolute value 1/2. All nontrivial exponential sums therefore have magnitude at most 2^{−s}. Fourier inversion gives positive mass at every target since (3^b−1)2^{−s}<1. A deterministic 3^b-state subset-sum DP selects the required cycles.

Otherwise stop at an incomplete span H. Choose nonzero λ annihilating H; no remaining λ-nonzero cycle has length at most L. At most bsL vertices were removed. The three-sheet symmetric multicut construction for λ, with fractional internal lengths 1/L, gives O(nF(n)/L) further vertices after whose deletion all SCCs are λ-neutral. This includes the already-neutral case. Keep both sets as portals, so x=O(b²L+nF(n)/L). If desired find the extra set by testing remaining vertex subsets in increasing cardinality, as neutrality is polynomially checkable. Taking L=max(1,floor(sqrt(nF(n))/b)) gives x=O(b sqrt(nF(n))) whenever the threshold is at least one before clipping. Otherwise use all n portals; n≤b sqrt(nF(n)) in that regime.

Choose π to GF(3)^{b−1} with (λ,π) invertible. Enumerate portal incidences and process exterior SCCs topologically, tracking pending arrivals and vector gain as in the scalar proof. For an SCC S let A be the vertices whose incoming edge is already fixed across its boundary and B the vertices whose outgoing edge is selected across its boundary. Enforce equal counts and vertex capacities. Each local completion is exactly a perfect matching from left S minus B to right S minus A, using every actual internal arc and an additional gain-zero identity option at each vertex for leaving the base pair unchanged. Vertices in both A and B are externally complete. Keep genuine loops, parallel choices, and all local cycles with nonzero projected gain.

A λ-neutral SCC has potential φ with λg(u,v)=φ(v)−φ(u). Every local completion has scalar gain sum of φ over B minus sum over A. Enumerate possible projected gains (3^{b−1} values), recursively deciding matching with labels πg. Restore full local gains using the fixed scalar coordinate and reachable projected labels. Gluing the found local matchings with the selected boundary arcs, respecting their chosen original edge IDs, yields the global matching. It is insufficient to discard all exterior cycles as in the scalar algorithm.

Enumerate only endpoint pairs of portal and intercomponent arcs. For each collection of such pairs determine its attainable total labels and representative IDs by syndrome DP over the corresponding edge-label lists. Distinct selected pairs have disjoint tails and disjoint heads; equality of a head and a tail is allowed. An arc between portals is counted once. Hence no factor (3^b)^x is charged. The number of portal assignments, pending states and transition endpoint choices costs n^{O(x+1)} times 3^{O(b)} at each dimension. Vertex counts never increase and dimension decreases at every recursive call. Multiplying over dimensions gives the displayed bound, with polynomial data handling per node after parallel edges are deduplicated. The method is for bipartite matching and uses no proposed polynomial exact-matching theorem. No priority claim is made.
