# What the OR-exception lattice does at a genuine October evaluation

The main counting polynomial does not generally give Boolean gates away from zero. In the source's equation for H(T), occurrences are substituted by polynomial scalar products; the authors explicitly say their nonzero-node values on Boolean vectors need not be bits. Even a binary OR polynomial evaluated at input 2 and 0 gives 2, whereas a Boolean exceptional-flat test would return a bit. A faithful gate reduction or a native weighted algorithm is needed there. This observation alone is not a lower bound on the particular published universal rows.

A genuine nonzero Appendix D evaluation can nevertheless instantiate precisely the exceptional flats of edge ORs. Fix the legal repeated-packet construction already given in ../tensor-expander/result.md and ../tensor-program/program.md. Take one live column and one live row at the same slot y; pin the other columns zero and freely sum all other rows. Directly from the Appendix D weights its two-by-two transfer, with rows Y and columns X, is [[1,1−g],[0,g]], g=W_yy(T). Multiply this entrywise with the transfer from an identically prepared packet with both logical bits complemented. The product is g times Boolean equality, without divisions. For each incidence of an edge and vertex in G use a complementary packet pair. Merge logical bit ports at the edge into a 0/1 OR tensor and at a vertex into a 0/1 COPY tensor, implementing complements by the designated port interpretations. Pin inactive columns and freely sum inactive rows as in the supplied layout. Per incidence this uses one original port per partner on each endpoint; hence degree is at most twice max(2,Δ(G)), apart from unary pins. At EVERY formal T the scalar is g(T)^(2m) times the number of vertex covers of G, where m=|E(G)|.

At the permitted node t=ζ=1, T=2L+1, g=N/T with v₂(N)=a+1, so its valuation is a+1. Use enough identically prepared packets for 4m copies in the source's equifrequent ordered-label catalogue. With the fixed-ε padding of program.md, M polynomial in n+m with a large fixed exponent yields at least 4m repeated copies and the published precision p_t≥8M(a+1) exceeds 2m(a+1)+n+1. Consequently dividing the computed even factor in an integer lift and inverting its odd unit yields the full count without precision ambiguity. More precisely recover modulo 2^(p_t−2m(a+1)), whose modulus exceeds 2^n. Computing g modulo 2^p_t is polynomial in L,p_t, and the whole padding is polynomial in n+m; the exponential sizes below concern logical variables or a fixed positive power of the padded M, not a claim of 2^Ω(M) or a bound on the source's chosen greedy layout. No assumption about the main universal swap construction follows.

For an OR on uv the exception flat H_e says x_u=x_v=0. Distinct intersections correspond exactly to endpoint unions U of edge subsets (plus empty U). Inclusion–exclusion over exception events has combined coefficient
     
    c_U = Σ_{F⊆E: endpoints(F)=U}(−1)^|F| = (−1)^|U| I(G[U],−1),

where I(H,z) is the independence polynomial. Indeed invert over vertex subsets: the signed sum over edge sets internal to W is (1−1)^|E(W)|, one on independent W and zero otherwise. The number of covers is Σ_U c_U 2^(n−|U|). For an induced matching, every union of its edge endpoints induces only those edges, and its coefficient is ±1. In connected bounded-degree expanders a greedy maximal induced matching has Ω(n) edges: picking an edge removes O(Δ²) edges from eligibility. Thus even on connected source-compatible layouts there are 2^Ω(n) distinct nonempty intersections with nonzero coefficients (apart from the single empty union). Writing out a nonzero list already takes that many operations. Disjoint matching examples admit product formulas; this is not a lower bound on all implicit representations.

Even a SINGLE coefficient query for specified U can encode an exact hard scalar. Given a bipartite graph B, direct edges lower→upper and replace each site by a bidirected cycle of length at least max(3,deg_B(site)), assigning each original incidence to a distinct cycle vertex. The resulting D has maximum indegree/outdegree three. Its cyclic-component antichains equal the independent sets of B. Use the hard-core graph H of cyclic-or.md, of maximum degree four and no isolates. Then c_{V(H)}=(−1)^|V(H)| I(H,−1) gives #IS(B) up to a known sign. The queried flat is attainable because every vertex has an incident edge. All variable and tensor degrees in the packet implementation remain constant. Thus polynomial-time preprocessing plus uniformly polynomial-time exact arbitrary coefficient queries would imply FP=#P under standard counting reductions. This says nothing against small circuits queried only approximately, special coefficient queries, or computations which never expand the OR-event lattice. [Bipartite counting hardness](https://doi.org/10.1137/0212053); [original Appendix D](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/quantitative-contraction.tex).

These coefficients use falsity markers z_v=1−x_v and Boolean multiplication z_v²=z_v. The product ∏(1−z_u z_v) is an exact size-O(m) circuit in that quotient even on the hard family. Efficient symbolic storage and Boolean evaluation do not provide an efficient general coefficient oracle. Since |c_U|≤2^n, hard queries have only polynomial-length answers. A coefficient oracle is a distinct task from computing one unmarked scalar. An independent exhaustive replay checked every coefficient on every one of the 1,024 labeled five-vertex graphs, totaling 32,768 comparisons.
