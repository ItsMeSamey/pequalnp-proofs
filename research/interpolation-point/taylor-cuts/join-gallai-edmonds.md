# Exact scalar gain reduction across every complete join

Consider a nontrivial complete join G=L∨B with |L|≥|B| and even total order. The two induced graphs and all original edge labels are arbitrary. A complementary component split discovers a complete join in polynomial time. Put k=(|L|−|B|)/2. If ν(L)<k, no perfect matching exists.

When k<ν(L), all original perfect matchings connect by parallel-ID changes, four-switches and genuine six-cycle flips. Normalize by switching paired internal edges across the cut until all B vertices are matched externally. As in complete-join-square-transfer.md, the k-matchings of L connect by single-edge replacements and the cross bijections connect by cross switches. Replacing a core edge by one sharing its endpoint needs one four-switch. If old xy and new ab are disjoint, the newly covered vertices a,b are currently matched by ports sa,tb for distinct s,t. Replace xy,sa,tb by ab,sx,ty, an alternating six-cycle. This needs no internal edge in B.

When k=ν(L), an internal B edge would force more than ν(L) core edges, so all ports cross. In the Gallai–Edmonds decomposition of L let D be the vertices exposed by some maximum core matching, A its external neighborhood, and C the remaining vertices. In every maximum matching all A vertices match into D and C is internally perfectly matched. Therefore every component of L[C] contributes its entire original perfect-matching family as an independent Cartesian factor, and all its exterior edges can be ignored. Compute D by testing ν(L−v)=ν(L). Every maximum matching of L extends to the join via a cross bijection.

Outside C the matching family connects by four-switches and parallel-ID changes. The symmetric difference of maximum cores consists of even paths and alternating cycles. Each path slides its exposed vertex along consecutive edges: with port s at x, replace sx,yz by xy,sz. Every alternating cycle meets D, since each matched A vertex has a D partner. For a chosen D vertex on the cycle, find a maximum core matching exposing it. The symmetric difference from the current core gives an even alternating path from a currently exposed vertex to the chosen one. At its first cycle vertex z the entering edge pz must be nonmatched since z is matched within the cycle. Slide the hole along the even prefix to p, then into the cycle by replacing zz' by pz. Slide the exposed z' around the cycle, finishing with its other neighbor of z exposed. Use the temporary pz edge to close the cycle and reexpose p. Reverse the prefix slides, restoring all exterior edges and port assignments while flipping exactly the chosen cycle. The original exposed vertex cannot lie on an alternating cycle. Once all core edges agree, cross switches rearrange the port bijection.

On any bounded-exchange factor the polynomial enumeration of four- or six-cycle edge patterns plus parallel-ID swaps suffices for quadratic nonvanishing. The difference of a quadratic across each flip is affine, so the Lovász matching-span algorithm over F₃ tests all residual matchings. Nonzero differences supply original witnesses by affine self-reduction, and constant-on-every-move tests together with a baseline cover all perfect matchings. Testing 1−(g−t)² gives the scalar target. Repeated queries also produce the full witnessed quadratic evaluation span of edge incidence or additive features.

For scalar products compute which factors have constant ternary gain using linear matching span. Obtain two actual alternative gains for each variable factor. If two vary independently, their two two-element gain sets sum to all of F₃: choose original witnesses for these and fix arbitrary witnesses for all others. If none varies, compare the fixed sum with the requested target. If exactly one varies, subtract the fixed gains and solve that one. A remaining C component can be processed recursively. Process disconnected graphs by the same product rule, rejecting a factor without a perfect matching first. Only one smaller potentially hard factor persists at each step. Thus every complete join is solved or reduced to a single strictly smaller ordinary component in polynomial total overhead. A connected, complement-connected component without another applicable solver is unresolved. NO always covers all original perfect matchings. A tight control joins B to the union of an arbitrary matchable H₀ and |B| isolated ports. All B vertices must match these isolated ports; setting new gains to zero preserves the unrestricted H₀ target exactly.

The scalar product simplification uses F₃ specifically. The bounded-exchange quadratic span factors do not restrict the dimension of an explicitly supplied additive feature map, subject to its quadratic output dimension.
