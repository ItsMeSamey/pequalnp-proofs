# Solving binary forbidden-flat systems from their quadratic span

Parameterize the ambient affine space by D independent binary coordinates. A rank-two flat forbidding (u(x),v(x))=(a,b) gives the Boolean equation (1+u(x)+a)(1+v(x)+b)=0 over F₂. Include the analogous unary and constant equations when applicable. Reduce all squares by x_i²=x_i. The coefficients of mixed squarefree products form the exterior-square vector u∧v, independently of offsets. The argument below applies to any explicitly listed Boolean quadratics. Ambient free coordinates must be included in D or factored out separately when counting.

**Full-span test.** Row-reduce the quadratic coefficient columns, carrying all affine tails through the same row operations. If the coefficient rank is binom(D,2), obtain x_i x_j=A_ij(x) for every i<j, with affine A_ij. On the formal (D+1)-dimensional space with basis 1,x₁,…,x_D, build M_i with column b equal to the coefficients of the representative for x_i b: x_i for the constant column, x_i for its own variable column, and A_ij otherwise. Every actual solution λ supplies the nonzero evaluation row w=(1,λ₁,…,λ_D), with wM_i=λ_i w simultaneously.

Start with the full covector space. For each i replace each surviving subspace E by its nonzero intersections with the left eigenspaces of M_i at 0 and 1. The two children are disjoint subspaces within E (zero intersection), so their dimensions sum to at most dim E. Thus the total dimension at each level is at most D+1; all intersections are ordinary binary linear elimination. At the last level a nonzero w obeys w_i=λ_i w₀ by the constant columns, so w₀=1 and the leaf specifies a unique candidate λ. Check every candidate against all original equations, including affine-only rows. All solutions survive and distinct leaves have distinct λ. This enumerates every model, gives its exact count (at most D+1), and supplies witnesses in polynomial time. No commutativity or simultaneous diagonalizability of arbitrary rewrite matrices was assumed. In particular the example with zero and all unit-vector models attains the bound when its wedge rank is full.

**Conditioning nonpivot pairs.** At partial rank, fully reduce the mixed-product columns so each pivot product is expressed using only affine terms and nonpivot pair products. Make a graph H on coordinates with these nonpivot pairs as edges. For a vertex cover T, enumerate assignments on T. Every nonpivot product becomes affine after restriction, while every remaining pair is a pivot with an affine expression. Apply the preceding solver to the unpinned coordinates and verify the original conditioned constraints. The 2^|T| fibers are disjoint. A cover of size τ can be found by the two-endpoint branching algorithm, in O*(2^τ) time (or use any supplied cover). Thus exact counting and witness recovery take O*(2^τ) time and polynomial space. The parameter refers to the chosen coordinate and pivot presentation.

**A sparse border refinement.** For this additional algorithm order the quadratic columns in decreasing order from a graded monomial order, such as degree-lex. In reduced echelon form a pivot pair is replaced by affine and strictly smaller nonpivot pairs. Let C be the number of cliques of H, including the empty clique, and regard each clique as its squarefree monomial. There is a deterministic solver polynomial in the input and C. Enumerate the cliques by extending them with later adjacent vertices. Build normal representatives only for the at most DC border products x_i b, with b a clique, processing distinct formal monomials in increasing graded order. Representatives are linear combinations of cliques agreeing on all solutions, using only monomials at most the input in this order. For a clique product keep the monomial. A square x_i² in x_i b reduces to b. Otherwise, when x_i b is squarefree and is not a clique, there is a v in b with iv a pivot pair; put q=b/x_v, itself a clique. Multiply that pivot's affine-plus-smaller-pairs expression by q. Constant and linear terms are already known cliques or lower-degree border products. For a quadratic term x_a x_h, look up the representative of x_a q, which has degree below that of x_i b. For each clique c appearing in it, multiply by x_h using the earlier border entry for x_h c. These lookups are legitimate because x_h c is at most x_h x_a q, strictly below x_i x_v q under the multiplicative monomial order. Every step preserves evaluation at actual models. This avoids enumerating all monomials through the maximum clique size.

The resulting border table supplies C-dimensional matrices M_i. Perform the same successive eigenspace intersections. The total surviving dimension per level is at most C. For a final common covector recursively divide each nonempty clique b by x_i∈b: x_i(b/x_i)=b is a clique product retained without modification. Therefore its coordinate equals λ_i times that for b/x_i. A nonzero covector has constant coordinate one and is the unique evaluation on all cliques for its λ. Filtering the original equations again yields exactly all models. Polynomial space in C suffices, which need not be polynomial in the input alone. For missing graph of degeneracy δ there are at most 1+D2^δ cliques by charging each nonempty clique to its first vertex in a degeneracy order. Thus this method costs 2^{O(δ)} times polynomial input overhead and includes polynomial cases with large vertex-cover number. At full quadratic rank the cliques are precisely the constant and singleton monomials.

**Relation to prior work.** Multiplication matrices and evaluation covectors are standard tools for solving finite polynomial systems. FGLM uses linear algebra in a finite quotient for change of monomial ordering. The proofs above exploit a directly checkable span or border condition and construct representatives valid on models without first proving that their formal coordinate space is the actual quotient or that their templates commute; final filtering handles extra candidates. No claim that the eigenspace idea is new is intended.
