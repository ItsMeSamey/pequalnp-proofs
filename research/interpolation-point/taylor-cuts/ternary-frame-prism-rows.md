# Native three-edge matching constraints between ternary frames

Fix a palette map for each frame, already validated against the input graph by triangle propagation. At site i of frame A the colour is a+s_A i over F3 with s_A∈{1,2}. The map may have many vertices at each site and different frames may overlap. A matching certificate consists of three original edges, one from each site i of frame A to site π(i) of frame B, where π permutes the three sites. Write π(i)=c+e i; e=1 for an even permutation and e=2 for an odd permutation. The three edges impose

    (a−b−s_B c)+(s_A−e s_B)i ≠ 0  for every i∈F3.

A nonconstant affine function of i assumes zero, so necessarily s_A=e s_B. With that equation in force the three edges also require a−b−s_B c≠0. Thus the orientation equation alone is a valid redundant constraint in a solver retaining all original edge conditions. Dropping those edge conditions would need the remaining translation disequality. Neither proper graph coloring nor a general three-edge matching licenses a constraint without first validating the two frame maps.

The detector indexes each vertex's frame memberships, scans both incidences of the unordered graph edges, and retains one original edge for each observed ordered site pair between two frames. Testing the six permutations per observed frame pair returns all orientation signs having this native matching witness. It does not require the same representative vertex whenever a site is used in different certificates. The scan costs O(sum_{uv∈E} m(u)m(v)) membership pairs plus constant work per observed frame pair, where m counts frame memberships. The optional solver integration obtains the frame maps independently from the native graph. Signed constraints can also be solved by graph propagation: a consistent component has one freely signed representative, and an inconsistent sign cycle is impossible because signs are nonzero. This exact substitution is implemented by orientation_classes. Merely appending equations can increase the rank used by a capped search; their structural soundness alone does not promise a runtime improvement.

For consecutive matchings A→M→B, suppose M is a private triangle, with just the three matched edges to each endpoint frame and no further constraints on its sites. Write π_j(i)=c_j+e_j i. A middle orientation exists precisely under s_A=e_1 e_2 s_B, and then s_M=e_1 s_A. Its translation m must avoid the two values a−s_M c_1 and b+s_B c_2. At least one of the three field values remains. The middle triangle can therefore be eliminated exactly for decision using the composite orientation equation, with no condition on endpoint translations from this gadget. The function lift_double_prism chooses a legal translation and reconstructs its three colours. With identity matchings and equal slopes, equal endpoint translations have two middle witnesses while different translations have one. A projected decision constraint therefore does not preserve multiplicities. Side conditions elsewhere in the graph, including shared endpoint vertices, remain effective.

The independent replay covers all 512 cross-edge sets between two triangles and all 36 endpoint placements per set. The optional native graph solver agrees with brute force in all 512 instances. All 1,296 endpoint and matching pairs for a private double prism agree with full middle-frame enumeration. Additional fixtures verify duplicate site representatives, overlapping frames, and inconsistent signed cycles.

The integrated projector substitutes the signed classes before its remaining syndrome DP. In a cycle of eight distinct triangles, join successive triangles by identity matchings except for one transposition on the wraparound. Traversing the cycle would require a nonzero slope to equal its negative. At syndrome-table cap one the original projector returns UNKNOWN, and the augmented projector returns UNSAT from this contradiction without enumerating syndrome states. The checker replays both outcomes.
