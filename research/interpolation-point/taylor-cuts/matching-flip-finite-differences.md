# Finite differences on actual matching flips

Let P be a polynomial of certified degree d over a field, evaluated on perfect-matching incidence vectors. Suppose the perfect matchings in each relevant induced residual are connected by an enumerable list of moves. Each move has two actual coverings A,B of exactly the same local vertices, including two-edge parallel switches. Each matching R of the graph with these local vertices removed gives real endpoints R+A and R+B.

Obtain a baseline perfect matching, returning infeasible if none exists and returning its original IDs if P is nonzero there. For positive degree test each move recursively on the residual for Q(x)=P(x+1_A)−P(x+1_B), extending residual vectors with zeros. Its highest homogeneous part in x cancels, so deg Q≤d−1 in every characteristic. If Q is nonzero on R, at least one completed endpoint has nonzero P and is an original witness. If all differences vanish, P is constant along every move and hence on the connected matching family; a zero baseline proves nonexistence. Degree zero is direct. Degree one can use Lovász's finite-field matching-incidence span with edge-deletion witness reconstruction, without any connectivity condition on the leaf. Handle affine constants by a star or evaluate separately on the empty graph.

For V=F3^b take P=product_j(1−(g_j−t_j)^2), of degree at most 2b. One may evaluate nested differences at depth h by at most 2^h original function evaluations with pinned coordinates; no formal polynomial expansion is necessary. With at most F uniformly enumerable moves per residual the deterministic time is (F+1)^{O(d)} times 2^d and the polynomial matching/evaluation and charged recognition costs per visited node. Fixed maximum cycle length L gives F≤(m+1)^{O(L)}. Only levels above the degree-one leaf need a connectivity guarantee when that oracle is used. In particular scalar target decisions require only root connectivity, since quadratics have affine first differences. Without certified connectivity a fruitless list search cannot prove NO.

If every even simple cycle longer than L has an odd-distance chord, a long alternating-cycle flip factors into two shorter actual flips: one path between chord endpoints has matching edges at both ends, the other path does not. Flip the former path plus chord to insert the chord, then the latter to remove it. Repeat. This property persists under vertex deletion. The symmetric difference of two perfect matchings decomposes into alternating cycles, proving the required connectivity. Ignore parallel copies for this structural condition and include their individual two-edge switches. Strongly chordal graphs satisfy L=4; chordality alone does not justify this step. More generally strongly orderable graphs have the required hereditary four-cycle connectivity (Bonamy et al., Theorem 6, https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2019.80). Dyer and Müller characterize the hereditary four-switch-connected class in https://doi.org/10.1137/18M1172910. Compare earlier exact red-count matching algorithms and related chord criteria in https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2024.33. The recursion concerns arbitrary labelled residue vectors and bounded-degree polynomial nonvanishing; no universal short-flip hypothesis or priority is asserted.
