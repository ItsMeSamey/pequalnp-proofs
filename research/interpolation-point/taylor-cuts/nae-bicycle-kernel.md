# A linear kernel for signed NAE decision parameterized by the cycle pairing

Let G be the bipartite occurrence graph of signed NAE constraints, one edge per occurrence. Let r be the rank over GF(2) of the ordinary edge-coordinate dot product on its binary cycle space. For NAE3 this is the polar rank of the Fourier quadratic regardless of signs. For larger arities the graph parameter is defined independently of any phase. Feasibility and a verified original assignment are computable in deterministic 2^O(r) poly(input) time and polynomial space. Counts are not preserved.

**Reductions.** Drop tautological gates containing both polarities of a variable; collapse equal repeated literals. Reject remaining NAE gates of arity zero or one, and remove isolated variables. A degree-one variable can satisfy its sole gate for any values of its other ports, so remove the gate and record its repair. A binary NAE gate identifies its variables up to a known parity, so merge and substitute. For a degree-two variable in two distinct gates, complement an entire NAE gate as needed to get NAE(A,x) and NAE(NOT x,B). Replace by NAE(A,B): monochromatic A forces x opposite A and monochromatic B forces x equal B, which conflict precisely when A,B together are monochromatic. Nonempty A,B may share other variables. Record signs and port lists to restore x. Repeat and retain reductions in reverse order for search. Every reduction decreases graph size and can be done in polynomial time.

Degree-two suppression merges the two neighbors of the suppressed vertex, keeping other edges separately in the intermediate multigraph. Each cycle vector on the fused graph extends linearly by giving both removed edges the parity on the surviving incidences at one former neighbor. The other side has the same parity, so the lift is Eulerian and preserves all dot products (the two extra coordinates cancel). Subsequent edge and gate deletions restrict the cycle space by zero extension. Thus the pairing rank never increases. A remaining nonempty kernel is simple bipartite with minimum degree at least three.

**Size lemma.** Write N,m,c for the vertex, edge and component counts of this kernel, p for binary Laplacian rank and rho for its cycle pairing rank. The bicycle-space identity yields rho=m−2N+2c+p: vertex potentials in the Laplacian kernel map onto cycle-cut intersection, with component constants as kernel. Let o=o_U+o_W count odd vertices by shore, D=sum_v(deg(v)−3−1[deg(v) even])≥0. Then rho=(D−o)/2+2c+p. The identity principal submatrix of all odd vertices on either fixed shore gives p≥max(o_U,o_W). Hence D+|o_U−o_W|+4c≤2rho and p−o_U,p−o_W≤2rho.

Let A be binary U×W adjacency, splitting into odd-W and even-W columns. Pivot odd W vertices in the Laplacian. The remainder has blocks [[K,A_even],[A_even^T,0]], where K=diag(deg_U mod 2)+A_odd A_odd^T, with total rank p−o_W≤2rho. Nonzero columns of A_even lie in the union of supports of at most 2rho basis rows. Each even W gives such a nonzero column because the graph is simple. Any s vertices have total degree ≤4s+D, giving ≤10rho even W, and likewise ≤10rho even U. By symmetry, nonzero row indices of K lie in the support union of at most 2rho basis rows. This is contained in those indices or their distance-two neighborhoods via odd W. Applying the same total-degree bound twice yields ≤44rho nonzero row indices. Mark as defects every even vertex, every positive-excess vertex and each nonzero K row. There are ≤65rho defects (nonzero excess is ≥2), with total degree ≤262rho.

Any U vertex u farther than three steps from defects has cubic neighbors and co-neighbors locally, and its own and its U co-neighbors' K rows are zero. Regard the cubic W neighbors of u as triples. The link of u is a three-edge loopless multigraph on its co-neighbors. Its degrees are even by the zero K row since all W neighbors are odd. Such a graph must be a triangle. Thus u belongs to triples (u,a,b),(u,b,c),(u,c,a). At a its link is also a triangle, forcing (a,b,c) as fourth triple. These four U and four W vertices have exhausted their degrees, forming an isolated tetrahedron incidence graph. The reasoning works with repeated unsigned scopes across distinct gates.

All remaining U vertices are within distance three of defects. Beyond the first edge, shortest paths from defects to these vertices have nondefective cubic internal vertices and at most two forward choices. Hence |U|≤65rho+7·262rho+4c≤1901rho. Another neighborhood and the total-degree bound show |W| and m are O(rho). Constants are conservative. Brute-force U on the kernel, undo the recorded reductions choosing a locally valid primal bit at each step, and verify the complete assignment. Since rho≤r this proves the claimed runtime. In particular r=O(log input size) gives polynomial time, without a positivity promise. The parameter can be linear. No unrestricted SAT improvement is claimed.

The binary Laplacian bicycle identity is standard (Godsil and Royle, Algebraic Graph Theory, Lemma 14.15.3). No historical priority claim is made for the parameterized result.
