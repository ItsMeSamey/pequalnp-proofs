# Quadratic gain tests using maximum-matching exposure

Let G be a finite loopless multigraph with independent reservoir D, core H=G−D, and |D|=|V(H)|−2ν(H), where ν is maximum matching cardinality. A perfect matching necessarily uses a maximum core matching. Label edges in F₃^b. We seek the witnessed span of (1,g_i,g_i g_j for i≤j) over all perfect-matching gains g. For scalar gain, 1−(g−a)² tests a prescribed target. All witnesses retain original IDs.

The sets exposed by maximum core matchings form bases of a rank-|D| matroid. In the symmetric difference of maximum matchings every alternating path has even length (otherwise one matching admits augmentation). For each vertex exposed only by the first matching, flipping its component exchanges it with a vertex exposed only by the second. Independence of S is equivalent to ν(H−S)=ν(H). Restrict to core vertices with some reservoir neighbor. If rank drops there is no perfect matching.

Let s count exceptions to a single most frequent neighborhood in D among eligible core vertices. For each subset U of the exceptional vertices T, require exactly U to be exposed. Check independence, contract U, delete T−U, and check residual rank |D|−|U|. The remaining candidate vertices share a neighborhood in D. Test bipartite matching to U plus |D|−|U| copies of this neighborhood. Success extends every basis in the minor, and failure extends none. A basis witness lifts by ordinary matching on H minus the exposed vertices, plus matching them to D.

In each feasible branch test whether g is affine over F₃ in the exposed-vertex indicators. Delete core edges incident to U and reservoir edges to T−U. Compute the finite-field linear span of all remaining perfect-matching indicators by Lovász’s matching-span theorem. A star functional homogenizes the constant; the zero-vertex graph is direct. Exposures are linear sums of incident reservoir-edge indicators. Fitting the proposed identities on this span certifies them on all original perfect matchings in the branch, including parallel edges. A failed fit is an unhandled branch. With a successful fit apply the single-matroid quadratic oracle of ternary-quadratic-matroid-slots.md, with the branch constant included. This uses the nonzero-common-basis algorithm of Hörsch et al., ICALP 2024, Theorem 3.7 over F₃. At most (1+b+b(b+1)/2)² oracle calls yield a witnessed spanning set. Unite and row-reduce the actual matching rows. Cost is 2^s poly(input,b) for supplied D. Returning NO requires coverage of all branches.

One may first condition on t supplied original edge IDs. Delete forbidden edges, validate forced edges are a matching and remove their endpoints. For i forced core edges and j forced reservoir edges, any feasible residual has core matching number ν(H)−i: a potential larger matching would extend by the i pinned core edges to contradict maximality in H; any feasible completion attains the bound. The residual deficiency therefore equals |D|−j. Reject if this equality fails. A forbidden reservoir edge may introduce one extra exceptional port, so running time is 2^(s+2t)poly, or 2^(s+t)poly for exceptional core edges only. Restore pinned gains and edge IDs. Dense nonlinear gain dependence on a small number of edge indicators is admissible when the remaining conditional affine fits succeed.

The cardinality promise is substantive. On H=P4 (1–2–3–4) with two complete reservoir vertices, exposures {1,2},{1,4},{3,4} violate basis exchange: deleting 1 from the first cannot be repaired by 3 or 4 from the last. Also, fixed exposed sets may have extensions with different gains; the affine test can fail. There is no unrestricted matching claim. Verification for complete reservoirs: check_exposure_matroid_independent.py enumerated 1,024 five-vertex cores and 32,768 eligible supports.
