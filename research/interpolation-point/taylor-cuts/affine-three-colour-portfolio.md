# Hidden ternary colours and exact residual counts

For a simple graph G take a two-dimensional binary normal space P_v for each vertex, all in direct sum. Each vertex gate excludes one evaluation on P_v, taken as zero after translation. Each nonzero functional on a vertex plane vanishes on exactly one of the three remaining evaluations, so its three nonzero directions name the three colours. For an edge uv the three gates on span(l_{u,c},l_{v,pi(c)}) forbid both indicated evaluations being zero. Here pi is a matching of endpoint colour names, potentially different on every edge. Thus exactly three of the nine locally allowed colour pairs are excluded. The special case of every matched pair receiving identical global names gives ordinary proper three-colouring.

## Recognition

Coalesce duplicate identical gates. Different forbidden points on the same plane cannot occur in this pure encoding. Count which of the three nonzero vectors of each homogeneous plane also occur in another plane. A nonisolated vertex plane has three shared vectors. An edge gate has exactly two shared vectors, one in each endpoint's vertex plane. Its third vector has support at both endpoints, so any different gate containing it would need exactly the same pair of endpoints and both identical local directions, contradicting distinctness. An isolated vertex plane has zero shared vectors.

Declare planes with three or zero shared vectors to be vertex candidates. Verify their direct sum by elimination. Require every remaining plane to have two shared vectors in distinct vertex candidates and to be their span. For each unordered endpoint pair that occurs require three planes bijectively matching the two triples of directions. Translate the bad vertex evaluations to zero; a simultaneous translation exists by independence. Verify that in every edge gate the bad pair after translation consists of both zero directional evaluations. This constructs the matching presentation in polynomial time. It is also necessary by the preceding incidence counts. To recognize globally identical colour names, propagate name permutations along the bijections, seeding each connected component arbitrarily, and reject inconsistent revisits. GL(2,2) realizes all permutations of the three labels. For only one synchronized distinguished colour, try each of three choices at a component root, propagate that colour along each edge, and retain a choice exactly when all cycles agree. The other two may be interchanged on cycles. An empty graph and isolated vertices require no propagation. These tests concern the specified core. Additional, unmarked guards can introduce shared vectors and defeat the simple recognition criterion on the combined list.

## Minimum hitting dimension

The core's exact intrinsic plane-hitting dimension is n+tau(G), even for arbitrary matching permutations. For the upper bound take each full vertex plane on a vertex cover and one line at every remaining vertex. For the lower bound choose w_v nonzero in W intersect P_v for each vertex in any hitting space W; these n directions are independent. Quotient the entire ambient space by K=span(w_v). For every edge there is a matched pair of directions with neither endpoint's direction equal to w_v, since the two endpoint exclusions remove at most two of its three pairs. Its gate plane maps injectively onto the span of the two quotient vertex coordinates. W/K must therefore contain a nonzero vector supported on the endpoints of each edge. Use the quotient vertex coordinates as part of an ambient basis. The leading coordinate of every nonzero vector of W/K is a pivot of its reduced row basis. The vertex pivots cover all edges, proving dim W−n≥tau(G), regardless of any unused external coordinates. Adding guards retains the lower bound for the enlarged list but can increase its optimum.

## Counting after synchronizing one colour

Let I be the set taking the distinguished colour. Test each of the 2^n subsets for independence. An independent I satisfies every edge incident to I once vertices outside I are restricted to their two other colours: the distinguished colour is matched only to the distinguished colour. On every surviving edge the two remaining forbidden pairs form a matching on two symbols. Its complement is also a matching, hence imposes one binary XOR equation with a computable offset. Propagate offsets, rejecting any inconsistent cycle. Every nonempty connected component of G−I has one free orientation bit; an empty graph has zero. With no guards the core count is the sum of 2^c over consistent independent I, where c is this component count. Multiply by 2^(D−2n) for unused ambient dimensions. This uses polynomial space and O*(2^n) time. For ordinary colourings consistency reduces to bipartiteness.

For additional affine-flat guards, on each such I express both vertex coordinate bits as affine functions of that component's orientation (or constants on I). The two residual colour vectors always admit this binary affine parameterization. Retain the D−2n complementary ambient coordinates and substitute all expressions into every guard. Gaussian elimination detects inconsistent forbidden descriptions and full-space forbidden events. Count the resulting instance on c+D−2n bits and sum over I. If its guard-normal span has rank r_I, each evaluation on that span has 2^(c+D−2n−r_I) lifts. Direct enumeration gives O*(2^n+sum_I 2^r_I) time and polynomial space. Guards may link orientations across components or mix them with the ambient complement; the unrestricted factor 2^(D−2n) and a product of independent component counts require checking these dependencies first.

## Bounded-degree alternative without synchronization

For any finite CSP on ternary domains whose primal graph has maximum degree four, a uniformly random order retains each vertex with at most one prior neighbor with probability min(1,2/(deg(v)+1)). The retained set induces a forest, since the last vertex on any cycle would have at least two prior neighbors. Expected size is at least 2n/5. Fix the order by conditional expectation: for an unplaced vertex with k already placed neighbors and j unplaced neighbors, the conditional probability is max(0,min(1,(2−k)/(j+1))), by uniform relative rank among those j neighbors and itself. Scores for placed vertices are determined. Try all remaining next vertices and select one that does not decrease the conditional expectation. This gives a deterministic forest of size at least ceil(2n/5) in polynomial time.

Enumerate the at most 3^floor(3n/5) assignments off the forest. After substitution every remaining constraint has scope either empty, a singleton, or an edge, since its scope forms a clique in a forest. Evaluate constants, combine unary/edge tables, and count on each rooted tree by sending for each parent value the sum of compatible child values times the child-subtree counts. Multiply across roots and sum over disjoint exterior assignments. Runtime is O*(3^(3n/5)) and space polynomial for explicit constraints. This bound applies to arbitrary matchings and general ternary CSPs meeting the stated primal-degree bound. Arbitrary extra affine guards generally destroy that primal bound; the guarded orientation formula above remains available.

For the hardness case choose ordinary matchings and no guards. Three-colourability remains NP-complete for planar graphs of maximum degree four; the affine encoding has 2n coordinates and n+3|E| gates and preserves the number of proper colourings exactly. This supplies decision hardness for this presentation and makes no exponential-time lower-bound assertion. [Garey, Johnson and Stockmeyer, Some simplified NP-complete graph problems (1976), Theorems 2.1–2.3](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~deloera/MISC/LA-BIBLIO/trunk/JohnsonDavid2.pdf).
