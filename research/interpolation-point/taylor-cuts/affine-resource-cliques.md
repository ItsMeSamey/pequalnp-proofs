# Recovering signed clique capacities from native binary clauses

Treat a signed literal l as the event that makes it true. For each deduplicated non-tautological binary clause (u∨v), make an undirected incompatibility edge between −u and −v. For each edge ab, intersect the two closed neighborhoods. Keep the result only after checking every pair is an edge. This deterministic polynomial procedure can miss cliques in arbitrary graphs; each emitted clique is valid. If the native graph has maximum degree Δ, each candidate has at most Δ+1 vertices and takes O(Δ²) adjacency tests after forming it. The proof verifier independently checks every pair in each proposed capacity against the native binary clauses.

Use event values p_l, interpreting p_−i as 1−p_i. A native clause supplies Σ p_l−1≥0. A witnessed incompatibility clique supplies 1−Σ p_l≥0, including singleton capacity. A single event also supplies p_l≥0. A certificate lists nonnegative rational coefficients on such rows. Expand complementary literals, aggregate exactly, and accept an UNSAT certificate iff all variable coefficients vanish and the constant is strictly negative. Every Boolean model obeys each row. The implementation uses integer/fraction arithmetic and no numerical optimizer. Its unit-weight finder can return unknown even when a general linear combination would refute the input.

For unpadded graph functional pigeonhole with simple incidence graph, pigeon degrees at least three, hole degrees at least two and fewer holes than pigeons, the wide clauses identify the pigeon demand sets. Incidence events sharing a pigeon or a hole are adjacent. Two events incident to a common endpoint have closed-neighborhood intersection exactly that endpoint's star: their other endpoints lie on the same side of the bipartition, so no edge joins those other endpoints. Every star of size at least two is recovered. Distinct stars on opposite sides share at most one incidence, so filtering out recovered pigeon stars yields exactly the hole stars. Each signed incidence occurs in exactly one demand and one capacity. Unit weights leave the negative constant number_of_holes−number_of_pigeons, with every variable coefficient canceled. Permuting variable names, flipping each variable's polarity independently or reordering clauses/literals preserves the proof. No incidence labels, decomposition or supplied graph are needed.

A second search also recognizes the ordinary complete instances with one or two holes: include events with at least two conflict neighbors and events asserted by unit clauses, then test the demand family consisting of clauses entirely on these events. In the two-hole case each positive incidence event has at least two neighbors, while each opposite incidence event has just its partner from the binary totality clause. Unit demands cover the one-hole case. All proposed rows are reverified for arbitrary inputs. Previously unaccounted demand literals may use unconditional singleton capacities.

The checker tests 251 independently permuted/signed graph-FPHP fixtures, including balanced satisfiable controls, one- and two-hole cases and a seven/fourteen-degree fixture. It checks every recovered geometric star against the withheld graph, tests rational rescaling, rejects missing capacity or native edge and negative weights, and checks soundness against direct assignments for 200 further small signed CNFs. The special integral finder is sufficient for this resource family and does not implement general LP feasibility.
Recognition of cardinality constraints from cliques of binary incompatibilities is established SAT preprocessing. See [Biere, Le Berre, Lonca and Manthey, SAT 2014](https://fmv.jku.at/papers/BiereLeBerreLoncaManthey-SAT14.pdf).
