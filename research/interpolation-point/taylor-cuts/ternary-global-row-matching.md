# Ternary global rows over a local exact-cardinality matching

Consider Hs=0 over GF(3), with all coordinates of s nonzero. Fix b exceptional rows. All other rows have four nonzero entries, and after reserving h exceptional columns each other column occurs at most twice in these local rows. This is a presentation-dependent promise. It is recognizable at budget k=b+h in O(4^k poly(input)) time: first reserve non-four-term rows. Whenever an unreserved column occurs in more than two remaining rows, choose three such rows. Either reserve that column or reserve one of the three rows. If no violation remains the found partition suffices. Optional valid short-row propagation can precede this recognition.

Branch over the 2^h signs of the reserved columns. A local row requires exactly two positive signed entries. Subtract the already fixed positives and require exactly q more among its d ports. Reject branches with q outside [0,d]. Introduce d−q private dummy vertices adjacent to all its ports. Every perfect matching assigns d−q inactive port edges to these dummies. A sign appearing twice with equal coefficients has a direct pair edge that selects both ports; with opposite coefficients, one private selector adjacent to the two ports selects one. A singleton port may match the shared clique of free reservoir vertices. Its size is at least the number of singleton ports, and has the same parity as the sum of local quotas minus the number of opposite pairs, which equals the parity of active singleton count. Unused reservoir vertices may be paired. For a free sign with no local occurrence use a disjoint four-cycle. This builds a graph of polynomial size.

For each unfixed variable choose the default sign making its first port inactive, or impose default −1 on the free four-cycle. Tag every edge that selects its first port, with a single tagged edge for the four-cycle. A matching uses zero or one tagged edge per sign. Its label at each exceptional row is the change from flipping that variable, equal to −2 times its default sign times its row coefficient in GF(3). Give all other edges zero label. The target syndrome is the negative contribution of the defaults including fixed values. Thus a source witness extends to at least one perfect matching of the requested label, and any such matching decodes to a checked full-support assignment. No matching multiplicity is required.

Let N be the even graph order. Over GF(2^e) with e even and field size at least max(4,N), insert independent random edge weights. For each of the 3^b group characters evaluate the Tutte Pfaffian with edge weights multiplied by their character values. Fourier inversion projects the perfect matching sum to the requested group label. Symbolically every distinct matching uses its own monomial, including when several matching witnesses decode to the same sign vector. Thus on feasible instances the projection is a nonzero polynomial of degree N/2. A random evaluation is nonzero with probability at least 1−N/(2·2^e). This assumes independent uniform samples; fixed seeds only give reproducibility. From a nonzero evaluation, expand the Pfaffian along one vertex and condition on a contributing edge, adjusting the target, until a witness survives. Equivalent partner restrictions preserving nonzero evaluation also work. Decoding is deterministic once a successful evaluation is fixed. In exact status mode a zero sample yields UNKNOWN. The one-sided witness search costs O*(4^k+2^h 3^b) for a found split, with polynomial factors in input and field size and the chosen repetition count. This use of algebraic perfect matching and finite-group Fourier inversion should be viewed in the established group-labelled matching framework.
