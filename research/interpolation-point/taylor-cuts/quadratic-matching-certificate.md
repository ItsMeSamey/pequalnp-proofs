# Quadratic observable certificates for ternary-labelled perfect matching

For K3,3 use row and column indices 0,1,2 and label ij modulo 3. Every matching corresponds to π(i)=ai+c with a∈{1,−1}; its residue is Σiπ(i)=2a. Equivalently the two reports π(1),π(2) must be distinct and the residue is their difference. Thus target zero is absent. Prescribing a particular edge leaves exactly two permutations, of opposite signs, so the conditional affine hull of residues is all of GF(3). Forbidding an edge also leaves both signs. Summing the three even permutation incidence vectors gives the same integer vector as summing the three odd vectors: both are all ones. No rational affine function of the incidence vector can equal zero on one class and one on the other.

**Deterministic certificate.** Write X_e for Boolean selected-edge indicators. Over GF(3) every perfect matching obeys h_v=Σ_{e incident to v} X_e−1=0, every pair of incident distinct edges has X_e X_f=0, and squares reduce to X_e. Put the h_v, all X_e h_v, and all those pair monomials in a row space. Optionally add X_e X_f for disjoint edges that have no simultaneous perfect-matching extension; this can be checked independently by deleting their four endpoints and running an ordinary maximum-cardinality matching test. Parallel edges remain distinct in the polynomials. These generators are all valid on actual matchings.

Let y(X)=Σ_e ℓ_e X_e be the b-coordinate label report and let t be the prescribed target. Run linear elimination on the augmented rows (generator,0) together with (φ(y(X)),φ(t)) for the constant, all coordinates and all quadratic output monomials including squares. A linear combination equal to (0,1) certifies infeasibility: at a hypothetical target matching the first component evaluates to the second. This tests the entire degree-two separator space generated by the stated rows in polynomial time; store the finite-field row coefficients and replay them. Absence of a proof is inconclusive. For fixed higher degree the same construction extends by including the prescribed monomial multiples within the degree cap.

On K3,3 consider 1−(z_1−z_2)^2, where z_i is the chosen column index at row i. At target zero this equals one. Using the unit row sums and within-row pair conflicts, its pullback equals the sum of X_{1j}X_{2j} over j. Each is a column conflict, so this pullback vanishes by the stated rows.

**Strict connected controls of unbounded size and one label coordinate.** Inflate each of the three left groups to an internal K_(k+1,k) and each right group to K_(k,k+1). In every group call the larger side principal. Add all cross edges between principal vertices of left and right groups; their labels depend only on group indices, ij modulo 3. All internal edges have label zero. Every internal minority side must be matched inside its own group; precisely one principal vertex per group therefore uses a cross edge. The six surplus vertices induce a permutation, and every such permutation extends. These connected bipartite graphs are nonplanar, and every edge extends to a perfect matching. Pinning a cross edge fixes one permutation entry, permitting both signs; an internal edge still permits every permutation. Excluding an edge also permits both signs. For each of the six permutations fix the same principal representative in each group and use the same internal perfect matchings on the remaining vertices. The equal even/odd incidence sums persist, excluding any rational affine 0/1 class lift.

For every i one can derive the macro group unit sum on its cross edges by subtracting the sum of minority-side vertex equations from the sum on the principal side. Its multiples by an edge variable are available in the same row space. Two cross edges using the same macro row or macro column are globally incompatible because exactly one such edge is available per group. Hence the argument for 1−(z_1−z_2)^2 carries over, with optional coextension conflicts. The coefficient identity uses only degree-two rows: replace z_i² by the sum of squared individual coefficients times X_e, replace constant/linear terms by cross-row products via the unit sums, then all remaining equal-index cross pairs are incompatible.

For each of the k+1 physical port numbers take the rectangle in principal groups 0 and 1 on each shore using that port in all four groups. The alternating sum in each rectangle is 1 modulo three, and these rectangles are vertex-disjoint. Under any vertex potential and ownership prescription, vertices of report dimension zero have constant reports. On a rectangle containing only such vertices, the edge labels would be sums of endpoint potentials plus these constants, forcing zero alternating gain. Consequently at least k+1 distinct owners have nonzero report dimension. With one label coordinate their joint kernel parameter κ is at least k. A potential-deletion modulator likewise needs at least k+1 vertices. The family thus separates the quadratic certificate from bounded owner rank/modulator, single-edge affine-hull tests, and planar matching counting; direct exploitation of the macro group structure or alternative methods can still solve it.

The implementation quadratic_matching_certificate.py searches for explicit coefficients and provides a separate row-combination verifier. check_quadratic_matching_certificate.py checked soundness against 100 small brute-force bipartite instances, verified the base and 18-vertex inflated certificates (23 and 360 rows), and rejected changed targets and malformed proofs. These routines detect UNSAT certificates and make no SAT claim when the span test fails.
