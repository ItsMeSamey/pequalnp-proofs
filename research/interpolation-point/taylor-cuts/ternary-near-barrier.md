# Near-barrier reduction for prescribed matching gains

Let B be a supplied vertex set in a loopless multigraph with N even and with edge gains in F3^b. Write q for the number of odd components of G−B and d=|B|−q. Negative d precludes a perfect matching. Fix a perfect matching and let t_H be the number of matched edges from B to component H. It has the parity of |H|. If e internal B edges are matched, the identity
d = 2e + sum over odd H of (t_H−1) + sum over even H of t_H
holds by counting covered B vertices. Declare all internal B edges and all edges to components other than the ordinary one-port odd and zero-port even components exceptional. Their B endpoints total at most floor(3d/2), since t≤3(t−1)/2 for t≥3.

Enumerate disjoint endpoint patterns of at most floor(3d/2) exceptional B endpoints and partners. Reject any component with a positive exceptional count inconsistent with its required parity or with the exceptional definition. Store all attainable gains and original-edge backpointers for parallel endpoint pairs via group sum DP. For positive-port components delete matched endpoints and compute the full perfect-matching gain profile of the remainder. For every untouched even component compute its full profile. Combine these independent profiles by group sum DP. Every untouched odd component H must match one remaining B vertex through u in H. Introduce an edge from the abstract H to each available B vertex v for every attainable combined gain of vu and a matching of H−u. Keep witnesses. Solve prescribed gain on this bipartite instance once per remaining target and join witnesses. Every original matching appears in its own exceptional pattern and every returned witness lifts. The reduction treats decision and witnesses; multiple profile descriptions may represent the same matching.

With maximum component size s, all induced residual profiles are available by subset DP in O(2^s 3^{2b} poly(input)) summed with polynomial overhead per component. Endpoint patterns number (N+1)^{O(d+1)} using original vertices, separately batching parallel gains. Calls multiply by 3^{O(b)} and use the bipartite solver on ≤N vertices. For an optional seed X compute Gallai–Edmonds A,D,C on G−X by ordinary matching and testing which vertices can be exposed by maximum matchings. B=X union A has q=|A|+def(G−X) since the C components are perfectly matchable and D components factor-critical. Thus d=|X|−def(G−X). Enumerating all seeds up to k costs (N+1)^{O(k)}. Component sizes and this discovery cost remain charged.

For r≥2 take the join of the clique B of r+2 vertices with r disjoint triangles, with no edges between triangles. It is 3-connected and bicritical: after deleting any two vertices, each remaining odd triangle fragment needs one connector and there are enough; all leftover connectors pair in the clique. It is a brick. Its independence number is r, so deficiency (2r+1)−r=r+1; deleting odd cycles needs at least one vertex per triangle. Yet d=2 and s=3. Removing three clique vertices gives r−1 clique vertices and r triangles; each triangle vertex can be exposed in a maximum matching, and all clique vertices are needed to obtain deficiency one, so the seed recovers B. With all clique edges gain 1 and other edges gain 0, only residues 0,1 occur, both achievable. Arbitrary labels are handled with the same structural bounds.
