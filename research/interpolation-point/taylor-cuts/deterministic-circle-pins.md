# Deterministic pins and transition bounds for the circle matching reduction

Use the [chord-word reduction](circle-graph-parity-matching.md). At any visit let f_in and f_out denote the two arc bits. At its first visit every chord has negative incoming and positive outgoing signs. Consequently its anchor-token occupancies are y_in=1−f_in and y_out=f_out. The chord is selected if and only if these two flags are equal. This holds for either induced-degree mark. Every perfect matching realizes a consistent pair of visits by the chord gadget; there is no extra independent pin at the second visit.

## Four ordinary matching calls for three positive pins

For prescribed membership b at a named chord, choose its incoming flag and prescribe its outgoing flag to be the same if b=1 and opposite if b=0. For an occupied token delete its incident non-anchor edges; for an unoccupied token delete its anchor edges. The token still must be matched. Thus an ordinary maximum-cardinality matching covering every vertex witnesses the branch, and the standard arc reconstruction returns a set satisfying all pins. Complementing every arc bit preserves both the integer guard equation (both differences change sign) and all chord memberships. The corresponding normalized flags all flip. Therefore fix the first named chord's incoming flag to zero. For p≥1 named chords, at most 2^(p−1) calls to deterministic general-graph perfect matching suffice. Three required positive pins need at most four calls, including arbitrary induced-degree marks elsewhere. For p=0 there is just one call. A circle representation may be supplied or obtained by polynomial-time recognition [Gioan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.3284).

More generally, allow an arbitrary number h of syndrome bits. Besides p assigned pins, let s be the number of nonzero-labelled unpinned chords, and let r be the binary rank of those s columns. Subtract the labels of required positive pins from the target. Gaussian elimination rejects an inconsistent residual syndrome or returns a particular solution and a kernel basis. Enumerate its 2^(s−r) membership vectors. For each, apply the pin algorithm with p+s assignments. This gives at most 2^(s−r+max(0,p+s−1)) blossom calls, with polynomial overhead per call. The exponential parameter is the support, not the number of bits h alone.

The executable pins.py uses NetworkX general matching and the original chord gadget. check_pins.py compares it with independent induced-subgraph enumeration, including every canonical word with at most three chords, every mark vector and every complete prescribed sign vector, plus random tests up to seven chords. All 1,332 checks passed. ETH's independent proof review confirmed the first-visit equivalence.
