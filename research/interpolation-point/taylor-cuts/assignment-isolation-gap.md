# Assignment-level isolation and relative 2-adic precision at the Boolean origin

Use an integral signed monomer–dimer compilation with one distinguished wire bit per logical variable and signed fiber sum 2^g F(x). Present monomers as ordinary perfect matchings if required: represent the monomer at each vertex by an external edge to a parity network, with that monomer's weight. Enforce that the number of such edges has parity equal to the number of original vertices; every original monomer/dimer configuration has this parity. Chain ternary even-parity perfect-matching gadgets with unique carries, and fix the final carry. Each ternary gadget has a mandatory center joined to all three mandatory terminals and a single edge joining the first two terminals. It has one completion on every admissible boundary pattern. Thus the reduction preserves individual weights and has linear overhead.

For nonempty support let lambda_x be the least individual valuation among realizable matchings with named wire values x; infinity denotes empty support. The full graph minimum lambda is computed by minimum-cost perfect matching after assigning each nonzero edge cost v₂(weight). For a model, lambda_x≤g by the fiber identity. A tag constant throughout each assignment fiber preserves that identity. Suppose its valuation u(x) uniquely minimizes over models at x*. The valuation of the signed scalar is g+u(x*), while its least term valuation is min_y(lambda_y+u(y)). Their difference is at least g−lambda_x*. Conversely the explicit assignment-only tag 2^((g+1)·HammingDistance(y,x*)) attains equality, since untagged valuations are nonnegative. Complement-bit tags are implemented with a forced degree-two toggle if necessary. Thus the best possible gap among all isolating assignment tags equals min over models of g−lambda_x. This characterization does not find a minimizing model. A tag of zero at the sole surviving model deletes the signal entirely.

## A linear gap inside the unique satisfying fiber

In the default uncentered signed OR3 graph with distinguished doubled terminal 3, free proper masks containing that terminal have completions of weights +3 and −1, summing to 2. These are exactly the cases where the clause is satisfied and its distinguished literal is false. On t consecutive windows (u_i,v_i,u_(i+1)), put the seven distinct signed ternary clauses forcing each window to 000. There is exactly one model on 2t+1 variables. In six of the seven clauses per window, choose a false literal of that model as distinguished; only the all-negative clause has no false literal. A matching in the unique model uses an odd completion on each of those 6t clauses. In every other valid factor fiber some term has valuation no larger than its fiber sum. Choosing those internal completions independently with consistent wire values gives lambda_x≤g−6t. Thus every nonzero assignment tag leaves a gap of at least 6t within this one satisfying fiber. After dividing the least-term power of two, scalar residues through 6t bits are zero. This is a statement about direct normalized residue extraction in this particular encoding; recognizing the forcing windows, changing gadgets, or computing higher-order cancellations is not ruled out. A linear bit requirement remains polynomial storage.

More generally a ternary integer matching tensor over characteristic two satisfies Σ T_x T_complement(x)=0, summed over the four unordered complementary pairs. Switch the alternating component at a fixed terminal in two matching configurations. It changes one or two of three ports and pairs different complementary classes without changing the product weight. OR3 and COPY3 give 1 modulo two in this identity, forcing an even normalization for integer matching realizations. This absolute normalization alone does not bound the relative gap after stripping common term powers. Independent internal-edge tags can break the fiber identity: in the signed COPY example with unit K4 edges, terminal monomers −1 and center monomer +1, the invalid 110 fiber contains +1 and −1. Tagging only the center-to-remaining-terminal dimer by z leaves z−1 instead of zero.

## An exact deterministic sparse-model regime

Let k=g−lambda+1. No support means zero. If lambda>g, no satisfying assignment is possible. For fixed positive k and a supplied upper bound s on the number of models, decide SAT deterministically in poly(input size,n,s)^{O(k)} time. For each of the first n(s−1)+1 primes p set the integer monomial weights w_i=2^i mod p. The tagged model polynomial over F₂ is Σ_x F(x) Z^(Σ w_i x_i). Fix any model x*. The product of differences between its binary integer encoding and all other model encodings is nonzero with fewer than n(s−1)+1 distinct prime divisors. A tested prime thus isolates its exponent from all others modulo p, giving an odd coefficient at its integer exponent. This is coefficient isolation, not necessarily unique minimum-weight isolation. Standard prime size bounds give polynomial degrees and sieve cost. The case s=0 is immediate under promise. For the surrounding sparse-PIT literature see https://cs-www.cs.yale.edu/homes/spielman/PAPERS/ran3.pdf .

Compute the odd coefficients using a second variable T for valuations. Replace every signed nonzero edge weight a by its odd part times T^v₂(a), retaining any logical Z exponent. Let C_h(Z) be the coefficient of T^h in the resulting hafnian. The original scalar polynomial divided by 2^lambda modulo 2^k equals Σ_{h=lambda}^g 2^(h−lambda) C_h(Z). Its coefficients are multiples of 2^(k−1), exactly reflecting model-count parity. Only working precision k is needed. Pack T and Z into one variable by placing valuation exponents in strides larger than total logical degree. The total valuation degree is bounded by the number of vertices times the maximum input-weight bit length. Theorem 2.1 of Hirai and Namba computes a polynomial-matrix hafnian modulo 2^k in f(vertices,entry degree)^k time for a fixed polynomial f (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.08073). All degrees and prime sizes here are polynomial in the displayed parameters. Prefix restrictions remove terms without changing g, weakly increase lambda, and cannot increase the number of models, so the same decision procedure recovers a witness with at most n further calls. It yields polynomial time for polynomial s and bounded k; the unique-model control already rules out uniformly bounded k for this representation.
