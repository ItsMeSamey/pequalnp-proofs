# Positive three-colouring certificates on high-girth regular graphs

A graph is three-colourable exactly when some independent vertex set S meets every odd cycle. A proper three-colouring gives such a colour class; conversely bipartition the graph outside S and assign S the third colour. A verified S and the bipartition are a certificate for an actual colouring. Finding S of size at most k is the classical Stable Bipartization problem, fixed-parameter tractable in k by Marx, O'Sullivan and Razgon. The elementary search below is independently bounded by n^k; it does not implement that stronger FPT theorem. [Primary FPT result](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2010.2485)

**Reference-bit completion.** Given a proposed two-valued reference h on every vertex, ask whether some vertices can switch to colour 2, leaving all others at h. Use one Boolean select variable per vertex. Add a negative two-clause for every edge prohibiting both endpoints being selected. For each edge monochromatic under h add a positive two-clause requiring at least one endpoint selected. Positive and negative unit clauses force marked vertices into or out of S. These clauses are equivalent to the desired completion, so a linear-time 2-SAT solver returns an actual colouring whenever such a completion exists. Failing on one h does not rule out other references. If a good reference has at most t monochromatic edges, enumerating all sets of at most t edges, bipartitioning the remainder whenever possible, and testing its resulting reference is complete for this promise. Indeed, at the correct deleted defect set all new monochromatic edges form a subset of those deleted; the same S still works. The cost is m^O(t) times a polynomial.

**Topology-only search and forcing.** Given forced-in and forced-out vertices, reject inconsistent pins or adjacent forced-in vertices. Delete the currently chosen independent S and run BFS to find a bipartition or an explicit simple odd cycle. In the latter case every completion must include one vertex of this cycle, excluding forbidden vertices and neighbors of S. Branch on those remaining eligible choices until size k. An empty candidate list rejects, a singleton forces the choice. If at every node the witnessed odd cycle has length at most L, work is O((L+1)^k poly(n+m)); girth gives no upper bound on L. More generally k+1 supplied odd cycles meeting pairwise only at v force v into every transversal of size at most k. Disjoint cycles certify a lower bound on its size. The implemented search validates a colouring without any supplied references.

**A nonbipartite positive control.** Start from a simple six-regular bipartite graph of girth g≥8. On a path a−b−c−d delete ab and cd and insert ac and bd; assume the graph left by the deletions is connected. Degree is preserved, and {a,d} is independent since ad would have closed a four-cycle in the original graph. Deleting {a,d} removes both inserted edges, so the remainder uses the original bipartition. On the other hand a and c are still connected by an even path after deleting ab and cd, making an odd cycle with ac. The new graph has chromatic number exactly three.

A cycle with one inserted edge has length at least g−1: the remaining path between its endpoints differs from their original length-two path, so their union contains an original cycle of length at most their summed lengths. For cycles with both inserted edges, the two old paths pair a with b and c with d, or pair a with d and b with c. In the first pairing each path has length at least g−1. In the second the a-to-d path differs from the original length-three path and so has length at least g−3, while the other path has length at least one. Thus such cycles have length at least g. Old-only cycles have length at least g.

The included checker constructs the symplectic generalised quadrangle W(3,5) directly from normalised points in F₅⁴ and isotropic projective lines for a nondegenerate alternating form. Its incidence graph has 312 vertices, 936 edges, and girth eight by direct verification. The switched example has girth seven. Two disjoint odd cycles certify that one deletion never suffices. The topology-only solver finds a stable transversal of size two and replays the resulting original-vertex colouring. This is a positive, nonbipartite instance in the same degree/girth regime as the radius-two SDP certificate.

**Unbounded girth.** For any requested L≥8, take the ball of radius L in the free group on three generators. For each positive generator, right multiplication is a partial bijection on ball elements whose image stays in the ball. Extend it to a complete permutation by pairing the remaining finite domain and range. Its inverse agrees with backward shifts wherever both points are in the ball. Every nonempty reduced word of length at most L, applied in order to the empty word, follows its proper prefixes inside the ball and ends at a nonempty word. In the finite permutation group generated by these permutations paired with flips of an extra C₂ coordinate, there is consequently no nontrivial reduced relation of length at most L. Its undirected Cayley graph on the three positive and three inverse generators is simple, connected, bipartite by the C₂ coordinate and has girth greater than L. Choose a three-edge path using three distinct generator labels. The first and third edge each admit a detour around the cyclic subgroup of their own generator. These detours cannot use the other deleted edge since its label differs. Hence the two deletions preserve connectivity and the switch above produces arbitrarily high-girth, six-regular, nonbipartite examples admitting a stable transversal of size at most two. This is a size-unrestricted existence construction and supplies no girth guarantee for random regular instances.
