# Six-regular high-girth colouring controls

For each fixed g≥6 there exist arbitrarily large simple six-regular graphs of girth at least g that are expanders, are not 3-colourable, and have a strictly negative direction for A+3I. The corresponding one-hot 3-colour CNFs are genuine UNSAT controls. The witness below only defeats the indicated graph-matrix certificate. It imposes no lower bound on other eigenvalue, semidefinite, or graph algorithms.

Consider the uniform configuration model on n vertices, with N=6n stubs. For a fixed vertex colouring let α be its three colour proportions. Permutation invariance lets us fix the pairing and assign colours uniformly among stub words of composition Nα instead. Generate this assignment by independent colours sampled from α, conditioning on composition Nα. This integral mean is a mode of the multinomial distribution: starting at Nα, transferring one unit from a coordinate at or below its mean to one at or above its mean never increases its probability; every other count vector can be reached by such transfers. Drop zero-probability categories. There are at most (N+1)^3 count vectors. Thus conditioning costs at most (N+1)^3. Before conditioning, each fixed pair is proper independently with probability 1−sum α_i²≤2/3. Summing over at most 3^n vertex colourings gives

    E[number of proper 3-colourings] ≤ (6n+1)^3 (8/9)^n.

The configuration model is simple with probability bounded away from zero for fixed degree. In the uniform simple graph the probability of no cycle of lengths 3 through g−1 tends to the positive product of the zero-count Poisson probabilities, for each fixed g. Markov's inequality therefore still excludes 3-colourings with probability tending to one after this conditioning. Every further fixed-degree high-probability event from the simple graph model, including expansion and the applicable polynomial-calculus degree lower bound, survives. Taking a diagonal subsequence gives examples with girth tending to infinity without a claimed rate.

For a graph of girth at least six the induced radius-two ball about any vertex has 1, 6, and 30 vertices at its three levels, with 6 and 30 tree edges between successive levels. Set a real vector to 6, −3, and 1 respectively on these levels and zero elsewhere. Then its squared norm is 120 and its adjacency quadratic form equals 2(−108−90)=−396. The quadratic form for A+3I equals −36 exactly. This defeats positive semidefiniteness as well as positive definiteness. The motivating one-sided test follows by assigning a unit regular-simplex vector to each of three colours: every edge has inner product −1/2, so the sum of column quadratic forms for A+3I is zero on a proper colouring.

Primary references include Wormald's fixed-length cycle limits (https://doi.org/10.1016/S0095-8956(81)80022-6), Friedman's regular-graph second-eigenvalue theorem (https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0405020), and the degree and size lower bounds discussed separately in colour-gate-obstruction.md (https://jakobnordstrom.se/docs/publications/GraphColouringAverage-casePC_ECCC.pdf).
