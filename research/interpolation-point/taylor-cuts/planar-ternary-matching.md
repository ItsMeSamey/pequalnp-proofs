# Exact additive ternary matching on planar graphs

For a loopless planar multigraph with m original edges and labels in F₃^b, first collapse parallel edges to compute an embedding of the underlying simple graph. Orient each component so that every face except one has odd orientation parity around its consistently traversed boundary. A postorder traversal of a dual spanning tree sets these signs: reversing the edge to a face's parent flips the face's parity and that of its parent. A bridge is traversed twice in its single incident face. Give every parallel edge the sign of its common simple edge. The weighted planar Pfaffian theorem makes the absolute Pfaffian the nonnegative weighted matching sum; adding parallel weights then expands into distinct original edge choices. This is an application of weighted FKT, also described by Anari and Vazirani in [Planar Graph Perfect Matching Is in NC](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.07822).

Let M be the unweighted count, t the target, and B=2^(m+1). For one representative u of each nonzero character pair {u,−u}, give each edge e the positive integer weight B^d, d in {0,1,2} equal to u·g_e modulo 3. Compute the partition function as the exact integer square root of the signed skew-adjacency determinant. Its base-B digits are the counts by total exponent, since even the unrestricted matching count is at most 2^m. Sum digits whose indices equal u·t modulo three to obtain S_u. Pairing conjugate characters in the finite Fourier inversion gives the exact target count

    (M + sum_u (3 S_u − M)) / 3^b.

Indeed a pair contributes 2 for zero dot product and −1 otherwise. There are (3^b−1)/2 pairs. Each determinant uses entries of polynomial bit length and the partition integer has O(nm+1) bits. The time is 3^b times a polynomial in the explicit input; polynomial workspace suffices.

For witness recovery fix a pivot vertex and try its original incident edges in order, deleting the two endpoints and subtracting the chosen label. Every residual matching extends with the forced edge, so all residual Pfaffian terms still have the same sign under the inherited orientation: the sign factor for adjoining that fixed edge is independent of the residual matching. This justifies using the same stored orientation even when face parities in a new embedding might differ. A positive residual mass gives the next step. Check the original matching and its full label at termination.

More generally, given k edges whose deletion makes the graph planar, enumerate all pairwise vertex-disjoint subsets of these edges. Fix the selected edges, discard the others and their incidence with used vertices, and solve the residual planar target. The cases are disjoint and cover all matchings, giving exact count and witness in O*(2^k 3^b). This extension assumes a supplied deletion set; the executable implements the planar branch. Unlike the general randomized characteristic-two test, zero certifies absence and even multiplicities are counted fully. The deterministic reduction uses a standard Pfaffian algorithm and does not give a general deterministic bound for arbitrary graphs.
