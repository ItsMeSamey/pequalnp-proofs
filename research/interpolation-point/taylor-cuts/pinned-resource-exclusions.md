# Certified exclusions and literal marginals

Give each signed literal event ℓ an unknown marginal m(ℓ) in [0,1]. Impose m(ℓ)+m(¬ℓ)=1 and, for each original clause C, impose ∑_{ℓ∈C}m(ℓ)≥1. A model defines integral feasible marginals. Any integral feasible point of these constraints defines an original Boolean model by its positive-literal entries, and its witness may be checked by direct clause evaluation. This does not require a promised integral polytope. Fractional feasibility alone leaves the result unresolved.

For each pair of distinct events prove that they cannot hold together before placing their edge in the conflict graph. A native binary clause (¬ℓ∨¬q) supplies one proof. Complementary literal events also conflict. For any candidate K, check every pair's edge certificate; then ∑_{ℓ∈K}m(ℓ)≤1 holds on all models and their mixtures. The original cover constraints need not form a complete description of those mixtures. Exact rational infeasibility has a Farkas certificate, checked by multiplying each inequality by a nonnegative rational weight, each equality by an arbitrary rational weight, and verifying a contradiction in the summed rows. An LP solver's floating-point infeasible status alone is insufficient as a certificate.

For a simple bipartite graph FPHP let x_e indicate selecting incidence e. On selected literal events the native conflict graph is its line graph. For two incidences ab and a′b on one hole b, their common closed neighbors are precisely the incidences of b. A further common neighbor would have to join a to a′, on the same shore. The same reasoning holds for pigeon stars. Complement edges add no common neighbor to this pair. The degree-one hole bound is supplied by m≤1; empty holes may be ignored. A pigeon of degree zero gives an empty clause. Summing the pigeon covers and the hole bounds shows |pigeons|≤∑_e m(x_e)≤|holes|. Thus the contradiction has unit integral row multipliers. Variable renaming and independently complementing any source variables permute the signed event labels and preserve the argument. On FPHP with additional distinct binary clauses, arbitrary extra edges can obscure the old common-neighborhood candidates. Recovering the native star guarantee calls for retaining the original graph's candidates whenever a second pass adds derived edges.

## Extending the conflict graph

Let a supplied bijective affine chart x=x₀+Bz parametrize a certified affine superset of all source models. The identity chart is always allowed. For candidate events ℓ,q, pin both true in this chart. Gaussian inconsistency proves their exclusion immediately, with the inconsistent linear combination as witness. Otherwise parametrize the pinned solutions z=z₀+Cy. Each native violated-clause indicator f_C is zero on every model. Substitute the parametrization and reduce using y_i²=y_i over F₂. If 1 belongs to the binary linear span of the resulting f_C, retain the XOR subset yielding 1. An independent verifier redoes substitution and compares all squarefree coefficients. No original model satisfies both pins, since there all summands would be zero. For example, (¬a∨¬b∨y) and (¬a∨¬b∨¬y) restrict at a=b=1 to violation indicators 1+y and y. Their sum certifies exclusion without a native exclusion edge.

This test is polynomial for bounded original width, since affine substitution preserves the degree bound and the number of squarefree monomials through fixed degree k is polynomial. With unbounded width one must separately bound intermediate support or explicitly charge for expansion. A fixed-degree Nullstellensatz test can add monomial multiples whose *formal* product degree before multilinearization is within the chosen bound: any such linear combination equal to 1 is again sound. Assigning an event to every available pair of affine forms is unnecessary; a supplied polynomial dictionary of signed affine events may be tested in the same manner.

In this extension, complemented real marginals represent truth of affine events; XORs of seed variables do not become ordinary sums of their real marginals. Keep the GF₂ proof and the rational LP proof distinct. If the variables of the LP are the original literal marginals, an integral feasible solution plus the original clause covers still recovers a genuine model. If the LP instead uses only marginals of chart-defined events and claims a witness in a restricted chart, require a simultaneous GF₂ realization of its integral event values and replay all source clauses and restrictions. A projection that drops any source model cannot justify a global UNSAT certificate without accounting for the omitted fibers.

The conflict-graph extraction overlaps the syntactic cardinality detection of Biere, Le Berre, Lonca and Manthey, who also study semantic inference by unit propagation. The pinned XOR-of-violations test above supplies an independently checkable certificate for the additional exclusions.
