# Summing a source packet before mode enumeration

At a parent interpolation point, expand old row tensors into Boolean monomials in Y, and old column tensors into Walsh characters of X. A selected global row monomial I and column character S have a scalar kernel factoring across the original Cauchy packets. In one packet let r be the number of selected real rows, m the number of real Boolean columns, and restrict S to this packet. The contribution is K=sum_X (−1)^{|X∩S|} product_{i in I}(sum_j W_ij X_j). This follows by first summing every old Y against its interpolation selector. Global row and column coefficient masses, including their normalizations, remain part of the algorithm.

Each map from the r selected rows to columns contributes zero unless its image includes S; for a surviving map the Boolean sum is (−1)^|S| 2^(m−size of image). Thus if r<|S| the answer is zero. Otherwise distribute d=r−|S| extra occurrences among the m columns, adding these to one required copy per member of S. Let μ be the resulting multiplicities. For each μ sum the term

    (−1)^|S| 2^(m−|supp μ|) per(W[I, repeated columns μ]) / product_j μ_j!.

This gives binom(m+d−1,d) terms. For the characteristic-zero Cauchy matrix C_ij=1/(a_i−b_j), duplicate columns are allowed in the permanent by confluent Borchardt. Index columns by (j,k), 0≤k<μ_j; put A_i,(j,k)=(a_i−b_j)^(−k−1) and B_i,(j,k)=(k+1)(a_i−b_j)^(−k−2). The determinant ratio det(B)/det(A) is the repeated-column permanent, by applying Borchardt before coalescing and dividing each determinant by the same vanishing Vandermonde factors. Multiply by the r-th power of the source's scalar Cauchy prefactor. Distinct real row and column slot indices ensure det(A) is nonzero away from Cauchy poles. If the evaluation is at a grid node, the source matrix itself is a scalar multiple of a partial permutation and the Boolean sum is immediate; alternatively take the removable symbolic limit. Empty rows or columns use the usual empty-product conventions. A complete fixed global sector is computable in polynomial time if the packet defects are bounded, even when their sum over packets grows.

Never invert the confluent determinant or multiplicity factorial directly in the residue ring of Appendix D. Evaluate the scalar kernel exactly over the source number field using the chosen integral lift of ζ, before reduction. There is no V-dependence in W. Combine the proper normalized Walsh coefficients with creation-tagged lifts of the old entries. For fixed sector and polynomial-size rational lifts, degree and bit bounds for these operations follow from polynomial-size products, rational linear algebra and determinant bounds; the finite extension degree is O(log s). If aggregating sectors with nonintegral Walsh normalization, use exact rational accumulators or enough guard bits to divide by the known total power of two only after summing. The working ring precision specified for the original contraction is unchanged. The number of required sectors and the bit cost of enumerating them must be counted. No bound on this number follows from the original theorem.

For an actual sparse CNF source, normalized Walsh coefficients of a binary subdivision equality are 1/2 on the empty and full pair, zero otherwise. A row at a positive unit clause selects its sole row, and a free unary variable row selects none. Distant-from-saturated sectors can therefore occur even in valid CNF reductions. At L=2, t=−1, α=4, ζ=1, two real slots give W=[[-20,−30],[−15,−20]]. For two selected positive rows and two independently summable columns, K=2650, compared with permanent 850 and determinant −50. Repetitions give the remaining 1800. The same local summation is compatible with subdivision equalities whose other endpoints feed free variable rows in another packet. This small example checks source coefficients and logical tensors; it does not invoke the large-s recursion on a four-wire input.
