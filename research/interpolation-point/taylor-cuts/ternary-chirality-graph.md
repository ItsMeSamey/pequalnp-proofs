# Nonzero ternary kernels as oriented triangle colourings

Every labelled proper triangle has a unique affine palette parametrization c(t)=a+st with s=1 or 2 in GF(3). A matching between two such triangles is properly coloured precisely when the two slopes agree and offsets differ. Indeed different slopes meet at a unique site. A private triangle between two consecutive matchings therefore enforces slope agreement with arbitrary terminal offsets. Its offset can be any of the three values outside the two endpoint offsets.

Create one anchor triangle per coordinate. In each nonempty normalized row use a private cycle of joints, one per nonzero coefficient. The incoming and outgoing joints are the first two sites of a fresh occurrence triangle in that order for +1 and in reverse order for −1. Wire that triangle to its anchor using the private middle triangle and the two matchings. Consequently the outgoing colour minus the incoming colour is exactly the coefficient times the anchor slope. Closing the joints enforces the row equation. In a row of length two both triangles use the same undirected joint edge, which simply appears once in the graph. A singleton row is contradictory and the compiler returns K4. An empty row is ignored.

To extend a kernel vector, choose arbitrary anchor offsets and one initial joint colour per nonempty row. Compute successive joint colours by addition around the row, fill the remaining occurrence triangle site, then choose a middle offset different from the two terminal offsets. Decoding reads each anchor slope. For a fixed kernel vector the number of extensions is the sum over these freely chosen offsets of the product over occurrences of (2 if the anchor and occurrence offsets coincide, 1 otherwise). Thus existence and witness extraction hold directly without a uniform multiplicity assumption.

With n coordinates and L nonzero normalized entries, the construction uses exactly 3n+5L vertices and at most 3n+12L edges unless a singleton row triggers the fixed contradiction. The encoder and decoder are linear in sparse graph size once rows are normalized; the supplied dense-row compiler scans its input once.
