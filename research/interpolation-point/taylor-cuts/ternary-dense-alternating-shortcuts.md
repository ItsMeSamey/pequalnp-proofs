# Bounded-support modular matching in dense graphs

Let M be any perfect matching in a simple graph with edge labels in the additive group GF(3)^b. Relative to M, represent an alternating cycle by its cyclic list of base matched pairs. Each pair has an entry and exit joined by its base edge, and each exit has an edge to the next entry. Length L below counts pairs. Choose a target matching minimizing the number k of pairs moved from M; its moved pairs form disjoint such cycles.

A chord from exit i to entry j, where j is between two steps and L−1 steps ahead, gives a new perfect matching: replace that segment by the chord and use base edges for the intervening pairs. This decreases k. One may also restore an entire cycle to base. Each operation has a gain defined as new total minus old total. Operations in intervals with disjoint outgoing-step sets are simultaneously applicable, even when successive intervals share one endpoint. At that endpoint the incoming and outgoing edges attach to distinct ports, and the endpoint pair remains moved. Gains add since altered matching edges and newly restored base edges differ across operations.

If there are at most Δ missing candidate neighbours per exit (on the opposite side for bipartite graphs), put h=Δ+2. For L>h divide the cycle into floor(L/h) intervals of h steps and a leftover. The first exit in each interval has Δ+1 potential chords to entries at offsets 2 through h, so at least one works. For L≤h take a whole-cycle restoration. Each cycle thus supplies q≥1 independent operations with L≤(2h−1)q.

More generally, suppose every two disjoint t-sets have an edge between them, or impose this only for opposite shores of a bipartite graph. Take h=2t. In each h-step interval use the t exits in positions 0 through t−1 and the t entries in positions t+1 through 2t. A crossing edge skips at least one pair. Entries and exits refer to different original vertices, and L>h ensures that no pair repeats in this window. The preceding packing proof again applies.

The combined sequence of gains over all cycles is zero-sum-free: any nonempty zero-sum subsequence would preserve the target and reduce k. Its length is therefore at most 2b, since the Davenport constant of GF(3)^b is 2b+1. In particular k≤2b(2h−1). This also holds for b=0 by using the base matching itself.

With a supplied k bound, colour-code the base pairs by k colours using a deterministic k-perfect hash family. For each colouring build the oriented states of the base pairs; each has its pair's colour. An arc from u to v is available when exit(u) can match entry(v), with gain equal to the new edge label minus u's base edge label. Exclude same-pair arcs. From each start state, subset path dynamic programming records reachable terminal states and gains, consuming distinct colours, then closes to that identical start state without spending its colour again. The sum of arc labels is the cycle's relative gain. Store the resulting (colour set,gain) table. Subset partition dynamic programming combines cycles of disjoint colour sets, allowing unused colours. Distinct colours certify pair disjointness and every k-pair witness is captured by some hash. Predecessors recover actual matching edges. Tables have at most 2^k 3^b poly(n) entries, and partition joins plus hash enumeration cost 2^O(k+b) poly(n). Treat b=0 or k=0 directly.

The Δ bound is directly observable. The t-bound assumes the indicated class promise or supplied bound, since failure to find a smaller witness by itself does not certify a no-instance. Related deterministic dense exact matching results for scalar red-edge counts use local search and structural classes including bipartite t-hole exclusion. [El Maalouly, Haslebacher and Wulf](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2024.33).

## General graphs of bounded independence number

Suppose the underlying simple graph has independence number at most a. Use t=R(4,a+1,a+1) and h=2(t−1). In each h-step window with L>h take positions 0,2,…,h. Colour each unordered pair of positions by a missing exit–exit edge if present as a nonedge, otherwise by a missing entry–entry edge if present as a nonedge, and give all remaining pairs the third colour. A monochromatic missing-edge clique of size a+1 would give an independent set of original vertices. Consequently there are four positions i<l<j<k where every pair has both of its same-port edges present.

Add the edges exit_i–exit_j and entry_l–entry_k; restore the base matching on the positions strictly between i and l and strictly between j and k. Drop the forward matching edges with step indices i through l−1 and j through k−1. Every vertex now has exactly one matching edge: the removed segment endpoints have exactly the four ports used by the new edges, and the segment interiors have restored base pairs. The forward edges on [l,j] remain in the matching; they are traversed backwards when describing the resulting cycle. Spacing makes both restored sets nonempty. For adjacent windows that share a boundary pair the left operation may use its entry and the right operation may use its exit. All gains are additive as before. Hence k≤2b(4t−5). This is deterministic FPT in b and the promised independence bound a. The cited earlier [bounded-independence exact matching algorithm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11988) treats a prescribed integer number of red matching edges.

Loopless multigraphs admit the same proof with defects and independent sets measured in the underlying simple graph. Distinct parallel edges retain their own labels. In addition to cycles of length at least two in the port-state DP, for each base edge add a one-colour singleton candidate for each different parallel edge with gain new label minus base label. Such a one-pair change is an entire short alternating cycle available for whole-cycle restoration. Parallel arcs between two different pair states may be retained individually or deduplicated by gain. Loops play no role in the ordinary perfect matching convention.
