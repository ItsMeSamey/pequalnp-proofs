# Local exponent matching

At a fixed baseline tuple and evaluation point, each separated factor S_g of taylor-diagonal.md has scope comprising both the indices in F_g and the indices j whose statistic has nonzero contribution from x_g. Supply a statistic tree decomposition in which every such scope fits in some bag. Attach the factor to one such bag. Set c_j=min(floor((J−1)/e_j), sum_g deg_{y_j} F_g). Larger y exponents vanish modulo z^J or are unreachable; larger u exponents cannot match.

Each bag retains z modulo z^J and one pair of marker exponents from 0 through c_j for each resident statistic. Multiply child messages and local factors by truncated polynomial convolution. At a statistic's last bag keep only equal powers a in both markers, multiplying by a! and summing. The running intersection property ensures that no factor outside the subtree uses this statistic, so its linear diagonal functional commutes with multiplication by all remaining factors. The final polynomial is the exact Taylor contribution. A bag has at most J times the product of (1+c_j)^2 entries. The square of this bound times polynomial overhead suffices for naive multiplication. Charge local polynomial construction and baseline tuple costs separately. This is polynomial even with growing global rank and precision at bounded primal width, or logarithmic width and bounded individual caps.

An interpolation example uses negative two-literal clauses along a path, with replacement P_i(T)=(1−T)x_i. The identity is restored at T=0, and at t=1 the polynomial is the sum over x of the product of (1−z²x_i x_{i+1}). Joint rank is n and scopes have bounded width. This witness concerns a single evaluation point. An independent cycle test used ℓ_i=x_i+2x_{i+1} and F_i=1+z x_i y_i, an invertible statistic map. At J=4, direct and tree-based evaluations matched for 3,4,5,6 variables with respective coefficient vectors (8,24,36,27), (16,64,128,144), (32,160,400,600), (64,384,1152,2144).

A succinct center F(y,z)=product_C(1−V_C(z y)), where V_C is a clause's Boolean violation polynomial, paired with singleton groups supplying ℓ_i=x_i, has incidence-treewidth one. At J=3m+1 evaluation at z=1 gives #3SAT for the m input clauses. Thus a bound solely in bare incidence-treewidth and polynomial-time pointwise access to a high-scope factor cannot imply efficient counting without a complexity collapse. The positive bound charges its full scope or an explicit valid internal factorization.
