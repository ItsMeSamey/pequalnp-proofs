# Arbitrary-arity NAE and a characteristic-two certificate test

For any signed k-literal NAE gate, k≥2, the constant Fourier coefficient is 2^(1−k)(2^(k−1)−1). Every nonempty even port subset has coefficient −2^(1−k) times its product of literal signs; odd subsets vanish. Selections form the binary incidence cycle space C. Their phase q is parity of touched gates plus the linear parity of selected negative signs. Deleting ports down to a pair restricts q identically. Expand idle weights as 1+(2^(k−1)−2)1[untouched]. If the negative locus is sum-free, the previous subspace argument yields #SAT ≥ 2^c product_{k≥3 gates}(1−2^(2−k)) when the binary equations are consistent, where c counts binary components including isolates. Any compatible pair involving a large gate's first literal may replace this gate. A compatible pair exists since a current model has some literal different from the first. Restriction preserves the certificate; parity union–find yields and verifies a witness in O(E α(n)). On arbitrary uncertified inputs failed greedy search does not certify UNSAT.

For deterministic recognition, project a cycle basis onto each gate. A gate with rank d is touched iff at least one of d independent linear measurements is nonzero. Equal projected kernels give identical touch predicates and cancel in q when they occur an even number of times. With remaining ranks at most d, initial recognition by Boolean polynomial identity runs in E^O(max(1,d)). For k ports meeting h components after deleting the gate vertex, the rank is k−h: the admissible port subsets have even parity in each component, and every such pattern can be extended using spanning trees.

For arbitrary rank and arity work in characteristic two and form
     
    R(X,Y)=sum_{x,y∈C} q(x)q(y)q(x+y) product_e X_e^(x_e)Y_e^(y_e).

Distinct monomials record negative triples uniquely, so R vanishes identically iff the condition holds. Degree is at most 2E. Each q is the field sum of a constant, individual marked-edge bits, and single-gate untouched tests. In q(x+y) split each marked bit as x_e+y_e. Expand O(E³) terms. Untouched constraints in the first two factors pin individual layer edges to zero; selected bit factors pin edges to one. The third factor gives at most ONE entire gate v with x_e=y_e on its incidences. For this case drop the parity equation at v in both layers, since every other equation in its component implies it. Merge each equal pair into an ordinary edge between the two remaining variable endpoints, with weight X_eY_e. Eulerian subsets of the new graph correspond bijectively to the original x,y satisfying those equalities. With no such equality use two separate layers. Propagate all pins through any equality. Conflicts contribute zero. Delete zero-pinned edges. Multiply weights of one-pinned edges and toggle syndrome at their endpoints before deleting them.

The remaining term is a weighted parity-subgraph enumerator of an ordinary graph. Construct one port terminal per incidence and give each external edge its input weight. At each vertex of degree d with demanded parity t, add (d−t mod 2) private dummy terminals and link all local terminals by unit-weight clique edges. Selected external edges leave an even local clique iff their count has the desired parity. An even clique on j vertices contributes (j−1)!!=1 in characteristic two, also for j=0. Thus the weighted perfect matching sum equals the target enumerator. Pfaffian signs disappear in characteristic two, allowing polynomial-time evaluation on a general graph. Summing all terms evaluates R, retaining cancellations.

Sampling independent field values from a field of size ≥8E detects a nonzero R with probability ≥3/4 by Schwartz–Zippel. Any nonzero evaluation is definitive evidence that the condition fails; a zero may miss a violation. Repetitions are independent. A small such binary field can be built by scanning degree-ceiling(log2(8E)) binary polynomials and testing irreducibility, within polynomial time. The random error concerns certificate recognition only. A verified SAT witness is valid regardless of the random test, and the guaranteed search on promised inputs is deterministic.
