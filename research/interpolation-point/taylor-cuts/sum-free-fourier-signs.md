# Formula-aware SAT certificate from signs of even edge selections

Assume distinct variables per clause and all widths between 2 and k. Unit propagation can run first. Let V be the binary space of occurrence selections S having even total at each variable. In Boolean sign variables write the individual false-literal polynomial as (1−s_e X_v)/2. Expanding clause indicators gives satisfying fraction 2^(−E) times the sum over S in V of (−1)^q(S) product over untouched clauses of (2^|c|−1). Here q is the parity of the number of touched clauses plus the linear sign recording all selected (−s_e). This follows by direct averaging of each original spin; E is the number of occurrences.

If Q={S:q(S)=1} is sum-free then for each homogeneous subspace W of V, its signed sum is nonnegative. If w∈W∩Q, translation by w embeds W∩Q in its complement in W. Conversely, any x,y,x+y∈Q yield a negative signed sum on their plane, since q(0)=0. Expand the magnitude as product_c [1+(2^|c|−2)1_{c untouched}]. Every term imposes homogeneous zero constraints on S and has nonnegative coefficient. Selecting all clauses leaves just S=0 and strictly positive coefficient. The satisfying fraction is therefore at least product_c(1−2^(1−|c|))>0. This proves SAT for each input passing the test.

Build a basis of V by eliminating one edge indicator per variable as the XOR of the others. The touched predicate is an OR of at most k linear indicators, hence q has algebraic degree ≤k over GF(2). Sum-freeness is exactly the identity q(x)q(y)q(x+y)=0 on the Boolean cube. Form the algebraic normal form reducing x_i²=x_i and y_i²=y_i, and test whether it is identically zero. Its degree is at most 3k, so expansion and testing cost E^{O(k)} deterministic bit operations. Recognition is a positive SAT certificate and failure leaves the decision open. A polynomial witness-search guarantee needs additional proof, since pinning a variable can change the test. No general SAT improvement follows.

For example take (x OR y),(x OR NOT y),(x OR z),(x OR NOT z). Every nonempty variable subset touches strictly more clauses than its size, so no nonempty matching autarky exists. Let A be the parity of the two x selectors on the y clauses; its value equals their parity on the z clauses. Let Y,Z be the equal selectors on y and z respectively. Then q=(A+1)(Y+Z), so the negative locus lies in Y+Z=1 and is sum-free. Assignment x=true satisfies the formula; x is pure. This separates matching autarky recognition alone. Uniform random assignments find a witness with expected trial count at most the reciprocal of the certified satisfying fraction.

The accompanying check_sum_free_fourier.py verified all Fourier sums against brute-force counts on 500 seeded formulas and the lower bound in all 265 accepted cases. Walsh–Fourier expansions of Boolean constraint functions are established; see https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0004370221001107 . No priority claim is made for the sum-free certificate.

## Deterministic witness and limitation after pure deletion

Set each pure variable to satisfy its incident clauses and remove those clauses. The even-selection space of the remaining formula embeds in the original by zeroing deleted edges, and the sign polynomial is its restriction, so sum-freeness persists. In the pure-free residue, for each occurring variable v its signs are mixed. Restrict q to selections on v alone, denoted V_v. Every even selection then touches an even number of distinct clauses and q is just its nonzero linear literal-sign parity. Its negative coset spans V_v.

For two distinct mixed variables u,v and selections x∈V_u,y∈V_v, the correction q(x+y)−q(x)−q(y) is mod-2 inner product of their touched-clause indicator vectors. Whenever both signs are negative this correction must be zero by sum-freeness. By bilinearity and the negative-coset spanning property, V_u and V_v are orthogonal in clause coordinates. If they have a clause i in common, choose any other clause j on u and k on v; both variables are mixed so those choices exist. The pair selections {i,j} and {i,k} have odd overlap unless j=k. Hence both variables have degree exactly two with the same two incident clauses. Each residual clause has width at least two, so its variables force a whole component consisting of exactly two clauses on identical scopes. Each variable has opposite signs in these two clauses. Satisfy them by making one chosen literal of the first clause true and another false, thus satisfying both; remaining variables are arbitrary. This produces a deterministic linear-time witness following pure peeling for every passing formula. It also shows that this sufficient criterion adds no recognition power beyond pure peeling plus accepting these simple two-clause components. The quantitative lower bound on satisfying fraction remains valid.
