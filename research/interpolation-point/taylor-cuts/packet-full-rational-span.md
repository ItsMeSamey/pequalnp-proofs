# One full shared-input packet spans the full rational polynomial space

Write D=2L−1, d=LD, U={1−L,…,L−1}, C(T)=∏_{s=−L}^{L−1}(T−s), c=C/C′(0). A physical word with active rows I and shared selected columns X has polynomial ∏_{i∈I}∑_{y∈X}c(T)/(T−y+i). Neutral rows equal the sum of their two literal outputs, so these words belong to the integral linear span of pins.

For each t=1,…,L choose D−t+1 singleton-column words, one for each consecutive block of t offsets in U. Each block fits in some column's offset interval [y−L+1,y]. Also choose t−1 words with rows 0,…,t−1 and column pairs {p,p+1} for p=0,…,t−2. Together with the neutral word these form a rational basis of all polynomials of degree at most d.

Induct on t. It suffices that the D leading coefficients in the layer of degree tD are independent. Divide by c(T)^t. This triangularly maps the coefficient test to the Laurent coefficients in orders −t through −t−D+1. Every linear test on them has the form [T^(−1)] f(T)F(T) at infinity where deg f≤t+D−2 modulo polynomials of degree ≤t−2, and F is the row product after division. Singleton tests give consecutive divided differences of order t−1 of f on U. Their vanishing permits subtracting a polynomial of degree ≤t−2 so that f vanishes on U. For t=1 this already proves the claim. Otherwise f=HG with H=∏_{u∈U}(T−u), deg G≤t−2.

For the two-column tests the poles of F of order two occur at d′=p−r for 0≤r≤t−2. Their leading coefficients are (−1)^r b_r with b_r positive, the absolute value of ∏_{i≠r,r+1}(1/(i−r)+1/(i−r−1)) over 0≤i<t. The residue of fF there is (−1)^r b_r H′(d′)G(d′). Simple poles contribute zero. Since H′(d′) has sign (−1)^(L−1−d′), summing residues gives the equations ∑_r b_r w_{p−r}G(p−r)=0 with w_j=(L−1+j)!(L−1−j)!.

By the beta integral w_j=(2L−1)!∫ s^j W(s)ds/s on (0,∞), with W=s^L/(1+s)^(2L). Write x=s/(1+s). Integration by parts with Euler derivative s d/ds replaces j^k in each moment by Q_k(x), where Q_0=1 and Q_{k+1}=(2Lx−L)Q_k−x(1−x)Q′_k. Derivatives of W are W times bounded polynomials in x and |p−r|≤L−2, so boundary terms vanish. The leading coefficient of Q_{k+1} is 2L+k times that of Q_k. Up to common positive factors the matrix in the monomial basis for G therefore has entries ∫ s^p Q_k(x) dμ for dμ=W(s)∑_r b_r s^(−r)ds/s. Triangular basis change reduces to Q_k=x^k. Expanding both Vandermonde determinants, applying Fubini, and permuting the integration variables shows the determinant is the integral over ordered distinct positive s_i of the product of the Vandermondes in s_i and x_i. Both are positive there, and μ has positive density. Thus G=0 and the induction follows.

Clearing κ=C′(0)^L sends all relaxed words to integral coefficient vectors. Each selected basis row has coefficient 1-norm ≤[2(1+L)^(2L)]^L, by multiplying the bounds for each cleared row (including neutral rows). The coefficient determinant Δ of the cleared basis is a nonzero integer; its adjugate sends Δ times the integral ambient lattice into the cleared physical span. Over the 2-local integers this gives b(L)≤(d+1)L[1+2Llog₂(L+1)] by Hadamard. The bound does not control the sharp doubling increment or establish a Boolean SAT algorithm.
