# Arbitrary signed 2-SAT with k independent parity equations

First find a 2-SAT assignment b by SCCs. If none exists the mixed instance is unsatisfiable. Write x=b XOR y, complementing exactly those occurrence signs whose coordinates are one in b. The new 2-CNF accepts zero and every nonempty clause has a negative literal. It is Horn and comprises implications, negative exclusions, or negative units. Translate every row (a,t) to (a,t XOR a·b). The rank k of the parity matrix is unchanged.

For a direct generator proof let y be an inclusion-minimal parity-feasible model. Its true variables form an upward-closed set in the directed graph of unary implications. Contract SCCs. Deleting any subset of the minimal selected SCCs *themselves* leaves an upward-closed set; negative exclusions and units remain satisfied. Each SCC has a k-bit signature obtained by applying an independent parity row basis to its support indicator. If more than k minimal SCCs existed, a nonempty subset would sum to zero and could be deleted, contradicting minimality. Thus guessing at most k representative variables and taking implication closure suffices. This gives a deterministic O(poly(input)·Σ_{j≤k} binom(n,j)) witness algorithm, including k=0. The implementation delegates the equivalent Horn closure step to horn_closure_xor.py. The larger Horn theorem follows from the degree-k target indicator and triangular evaluation on feasible closures of sets of at most k seeds. No union-closure assumption is needed.

For hardness, reduce from k-Clique using k labeled copies of the vertex set, one per color. Forbid selection of two vertices in one class. Across distinct classes forbid pairs corresponding to the same or nonadjacent original vertices. All these are negative binary clauses. In each class require odd selection with one XOR row. This enforces exactly one selection per class, and all choices form a clique precisely when the original graph has a k-clique. Rows have disjoint nonempty supports and rank k on nonempty vertex inputs. The translation is polynomial in the graph size and k. Thus rank-parameterized decision is W[1]-hard even on a zero-valid negative 2-CNF, ruling out FPT under FPT≠W[1]. The argument gives a hardness lower bound; it does not establish W[1] membership for unrestricted inputs.
Brakensiek, Gopi and Guruswami already give the XP exponent for k modular constraints in §3.2 of [CSPs with global modular constraints](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2019/013/). The closure-based proof and reference implementation here specialize to XOR rows.
