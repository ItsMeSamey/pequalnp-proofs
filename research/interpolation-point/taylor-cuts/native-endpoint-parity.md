# Parity of a single normalized active-node scalar is counting-hard

For a simple maximum-degree-three graph G without isolates, build its vertex-cover CNF with a positive binary clause per edge. The variable tensors are unary neutral or binary/ternary equality, so no tree-splitting ports occur. Stack L copies in diagonal full packets by the genuine minimum conditional-expectation labeling in native-odd-grid.md. Outside filler units receive their signs after packing, which depends only on incidence.

Take t=1−L and lift z=1. In the quotient F(t+2L)/2^U modulo two from native-endpoint-content.md, row layer L−1 routes to column layer zero normally, while rows at x<L−1 wrap to x+1. Each logical bit in column layer zero is ordinarily read (it has a positive clause endpoint), and every other special bit is unread. The ordinary layer computes vertex-cover parity, equal by complementation to independent-set parity. A wrapped port in state zero contributes 1+Y and in state one contributes Y. Gluing the two ports with Y²=0, ℓ(1)=ℓ(Y)=1 yields NAND edge matrix [[1,1],[1,0]]. After equality tensors identify variable states, each binary clause counts local OR-satisfying states through the two NAND edges: if zero, one or two of its adjacent variable states are zero, there are respectively 0,1,3 possibilities. Thus the effective clause is NAND modulo two and the wrapped layer gives independent-set parity as well. The product of L copies is this same bit.

Every outside active unary clause reads an ordinary bit, a normalized bit, or zero. For every ordinarily read bit make all referencing units negative (pin zero, sum one). For every normalized bit Y with references choose one corresponding unit positive and the others negative, so ℓ(Y(1+Y)^r)=1. An unreferenced normalized bit contributes one, and zero-input clause rows are made negative. Define matched reads using active clauses only, since variable rows of these units are neutral. The full outside factor is one and the encoding is constructive.

Independent-set parity on degree-three graphs is ⊕P-hard by a direct reduction. A triangle with two rail terminals enforces XOR modulo two on them after summing its remaining vertex. A length-three path enforces equality since the Boolean independence edge matrix has cube equal to identity mod two. Make such dual rails per Boolean variable, and copy each rail to its literal occurrences along equality paths arranged as a chain. Each rail terminal already has two triangle edges and needs only the first path edge. Each occurrence copy uses at most two path edges and one clause edge. For each original three-literal clause add one free vertex adjacent to the three copies encoding its satisfying literal states: it has two extensions if every literal is false, otherwise one, implementing OR modulo two. The resulting degree-three graph has independent-set parity equal to the parity of the input satisfying count.

Therefore one full normalized scalar at a specified active node/lift of a legal native initial SAT packing is ⊕P-hard. Its required precision exceeds U. This distinguishes arbitrary nonlinear old tensors from polynomial-time affine-row parity Pfaffians. It does not prove a lower bound for SAT decision.

The normalized parity polynomial in z has degree at most U because [z^k]F is divisible by 2^k. Thus the active odd-node truncation q_t>U and precision p_t>U already determine the parity at z=1, by summing its supplied coefficients. The independent script check_native_endpoint_parity.py evaluates the original rational kernels directly (768 jet-content cases, 96 outside filler layouts and six complete small stacks).
