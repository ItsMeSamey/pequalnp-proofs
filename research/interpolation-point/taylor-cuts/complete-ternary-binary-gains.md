# Binary-valued ternary matching gains on complete hosts

On a complete bipartite K_{n,n} with n≥4, or a complete ordinary K_N with even N≥8, let a scalar F₃ edge-additive gain omit one residue. Normalize its two allowed residues affinely to 0 and 1. As a rational Boolean-valued function on perfect matchings the result f is affine in edge incidence.

Any 2-versus-2 incidence relation among actual perfect matchings gives an integer difference of f-sums between −2 and 2, congruent to zero modulo 3. Hence it holds over Q. On four distinct vertices i,j,a,b define d_ij(a,b) as the exchange difference from ia,jb to ib,ja, with the former sign positive. It is independent of the residual perfect matching: the two ways of adding any two residuals give an exact two-versus-two relation. Bipartite indices use rows i,j and columns a,b. The derivative negates if rows or columns are switched. For three distinct rows i,j,k and three distinct columns a,b,c, all six vertices distinct in the ordinary case, the two length-three paths in the braid relation imply
d_ij(a,b)+d_ij(b,c)−d_ij(a,c)=d_jk(a,b)+d_jk(b,c)−d_jk(a,c).
To see this, represent the three-row assignments by column lists. The steps (ij),(jk),(ij) from [a,b,c] have differences d_ij(a,b),d_jk(a,c),d_ij(b,c). The other three steps (jk),(ij),(jk) have d_jk(b,c),d_ij(a,c),d_jk(a,b). Both terminate at [c,b,a]. Complete the other vertices arbitrarily.

For fixed columns a,b,c use four eligible rows i,j,k,l. The directed row pairs along i→j→k→i→l→j→i transfer the same defect from (i,j) to (j,i), while antisymmetry changes its sign. Hence every column defect vanishes over Q. The row triangle holds by transposition. In the bipartite case fix one reference row and column. The triangle identities express d_ij(a,b) as the rectangle difference of edge costs c_ia=d_{i,i₀}(a,a₀) (zero at a repeated row or column). Since transpositions connect all perfect matchings, f−Σ c is constant.

In the ordinary graph let h_ia be the mean of f over all matchings containing ia. Pairing by the vertex transposition (ij) gives h_ia−h_ja=Σ_{x∉{i,j,a}}d_ij(a,x)/(N−3), since the partner of j is uniform. Subtract the corresponding b formula. The two exceptional terms each give d_ij(a,b), and the other N−4 differences equal it by the column triangle. Consequently c_ia=(N−3)h_ia/(N−2) has exactly the desired exchange differences. Physical four-switches connect the complete graph's matching family, proving the assertion. The proof applies to any rational f satisfying the pair relations, establishing rational linear generation of the dependency space without asserting quadratic generation of the toric ideal.

The real Boolean degree-one classification of Filmus, Lifshitz, Lindzey and Vinyals (Theorem 1.3 of Complexity measures on symmetric group and beyond, https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~yuvalf/measures.pdf) therefore applies to normalized ternary gains: they are constant, depend on the mate of one vertex, or depend on hitting a fixed triangle. The complete bipartite version gives a row- or column-partner function by the real symmetric-group classification of Ellis, Friedgut and Pilpel, also discussed there. These structures themselves omit a residue, including complements. No result for arbitrary graph restrictions follows from this complete-host theorem.

The size condition matters. On K₆ label a fixed three-edge perfect matching by 1. Its overlap with another perfect matching is 0,1, or 3. A hypothetical rational binary lift could be averaged over the stabilizer of the marked matching; the two edge orbits would force an affine function of the integer overlap, inconsistent with normalized values 0,1,0. On K₃,₃ a marked diagonal perfect matching gives overlap 0 or 3 on even permutations and 1 on odd ones, contradicting the rational equality of the two parity triples' incidence sums. These examples concern representation rather than decision complexity.
