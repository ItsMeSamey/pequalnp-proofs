# Reachable tensor rank for signed permutation rails

Cut a rail into k contiguous passes. A certificate gives one order of the n controls and an orientation for each pass. In a forward pass all occurrences of each control form a single block, and the blocks follow the supplied order, permitting omissions. A reverse pass uses the opposite order. Site transitions and signs can vary throughout. For each block compute its permutation for either control value. The certificate can be checked by scanning. For fixed k one can also try the polynomially many sets of k−1 cut locations and 2^k orientations, check contiguity and topologically sort the precedence constraints after orienting each pass.

Process controls in the common order. For every prefix assignment record the tuple of its k pass permutations as the pure tensor of their permutation matrices. Extending by a fixed control value acts linearly: multiply each forward-pass factor on the left and each reverse-pass factor on the right, then tensor these local linear maps. The total over both values is the sum of these two maps. Keep a linearly independent list of actual prefix tensors spanning every reachable tensor, and exact rational coordinates of the sum over prefix assignments. For each next value and basis member generate its pure image. At most twice the previous rank of candidate tensors span the next reachable space. Keep candidates even if their current coefficient is zero when certifying reachable rank.

No D^k by D^k matrix is required. Over the rationals the Frobenius inner product of two pure tensors is the product, over passes, of the number of inputs on which their two permutations agree. A Gram matrix of these inner products has exactly the same linear dependencies as the listed tensors, by positive definiteness. Gaussian elimination picks independent columns and expresses the weighted prefix sum in that basis. At termination, taking the trace of the ordered product of all pass matrices is linear on their tensor product, so evaluate it separately at each basis tensor and combine with its coefficient. For several independent closed rails with common controls one instead groups the factors by rail, multiplies the traces, then extends linearly. This latter variant is not part of the single-rail reference API.

If r is the maximum reachable prefix rank, this is a deterministic algorithm polynomial in r, n, k, the state count D and the explicit input size. Gram entries have at most O(k log(2D)) bits. In any independent prefix basis the Gram right-hand side for the actual sum has at most O(n+k log(2D)) bits; its entries are sums over at most 2^n assignments. The common denominator of the coordinates divides the Gram determinant of the basis, of polynomial bit length by Hadamard's bound. Gaussian elimination on each layer therefore has polynomial bit cost. The implementation can stop when a supplied rank cap is exceeded. To recover a witness, successively fix each control whenever a positive count remains; restricted prefix spaces are contained in the unrestricted prefix spaces. Replay the final selected permutations for a closing start. This computes the number of pairs (control assignment,closing start), including every valid free-start state.

For an unbounded-pass example take a single three-state rail whose pass is the word 1 through n with alternating optional transpositions (12) and (23). Repeat it k≥2 times. The noncommuting precedence graph has one strongly connected component (n≥2), and the occurrence alternation graph is complete. Yet every reachable prefix tensor has the form P tensor-powered k times for P in S₃, giving rank at most six independently of k and n. The same bound holds after independent constant conjugations of the transitions in each pass, and more general instances receive the measured rank bound. Arbitrary Barrington words need not have small rank for any useful pass certificate.

A separate sufficient rerouting test exploits the trace itself. Around a proposed adjacent swap, compute the rational spans of possible prefix matrices U and suffix matrices V by treating selector occurrences independently. Each span has dimension at most D² and is obtained by propagating a matrix basis through the binary site choices. For all basis U,V and admissible adjacent values, test tr(C V (AB−BA) U)=0 for the fixed closure twist C. Bilinearity makes this exact on the relaxed independent-occurrence contexts and sufficient when controls repeat. A finite sequence of certified swaps preserves the number of closing states for every actual assignment; an original witness start should be recovered by replay. The Gram-pass implementation does not perform this optional rerouting search.
