# A Fourier certificate for greedy signed NAE2/3 witness search

Pair complementary CNF clauses on the same distinct variables to expose signed NAE factors. Write the literal spin as s_e X_v. The NAE3 indicator is (3−Σ_{i<j}s_i s_j X_i X_j)/4, and the NAE2 indicator is (1−s_i s_j X_i X_j)/2. Fourier port selections form precisely the binary cycle space of the clause-variable incidence graph. Every selected clause uses exactly two ports. With b binary and t ternary factors the Boolean count is
     
    2^(n−b−2t) sum_S (−1)^q(S) 3^(number of untouched ternaries).

Here q(S)=|S|/2 plus the number of negative literal marks in S, modulo two. Its polar is the ordinary binary edge-intersection form S·R. Use a spanning-forest cycle basis and compute the quadratic polynomial q on basis coordinates. Certify that its negative locus Q is sum-free by testing q(x)q(y)q(x+y)=0 in Boolean multilinear algebra, in polynomial time (degree at most six). A linear q or q expressible as a product of two homogeneous linear forms satisfies this condition.

On every cycle subspace W the signed sum is nonnegative: translating W∩Q by any element of W∩Q embeds it in W outside Q. Expand each ternary weight as 1+2 times the indicator that the clause is untouched. Each resulting term restricts to a cycle subspace. Requiring all ternaries untouched leaves only binary inequality cycles; their signed sum is positive exactly if the binary parity equations are consistent, as follows directly by expanding the pure NAE2 count. If they are consistent and have c components including isolated variables, this single term gives the lower bound 2^(c−t) on the count. Inconsistent binary equations already imply UNSAT.

For deterministic search start with the consistent binary system in parity union–find. Replace one ternary clause by any of its three pair inequalities compatible with that system. Such a pair exists by current satisfiability. The replacement deletes the third incidence; its cycle space is a subspace, and q restricts unchanged, since a selected clause still uses two ports. Thus sum-freeness and binary consistency persist, ensuring current satisfiability inductively. At termination solve the binary equations and verify the resulting witness on the original clauses. Runtime after recognition is O((n+m)α(n)). A failed sum-free test leaves the formula undecided.

Under variable degree ≤2 the polar vanishes, as shared incidences at any variable occur in pairs. This covers all signs and gives at least 2^(n−t) solutions for pure NAE3. The unsigned read-twice always-satisfiable result appears in https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2019.21 . A degree-three-variable example consists of NAE(x,NOT a,b), NAE(x,b,c), NAE(x,c,a). The common marked-port bits A,B,C on its degree-two variables give q=(A+B)(A+C), a split quadratic of rank two. The formula has eight solutions. All its complementary OR pairs contain both signs, so the earlier pure-literal simplification cannot explain this example. General NAE3 and unrestricted SAT are outside this certificate.
