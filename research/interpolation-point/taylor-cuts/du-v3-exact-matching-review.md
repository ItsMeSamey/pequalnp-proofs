# Independent review of Du v3, 9 April 2026

The unrestricted algorithm of Proposition 2.3 can give a false positive under its printed independent block convolution. Label a six-cycle 0 through 5 in cyclic order and color only 01 and 45 red. The two perfect matchings have respectively two and zero red edges. The tight cut 012 versus 345 has only blue crossing edges 23 and 50. Each contracted square has red supports {0,1}. Their unconditional sum contains one. Gluing requires agreement on the actual crossing edge. Section 3.2 recognizes boundary compatibility, but Section 8 invokes Proposition 2.3. This example leaves the specialization on braces unresolved.

The valuation claimed in Proposition 7.1 step (a) can fail under the stated hypotheses. Take bases λ+1, λ, λ+2, exponents (0,1,3), and mask diag(1,J₂), where J₂ is the all-ones 2-by-2 matrix. Its first cofactor is λ(λ+2)((λ+2)²−λ²)=4λ(λ+2)(λ+1). Both nonzero terms survive modulo λ+1 individually and their difference vanishes. This contradicts the claimed order zero, without contradicting the nonzero determinant conclusion of Proposition 7.1 on this example. Proposition 7.6 invokes the matching-to-nonvanishing direction of this criterion. The numeric unmasked Vandermonde in Remark 7.2 has determinant 6.

Appendix A reports eight hypotheses in four groups: three visible exceptional-family/bridge statements, one state-level narrow-pair closure, two full-state base/propagation statements, and graph-to-packet extraction plus the McCuaig classification input. Its conditional theorem does not discharge these hypotheses. For a brace the evaluation grid has O(n³) points, with O(n³) arithmetic operations per determinant. Small integer grid points yield input entries with O(n log n) bits and minors with O(n² log n) bits, so the printed arithmetic time is distinct from bit time. No independently validated unrestricted polynomial solver follows here.
