# Deterministic witnesses for affine flat avoidance

On a Boolean affine seed space of dimension D, suppose each of the input constraints excludes a flat of codimension at most k, described by linear equations. For any fixed k, decision and witness recovery take polynomial space and time γ_k^D 2^{O_k(D log log(D+3)/log(D+2))} times a polynomial in the input length, where γ_j=(2^j−1)/2^{j−1}. The rank-two case has γ₂=3/2.

Gaussian elimination parameterizes the current affine space, skips empty forbidden flats, and detects a forbidden flat equal to the whole space. Rank-one constraints are enforced by a linear equation. At current maximum rank j≥2, select greedily a maximal family of r rank-j normal spaces in direct sum. Write A=2^j−1. Each selected constraint excludes one output value. Translate the j affine outputs of each selected constraint so this value is zero.

If A^r γ_{j−1}^{d−jr} ≤ γ_j^d, enumerate the A^r possible nonzero output vectors on the selected constraints. Each restricts exactly jr dimensions. Every other rank-j normal space meets the full jr-dimensional span by maximality, so its image has dimension at most j−1. Recurse at that smaller rank. Rational powers with integer exponents allow exact polynomial-bit comparisons.

Otherwise r>β_j d for β_j=log(γ_j/γ_{j−1})/log(A/γ_{j−1}^j)>0. Indeed γ_{j−1}/2=1−2^{1−j}, whose jth power is less than 1−2^{-j}. A nonzero j-bit output satisfies u·output=1 for exactly 2^{j−1} of the A nonzero directions u. For a block of h constraints, every target in the alphabet of A^h nonzero output tuples is thus covered by a γ_j^{-h} fraction of all direction tuples. Repeatedly choose a direction tuple covering the largest number of uncovered targets. Each iteration removes at least this same fraction of the remaining targets, producing at most γ_j^h(2+h log A) tuples. All choices can be constructed by exhaustive comparison in A^{O(h)} time and space. For each j and each block size through b=ceil(log₂(D+2)) precompute these lists once, in polynomial time and space for fixed k.

Split the r constraints into blocks of at most b, using full blocks then one possibly shorter block. For each tuple from the Cartesian product of the block lists impose its r one-form equations. All r normal vectors are independent by directness. Every feasible witness survives somewhere. Other constraints may still have rank j. Recurse with d−r free dimensions. Composing the recorded affine maps recovers original witnesses.

With potential γ_j^d, the sum of children's potentials is at most the parent's in the full-vector case, and at most its value times (2+b log A)^{ceil(r/b)} in the direction case. On any path there are O_k(log(D+2)) direction steps since each removes a positive fraction of the remaining dimension. Full-vector steps lower the rank. The sum of r across direction steps is at most D. Hence all pathwise multiplicative excesses are at most (2+b log A)^{D/b+O_k(log(D+2))}. By distributing parent mass among children in proportion to their potential, this bounds the total leaf count by the asserted time bound. Every internal level incurs at most polynomial additional work per descendant and depth-first enumeration stores only polynomially many bits. Linear or empty base cases require Gaussian elimination only.

For rank two, no assignment-independent scalar weighting turns a shortened list of these templates into exact counting. Up to reordering, the compatibility matrix is J−I on three symbols. Its tensor power over r coordinates is rationally invertible and its unique weights giving constant one are all 2^{-r}. This statement concerns this particular linear combination.

On native three-literal NAE constraints, two pair differences specify the excluded event even for signed literals. In a hypergraph on n vertices with c components including isolated vertices the homogeneous difference span has dimension n−c. Ordinary 3-CNF can be converted by splitting each clause with a private auxiliary and using a shared reference, at dimension up to n+m. This transfer gives no uniform unrestricted 3-SAT speedup.
