# Safe exchanges for prescribed matching cardinality

Let H be a finite loopless undirected multigraph, N a maximum matching of size ν, and k≤ν a nonnegative cardinality. Write s=ν−k. For k<ν all original k-matchings are connected by single edge exchanges e↔f satisfying ν(H−(V(e)∪V(f)))≥ν−2. Enumerate candidate pairs by ordinary maximum matching. Their common residual has cardinality k−1.

Given M, aim to increase its overlap with N. No alternating path in M△N has more M than N edges, since that would augment N. A mixed path has an N end edge f with precisely one M blocker e. Replace e by f; deleting all their endpoints loses only f and at most one other N edge. When no mixed path exists and M is not contained in N, all off-N edges lie on alternating cycles, and s>0 supplies an isolated free N edge f. Select e on any cycle; switching the entire cycle in N gives a maximum containing both e and f. Their endpoints thus cost at most two edges from a maximum. The exchange increases overlap. This includes parallel two-edge cycles. Once in N, k-subsets of N exchange safely. This proves the stated connectivity; k=0 has just one state.

For degree-d polynomial P on edge incidence, or after linear substitution on additive gains, first evaluate a baseline k-matching. Test all safe pairs on Q(R)=P(R+e)−P(R+f), where R is any residual (k−1)-matching avoiding both endpoints sets. Every Q has degree at most d−1 and the new slack is at least s−1. Discard infeasible residual levels. A nonzero Q yields two actual original matching candidates; at least one has nonzero P. If the baseline and all differences vanish, the safe-exchange connectivity proves P vanishes everywhere. Handle k=0, no feasible k-matching, and constant P directly.

An affine F₃ test works at every slack: attach |V(H)|−2k independent ports joined to every core vertex. Perfect matchings project to exactly the k-matchings, and each desired linear label on actual edges extends by zero on port edges. Use Lovász's finite-field perfect-matching incidence span, homogenize affine constants by a star, and recover an original witness via edge-deletion self-reduction. Empty-graph cases are direct. Consequently s≥d−1 suffices for d≥1 over F₃. Over any field with effective arithmetic s≥d suffices using constant leaves and ordinary matching only. Each level scans O(m²) pairs; direct evaluation of nested differences multiplies evaluation cost by at most 2^d. Overall cost (m+2)^{O(d)}poly(input and arithmetic costs). Repeated nonvanishing queries on the annihilator of evaluated rows recover the entire witnessed degree-d span with polynomial overhead in its explicit feature count. A prescribed F₃^b gain has degree 2b, hence works for s≥2b−1. Degree-two scalar targets are solved at every positive slack.

At the first excluded degree unrestricted scalar perfect matching embeds. Check the original even-order graph H has a perfect matching first; otherwise the answer is NO. Put k=|V(H)|/2, add s disjoint dummy K₂ edges, and retain k. The resulting graph has slack s. The polynomial (1−(g−t)²) times the product of (1−x_i) over the s dummy edges has degree at most s+2. Its nonzero k-matchings are exactly the original target perfect matchings, with original IDs. For fixed s expanded input remains polynomial; for varying s charge its expansion or use a circuit. This is a reduction only, not a hardness lower bound.

For s≥d+1 there is also a direct (m+2)^{O(d)} catalogue over every field, without recursive matching-span tests. It suffices to use matchings with at most d off-N edges. If r=|M−N|>d, at least s N-heavy components exist. Each nontrivial N-heavy path supplies an end N edge with a unique off-N blocker, distinct between components. Isolated N-heavy edges are free and can be paired with other distinct off-N edges. This gives min(s,r) distinct replacements whose new edges are in N and which are simultaneously feasible in every subset. A (d+1)-cube alternating sum vanishes, reducing evaluations to states with fewer off-N edges. For each fixed off-N set of size at most d, the remaining choices form a uniform slice of unblocked N edges; all degree-d evaluations on that slice are spanned by those within d replacements of any one slice reference, by the same cube argument. All retained rows are actual k-matchings.
