# Full-support ternary systems of width four

For a row of d nonzero signed terms over F₃, q positive terms give sum 2q−d. In a homogeneous row of width one this precludes a solution. At width two the terms must be opposite. At width three they must be identical. Substituting each such relation in every row and combining repeated columns preserves all assignments subject to the recorded signed equality. There are at most n−1 nontrivial unions. Discard zero rows with zero target and reject zero rows with nonzero target. The backend checks width after reduction.

Every surviving homogeneous row has exactly two positive terms. If each variable occurs at most twice, draw an edge per variable between its rows, attaching singletons to a common hub. Every row has degree four, and the hub has even degree by the degree-sum identity. Traverse an Euler circuit in each component, resetting the initial sign at hub departures. At each real visit use opposite signs on arrival and departure. Variable coefficients fix the transport of signs along each edge. A component with hub always works. In a closed component, the propagation factor around the circuit is the product of all incidence coefficients times (−1) raised to the number of edges. That number is twice the row count. Thus propagation closes precisely when the incidence product is +1. Necessity follows by multiplying the four signed terms at every row. Each balanced row has product +1 and every variable occurs twice, so all variable values cancel. The constructed signs balance every row.

For general multiplicities branch on each of the h signed representatives occurring more than twice. For each row enumerate residual quotas q with 2(q+fixed positives)−d congruent to its target. There are at most two choices and just one possible original quota in a homogeneous width-four row. For each resulting vector of quotas construct an ordinary graph. The q slot vertices of a row connect to every variable occurrence port in that row. Ports matched to slots represent positive terms. Connect equal-coefficient double ports by an edge, allowing exactly both or neither to be positive. For opposite coefficients add a vertex adjacent to both ports, enforcing one positive. Make the singleton ports a clique and add one vertex to this clique if the graph otherwise has odd order. Under any feasible sign assignment match the designated positive ports to slots and inactive double ports using their gadgets. The remaining singleton ports and optional padding have even cardinality by total parity and hence pair inside their clique. Conversely every perfect matching supplies compatible signs for all variables meeting every quota. In the absence of singletons an isolated padding vertex correctly signals odd unmatched order.

Time is polynomial per enumerated branch, O*(2^(h+a)) for at most a ambiguous rows per surviving branch. The graph can have quadratically many edges due to the singleton clique. Both h and a refer to the post-substitution presentation. A cap hit yields UNKNOWN unless an explored branch already supplies a witness. Signed equalities backlift the solution and the implementation checks every original row. This algorithm decides feasibility and does not count assignments.

The deterministic checker compares 594 small systems with exhaustive sign enumeration, including every signed second row and all nine target pairs for two parallel four-variable scopes. Additional controls exercise the high-occurrence cap and an inconsistent closed Euler component. The source adapter is maintained separately.
