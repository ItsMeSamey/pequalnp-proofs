# Closing a five-state permutation program

Barrington's Theorem 1 converts a depth-d fan-in-two AND/OR circuit over input literals into a width-five permutation branching program of length L≤4^d. Its output is the identity on false inputs and a five-cycle on true inputs. Lemma 1 conjugates all transition alternatives to prescribe any desired five-cycle without increasing length. The proof gives a deterministic construction. [Barrington, JCSS 38 (1989), 150–164, Theorem 1 and Lemma 1](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~barring/publications/bwbp.pdf).

Choose ρ=(1 2 3 4 5) as the true output, using the program's sequential action convention. After the L instructions insert the fixed transition ρ⁻¹=(1 5 4 3 2), and identify its destination state with the initial state. The closed rail has L+1 state blocks. Conditional on an input and its initial state the entire trajectory is unique. If the formula is true, the closed product is the identity and all five starting states work. If false, the product is ρ⁻¹ and no starting state works. Therefore the rail model count is exactly five times the input formula's count. Prescribing one legal starting state instead yields a parsimonious reduction.

Encode the five states by distinct three-bit codes. Each instruction reads its original Boolean variable, including on repeated queries. Forbid every local seven-bit corner except the ten legal transitions, one for each selected input bit and legal starting code. This costs 118 clauses of width seven per instruction. The fixed final transition excludes 59 six-bit corners. The full lists exclude illegal state codes without separate domain clauses. The encoding uses n+3(L+1) declared variables. Include any input variable absent from the program in the ambient assignment universe, or account explicitly for its factor of two. No bound on input-variable occurrences follows from rail width.

For a 3-CNF with m≥1 nonempty clauses, a fan-in-two OR for each clause has depth at most two, and a balanced conjunction has depth at most ⌈log₂m⌉. Thus L≤16·4^⌈log₂m⌉≤64m². Constants, an empty conjunction, or an empty clause can be handled directly. For width k≥1 replace the additive two by ⌈log₂k⌉. The polynomial blowup gives a polynomial reduction and exact counting factor; a subexponential-in-rail-length algorithm does not automatically have subexponential cost in the original variable count.

## Executable compiler and search

The [compiler](/workspace/scratch/compression/barrington_cnf_rail.py) chooses two five-cycles whose chronological commutator gives the requested target. It implements negation by reversing and inverting the program and absorbing a fixed target into the final gate. OR is obtained by applying this negation to both children and their AND. Tautologies, contradictory literals, empty clauses and the empty conjunction retain their Boolean meanings. A constant formula has no input instructions and one fixed tail.

For M≥1 total literal occurrences, the implementation's balanced trees give O(M²) instructions even with varying clause widths. The dyadic weight of a tree of height h is 2^h. Merge the two smallest weights a≤b into 2b. If a=b the sum is unchanged. Otherwise every other weight is a multiple of b, and rounding the former sum up to the next multiple of b does not change its least power-of-two upper bound. Thus the final weight per clause is at most twice its width and the final conjunction weight is at most 4M. Induction on depth bounds instruction count by the square of this weight. The compiler is polynomial; the contiguous-cluster witness search has no general polynomial rank guarantee.

The [fixture checker](/workspace/scratch/compression/check_barrington_cnf_rail.py) compares the entire permutation and its closed trace with the CNF truth value on every assignment of nine small formulas. It also checks the actual witness returned by the cluster search, including on formulas with an even number of solutions.
