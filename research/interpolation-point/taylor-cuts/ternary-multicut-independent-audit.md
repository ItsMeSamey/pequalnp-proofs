# Audit of the split-cover multicut algorithm for prescribed matching residue

Let D be the exchange contraction of a bipartite perfect matching, with n matched pairs and GF(3) arc gains. If no nonzero directed cycle exists only the base gain is attainable. Otherwise let k be its minimum arc length, found by shortest paths in the three-state lift: a nonzero closed walk decomposes into simple cycles, at least one nonzero. Loops and distinct parallel gains are included.

Use the labelled three-sheeted cover. Split each (v,i) into an in-node and out-node joined by a directed internal arc. A lifted original arc runs from an out-node to the next in-node. Impose the symmetric multicut demands ((v,0)_in,(v,1)_in), one per v. The LP of Iwata and Yoshida asks for total arc length at least one on each pair of oppositely directed paths joining a demand. Set every internal arc length to 1/k, all transition arcs to zero. Either directed path projects to a closed walk of nonzero gain, hence uses at least k original transitions, each preceded by one internal arc. Thus these lengths are feasible and have total cost 3n/k. The graph has 6n nodes. [Theorem 6](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2013.127) deterministically finds an edge cut within O(log n log log n) of LP cost. One may even use 1/(2k) on each internal arc, since the constraints sum the two path lengths.

For each cut internal arc delete its original vertex, and for each cut transition delete its original tail. The number of distinct deleted vertices is at most the edge-cut cardinality. A remaining nonzero cycle through v lifts, by repeated traversal, to paths in both directions between sheets 0 and 1 at v. This uses that a nonzero ternary element generates the group. Every arc of these surviving paths avoids the edge cut by construction. This contradicts a separated demand, so each remaining SCC is conservative. The transversal X may be computed irrespective of any target solution.

On the short side, test the shortest cycle's gain and the presence of any nonzero cycle disjoint from it. These conditions decide success immediately when applicable; otherwise the cycle itself is a transversal. On the long side use X. The boundary enumeration and component routing in bidirected-ternary-matching.md require only conservative SCCs outside the chosen vertices. Choose whichever verified transversal is smaller. The running time is m^{O(sqrt(n log n log log n))} with polynomial preprocessing, hence 2^{o(n)} after retaining at most one original arc per ordered pair and gain. Predecessors in the component dynamic program and ordinary integral flows recover actual cycles and then original matching edges. Small n can be handled by direct enumeration.

## Literature check

The [2025 ESA paper](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ESA.2025.19) discusses the lack at that time of a deterministic subexponential algorithm for unrestricted unary exact-weight perfect matching. Exact red count, prescribed ternary residue and general graphs are distinct scopes. The [2025 preprint on blown-up graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.12552) gives a subexponential method under bounded bandwidth of the prototype.

An April 2026 [preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01571v3) claims a polynomial algorithm for arbitrary bipartite exact matching. Its Proposition 2.3 uses an unconstrained convolution of brace target sets at tight cuts. On a cyclically indexed C6, colour 01 and 45 red and all other edges blue. The only perfect matchings have zero and two red edges. The tight cut between {0,1,2} and {3,4,5} yields two C4 blocks each independently allowing zero or one red edge; both crossing edges are blue. Therefore the proposed convolution returns a false positive at target one. Also its Theorem 2.7(iii) identifies slice vertices with exact matchings: in K2,2 with both diagonal edges red, the t=1 slice has one fractional vertex and no integral matching. These counterexamples concern the stated decomposition algorithm and slice lemma, and do not independently settle its brace nonvanishing conjecture. No priority claim follows from this literature check.
