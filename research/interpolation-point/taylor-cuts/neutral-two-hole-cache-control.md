# Cache growth after vertex-potential checks

Take two independent connectors x,y joined to all vertices of two disjoint odd cliques L of size 2q+1 and S of size 3. Give internal edges gain zero, connectors to L gain one, and connectors to S gain zero. Every full perfect matching uses one connector per clique and has gain one. Every edge is allowed. Neither clique admits any nonzero vertex potential inducing its zero gains, so a common connector potential cannot exist for |L|≥3.

Fix a perfect matching M. For each a in L, deleting a leaves a factor-critical graph: deleting another L vertex leaves two odd cliques for two connectors; deleting an S vertex leaves both cliques even and both connectors can go to L; deleting a connector leaves only S odd. In the all-second-holes Gallai–Edmonds recursion, if a belongs to an internal L pair of M, the entire graph minus a is the one odd component, exposed at the mate e of a. The neutral child deletes exactly a,e. Since e sees every remaining L vertex, all remaining internal pairs can be chosen next. Of the q original internal L pairs, any subset containing the first removed pair and leaving at least one internal pair gives a distinct still-nonpotential induced state. The exponential bound concerns this canonical recurrence with optional direct potential stopping.

A fixed splitter {x,y} solves every two-hole profile in polynomial time. Enumerate partners of its at most two surviving vertices, then match each residual clique if and only if its size is even. All residual gains are zero, and the chosen connector edges determine the gain. This also specializes the zero-slack near-barrier decomposition.

The bound also survives recomputing an arbitrary deterministic baseline matching per induced graph. Once all first-hole queries are made on a common remaining L of size 2s+1, its baseline gives s next internal L pairs. There are at least (s)_j ordered histories of j deletions. A fixed final set of 2j removed vertices accounts for at most j! histories: at each step the matching fixed by that induced graph has at most the number of pairs still to be removed entirely inside the prescribed remaining deletion set. Thus at least binom(s,j) different induced states survive. This counting argument does not require a hereditary choice of matching.
