# Selector-free systems that defeat affine hitting spaces

For every even p≥40 construct binom(p,2) pairwise-disjoint rank-two normal planes in F₂^p. Their wedges form a basis of Λ² and each has generators u,v with disjoint coordinate support. Start with the direct coordinate-pair packing. If wedge rank is deficient, find a nonzero alternating matrix A annihilating all current wedges, and choose i,j with A_ij=1. For each of the p−2 other coordinates choose (u_k,v_k) from 00,10,01. The expected number of matches of any of u,v,u+v with any restriction of an existing plane direction or zero is at most 3(1+3m)(2/3)^{p−2}<1 for p≥40 and m<binom(p,2); the bound decreases thereafter. Fix these coordinates by conditional expectation using exact integer arithmetic over powers of three. Then choose (u_i,v_i)=(a,0),(u_j,v_j)=(0,b). The four resulting values of uᵀAv have mixed coefficient one, so one value is one. The new wedge increases rank and the three new directions avoid all earlier directions. The construction has polynomial bit complexity, O(p²) gates and O(p³) bits in the usual dense mask encoding.

Forbid (u·x,v·x)=(1,1) on each plane. Zero and every unit vector satisfy the instance, so even complete affine consequence closure leaves the full ambient span. No two normal planes share a direction, precluding a verified selector clique on three independent normal directions. More generally three distinct directions in a single plane can certify a low-rank local predicate, but its active rank is only two and cannot explain large-dimension selectors. If a hitting space W met every plane, the wedges in the quotient would all vanish. They span the whole exterior square in that quotient, forcing its dimension ≤1. Therefore the minimum hitting dimension is p−1, despite a maximum direct packing of p/2 planes. Under every affine restriction the same wedge spanning property holds on the raw quotient because its induced exterior-square map is surjective. A gate whose projected wedge survives is rank two and cannot be removed merely from the conditioning values. A nontrivial direct-sum split X⊕Y of surviving gate planes would omit all cross wedge directions X⊗Y. The result concerns the stated presentation and does not give a general lower bound on decision; the displayed systems have the zero witness.
