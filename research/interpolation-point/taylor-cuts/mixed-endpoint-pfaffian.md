# A single Pfaffian sums ordinary and normalized bits at residual parity

Work over a commutative ring of characteristic two, for instance the truncated polynomial ring F₂[z]/(z^J). In a product of affine rows, each term selects a row constant or one weighted bit reference per row. An ordinary bit is summed over both Boolean values. An unused normalized bit Y=2X instead has local moments ℓ(1)=ℓ(Y)=1 and ℓ(Y^d)=0 for d≥2 after reduction modulo two. Assume every ordinary bit occurs in at most two rows of the product. Count occurrence slots in the actual row expansion, merging repeated mentions in a row by adding coefficients when desired.

Create a vertex for each row, with monomer (unmatched) weight its constant. For each normalized bit use one vertex with monomer weight one and give its reference edges their row coefficients. This vertex permits zero or one chosen reference, supplying weight one in both cases. A weight z from wrapping stays on the reference edge. For an ordinary bit with two occurrences, make a separate terminal for each reference, put monomer weight one on both, and join them by an edge of weight one. If neither reference is chosen, the terminals have two completions and contribute zero. If one or both are chosen, there is exactly one completion. This agrees with the unnormalized Boolean moment sum_x x^d, which is zero for d=0 and one for d≥1 in characteristic two. For a bit with a single occurrence use one terminal of monomer weight zero. An ordinary bit with zero occurrences forces the whole branch to zero. A normalized bit with zero occurrences contributes one.

Expanding the weighted sum over all matchings of the graph, each row vertex enforces one local choice, and the disjoint bit gadgets implement exactly the above moments. Therefore this matching sum is the desired mixed contraction, including all ordinary assignments. To compute it, add one private dummy to every vertex, placing the original monomer weight on its private edge. Join all dummies by unit edges. A matching with d original edges extends to a perfect matching in (2d−1)!! ways, odd even when d=0 under the empty convention. The Pfaffian equals the perfect matching sum in characteristic two. For a single truncated polynomial coordinate it can be computed in polynomial time by lifting the alternating matrix to F₂[z], computing its determinant, and taking its unique polynomial square root (extract even powers and halve exponents) *before* truncation. Alternatively retain determinant coefficients through degree 2J−2 to recover Pfaffian coefficients through J−1. Computing the determinant only modulo z^J can lose information. For an arbitrary ring the Pfaffian identity remains valid independently of the choice of evaluation algorithm.

On a native grid at residual parity, the routing premise is that each active row reads at most its one routed slot, and each original subdivision bit occurs in at most two such slots. This must hold for actual surviving rows; the argument also applies to any affine row list satisfying the same ordinary degree bound. A rank-one product of affine ports now needs just one Pfaffian. A nonlinear OR on ports has the expansion 1 minus the product of its false-literal forms. Equality/COPY on a nonempty port list has expansion product_i(1−s_i) plus product_i s_i. Here the s_i are the actual deformed port forms, with literal signs absorbed as needed. Each rank-two factor creates two branches; an OR constant branch deletes its port rows. A branch can only remove references. An absent ordinary bit in such a branch has sum zero even if it had references in the original encoding. Binary factors can be included directly as shown below. With q remaining nonlinear factors of arity at least three this gives at most 2^q polynomial-size Pfaffians, polynomial space on a sequential traversal, and no separate factor 2^b for the ordinary bit count b. The two summands may cancel. This result is limited to the first residual bit and does not remove an unbounded q.

For a binary tensor, absorb both port constants into the coefficients and write α+βU+γV+δUV with U,V homogeneous linear forms. If δ=0 this is one affine row. Otherwise introduce a terminal for each of U and V with the usual reference edges. Give these terminals monomer weights γ/δ and β/δ respectively, join them with edge weight α/δ+βγ/δ², and multiply the graph value by δ. Depending on whether neither, just U, just V, or both terminals use reference edges, the four resulting local weights are α, β, γ, δ. For scalar native tensor entries δ is a unit whenever nonzero, even if the port coefficients are jets, since absorbing constants does not change δ. For genuinely polynomial tensor entries first lift their representatives and all port coefficients to F₂[z], construct the graph over F₂(z), and clear denominators before evaluating the Pfaffian. Multiplying all matrix entries by one common denominator scales its Pfaffian by the denominator to half the matrix order; restore this scalar and the product of local δ factors by exact polynomial division before truncation. All degrees stay polynomial in graph size and the input degree bound. The lift is needed if δ has no inverse in the truncated ring. A zero δ polynomial uses the affine case.

The accompanying checker compares the graph against explicit row choices with both moment rules and against the direct Boolean/integer normalized average on random scalar examples. It also checks polynomial edge weights by coefficientwise parity and fixed nonlinear OR/COPY fixtures.
