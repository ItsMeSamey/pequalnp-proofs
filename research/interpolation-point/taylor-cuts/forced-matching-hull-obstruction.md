# Forced-edge filtering and girth

Force each matching edge e by deleting its endpoints, computing the affine label hull of the residual perfect matchings, and adding the edge label. Delete e if there is no completion or the resulting hull H_e excludes the target t. Batch deletion is safe and reaches a fixed point after at most one nonstationary round per edge. The intrinsic matching syndrome note gives the polynomial hull oracle for general graphs; forced_edge_affine.py uses the independently implemented bipartite oracle.

Let D_e be the direction space of a surviving H_e. After a round, all remaining matchings have gains in t+K, where K is the intersection over vertices v of the sum of the surviving incident D_e. A matching uses one such edge e per v; both its gain and t belong to H_e. Thus K=0 gives a deterministic exact decision and original witness by ordinary matching. If labels are sums of private coordinates at both ends, forcing e fixes the two endpoint contributions. Every incident D_e vanishes on the corresponding vertex's private coordinates, proving K=0, even after dense invertible output mixing. In general unresolved fixed points may return UNKNOWN.

Take a connected graph G of girth g>k and a signed cycle-basis matrix H over GF(3). Construct a separate two-vertex matching component for each graph edge j with parallel choices of labels H_j and −H_j. Zero-target matching asks for an everywhere nonzero ternary tension, since ker H consists precisely of differences of vertex labels. This is proper three-coloring of G. Every nonzero linear combination of H's rows is a circulation supported on a subgraph containing a cycle, so its support has size at least g. After any k columns are removed the remaining columns still span the full syndrome space. Consequently after fixing any compatible k matching edges the conditional affine hull is still the full space: choices on other pairs are independent and changing one has gain 2H_j. Graphs of arbitrarily large girth and chromatic number provide UNSAT instances invisible to every such fixed-k test. Independently the reduction gives unrestricted-dimension NP-hardness even for disjoint parallel pairs. No such conclusion is asserted at fixed label dimension.
