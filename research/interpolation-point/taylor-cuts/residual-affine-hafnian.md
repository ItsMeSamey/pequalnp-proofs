# Contracting normalized unused bits at low residual precision

Fix any remaining ordinary bits and any tensor indices on which the integrand depends. Suppose the remaining integrand is a product of b affine factors in m independent unused bits, with Y_j=2X_j and uniform X_j in {0,1}. Write

    Z = E product_i (c_i + sum_j a_ij Y_j).

Here m counts only these unused bits, not all edges of the native instance. Coefficients are integral at two, or univariate polynomials over such scalars reduced to a specified degree. This lemma contracts that product. Any preceding expansion and remaining index sums must be charged separately.

## Collision expansion and low precision

Expand a row by choosing its constant or one column. A column used d>0 times contributes E[Y^d]=2^(d−1); an unused column contributes 1. The contribution of an assignment of rows to columns or constants is its coefficient product times 2^e, where e is the sum of d−1 over used columns. This identity establishes integrality before modular reduction.

Modulo 2^h, retain only e<h. Enumerate exactly the columns used at least twice and their disjoint sets of assigned rows, in increasing order of column index. With excess e these sets contain at most 2e rows and use at most e columns. Remove those rows and columns, and multiply their coefficient product by 2^e. On the remaining row–column graph the unused choices form an arbitrary matching, with edge weights a_ij, unmatched row weights c_i and unmatched column weights one. Every surviving row assignment occurs exactly once. For fixed h the enumeration has at most (2+b+m)^O(h) possibilities, also as h varies: choose the ordered colliding rows, partition their sequence by block boundaries, and label the blocks. Avoid repetitions by sorting rows within blocks and blocks by column.

For the weighted sum over matchings put one private dummy next to each original vertex, with edge weight its unmatched weight. Join every pair of dummies by a unit edge. Mark each original row–column edge by u. For a matching of d original edges, the remaining original vertices use private edges. Its 2d unmatched dummies pair in (2d−1)!! ways, odd and hence invertible modulo 2^h, including the empty matching with factor one. Consequently the coefficient of u^d in the perfect-matching hafnian divided by this odd factor gives the total weight at size d. Sum all these coefficients.

Hirai and Namba, *Shortest (A+B)-Path Packing via Hafnian*, Theorem 2.1 and its proof in Section 2.1, compute the hafnian of a symmetric zero-diagonal integer univariate polynomial matrix of size 2s and entry degree at most N modulo 2^h in time poly(s,N)^O(h). The proof bounds the precision recursion by a factor O(s^4) per level plus polynomial work in the truncated degree. Reduce odd-denominator scalars to their integer residues before calling the algorithm. The theorem supplies deterministic polynomial time for fixed h, and the enumeration above gives overall (2+b+m)^O(h) scalar work with polynomial input-reading/bit overhead. [Primary theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.08073)

For coefficients polynomial in z of entry degree D, including representatives modulo z^J, replace the marker u by z^K with K=1+(b+m)D. The unmarked weight of any perfect matching then has degree <K. Disjoint degree bands recover the marked coefficients; apply the odd correction within each band and truncate after summation. Polynomial multiplications outside the hafnian can be truncated throughout. Thus the bound is poly(2+b+m+D)^O(h). A fixed number of polynomial coordinates of polynomially bounded degrees permits analogous packing with the product of all degree budgets as overhead. The integer univariate theorem does not imply this runtime for an arbitrary symbolic coefficient algebra or an unbounded number of jets.

At h=1 the collision sets are empty. Over any commutative ring of characteristic two the Pfaffian of the dummy construction without u equals the matching sum, since signs agree and all completion multiplicities equal one in that ring. This is a polynomial identity, so holds also for truncated jets. For scalar F₂ there is a smaller determinant on the original vertices: place c_i or 1 on their diagonal and place A,A-transpose in the two off-diagonal blocks. Noninvolutive permutations cancel in opposite orientations and edge squares equal the edge weights. The scalar shortcut does not apply unchanged to indeterminates.

## Further divisibility and row content

For scalar inputs, route one unit per row either directly to the sink at cost v₂(c_i), or to column j at cost v₂(a_ij). Each column has a free unit of sink capacity and further capacity at unit cost. Omit zero entries. An integral minimum-cost flow gives D equal to the minimum valuation of an individual row-assignment contribution. No feasible flow gives Z=0. Otherwise 2^D divides Z, so D≥h certifies vanishing at residual precision h without enumerating matchings. Cancellation can only improve this bound. For example b rows with no constants and support only on q<b common odd columns force D=b−q if all these edges are present. This extra divisibility is independent of the original power from normalizing the bits.

A complementary normalization can be stronger when few columns are available. Define d_i=min(v₂(c_i),1+min_j v₂(a_ij)), setting Z=0 for an identically zero row. Let C=sum_i d_i−m and let L_i(x) be the original row in X variables divided by 2^d_i. Then Z=2^C S where S=sum_x product_i L_i(x) is integral at two. Its parity is the number modulo two of common solutions to L_i(x)=1 over F₂. This is one exactly when this affine linear system has a unique solution.

More generally compute S modulo 2^h by enumerating the set E of even rows with |E|<h; the other parity patterns contribute zero. On the affine Boolean fiber with those parities, expand each odd row as 1+(L_i−1), retaining sets J of odd-row deviations with |J|+|E|<h. The retained summand is the product of the L_i on E and the L_i−1 on J. It has total degree less than h. Sum each resulting Boolean monomial on the affine fiber by adding coordinate-one pins and counting the resulting linear system. This takes (2+b+m)^O(h) arithmetic operations, independent of C. With C≥0 it yields Z modulo 2^(C+h). For C<0 one must increase precision by −C to obtain the same target number of quotient bits. This argument uses scalar residues modulo two and is not an arbitrary coefficient-ring algorithm.

These low-residual-precision evaluations do not imply fast evaluation at the full Hermite precision, nor do they bound the number of surviving ordinary bits or conditional tensor terms.
