# Signed independent sets and cyclic OR constraints

For each vertex i of a digraph D build x_i−u_i−v_i; every arc j→i adds x_j−v_i. Hidden activities u_i,v_i are −1, visible activities λ_i may be arbitrary signed rationals. Conditional on visible spins the hidden edge sums to 1−1[x_i=0]−1[all predecessors zero]. This enforces x_i=OR(pred_i), with sign −1 at zero and +1 at one. Conditional hidden edges are independent. Thus Z=(−1)^n times the sum over fixed points of the product of −λ_i on active bits.

The active set is successor-closed and every active vertex has an active predecessor. Its minimal active SCCs under reachability must therefore contain a cycle, including singleton loops. Conversely all descendants of any antichain of cyclic SCCs give a fixed point; cyclic SCCs supply internal predecessors and other vertices inherit activity along paths. The minimal SCCs recover the unique antichain. Enumeration costs at most 2^t poly(N), t cyclic SCCs, or polynomial per enumerated antichain. A strongly connected cyclic D gives (−1)^n+product λ_i. The fixed-point correspondence is established research in disjunctive networks. [Boolean network dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.0275).

For uniform activities −1, Z is the known sign times the number of upsets of the cyclic-component poset, i.e. the model count of an explicit implication-only 2CNF there. For general λ, an acyclic singleton is active exactly when one of its maximal cyclic ancestors is active. Group products of effective activities −λ_i by these ancestor profiles. Empty profiles never activate; singleton profiles become cyclic unary factors; unit products can be discarded. When only these profiles survive, weighted implication 2CNF suffices. All reductions are polynomial for exact rational input.

Unmarked recognition: take all degree-two vertices as u-candidates. They must have activity −1 and distinct noncandidate neighbors, whose resulting endpoint pairs form a perfect matching on the noncandidates. Suppress candidates, mark the resulting matching links, and keep ordinary edges separately. Test bipartiteness after suppression. In each connected component choose a coloring such that every vertex on the hidden v shore has activity −1. Matched pairs name the indices, ordinary x_j−v_i edges recover the arcs; an ordinary edge parallel to a marked link denotes a self-loop. This linear graph procedure recognizes all displayed representations in which x and v are not degree two (e.g. all in/outdegrees ≥2). Other instances can be checked with supplied roles. Bipartiteness of the original unsuppressed graph is not assumed.

For a strongly connected bidirected bounded-degree expander D, contracting the disjoint connected triples gives its underlying expander as a minor. Across visible versus hidden spins the signed truth-table rank is nevertheless 2^n for nonzero visible activities: set every v_i=0 and the remaining matrix is a tensor power of NAND on x_i,u_i times invertible diagonal sign/weight matrices. The exact uniform signed scalar is 2(−1)^n despite unbounded treewidth. The rank assertion concerns this specific cut only.

For an arbitrary bipartite graph direct its edges from lower to upper shore, forming a height-two poset. Replace each poset vertex by a bidirected triangle, adding arcs along the original edges. Every SCC is cyclic, all local in/outdegrees are at least two and the unmarked recognizer applies. Antichains are precisely independent sets of the original bipartite graph, hence general exact evaluation even on this recognizable class is #P-hard. [Provan and Ball](https://doi.org/10.1137/0212053). Independent replay compared signed sums and OR fixed points on all 512 digraphs on three labeled vertices, including loops.
