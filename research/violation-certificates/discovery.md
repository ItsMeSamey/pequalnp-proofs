# Finding a signed complete bipartite split

Consider a binary CNF with distinct-variable, nontautological clauses, deduplicated. Form its unsigned primal graph G, including isolated declared variables. Compute the connected components of the complement graph. If there are at least two, take A to be the first component and B the union of the rest. Every A–B pair is adjacent in G. Scan all crossing clauses and check that each variable x occurs there with a single polarity. If this succeeds, write b_x for the unique bit falsifying its crossing literal. The cross constraints now forbid simultaneously setting some a in A to b_a and some b in B to b_b. Indeed every pair has a crossing clause, and consistency fixes both literals of that clause. The scan and dense complement traversal take O(n²+|F|) time. The test can also try each individual complement component against the rest. This is a sufficient recognition procedure; it need not discover arbitrary signed splits that group several complement components on both sides. For globally unate formulas, every complement-disconnected graph passes on the first try.

Let F_A and F_B contain all respective internal clauses, Z_A and Z_B be their model counts, and e_A and e_B be their truth values on the unique respective assignments with every variable avoiding its bad bit. Then

    Z(F) = e_A Z_B + Z_A e_B − e_A e_B.

In fact the complete crossing family holds iff all variables on A avoid their bad bits or all variables on B do. The two terms count these events among internal models, and the last term subtracts their intersection. Isolated declared variables in subinstances contribute factors of two as usual. Ordinary disconnected splits multiply counts. Recursive applications with constant-size terminal components run in polynomial bit time: each split partitions the remaining variable set, the decomposition tree has O(n) nodes and count integers have O(n) bits.

For a strict example take F_r to contain (¬a_i ∨ ¬b_j) for every i,j in [r]. Its primal graph K_{r,r} has treewidth r (for a lower bound, contract r−1 disjoint matching edges, leaving a K_{r+1} minor). Its incidence graph is the once-subdivision and has the primal graph as a minor. The primal complement has exactly two connected components for r≥2, giving the hidden partition. There are no internal clauses, e_A=e_B=1, and the exact count is 2^{r+1}−1. Fix a_1=1 and all other a_i=0. Remaining violations v_{1,j}=b_j vary independently and all others vanish. Thus every pointwise satisfaction polynomial in clause violation bits has degree at least r by uniqueness of the multilinear polynomial for the all-zero indicator.

Even branching on an arbitrary fixed number k<r of individual variables, interspersed with ordinary component splits and bounded-degree certificate tests, does not remove this obstruction along the all-zero branch. The residual graph is still K_{p,q}, with p,q≥r−k, so it stays connected and has certificate degree at least max(p,q) by choosing a singleton on either side. This claim concerns exactly those operations, without other inference rules. The family is globally unate; setting pure literals to satisfying values alone does not preserve model counts.
