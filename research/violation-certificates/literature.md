# Low-degree certificates for clause unions

Let the event indexed by a clause consist of its falsifying assignments. A certificate expresses the union indicator as a linear combination of indicators of nonempty intersections of at most d events. The coefficients may be arbitrary rationals. The following comparisons place both the identity and its recognition algorithm.

**Exact inclusion–exclusion.** Goaoc, Matoušek, Paták, Safernová and Tancer characterize all exact inclusion–exclusion vectors by a linear system in Lemma 2.1. Rows are nonempty Venn regions, columns are nonempty event intersections, and an entry records containment of the column's index set in the row's active event set. Restricting their columns to size at most d gives precisely our certificate condition. The condition and its interpretation as interpolation on attainable patterns are established results. [Paper, Lemma 2.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.2591)

**Bonferroni and abstract tubes.** At a point contained in k events, truncating classical inclusion–exclusion at degree d gives the alternating sum of the first d nonconstant binomial coefficients with top index k. It equals one for 1 ≤ k ≤ d and differs from one by a signed binomial coefficient for k > d. Abstract tubes give exact identities from selected intersections using alternating coefficients supported on a simplicial complex. A tube whose faces have size at most d supplies a degree-d certificate. The general linear system also allows unrestricted coefficients without requiring a tube witness. [Naiman and Wynn](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1069362380) · [Dohmen](https://doi.org/10.1214/EJP.v4-42)

**Probability bounds.** Prékopa studied sharp probability bounds obtainable from partial moment data by linear programming. Bertsimas and Popescu developed a semidefinite approach to optimizing event probabilities under moment information. If an exact pointwise identity is available on the specified support, taking expectations determines the union probability for every distribution on that support. Partial moment bounds optimized over larger supports can admit a range. The Gram test below uses its specific CNF to access moments of the actual distribution. [Prékopa](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.38.2.227) · [Bertsimas and Popescu](https://doi.org/10.1137/S1052623401399903)

**Projection and recognition.** The Gallot–Kounias union bound already uses a Gram matrix of event indicators. For intersection features f, their union is still the original union whenever the features include all singleton events. Writing G = E[ffᵀ] and b = E[f], the squared projection norm bᵀG⁺b lower-bounds the union probability, with equality exactly when the union indicator lies in the feature span. This follows by projecting the union indicator, whose inner product with f equals b. Its unweighted residual norm requires the unknown union probability. Weighting by V, the number of violated clauses, instead minimizes E[V(1−wᵀf)²]. The constant term E[V] is known, and the weight is positive everywhere in the union. This detects the same exactness condition with intersections through order 2d+1. Each such intersection is a subcube or is empty. The resulting exact rational test runs in m^{O(d)} poly(input size) time without constructing the Venn diagram. This identifies a specific implementation for CNF. The coverage of this literature search does not establish priority for that implementation. [Kounias, original paper](https://scispace.com/pdf/bounds-for-the-probability-of-a-union-with-applications-449ex2f0b8.pdf)

**Symmetric polynomial interpolation.** If the attainable positive violation counts form a known set K, the polynomial 1−∏_{k∈K}(1−V/k) interpolates the union indicator and has degree |K|. Boolean multilinearization puts it in the intersection-feature span. Conversely any polynomial in V that equals zero at zero and one on K has degree at least |K| by root counting. The multivariate certificate can use asymmetry among clauses. On independent unit clauses all Boolean violation patterns occur, and uniqueness of the multilinear representation forces degree m. These facts bound the certificate test's coverage while leaving general model counting open.
