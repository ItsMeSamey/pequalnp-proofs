# The six edge coordinates

Restrict the singleton inputs to H = {7,8,9,10,11,12}, using original point labels. Each of the six external projective lines contains exactly one H point, so every supplied primitive becomes a singleton or empty. The seven targets induce four triples (the vertex stars of a complete graph on four vertices) and three pairs (its perfect matchings).

Seven distinct target outputs must be computed. Suppose j of the four triples first appear by intersection. Each remaining triple must appear by union with an auxiliary clean pair, and that pair serves only its own triple. Each of the j intersections needs two distinct strict supersets, neither a target nor a clean pair; every unordered pair of these helpers gives at most one triple. With only ten gates, there are at most j-1 such helpers, and no j between 1 and 4 satisfies j ≤ binomial(j-1,2). With j=0 the four clean pairs themselves give eleven gates.

At eleven gates there are at most j dirty helpers. Draw the j intersections as edges on these helpers. No edge can have degree at least two at each endpoint. Otherwise two other triple labels, one at each end, have their intersection inside the central label. The four stars of K4 have all six pairwise intersections at distinct H points, each on just two stars. Hence each connected component of the helper graph is a star, requiring more vertices than edges if j is positive. Thus j=0. All four helpers are clean pairs, all paid masks lie in one target, and no pair can be obtained as an intersection of two available proper supersets. All eleven gates are therefore unions.

The remaining seven coordinates comprise four private points v (one per vertex star) and three points n_m, one per perfect matching. In the full representation each vertex target consists of its star and its private point. Each matching target consists of the two edges of matching m plus the two n_j for j different from m. The external line corresponding to e in matching m consists of e, the two private vertices not incident to e, and n_m.

Adding one private coordinate to H raises the minimum to twelve. If only eleven gates were used, restricting back to H would leave an optimal eleven-gate all-union circuit with every gate essential and distinct, hence all eleven full gates would be unions. A union network needs at least three gates for the now four-point target, two for each of the other three triples, and one for each of three pairs. Paid submasks spanning two points cannot be shared across these targets, and free features meet each target at most once. Individually restoring one matching coordinate raises the exact minimum from eleven to thirteen, as checked by closure.py 0; all three choices are equivalent under a permutation of the four vertices.

closure.py enumerates candidate paid singleton masks on these coordinate slices, granting every subset of each external line as free. All target masks are mandatory and each other mask costs one. Every selected mask must admit two available parents whose union or intersection gives that mask, with both parents distinct from the result. If selected signals are unavailable due to a cycle, the solver demands a new helper obtainable from the existing reachable closure. Absence of solutions even before the acyclicity check is already a valid exclusion. A baseline made of separate unions attains the upper bounds.

The eight-coordinate extensions H plus two private points, one private plus one matching point, or two matching points have exact costs 13, 14 and 15 respectively. Each type is transitive under S4. The three finite checks reject bounds 12, 13 and 14, respectively. See closure-small.log.
