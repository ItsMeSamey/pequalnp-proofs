# Seven-neighborhood singleton synthesis

The six-target transversal result gives 18 gates for every proper six-line restriction. In 19 gates, deleting one target would leave precisely 18, so there would be no additional exclusive gate on that target. Every target must therefore be a full intersection of two proper supersets, and every nonoutput signal hits at least two targets twice. The full-parent graph consists of stars or components having three concurrent labels. Include both the triangle and the length-three path for a three-edge concurrent component. At least three gates outside the parent graph and seven outputs are needed, by the common-first-gate argument. Sixteen canonical role instances remain, all UNSAT in seven19.log. The minimum is therefore at least twenty, and the separate four-point unions cost 21.

At 20 gates every target is hard: otherwise removing a pure union cone would leave at most seventeen gates on six targets. Every target is marked as in five_certificate/README.md. Let r count three-point intersections followed by singleton unions. There are 7+r clean output/core gates and 13-r remaining dirty or auxiliary gates. The distinct parent masks give a simple graph with seven edges, each labeled by its intersection core C_e. A truncated core is its target less one point. The reconstruction checks for every incident parent pair that its intersection equals C_e.

Let v be the vertex count and Q=13-r-v the number of remaining auxiliary gates. When r=0 the common-first-gate lemma gives Q≥3. Otherwise Q≥2: if there were only one auxiliary gate, only that gate could fail to span three points of some target. Every signal spanning three points must then contain that first gate, by the same induction. This bounds v≤10. The independent graph7.py enumerator constructs unlabeled graphs from connected components: connected components on at most seven vertices come from the complete graph atlas, and an eight-vertex component with seven edges must be a tree. It includes all nonisomorphic trees of that size. Vertex and ambient incidence automorphisms quotient the seven edge assignments. For every proposed choice of omitted points the mandatory mask at a vertex is the union of all its incident cores. Every parent intersection must at least contain the overlap of these mandatory masks. The resulting feasibility test is equivalent to requiring every intersection point of two neighboring edge labels seen across opposite endpoints to lie in the middle core whenever both neighboring cores retain it. The bitset computation checks all 5^7 hole choices, where the fifth choice is a full line. The maximum possible parent mask omits points forced into a neighboring parent but forbidden in the core. These interval bounds, budget constraints and smaller-family projections leave 8,128 role graphs with admissible core choices.

## The two-auxiliary case

Call a signal large if it spans at least three points of some target. All parents, cores and outputs are large. When Q=2, both auxiliary gates must be small: if only one small paid signal g existed, every large signal would contain g. Indeed any union forming a large result must consume a previously large signal or g, and both inputs of a large intersection are large. The first small signal g is a union of two free masks. All large signals before the second small signal h contain g. If h is constructed from any previous large signals only by intersection, it therefore contains g. An intersection with g either retains g or has a free operand; a union with a large signal stays large. A union involving g contains g. In every such case all subsequent large masks would still contain g. Hence h is another independent union of two free masks. Every large mask contains g ∩ h by induction, so g,h are disjoint since the full output lines have empty common intersection. Both can be ordered before every other gate.

The first large signal that omits a point of g must be produced by h union a free mask. No earlier large signal can participate: union would preserve g, and a large intersection needs two large operands, which would also preserve g. This first signal spans at most three points on every line. It is a parent (all other nonauxiliaries are intersection cores or full outputs), and all its incident edges are truncated. Similarly a different parent must be produced from g and a free mask, without containing all of h. The two parents cannot share an incident edge: on its common three-point core each of the disjoint helpers would have to cover at least two points. This removes 3,070 role graphs.

There are 112 free transversal masks and 1,056 distinct nonfree unions of two of them. filter7.cpp enumerates unordered disjoint helper pairs. For a parent interval [A,B], a helper x can start a parent with a free input only if x lies in B and A minus x is transversal. The two starting parents must be distinct and miss a point of the opposite helper. The subsequent optimistic closure retains an interval rather than the specific value at each newly reachable parent. An interval can be reached by a union of two previous interval choices if both lower endpoints lie below its upper bound and its lower bound lies below the union of their upper endpoints. The dual rule tests intersections. A free operand in a union can supply a residual transversal mask. Once both endpoints of an edge are available its fixed core and target are offered. Inconsistency between separate uses of an interval is allowed, so failure of this closure safely excludes the pair. Running writefilter.py then compiling and running filter7.cpp on filter7.in leaves 70 hole configurations across 41 of the 7,082 two-auxiliary graphs.

All other cases (971 with Q=3, 73 with Q=4, two with Q=5) proceed directly to the role SAT checker. A case with Q=2 and no surviving filter witness is already excluded. For the surviving Q=2 families the checked hole list can be restricted to surviving configurations; the SAT checker can still choose any disjoint pair of independent free unions. See verify_all.py. No bounded conflict limit counts as a proof unless the solver returns False.


The definitive final coverage is 8,128 graph orbits, split into 3,070 elementary source exclusions, 3,971 further interval-closure exclusions, and 1,087 SAT exclusions. There are no unresolved or satisfiable graphs. Seven independent four-point unions cost 21, proving optimality.

For clarity, a parent in a minimum circuit cannot be contained in the line of any incident edge. It strictly contains the edge core. In the truncated case the only larger subset lying inside the line would be that target itself, duplicating a paid mask or forcing a cycle. Every parent, core or output is large in the two-auxiliary argument.

The optional stronger SAT model adds lower bounds on rich unions. A truly hard full target T needs at least four separate unions meeting it twice. Truncate its cone at signals meeting T in at most one point. A last clean intersection on a union-only path to the target survives. Prune every intersection to either input. On the coordinates of T this only increases values and the cone still covers all four points. If there were only three unions, all three would survive in a tree with four distinct singleton leaves. The two inputs of the earliest intersection of the truncated cone are unions with overlapping traces (at least two common target points). Both must survive in the pruned tree, in which overlapping nodes are ancestor-related. The path between them precedes the earliest intersection, so it uses only unions in the original truncated cone. Their full masks are comparable, contradicting minimality. Fewer than three unions cannot cover T at all.

At least six paid gates meet T at least twice. If two intersections are rich on T, the previous four unions suffice. Otherwise truncate away the at-most-one-point signals. The only remaining intersection has pure-union parents. For an OR-root their intersection is a clean triple. Each parent needs at least two rich unions. A union common to both truncated cones would be a further clean rich T-mask, impossible since the marker and root already use the two exclusive slots. Add the final union, obtaining five unions. For a full intersection root each parent needs three rich unions and at most one is common, since any such shared union would be clean and earlier than the output. Again there are five rich unions and the marked intersection. These counts are not used by the default weaker verification mode.
