# A parity query on an optimal perfect-matching face

For an arbitrary general graph with integer edge costs w and independent binary edge labels b, computing both labels attained by minimum-cost perfect matchings is deterministic polynomial time, including recovery of witnesses. The optimal cost is unconstrained by b.

Take a laminar optimal blossom dual from the weighted matching algorithm. Discard nontight edges and keep the laminar family of positive odd-set dual variables. Complementary slackness characterizes optimal perfect matchings by using tight edges and crossing each retained odd set exactly once. Build its inclusion tree with singleton leaves and the full vertex set as root.

For each proper node S and each incoming tight edge e incident to S from outside, tabulate the attainable b-labels of internal edges of matchings that cover S except for the interior endpoint of e, respecting the child cuts when e is included. A singleton has label zero. Contract maximal child nodes including remaining singleton vertices. If e arrives in child U, remove U and allow its precomputed labels for e as additive offsets. For each internal skeleton edge f between other children C,D, use all parallel label options b(f)+p+q for p,q taken from the C,f and D,f tables. Solve for attainable single parities in this skeleton with the ordinary deterministic correct-parity perfect matching algorithm. At the root there is no incoming edge or offset. Children are disjoint and each has exactly one assigned incident edge, so every table witness expands, and conversely all optimal matchings appear in the root table. The recursion has polynomially many states and calls. Keep pointers for witness recovery. Parallel edges may equivalently be encoded by internally disjoint three-edge paths, with b carried by one outer edge per path. Unused paths are matched at their middle edge.

## A structural parameter for two parities

Suppose an integer edge cost w lifts one nonzero binary marker combination, up to a known constant on perfect matchings. Suppose at most k distinct costs occur across all perfect matchings. For each compatible set F of at most 2 floor((k−1)/2) edges, force F and solve for both extreme remaining costs and the independent marker parity attainable on those faces, including the offsets from F. Accept exactly the target parity pairs observed.

For a fixed matching M at the j-th lowest distinct cost, a subset F of M of size at most 2(j−1) makes M optimal among matchings containing F. Iteratively force one nontight edge of M, or two edges of M crossing the same positive-dual odd set. Each round removes the previous minimum level and preserves M. This is the proof of Theorem 9 in the primary source below. The fact that F lies inside the particular target matching preserves its second marker. At most j−1 edges suffice in bipartite graphs. Applying the argument to both w and −w allows the nearer end of the list, at distance at most floor((k−1)/2) from the chosen level. In particular only the minimum and maximum faces are needed when k=2. Running time is m^{2 floor((k−1)/2)}poly(input) in general and m^{floor((k−1)/2)}poly(input) in bipartite graphs. A supplied bound suffices. For fixed k it can also be checked by testing for a (k+1)-st level using the source algorithm. Weights may be binary encoded.
