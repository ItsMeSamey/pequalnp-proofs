# Mixed vertex parities and induced even subgraphs near subdivided graphs

All graphs here are simple. A support S is feasible if each vertex in G[S] has even degree. Each vertex has a gain in F2^t and S must attain a prescribed sum. The algorithms decide feasibility and return a witness. Summing counts across compressed representations need not count original supports correctly.

## Subcubic cover and quadratic projection

Let k be the size of a supplied vertex cover of the edges joining vertices of degree three. Enumerate independent sets I of at most t vertices, include them, remove their closed neighborhood and shift the target. This guesses a score-equivalent subfamily of the isolated selected vertices in any solution; all other original selected isolates can be dropped. In the remainder guess at each surviving cover vertex either no selected incident edges, or one of at most three pairs of edges, forcing its two edges and requiring any other neighbor inactive (forbid its entire incident star). For an unused cover vertex forbid its incident star. Reject conflicting requirements. Solve for an edge subset via linear parity zero at every vertex, honoring forced and forbidden edges.

Every resulting positive-degree support is an induced cycle union. An omitted edge joining two positive-degree vertices would give each endpoint two chosen edges and one unchosen, hence would join two vertices of original degree three. Its cover endpoint was active and its other endpoint was required inactive, a contradiction. Conversely a feasible cycle union supplies precisely such edge choices. A vertex with even edge degree zero or two is active precisely when the binary sum of pairwise products of its incident edge bits equals one. Parameterizing the affine edge space therefore yields t quadratic equations for the score. Apply the binary character expansion over the 2^t linear combinations: each quadratic Walsh sum is computed by Gaussian elimination, and their signed sum divided by 2^t counts satisfying assignments within this affine space. Fixing edge variables individually recovers a witness. Runtime is 4^k 2^t poly(n,t) times the number of subsets of size at most t, hence polynomial for fixed k,t. A bounded-search vertex cover algorithm can find the cover if needed.

## Arbitrary degree with few interacting high vertices

Call a vertex high if its degree is at least three. Let h count the high vertices having a high neighbor. For arbitrary simple graphs there is a second algorithm using 2^h poly(n,t) times the square of the number of subsets of size at most t.

Guess I and remove its closed neighborhood as above. Reclassify high vertices in the remainder; the number with high neighbors cannot increase. Guess which of those at most h vertices are selected. Every other high vertex has no high neighbor. Starting from the original witness with no remaining selected isolates, any unused such vertex can be added as an isolate: a selected low vertex has two selected neighbors and therefore cannot be adjacent to an unused vertex. Newly added high vertices are mutually nonadjacent and have no high neighbor. Among the originally unused high vertices having no high neighbor, retain a subfamily O of omissions of size at most t with the same gain sum as the whole omitted family. Activate the others, preserving the score. Enumerate O.

Now the complete selected high set is specified. Delete all inactive high vertices, and force all surviving high–high edges into an edge subset. Require even edge degree at every remaining vertex. For each low vertex its activity is the bit of an arbitrary incident edge, or zero for a degree-zero vertex. These choices are consistent by even parity and degree at most two. Impose the remaining target as linear equations after accounting for I and all selected high vertices. Gaussian elimination suffices. For the returned support, include all specified high vertices even if isolated, add each low vertex with positive edge degree, and restore I. Any surviving high–high edge is forced, and every active low vertex uses both incident edges. Thus all induced edges occur in the computed even edge set. Both directions follow from the construction.

In particular neither algorithm assumes bounded treewidth or bipartiteness. When no two high vertices are adjacent, the latter algorithm permits unbounded maximum degree with polynomial runtime for any fixed number of markers.
