# Small gain-preserving odd edge supports

For every d there is a computable f(d) with the following property. In a finite undirected multigraph with edge gains in F2^d, if a given gain is attained by an edge set in which every nonisolated vertex has odd degree, some such set uses at most f(d) edges. Loops contribute two to degree.

Choose an edge-minimal witness F and discard unused vertices. The binary cycle space of F maps injectively under edge-sum gains; deleting any nonempty zero-gain even subset would keep every degree odd. Hence its cyclomatic rank is at most d. Its maximum degree D is at most 2^d+2d. At a vertex v, consider the components of F−v in its component. Each attached by one edge gives a branch consisting of that edge and the entire component. Deleting two branches of the same gain preserves oddness at v and all remaining vertices, contrary to minimality. There are at most 2^d single-attachment branches. A component joined by j≥2 incidences consumes j−1 independent cycles; loops each consume one. Their total degree contribution at v is at most 2d.

Write q=2^d and use D above. Take R_0=2d+10 and R_(i+1)=100(D+1)(q+1)(R_i+d+1). Among q+1 half-open intervals (R_i,R_(i+1)] one contains no maximum-matching size of a nonloop color class. For that pair R,R', every nonloop color is rare (matching size ≤R) or rich (>R'). The rare nonloop edges number at most 2DqR, by taking endpoints of a maximal matching in each rare class. Put their endpoints and all loop endpoints in S. Thus |S|≤4DqR+d. Iteratively adjoin any outside vertex having at least two incident edges to S. Each step merges components of the induced subgraph or increases its cycle rank; more precisely the sum of cycle-rank increase and component-count decrease is at least one. Thus there are at most |S_initial|+d steps. Final |S|≤2|S_initial|+d.

Take F[S]. At every even-degree vertex of S select one edge to the outside: such an edge exists because its degree in F was odd. The chosen outside leaves are distinct by closure. All resulting nonisolated vertices have odd degree; every rare edge and every loop has been retained. Its score differs from the target only by a sum of rich nonloop labels, representable by at most d such labels. Greedily add a single fresh nonloop edge of each needed color disjoint from all edges already selected. A matching of size >R' is larger than 2|S|+2d, so this is possible at every step. The result realizes the target with at most D|S|+|S|+d edges. Replacing R by R_(q+1) gives a uniform computable bound. The empty target may always use no edges.

The proof supplies a witness-size bound, not an efficient method to extract the hidden intermediate minimal graph from an arbitrary graph. Enumerating at most f(d) edges gives a deterministic XP decision algorithm.
