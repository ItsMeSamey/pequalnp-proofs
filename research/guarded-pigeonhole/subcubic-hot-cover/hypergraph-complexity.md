# Odd incidence supports with bounded item rank

R_j on j distinct incident items permits zero or an odd number.

## Four-uniform NP-completeness

Reduce positive 1-in-3 SAT (Schaefer, 1978, https://doi.org/10.1145/800133.804350). Pin the first two variables of a sequence of t_i and place R3 constraints on every consecutive triple. All variables in the sequence are forced true. Each participates in at most three chain constraints and one external use. To implement a clause (x,y,z), add R4(z,z',1,1), R4(x,y,z',1), R2(x,y); these successively force z'=1−z, parity x+y+z odd, and exclude all three true. Use a distinct supply variable for every displayed true input. For each ordinary logical variable use a new occurrence copy per local operation and link consecutive copies in a path of equality constraints R3(a,b,1). Every occurrence item has degree at most three. Every item can be padded to exact rank four by private degree-one constraint vertices. There are two actual literal pins. All local arities are at most four and assignments verify in polynomial time. Naive signed bipartite transfer fails: a selected hyperedge checking odd incidence cannot distinguish one of its three endpoints from all three.

## Minimal witnesses at rank three

For maximum ambient row arity four, every selected row in a feasible F has size one or three. On subsets of an inclusion-minimal witness F for a t-bit gain, the restricted feasible family is Horn: each pair in an active triple forces the third. Its target indicator is the unique top-point indicator there. The degree-at-most-t polynomial for the target expands as squarefree monomials; each monomial for S is the principal filter for its Horn closure. Distinct principal filters are linearly independent by the triangular zeta matrix. Some at-most-t item subset therefore generates F. In closure order each additional item completes a fresh triple. Previously selected incidences on unfinished rows number at most 3t: each seed adds at most three, each forced step removes two at the trigger and adds at most two elsewhere. Bags of earlier items in unfinished rows plus the next item give primal width at most 3t. Inserting a bag copy plus each row at its last item gives restricted incidence width at most 3t+1. No ambient host width is asserted.

For arbitrary ambient arity, put h equal to the number of rows of selected degree at least three. The map on chosen items recording these h parities and t score bits is injective, since deleting a kernel vector preserves oddness for high rows and singleton rows may become zero. Thus h≥|F|−t. Subtracting 3h from the total capacity 3|F| shows that the sum of row-degree excess over three at high rows, the number of singleton rows, and unused item incidence slots is at most 3t. At most 3t/2 rows have selected degree at least five, containing at most 15t/2 selected items together. Fix these items on. On the remaining lattice of supersets of these items respecting restricted triple rules, all exceptional rows stay valid. The same polynomial proof supplies at most t additional generators. This extends the selected primal/incidence width to O(t): exceptional rows may be carried in every incidence bag, and their selected items may be kept throughout primal bags. It does not provide an ambient decision procedure.

If ambient row arity is at most three, the original full CSP is Horn, with R2 giving a forbidden pair. Group the monomials of the target polynomial by their least Horn closure, omitting inconsistent ones. The target polynomial is nonzero on models iff some collected coefficient is nonzero. Testing all closures takes n^(O(t)); an inclusion-minimal closure with a surviving coefficient realizes the target. No item-rank bound is needed here.
