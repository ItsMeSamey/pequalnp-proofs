# A linear size bound for near-square cores close to a Ferrers matrix

Work over the binary field. The ordinary matroid has opposite disjoint cycle and cocycle supports (A,B) and (B,A), with A and B a partition. Each is the unique raw model for one specified four-bit score (allowing a free offset in H). At least one other score is missing. Use the near-square chart lemma of joint-forcing-rank.md. In a basis I maximizing the number of elements of A, the exceptions (basis elements of B and nonbasis elements of A) number e≤3. In a basis J maximizing B the reverse exception count e' is at most 3. In either chart, clean rows and columns have tags: four score bits together with boundary bits on the opposite exceptional sites. If two rows have identical row tags and two columns have identical column tags, their 2-by-2 submatrix cannot be a permutation matrix. Indeed toggling both fundamental cycles and both fundamental cocycles cancels ordinary scores and exceptional bits, and exactly the matching incidences give an admissible distinct model at the same score.

**Theorem.** Suppose the clean matrix in the first chart is the sum of a Ferrers matrix and a matrix of rank at most k. A Ferrers matrix has its row supports ordered by inclusion; an arbitrary row or column permutation is allowed. Then the ground set size is at most
    4 times 2^(8+e') times (4k+2e+1), plus 2(k+e)+e+e'.
Thus cores in this class have linear size in the perturbation rank. An upper bound suffices. Recognition of the smallest possible perturbation rank is unnecessary for the implication.

Let K be the submatrix of the I-chart on rows I minus J and columns J minus I. This is an invertible s-by-s matrix since J is a basis; the corresponding reverse submatrix is its inverse. At most e of its rows and columns together are exceptional in the first chart. Restrict the Ferrers approximation to its clean positions and pad with zeros elsewhere. This matrix has rank at least s−k−e since changing e rows and columns contributes rank at most e. Take an invertible minor U of that rank m, entirely clean. A nonsingular square Ferrers matrix is, after separate permutations, the triangular matrix of ones: the nested rows have all the distinct positive cardinalities. Hence its inverse is bidiagonal and contains a square identity submatrix on at least m/2 alternating diagonal indices.

Order K by the chosen rows and columns of U. Put X=(K inverse)11, Y=(K inverse)12. The block identity gives XU−I=X(U−K11)−YK21, of rank at most k+s−m. Consequently X differs from U inverse by rank at most a=k+s−m≤2k+e. All selected rows and columns are clean in reverse, since the formerly chosen A rows have left the basis and the formerly nonbasic B columns have entered it.

Group the alternating diagonal indices by their separate reverse row tag and column tag. There are at most 2^(8+e') groups. Within each group's full square matrix P, any induced permutation of order two violates uniqueness, so P is Ferrers. Moreover rank(P+I)≤a. Simultaneously permute rows and columns to order rows by inclusion. On indices i with Pii=0 the matrix P+I is lower triangular with unit diagonal: for i<j, Pij≤Pjj. These indices number at most a. On indices with Pii=1, the all-ones matrix added to P+I gives an upper triangular matrix with unit diagonal since Pji≥Pii for i<j. These indices number at most a+1. Thus each group has size at most 2a+1. We obtain m≤2·2^(8+e')·(2a+1), and s≤m+k+e. Each element in both bases or neither basis is exceptional in at least one chart. The total size is at most 2s+e+e', as claimed.

Independent tests exhaust every binary square matrix through order four, including 7,148 Ferrers matrices for the identity-perturbation lemma and 81,157 inverse block choices. Run node check.js. The geometry applies in particular when toggling a fixed subset of whole tag blocks makes the entire clean matrix Ferrers; the block perturbation has rank at most the smaller number of row and column types. The assertion that individual tag blocks are Ferrers is weaker.
