# Two red pins on a cubic graph with a supplied near-bipartition

The implementation takes a finite simple cubic graph, its exact set of violating edges for a supplied bipartition, and two edges required to be red. It handles zero or two violating edges, including overlaps with the pins. With two defects, cubic degree balance forces one within each shore and the shores have equal size. In a proper three-edge coloring, the red edges form a perfect matching. Shore balance forces both defects red or neither red. When both are red, a matching containing the defects and pins suffices, because all complementary cycles are bipartite. With zero defects apply the same ordinary matching test to the pins.

When neither defect is red, delete the defects from the red graph and force the pins into the matching. If infeasible return NO. Retain exactly the candidate edges belonging to some such matching, using a forced-edge matching test for each edge. The connected components of the allowed-edge graph have independent matching choices and equal shore sizes. An isolated forced pair is a component. All remaining edges are mandatory nonred wires, including edges with both ends in one component. Each wire contributes one port at each endpoint. A local perfect matching leaves internal even cycles and paths pairing the ports. The global complement is blue/green colorable exactly when its two defect wires lie on the same circuit, since each circuit length modulo two is the parity of the number of defect wires on it.

## Computing the four-port responses

Zero-port components require no wiring, and two-port components have a unique pairing. At a forced pair with four ports the two ports on each endpoint are paired. Every other connected allowed-edge block is matching-covered and bridgeless: relative to a perfect matching every optional edge lies on an alternating cycle, while a forced matching edge has no other allowed edge incident with either end. Nontrivial such blocks have minimum degree two, so their four ports are distinct, two on each shore. They also satisfy strict Hall expansion for every nonempty proper subset S of one shore: equality would make edges from its neighbor set to the outside impossible in any perfect matching, contrary to connectedness and allowedness.

Order the four ports by shore, and call the sibling matching_multipole.solve routine on blue/green patterns 0000, 0101, 0110. For either mixed pattern a matching of color 0 covering the graph except its two opposite-shore 0-ports exists by strict Hall. Its complement consists of even cycles and a path joining the two opposite-shore 1-ports. Alternating the remaining colors extends the matching. For 0000 a matching after deleting all four ports also exists: any Hall-deficient nonempty S on the remaining left side has no left port, hence emits 3|S| internal incidences. Strict Hall before the deletions would force its original neighbor set to have size exactly |S|+1 and include both deleted right ports. Those neighbors have total capacity 3|S|+1, so exactly one edge goes to the outside of S, a bridge. The two deletions per shore preserve balance, so Hall suffices. The remaining cycles are even. These constructions are polynomial.

For each witness extract its internal color-2 perfect matching (the ports use only 0 and 1). In the complement of that matching, two paired ports see equal colors on opposite shores and unequal colors on the same shore, by alternation along the connecting path. Thus the same pairing cannot support all three prescribed patterns: same-shore pairing excludes 0000, and either opposite-shore pairing excludes one mixed pattern. The witnesses give at least two distinct pairings.

## Circuit merging and verification

Starting with one response per component, switch any flexible four-port component whose two pairs currently belong to distinct circuits. Either other pairing merges those circuits. Merges only coarsen connectivity, so one pass suffices. In the final partition all four ports of every flexible component lie in one part. No combination of supported responses, including an unused third pairing, can cross this partition. Test whether the defect wires now lie on the same circuit. The skeleton is a multigraph because a self-return wire can parallel a local response edge.

Decision is polynomial when every component has at most four ports. In this implementation a component with more ports returns an explicit abstention in the nonred branch. No large-block oracle is assumed.

Run python check.py with NetworkX installed and the sibling subdivision-single-hole/matching_multipole.py on disk. In 1,512 deterministic sampled cases on 72 connected cubic graphs with 6 through 16 vertices, 632 YES and 740 NO decisions agreed with independent exhaustive matching enumeration. There were 140 abstentions (131 YES and nine NO by enumeration). The merge step changed separate defects to coincident in 20 cases, 102 stayed separate and 307 started coincident. All 159 nontrivial four-port component occurrences furnished two responses. The replay also checks all 36 pairs of pins on the zero-defect K3,3 control.
