# A six-wire patch test for simultaneous circuit merging

In a native network, a disjoint unmarked patch with six boundary wires realizes a nonempty set T of permutations on three inputs/outputs. Compute it by direct internal enumeration or use a separately certified exact table with a stored internal setting per entry. Marked crossing wires remain exterior. For a chosen baseline t0 in T, put H=t0^{-1}T with a consistent input-side labeling (thus identity belongs). Let Q partition the three input positions into orbits of the subgroup generated by H. The following local property on arbitrary p inputs is sufficient for exact exterior rooted routing by contractions.

For every permutation k of the p positions, there exists h in H such that the cycle partition of kh is the join of the cycle partition of k with Q. Here k records the next marked input reached when the current global circuits are followed from the p positions at this patch, including the baseline local map. Replacing the baseline by t0 h replaces k by kh on those positions; cyclic orders elsewhere are irrelevant. Each intervening wire segment persists in the new traversal. A successful choice merges exactly the old circuits stipulated by this partition and never splits any old circuit. Detached circuits never touching the ports are unchanged, except for unmarked patch-internal circuits, which can be ignored.

Given pairwise disjoint patches passing the test, start with all their reference settings and process each once. At a step take the current k and realize an h from the test. All original baseline circuits are preserved as subsets of single circuits throughout, and the inputs of every processed Q-block now lie on the same circuit. Thus the final state realizes all possible exterior joins simultaneously: any trajectory under any other choice stays inside the equivalence classes generated by the original baseline cycles and the Q-block identifications. Patches having fixed internal marks can instead be left explicit. Any remaining r independent switches may be branched first for an additional factor 2^r. The choices reconstruct a physical mask; no count of settings is implied.

For three inputs, some reference passes precisely in these cases:
* one permutation;
* two differing by a transposition;
* four with two of each parity;
* five or six.

Indeed an intransitive H is either trivial or the order-two group on two positions. Both pass. For transitive H, k=id needs a nonidentity 3-cycle in H. For k any transposition the only ways to make kh a 3-cycle are to choose a different transposition h. This requires at least two distinct transpositions in H. If k itself is a 3-cycle, h=id suffices. Thus the reference must have at least two elements in its parity class in T (including itself) and at least two in the other. The list follows. An ordinary indigenous switch near a trivial cut, whose two options differ by a 3-cycle, fails this universal merging test. Failure only rejects this particular simultaneous-merging property; specific exteriors and pair queries may still be feasible. Conversely each failed k is physically testable by fixed exterior wiring giving return permutation k, so the classification is exact for the local simultaneous property.
