# Rooted reachability across independent permutation patches

Consider disjoint native patches, each with p entering and p exiting wires and no mark internal. Tabulate the externally realizable permutations T and one internal witness per permutation. Retain every crossing wire as a separate graph vertex. For a patch insert an arc from wire i on its input side to wire o on its output side whenever an element of T sends i to o. Use these arcs for both distinct and self-returning external wires. For fixed settings, following the unique successors gives the actual physical circuits outside the patches. Thus a directed path in this relaxed graph is necessary for joining the two marks.

A sufficient local test for arbitrary p is as follows. Every ordered sequence of turns (i_j,o_j) with distinct inputs, distinct outputs, all turns individually feasible, and no individually feasible forward shortcut (i_j,o_k) for j<k, must extend to an element of T. For fixed p this finite test is effective. If every patch passes, any shortest directed wire path from the first mark to the second realizes a physical witness. A shortest path with distinct endpoints has no repeated wire vertex. Thus the turns in each patch have distinct inputs and distinct outputs; a shortcut would shorten the global path. Choose a permutation extending all its turns, independently in every patch, and use that entry's stored witness. The actual global successor permutation then sends the first mark along the entire path to the second. Identical marks accept immediately. Additional explicit switches may be fixed by branching first. Detached internal cycles carry no exterior marks.

**Exact classification for p=3.** The pass tables are singletons, pairs differing by a transposition, triples all of the same permutation parity, and all tables of size at least four. For singletons all restrictions are compatible; for a pair differing by transposition the deterministic third channel is independent of the flexible pair, whose single-point transitions go to either other-side channel of that pair and hence cannot both be used in a shortcut-free list. A same-parity triple permits all nine turns individually, so a shortest path visits the patch at most once. With four or more entries an absent transition (a,b) accounts for two omitted permutations and leaves exactly four present. No second transition can be absent: the two permutations sending a to b share no other transition. If no turn is absent the previous argument applies. Otherwise three visits would require distinct missing shortcuts from the first input to the second and third outputs. For two visits, the missing shortcut is precisely (i1,o2); the unique permutation taking i1 to o1 and i2 to o2 does not take i1 to o2, so it belongs to T.

The remaining cases admit actual counterexamples to the relaxed reachability decision, for every such T. Cap the three outputs back to the three inputs by a fixed permutation k. For a mixed-parity triple choose k as the inverse of its unique minority-parity permutation. The closed permutations kT then consist of identity and two distinct transpositions. For a pair of the same parity take k of the opposite parity, so kT are two distinct transpositions. In both cases name the two incoming wire positions exchanged by the missing third transposition. They lie in the same directed reachability component of the union, since the two included transpositions generate a transitive group, but no allowed closed permutation has them on the same circuit. The argument applies to any physical patch having that specified external table; no internal color simulation is required. This is exact classification for literal relaxed wire reachability over all exteriors, not a hardness characterization for failed tables.

The sufficient test may also be expressed without orders. For each partial permutation F of individually allowed turns, put a directed edge between distinct turns (i,o) and (j,q) if i to q is allowed. A reverse topological ordering is exactly a shortcut-free ordering. Hence it suffices that every partial permutation failing to extend to T has a directed cycle in this auxiliary graph. This equivalent finite test charges all local choices and preserves actual physical witnesses.
