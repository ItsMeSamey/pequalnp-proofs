# Four named channels determine every two-permutation table

Consider an unmarked independent open patch on p inputs and p outputs, and the existential question whether two exterior wires lie on one orbit. Choose output coordinates making one candidate permutation the identity. For a set S of inputs, a restricted probe closes the channels outside S individually by identity, applies an arbitrary return permutation k on S, and marks two distinct closure wires in S. Define tr_S(h) by deleting symbols outside S from every cycle of h. Under this partial closure a physical h acts on S by tr_S(h); the fully capped first-return map on S is k tr_S(h). Detached internal cycles do not enter this equality.

Let T={id,π}, with π nonidentity. A permutation h satisfying every negative restricted probe of T on at most four names belongs to T. On two names in different π-cycles, the identity cap forbids h joining them. Thus h-cycles refine π-cycles. On each triple S the projected target has at most two permutations and is exactly determined by the eighteen three-track tests in three-track-cap-profiles.md. More precisely the projected h cannot lie outside that target: the union of all negative triples of test permutations equals the complement. If two points of a π-cycle lie together in h, then every third point of this π-cycle must lie in that same h-cycle, since the triple trace cannot be a transposition while the target traces are identity and a 3-cycle. Every triple in this active cycle has the cyclic order of π. Fixing one point as an origin, the triple orders give every comparison of the remaining symbols, so the entire h-cycle coincides with π's. The length-two case has unique nontrivial order. Consequently h is the product of some subset of π's nontrivial cycles.

If this subset is neither empty nor full, choose two symbols in an active cycle and two in an inactive cycle. On those names π restricts to (01)(23) and h to (01). Return k=(123) and mark 0,3. For id, k fixes 0. For π, kπ=(021)(3). For h, kh=(0231), and joins 0 and 3. This contradicts a negative test. Thus h is id or π. To distinguish a proper singleton subtable, take two elements in a nontrivial π-cycle: direct and crossed returns have opposite verdicts for the two traces. If π contains at least two nontrivial cycles, three names alone cannot exclude a partially active product: such a triple contains two or more symbols of at most one cycle, and its hybrid trace equals one of the two allowed traces. Four is sharp for this restricted probe model.

One can identify whether a physical open patch has at most two responses with O(p^4) calls to an exact rooted solver returning witnesses. First choose an arbitrary physical mask and normalize its response to identity. Test all pairs under direct closure; any nonidentity response joins some pair. If none, the table is the singleton identity. Otherwise take a witness for a positive pair and call its nonidentity response π. Run all restricted probes of size at most four which are negative on both candidates. A positive yields a third actual response. If all are negative, the preceding theorem gives exactly the two already witnessed responses.

Each physical native switch has three incoming and outgoing sockets. Switching one gate in a fixed open setting can change the external outputs of at most three input paths: in the original setting these paths are vertex-disjoint on wires and each affected path must have touched at least one of those three input sockets before the first difference. Repeated visits of a single path or detached circuits can only reduce the count. If a native independent-switch gadget had exactly two attainable permutations, a path in its Boolean mask cube between representatives would have an adjacent step changing one response to the other. Their relative permutation must therefore move at most three symbols. Since every two-entry table is rooted-cap rigid, no such independent gadget can even preserve all exterior two-wire existential queries for a tied control whose two choices differ on more than three channels. This includes tied disjoint rotations and tied disjoint swaps. Auxiliary internal feedback and detached trajectories are allowed. The argument does not exclude reductions that use the specific instance's exterior or discard assignmentwise behavior globally.
