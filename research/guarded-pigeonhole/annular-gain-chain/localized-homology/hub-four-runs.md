# Two components at a single fixed hub

Keep the free subcubic assumptions and indexing of hub-multiplicity.md. For two components the cyclic hub word has equally many transitions in each direction, say m. At m=1 the two component intervals close separately and their seam orbits splice, as in hub-crossing-blocks.md. At m≥3 all passage pairs join by the multiplicity criterion. At m=2 the four nonempty cyclic runs are X₀,Y₀,X₁,Y₁.

For a candidate direction from passage p to passage q, let a be the head leaf of p and b the tail leaf of q. If they have the same color, a single simple-component route works unless a=b. If their colors differ, use a crossing passage from color(a) to color(b). Failure of this certificate means a is the tail of one of the two candidate crossings and b the head of the other. Thus a must be last and b first in their respective runs, and the run of b must not immediately follow that of a.

Two internal marks of the same color succeed in one direction since both endpoint coincidences would require hub size two. For internal marks of different colors, their runs are adjacent in one direction around the four runs, so that direction cannot fail the crossing test. For two crossings of opposite directions, both searches stay within a color, where the two coincidence failures again require size two. For crossings in the same direction, say X₀→Y₀ and X₁→Y₁, if their intervening marked leaves are both forbidden then both exclusions hit the same crossing Y₀→X₁, leaving Y₁→X₀ available.

It remains to check one internal mark of color X and one crossing. Reversing the cyclic word and all rotations if necessary, take the crossing entering X. (Inverse hub orientation is used in the mirror instance; recover by reversing all rotations and mark darts.) The within-X direction fails only if the internal marked passage begins at the first leaf of the X-run just entered. Denote these first two leaves by a,b. The reverse direction fails only if b ends its X-run and the Y-leaf immediately preceding a is first in its own run, i.e. that Y-run is singleton. Conversely these conditions exclude opposite crossings of the two available choices. This is the entire exceptional family for the one-visit-per-component certificate.

In the exceptional family write the cyclic leaves as a,b,v₁,…,v_l,x₁,…,x_t,s with a,b,x_i in X and v_i,s in Y; l,t≥1. The marked departure labels are a,b. Partially close the Y component by the passages v_i→v_(i+1) for i<l. Its two entries are s,v₁ and its two corresponding exits are s,v_l. Every mask has a bijective entry-to-exit map regardless of closed internal orbits. Swap is realizable by a simple path from s to v_l. When l≥2 identity is realizable by the distinct-leaf simple path v₁ to v_l. For l=1 Y is genuinely a two-port component; its identity realizability is a free subcubic skew query after capping its two leaves with a transposition: the two capped passages join precisely for identity return.

Identity gives X one cap (a b x₁ … x_t); its two marks are joinable in any connected free component at hub order ≥3. If identity is impossible, the realized transposition instead induces (a b)(x₁ … x_t) on X. Its a,b departure marks are the two sides of the edge obtained by suppressing the deterministic two-port cap of a,b. The remaining x_i cap is one fixed hub, without loops at that hub before suppressing any parallel spokes. Thus the residual question is precisely whether this edge can be monofacial in the resulting one-hub rotation graph. This is a reduction to that separate kernel, including t=1 and repeated underlying attachments.

check_hub_four_runs.py checks the exceptional-layout claim for every run length up to five (43,750 pairs, 200 exceptions) and compares unreduced versus reduced cap trace over 4,800 complete pairs of local permutations. Component route realizations use simple paths in the actual free graphs; the transfer-identity check itself is independent of which permutations a particular component admits.

## Polynomial completion by the monofacial-edge criterion

In the remaining case remove the marked leaves a,b of the connected X component, recording their attachment vertices u,v, and cap the other leaves with their single cyclic hub H. Apply the [one-hub monofacial theorem](../../six-cycle-sat/feedback/one-hub-monofacial.md), reviewed at SHA-256 719103d653d43639ceedc32aa0c0b000a688d22890d0ef30848b1d634953ed4b. The graph J so obtained has a loopless H, freely rotatable subcubic vertices, and J−H connected; deleting pendant leaves preserves connectivity among the remaining internal vertices. The attachment degrees are at most two, or at most one if they coincide. Inserting uv restores permissible degrees. The exceptional X component has at least three leaves so the degenerate isolated two-leaf edge cannot arise here. The two marks join exactly when the u-to-v path in the bridge tree of J meets a bridge-deleted component that contains an edge. This handles repeated attachments and parallel hub edges. Reconstruct the X orientations by that theorem, use the already found Y transposition setting, and restore the hub. Consequently every two-component instance with both marks on cap passages has a polynomial decision and witness algorithm across all m.

An independent end-to-end control in check_hub_monofacial_composition.py constructs 48 actual exceptional-layout graphs with 3–6 X leaves and 0–2 added independent cycles. For each graph it compares the original five-or-more-port return under direct physical mask search with the graph-theoretic corridor predicate, recovering 40 positive and eight negative answers.
