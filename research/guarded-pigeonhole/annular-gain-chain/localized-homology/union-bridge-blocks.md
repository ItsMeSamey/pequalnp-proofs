# Local cycle rank along the bridge path for arbitrary native options

Use the union multigraph G of union-band-core.md for the supplied artificial reference. Remove its graphic bridges and form the forest of resulting bridgeless blocks. In the component of the two marks take their unique block path. If they are disconnected report NO. Its maximum individual block cycle rank h suffices for deterministic time 2^{O(h+1)}poly with an original mask, without restricting any off-path block.

Every bridge along the path must be present. An optional bridge belongs to exactly one gate and requires one value of that gate. Impose all such units at once and reject conflicts. Its actual sockets are now determined. On a compulsory bridge the socket at the common end a is fixed; the alternative sockets b,c belong to the same disk on the opposite side. Assign any freedom in this bit to that block only. In particular no gate on a compulsory bridge also controls an internal edge. Every remaining gate with a nonbridge option can affect internal edge presence and rotation in only one block: its two alternatives share their a-disk, and every nonbridge lies entirely in the block of that disk. If the other option is an off-path bridge, model that choice as no internal band.

For every fixed global mask, an off-path branch attaches by at most one present bridge. Gluing that bridge merges just one boundary on each side; it does not affect boundary equivalence between any retained corners. At a path block the required local terminals are the two bridge attachment corners facing along the path, substituting the original marked corners at its ends. Deleting all external bands, the two original marks share a global boundary iff the two local terminals share a boundary in every path block. To see necessity, glue along the path one bridge at a time: after distinct boundary components form between the initial mark and the next attachment, later one-bridge extensions can never join them. Sufficiency follows by tracking the same boundary through the splices. Each bridge attachment is taken at its socket before the successor jump, as for an ordinary mark; with the bridge deleted the two sides of its unused socket are consecutive on a single boundary.

Independently test every path block. Enumerate at most two free incoming portal bits. On its internal union multigraph solve the resulting fixed-two-corner question by the corridor algorithm in union-band-core.md. Optional edges whose other option lies outside the block still have a single ordinary presence literal; through and broken constraints continue to apply. Respect the initial globally fixed units, and physically evaluate each representative inside the block with only selected internal bands. An isolated disk contributes its sole boundary. Unpinned bits owned by different block solvers are disjoint, so their successful masks may be combined, extended arbitrarily off path, and checked on the given physical instance. The runtime follows since there are at most linearly many blocks and each handles at most two free portal bits in addition to its internal cycle-rank cost.

For a limitation on supplied-reference optimization, consider the native ring on n gates with three equally directed wires running from each gate to the next. Every artificial-reference trajectory travels around the physical ring, so its length is at least n and the number of baseline disks is at most three. The union graph is connected (every gate links all three of its sockets) and has at least n edge indices, hence whole-component cycle rank at least n−2 for every reference. This does not assert a lower bound on every pair's bridge-corridor parameter or on general routing complexity.
