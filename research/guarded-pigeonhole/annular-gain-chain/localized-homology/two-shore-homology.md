# An ambient homology test using both sides of a smoothing

Fix a physical mask in a cellular orientable native rotation system and cap the surface. Thicken its black disks and the full stars belonging to the black side. The complementary white disks and complementary stars give a spine of the other side. These two compact subsurfaces meet along the disjoint closed wire trajectories. For each named wire take a short transverse bridge between its black and white spines, crossing their common boundary only at its mark. All statements use binary chains.

The two marks lie on one trajectory if and only if one can take black and white chains connecting their respective bridge endpoints such that the sum of the two chains and the two bridges is zero in ambient first homology. Chains may include arbitrary closed components. For necessity, take either arc of a shared boundary circle between the marks and push a copy slightly into each side. Together with the transverse ends these copies bound a narrow rectangle. Continue each transverse bridge to its spine endpoint and retract each side to its spine, adjusting endpoints by those same bridge tails. These changes add boundaries. Coincident marks can be subdivided along the same wire, or treated as immediate success.

For sufficiency, represent the two spine chains away from all boundary circles. If the marks were on distinct circles, the combined chain would have transverse intersection one with the first marked circle, entirely from its bridge. All boundaries have even intersection with every closed curve, so this is impossible. Additional loops on either side are disjoint from that circle and cannot cancel the obstruction. No choice of orientation of the individual trajectories is needed. This equivalence concerns a fixed complementary assignment of stars, and does not supply a method of selecting the mask.

For an explicit test subdivide wires at their midpoints, add the face centers, and triangulate each switch corner along its radial arm. A wire bridge uses the two midpoint-to-disk edges. Test whether the sum of the two bridges lies in the binary span of all triangle boundaries and all permitted arm edges. An affirmative linear combination automatically satisfies the black and white endpoint equations: a surface boundary has zero vertex boundary, the permitted spines are disjoint, and only the bridges contribute prescribed endpoints. Parallel arms and halfedges retain separate coordinates, including loop wires.

The [checker](check_two_shore_homology.py) builds this cellular span independently of the native permutation oracle. It checked 305,712 mask/root queries on 861 native charts, including every socket permutation on one and two gates. It uses the full capped cellular surface for each fixed rotation, with no genus-zero filter.
