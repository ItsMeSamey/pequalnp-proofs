# A single asymmetric closing arc destroys bounded odd-syndrome radius

Over GF(2), let A be the symmetric adjacency matrix of the ordinary path 1,…,n, with n odd and at least three. Set M=A+e_n e_1^T. Then M is a rank-one deviation from a symmetric alternating matrix, and M+M^T has rank two. The only nonsingular odd-order principal submatrix of M is the entire matrix.

A nonzero term of a principal determinant using the asymmetric arc n→1 must complete its permutation cycle by a simple directed path 1→n using the edges of the undirected path. This uses every vertex, with unique directed order. Thus proper minors are unchanged from A. Every odd-order principal submatrix of A is alternating, hence singular. At full order the extra determinant term is unique, so the determinant changes from zero to one. The single required syndrome |X|=1 mod 2 forces the whole support.

The same example satisfies the invertible-shift convention of a diagonal-completion chart when n=5 mod 6. The leading determinants of I+A obey d_0=d_1=1 and d_j=d_(j−1)+d_(j−2), by expansion of the tridiagonal matrix. Therefore d_n=0 for n=2 mod 3. Adding the closing arc changes the full determinant by one: any permutation using it still has to return from 1 to n by the entire directed path, irrespective of diagonal loops. Hence I+M is invertible. Its inverse D has skew defect rank two, since D+D^T=D^T(M+M^T)D. It can be written as a rank-one deviation from a symmetric matrix: any rank-two alternating form over GF(2) is uv^T+vu^T, so adding uv^T symmetrizes D. Then M=I+D^−1 exactly. This rules out a bound on distance from the all-zero principal set in terms of syndrome rank alone even on these asymmetry-rank-two charts. It does not rule out polynomial decision by a long-path or other algorithm, nor does it show realizability with additional paired native switch constraints.
