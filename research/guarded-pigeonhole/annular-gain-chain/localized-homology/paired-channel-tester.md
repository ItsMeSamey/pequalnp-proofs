# A two-mark cap detects every missing product pattern on paired channels

Let a nonempty permutation table T preserve each of m disjoint two-track channel pairs, after known input and output relabelings. Write its entries as bit vectors of swap choices. It passes the universal cap reachability test if and only if T equals the product of its one-coordinate projections.

If the table is the product, each variable coordinate acts as an independent two-port flexible patch; constants are deterministic wiring. Directed reachability then solves every capped instance by the two-port theorem. Conversely suppose a pattern a is in the product but absent from T. Discard fixed coordinates when designing the active portion of the cap; they may be capped separately. Index the remaining pairs (L_i,R_i), i=1,...,d. Use an auxiliary cap permutation k0 with singleton cycles L_1 and R_d and two-cycles (R_i,L_{i+1}) for i<d. A swap on pair i joins neighboring vertices i−1 and i of the path of baseline cycles. Whatever subset of swaps is enabled, its edges form a forest of distinct transpositions and its resulting circuits are exactly the union of the baseline cycles in each forest component (inductively merge distinct cycles). Thus L_1 and R_d are on one circuit precisely if all d swaps are enabled. Compose k0 on its input side with the fixed coordinatewise exchange pattern equal to the complement of a. After composition the all-swaps event for the effective setting means precisely that the raw pattern equals a. Every tested coordinate individually allows both transitions, so the relaxed wire graph connects the two marks. Since T omits a, every actual table entry separates them. The cap uses no external choices and only the 2d positions already on the varying channels. This is an exact relation-level distinction, leaving constructions that change the interface or only preserve one decision target outside its scope.
