# Two-track patches with a rainbow-forest torso

Allow independent optional transpositions alongside the strict three-socket gates. Fix all optional gates off and assume the remaining strict torso admits an artificial reference with a full rainbow forest. For each optional gate adjoin a fresh color with one real band and one isolated edge between two private dummy vertices standing for off. Adding all dummy edges to a mandatory forest proves full rank. Every physical join gives a rainbow path by the star argument of attainable-forest-reference.md. The proper-colored path algorithm uses the real optional edges, each with a unique color. Closure-preserving augmentation produces a full forest still connecting the marked disks. Removing isolated dummy edges translates back to an actual joining mask. Thus any number of optional transpositions is allowed without increasing the asymptotic polynomial degree. A failed reference recognizer leaves the residual unresolved.

Any independently controlled two-in/two-out open patch induces for each mask one of two bijections, possibly with detached closed trajectories. In the direct cap the two distinct marked closure wires join precisely for swap; in the crossed cap they join precisely for identity. Two exact rooted queries produce the complete table and one witness per attainable permutation. Replace a singleton by fixed wiring or a doubleton by one optional transposition. All exterior trajectories, including repeated feedback visits, are preserved. Expand representatives recursively using the disjoint child witnesses.

On a supplied two-track decomposition, process leaves first. Use either the forest recognizer and solver for each capped torso, setting optional switches off for recognition, or an independent exact local solver. Preserve the original marks on the root torso or its exterior seam. An unresolved required query gives UNKNOWN. After all children are replaced, decide the actual root question. Time is polynomial in total explicit tree and network size for polynomial local algorithms.

For greedy search in a connected host, enumerate quadruples of distinct wires. Deleting them leaves at most five weak components; enumerate their unions and check for exact two-in/two-out boundary, no interior mark, and at least one mandatory gate. A successful replacement strictly decreases the number of mandatory gates. Unmarked disconnected components have arbitrary settings; distinct marked components give NO. This polynomial peeling does not guarantee finding every viable nested decomposition.
