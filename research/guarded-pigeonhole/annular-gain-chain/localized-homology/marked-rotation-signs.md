# Correcting prescribed cubic rotations by marked automorphisms

In the bipartite rotation map for native routing, black vertices have independently reversible cubic orders and each white vertex has the fixed cyclic order of its forbidden transition cycle. Here white degrees are at most three. Only the d cubic white orders are prescribed binary choices; white degrees one and two have a unique cyclic order. Distinguish the two marked oriented darts individually. Relax the cubic white orders to be independent, so that the graph now has free cubic rotations everywhere, with ordinary suppressed degree-two vertices and leaves. The skew-regular solver tests whether a directed face trail joins the marked darts and returns its turns. Maintain the marked endpoint positions through suppression or work directly with the directed darts of the subcubic graph, where edge reversal is the skew involution and each cubic vertex's two orders supply its two outgoing turns. Degree-one or degree-two vertices have a unique continuation; if an arc is its own reversed continuation at a leaf, provide two parallel mate copies. A simple path uses at most one such leaf turn and either realizes the same physical transition.

## Full rank

Use the automorphism group of the bipartite incidence map *before* imposing the prescribed signs. Preserve black and white types, fix each exceptional white vertex individually and fix both marked darts individually. At each cubic white vertex the action on its three incident flags has a permutation parity, which records exactly whether it reverses the cyclic order. The vector of these parities is a homomorphism since the white vertices are fixed. Its image H is a binary subspace. Any automorphism sends a relaxed setting and its face witness to another with the same marked darts and with sign vector translated by its image; black choices remain available.

Given generators, Gaussian elimination finds H and records generator products with each required translation. If H has rank d, apply the translation from the returned relaxed orientation to the prescribed one. The resulting face witness and black choices solve the original instance. A negative relaxed answer is always decisive; trace positive witnesses after translating. The fixed group is a sufficient source of corrections; allowing permutations of exceptional vertices would give a signed permutation action and needs a different argument.

This group and its sign generators can be obtained in polynomial time at bounded valence. Encode each edge with its own edge vertex and two separate flags on the path from one endpoint vertex through its flag, the edge vertex and the other flag to the other endpoint vertex. Give vertex, edge and flag types appropriate colors; direct marks are specified flags with individually distinguished colors. Exceptional white vertices are individualized. The encoded graph is simple, has maximum degree three here, and parallel original edges retain separate flags. A bounded-degree colored graph isomorphism oracle suffices to build generators: successively individualize vertices along a stabilizer chain, test each possible image for the next vertex, and recover one realizing permutation by further individualization and oracle queries. The coset representatives at all levels generate the full group. This takes polynomially many bounded-degree colored isomorphism tests. [Luks, Isomorphism of graphs of bounded valence can be tested in polynomial time](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(82)90009-5)

Independent signs occur for arbitrarily many exceptional vertices. Place a ring of cubic pairs b_i,w_i, each joined by two parallel edges, and add an edge from w_i to b_(i+1) cyclically. Mark two of these single connector edges as directed darts. Each private parallel-edge exchange fixes the marked darts and all vertices, and reverses exactly one white order, so the image is the full sign space.

## Partial correction and unused vertices

A simple relaxed trail from one marked dart to the other fixes an order only at the cubic vertices where it uses a turn. Let V be the visited exceptional vertices. Correcting its prescribed signs is possible via this group exactly when the discrepancy restricted to V lies in the coordinate projection of H. In that case select a realizing automorphism, apply it to the witness, and choose the prescribed rotations independently at unvisited vertices. In this statement 'exactly' concerns the specified orbit of this particular trail and the free settings at its unused vertices. Other relaxed trails may exist.

Let C have row space H-perpendicular and let c=d−rank H. If the columns of C at unused vertices span the c-dimensional syndrome space, every discrepancy on V can be corrected. Indeed the unused coordinates can be chosen to zero the syndrome, giving a vector of H with the desired restriction. Search explicitly for this sufficient kind of witness by enumerating the at most binomial(d,c) column bases J of C. Remove all turn arcs at vertices of J in the relaxed graph, which is a skew-invariant deletion. A regular path in the remainder can be corrected. Conversely any path whose unused columns span the syndrome avoids some enumerated basis. This costs d^{O(c)} polynomial time and does not decide instances in which every useful path visits an indispensable parity support. For c=1, failure of these tests certifies that every relaxed joining path visits every vertex in the support of the nonzero check row.

## A deficiency-one obstruction

Take two exceptional white vertices X,Y joined by three parallel length-two paths X–b_i–Y, i=0,1,2. Each black b_i is made cubic by attaching a private white leaf. Mark the darts X→b_0 and Y→b_0. The effective theta rotations at X,Y have signs relative to their common path labels. Deleting pendant excursions shows that equal signs give a single face, whereas opposite signs give three digon faces. The marked darts traverse opposite sides of path 0; thus the accepting white sign patterns are precisely 00 and 11, regardless of black orientations. A marked automorphism fixes path 0 and permutes the same remaining path labels at both ends. The exchange of 1 and 2 realizes sign 11. Consequently H={00,11}. For prescribed sign 01, either coordinate by itself has a joining completion but their required parity does not. The subgroup that keeps either single sign fixed cannot correct the other. Deficiency one therefore is not the same as one independently pinnable white rotation; this example does not exclude a different parameterized algorithm that enforces parities. Run check_sign_theta.py for the four sign patterns and eight black masks each.
