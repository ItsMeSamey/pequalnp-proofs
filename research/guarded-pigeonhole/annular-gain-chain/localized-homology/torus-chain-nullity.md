# Native instances forcing one used chain cycle per handle

For every k≥1 there is a connected capped orientable native instance of genus k in which every successful mask requires total cycle rank at least k in its two partial colored chains. This counts only edges actually used by the binary chains. A version with two planar end boxes also forces opposite values at its two end switches and distinct root disks on both shores.

## Physical network

Use k one-gate boxes in series. Give each three incoming sockets 0,1,2 locally; physical bit one rotates them by ρ=(012) before fixed feedback. The fixed feedback of each torus box is ρ. Its left wire mark is the incoming socket 0 and its right mark is 1. Both gate states have one local trajectory since ρ and ρ² are three-cycles. For the stronger version first prepend a one-gate box with identity feedback and append a one-gate box with feedback ρ⁻¹. Their marks are again 0 and 1. The left pair joins only with bit one; the right pair joins only with bit zero.

For either version form the disjoint union of the local feedbacks, then postcompose with the disjoint transpositions exchanging incoming labels 1 of each box and 0 of the following box. Keep the leftmost 0 and rightmost 1 as the two global marks. Each such splice merges the two marked orbits from formerly separate subchains. On adjoining another box the old left root and the new right root join exactly when the old endpoints joined and the new local endpoints joined. Thus every torus bit is free; precisely the two prescribed cap settings are necessary in the capped version. In particular neither uniform mask solves the capped version.

Use the native orientable rotation in which all-off follows black faces and all-on follows white faces. Under any splice between disjoint subchains, the corresponding two black faces become one and the two white faces become one; other faces are unaffected. This follows by applying the preceding orbit splice in the all-off and all-on routings. Every torus box originally has one face of each color, with all three incidences at one disk on each shore; these equalities persist after splicing. Each planar end box has facial counts 3 and 1 in the appropriate order. Euler's formula gives genus k in both versions. When capped, the global roots lie at different black disks by the all-off test and different white disks by the all-on test.

## The independent obstruction in each torus box

Use the dual cellulation of the native embedding: vertices are its black and white faces, one bridge edge e_x crossing the wire with incoming label x runs between its two face centers, and every physical gate corresponds to one hexagonal face. Its boundary consists of the three incoming and three outgoing wire edges with multiplicity. Hexagons may have repeated vertices and edges. The black/white chain incidences are radial arms in these hexagons, to corners of the appropriate shore.

Fix a torus box i. Give every bridge e_x an additive value in the binary vector space with independent unit vectors u₀,u₁,u₂. Use u₀ for incoming labels in earlier boxes, u₁ for labels in later boxes, and u_j for label j in box i. Each exchanged pair of incoming labels has equal value under this assignment: at the left splice of i both give u₀, and at the right splice both give u₁. Thus every hexagon's boundary has value zero. Indeed the outgoing bridge values are the same multiset as those of the three local inputs, even after splicing. The assignment vanishes on cellular boundaries, so a null-homologous cycle in the dual one-skeleton must have zero value.

Any permitted binary pair of chains uses either no arms or two arms at each gate, by the cycle boundary equation at its center and the complementary shore condition. Push a used pair of arms across its hexagon to a boundary path between its two corners. This preserves relative homology, including for repeated face disks, using a characteristic disk with corners kept distinct before gluing. Its length is even since both corners have the same shore. For each box other than i all six boundary values are identical, so any such path has value zero. The two outer marked bridges together have value u₀+u₁, including when i is an end box of the uncapped series. Consequently every null completion must use two arms in box i. These two arms lead to the very same disk, on either shore, forming a literal two-edge cycle in the used support. The k forced cycles are edge-disjoint and independent in the cycle space, even when they share a disk.

Every joined witness admits a null completion by the [two-shore criterion](two-shore-homology.md). In the uncapped family the lower bound is exact: on either shore each used torus gate contributes two parallel arms at a single disk and has no other used edge. The same equality holds with caps: the forced left gate is black and its three arms meet three distinct black disks, the forced right gate is white and its three arms meet three distinct white disks; subsequent splices through distinct boxes never identify two disks already in one box. There is just one such added pair per shore and it joins different disks, adding no cycle. Hence the minimum total used-support cycle rank is exactly k.

The [checker](check_torus_nullity_boxes.py) enumerates all physical masks in both versions through k=7. It tests the genus, root orbits, and null completion by direct binary elimination in the subdivided cellulation, then repeats elimination with each torus box's chain arms forbidden. All 1,270 masks, including 508 joined masks, and 7,690 separate box-deletion checks passed. These are examples with growing genus.
