# Rooted parallel-band routing along the bridge tree

Use the supplied parallel-band reference of parallel-band-core.md. For each gate the two transpositions use (a,b) and (a,c), with b,c on the same reference disk. The two choices give the same abstract multigraph edge. Remove all bridges of this multigraph, counting parallel edges and loops as usual, and contract each surviving connected component to a node of a forest. Call these components bridge blocks, including isolated disks. Reject if the two marked disks lie in distinct trees. Otherwise take the unique block path between their blocks; it may consist of one node. Write h for the largest internal cycle rank of any block on this path.

A bridge band between two disjoint ribbons joins exactly the boundary components containing its two attachment intervals. Cutting open each of these boundary circles at its attachment gives an interval; the two long sides of the band splice the intervals into a single circle. All other boundary circles retain their corners. Equivalently, on permutations, swapping successors at two cuts on disjoint circuits merges precisely those two circuits. In each isolated block a mark is the wire position immediately before its socket, as in the original solver. A portal for a deleted bridge is likewise the corner at its chosen attachment socket; no other band can use that socket.

For any fixed mask, deleting a subtree that meets the rest through one bridge preserves all boundary equivalences between corners remaining on the root side: adding the subtree back merges only one of its own boundary components with a single old boundary component. All subtrees outside the block path can therefore be ignored regardless of their internal cycle ranks. In each retained block specify two terminals, one for each direction along the path; at an endpoint use the actual mark in place of a missing path bridge. If both marks belong to one block they are its only two terminals. The global marks share a boundary circle precisely when the two terminals of every path block share a local boundary circle. Successive bridge splices propagate the boundary containing the first mark forward exactly when it reaches the next attachment. Once the attachments disagree, the remaining suffix has only one bridge into the processed prefix and cannot reconnect its two boundary circles.

At the a-side of a bridge its attachment is fixed. Its other side alone chooses b or c; both are on one disk there. Assign this choice exclusively to that block, so the tests on all path blocks are independent. Any combination of successful local masks, extended arbitrarily on ignored edges, gives an original physical mask. The solver verifies it on the untrimmed network.

Within each retained block enumerate its at most two variable portal bits. For fixed terminal corners invoke the degree-two observation of parallel-band-core.md. Retain bits of internal bands incident to a terminal disk or to a disk of degree other than two, with degree taken inside the block. On any other disk the cyclic order of the two retained band ends is unique, regardless of the actual attachment sockets. Such changes preserve all terminal boundary equivalences. A nontrivial bridge block has minimum degree at least two. With cycle rank β≥1, the sum of (degree minus 2) is 2β−2, hence the total degree of disks of degree at least three is at most 6β−6. The remaining terminal disks add at most four internal incidences, and portals add at most two bits. Thus at most 6β bits are enumerated for a nontrivial block. An isolated disk uses at most two portal bits, always placing both on its sole boundary. The total running time is polynomial in input size times 2 to the power max(2,6h), with no dependence in the exponent on the number of blocks or any off-path cycle rank.

The companion parallel_bridge_blocks.py implements the local physical-permutation checks and combines the witnesses. It compares the result with exhaustive original masks on 1,600 seeded parallel-band instances through eight gates, including loops, parallel edges and disconnected examples.
