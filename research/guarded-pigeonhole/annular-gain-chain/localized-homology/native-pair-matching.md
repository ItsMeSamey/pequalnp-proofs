# Recognizing compatible pairs, including a six-edge-connected class

Given a connected independent-switch instance, consider every single gate and every pair of gates as a possible patch. Skip a patch containing a marked wire internally. Its number p of external tracks is at most six. Enumerate its at most four settings and trace each external input; store every distinct resulting permutation together with a realizing setting. For p=0 handle the unmarked component separately. Test the shortcut-free partial-permutation condition of six-wire-reachability.md; the enumeration size is constant independent of the network. A single gate failing this test or excluded due to an internal mark has baseline cost one, an individually passing gate cost zero. Build a graph on gates with an edge for every passing pair, weighted by the sum of its endpoint baseline costs. A maximum-weight matching gives a disjoint set of passing pairs minimizing r, the number of leftover gates which do not individually qualify. This optimizes over the explicit one- and two-gate passing-patch model, even when candidates overlap. Disconnected candidate pairs are allowed or may be consistently excluded. Compact the chosen pairs, keep passing singletons, branch on the r other switches and use the directed reachability solver on the resulting tables in each branch. Deterministic running time is 2^r poly(n), with an original mask obtained by table witness lifting. No graph-connectivity promise or supplied decomposition is required.

**A genuine six-edge-connected family.** Take a loopless simple three-edge-connected, two-vertex-connected cubic graph H. For every vertex v create gates A_v and B_v, with three distinct parallel wires from A_v to B_v, and no other wires internal to that pair. Bidir every original edge uv by adding wires B_u to A_v and B_v to A_u. This gives three inputs and outputs per gate. With arbitrary labellings of its three internal connections, the serial product of the two independent native optional rotations produces exactly all three permutations in a parity class between the exterior tracks. Indeed conjugation by any matching of three wires sends a 3-cycle to itself or its inverse, and a+b or a−b with a,b in {0,1} takes all three residues. Every pair lacking an internal mark therefore passes the rooted shortcut test. At most two pairs can have an internal mark, so r≤4 for arbitrary marked wires.

Every proper wire cut in this network has size at least six. Two or more split pairs incur at least six parallel wires. One split pair incurs three such wires and, for each of its three neighbors, at least one of the oppositely routed exterior links to the unsplit neighboring pair. When no pair splits, the cut is twice its base cut. Removing any one gate leaves the intact pairs connected by two-vertex-connectivity of H, and its partner still connects to them. In particular every gate is fully injected by the raw socket-rank formula, and there is no four-wire cut to contract.

For explicit unbounded minimum genus and treewidth, start with the 4-regular high-genus graph of four-cut-boxes.md containing the toroidal grid as minor. Truncate every vertex to a four-cycle, distributing its four external edges to separate vertices, to get H. The old graph is a minor. H remains two-vertex-connected after losing a vertex: its replacement cycle becomes a path, with at most one external link lost, and the other base vertices remain connected after deletion of the affected base vertex. For three-edge-connectivity, splitting two replacement cycles costs at least four; splitting none costs at least the original mincut four. If one cycle splits, a cut of size at most two could have no external crossings and exactly two cycle crossings. All other cycles would have to lie on one side by connectivity of the base minus the affected vertex, forcing all four vertices of the split cycle to that same side. This contradiction proves the required connectivity. Finally the expanded native graph contains H as a minor by contracting each pair and discarding duplicate edges. This gives unbounded minimum compatible genus and underlying treewidth among instances with r≤4 and no proper cuts below six. It separates the parameter guarantees of the raw rank, four-cut, and fixed-genus solvers; other specialized solvers can overlap on particular inputs.
