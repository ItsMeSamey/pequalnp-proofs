# Eliminate unmarked four-wire boxes on independent native switches

A proper gate set with four wires to the rest of the network has two entering and two exiting wires, by conservation of indegree and outdegree at each gate. Assume neither marked wire is wholly internal. Every setting in the set induces an external permutation in S2. Starting at an entrance cannot enter a detached cycle, by injectivity of the internal routing. Thus either there is one fixed external permutation or both are possible. Tabulate the possibilities with one internal setting for each by direct enumeration. Arbitrary internal detached circuits have no marked wire and may be dropped. A marked crossing wire retains its identity in the contracted network. This remains true when two different boxes share crossing wires.

Given disjoint boxes on at most b gates apiece and r leftover gates, branch on the leftovers. Fix an available baseline permutation in each box, recording its two external entering wire sockets. Changing a flexible box performs a transposition of trajectories at these two sockets. Form the graph on baseline circuits with an edge for each flexible transposition. Under any settings the trajectories stay inside its components. Conversely flip exactly the boxes on a spanning forest; each flip merges two previously distinct circuits by transposition, so all marks in a common component are joined. Lift choices with the saved internal settings and verify directly by traversal. Runtime is (2^b+2^r) poly(n), with no embedding assumptions.

**Exact packing.** Suppose the undirected gate multigraph has minimum edge cut at least four (loops do not cross cuts). Enumerate all proper shores with four crossing wires, at most b gates, and no internal mark. There are polynomially many since deleting a minimum cut yields exactly two connected shores, and sets of four wires can be enumerated. Call such a shore eligible. While any eligible shore lies in the as-yet uncovered U, choose an inclusion-maximal one A there and remove it. This minimizes the leftover gate count, even with nonnegative weights. Compare to an optimal disjoint eligible family inside U. Discard its pieces contained in A; each overlapping incomparable B can be replaced by B minus A. Cut posimodularity gives d(B minus A)+d(A minus B) at most 8. Both differences are nonempty proper sets and mincut is at least four, hence B minus A is itself a four-wire shore. Size and internal mark exclusion persist. A proper superset of A is ruled out by maximality. The replacements and A cover everything previously covered, so there is an optimum making the greedy choice. Induct. For graphs with small cuts one can still supply boxes directly or use disjoint pairs of loopless gates joined by four wires (these pairs never overlap because each gate has only six ends). Failure to find small residual bounds imposes no verdict on routing.

**Unbounded topology.** Take the square toroidal grid of side m at least 4. Replace each vertex by K3,4, attaching the four old incident edges one each to its four degree-three vertices. The resulting simple 4-regular graph has the grid as minor, and contains disjoint copies of K3,3, one per grid vertex. Its orientable genus grows: disjoint regular neighborhoods of the K3,3 embeddings each have positive genus since capping all neighborhood boundaries yields an embedding of K3,3. The sum of genera of disjoint compact connected subsurfaces is at most ambient genus. The base is 2-vertex-connected and 4-edge-connected. Indeed each replacement is a 4-edge sum obtained by deleting one degree-four vertex each in the previous base and K4,4 and pairing the loose ends. A cut splitting both surviving pieces internally costs at least two internal crossing edges in each: complete either side of its internal partition by placing the removed vertex there, so internal crossings plus the smaller number of incident loose ends is at least four. If one piece is unsplit contract it and use the original edge-connectivity. Vertex connectivity also follows since each K3,4 remains connected after one deletion and at most one exterior link is then lost.

Orient the resulting connected 4-regular base along an Euler tour, giving two inputs and two outputs per base vertex. Replace it by gates A,B, each with external input and output at its socket 0. Outputs at sockets 1,2 of each gate feed sockets 2,1 of its partner respectively. Both gates off yield the identity on external tracks. Both on send the external input of A via its output 1 to B input 2 and thus to B output 0; symmetrically the other input goes to A output 0. Each pair is a flexible four-wire box. This native graph remains 4-edge-connected: any cut splitting a pair already crosses four internal wires, and every other cut is a base cut. It stays loopless and 2-vertex-connected, as the surviving member of a deleted gate's pair still meets the rest of the base. Its base graph minor gives unbounded treewidth and minimum orientable genus, and every raw gate is fully injected by the socket rank formula. The recognition procedure removes all pairs for exterior marks (at most four leftovers if both marks are internal to different pairs). These positive families illustrate solver scope; this is not a reduction of arbitrary independent routing to small four-wire boxes.
