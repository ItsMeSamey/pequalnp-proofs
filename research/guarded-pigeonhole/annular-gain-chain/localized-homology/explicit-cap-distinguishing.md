# NP-completeness of distinguishing explicit rooted cap profiles

Input consists of two explicitly listed nonempty tables of permutations on p names. A certificate of profile inequivalence is a return permutation k and two distinct input labels s,t for which at least one member of exactly one table joins the marks in kh. Evaluation over both explicit lists is polynomial. Profile inequivalence is NP-complete even when the two tables are B and B plus identity and every member of B differs from identity at at most three symbols. Profile equivalence is therefore coNP-complete. The same reduction works when s,t are specified as part of the instance. This is an unbounded-arity explicit table comparison problem, not existential joining in a fixed closed independent-switch network.

Start from a not-all-equal 3SAT instance with at least one clause, introducing one distinct occurrence symbol for each literal position, including repetitions. Use two fresh names for the canonical marks. For each variable order its occurrence symbols arbitrarily. Consecutive symbols of opposite polarities receive a swap row (u v). Consecutive symbols of equal polarity receive rows (u z),(z v) through a fresh auxiliary z. A variable with only one occurrence receives one swap from that occurrence to a fresh leaf. This yields disjoint paths of swaps and touches every occurrence. For each clause with occurrence names a,b,c add the single cyclic row (a b c). The cycle supports are pairwise disjoint, and each name belongs to at most three nonidentity rows. Even full permutation arrays have polynomial total size. For hardness of the starting language one may also reduce ordinary three-literal SAT by replacing each clause (a or b or c) by NAE(a,b,d) and NAE(not d,c,f), with one fresh d per clause and common f. Global complementation allows f=false; the first two choices a=b=false force d=true and hence c=true. Every other satisfying case extends. The general source classification is Thomas J. Schaefer, The Complexity of Satisfiability Problems, STOC 1978, https://doi.org/10.1145/800133.804350.

Suppose the two profile tests differ under a return k and some distinct marks r,s. Necessarily identity joins and each listed row separates. On the k-cycle containing r,s, partition the source positions of its successor arcs into the half-open intervals [r,s) and [s,r). If after exchanging successors at u,v the marks separate, at least one departure arc in each interval was changed: an unchanged interval would give an intact path between marks. Thus swap endpoints lie on the root cycle, on opposite sides. This induces consistent literal bits along every variable path. A clause row also has to change a departure on each side, making its literals not all equal. This proof permits arbitrary chosen marks coinciding with constrained symbols.

Conversely take any satisfying truth assignment. Give each occurrence its literal bit and extend the swap paths by alternating bits. Build a single return cycle in the order fresh mark r, all side-zero names, fresh mark s, all side-one names. Each swap separates the two marks. For a clause, its three cuts meet both sides. The relative order of its two same-side occurrences can be chosen so that the first-return permutation of k on a,b,c equals (a b c) inverse. Such comparisons are pairwise disjoint across clauses, so choose the orders simultaneously. Now the first return of k(a b c) fixes each cut, producing three separate cycles from the three intervening segments. Since each of [r,s) and [s,r) contains a cut, the marks occupy different segments. Thus every row separates and the comparison distinguishes. In particular hardness persists when the sought cap is constrained to a single cycle in this mixed-row version.

## Only three-cycle entries

Replace every swap (u v) by both cyclic rows (u v z) and (u z v) using one common new symbol z. Keep the clause rows. If both orientations separate the marks, the three cut symbols must include one on each marked interval. They cannot all lie on the root cycle: one orientation agrees with its first-return cycle on the cuts, and its square is also a three-cycle, preserving a single circle through all its original positions. Hence exactly two cut symbols lie on the root cycle, one per side. If z lay there, every occurrence lying on the cycle would have to be on the side opposite z since each occurrence is touched by at least one replaced swap. A clause could then never change a departure on the z-side. Thus z is outside the root cycle and every replaced swap still forces u,v to be on opposite sides. The original necessity proof applies. Conversely add z as a singleton orbit to the constructed cap. Each replaced swap's two original cut arcs lie on opposite sides. The induced base return on its three cut symbols is (u v)(z); multiplying by either 3-cycle gives a permutation separating u and v. Its cycles attach to the intervening segments ending at u and v, respectively, and those segments contain different marks. Both orientations therefore separate. The pure-three-cycle variant is NP-complete even when an accepting cap may have at most two cycles.

The entries in this last version move three symbols each. They are an explicitly listed exclusive choice of rows. Treating them as independently selectable gates changes the decision problem.
