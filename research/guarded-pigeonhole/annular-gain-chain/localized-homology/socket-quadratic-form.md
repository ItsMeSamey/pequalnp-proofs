# The native socket quadratic space

Work on a connected component with n three-port gates. There are 3n directed wires; loops and parallel wires are allowed. At gate i take even subsets of its six distinct endpoint sockets modulo the all-six subset. Dot product descends to a nondegenerate alternating bilinear form on this four-dimensional space A_i. Its quadratic refinement is half the Hamming weight plus the number of incoming endpoints, modulo two. Complementation changes the two terms by the same parity. A nonzero class has a unique weight-two representative; it is singular precisely when it has one incoming and one outgoing endpoint. The nine singular nonzero points split into three disjoint sets of three, each the nonzero part of a maximal singular plane. They correspond to the three cyclic in-to-out matchings. Exactly two of these planes are allowed by a native switch.

Let D from the direct sum of A_i send endpoint bits to their xor on each wire modulo the ordinary binary cut space of the underlying gate graph. Changing all endpoints at one gate adds its vertex cut (and adds zero on its loops). The image on a chosen product of local planes is the span of the incidence columns of the graph whose vertices are wires, with an edge joining every matched pair of wire endpoints, modulo the gate cuts. For any chosen mask its incidence span already contains those gate cuts, obtained by summing all three matching edges at each gate. Thus the physical marks r,s are joined exactly when the image contains the class of the binary word e_r+e_s: binary incidence span tests component membership. Solving local vector lifts produces a compatible mask; direct permutation traversal verifies it.

The kernel L consists exactly of assignments from the binary cycle space of the gate graph duplicated on the two ends of each wire, modulo the global all-ones cycle. Indeed if endpoint xor is a cut, add whole gates to clear it and then the endpoint labels are duplicated, with even incidence at each gate. Two duplicated lifts differing by whole gates differ by a global constant by connectedness. Hence dim L=(3n−n+1)−1=2n. Dot product vanishes on this space and its quadratic form vanishes since the sum of endpoint half-weights and the total selected incoming sockets both equal the number of selected wires. This proves maximal total singularity. The image has dimension 2n and is the whole even wire-word space modulo cuts; all gate cuts are even. The original target coset of L meets a product of two allowable planes per site precisely when the answer is YES.

For a single gate i, put c equal to the number of components after deleting i, including isolated neighbor vertices, and l equal to the number of wire loops at i. If a duplicated cycle labels zero in every other local quotient, all wires incident to each other gate have equal bits. There is therefore one free bit for every component of G−i and one for each loop at i. Each component contributes evenly many edges to i, by even gate degrees, so the i parity equation is automatic. Modulo global complementation this shows dim(L intersect A_i)=c+l−1. In a singleton component the three loops supply all the bits. In particular every vertex of a loopless 2-connected gate graph has local image rank four.

The full unconstrained-support local match predicate on literal sockets is not an even delta-matroid. Take X={in0,out0} and Y={in1,out2} with local matchings ID and rotation +1. At in1 in the symmetric difference, the possible exchange partners in0, out0, out2 give respectively a forbidden directed pair, a pair of input sockets, or a four-set whose complement is the forbidden pair {in2,out1}. Thus symmetric exchange fails. Perfect-matching gadgets with boundary vertices deleted according to the literal socket pattern cannot realize this support: two matchings for boundary sets X,Y have alternating path and cycle symmetric difference; the path at the nominated socket yields a valid symmetric exchange. Internal auxiliary vertices and any fixed twist of the six boundary bits preserve the obstruction. This tests literal boundary signatures and leaves global target-specific reductions open.
