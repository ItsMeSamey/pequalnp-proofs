# A short-cycle certificate on orientable native charts of arbitrary genus

Input is a supplied connected checkerboard native chart on a closed orientable surface Σ, with independent full three-arm stars, correctly oriented for the allowed pairings. Black disks, selected stars, two black roots, and two opposite white germs use the [state-neighborhood construction](native-plane.md). The certificate requires every simple two-arm digon and every simple four-cycle of the black incidence multigraph to have zero class in H1(Σ;F2), **before adding any auxiliary band or handle**. Equivalently every such simple cycle separates Σ. No restriction is placed on the genus, on the homology of longer cycles, or on the genus of neighborhoods of arbitrary states.

Take a fixed seam joining the two white germs and assign its crossing parity to each arm. The two marked arcs are joinable if and only if some selected balanced full-star component connects the black roots, where balanced means all incidence cycles have even seam parity. This is an existential equivalence; individual untrimmed states need not satisfy the plane mask-by-mask criterion.

**Trimming a positive state.** In a joining state the black roots lie in the same selected component and the opposite germs in the same complementary component, since their marked points lie on one boundary circle. Among this state's stars take a shortest path in the full intersection graph between the two singleton formal root stars. Keep only its full original stars P. Nonconsecutive stars along P are disjoint. Deleting the others preserves black connectivity and cannot disconnect the germs: use nested thin neighborhoods with the same marked conventions. Equal root disks need no stars and are handled directly.

Every cycle of the incidence graph of P is in the binary span of original digons and four-cycles. First retain one representative of each parallel arm; reinstating another arm adds a digon generator. Add the remaining simple stars in path order. Each new star meets the previous union only at disks in its intersection with its immediate predecessor. A single shared disk adds no cycle. With q shared disks, there are q−1 new generators: fix one shared disk and use the length-two routes through the previous and new stars to each other shared disk. These four-cycles are independent modulo the old cycle space by their new arms and account for the full increase of q−1. Consequently the entire H1(P) has zero image in the original Σ. The same applies to a shortest chain taken within an arbitrary balanced solution.

Each boundary circle of the regular neighborhood of P maps to zero in H1(Σ;F2) since it retracts into P (and an isolated black disk has contractible boundary). Such an embedded circle separates the orientable surface. The adjacency graph of complementary and neighborhood regions across these disjoint circles is a tree: each separating circle splits its containing region in two. Marked boundary points therefore lie on one circle exactly when both incident region components agree. In the positive direction the trimmed mask still joins. Also every simple cycle of P separates Σ, and intersects the seam oddly exactly when it separates the two germs. Their complementary connectivity thus makes P balanced.

**Sufficiency.** Given a balanced root connection, trim it to a shortest full-star chain P as above. The black roots remain connected. If the germs were in distinct complement components, the boundary of the closure of the component containing one germ, counted as a mod-two edge set in P via its facial walks, would have odd intersection with the seam. This even-degree edge set decomposes into simple cycles (digons included), contradicting balance. Equivalently one can retract the boundary circles of a thin neighborhood to the incidence graph and use the same mod-two boundary argument. All individual boundary circles separate by the short-cycle certificate, so the region-tree test gives the required common marked circle.

**Decision.** Run the [annular gain sweep](README.md) on this supplied surface. Remove stars containing an odd-gain digon; collapse equal-gain parallel copies while retaining their embedded representative. Every remaining conflict witness is an odd-gain simple four-cycle. Null original homology implies it separates the two designated germs. Disjoint such separating curves are nested between these germs on a surface of any genus. The strips may carry handles; the rank-three profile coverage, noninterference of nonconsecutive profiles, and finite connectivity transfer depend only on separation. A piece need not be an annulus topologically. The resulting allowed connectivity graph may have cycles of nonzero original homology: extract a shortest chordless witness before returning the physical mask. Profile number and number of separator symbols stay polynomial uniformly in genus.

The short-cycle certificate can be checked in the original cellular incidence embedding. Enumerate parallel arc pairs and the four-edge cycles, using actual choices of arms, then test separation by deleting just the corresponding adjacencies in the full cellular dual. Other parallel dual adjacencies must remain. If the chart has already discarded stars or arms, carry out each geometric test against the original embedding/triangulation, since the surviving graph need not be cellular.

**Weaker root-local certificate.** In the full star-intersection graph including singleton formal terminals, retain just the vertices belonging to blocks of the block-cut-tree corridor between the roots (include the appropriate terminal blocks or cutvertices). A simple root path cannot enter an off-corridor branch, and every positive state's shortest path lies on the corridor. Thus it suffices to require the original-homology nullity test only on the retained stars and their mutual cycles, allowing unrestricted exceptions elsewhere. If the roots are disconnected, return NO. The same argument can use the chart with black and white roles interchanged. A supplied bounded set of exception switches may also be branched on if the induced native charts after each consistent smoothing pass the certificate; simply dropping a switch fixed on does not prove such a reduction.
