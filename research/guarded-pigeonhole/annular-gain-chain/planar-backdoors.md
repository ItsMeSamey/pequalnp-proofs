# Recognizing native planar backdoors

The input is a closed switch network with independently controlled gates. The two gate states are the complementary adjacent-port matchings in its prescribed six-cycle order. A strong gate backdoor of size at most k is a set S such that every setting of S leaves a port-order-planar network after smoothing those gates. A weak planarizing choice requires one such setting. It is useful to distinguish either object from an adaptive binary decision tree of depth at most k: at each internal node choose a gate and recurse on both of its settings; every leaf must be planar. Fixed wires and any passive marked points are transported through every smoothing.

**Fixed-parameter recognition.** Each of these three recognition problems is FPT parameterized by k. The argument applies on arbitrary native hosts and does not require a prior genus or treewidth bound.

Use the degree-twelve wheel expansion of [native-plane.md](native-plane.md). Its rim alternates six real port vertices with six gap vertices. Each real vertex has degree four; each gap vertex has degree three. At a selected wheel, delete its hub and one alternating triple of gaps. This leaves three two-edge paths connecting the paired adjacent real ports. The other alternating triple gives the other smoothing. Consequently the remaining wheel graph is, up to irrelevant isolated circuits and subdivisions of physical wires, the wheel expansion for the conditioned network. Its planarity is exactly admissibility of the prescribed rotations of the unfixed gates. In particular the surviving wire skeleton is still even-degree, so the no-bridge argument used to interpret the wheel remains applicable. Smoothing further gates in an admissible plane drawing preserves planarity since it joins disjoint adjacent port pairs inside their own disks.

For a prescribed deletion list D of at most 4k named vertices, planarity of the graph minus D is expressible in first-order logic with disjoint-paths predicates (FO+dp). Exclude subdivisions of both Kuratowski graphs. Containment of any fixed topological minor has a fixed formula using this logic. Relativize ordinary quantifiers to vertices outside D and append the trivial (v,v) path for every distinct v in D to every disjoint-paths predicate. This forces its routed paths to avoid D. To see the topological-minor formula directly, canonically subdivide each edge of the host once if desired, or use the internally vertex-disjoint variant of the predicate with shared branch endpoints. The ordinary vertex-disjoint and internally vertex-disjoint variants have the same expressive power. The relativization applies to the ordinary vertex-disjoint formula, after translating variants.

In the original expansion a hub is recognizable by degree twelve. Its six adjacent degree-three vertices are its gaps. Two distinct such gaps are consecutive if they share a real port neighbour, thereby defining a six-cycle. First-order conditions at the named hub specify a choice of one of its two alternating triples and its complementary triple. This definition uses the full original host regardless of D. There is no need to encode the cyclic orientation: for the strong condition both triples are tested, and for the weak condition their union of choices is tested.

For a strong set, existentially choose at most k distinct hubs and their alternating triples, then conjoin the at most 2^k planarity sentences for those t choices. For a weak set take a disjunction instead. For adaptive depth use the recursive formula: accept if the present deletion list gives a planar graph, or if depth remains and some unused hub has both recursively acceptable extensions of the deletion list. At each descent one appends that hub and three gaps. The formula size is a computable function of k. The original expansion has maximum degree twelve and therefore excludes the complete graph on fourteen vertices as a topological minor, irrespective of its ordinary minor structure. Apply [Schirrmacher, Siebertz, Stamoulis, Thilikos and Vigny, FO+dp model checking on topological-minor-free classes](https://doi.org/10.1145/3661814.3662089). Witness hubs can be obtained by testing candidates at successively fixed quantifiers, with polynomial overhead per gate choice. Fixed candidates can be named using unary marks, or by attaching bounded distinct pendant tags; the latter yield another bounded-degree class depending only on k. If tagging degree changes are used the original hub/port definitions are evaluated on the interpreted original graph. Pendant tags cannot participate in a path between original vertices except at its ends. Only the strong or adaptive certificate supplies exhaustive branches for the unrestricted rooted question. Apply the plane solver at the at most 2^k leaves and check any resulting full physical mask. A weak certificate by itself gives a safe positive search on its particular planar branches.

An elementary implementation can instead enumerate all subsets of at most k gates and their settings, testing each conditioned graph for planarity. This gives n^{k+O(1)}2^k for the static variants without invoking the parameterized model checker. For metadata on a fixed gate, each marked wire arriving there follows the prescribed outgoing connection until the next surviving gate, or lies on a fully fixed closed circuit. If both roots belong to the latter case compare their circuit identities; if only one does they cannot join. Otherwise pass the two surviving incoming port occurrences to the native solver and lift its returned mask.

[planar_backdoors.py](planar_backdoors.py) implements the elementary static searches and root lifting. Its deterministic checks compared 180 root queries and all 744 full-mask refinements of the selected strong sets against direct tracing. A strict example has socket permutation [7,6,5,11,8,10,2,0,3,4,9,1]. Pinning gate 0 to 1 is weakly planarizing. Exhaustion shows there is no strong singleton; gates 0 and 1 form a strong pair. Thus the two recognition objectives already differ on four native gates.

The permutation [4,6,8,2,11,10,5,3,1,9,7,0] separates adaptive and static parameters. First branch on gate 0. If its value is 0 branch on gate 2; otherwise branch on gate 1. All four leaves are planar. Exhaustive static testing finds minimum strong size three, which also rules out adaptive depth one.
