# Balanced full-star connections in the plane

## Gain-chain criterion

An incidence of a disk v with a selectable star h has a bit g(h,v). A selected component is balanced if there are phases a(h),p(v) satisfying a(h) XOR p(v)=g(h,v) on every incidence. Selecting a star retains all its incidences. We ask whether a balanced component can connect two root disks s,t, whose relative phase is free. In the plane assume the gains come from mod-two crossings of a seam between two face points. Equivalently a simple incidence cycle has odd gain exactly when it separates those face points.

Delete a star with conflicting parallel incidences at one disk; it cannot participate in a balanced solution. Parallel incidences with equal gains can be represented by any one of their embedded arcs: deleting the others preserves the abstract constraints and the separation property on retained cycles. The two face points remain fixed even when their containing faces merge. Every surviving star has at most three distinct disk neighbors.

Two touching stars are compatible if the differences of their gains agree at every shared disk. Otherwise they are a forbidden pair, witnessed by an odd incidence four-cycle using two distinct shared disks. Adjoin singleton formal stars at s,t and take the full star intersection graph. A balanced connection exists exactly when there is an induced path between the terminals whose consecutive stars have compatible overlaps. Necessity follows by choosing a shortest intersection path within a feasible set. Conversely assign phases in order along such a path: adjacent star phases have a unique difference, and nonconsecutive stars do not intersect. The singleton terminal convention carries no phase restriction.

Equivalently use ordinary paths on compatible adjacencies and forbid the two members of every incompatible pair from appearing anywhere on the path. Any shortest surviving path has no chord. In rank three two double overlaps cannot be consecutive on such a path, since disjointness of nonconsecutive neighbors would force four disks in the middle star. All stars of a witness trimmed this way have the property that at most two of them meet each disk.

For arbitrary gains one parameterized test uses the disk graph D on conflicting pairs of disks: uv belongs to D when input stars containing both prescribe both possible relative signs. Enumerate the cut space of D (dimension |V(D)| minus its component count) and remove all stars violating chosen differences. Remaining overlaps are compatible so ordinary connectivity suffices. A feasible component's phases extend to the full disk set, proving the converse. For all-incompatible double overlaps with distinct triple supports, the [rank-three loose-path algorithm](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/en/articles/10202) applies. Bare [skew-symmetric regular paths](https://alexander-karzanov.net/Publications/93_path_probl_skew.pdf) omit unused arms: take h={s,u,v}, k={t,u,v}, with only g(k,v)=1. The lifted incidence path via u has no conflict but selecting h,k forces their unbalanced four-cycle. Adding a third star {u,x,y} yields a path of compatible adjacencies without curing the global conflict.

## Polynomial plane algorithm

Enumerate all odd incidence four-cycles and greedily take a maximal vertex-disjoint subfamily C_1,...,C_m. Every chosen cycle is a simple Jordan curve separating the two face points. They are nested with respect to either point; order them from that point toward the other. Interiors can be found from the supplied rotation or dual flood-fill blocked at each separator. They produce m−1 annular strips and two end disks. If no cycle is selected, no forbidden pair exists and root connectivity alone suffices.

Let U_i consist of the two disk vertices of C_i. Let P_i be the set of input stars incident to at least one member of U_i. Every forbidden pair is covered by at least one P_i, meaning that both of its stars belong to the same P_i. Indeed any witnessing odd four-cycle meets a chosen C_i by maximality. If they share a disk it is used by both conflicting stars. If they share a star h, its neighbors already include both disks of C_i; the two shared disks witnessing the conflict include at least one of them since h has at most three distinct neighbors. Hence the other conflicting star also belongs to P_i.

A trimmed feasible witness therefore has profiles Q_i consisting of at most four stars of P_i, with at most two per disk in U_i. Enumerate candidate profiles with these bounds and no incompatible pair. Impose exact consistency between consecutive profiles on P_i∩P_(i+1). More distant sets P_i and P_j are disjoint: the incidence path of length two joining a disk of C_i and a disk of C_j through a common star h would meet an intermediate C_k, necessarily at h. Such h already uses two disks on C_k, leaving just one neighbor for both other boundaries, which are disjoint.

For any consistent profile sequence delete each star in P_i minus Q_i. By the coverage assertion, every two surviving intersecting stars are compatible. Consequently ordinary connectivity between the roots in the surviving incidence graph is necessary and sufficient: its shortest intersection path has no chords and phases propagate along it. The Q_i need not all appear in the final witness. They specify which boundary-adjacent stars may survive.

Do not enumerate profile sequences. Sweep across the strips. For a fixed current and next profile compute ordinary connectivity inside the closed strip after the deletions. All stars strictly inside can touch only these two separator disk sets, by planarity. A star on separator C_i can additionally belong to the profile of the preceding or following separator; its allowedness is fixed by Q_i, with overlap consistency checked when the adjacent profiles are joined. Partition connectivity at the active separator using its four incidence vertices (omit deleted stars), and keep symbols for s,t. Before processing their own locations these root symbols are isolated; when a root occurs in a piece, identify its symbol with its disk. Merge the previous connectivity partition with the new strip connectivity and restrict to the next boundary and the root symbols. The two end pieces are processed with just one profile each. Any invariant path crossing from one strip to the next passes through a retained separator vertex, so standard union of connectivity partitions is exact. Separator edges may be included in both pieces harmlessly. Recording predecessors recovers profiles; BFS in the surviving graph and chain trimming recover a balanced witness.

There are O(n^4) boundary profiles and a constant number of partitions of at most six symbols. Even the direct pair-of-profiles sweep is polynomial. The argument applies to the gain subproblem with a planar incidence representation and a specified seam between two germs. check.py independently replays the abstract chain equivalence on 1,000 small random gain instances and two obstruction controls.

sweep.py implements the partition transfer on a supplied ordered separating family. Its piece assignment can use graph adjacency alone after the ordering is known: every component after deleting all separator vertices attaches to at most two consecutive separators; a component touching just one may be processed on either side of it. Separator-to-separator edges are assigned to the first piece containing both endpoints. check_sweep.py agrees with the independent full induced-path oracle on 65 random strip-structured tests and 30 nested gate controls (including 26 negative controls). It does not reconstruct an embedding from abstract input.

plane.prepare provides the end-to-end preprocessing for a connected simple incidence graph with a supplied NetworkX rotation and two numbered faces. It computes a dual seam, its crossed primal edges, a maximal disjoint family of odd four-cycles, and their order by the sizes of the face sets on the first-germ side. When multiple primal edges separate the same pair of faces, blocking a cycle disconnects that dual adjacency only if it blocks every such edge. check_plane.py passes the resulting profiles to ordered_sweep and compares to exhaustive full-star selection: 59 YES and nine NO, with up to three selected nested separators. Face numbering can be obtained from extract_faces.
