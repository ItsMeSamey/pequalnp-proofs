# Joining two marked arcs on a fixed orientable surface

Use a compatible cellular genus-g chart of an independently switched network. Work on the common connected component of the two distinct marks (different components immediately reject). In its radial graph the black and white disk vertices have their respective colors. A switch center may use either color, one per setting. Put private degree-one black and white terminal leaves at the disk germs of each root, drawing the spokes in their sectors. Fix short transversal connectors across each mark joining its paired leaves. The extension from the disk to its mark is drawn in its own region for either setting.

**Criterion.** Seek two binary edge chains, one of each color, with odd boundary equal to the two leaf terminals of that color and using no common switch center. Require their sum, closed by the two fixed transversals, to be nullhomologous in the original surface over F2. Detached components are permitted. Necessity follows by pushing a segment of the common state circle slightly to either side: the two resulting paths and the narrow transverse connectors bound a strip. Retract the paths within their two regions onto their skeletons relative to the root extensions and reduce modulo two. Conversely complete every used center to its prescribed state and set unused centers arbitrarily. In these regions both chains are representable away from all boundary circles. Their closed union meets the state boundaries only in the two transversal root crossings. By mod-two intersection each individual circle must have an even number of crossings, so both roots lie on the same circle. This argument retains all detached components.

**Forest bound.** Minimize total edge support. The entire binary cycle space of its support injects under the map to surface homology: otherwise subtract a nonempty nullhomologous even edge set, strictly reducing the supports while preserving vertex boundaries and the color exclusivity. Thus at most 2g nontree edges suffice to cut the support into a forest. Terminal spokes are bridges. Euler trails and tours orient this support with conserved unit flows except at the four leaves. Guess at most 2g nontree edges and their orientations. For each guessed edge retain the embedded edge but disable it, and put a private new degree-one terminal at each end on short spokes of the same color, with opposite signed demands consistent with the guessed traversal. Join their tips by a reserved topological connector tracing the removed edge. Their two spokes and the connector restore that edge up to homotopy. In particular both endpoints are reserved for the right color also on returned solutions. The embedding remains cellular; any extra cellulation arcs must be disabled. There are at most 4+4g terminal faces.

**Algorithm.** Use the free group on generators b,w, orient the input graph arbitrarily, and encode directed unit flow on a color edge by its generator or inverse. At monochrome internal vertices conservation gives the identity in cyclic edge order. At each degree-one demand leaf set the corresponding signed generator. A correct branch contains a delta-join supported on a forest, hence elementary with arc magnitude at most one.

Apply problem (81), Theorem 9 of [Schrijver](https://homepages.cwi.nl/~lex/files/dppg.pdf) with m=1. For fixed orientable surface and number of terminal faces R this lists representatives covering every such elementary R-homology class in polynomial time. Map both generators to 1 in F2. Add the two root transversals and all reserved connectors to the projected chain of a representative. Discard a nonzero total class, tested by cellular boundary elimination after subdivision. R-homologous representatives differ after projection by the boundary of the chain of projected face potentials, hence this test is invariant.

In each surviving class apply the R-cohomology algorithm on the extended dual as in the proofs of Theorems 10 and 11. On ordinary dual arcs permit identity and either signed unit of the single edge's generator, or identity alone on disabled edges. On other vertex-chord arcs permit the union of the two cyclic subgroups, exactly the off-edge constraint in (85). The signed single-generator unit interval is closed, as the intersection of the symmetric unit set (83) with that cyclic subgroup. Membership is elementary. A monochrome forest realization passes every constraint. Conversely at an internal vertex with both colors choose consecutive nonidentity incidences of distinct colors in the cyclic order. Their intervening interval has a mixed product, violating the chord constraint (or conservation if its complement has size at most one). Thus the output realization is monochrome at each center. Read its actual projected chains, retaining all detached cycles. Glue the guessed edges, remove their private stubs, and complete to a physical witness by the criterion above. With n^{O(g+1)} guesses and polynomial class enumeration for fixed g this gives deterministic n^{f(g)} decision and witness. The separate compatible rotation recognition procedure may supply the chart for fixed g. No uniform polynomial for growing genus or solution count follows.
