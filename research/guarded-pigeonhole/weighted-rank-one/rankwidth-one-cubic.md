# Cubic PC on undirected graphs of rank-width one

Consider Boolean GF(2) guards x_i(b_i+Σ_j A_ij x_j)=0 on a simple undirected graph, arbitrary unary pins, and one arbitrary dense parity marker λ·x=v. Given a binary vertex decomposition of cut rank at most one, every negative instance has an ordinary PC refutation of degree at most three. The weighted rooted-edge formulation is the case b=0 with its two endpoint pins. This proof covers nonbipartite graphs and arbitrary input table contents. It does not need finite enumeration of reachable tables.

Solve connected components separately, restricting and suppressing nodes in the supplied decomposition: taking induced vertex subsets cannot increase a cut rank. Component fibers form a direct product. A prohibited global weight bit thus comes either from an empty component fiber or from every component weight being fixed; in the latter case add the component equations to the global weight marker. Singleton components are immediate. In a connected nonsingleton graph every nonempty proper cut in the decomposition has rank exactly one.

On a side U of a tree edge, retain the parity of its internally active vertices (those having an outside neighbor) and the parity of the outside active vertices (those having an inside neighbor). Every nonzero cross-row is the same, so these two bits determine all crossing contributions. For each two-bit state let its local fiber consist of assignments to U satisfying its own pins and guards with the prescribed external contribution and with the specified internal parity. Record the set of possible values of the partial weight p=Σ_(i∈U) λ_i x_i in this fiber. For the state's indicator I, derive I=0 if the fiber is empty, and I(p+p₀)=0 if only weight p₀ is possible. A fiber with both weights needs no derived equation. These are consequences of the original unmarked guard and pin equations, proved inductively at degree three.

A bit value i has indicator I_i(a)=1+a+i. At a leaf the two state bits are the leaf variable x and its neighbor parity e. An empty atom violates either a pin or the guard x(e+b)=0: its indicator is a pin multiple or the guard itself. For a legal atom the recorded weight is λ_i x, fixed by x's state, so its weighted record is a Boolean identity. These checks take degree at most three. Normalizing any Boolean identity of raw degree d via x_j²=x_j uses the Boolean axioms at degree at most d.

At a three-arm node with blocks A,B,C, let x,y,z denote the active parities of those arms. For each pair of arms with any crossing edge, its whole edge set is the complete bipartite product of their two active sets: nonzero rows and columns in the respective arm cuts are identical. Each arm links to at least one other, so this graph on three arms is a two-edge star or a triangle. Take C as exterior. The child interfaces are their active parity and the sum of active parities of adjacent arms. The parent interface is its interior active sum toward C and the exterior active parity z. A compatible triple of interface states glues child assignments independently: their active parities fix the whole contribution to the sibling and outside arms. Conversely every parent witness supplies such a triple of states.

When the three-arm interaction is a star, condition on the active bit g of the center. If C is the center, the two child coordinates are (g,a) and (g,b) with a=x,b=y, and the parent coordinates are (g,h) with h=a+b. If A is the center, take g=x, a=y+z, b=y and h=z; interchange A,B for the remaining star. In a triangle take **g=x+y+z**, a=x, b=y and h=x+y. The child coordinates before reparameterization are (a,a+g) and (b,b+g), and the parent coordinates are (h,h+g). In each case (g,a),(g,b),(g,h) are invertible linear reparameterizations of the corresponding two boundary bits. Atom indicators computed in two such bases describe the same Boolean function, so their difference has zero squarefree reduction. Conversions cost degree two, or degree three when multiplied by a partial weight.

**Unary convolution lemma.** Fix g. The two children now have independent unary tables F(a),G(b), whose entries are subsets of the two possible child weights p,q. For an empty F(i) use the input axiom I_i(a)=0; for a singleton F(i)={v} use I_i(a)(p+v)=0. Similarly use b,q for G. These are initially formal, ungated axioms; they will be gated by the fixed g indicator. The parent index is h=a+b and its weight is p+q. Fix h₀. At most two pairs of viable indices match h₀. Parent records follow at degree two:

1. If an entire table is empty, adding its two empty atom axioms gives 1=0. If both tables are nonempty but there are no matching pairs, each domain is a singleton, and the two viable bits have sum different from h₀. Their linear pins imply the forbidden atom I_(h₀)(h)=0.
2. If exactly one pair matches, then at least one child's entire domain is a singleton, because otherwise both binary domains would be full. Say the a domain consists of i, and j=i+h₀. The other domain can have either one or two entries. If the parent weight is fixed, then F(i)={p₀} and G(j)={q₀}; the two choices of child weights are independent, so neither can be free. The pin a=i converts I_i(a)(p+p₀)=0 to p=p₀ in degree two. Multiply this affine equation by I_(h₀)(h). The other axiom supplies I_j(b)(q+q₀)=0, and I_j(b)+I_(h₀)(h)=a+i; add the pin multiple (a+i)(q+q₀) to replace its indicator by I_(h₀)(h). Adding gives the desired parent record I_(h₀)(h)(p+q+p₀+q₀)=0. The case when b's domain is a singleton is symmetric. If the parent weight is free, no record is required.
3. If both pairs match and the parent weight is fixed, both child weights are fixed individually at both input indices by independence. Adding a child's two weighted records gives its affine equation p=p₀+A a, and likewise q=q₀+B b. Constancy along b=a+h₀ implies A=B. Add the affine equations and multiply by I_(h₀)(h) to give the fixed parent record, using I_(h₀)(h)(h+h₀)=0 by Booleanity. Again a free parent weight needs no record.

Gate each calculation by I_(g₀)(g), adding at most one degree. Its gated inputs are the actual child records already derived, up to the change-of-basis Boolean identities above. Gated Boolean identities follow by normalizing at degree three. Thus the combined parent records have degree at most three; the bound does not accumulate with tree height because an inference uses the degree of its available premises, not the degrees previously used to derive them. An affine substitution for a formal coordinate preserves the ordinary PC degree bound: multiplying a line by an affine form is simulated by single-variable multiplications and addition, and Booleanity of an affine form is a linear combination of the original Boolean axioms. In particular the concrete weights may be linearly correlated with boundary parities without invalidating any derivation.

At the final split there are two nonempty arms and no exterior. Connectivity gives one cross product. Both sides have the same two-bit boundary (reversing the order as needed), and at each atom their witnesses combine independently. If the root fiber is empty, sum empty-child atom certificates. If its weight is fixed, on each nonempty atom sum the two fixed-child weighted records; multiply any empty atom by the global affine weight residual. Summing over the four atoms gives the root affine implication. This last step also costs at most three. Adding the contradictory marker completes the refutation.
