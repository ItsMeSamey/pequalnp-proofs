# Quartic ordinary PC for odd total weight at a rooted edge

Let G be a finite simple bipartite graph, st an edge, with pins x_s=x_t=1, native even-induced-degree guards, and marker Σ_v x_v=1. Every negative instance has an ordinary GF(2) PC refutation of degree at most four. For target zero, any simple shortest s–t path after deleting st gives an even induced cycle; if no such path exists the edge is a bridge and summing guards across its cut contradicts the pins in degree two.

Put H=G−st. If no s–t path exists use the same bridge proof. Otherwise, on a negative instance every s–t path dominates H: a vertex outside its closed neighborhood can be appended as an isolated spectator to a shortest s–t path avoiding that closed neighborhood, which closes via st to an induced even cycle. Consequently H is connected. Use the rooted-layer geometry from the guard package plucker-lines/rooted-cycle/README.md. Briefly, for odd s–t distance ℓ≥3 let C_i have distances (i,ℓ−i) and E_i (i+1,ℓ−i+1) from s,t respectively. Every vertex is in one of these sets since it is on, or adjacent to, a shortest path. Edges are consecutive within a C or E track, or between C_i and E_i, and every such latter bipartite pair is complete: choose a shortest path through the specified C_i vertex and use that E_i must be dominated by it. For R=C or E, and p∈R_(i−1), q∈R_(i+1), the two neighborhoods within R_i are disjoint or cover R_i. A common neighbor v gives an s–t path through p,v,q; for C use geodesic ends, for E use the complete vertical links to C_(i−1),C_(i+1) before and after this segment. Any other vertex in R_i could be dominated on this path only by p or q, proving cover. The following argument lifts these graph facts to ordinary PC on the selected vertex variables.

On either track let r denote the selected count of R_i modulo two, w the selected count of the other track at i. For v∈R_i write a_v,b_v for the sums of selected previous- and next-track neighbors. For an internal index the guard reads x_v(a_v+b_v+w)=0, including when sets are empty. Define D_i=Σ_(uv) x_u x_v and T_i=Σ_(uv) x_u x_v b_u a_v over track edges u∈R_i,v∈R_(i+1). D_i is quadratic and T_i quartic. Summing the internal guards yields D_(i−1)+D_i=rw.

For each pair p,q as above let A_pq be the selected sum of common middle neighbors. If disjoint this is zero; if they cover then A_pq=r+b_p+a_q identically. Thus A_pq(1+r+b_p+a_q)=0 modulo Boolean squares. Multiply by x_p x_q and sum all pairs. This is the zero sum over horizontal two-edge paths p,v,q of x_p x_v x_q(1+r+b_p+a_q).

Its (1+r) term equals (1+r)(1+w)D_(i−1): multiply each middle guard by a_v to replace x_v a_v b_v by (1+w)x_v a_v, using a_v²=a_v. The b_p term equals T_(i−1)+wD_(i−1), by multiplying the middle guard by x_p b_p, replacing b_v by a_v+w under x_p x_v b_p, and summing (Σ_(v~p)x_v)²=b_p. Symmetrically the a_q term is T_i+wD_i. Substitute D_(i−1)+D_i=rw and w²=w to get

    T_(i−1)+T_i = (1+r)(1+w)D_(i−1)+rw.

Every step consists of additions, multiples of a guard by at most two affine factors, and Boolean reductions within degree four. In particular no unguarded use of a selected vertex's local parity is needed.

Put c_i=Σ_(C_i)x_v and e_i=Σ_(E_i)x_v. Every cut through both tracks between two consecutive levels separates s and t, and contains the omitted original edge st when computed in G. Its guard sum and the pins give D_i^C+D_i^E=1. Sum the two track identities, substituting this cut equation multiplied by (1+c_i)(1+e_i), and use D_(i−1)^C+D_i^C=c_i e_i. For U_i=T_i^C+T_i^E+D_i^C the result is

    U_(i−1)+U_i = 1+c_i+e_i.

At the left end C_0={s}, each C₁ vertex sees s, and hence T₀^C=D₀^C by x_s=1 and squaring the sum of its selected C₁ neighbors. An E₀ vertex u has x_u(b_u+1)=0 because it sees s. Thus T₀^E=D₀^E by multiplying this guard by x_v a_v along each edge and squaring the incoming sums, and D₀^E=e₀ by summing these end guards. This gives U₀=e₀. Reversal yields U_(ℓ−1)=e_ℓ. All endpoint simplifications are within degree four, as is substitution of a pinned variable in a quartic line by appropriate multiples of its pin. The ℓ−1 internal identities have even total constant, so their sum, together with the endpoints C₀={s}, C_ℓ={t}, derives Σ_v x_v=0. Adding the odd marker concludes. The proof does not apply to mixed weights merely by ignoring zero-weight vertices: those vertices need not be dominated.
