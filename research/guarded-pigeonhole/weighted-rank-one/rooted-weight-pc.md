# A weighted rooted edge and ordinary GF(2) polynomial calculus

## Degree-preserving reformulation

Let a bipartite guard graph have left/right states x,y and adjacency B. Assume the first two affine markers require α·x=β·y=1; the third is λ_L·x+λ_R·y=z. Construct a larger bipartite graph with adjacency

    [ 1    βᵀ        ]
    [ α    B+αβᵀ    ],

where the new row s and column t are both pinned to one. Retain the weight marker with zero added weights. With the pins, the s and t guards give β·y=1 and α·x=1. An old left guard differs from its enlarged counterpart by α_i x_i(t+β·y), derivably zero at degree two, and an old right guard similarly differs by β_j y_j(s+α·x). The original guards and markers therefore have degree-two derivations on the enlarged system; append any original proof directly. Conversely, substitute s=t=1 throughout an enlarged proof. Both new guards are original marker equations; each changed old guard follows from the corresponding original guard and a variable multiple of its opposite private marker. This gives an original proof of degree at most the maximum of two and the enlarged degree. Thus the two encodings transfer ordinary PC degree in both directions, including the unweighted or arbitrary-weight choices.

## A rank-width bound for arbitrary weights

Consider an arbitrary simple undirected graph with x_i(b_i+Σ_j A_ij x_j)=0 at each vertex, arbitrary unary pins, and one weighted parity λ·x=z. A rooted edge is covered by b=0 and two endpoint pins. In a binary decomposition tree each vertex is a leaf. Assume every tree edge gives bipartition U,V with rank_F2 A[U,V]≤k. Every unsatisfiable instance then has an ordinary GF(2) PC refutation of degree at most max(2,3k+1). In particular degree four suffices for every weighted rooted-edge instance on a supplied rank-width-one decomposition. The bound depends on the graph cut ranks and not on the arity of individual guard polynomials.

For a node U define two lists of independent linear forms. On x_U retain a basis of the row space of A[V,U]; on x_V retain a basis of the row space of A[U,V]. They determine, respectively, every external vertex's incoming contribution from U and every vertex in U's incoming contribution from V. Call their union B_U (the supports on the two sides are disjoint). Given a fixed state a of these forms, its local fiber consists of assignments on U realizing the first list, satisfying U's own pins, and satisfying all U guards with external contributions prescribed by the second list. Let W_U=Σ_(i∈U) λ_i x_i. The table records which bits can be attained by W_U in this fiber.

For an independent list of forms with assigned values, its atom is the product of their matching bit indicators 1+f+a. Use I_a for the atom on B_U. Prove, from the unmarked guard and pin axioms, the following records: an empty fiber gives I_a=0, and a fiber of weight fixed to w gives I_a(W_U+w)=0. A fiber realizing both bits needs no equation. At a leaf use the joint independent span of its boundary forms and its own bit x_i. Its dimension is at most two: the entire outside linear neighbor sum is at most one form. On each joint atom satisfying the wrong leaf guard, both linear factors of the guard are fixed to one; choose an atom basis containing those factors if independent, remove redundant or constant factors, and multiply the guard by the other atom indicators. Normalizing Boolean powers gives the atom itself. A violated pin is handled the same way. For legal atoms the weight is fixed by x_i, so the corresponding weighted record is a Boolean identity. Sum atoms to obtain the leaf records. For k≥1 this costs at most degree three; k=0 has only unary constraints, and all leaf and merge records have degree at most two. Affine factors such as a diagonal-adjusted mask equal to an affine constant cause no problem.

At a merge write U=U₁∪U₂ and V its complement. The span J of all three boundary lists B_U,B_U₁,B_U₂ has dimension h≤3k. Indeed within block U₁ the restrictions of every list lie in the row space of A[outside U₁,U₁], of rank ≤k. The same holds for U₂. Within V all restrictions lie in the row space of A[U,V], also of rank ≤k. These supports are disjoint. Every assignment to a basis of J is a genuine linear state, simultaneously determining the three lists.

For each such joint state the two child fibers are independently compatible. Given arbitrary child witnesses and any realizing exterior assignment, replacing the child bits by the witnesses leaves all external contributions unchanged: their outbound basis values determine their inputs to the sibling and outside blocks. This realizes the parent state and satisfies its guards. Conversely any parent witness, with some realizing exterior, determines a joint state and child witnesses. Hence the parent possibilities are the union over compatible joint states of the sums of the two sets of child weights.

Write J_b for the joint atom. If one child fiber is empty, multiply its zero-atom record by the indicators from extending that child's independent boundary basis to a basis of J. The resulting polynomial is J_b up to Boolean identities. If both child weights are fixed, use the same extension on their weighted records and add to obtain J_b(W_U+w₁+w₂)=0. Either extension costs at most h+1 total degree. Any two products representing the same atom in different bases agree as Boolean functions; their difference is squarefree-zero and can be reduced using Boolean axioms without exceeding its original degree. The same holds after multiplying by an affine weight. These multiplications can be performed in ordinary PC by a variable multiplication and linear addition for each affine factor.

If a parent fiber is empty, every joint extension has an empty child; summing the joint zero-atom records gives I_a=0. If its weight is fixed, every nonempty joint extension has both child weights fixed and the same required sum. At empty extensions multiply J_b=0 by W_U+w. Summing the weighted records gives I_a(W_U+w)=0. This propagation bound does not accumulate along the tree: each step multiplies a previously derived low-degree record, not its proof. At the root the boundary atom is one. The unmarked system then derives 1 or the fixed root weight that contradicts the marker. Enumerating O(2^(3k)) joint states per node builds the table and recovers witnesses by backtracking. The degree proof allows expanded polynomials on many coordinates and does not assert a size bound independent of their expansion.

## Degree three for a unique cycle through the pinned edge

Suppose st lies on exactly one simple cycle K; the rest of the graph can have arbitrary rank-width. Every vertex a of K is forced to one in degree at most three. For a internal to the alternative s–t route, the s component in G−st−a has crossing edges st and edges to a alone. Sum the original vertex guards on it: internal selected-edge monomials cancel and x_s x_t+x_a L=0 for an affine neighbor sum L. The pins give x_a L=1. Multiplication by x_a and Boolean reduction give x_a=1. The endpoints were already pinned. If st is on no cycle the analogous bridge cut refutes at degree two for either weight target.

The cycle has no chord, and every connected component C outside K attaches to at most one K vertex; otherwise an outside path or a chord yields a second simple cycle containing st. After pinning K, each C is the unmarked symmetric system on its own adjacency with offset d_v=1 exactly when v touches K. Its affine hull is ker d: every d=0 singleton is feasible, and shortest paths between d=1 endpoints with only d=0 interior vertices are feasible. Breaking ordinary paths at the d=1 vertices shows that these path vectors, together with the singletons, generate all even-parity vectors on the d=1 coordinates and all remaining units. Summing the local guards derives d·x=0 at degree two. This equation is also C's entire contribution to the guard of its possible attachment vertex. Thus the rooted fiber is precisely the product of these component fibers with K fixed. Its affine hull is given by the derived pins and component parity equations, so any impossible weighted marker refutes by linear addition at degree at most three.
