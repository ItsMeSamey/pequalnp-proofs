# Singleton marginals and full degree-two PC export

Fix ordinary polynomial calculus over GF(2), with Boolean equations. For each derivable equation q=0 of degree at most two, take the convex hull P(q) of the Boolean vectors satisfying that individual equation. All polytopes share only the singleton coordinates. The full export is their intersection. An alternative LP sharing higher moments or demanding a common distribution defines a different set.

## One dense quadratic

Linear optimization over P(q) takes polynomial time. Fix an unconstrained maximizing cube vertex u for the given rational objective. Each flip costs the absolute value of that coefficient. If q(u)=0, u is optimal. Otherwise consider the multilinear normal form f(S)=q(u+1_S)+q(u), a degree-at-most-two polynomial in flip indicators with zero constant. For every feasible flip set S, f(S)=1. If a singleton subset has value one, keep that singleton. Otherwise all linear coefficients indexed by S vanish and some pair inside S has nonzero quadratic coefficient, so that pair has value one. Shrinking never increases cost. Testing all flips of size at most two finds an optimum or proves P(q) empty. The rational optimization/separation equivalence for well-described polytopes then gives polynomial-time separation; the facet coefficient lengths of a 0/1 polytope are polynomial by determinant bounds. This argument also works with any fixed-degree equation and a degree-sized flip search. It only treats objectives linear in the original bits.

The ordinary degree-two closure has a polynomial-size basis, computed by closing the line span under each variable times the subspace of affine lines until the span stops growing. For a fixed polynomial list of exported equations, including any specified basis, combine the individual separation oracles. Exporting all bounded-variable-support equations is also polynomial at any fixed bound w: for each scope of size at most w, use Gaussian elimination to intersect the multilinearized derivable-line space with the coordinates supported on that scope. Require a distribution on the scope supported on the simultaneous zero set of a basis of this intersection, summing its 0/1 atoms exhaustively. This last *joint* version can be stronger than independent singleton hulls. For independent hull semantics enumerate all equations in that local span instead, at cost at most 2^(1+w+binom(w,2)). Taking the image under multilinearization is legitimate since the Boolean axioms normalize every square at degree two.

## Membership hardness for full singleton export

Given a bounded-width, bounded-occurrence guarded instance of maximum hidden deficiency one and rational singleton marginals, detecting a violation of full export is NP-complete. The constructed instances are satisfiable. Thus this theorem concerns the oracle at arbitrary points, including boundary points, and says nothing by itself about deciding nonemptiness.

Start from binary linear-code nearest-word decision: given C, v, k decide whether some c in C has Hamming distance at most k from v. The NP-completeness of general decoding is due to [Berlekamp, McEliece and van Tilborg](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/aw9vs-ann16). Assume k≥1, since k=0 is decidable directly. In coordinates (s,u,w) form the binary row space

    D = {(b, c+bv, c+bv) : b in GF(2), c in C}.

Give s singleton marginal 1 and every u,w coordinate marginal δ=1/(2k+1). For a binary check h the convex hull of its even assignments is described inside the box by the requirement that the sum of singleton mismatch probabilities with each odd tuple on its support be at least one (the forbidden-set inequalities). For this standard description and the distinction between maximal violation and ordinary separation see [Puchert and Tillmann](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.03387).

If h has s-coordinate one, its rounded support tuple has exactly one 1 and is forbidden; every other forbidden tuple costs at least as much. Its mismatch cost is 2δ wt(c+v), below one exactly when wt(c+v)≤k. When h has s-coordinate zero, any nonzero h has support size d≥2, with each marginal δ. The cheapest forbidden tuple costs 1+(d−2)δ≥1. The zero check has no forbidden tuples. Thus a single query detects a violated check exactly for yes instances of decoding.

To bound arities and occurrences, generate D as the rowspace of a matrix H. Express Hx=0 using binary XOR gates, unary zero checks at the outputs, and bounded-fanout copy trees where an input is reused. Let B(x,y)=0 denote all gate equations, with y the internal wires. Each equation has at most three variables and each variable has bounded occurrence. The projection of the kernel onto x is exactly ker H. Consequently the vectors (h,0) in rowspace B are exactly those with h in D, by orthogonality. Duplicate every internal-wire column of B to give a matrix B'. Its row combinations are precisely (h,g,g) with (h,g) in rowspace B. Assign both copies of every internal bit marginal 1/2. Any check with g nonzero has two fair coordinates, so every forbidden tuple costs at least one. The internal coordinates therefore introduce no additional violations.

Finally, for each row L_j of B' put in a fresh pivot t_j and the guard t_j L_j=0. Give each coordinate of B' a trivial pivot guard with zero affine factor. With no marker, the multilinearized ordinary degree-two closure consists exactly of the span of these guard polynomials: any nontrivial combination is homogeneous quadratic, since each nonzero row has its own pivot, while Boolean axioms normalize to zero. Before normalization, no linear combination of Boolean axioms and these guards gives a nonzero affine polynomial either, since square terms have unique coefficients and squarefree guard terms cannot cancel them. Hence ordinary degree-two multiplication adds no lines. Add an isolated dummy variable m with zero guard and the marker m=1. Restricting m=1 in any degree-two derivation yields a derivation of at most that degree in the marker-free system, so no extra checks appear on this face. Every original row combination is still derivable. The pivots of all guards are distinct unit normals and the marker supplies deficiency exactly one. Row masks have size at most six and column occurrence remains bounded.

Query all t_j at one and m at one. In *each* individual hull every witnessing distribution must put these bits at one almost surely. A derived equation then restricts to a parity equation whose coefficient row belongs to rowspace B'; conversely every such parity is obtained. Thus the query is outside the full export exactly when the decoding answer is yes. This is a polynomial-time many-one reduction.

For NP membership guess a degree-two line q and a strictly separating rational inequality for P(q). Membership in the degree-two closure is linear algebra. If P(q) is empty this is testable directly. Otherwise polynomial-bit separating certificates exist for binary polytopes, including affine-hull equalities, by the usual determinant bounds. Check validity by optimizing the linear objective over P(q) with the flip algorithm and compare to the query. Membership in the intersection itself is therefore coNP-complete.
