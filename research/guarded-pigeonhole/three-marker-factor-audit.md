# Three marker completeness on cactus graphs

Use homogeneous vertex rows x_v sum_{u adjacent v}x_u=0 on a simple undirected cactus. These statements apply in particular to alternately bipartitioned cactus graphs. Additional GF(2) markers are affine parity equations. Every NO instance with at most three markers has an ordinary GF(2) PC refutation of degree at most three.

Write E_uv=x_u x_v. The native rows span cut-space relations in these edge monomials. A bridge hence gives E_uv=0. Any two edges of one cycle C give E_e=E_f: their pair intersects every graph cycle evenly, since each cactus edge is in at most one simple cycle. Equivalently the pair belongs to the binary cut space. These are linear combinations of the input rows at degree two. In a Boolean model the edges induced by the selected vertices are therefore bridges never, and each cycle either entirely occupied or entirely edge-free. Conversely these conditions make every induced vertex degree even; several occupied cycles at an articulation still contribute even total degree.

A squarefree monomial on a vertex set S of size at most three is an inclusion test for S. Classify its least containing model if one exists. An independent S is itself a model. A bridge edge makes S impossible, with the corresponding monomial derived zero. Otherwise an edge of S lies on a cycle C, forcing all of C. If there is no extra vertex outside C, its closure is C. If the third vertex v is outside C, there are three cases. If v has no neighbor on C, the closure is C∪{v}. If a bridge joins v to a vertex on C, the support is impossible. Otherwise v reaches C by an edge of another cycle D. This cycle meets C at one articulation u, and the closure is C∪D. In a cactus v cannot have two distinct neighbors on C, since a path through v plus a portion of C would form an overlapping cycle. Also, the union of cycles C and D sharing one vertex is an induced union without extra edges. Thus each claimed closure is itself a feasible model.

Show at degree three that monomials with identical closure have identical values. For C alone replace a selected edge of C by one incident to the extra vertex when present, then reduce its repeated Boolean factor. For C∪{v} replace the C edge by a chosen representative. For the impossible bridge case replace the C edge by one incident to the bridge endpoint, then multiply the bridge equation by the remaining bit. For C∪D, replace the chosen C edge by an edge xu incident to the articulation, leaving a triple xuv with uv an edge on D. One may now alter the C edge at u by multiplying an edge equality by v, or the D edge at u by multiplying its edge equality by x. This yields a common canonical representative independent of which of the two cycles was originally activated. All raw products fit degree three, including Boolean normalization.

The surviving representatives are linearly independent as functions on the native models. Each is the inclusion indicator of its least containing model, and evaluating these indicators on the distinct least models gives a unit triangular matrix when ordered by set inclusion. Consequently every polynomial of degree at most three vanishing on the native model set reduces to zero at degree at most three. Multiply the at most three satisfaction literals of the marker parities to form their target indicator F, with degree at most three. On a NO instance F vanishes on all native models, so F=0 is derivable. From the markers, F=1 is derivable by the telescoping product identity using affine literal multiplications up to degree three. Their sum refutes. No claim about completeness on general bipartite graphs follows.

# General ordinary PC bound from normal spaces

Let the leaves of a supplied binary tree carry the factors. At a leaf a normal span has dimension ≤2; constants are kept separately. Let U_T be the span of all variable normals of the factors in subtree T, and let B_T be its intersection with the outside span. Set r=max dim B_T. An assignment to all variables is a GF2 linear functional on the space of normals. A separator atom I(B,λ) is the product, over a basis of B, of the affine literals asserting its values under λ. Its degree is at most dim B. A different basis gives a Boolean-equivalent polynomial, and the two squarefree reductions coincide. Each ordinary reduction x_i²=x_i uses monomial multiples of Boolean axioms of degree no higher than the expression reduced.

At each node derive as zero the indicator of every separator value which cannot be extended to a satisfying linear functional on U_T. At a leaf enumerate the at most four values on its factor span. Violations of a guard coincide with its product polynomial; with dependent forms this holds after degree-two Boolean reduction. Sum full-span violation atoms for any absent boundary value. The unary marker leaf works in the same manner.

At a merge put V=B_1+B_2. The parent boundary B is contained in V: for b in B write b=u_1+u_2; each u_i is in its child span and outside-generated because b is outside-generated. If a λ on B is absent, every γ on V restricting to λ has an absent restriction to at least one B_i. Otherwise child witnesses glue, since U_1∩U_2 lies in B_1∩B_2. For each γ multiply the corresponding bad child atom by the affine literals of a basis extension B_i→V. This derives I(V,γ)=0 through degree dim V≤2r. Use Boolean reduction to change to a common basis extending B; summing all such γ telescopes to I(B,λ)=0. At an infeasible root the unique zero-dimensional boundary atom is constant one. The overall ordinary PC degree is at most max(2,2r); the proof can be reconstructed by the same tables.

For a symmetric guarded vertex matrix M and a vertex split S,T, the intersection of the two native normal spans has dimension rank M_ST+rank M_TS=2rank M_ST. Each side contains every coordinate unit on its side and projects across the split to the corresponding matrix row space. Adding t marker normals raises this intersection dimension by at most t. Thus a binary layout whose maximum adjacency cutrank is k yields ordinary PC refutation degree at most max(2,4k+2t), with arbitrary affine offsets.

In a forest, root each tree and aggregate child subtrees sequentially before adding the root's singleton leaf. The cross edges of every partial group are a star or a single edge, so cutrank ≤1; disconnected trees can then be joined arbitrarily. If deleting f vertices leaves a forest, attach those singleton leaves above its layout. The remaining crossing matrix has rank ≤1, and the rows or columns indexed by deleted vertices add at most f over any split. The resulting bound max(2,4(f+1)+2t) is independent of n. It applies without conditioning or assuming homogeneous neighbor masks.
