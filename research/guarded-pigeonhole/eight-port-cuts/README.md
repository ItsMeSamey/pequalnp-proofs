# Four-versus-four tip separations in a native eight-pole

Take a connected simple matching-covered bipartite multipole with four fixed blue and four fixed green tips, two of each color on each shore. A supplied internal edge cut partitions the original tips four on each side. For every color, the number of ports of that color on the left minus the number on the right in either side equals its internal left-minus-right vertex count. A side's ports include the newly cut half-edges.

## Two cut edges

Each side has six ports after the cut, hence its vertex-count difference is even by degree parity. In an internal perfect matching, the signed number of matching edges crossing the cut equals that difference. Difference ±2 would force both cut edges into every matching: in a nontrivial connected matching-covered graph a forced edge has no other incident allowed edges, impossible here. Thus each side is balanced, and the cut ends lie one on each shore, since both cut edges are allowed.

Enumerate the colors of the two cut edges, matching the colors on their opposite ends. If the original exterior tips on a side are imbalanced for some color, a red cut edge would require both cut edges red, violating that imbalance. All remaining feasible candidate patterns then use only blue and green, so each six-port side has a polynomial dominant-color matching test. If each exterior color is balanced, the cut colors must coincide. Blue and green choices have the same polynomial test. Red gives a two-color six-port profile unless the four exterior tips on the side contain one blue and one green on each shore. Since the full input has two of each color on each shore, this exceptional arrangement occurs on both sides or neither. Thus the sole unresolved branch is the conjunction of two prescribed opposite-shore rainbow six-port decisions.

That exception is sharp under elementary inputs. To any matching-covered bipartite six-pole with distinct tips and a prescribed opposite-shore rainbow pairing, attach a six-cycle in place of the two red ends. Label the cycle by left L_i and right R_i for i=0,1,2, adjacent iff the indices differ. Join L0 and R0 to the old red holders on the opposite shores; retain blue tips on L1,R1 and green on L2,R2. Both joins have a common color by balance. If it is blue, each internal neighbor of L2 carries a blue tip; L2 itself has a green tip and cannot receive its required blue incident edge. The green-join case is symmetric. For red joins the prescribed same-index rainbow pairs extend (give each edge the color different from the tip colors at its two ends). The constructed eight-pole is feasible iff the original specified rainbow is feasible. It is matching-covered: avoiding the joins suffices to realize each internal edge, while using both joins is possible since deleting any one opposite-shore vertex pair in a connected matching-covered bipartite graph leaves a perfect matching. The latter assertion follows from strict Hall expansion for nonempty proper subsets of a shore. The cap has no effect on the four original blue/green ends. With connected cyclic sides this is a cyclic two-edge cut.

## Tight three-edge cut

Suppose every internal perfect matching uses precisely one cut edge. Matching-coveredness makes all three cut edges usable, so all three have the same direction between the shores of the sides. Orient a side so that its three cut ends are on the positive shore, giving a vertex-count difference of one. Total degree balance shows that its four original ends split two to each shore. Precisely one cut edge must be red. For blue or green c, its total multiplicity among the seven local ports is one plus twice the number of original c ends on the negative shore. Therefore, if the two negative-shore exterior tips have a common color, every balanced local candidate has profile 5,1,1. Its most frequent color leaves two ports, yielding an exact matching extension test. If the two negative-shore tips differ, balanced profile 3,3,1 remains. Enumerating the cut permutations is polynomial when both sides have monochrome negative-shore exterior tips. This is a sufficient condition.
