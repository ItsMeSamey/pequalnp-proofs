# A finite cubic-completeness test with one bounded side

Fix s vertices on the left of a bipartite graph, t=3 marker bits, their left labels, a target, and an allowable right-label subspace. Right vertices of identical neighborhood and marker label may be contracted to one vertex. Replace that representative's pivot by the parity of its twins. Its guard is the sum of their guards, all left guards and markers use the twin parity, and the Boolean axiom for a binary parity is a sum of Boolean axioms. Restricting additional twins to zero proves the converse. Thus ordinary PC degree at least two is preserved under contraction. When the right labels span the whole marker space, the right side alone realizes every target. In a NO with three independent markers their span therefore has dimension at most two. At most 4·2^s types (v,c) occur, where v is the binary neighborhood and c its label.

For fixed selected left vector x, the eligible right types have v·x=0. On those columns the remaining constraints are linear. Their target vector is (0 on the selected left vertices, t+Lx on the three label coordinates); each right column is (v restricted to the selected vertices, c). If the target lies outside the span of the available eligible columns, there is a binary separator functional that is one at the target and zero on every available eligible column. For a given such separator let its mask consist of all ineligible types together with every eligible type on which it vanishes.

Thus an instance fails for x precisely when its right type set is contained in one of the masks for x. To fail for every x it must be contained in an intersection of masks, one from each list. If a particular x's marker residual is outside the right label space one may omit its vacuous test. Enumerate the intersections incrementally, removing duplicate masks and every mask contained in another. The deletion is sound at every stage because further intersections preserve containment. At termination this gives all inclusion-maximal NO type sets. A refutation of any such system restricts under the pinning of extra right vertices to a refutation on each contained system, without raising degree. Hence for fixed s this finite computation decides whether ordinary degree d suffices for *all* right-side sizes. Conversely any maximal NO system surviving ordinary degree d is itself a counterexample.

The test does not assume a bound on the right multiplicities. The marker normalization and numerical degree computations are separate finite steps; the construction above applies for all s independently of their outcome.

## Exact result when s is at most four

Degree three suffices for every three-marker homogeneous bipartite NO instance with at most four vertices on either shore, with the opposite shore arbitrarily large. This is sharp because an eight-vertex example with four vertices on each shore needs degree three.

Pad the bounded shore with isolated zero-label vertices to size four. If either shore's label span contains the target, that shore alone realizes it. Otherwise choose a plane containing the right labels and avoiding the target, choose its basis, and make the target the third basis vector. If the original marker equations are globally inconsistent they refute linearly. The normalized right vertex types number 64. Up to permutations of the four bounded-shore vertices and basis permutations on the right plane, the present finite test has 17 left label multisets (the enumerator retains all cases where the target has a chance of falling in the combined spans). The mask-intersection test above gives 17,369 inclusion-maximal NO right type sets. At most 34 right types occur in each.

A degree-three calculation confined to products by left variables succeeds on 15,053 of the systems. Every one of the other 2,316 systems has 15 right types. Unrestricted ordinary degree-three PC saturation derives one on all of them; this final step was reproduced on the original, unprojected equations with a separate implementation. Boolean squarefree line calculations use only variable multipliers on lines with degree at most two, so ordinary Boolean axioms lift them through degree three. A degree-four example, if one exists, must consequently have at least five vertices on each shore after deletion of duplicate identical types.

The exact reproduction pipeline and sources are [maximal type enumeration](four-types.cpp), [restricted cubic refutations](four-cubic.cpp), and [unrestricted cubic refutations](four-full.py). Each finite proof checks sufficiency of a proof degree, so encountering the constant one is decisive. The inclusion-maximality argument transfers this computation to all sizes of the second shore.
