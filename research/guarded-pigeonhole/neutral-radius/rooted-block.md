# Weighted rooted even supports reduce to one biconnected block

Take a simple graph with adjacent vertices s,t pinned to one and a single affine parity marker on the support. Every selected vertex must have even degree in the induced graph. We use homogeneous quadratic guard polynomials over GF(2). An unweighted rooted support exists exactly when st lies on a cycle. Indeed a selected even graph has no bridge, and a shortest s-to-t path avoiding st closes to an induced cycle. If st is a bridge, summing all guards on one shore gives st=0, contradicting the pins in degree two.

Otherwise let B be the biconnected block containing st. Every component of the graph outside B in the rooted connected component attaches at a single vertex v of B. Use H for that exterior component together with v, including only its incident branch edges. Allocate the weight on v to B. An even rooted support restricts to even supports separately on B and each H: summing the guards at all vertices of H except v yields v times its neighbor sum inside H, so the separate v guard is an ordinary degree-two consequence. Subtract these separate guards from the full v guard to recover its core guard. Conversely independent even selections glue whenever the common vertex bit agrees.

In a separate connected component any marked singleton toggles parity and the empty support does not. Thus with such a component every target is feasible. Within a branch attached at v, zero contribution is available for either value of v by selecting no exterior vertices. At v=0 a contribution of one exists whenever the branch contains any nonzero weight. At v=1 whether a contribution of one is possible is the ordinary two-marker problem in H with targets v=1 and p_H=1, where p_H is the sum of weights outside v. Use the two-marker cut algorithm.

A nonroot v is avoidable in a feasible rooted core support precisely when B minus v and the edge st has an s-to-t path. One direction follows by considering the selected core graph, the other by taking a shortest such path. The roots themselves are mandatory. If a marked branch attaches at an avoidable v, take a core support avoiding v and use either zero or the marked singleton, choosing the required total parity. If v is mandatory and its branch allows contribution one at v=1, start with any rooted core cycle and adjust parity there. Either construction realizes both values. In every remaining case each external branch has contribution identically zero on all rooted solutions; remove it and solve the original weighted problem on B. All peripheral tests and witness extensions use polynomial time.

## Ordinary PC transfer

The periphery can be removed at degree at most three on every NO instance. Consider a nonzero-weight branch attached at a mandatory nonroot v. A cut separating s,t in B minus v and st has only st crossing away from v in the full B. Assign v to one shore arbitrarily and sum its recovered core guards on that shore. This gives st+vL=0 for a linear form L. The pins give st=1. Multiplying by 1+v and using the Boolean axiom derives v=1 in degree three. A mandatory root already has its pin.

The two-marker cut certificate for H directly gives vp_H=0 from that branch's native guards: with target (1,1), no individual column is (1,1) because v has weight zero in H, and the missing target gives the cut identity. See the [two-marker certificate](components.md). Since v=1, we derive p_H=0. Unweighted branches have zero polynomial contribution. Thus the global marker reduces to the core marker. For any d≥3, a NO instance has an ordinary degree-d refutation if and only if its core instance does. In one direction use these derived core guards and marker, and in the other restrict all exterior vertices to zero. This result covers every off-block weight, including those at arbitrary distance.

## Exact transfer through the weighted rooted construction

For bipartite guard matrix C, suppose the two private linear markers are αᵀx=1 and βᵀy=1 and the third marker is arbitrary. Add one root on each shore with adjacency in block form [[1,βᵀ],[α,C+αβᵀ]], pin the roots, and leave their weights zero. After substituting root values one, the two root guards become precisely the private marker equations. A new nonroot guard differs from the corresponding original guard by its pivot times the opposite private marker polynomial. Thus the substituted axiom systems derive one another in degree two. Restoring pinned variables changes each axiom by degree-at-most-two multiples of the pins. Conversely restriction to their pinned values sends any ordinary degree-d proof to one of at most that degree. Ordinary PC feasibility of degree d is identical in the two formulations for every d≥2. This statement assumes the private-marker normalization displayed here.

## Cubic completeness on chains of parallel paths

Suppose deleting st from the root block leaves a chain of distinct junctions s=v₀,v₁,…,vₘ=t. Between consecutive junctions there is a nonempty bundle of internally vertex-disjoint paths, with no further vertices or graph edges in the block. At most one path per bundle can be a direct edge. The number and length of paths are unbounded. All NO targets under arbitrary weights have ordinary PC degree at most three.

Every interior junction separates s from t after deleting st; the cut derivation above pins it to one in degree three. For a path let p be its first internal vertex bit, or constant one for a direct edge. Consecutive edge products along the path agree by the guard at their common interior vertex. Therefore each edge product equals p by degree-two steps once the junction pins have been derived. The final internal vertex also equals p. The root or junction guard gives Σp=1 in each bundle: the root edge carries one, and the sum in the next bundle equals that in the preceding bundle across their shared junction. Every set of p values satisfying these affine equations is attainable by selecting all junctions and exactly the interiors of nondirect paths with p=1. A bundle with only a direct edge already has sum one, and any available nondirect path can be set to the required residual parity.

If a nondirect path can have p=0, take any such choice and its full-path realization. Any interior vertex other than the first and last on this inactive path can now be added as an isolated vertex. If any such vertex has weight one, both target values are attained. In a NO instance none of these weights occur. If a path has p forced to one, every interior vertex is one: multiply an incident edge-product equation equal to one by one plus that vertex and Boolean-reduce in degree three. The linear statement p=1 in this case follows from the bundle's affine equations.

The marker now reduces by degree-three lines to an affine function of the p bits. Count first and last only once on a path with one interior vertex. All allowable p assignments are realizable. An absent target gives an inconsistent affine system and Gaussian elimination refutes. For a present target the full-path assignment, or the singleton toggle described above, supplies a polynomial-time witness. Reattaching peripheral pieces by the preceding theorem preserves the degree bound.
