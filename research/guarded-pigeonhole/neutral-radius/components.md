# A cubic certificate when three markers occur on disconnected components

Let G be any simple graph. Use the homogeneous alternating guards xᵢΣⱼAᵢⱼxⱼ=0 over GF(2), and impose three linear markers Lx=t. If at least two components carry nonzero marker columns, every infeasible target has an ordinary degree-three PC refutation.

First consider any unpinned graph with two marker coordinates and a missing target. Zero is attained by the empty support. Choose coordinates with target 11. No vertex has label 11 by the singleton assignment. If either class 10 or 01 is absent, its coordinate vanishes identically. Otherwise these two classes must be completely joined by edges (a nonedge is a witness), and no connected zero-label component touches both (a shortest path plus the edge closes an induced cycle). Place each zero-label component with a class it touches. The cut crossing edges comprise precisely the pairs of vertices in the two marked classes. Summing guards on one side derives the product of the two class parities as zero. This is the quadratic Boolean indicator of the missing target. The construction uses no two-marker input axioms.

Now partition components into two unions, each carrying a nonzero marker column, and let A,B be their sets of attainable local three-bit syndromes. Both sets contain 0 and at least one nonzero vector. Since t cannot be achieved, A and t+B are disjoint. Both sides therefore omit at least two points of the eight-element space.

For a side missing distinct s,u, project syndrome space onto the quotient by span(s+u). The image class of s is missing; its inverse image is exactly {s,u}. Apply the preceding two-coordinate certificate. Multiply by one affine agreement indicator separating s from u to derive the singleton indicator I_s=0 at degree three. Any two formulas for the singleton indicator agree as Boolean functions, and hence their multilinear reductions agree using Boolean axioms of degree at most three.

For every local candidate a in the first side, either a is missing there or t+a is missing in the second side. Thus one of the two singleton indicators is zero by the local certificate. Under the global marker equations the indicators coincide: each is a product of three affine factors, and the two products differ by a telescoping sum of global affine marker equations multiplied by products of at most two affine factors. Derive the first-side indicator I_a=0 in degree at most three. Summing the eight indicators gives one. This also shows that a prospective degree-four example must have only one marker-active component, whose reachable syndromes include every syndrome other than t (if another were missing the two-coordinate projection would refute the original target in degree two).

## A bridge separating two remaining marked vertices also suffices

Let uv be a bridge. Suppose the u shore has a nonzero marker column away from u, and the v shore also has a nonzero marker column away from v. Under these assumptions every missing three-marker target again has an ordinary PC refutation of degree at most three.

Summing the native guards on a shore derives uv=0, so subtracting this line from each endpoint guard yields all native guards within the separated shore graphs in degree two. Write h for the syndrome in the first shore. Use t plus the syndrome in the second shore as its translated coordinate, identified with h via the global marker equations. Let A,B be the corresponding attainable h values for each full shore, and A₀,B₀ the attainable values after setting u or v to zero on the respective shore. The two latter sets each have at least two members by the empty support and a remaining labeled singleton. On a NO instance A₀∩B=A∩B₀=∅, since any such common syndrome would glue with compatible endpoints. Hence A,B each omit at least two points. As in the disconnected proof, any missing local singleton indicator is derivable in degree three. Consequently every Boolean function vanishing on I=A∩B is derivable as zero in degree three by summing its singleton indicators. If I is empty this already gives a contradiction.

Choose a Boolean function Q supported precisely on I when its size is even, and on I together with one member of B₀ when its size is odd. Its support has even size, so its multilinear polynomial in three bits has degree at most two (the coefficient of the cubic term is the parity of the truth table). It equals one on I and zero on A₀. Pair its support points arbitrarily. On the first shore with u deleted each pair's indicator is zero by the two-marker cut theorem, since both points are missing. Therefore the degree-two polynomial Q is a sum of degree-two consequences of the u-deleted guards, up to degree-two Boolean reduction. Lift the identity by multiplying by 1+u. The full guards restricted to u=0 agree with the deleted ones, so ordinary Boolean reductions give (1+u)Q=0 in degree three. More explicitly, terms in a guard removed by vertex deletion all contain u and are killed modulo u²+u.

On the other hand Q+1 vanishes on I, so it is derivable as zero at degree three, with a final polynomial of degree at most two. Multiply just this final line by 1+u and add to the previous line. This gives u=1 in degree three. Interchanging shores (use a padding point in A₀) gives v=1 in degree three. The bridge product uv=0 now contradicts these linear equations. All transitions between the translated marker bases use telescoping products of affine linear factors, at ordinary degree at most three.

Thus a prospective quartic example has no bridge separating nonzero marker columns away from its two endpoints simultaneously. This improves the direct two-branch bound of four by using even-support interpolation on the three-bit marker space.
