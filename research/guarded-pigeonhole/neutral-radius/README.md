# Replacing neutral rows by short sums of permitted rows

In the one-sided three-marker model the left vertices are colored or have label zero. Right vertices have the two right colors. The guards say that the selected induced graph has even degrees. Let R consist of the distinct nonzero binary neighborhoods of neutral vertices on the full right side, and let U=span R have dimension r. For each σ in the dual of U, put Rσ=R∩ker σ. Define δ to be the maximum, over all σ and E in span Rσ, of the minimum number of elements of Rσ summing to E. The empty sum is allowed.

**Theorem.** If δ≤3 then any feasible target in the normalized mixed three-marker model admits a witness with one colored left vertex and at most three neutral left vertices. This holds at any incidence rank. Both membership in this class and witness search are polynomial-time.

For a witness with right indicator y, take σ(v)=v·y. Every selected neutral row belongs to Rσ. Their sum E records their complete contribution at all right vertices. Replace the selected neutrals by representatives of a shortest representation of E in Rσ. Their own degrees are even by construction. All right guards retain the same values, and the colored guards and marker values are preserved. The resulting feasible support has at most δ neutral vertices. Restrict to this support and apply the rank-three singleton theorem there. A witness in that induced subgraph remains a witness in the full instance because discarded vertices are unselected. Write m=|R|. Radius at most d implies 2^r≤Σ_{i=0}^d binom(m,i) by taking σ=0. Reject if this fails. Otherwise enumerate at most this many dual functionals using a basis of U. For each σ enumerate the sums of at most d allowed rows; they all belong to span Rσ and cover that span exactly when their number is 2^{rank Rσ}. This recognizes the class in polynomial time for fixed d. To find a witness for d≤3, enumerate a colored vertex and at most three neutral rows. Discard right columns of odd degree into these chosen vertices, then solve the left even-degree equations and marker equations for the remaining right selection by binary elimination. There are polynomially many choices even when the colored blocks have unbounded rank.

For examples take U as a direct sum of d coordinate subspaces and include every nonzero row in each individual summand, with no rows mixing summands. For any σ the permitted rows in a summand comprise the entire nonzero local kernel. Every permitted total has a representation using at most one row per summand, so δ≤d. If all summands are nonzero, σ=0 gives equality. Each summand can have arbitrarily high dimension. One summand may be supported exclusively on the first right color and another may meet both right colors, so the mixed neutral rows need not span U.

For ordinary polynomial calculus, branch on the r coordinates of σ and the r coordinates of E; these are linear forms in the right variables and neutral selectors respectively. On a branch all forbidden neutral selectors are zero by their guards. If E is outside span Rσ there is an immediate linear contradiction. Otherwise choose a shortest allowed representation. Its rows' even-degree equations follow from the σ branch. The branched original right guards have the same offsets as the smaller synthetic system, since their neutral sums equal the specified E. In a NO instance, any singleton-colored witness in that synthetic instance would lift to a witness by selecting the corresponding actual rows. Hence the rank-three bad-row proof on a branch uses at most three active representatives. Its group parity and presence stages depend on their count, with at most 16 groups; even the unrefined version costs at most 32 additional affine-bit/presence branches and two inner degrees. Thus ordinary PC degree at most 2r+34 suffices for δ≤3. By the counting bound, this is O(log(m+1)) on the recognizable class. The rest of the input may have arbitrarily many colored vertices. No bounded rank-only claim for all neutral systems follows.
