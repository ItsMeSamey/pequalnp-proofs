# Supplied bounded-row lift of a graphic representation

Let v_e=(b_e,γ_e) with b_e a graph incidence column and γ_e∈F₂^h. Fix the h extra coordinates κ of the dual. A vertex potential x must satisfy x_a+x_b=κ·γ_e on every selected edge. The primal support is Eulerian and has total γ zero. Its primal score is an additive p-bit edge label, while its dual score is a known κ-offset plus Σ_v x_v w_v, with q-bit vertex weights. A covector on the represented span extends to this ambient format; when dual moments are points in that span, every extension gives the same requested moments. Enumerate κ and the primal target consistent with the full mixed target.

In a genuine solution fix the potential and minimize the support of its primal cycle at the given score and zero total γ. A nonzero subcycle of zero combined (γ,primal score) could be removed, so the ordinary graphic cycle nullity of the chosen support is at most g=h+p. The number of positive-degree branch vertices, the sum of their degrees and the number of single-cycle components are O(g); every degree is at most 2g (an even subgraph decomposes into at most g edge-disjoint cycles). Empty support is allowed.

**Sparse dual moments.** Suppose at most t vertices have nonzero w_v. Guess their colors and which lie on the selected support. Suppress all unmarked degree-two vertices; retain cycle anchors, branch vertices and selected scored terminals. This produces a bounded-size subdivision pattern. Enumerate its placement, vertex colors, and finite edge-label totals (γ,primal weights) on its path segments. The total primal score and γ are prescribed. For each segment require κ times its γ-total to equal the endpoint color difference; the internal colors then propagate uniquely with no conflicts because the paths have disjoint interiors. Exclude unselected terminals from the paths. The finite-group constrained subdivision/disjoint-path algorithm finds the pattern, or proves its absence. For fixed h,p,t this is polynomial, even when the represented binary matroid is nonregular.

**Dense dual moments under a bounded switching transversal.** For each κ suppose a set K of bounded size is supplied or can be found such that its signed edge function κ·γ is switchable to zero away from K. Compute the switch δ by traversal of G−K, change x accordingly and translate the dual target by Σ_v δ_vw_v. There are at most 2g|K| selected edges incident to K. Enumerate them and their endpoints as marked pattern vertices, setting their required signed differences explicitly. Every remaining connecting path avoids K and has constant switched color. To obtain its internal dual vertex sum in the group query, label each edge ab by the sum modulo four of the integer-lifted bits w_a+w_b, one coordinate for each bit. Subtract the two endpoint lifts: the remaining length equals twice the internal bit sum. Count each marked pattern vertex just once. The color-one region outside the selected support, when needed, can be replaced by ≤q exterior vertices supplying the residual dual contribution. Guess those vertices before the linkage query and exclude them from the support. These decisions exactly reconstruct a witness; every feasible solution has one of the enumerated patterns. Fixed h,p,q,|K| give polynomial time. K can differ with κ. The sparse-moment result has no such switching condition.

Treat originally adjacent marked vertices as one-edge segments when appropriate. The bounded-subdivision theorem allows common branch endpoints; alternatively guess the incident first edges, remove branch vertices, and use disjoint distinct exposed terminals. Pure cycle components can be split at auxiliary anchors. Loops and parallel edges may be handled separately or replaced by internally disjoint length-three paths (triangle for a loop): put γ and the primal label on one segment, give intermediate vertices zero vertex moments, and keep original ambient dual moment coordinates. Degree two at new vertices forces selection of all segments or none. If dual scores are expressed by edge-coefficient sums instead of vertex moments, copy the original dual-edge coefficient onto all three segments to telescope. Place a signature-bearing segment next to K on exceptional edges.

For a nonregular example, the seven nonzero three-bit binary columns can be presented as two graphic incidence rows for a triangle (using parallel copies and a graphic loop) plus one signature row. The resulting Fano matroid is not graphic or regular. Additional arbitrary graphic columns can be included without removing that restriction. This theorem does not assert recognition of suitable presentations or solve the unrestricted dense-moment, unbounded-transversal case.

Primary source: [Liu and Yoo, Disjoint Paths Problem with Group-Expressable Constraints, STOC 2025](https://people.tamu.edu/~yyoo/stoc25.pdf).

## Supplied virtual two-sum decompositions

The dense-moment class is stable under arbitrary fixed-dimensional changes of the primal and dual weights on virtual elements. In a rooted tree of proper binary two-sums use each child's conditional score set at port states 00,10,01. A baseline score per state is an affine function of the two port bits; at most the global marker rank many exceptions suffice to reproduce any total score by linear dependence. A parent port state uses one extra affine row. The dense-moment torso algorithm therefore applies unchanged when all torsos have the stated bounded lift and switching parameters, including arbitrarily many nonregular torsos. This is an application of the virtual two-sum algorithm in /tmp/guard-publish/plucker-lines/four-marker-trace/virtual-two-sums.md. For sparse-dual-only torsos unconditional composition would be incorrect: virtual dual port labels may introduce graph-vertex moment coefficients at every port endpoint. Bounded total endpoint support, or testing the resulting weights, suffices.

check_lifts.py compared 362 complete attainable mixed-score images with direct enumeration, including 19,134 colored selected subgraphs and 3,028 checks of nontrivial color-one internal path contributions after switching. This checks the combinatorial reduction and offsets; the fixed disjoint-paths algorithm itself is used via its theorem.
