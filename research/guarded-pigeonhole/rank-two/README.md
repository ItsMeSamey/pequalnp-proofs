# Two colored cycles and an adaptive rank potential

Fix a prospective primal score α and dual syndrome β on a q-dimensional space U of even vertex subsets (using reduced graphic incidence, or full potential with component shifts immaterial). A selected Eulerian subgraph consists of monochromatic edges. A minimal support of primal score rank two decomposes into at most two simple cycles, with independent scores if there are two: a nontrivial dependence lets one subtract a nonempty supported Eulerian subword. A same-color witness can be colored zero on its support; guess at most q exterior color-one vertices realizing the required moments, exclude them, and solve cycle equations linearly. The remaining case has two vertex-disjoint cycles C0,C1 with different colors and prescribed edge scores.

Maintain X excluded from both cycles and a subspace T of U on which |C1∩u|=β(u) is prescribed. Find a signed pair in G−X satisfying the old residues. Try to extend its colors by linear algebra. On failure find u∈U supported within C0∪C1 with |C1∩u|≠β(u). It lies in U_X (coordinates zero on X) and outside T. A valid pair either omits some w in supp(u) or covers the support and satisfies the correct parity. Branch on X∪{w} for each w, and once on T+span(u). Keep old T rows even after an exclusion. The integer potential dim U_X + q − dim T drops on every branch, bounding depth by 2q.

The required pair oracle follows from the fixed-disjoint-paths theorem for unoriented finite abelian edge labels. For each u put the integer sum of endpoint bits on each edge, modulo four; around a cycle the resulting length is twice the number of u-vertices. Combine with ordinary binary edge labels, open each cycle at a guessed edge and correct the target lengths. More directly, guess at most q outside vertices of color one and impose all q C1 residues from the beginning. This is a rank-two analysis of a known-tractable graphic case, not a solution on arbitrary binary representations.

For one dense dual moment there is a simpler signature argument. It does not generalize merely by calling reachable scores a subspace after deleting one extra vertex. Take two triangles sharing z, bearing independent cycle labels 1 and 2, and a,b noncentral vertices of the two triangles. The moment x_a+x_b=1 allows each triangle separately but not their union, hence the attainable scores at the fixed dual syndrome are {0,1,2}. Adding a third disjoint score-1 triangle through t and second moment x_t+x_z=1 can restore score 3 while deleting t and quotienting that moment yields the same non-subspace.

Primary source: [Liu and Yoo, Disjoint Paths Problem with Group-Expressable Constraints, STOC 2025](https://people.tamu.edu/~yyoo/stoc25.pdf).
