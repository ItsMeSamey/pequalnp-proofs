# From binary complementarity of asymmetry rank 2h to h+2 undirected pins

Let beta.x=1 be the required parity and x_i(Mx)_i=0 the coordinatewise support equations. If beta=0 reject immediately. Decompose M+M^T into a sum of h alternating rank-two terms u_j v_j^T+v_j u_j^T by ordinary alternating elimination over F2. Then M=A+diag(d)+sum_j u_j v_j^T with A alternating. Guess the h bits b_j=v_j.x. Put delta=d+sum_j b_j u_j. On the slice the matrix equations on selected coordinates are exactly those of A+diag(delta), since x_i=1 there. Summing after multiplication by x_i gives delta.x=0 because A is alternating. On the slice beta.x=1 and delta.x=0, the alternating matrix A'=A+delta beta^T+beta delta^T adds precisely the needed delta_i on each selected row.

Thus it suffices to solve an alternating-matrix support system A' with h+2 prescribed parity forms beta.x=1, v_j.x=b_j, delta.x=0. Keep even dependent forms: contradictory forms simply yield an infeasible graph. The right-hand-side vector is nonzero. For any k>=2 such equations write them as Gamma^T x=t. By invertible row operations put t=(1,1,0,...,0)^T. Write w for the all-ones k-vector. Choose alternating binary k-by-k K,B with Kw=t and Bt=w. Such choices exist since both targets are orthogonal to their arguments. In general for a nonzero argument z and desired y orthogonal to z, pick c with c.z=1 and take y c^T+c y^T. Form the alternating graph matrix
    [ A'+Gamma B Gamma^T   Gamma ]
    [ Gamma^T                K   ].
Require all k new vertices to be selected. Their equations impose exactly Gamma^T x=t; on this slice the original rows receive Gamma Bt+Gamma w=0 as required. Reading the original coordinates recovers a witness. There are at most 2^h queries, each of polynomial size, with k=h+2 pins.

For h=1 this gives two literal three-pin queries. In the reverse direction three distinct literal pins p,u,v in an alternating A are handled by M=A+e_u e_v^T+e_u e_p^T+e_p e_u^T+e_p e_p^T, beta=e_p. The scalar x^T Mx=x_p+x_u x_v forces both other pins, and on the three-pin slice every added row contribution vanishes. Hence the one-parity asymmetry-rank-at-most-two problem and the three-pin undirected problem have the same deterministic polynomial-time decidability. The argument gives no unrestricted SAT reduction.
