# Physical exact-plane with a graphic private representation

Private columns form a supplied binary graphic representation. Each physical edge also has a label in the direct sum T+H of two binary planes. For a subset its private dependencies are exactly the cycle space of the selected graph. Their label sums form the physical trace in the named space. The required trace is H. Graphic recognition and realization can first convert a matrix input while retaining its labeled dependency space.

Tag balance is equivalent to a common T-valued potential, by assigning potentials along a spanning forest. In a feasible subset fundamental cycles span the cycle space, so two of them have independent heights. Each comprises a forest path and a distinct chord outside the forest. Their two paths intersect in a single path, in one vertex, or not at all. Thus these fundamental cycles form either a theta, a pair of cycles sharing just one vertex, or two vertex-disjoint cycles. This includes loops (their forest path is a singleton). In the latter two types require both cycles to have zero tags and independent heights. In a theta require the three branch gains to have one common T component and three distinct H components. These checks suffice: the physical cycle space of the union has rank two with the desired label image. Unselected ambient edges impose no restrictions.

Enumerate the finitely many valid gain assignments on these fixed patterns over Gamma=T+H. For disjoint cycles or two cycles sharing one vertex use two loop branches at distinct anchors or at the same anchor, respectively. For a theta use three parallel branches. Guess distinct images of distinct pattern vertices. For each branch guess whether it is a direct edge, length two, or longer. For direct edges verify endpoints and gain, including physical loops. For length two guess both edge IDs and reserve the common interior vertex; a returning digon uses distinct parallel IDs. For longer branches guess their distinct first and last edges and the two distinct interior endpoints of those edges. All guessed edge IDs across all branches must be distinct; all interior endpoints and reserved interiors must be globally distinct and outside the anchors. Remove the anchors and length-two reserved interiors from the graph. For each longer branch call simultaneously for vertex-disjoint simple paths between its two interior endpoints, with gain equal to the desired branch gain minus the two fixed incidence gains. All requested terminal nodes are distinct and nonempty remaining paths are forced by distinct ends. Attach the guessed edges to the returned paths. This gives exactly the requested labelled subdivisions, sharing only the template vertices. Each actual witness appears under its own guesses.

For the constant group and at most three paths, the deterministic constructive group-labelled linkage theorem gives polynomial time. It covers finite abelian groups, permits multigraphs and uses oriented gains, identical to unsigned XOR here. The external theorem supplies the subroutine; this packet does not implement the general linkage solver. Tony Huynh, *The k-disjoint paths problem for group-labelled graphs*, Theorem 1.1: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34307 .

Vertex-disjoint cycles in the four-sheet lift can fail even with individually simple projections. Four internally disjoint s-t paths with combined gains (0,0),(0,e1),(g,0),(g,e2) give balanced cycles of independent heights. Choose starting sheets 0 and k outside {0,g} for the two equal-tag path pairs to obtain disjoint lifts. But one potential fixes p(s)+p(t), so at most two whole paths can be active. The physical cycle rank is at most one. The base-graph subdivision algorithm excludes this false witness.

Liu and Yoo, *Disjoint Paths Problem with Group-Expressable Constraints*, Theorem 1.3, also solve the exact required linkage. Use four GF(2) coordinates, forbidding the one undesired value for each path and coordinate. Their algorithm returns disjoint paths or certifies failure in O(|V|^8+|E|) for each fixed k<=3,d=4,f=1. https://people.tamu.edu/~yyoo/stoc25.pdf

Only the anchor guesses are necessary with the following variant. Replace every physical edge, including a loop, by a central edge joining two distinct private incidence vertices, carrying its combined label. Join each incidence vertex to its original endpoint with a zero edge. Replace every chosen anchor by one distinct socket per required branch end; connect every socket to every incidence vertex formerly adjacent to that anchor. Each socket is an endpoint of exactly one demand, so no linkage path can travel through another socket. Paths cannot share incidence vertices or nonanchor vertices. The one spurious possibility is a two-step path between sockets on the same anchor via a single incidence vertex and without any central edge. Its zero gain cannot satisfy a returning branch, whose required height is nonzero. Apart from that exception, every visited incidence vertex forces traversal of its central edge because it has only one neighbor other than sockets at an anchor, and degree two elsewhere. A true physical loop traverses its own central edge and two distinct incidence vertices. Thus the linkage, with sockets glued back, gives exactly the proposed branches, including digons and loops. Conversely split a witness at its anchors. There are O(1+|V|^2) anchor guesses and constantly many gain patterns. The socket graph has O(|V|+|E|) vertices and edges. The total bound for a supplied graph is O((1+|V|)^2(1+|V|+|E|)^8).
