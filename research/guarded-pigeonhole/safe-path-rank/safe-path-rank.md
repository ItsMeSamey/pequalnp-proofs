# Safe paths parameterized by paired-edge difference rank

Give every edge its binary vertex incidence column and contract the span of unpaired edges. A safe path is equivalent to a subset with boundary s+t using at most one member per pair: the two odd vertices must lie in the same component, from which a simple path can be recovered.

Let a_i,b_i be the quotient columns, u the quotient boundary, and Delta the span of delta_i=a_i+b_i of dimension r. Define the affine space Z by sum_i a_i z_i=u mod Delta. A point z specifies precisely which pairs contribute. It succeeds exactly when h(z)=u+sum_i a_i z_i belongs to the span of the deltas with z_i=1. A representation gives the replacements of a_i by b_i; solving the remaining boundary with unpaired edges produces an original safe subgraph.

At a successful point let H be the active delta span and k its dimension. Fix k used bits whose deltas span H and impose h(z) in H. These equations have total relative codimension at most k+(r-k)=r within Z, and all points on this nonempty flat succeed. Enumerate the established chart seed family on free coordinates of Z, testing each point by elimination. For free dimension l the seed construction evaluates binary coefficient polynomials of degree <l on a field of size q>2^r l. At most 2^r-1 nonzero combinations of the hidden constraint rows can vanish, each on fewer than l field elements. Thus there is an x where all parity constraint rows evaluate to binary-independent field elements, and enumerating all binary linear functionals on the field realizes the required right-hand sides. Time is 2^O(r) poly(input) including deterministic modulus search and path recovery. This proof applies to arbitrary binary columns up to the final graph path extraction.

The flat bound can be attained: pair the r bridge edges of a chain with distinct loops as their alternatives. With the loops as a_i, Z is the whole cube, r is the chain length, and only its all-ones word succeeds.

On the serial variable/corridor and parallel-clause source reduction, identify all variable-region vertices with the initial clause vertex. This linear quotient kills all unpaired edges (these are confined to variable corridors), and sends each paired difference to its clause link in the clause chain. The distinct clause incidences are independent, and each nonempty clause supplies at least one difference. Thus r is at least the number of clauses on every instance of that encoding. The bound is attained if all variables have just one occurrence: all variable-block endpoints become identified by their unpaired empty corridors, and the one occurrence edge in each other corridor then has zero quotient. The rank bound provides no general polynomial guarantee on this reduction.

## Optimally translating each menu

Translate each menu {0,a,b} by any of its three labeled options w, shifting the target by the sum of translations. The two nonbaseline options now differ by a,b or a+b depending on w. Let r* be the least rank over these independent choices. At search depth k maintain a span S initially zero. If a menu has no difference in S, branch on adjoining each of its three differences, increasing dimension by one. Some branch stays inside any optimal extending span. Stop when all menus meet S; increasing the depth budget finds r* in 3^O(r*) poly(input). Invert translations on recovered labels, allowing equal vectors for distinct labels. Total time is 2^O(r*) poly(input).

## Computing r* in the serial SAT source

Write the incidence space after free contraction as the direct sum of the variable-block spaces V and the independent clause link axes C. Indeed, gluing blocks at one vertex introduces no cycles between them. A variable with k occurrences has a k-cycle after contracting each empty corridor, including a loop for k=1 or two parallel edges for k=2. Any u of its columns have rank min(u,k-1).

Any span S meeting each triple {a,c,a+c}, with a a variable occurrence column and c a clause link, can be replaced by proj_V(S) plus (S intersect C), of equal dimension. This preserves pure options and replaces mixed options by their variable projections. Thus a pure optimum exists. Pay one for every chosen clause link. For each variable with u remaining occurrences, the cost is min(u,deg(v)-1). When this equals the full variable cost select that variable, otherwise one unit per unpaid incidence suffices to buy all still-uncovered clauses touched there. This gives a vertex cover of no greater cost. Conversely any such cover gives a hitting span by taking whole selected variable blocks and selected clause axes. Thus r* is exactly the minimum cost of a vertex cover of the incidence bipartite graph, with weight deg(v)-1 per occurring variable and one per clause.

For exact width-three clauses and minimum variable degree two, the value is the clause count m. Put a charge of 1/3 on each occurrence; clause load is one and each variable load is at most its cover weight. Every cover costs at least the total charge and buying all clauses attains it. The same counts hold with repeated literals. On distinct singleton variables, r*=0 is possible even when r=m.
