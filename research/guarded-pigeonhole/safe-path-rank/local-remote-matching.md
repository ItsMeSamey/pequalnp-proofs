# Safe boundary with local and remote forbidden edge pairs

In an undirected multigraph, edges are unconstrained or partitioned into disjoint two-edge conflicts. Given an even boundary T, seek a subset of edges with boundary T and at most one member of each conflict. All linear calculations use GF(2). A two-point boundary yields the safe path problem by cycle deletion and path extraction.

First contract the components of the unconstrained edges, mapping T by parity per component. Discard loops since they have zero boundary. A surviving partner of a discarded loop becomes unconstrained, so contraction can be iterated until stable. To lift any contracted solution, keep its original edge IDs and solve the residual boundary in each contracted component by its recorded forest, in reverse contraction order. At each stage a newly free partner has its competitor discarded permanently. There are at most |E| nontrivial edge-removal stages. A pair in the stable quotient is local if its members have a common endpoint, remote otherwise.

If one option of every remote pair has been disabled, keep the remaining member as an optional edge. For each local pair select one common endpoint as its center. Give the pair a shared port at this endpoint and give each edge a private port at its other endpoint. Give optional edges two private ports. Connect each pair of ports of a physical edge by one external matching edge indexed by its original ID. At each vertex make all its ports a clique, adding one dummy port iff the port count disagrees modulo two with the desired boundary bit. A perfect matching selects a valid boundary by its external edges, since each shared port can be used at most once. Conversely the residual number of ports in each clique is even after choosing a safe edge subset of the right boundary, so it extends to a perfect matching. An isolated vertex requiring odd boundary yields one unmatched dummy. The matching graph has O(|V|+|E|) vertices and O((|V|+|E|)^2) edges. Parallel physical options sharing both original endpoints retain distinct private ports. Every matching witness decodes to original edges.

For m remote pairs with boundary columns a_i,b_i let r=rank{a_i+b_i}. Assume a successful edge subset and fix the selected local edges and the set I of used remote pairs. The binary controls enabling a_i or b_i produce a combined boundary for those same slots that is affine of rank at most r. Its fiber at the correct boundary is nonempty of codimension at most r in the full m-dimensional cube. A deterministic hitting set for such flats suffices, with one matching test per point. For m>0 choose a binary field of order Q at least 2^(r+1)m and at most 2^(r+2)m. For each (u,v) in the field squared sample the vector of bits Tr(v u^i), 0<=i<m. Each nontrivial character has bias at most (m-1)/Q by polynomial root counting and orthogonality in v. Fourier expansion of a codimension-h flat gives hit probability at least 2^(-h)(1-(2^h-1)(m-1)/Q)>0. Enumerate an irreducible polynomial by trial and deterministic polynomial degree tests in Q polylog Q time. With m=0 run just one matching test. Thus runtime is 2^O(r) poly(input), including witness recovery.

Alternatively independently choose delta_i among a_i,b_i,a_i+b_i to minimize joint rank r*. For budget k branch only when a pair has no option in the current span, adding one of its three choices; depth k and iterative deepening find an optimum. The union B of the supports of chosen basis vectors has at most 4r* vertices. At least one option of every remote pair has both endpoints in B: for a_i+b_i the two edges are disjoint. Designate one such option per pair. A minimum-cardinality solution is a forest, since deleting a cycle preserves feasibility. Its designated options are a forest on B and number at most max(0,|B|-1). Enumerate all subsets of designated IDs up to that bound. Disable their mates and disable unguessed designated edges. The original solution survives its particular guess, and any matching answer respects every pair. This yields deterministic |input|^O(1+r*) decision and witnesses even for arbitrary T. Neither rank requires a bound on the number or rank of local pairs.
