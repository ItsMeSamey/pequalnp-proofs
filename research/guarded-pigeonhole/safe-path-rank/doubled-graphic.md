# Exact boundary decision for doubled graphic deficiency one

Let G be connected with v vertices and e edges; each color corresponds to one edge with boundary column c and options 0,(c,0),(0,c). The two spaces are identical labeled copies of the graphic incidence representation. Assume a full independent colorful baseline exists and e=2(v-1)-1. The baseline partitions E into two forests, one per coordinate. Their sizes sum to 2v-3, so one is a spanning tree and one a spanning forest with two components. In particular adding one edge between the two components makes two disjoint spanning trees. A zero-column loop cannot occur in a full baseline.

Translating each edge menu by (c,c) gives the three nonzero labels of the group A=GF(2)^2 on that edge. Thus translate the target by the sum of these column pairs. Keep coordinates for all vertices; the total demand must be zero. If G has no bridge, every such demand has a solution. If G has a bridge, it is unique, and a demand has a solution exactly when its sum on either shore is nonzero.

Indeed, the Catlin reduction theorem for graphs at most one edge short of two edge-disjoint spanning trees gives reduction K1 or K2 upon contracting maximal collapsible subgraphs. Each such subgraph is bridgeless (take a spanning connected all-even subgraph). Consequently the bridgeless case is collapsible, and otherwise two collapsible parts meet at precisely one bridge. A collapsible graph supports every zero-sum A-demand by nonzero edge values, as shown by Hong-Jian Lai. Here one can see that implication directly: choose a spanning connected subgraph H with prescribed odd set equal to the first demand coordinate. Set its edges' first coordinates to one and all others to zero. Set the second coordinate to one off H. Solve the residual second coordinate on H, which spans and is connected. In the bridge case conservation forces exactly the shore-sum value on that bridge; when nonzero, subtract that boundary and solve both sides separately. The proof yields a linear-time decision on a promised graph by bridge detection and summation. Polynomial witness extraction from an arbitrary collapsible input is not established in this note.

For a disconnected graph with a full baseline and global doubled corank one, each component has nonnegative deficiency 2(v_i-1)-e_i, and their sum is one. In each deficiency-zero component the forests are two spanning trees and all zero-sum demands are realizable constructively (set second bit one on tree one and first bit one on tree two; solve the remaining bits on their respective trees). Apply the preceding test only to the exceptional component. Isolated vertices require zero demand.
