# Localizing the degree assumption to neighbors of marks

For a vertex u let c(u) be the number of incident edges that are not graph bridges. For any fixed maximum degree Δ, the constant-degree PC theorem in weighted-pc.md holds if each marked h and each nonbridge edge hu satisfy c(u)≤3. The number of higher-degree vertices may grow with the input. Pinned roots can be removed from the marker, adjusting its required parity.

Use the same no-spectator preprocessing and bounded-width torso, with terminal separator bound Δ and four neighborhood expansions increasing maximum bag size by at most a factor of Δ+1 each. The enlarged primal decomposition adds at most Δ+1 variables per original bag vertex, plus three accumulator variables; consequently the bound on original PC degree depends only on Δ and its separator torso bound.

The lifted positive edge set is even; every positive edge lies on a cycle and is nonbridge. Choose any simple cycle C containing st among the positive edges. Every positive-degree vertex u with c(u)≤3 has positive degree two. As in weighted-pc.md, every vertex of C within distance two of a mark has selector one. For a selected h outside C, any edge hu to C would be positive since u is selected. Then hu would be nonbridge, c(u)≤3, and u would have an extra positive edge besides the two already on C. So h is a spectator. For h on C a chord at h would be positive, would join h to some u∈C via a nonbridge edge, and would exceed the positive capacity of u. A chord at either cycle neighbor u of h would likewise exceed u's capacity, since the cycle edge hu is nonbridge. Exactly the preceding shortcut proof now applies. In particular h itself can have degree greater than three.

## Moving the marker by affine laws of mandatory vertices

Every vertex v that occurs on all s-to-t paths in G−st belongs to every rooted even support. For internal v, sum the guards over the component of s in (G−st)−v. The only leaving edges in G are st and edges to v; hence x_s x_t=x_v L(x) for a neighbor sum L. Multiply by 1+x_v, use the Boolean axiom, and use the two root pins to obtain x_v=1 at degree three. The root pins themselves suffice when v=s,t. The guard now gives Σ_{u∈N(v)}x_u=0 in degree at most three, by adding x_u-multiples of the pin x_v+1. Ordinary PC retains the earlier degree-three derivations of the pin and can multiply a resulting degree-one line by x_u at degree two. Thus the marker can be shifted by any binary linear combination of these neighborhood vectors and by mandatory coordinates (updating τ), at degree three. Search this affine space by binary Gaussian elimination to annul the coefficients of every h violating the local condition. A successful replacement proves the same bound for the original marker, using a marker that need not be unique. If the root edge is a bridge it instead refutes directly at degree two.
