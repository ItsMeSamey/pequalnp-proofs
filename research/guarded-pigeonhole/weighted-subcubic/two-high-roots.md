# Two exceptional roots and an arbitrary binary weight

Suppose every vertex other than the endpoints s,t of a pinned edge has degree at most three. The two roots may have arbitrary degree. Binary-weight reachability on rooted induced even supports is decidable constructively in deterministic polynomial time.

Let D be the nonroot neighbors of either root. Removing both roots from an even induced support yields individual paths of types SS, TT or ST according to their endpoint attachments, plus components anticomplete to both roots. Their interiors avoid D, and a common root neighbor is a singleton ST path. Every selected nonroot has degree zero or two. Each ST path plus the roots forms an induced cycle. The number of ST paths is odd because st is present and the SS paths contribute twice at s.

For each possible ST endpoint pair (or singleton common neighbor) keep the two roots and those endpoints, deleting all remaining D. The resulting graph is subcubic. Test the marker parity by the known subcubic solver. Each returned support works in G, and these tests cover every rooted induced cycle plus at most one spectator, since a spectator cannot neighbor either root. Return a successful witness. If there is no ST cycle at all then st is a bridge. Otherwise failure implies every ST cycle has the same wrong weight and every spectator of every ST cycle is unmarked. Thus each ST path has the same nonroot weight p. Their odd number contributes p in a genuine witness; any other component away from roots has only spectators. Hence there must be an additional odd SS/TT petal Q. Its interior vertices are individually spectators of the witness's ST cycle, so all are unmarked. Its endpoints b,c have different weights.

Seek a mutually anticomplete pair consisting of one ST path P and one odd SS/TT petal Q. Enumerate their endpoints, orient the petal, and guess which paths are singleton or direct terminal edges. Require the induced endpoint adjacencies to match the proposed path forms. For each path requiring interior vertices, guess the unique interior neighbor for each endpoint; the two guesses of one path can coincide. Delete all other D and all other nonterminal neighbors of the endpoints. Reject any guess if it removes a required vertex or permits contact between a terminal and a required vertex on the opposite path. For each path with an interior join its guessed neighbors by a shortest route in the graph without the terminals. When both interiors are nonempty require the used components to be distinct. The resulting two paths are induced and anticomplete. All extra-petal interior vertices are spectators of the reconstructed ST cycle, hence unmarked. Their union with the roots realizes the target. Direct and singleton paths need no interior route. Only constantly many vertex guesses are made.

For completeness take real P,Q matching their own guesses. If their interiors lie in the same component, suppose Q attaches to s. From the T-only end j of P take a shortest path into the interior of Q, using only j and the component. Let z be the last vertex before first entry. No earlier prefix vertex touches the interior of Q. From z use its first neighbor along the ordered Q-interior to reach b, or its last to reach c. These two paths are induced: the prefix was shortest, the first/last choices exclude chords at z on the respective retained subpaths, and the trimmed endpoint neighborhoods exclude all other endpoint contacts. They form two ST cycles with the same prefix weights, all differing interior vertices unmarked and the ends b,c oppositely marked. This contradicts failure of the base tests. Exchange the roots for a TT petal. Thus a genuine witness always has detectable separate interiors or yields a base witness.

## Native polynomial calculus for bounded root degrees

If both root degrees are at most Δ, the ordinary Boolean PC degree on NO instances is bounded in terms of max(3,Δ). Use the relaxation and native simulation of weighted-pc.md, protecting radius three about all marks and both roots. The positive edge set is even and may branch only at the roots. Deleting the roots splits it into paths as above. Every vertex on a positive path within distance two of a mark or root has selector one because its incident products are all enforced. Thus the only vertices of an ST path adjacent to a root are its prescribed endpoints: any extra root contact would force a third positive edge at a nonroot. Every positive ST path shortens to an actual ST cycle of the same weight. Keep its endpoints and shorten by forward shortcuts on its ordered nonroot vertices. An internal marked h is nonadjacent to either root. Any chord incident to h or its path neighbors would be positive and would violate degree two at a nonroot vertex. Any shortcut skipping h thus removes both its neighbors; the root cycle it creates by shortening avoids its closed neighborhood. This contradicts absence of any marked spectator. All marks on the path have selector one, and no shortening introduces vertices.

On an original NO input, the base tests fail. Every selected marked nonroot vertex away from D and off a fixed positive ST path must be anticomplete to that path and the roots: an edge to a nonroot path vertex would be positive, contradicting its saturated degree. This would give a spectator after shortening. Every selected mark in D has a mandatory positive root edge, so belongs to some positive terminal path. Consequently if the relaxation attained the target, some positive SS/TT petal Q would have differently marked endpoints. Every internal vertex of Q is unmarked: if marked it would be selected by its positive path edges and then would be a spectator. Choose a positive ST path P. These paths have disjoint nonroot vertices. No endpoint touches any vertex of the opposite path because such contacts near the roots have enforced positive products, contradicting nonroot degree two. Shorten Q on its own vertices and P preserving its marks. Their endpoint-neighbor guesses survive trimming. Direct and singleton paths have no cross contacts. For two nonempty interiors, distinct components give a genuine pair with no cross edges and the same target; a shared component gives two true ST cycles of different weights as proved above. Both contradict NO.

Hence the bounded-width relaxation is inconsistent. Apply the original substitution into native PC from weighted-pc.md. No auxiliary pinning is assumed. The fixed bound uses bounded root degrees only to control the four neighborhood expansions of the bounded-treewidth separator torso and the widths of its local constraints. The polynomial-time decision result allows unbounded degrees at both roots.

The independent cycle-space/model checker check_high.py passed 157 fixed-seed no-spectator instances through 14 vertices. Both roots had degree above three in 46 of them; six used a proper buffer.
