# Constant native polynomial-calculus degree for weighted subcubic graphs

Let G be a simple graph of maximum degree three, let st be an edge and let M be any subset of vertices. Over GF(2) take Boolean axioms, pins x_s=x_t=1, guards x_i Σ_{j∈N(i)} x_j=0, and one prescribed affine equation Σ_{i∈M}x_i=τ. There exists an absolute constant D such that every unsatisfiable instance has an ordinary PC refutation of degree at most D. The constant supplied here depends on the treewidth-reduction constant for separators of size three; this argument does not give a small numerical value.

**Elementary cases.** Write H=G−st. If s,t disconnect in H, sum guards over the vertex set of the s-component. Internal edges appear twice and cancel, giving x_s x_t=0, contradicting the pins in degree two. If for any marked h outside the roots and nonadjacent to them there is an s-to-t path in H−N_G[h], take a shortest such path. Adding st gives a chordless cycle, and adjoining the isolated marked h toggles the parity, realizing either target. We may assume neither case occurs.

**Torso.** By the treewidth reduction theorem for bounded-size terminal separators, a set S containing every inclusion-minimal s–t separator of size at most three has a bounded-treewidth torso in H. Adjoin the two roots to S (include both in every bag; new torso edges away from the two roots are a subset of old torso edges). Each mark h is within distance one of S. This holds for roots and their neighbors; for other h, deleting N_H(h) separates the roots, since the additionally surviving isolated h cannot join their components. This neighborhood contains an inclusion-minimal separator of size at most three. See Marx, O'Sullivan and Razgon, [Finding small separators in linear time via treewidth reduction](https://www.cs.bme.hu/~dmarx/papers/marx-tw-reduction-talg.pdf), Lemma 2.11.

Expand S by four rounds of closed neighborhoods in H, obtaining K. Its torso has absolute bounded width W. At each enlargement each complementary component with boundary B has B a clique in a bag of the preceding torso. Attach there a bag with B and all new vertices of that component (at most 3|B| additions). All new edges and clique-completion edges are covered there; the intersection on old vertices is precisely B. Components with empty boundary may be handled separately. Add st to the torso, already covered by placing both roots together. Thus K contains the closed radius-three neighborhood of every mark in G (paths crossing st are covered since both roots lie in S).

**A bounded-width edge relaxation.** Keep x_i for i in K. On edges inside K use e_ij=x_i x_j; on crossing edges introduce free binary e_ij. For i in K impose the even sum of incident edge expressions. For each connected component of G−K impose the even sum of all its crossing edge variables. Retain the pins and the marker. Each outside component equation suffices to extend the crossing values to internal edge parities: on a spanning tree, process leaves to the root to meet each vertex parity; the final root condition follows from the crossing sum. The combined values give a spanning edge subgraph of degrees zero or two. Its component C through the forced edge st is a simple cycle.

Each vertex on C at distance at most two from any mark has x=1, since all its incident edges are inside K and it uses two positive edge products. If a mark h with x_h=1 lies outside C, then it has no neighbor on C. Indeed such a neighbor would have x=1, forcing its edge to h positive and placing h in the same component. Hence C could be shortened to a chordless root cycle avoiding N[h], contrary to exclusion of a marked spectator. Conversely every mark on C has x=1. Therefore C carries exactly the selected marks of the relaxation.

Every mark h on C and both its cycle neighbors are chordless on C in the specific sense that no chord of C is incident to any of the three. For a proposed chord touching one of them, both endpoints are at distance at most two from h and thus have x=1. Its edge lies in K and must be positive, contradicting that its endpoint already has two positive cycle edges. View C−st as an ordered s–t path P in H. Choose a shortest s–t path in the graph on V(P) using only increasing edges of this order. It is an induced path, since any chord gives a forward shortcut. Any skipped marked vertex h must have been skipped by one such shortcut across an interval. That interval also contains both neighbors of h on P: otherwise a shortcut edge touches h or a neighbor of h, which have no chords. (The roots are endpoints, hence are never skipped.) The retained path then avoids h and its entire neighborhood, because any further neighbor on P would be a chord at h. This gives a marked spectator, again impossible. Thus the shortened path with st is an induced even witness of the target parity. In particular on NO inputs the reduced system is inconsistent.

**Refutation and substitution.** Start from a tree decomposition of the torso and add x variables for all neighbors in K of any bag vertex, and the port variables for its crossing edges. A local constraint for a K-vertex now fits in any bag originally containing that vertex. Each component's crossing variables fit in a bag originally containing its boundary clique. For a vertex x_u the added bags lie in adjacent-vertex occurrence subtrees, each intersecting the original subtree for u; a port variable follows its endpoint's subtree. The enlargement thus preserves connectedness of occurrences and bounded width. Keep the tree binary by duplicating bags. Each mark is assigned one home bag containing its vertex. Introduce one Boolean accumulator at each node equal to the sum of the marks homed in its subtree, requiring the local linear sum of its home marks and child accumulators to equal its accumulator. Require the root accumulator to equal τ. Include node and child accumulators in that node's bag; widths rise by at most three. Denote the resulting maximum bag size by b, an absolute constant.

For completeness, bounded bag size yields bounded-degree Boolean PC for these local polynomial constraints. For a bag assignment σ use the multilinear indicator δσ, a product of its literals. A violated local polynomial p yields δσ=0 from p=0 by multiplying by those literals and reducing via Boolean axioms (since δσ p=p(σ)δσ). This costs degree at most b+2. For an impossible subtree assignment, either it violates a hosted constraint, or some child has no consistent feasible assignment extending the separator restriction. Sum the already derived indicators of all child assignments matching that restriction to get its separator indicator; this identity follows by expanding x+(1+x)=1. Multiply by the remaining parent literals to get δσ=0. The root sum of all its assignment indicators gives 1=0. Assign every constraint to one containing bag. This bottom-up proof has bounded degree independent of n and uses only polynomially many lines.

Substitute x_i x_j for each crossing e_ij and the sum of original home variables in the appropriate subtree for each accumulator. Internal K-rows become original guards. Component rows become sums of original guards on that component. The local accumulator equations become identities, and the root equation becomes the actual marker. A substituted Boolean axiom for an edge expression follows at degree four from the two endpoint Boolean axioms (expand (xy)^2+xy as y²(x²+x)+x(y²+y)); an accumulator Boolean axiom is a sum of original Boolean axioms in characteristic two. Multiplications by substituted quadratic monomials or linear forms simulate the original multiplication rules at at most twice the original degree, plus harmless constants for the initial Boolean derivations. Hence D=2(b+2)+2 suffices; no extension axioms are needed in the final derivation.
