# Weighted rooted even supports depend on the biconnected root block

Consider any simple graph with adjacent pins s,t, a weighted vertex parity, and native Boolean guards x_v Σ_{u adjacent v} x_u=0 over GF(2). If the block B containing st belongs to any class admitting a weighted rooted parity algorithm and a native degree D refutation bound, the whole graph inherits the algorithm (with polynomial overhead) and bound max(D,3). In particular only B itself need be internally subcubic. Degrees and treewidth elsewhere can be arbitrary.

If st is a bridge, sum the guards on one component of its deletion to obtain x_s x_t=0 and contradict the pins. Otherwise B contains a cycle through st, hence an induced root cycle by taking a shortest s–t path in B−st and inserting st. Every component H of G−B has at most one attachment vertex v in B: a path with ends at distinct B vertices and interior in H would enlarge the biconnected block. If H has no attachment, its every singleton can be selected along with a root witness. A mark there therefore realizes both marker parities.

For an attached connected H let d be its binary neighborhood vector from v, and w its restricted marker. Summing guards over H gives x_v d·x_H=0 since internal edges occur twice. Adding the charges to the corresponding full vertex guards gives precisely the internal B guards. Conversely each feasible assignment on B extends by empty sides. If x_v=0, every singleton on H is a legal side assignment. If x_v=1, its guards form the shifted system x_i((A_H x)_i+d_i)=0. The charge d·x_H=0 follows. Its solutions span the whole hyperplane: zero-defect singletons and induced paths between defect-one vertices with defect-zero interiors give all pair differences modulo the singletons. For connected H those pairs span the even subspace of defect vertices by decomposing any connecting walk at its successive defect vertices and shortening each segment. The empty side assignment always realizes weight zero.

An attachment v is present in every feasible B support exactly when v is a root or deleting v disconnects s and t in B−st. If deletion preserves connectivity, a shortest return path avoiding v gives a root witness omitting v. In the other direction any selected induced even graph containing st has a simple cycle through st since a finite Eulerian graph has no bridges. Its complementary path must meet the separator v. The forced vertex also has a native cubic proof: sum internal B guards over the s-component S of (B−st)−v, giving x_s x_t+x_v Σ_{i in S adjacent v} x_i=0. Using the pins first derive x_s x_t=1 in degree two. Multiply the cut identity with the product already replaced by one by 1+x_v and use x_v²=x_v, obtaining 1+x_v=0 within degree three. Root attachments already have that equation.

Therefore each nonzero side weight has these possibilities. If its attachment is avoidable, the side singleton toggles on an avoiding root witness. If v is forced and w differs from d, the shifted-hull result gives a toggling side. Otherwise w=d and this side contributes zero. To reconstruct a toggle at v=1 enumerate defect-zero singletons and shortest paths between defect-one endpoints with no other defects; some generator has weight one. When no side can toggle, use the original marker restricted to B. No oracle for multi-pinned subgraphs is needed.

On a NO instance each marked attached side has w=d at a forced v. Multiply the forced affine line 1+x_v=0 by d·x_H and add the known cut charge, deriving d·x_H=0 at degree at most three including the derivation of the forced pin. Sum the resulting side equations with the original marker to derive its B restriction. Simulate the B refutation, whose guards have already been derived at degree two. No new variables or degree growth proportional to the number or size of sides is needed.

A constant number of degree-four vertices inside B is a separate case. Detecting a chordless cycle through two specified nonadjacent degree-two vertices in a subcubic graph is NP-complete (Lévêque et al., Detecting induced subgraphs, https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.0971). Thus a generic subroutine for prescribed pairings after conditioning would need further hypotheses. This does not give hardness of weighted adjacent-root supports, which can include spectators or separate even components.

## Branching away from the marks inside the block

For fixed maximum degree Δ the weighted subcubic torso proof needs degree at most three only on the closed neighborhood of the mark set M within the root block. First handle a marked spectator by choosing an induced root cycle avoiding its closed neighborhood and toggling the spectator. Otherwise each mark is a root, neighboring a root, or has its open neighborhood in B−st separating s and t. All marks lie within distance one of the bounded-width separator set for size Δ with both roots adjoined. Expanding the torso to cover the closed radius-three neighborhoods of marks still gives width bounded as a function of Δ. Apply the same cut-edge relaxation and guard-sum simulation as for the subcubic case.

Its even edge extension can now have vertices of degree four or larger. Nonetheless every positive edge, including st, belongs to a simple cycle C of positive edges. Each vertex of C within distance two of a mark has selector one because its incident edges on C are internal products. If a selected mark h off C has a neighbor u on C, both selectors are one, so their edge is positive. This would give u a third positive edge, impossible since u has degree at most three and even positive degree. Hence h would be a spectator, contradicting the preliminary test. All marks of C have selector one. Further, a chord at a mark on C or either of its cycle neighbors has endpoints in the protected selector region, making it positive and giving its low-degree endpoint an impossible third edge. The monotonically shortened ordered return path therefore retains all marks by the original spectator argument. The relaxation decides the true parity. Bounded-width ordinary PC simulation gives degree bounded solely in terms of Δ. This argument permits arbitrarily many degree-four vertices within the root block when they and their neighbors carry no marks. Forced marks can first be removed, adjusting the target with the degree-three pin proof above.
