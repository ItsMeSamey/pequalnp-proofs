# Degree six for a single third pin

Use ordinary polynomial calculus over GF(2) with Boolean variables, root pins x_s=x_t=1 for an edge st, a singleton marker x_v=1, and vertex axioms x_i Σ_{j∈N(i)} x_j=0. Every unsatisfiable instance on a simple subcubic graph has a degree-at-most-six refutation.

**Branch and propagate.** For any pinned vertex i its guard implies its neighbor-sum as a degree-at-most-two consequence: multiply x_i+1 by each incident x_j and add the guard. At v, branch on all but one of its as-yet undetermined neighbors and use the neighbor-sum to force the last. This takes at most two queries. The number of assigned-one neighbors of v is zero or two. If it is two, call them a,b. At each of a,b its neighbor v is already assigned. Query at most one of the at most two remaining undetermined neighbors, forcing the last. Inconsistent fixed parities close immediately. On every consistent leaf, v and its neighbors are assigned; all neighbors of any assigned-one neighbor of v are assigned. The tree depth is at most four. A zero-neighbor vertex incurs no queries.

**Edge relaxation.** At a leaf allow one binary edge variable e_ij per edge and impose even sums at every vertex. Edges incident with assigned-zero vertices are zero, and edges between two assigned-one vertices are one. All these linear equations, after replacing e_ij by x_i x_j, have degree-at-most-two derivations from the restricted axioms and propagated pins. For example an edge pinned one uses x_j(x_i+1)+(x_j+1). Thus linear inconsistency yields a degree-two leaf refutation.

To establish completeness of the relaxation for deciding the unbranched instance, suppose an edge solution exists. Its nontrivial components are vertex-disjoint cycles since every degree is zero or two. Its cycle C through st exists as st is forced. Assigned-zero vertices lie outside C. If v has no selected neighbors, C avoids N[v] unless v is a root, in which case the forced edge already excludes that case. If v has two selected neighbors they and v belong to the same edge cycle, since both incident edges are forced. If this is a cycle other than C, C avoids v and its neighbors. In both situations shorten C to an induced cycle through st and adjoin v as an isolated vertex.

It remains that C contains v. If v is a root, shorten the s–t return path. If v neighbors a root r, r is assigned one. Its edge to v and st are forced (unless v is itself a root, just handled). All neighbors of r are assigned since r neighbors v, and any third neighbor must be assigned zero. A shortest return path on the vertices of C around the prescribed length-two segment therefore gives an induced cycle through all three pins. Otherwise write H=G−st. If s,t are connected in H−N[v], shorten a path there and use isolated v. If disconnected, the s-to-a arm and b-to-t arm of C around v have interiors in distinct components of H−N[v], because C cannot use zero-assigned neighbors of v. Each of a,b has exactly its two edge-cycle neighbors among the potentially participating vertices: every neighbor is assigned, and edges between assigned-one endpoints are forced. Consequently they have no chords to the opposite arm. The a,b adjacency could only occur if C were the triangle v,a,b, which cannot contain st in this case. Shorten the arms independently and recover an induced cycle on all three pins. The witness may change values queried during branching; completeness requires only a witness for the original instance.

Thus every leaf of an unsatisfiable input is linearly inconsistent or closes during propagation. To combine the two degree-d refutations with branch pins x=0 and x=1, multiply every line of the first by 1+x and every line of the second by x. The added branch axioms become Boolean axioms; ordinary variable multiplication and addition simulate the gated proofs at degree d+1. The final lines add to one. Iterating along at most four branch variables proves the bound.
