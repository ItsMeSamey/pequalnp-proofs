# Arbitrary bounded Tait-edge prescriptions on coned toroidal quadrangulations

Let H be a connected bipartite graph cellularly quadrangulating the torus, with simple facial boundaries. Insert a center and four spokes in each face. In the resulting triangulation prescribe nonzero binary two-vectors on at most k arbitrary edges, for fixed k. In polynomial time one can decide whether all edges can be labeled so that every triangle has three distinct labels. Equivalently this decides prescribed Tait edges of the cubic dual. For a native six-port opposite-shore rainbow instance, rejoin each equal-color pair and mark its joining edge. The result applies whenever the completed graph has a supplied dual of the indicated form. No global four-color potential, global canonical hue, or vanishing period is assumed. The disk variant below allows arbitrary interior marked edges as well.

## Reduction to a free group

For every quadrangle with a marked spoke, enumerate the colors on its four rim edges. There are at most k such quadrangles. Test its prescribed spokes directly by enumerating four spoke colors, enforcing distinctness on each of its four triangles. Shared rim edges must have consistent colors. All remaining pins now specify labels on at most 5k edges of H.

Let D consist of two poles joined by three parallel edges carrying the nonzero labels. Every H-edge labeling defines a graph map H→D by sending the two shores to the two poles. A facial four-edge boundary permits a choice of spoke labels exactly when its walk in D is null-homotopic, with no boundary-wide assumptions. Indeed the sum of its four rim labels must vanish by the triangle equations. Relative to a starting corner of color zero, the successive partial sums give four cyclic corner colors. A center can be assigned precisely when those corners omit a color; spoke labels are differences from that center. The only zero-sum words are monochrome or two pairs of equal labels. The alternating word on two distinct labels visits all four corner colors and is cyclically reduced in D. The other words omit a corner color and cancel by backtracking. Face centers are independent once the rims are labeled. Finitely many explicitly tested face choices can be restored at the end.

The map therefore extends continuously over the faces to a map from the torus to D. Its image on the fundamental group is cyclic or trivial: two commuting elements of a free group lie in a common cyclic subgroup. This also follows by their shared translation axis in the universal covering tree. The graph map consequently lifts to the cover of D associated with that image, a tree or a connected unicyclic graph with possibly infinite hanging trees. In the latter case its finite core circuit can have length two (parallel edges). This step retains edge labels even when they have nonzero binary periods.

## Compressing the possible target

There is an equivalent search over bipartite trees and unicyclic multigraphs K of size bounded in terms of the number of pins, equipped with a shore-preserving map K→D. Require a graph map H→K realizing the pins and sending every facial boundary to a null-homotopic walk. Composition gives the reverse implication.

For completeness retain in the preceding cover each pinned image edge and its endpoints. Keep the minimal connecting subtree in the tree case. In the unicyclic case keep its circuit and the minimal trees attaching the pins to the circuit. At least one edge may always be retained, including when no pins exist. Retract any remaining branch to its attachment point on even layers and a chosen neighbor of that point on odd layers. This graph homomorphism preserves the retained edges pointwise and preserves null-homotopy of facial walks. Its composition need not preserve the labels on unpinned edges, which are free.

Distinguish the endpoints of every pinned edge, all branching vertices in the retained graph, and one extra vertex on the circuit if necessary. The number of branching vertices is bounded by the number of leaves, and every leaf comes from a pin (apart from the harmless no-pin choice). Every chain between distinguished vertices with no distinguished interior can be shortened to one edge when odd and two when even. Keep every pinned edge as its own chain. The old chain maps onto the new one by traveling to the end with extra steps spent in backtracks, fixing the endpoint correspondence. A closed even chain can similarly map to a two-edge circuit, retaining the two parallel edges. Give each new edge any dipole label, keeping the labels of retained pinned edges. Bipartition is preserved. This gives K of bounded size and proves completeness of enumeration. One explicit conservative bound for k'≥1 pinned rim edges is 30(k'+1) edges: at most 2k' named endpoints, 2k' branching vertices and an extra circuit vertex yield at most 4k'+2 chains before doubling their lengths. No enumeration of long free-group words is required.

## Winding and tree extension for each bounded target

For tree K take every winding to be zero. For unicyclic K, its universal covering tree T has a bi-infinite axis with bounded attached trees; write τ for translation once around its even circuit. The supplied torus cell structure permits a polynomial-time integral homology basis with short generating walks. For clarity take a spanning tree of the primal graph and a disjoint spanning tree of the dual among the remaining edges. The two leftover primal fundamental cycles are integral generators: each omitted dual-tree edge can be eliminated using the face relation at a leaf of the dual tree, and after all such eliminations one face relation is redundant. Equivalently contract the primal tree and merge faces along the dual tree to obtain the one-face, two-loop torus schema. With a root on the primal tree the two based generating walks have length at most 2|V(H)|+1. Enumerate the images of these generators in π₁(K)=Z. Their absolute values cannot exceed their lengths. The remaining edge winding coefficients s_e can be computed by extending these two choices linearly, taking tree edges to zero and imposing zero signed sum on every face via dual-tree elimination. Each coefficient is at most twice the sum of the initial absolute values, since it is measured by a cut in the dual tree and either exceptional edge has just two incident faces.

Choose a lift of the root image among a finite set of deck representatives. With paths from the root in the primal tree as references, write y_v for the chosen lift of each image to T. For each oriented edge vw impose

    dist_T(y_v, τ^(s_vw) y_w) ≤ 1.

Reverse the signs consistently if using the opposite deck convention. All y_v lie within |V(H)| of the root lift for a genuine solution. This ball has polynomial size since K is bounded and T consists of periodic copies of bounded trees attached to a line; it is finite for tree K. Enumerate the values in this ball at every endpoint of a marked rim edge and at the root. Their number is constant for fixed k. At the root and named vertices require correct bipartition; for each marked edge check that its lifted endpoints, after applying the edge shift, are adjacent and project to the prescribed dipole edge label. This handles possible parallel edges of K without ambiguity.

For all other vertices start with that finite ball as a domain. Named vertices start with their singleton lifts. Propagate each edge constraint in both directions: intersect the domain at v with the closed radius-one neighborhood of the shifted domain at w. Domains stay convex subtrees of T. Reject if any domain becomes empty; iteration to stability is polynomial since each strict change removes a vertex from a polynomial-size list. Every true lifted solution survives.

Here stability is sufficient. Orient T toward one axis end, which is preserved by all deck translations. Each finite connected subtree has a unique vertex nearest this end, meaning that paths toward the end from every vertex of the subtree pass through it. This selector changes by at most one edge for subtrees at Hausdorff distance at most one: the endmost vertex of the closed unit neighborhood is one parent step above the endmost vertex of the subtree, and inclusion of connected subtrees orders their endmost vertices by ancestry. Applying the inclusions in both directions bounds the distance by one (equivalently both selected vertices lie in each other's parent cone at distance one). More explicitly, each selected vertex is a descendant of the other's parent. If they were incomparable, their shared parent would make each a strict sibling, but membership within one step of the other subtree then fails: every point of that subtree is a descendant of its endmost vertex. Thus they are equal or adjacent. The selector commutes with shifts. Apply it at each stable domain to get the required weak edge bounds. For finite T attach an infinite ray at a root and use its end; all shifts then act trivially.

Round each vertex whose selected point has the wrong bipartition by moving one step toward the same end. A pair of adjacent selected points is either already correct at both ends or moves at both ends, and the common parent map is 1-Lipschitz. A pair of equal points moves at exactly one end because the domain graph is bipartite and shifts preserve parity. Thus adjacent domain vertices now map to adjacent points after the shift. Correctly enumerated pins and root remain fixed. In the finite-tree case fold any added ray back over an incident tree edge, preserving parity and fixing the original tree. Artificial search-ball membership is no longer necessary after rounding.

Quotient by the deck action to obtain a graph map to K. On each facial boundary the shifts sum to zero, so its image lifts to a closed walk in T and is null-homotopic. This constructs all required edge colors. Finite ambiguity at the previously tested centers is resolved by retaining a passing local choice. On a simply connected quadrangulated disk only the finite-tree case is needed; the same proof works for labeled interior rim edges without cofaciality.

This theorem covers the stated coned class. A general toroidal triangulation need not have such a quadrangulation, and a bounded apex set need not yield disk pieces with solvable boundary data. In particular general bounded-rim four-precoloring of a near-Eulerian triangulation is Conjecture 3, rather than the unrestricted conclusion of Theorems 1–2, in [Dvořák, Moore, Seifrtová and Šámal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.13061).
