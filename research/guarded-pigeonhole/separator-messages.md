# Communication lower bounds across a native guard separator

Partition native guarded rows between two processors. Each knows its own input, fixed public wire names and the construction. Local computation is unrestricted; communication is measured in bits, counting representations of equations, inequalities, supports and certificates. Both results concern this partitioned model. A centralized solver reading both sides can handle the graph instances below with ordinary reachability.

## A linear-size bounded-degree family requiring Ω(r log r) interactive bits

Use 2r public vertex bits U_i,V_i. Alice privately receives a permutation π of the r indices and Bob a permutation σ. All directed graph vertices have one native row x_v(1+Σ_{w:v→w} x_w)=0 over GF(2). A selected vertex therefore has at least one selected successor. Conversely the vertex set of a shortest directed cycle is feasible in a loopless simple digraph: any additional arc internal to that cycle would give a shorter directed cycle. All zero is feasible before the marker.

Alice owns all U rows and r private chain vertices a_k, with k running from 1 through r. Her edges are

    U_(π(k)) → a_(k+1)      for k<r
    a_k → a_(k+1)           for k<r
    a_k → V_(π(k))          for every k.

Bob owns all V rows and an independent analogous σ chain, going from V_(σ(k)) to later U_(σ(j)). Consequently the first arm can travel U_i→V_j exactly when i precedes j in π; the second can travel V_j→U_i exactly when j precedes i in σ.

Alice also owns a binary selection tree with r private leaf vertices z_i, each pointing to U_i; all tree edges point from parent to children. The sole marker sets the private tree root to one. This forces some leaf and hence some U_i to be selected. Any feasible directed cycle in the two arms contains a U_i, and activating one root-to-leaf path at such an index extends the cycle to a marked model. The separate leaf vertices allow selecting a single branch even when multiple public U bits are one. For r≥2 the entire system has maximum row weight two, maximum column occurrence at most three, O(r) variables, and a singleton marker. The independent pivot normals give maximum hidden deficiency exactly one.

If π=σ, any journey from one public index to the next strictly increases the common order, and neither private chain has a directed cycle. A marked support cannot exist in a DAG. If π≠σ, take an inversion i,j. The two corresponding chain segments form a cycle U_i→V_j→U_i. Only U_i,V_j and these two private segments are needed, and their selected vertices have exactly one selected successor. Thus feasibility computes inequality of the two permutations exactly. The full separator normal dimension is 2r, not just an upper bound: Alice owns the U units, and can extract each V unit from its chain-row mask by canceling the next private chain unit when present. Bob symmetrically spans all U,V. Their remaining private normals are disjoint.

Every deterministic interactive protocol computing the marked decision has distinct transcripts on the r! equal-order inputs. A transcript of an interactive bit protocol defines a combinatorial rectangle, by induction on the sequence of speakers' messages. A rectangle containing two different equal-order inputs also contains their crossed pair, which has the opposite answer. Thus worst-case communication is at least log₂(r!) = Ω(r log r) bits, even with arbitrary rounds and exact local hull oracles. This bound concerns deterministic protocols; the restricted family computes equality, so public random fingerprints change the situation.

## Quadratic one-way bound and complete failure of affine-only summaries

In the one-way model Alice must send her summary before seeing Bob's choice of completion. The following direct array family makes the required information explicit. Alice owns r pivot rows

    U_i (1 + Σ_j A_ij V_j) = 0

for an r-by-r Boolean matrix A. On query (i,j), Bob owns the V pivots, imposes V_k=0 for k≠j by taking their masks zero with offset one, leaves V_j free, and uses marker U_i=1. Feasibility is equivalent to A_ij=1, by setting all other U to zero and trying V_j=1. Alice's summary must distinguish all 2^(r²) matrices, requiring at least r² bits in worst-case fixed-length or prefix-free encoding. Even imposing that no matrix row is zero leaves (2^r−1)^r choices. All those Alice relations have exactly the same full affine hull on (U,V): all (0,V) are feasible, and for each i one can turn U_i on at some V without turning on another U. Thus one-way communication consisting solely of any number of valid affine equations cannot distinguish even two suitable completions. Matrix support transmitted as full real inequalities or other data is charged in bits.

A bounded-degree version preserves the one-way information bound. Give Alice a directed acyclic private routing network from the U sources to the V sinks, with a U_i→V_j path exactly for each A_ij=1. Use one outward binary tree per row and one inward binary tree per column. Each leaf in either tree is indexed by the other coordinate; join the corresponding pair of leaves by a directed edge exactly when A_ij=1. The trees are disjoint, with U_i and V_j their respective roots. Bob owns only the outgoing rows of the V vertices, putting the single edge V_j→U_i and making all other V vertices sinks, with marker U_i=1. Every marked support contains a cycle and every such cycle traverses the Bob edge, so this works exactly when the desired Alice path exists. Choosing one path gives an actual model (there are no internal chords on that chosen tree route). Sizes are O(r²) and the row and column weights are bounded by two, apart from any optional observation rows below. If one requires the span of normals on each side to contain *every* U coordinate for every query, give Bob private unused observer pivots d_i with rows d_i U_i=0. They can always be zero and add one occurrence per U. Bob already owns every V pivot and Alice can extract each V unit from its two incoming unary tree-row masks. Thus the algebraic separator dimension can be fixed at exactly 2r, independently of A and the query. The latter bounded-degree construction establishes the communication lower bound; the full-affine-hull calculation above was for the direct matrix presentation.

The argument also gives the usual bounded-error one-way bound on this array family. Under uniform independent matrix entries and public coins R, let M be the message. A decoder using M,R guesses each queried bit with error at most ε<1/2. The entropy of a bit given M,R is at most the binary entropy h₂(ε), since it is determined by its guess and its error indicator. Subadditivity gives H(A|M,R)≤r²h₂(ε), so a worst-case fixed-length message needs at least r²(1−h₂(ε)) bits. This reasoning does not give a bounded-error interactive lower bound of the same order.

## Linear synchronous-round lower bound for full affine exchange

Consider the two-engine algorithm in which each engine computes the complete GF(2) affine hull of its own integral solutions projected onto all public U,V, subject to all currently shared affine equations. At each synchronous round they exchange their whole hulls and intersect. This permits arbitrarily wide equations and unlimited local optimization. Private auxiliary variables are not communicated. On the equal-order instances above this algorithm needs Ω(r) rounds even though its messages may have arbitrarily many bits.

Relabel the common order as 1,…,r. Suppose the shared equations pin U_i=V_i=0 exactly for i>s and do not constrain earlier coordinates. Initially s=r. For s≥3 the affine hull on Alice's side adds exactly U_s=0, and Bob's adds exactly V_s=0:

* A selected U_s on Alice's side would require a selected V strictly after s, since following selected successors through her finite private chain has to stop at such a V. Conversely set V=e_s and activate the private chain a_2,…,a_s (and no other chain vertices). Every nonempty subset of U_1,…,U_(s−1) can now be selected; connect the private selector root to any one of its selected U vertices through its private leaf. The nonzero vectors of a binary space of dimension at least two have full affine span. Thus any affine equation valid for Alice has zero coefficient on these U bits. For any live V with at least one 1 among V_2,…,V_s, set U_1=1 and activate the private chain from a_2 to the first such 1, leaving U_i=0 for i≠1. No internal chain vertex sees a second selected successor. These V project onto a set with full affine span in the s live coordinates (V_1 is free and at least two eligible V coordinates remain). Thus no further linear relation is possible.
* Bob has no marker. Every choice of live U is feasible with V=0, and each V_i for i<s can separately be turned on with U=e_s via the appropriate chain. V_s itself always requires a strictly later U. These witnesses imply exactly the stated projected affine hull.

The combined equations therefore reach precisely the same condition with s reduced by one. The exact algorithm has a nonempty shared space after each of the first r−2 rounds. In particular no constant-round affine feedback of this kind is complete. The bound also applies to sound local engines returning weaker affine consequences: their shared space contains that of the exact engines at each round by monotonicity. The round comparison is for boundary-affine messages only. Allowing arbitrary real inequalities as messages can transmit the support inequalities U_i≤Σ_{j>i}V_j and their Bob counterparts, which collectively kill every public marginal at once. It would be incorrect to extend the round lower bound to that larger interface.
