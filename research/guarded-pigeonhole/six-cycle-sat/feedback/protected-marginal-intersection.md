# Intersection of individual protected classes decides rooted feasibility

Let x(s),y(s) denote the classes of the two roots at physical mask s. There is one protected landmark per trajectory at every setting. Let X_i,Y_i be the binary physical-mask polynomials for x=i and y=i. The companion [common-chart Pfaffian identity](marginal-plucker.md) proves the divisibility E | X_i Y_j + X_j Y_i for i≠j, where E=∏(1+h_l). This is an ordinary polynomial identity; a Boolean quotient identity would not suffice. Under this identity the two roots can coincide in class at some setting precisely when their individually attainable class sets intersect.

Indeed, the forward implication uses the shared setting. For the converse suppose the roots are always separated, and choose x(s)=y(t)=i minimizing their Hamming distance d over all choices. Pin coordinates on which s and t agree and complement coordinates so s=0,t=all ones in the resulting d-cube. Restrictions preserve the identity: writing E Z_ij=X_iY_j+X_jY_i, setting one variable to zero selects its zero bit and comparing its quadratic coefficients selects its one bit. The quotient Z_ij is multilinear since every product has individual degrees at most two. Reversal of a mask bit replaces each multilinear factor P by h_l P(...,1/h_l,...) and fixes E. Here d≥1 and class i occurs for x only at zero and for y only at all ones, by minimality. Thus X_i=1 and Y_i=H, the product of the d variables.

For j≠i and any coordinate l, setting h_l=1 yields

    Y_j|_{h_l=1} = H_without_l X_j|_{h_l=1}.

The left side is multilinear, whereas the multiplier already uses every remaining variable. Consequently the restricted X_j is a constant and the restricted Y_j is that constant times H_without_l. The coefficient at mask u of X_j restricted this way is the XOR of the two x=j indicators at the ends of the l-edge with other bits u. Hence x can change only on edges incident to zero, and y only on edges incident to all ones. For j=i the same statements follow from the known support.

Each punctured cube is connected, including when only one vertex remains. Therefore x takes one fixed class J≠i away from zero and y takes one fixed class K≠i away from all ones. For j=J the exceptional edge difference for x equals one; the displayed identity makes the opposite exceptional edge difference for y equal to one, so K=J. For d≥2 any interior vertex now joins the roots, a contradiction.

For d=1 the root classes would exchange along one physical switch. The two settings differ by cyclically reconnecting three arcs. If they lie on three orbits, the flip merges them, violating the protected count. If on one orbit, count preservation leaves that orbit's entire set of occurrences intact. If on two orbits, the one with a single cut survives wholly inside a new orbit, gaining one of the two segments from the doubly cut orbit. Its old landmark remains there, and the doubly cut orbit's landmark must remain in the other orbit. Thus all changes of class across the flip go in one direction. Exchanging two root classes is impossible.

## Deterministic decision and witness

For a root and target class i, take the root together with the k−1 protected landmarks other than i and cyclically reconnect their k outgoing arcs. Subdivide repeated positions first. A setting gives one capped trajectory exactly when the marks hit all k original trajectories, equivalently when the root is in class i. Thus the positive polarity of the capped one-tour test gives this marginal directly. Use the deterministic physical one-tour feasibility oracle of cycle-parity.md (linear delta-matroid parity) on each of the at most 2k marginal networks. A disconnected untied capped network cannot have one tour. If k=1 every setting works. The two roots have a common achievable setting iff the marginal YES lists intersect. No extraction of a shared setting from two independent marginal oracle witnesses is required.

For witness recovery, repeatedly tentatively pin one still-free gate off, absorbing the fixed transition and preserving both landmarks and roots. Retain that pin if the new marginal lists intersect, otherwise pin on. The same decision theorem applies on each face. After all gates are fixed, trace the original routing and verify the roots share a trajectory. The procedure uses at most 2k(m+1) deterministic single-tour queries on networks of polynomial size for m gates; it is polynomial in k and input size. The companion identity supplies the required divisibility on every protected instance. The algorithm assumes the protected landmarks promise. The proof uses no GIM optimizer tie-breaking.
