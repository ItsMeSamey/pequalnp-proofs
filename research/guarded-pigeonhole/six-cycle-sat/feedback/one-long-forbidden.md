# One long forbidden-turn cycle is universal

At each independent switch name the input sockets I0,I1,I2 and the outputs O0,O1,O2 in cyclic order. The physical modes send i to i or i+1 modulo three. The missing local permutation sends i to i-1; following every missing turn in a closed wiring gives a permutation F of its wires.

Take a fresh switch and connect O1 back to I2 by a private wire. Its two other inputs are I0,I1 and its two other outputs are O0,O2. OFF routes 0 directly to 0 and 1 through the private connection to 2. ON routes 0 through the private connection to 0 and 1 directly to 2. Neither has a detached orbit. The external transfer is therefore the same fixed identity in both physical modes. When all missing turns are followed the switch routes 0 to 2 and 1 to 0, and the private wire forms one detached F fixed point.

Choose wires u,v from different old F cycles. Cut them and insert the box so its external physical identity restores each continuation. Keep any marks on the incoming side of the cuts. Then every original setting with either choice at the new switch induces exactly the same original trajectories after suppressing the subdivided paths. This is an assignment-wise and orbit-wise equivalence. On F, the two old continuations cross; this joins exactly the two old cycles into one, and adds precisely one private fixed point. All other old cycles are unaffected.

Starting with k cycles, attach k-1 boxes iteratively joining the growing old component to another original cycle. Ignore the new private fixed points. The output has one distinguished F cycle containing representatives of every old wire and k-1 additional private F fixed points. The construction is linear size and uses no tied controls. A rooted setting on the output projects by forgetting fresh bits, and every valid input setting has exactly 2^(k-1) valid extensions. For a one-cycle input no box is needed. The construction gives a polynomial equivalence between the unrestricted problem and the subclass with at most one F cycle longer than two. It does not establish hardness for either problem.

A distinct, elementary positive case is when F consists of a single cycle with no other cycles at all. Starting from the missing-turn setting, choose the physical turn at each as-yet-unchanged switch. Compared with its missing permutation, the two physical permutations are the two inverse triples. When all three sockets are in a single current orbit, one of these two orientations retains the orbit and the other splits it into three. Maintaining a single orbit to the end therefore joins any two marked wires. The private fixed points in the reduction above are essential to this distinction.
