# One unweighted closed join count recovers an open transfer count

The open input has n independent binary native switches and p external channels in each direction. The requested integer C counts masks sending a designated input s to a designated output t. An oracle for the closed problem returns the number of binary switch masks putting two designated wires on one circuit. All probabilities below refer to independent, uniform physical mask bits; neither paths nor detached circuits are counted as additional witnesses.

## Fix one return wire

Adjoin one deterministic private through-channel b, distinct from all old inputs and outputs. Write h for the resulting permutation of p+1 channels at any fixed open mask. Construct an exterior cap from outputs back to inputs with the wire t→b fixed (b here is the input of the private channel). On the p remaining outputs and p remaining inputs use a uniform random bijection k, labeling the full cap including its fixed wire by k. The closed return on inputs is k composed after h. Mark the fixed wire entering b and a dedicated last wire entering s from the cap. They are distinct. Deterministic wire segments can be concatenated, retaining these distinguished wire positions. For a nontrivial input those positions remain distinct whenever their orbits are distinct; if simplification coalesces them, their join event is constant one and can be evaluated directly. One may instead retain distinct marked wires using the identity padding described below.

Let c be the unique input mapped by h to t. Since h sends b along the private channel, c≠b. Conditional on h, the ideal closed permutation is uniform among permutations sending c to b. For c=s the two marks meet with certainty. Otherwise s,c,b are distinct. Contract the directed adjacency c→b in the cycle notation into one symbol. This gives a bijection to unrestricted permutations on p symbols, and the marks meet exactly when s and the contracted symbol share a cycle. In a uniform permutation of p≥2 symbols, two prescribed symbols share a cycle with probability 1/2: composing with their transposition interchanges joining and splitting. The case p=1 always has c=s. Averaging gives the ideal join probability J=(1+C/2^n)/2.

## Realize the cap modulo an odd integer

Let M=2^(n+2)·p!+1 and choose d with D=2^d the least power of two at least M. M is odd and exceeds 2^n. Its bit length and d are O(n+p log(p+1)). Every integer from 1 through p is invertible modulo M, as is 2. Fix any initial ordering of the p remaining output wires and any labeling of their destination inputs. Build an insertion shuffle. Starting at j=2 and ending at p, process the adjacent boundaries i=j,j−1,…,2, swapping tracks i−1 and i independently with probability (i−1)/i. The newly inserted label reaches position i with probability i/j and stops there with probability 1/i (at position 1 it stays). Hence its final position is uniform. Conditional on every bit of this insertion stage, the other labels undergo a fixed reordering of their former uniform permutation. Consequently their relative order remains uniform even conditional on the inserted position. Induction gives a uniform bijection.

For every rational bias a/e used here choose r to be the least nonnegative residue of D·a·e⁻¹ modulo M. Implement the actual swap with probability r/D. This is feasible since r<M≤D. For fixed open mask, expand its probability of joining the two marks as a sum over subsets of successful swaps of products of their individual success or failure probabilities. The expression is a polynomial over the integers in the independent swap biases. The substitution preserves its value modulo M because all involved denominators are invertible. The same holds after averaging over the n mask bits. Thus the physical cap has join probability congruent to J, regardless of how close its ordinary real-valued distribution is to uniform.

## Native dyadic swaps

A two-track element implements event E when its external permutation swaps the tracks exactly on E, and otherwise preserves them. A fair element uses one native gate whose off map is identity and on map cyclically shifts its three sockets. Connect its third output to its third input; follow the two exterior tracks. Off gives identity and on gives the transposition. A fixed crossing placed after an element implements the complement of its event without adding a mask bit.

To form the AND of two independent two-track elements X and Y, provide three rails labeled 0,1,2. Apply X to rails 0 and 2, then Y to rails 1 and 2; bypass the unused rail at each stage. Return final rail 2 to initial rail 2, retaining rails 0 and 1 as the external channels. Closing rail 2 deletes label 2 from the cycle notation of the three-rail permutation. If neither fires, or only one fires, deletion yields the identity. When both fire the product is a three-cycle and deletion leaves the transposition. Internal detached orbits cannot modify this external permutation. OR follows by complementing both operands, forming AND, then complementing its output. Each operand appears once.

Start with deterministic false (straight wires). Read the d binary digits of r from least significant to most significant, including leading zeros in the d-bit word. For a zero replace the element by its AND with a fresh fair element; for a one use OR with a fresh fair element. The update of probability q is q/2 or (1+q)/2. After d steps the swap probability is r/D. Wires can be simplified to remove closed wire-only circles, which carry neither a mask bit nor a mark. Each shuffle location has its own bits; the shuffle has p(p−1)/2 independent locations. Before optional marking pads, the whole construction contains N=n+d·p(p−1)/2 switches.

If the wire representation requires both marks to be literally distinct gate-to-gate wires, pad each marked position by its own one-track identity element. A native gate on sockets 0,1,2 uses its socket-0 external input and output; loop each of outputs 1 and 2 back to its own input. Both settings send the sole input to the sole output, and contribute just one unused fair bit. Insert one such gate on each marked connection, and mark its incoming wire. The two head gates are different, so the marked wires are distinct even after all other deterministic wire segments are suppressed. Include these two switches in N.

## Recover the integer

Let Z be the single closed oracle count. The physical probability is Z/2^N. Compute
`C = (2^n · (2·Z·(2^N)⁻¹ − 1)) mod M`
as its least nonnegative residue. This is the desired integer since 0≤C≤2^n<M. All integer sizes, physical graph sizes, and the decoder's arithmetic cost are polynomial in the open input length. The construction is a deterministic one-query counting reduction. It transfers hardness of deciding C>0 as well, without asserting any parsimonious reduction from an upstream SAT problem.
