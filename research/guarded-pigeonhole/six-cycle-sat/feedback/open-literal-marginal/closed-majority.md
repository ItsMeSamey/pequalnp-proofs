# Strict majority of native closed rooted joins is PP-complete

The question is whether strictly more than half the physical switch settings join two marked wires. The following polynomial-time many-one reduction from majority circuit acceptance does not apply to ordinary existential join.

Start with a circuit accepting S of its 2^n inputs, n≥1. Write AND using four exact-one triples on (not x,a10,a11), (x,a00,a01), (not y,a01,a11), (y,a00,a10), with output a11. These uniquely specify a 2-by-2 binary point mass at (x,y). Model NOT by literal complementation; force final literal z true by exact-one on (not z,not z,z). The m exact-one constraints have S satisfying extensions. Replace each by its ternary OR and three pairwise binary clauses forbidding simultaneous true occurrences. Use the native open-transfer compiler in README.md, each occurrence a distinct switch. Route OR3 through a full four-leaf binary fork and merge, its dead fourth fork output a private sink and unused fourth merge input a separate private source. The selected real route fixes four of six fork/merge bits and leaves two free. All variable menus and binary clause menus use both their switches. In a clause with t true occurrences, there are t choices of the true test and 2^(t−1) masks on the other true occurrences; each false occurrence is fixed off. For a satisfied exact-one triple the ternary clause has t=1 and the binary clauses t=1,1,2, so the open count is C=16^m S, with factors four each from the ternary menu and the binary occurrence choices. Repeated literal positions cause no exception. Denote the total open switch count by N.

Use the physical fair-signal AND, OR, NOT and insertion shuffle in closed-counting.md, here with ordinary probabilities. A two-track permutation encodes false/true by identity/swap. A native optional three-cycle with one private return supplies a fair signal. Two independent signals on (0,2) and (1,2) composed in series, with 2 returned privately, produce AND. Fixed crossing complements the signal; hence each dyadic r/2^d uses d fresh fair switches, starting false and running the binary digits least significant first, ANDing for a zero and ORing for a one. Constants may instead be deterministic.

Add an untouched spare b to the open patch, close its desired output t to input b, and uniformly permute the l remaining outputs back to the l remaining inputs. Mark inputs s,b using the marking convention of closed-counting.md. If h(s)=t the marks join; otherwise c=h^{-1}(t) differs from s,b and the uniform closed permutation has the forced step c to b. Contract it. The remaining uniform permutation joins s and the merged position with probability 1/2, by composition with their transposition. Ideal join probability is P=1/2+C/2^(N+1).

Insert item j into a uniformly ordered prefix of length j−1 by independently swapping adjacent positions at boundaries i=j down to 2 with bias (i−1)/i. Its stopping position is uniform by telescoping, and conditional on stage bits the remaining labels undergo a deterministic reordering of their old uniform order. Across all insertions there are J=l(l−1)/2 biases. Replace each by a dyadic within 2^(−d) for d=N+ceil(log2(max(1,J)))+10. Coupling gives total variation at most J2^(−d)≤2^(−N−10), hence this is also a bound on the change of join probability. Neutral marking pads add independent masks without affecting probabilities.

Let K=2^(n−1) and T=1/2+16^m(K+1/2)/2^(N+1). If 16^m(K+1)>2^N then S≤K and a fixed no-majority instance suffices. Otherwise 1/2<T≤1. Ideal P is separated from T by at least 2^(−N−2). Cut the marked wires of the approximate closed network to form its two-track signal; this swaps precisely on joining masks. AND with an independent dyadic signal approximating q=(1/2)/T within 2^(−N−10). Close by identity and mark its two closure wires. Its join probability differs from qP by at most 2^(−N−9). The ideal rescaled margin from 1/2 is at least 2^(−N−3), since q≥1/2. Thus strict native majority is equivalent to S>K. Network size, dyadic precisions and arithmetic bit lengths are polynomial. Membership in PP follows by sampling all switches fairly and tracing the marked circuit.
