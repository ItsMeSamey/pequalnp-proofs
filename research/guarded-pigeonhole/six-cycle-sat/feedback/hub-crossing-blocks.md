# Independent reduction to interleaving hub blocks

Cut the one fixed hub H into leaves. Assume both marks are directed H passages and all exposed components are free subcubic rotation networks. Write the cyclic hub leaf word, labeling each occurrence by its exposed connected component. If the occurrences of a collection U form one nonempty proper contiguous interval, the two hub passages between U and its complement can be replaced by two local seams closing the induced cyclic words. All physical switches on the two sides are independent. Reversing this operation cross-splices the two seam orbits; since they came from separate graphs these orbits merge and all others stay distinct. This identity holds for every setting.

For a query with both marks internally on one side, recurse with the same query there. Internal marks on opposite sides require two independent local mark-to-seam queries. With exactly one crossing mark, test the internal mark against its side's seam. If both marks are crossing passages return YES. These rules also apply to newly named seams at subsequent splits. Witnesses are combined independently and a side without a requested query may be filled arbitrarily. Each call on either child retains at most two marked positions.

Make a graph on component labels by joining X to Y when their occurrences alternate somewhere in cyclic order X,Y,X,Y. Partition into the connected components of this graph. These larger label blocks have noninterleaving occurrence sets. For distinct clusters U,V and any color C in V, each color of U occupies a single cyclic gap of C; two alternating colors must occupy the same such gap, so all U is in one gap of C. Equivalently C lies in a single gap of U. Alternation again propagates throughout V, which lies in one gap of U. The equivalence includes singleton colors via the unique cyclic gap convention. Any noncrossing partition with at least two blocks has an interval block: linearize at an arbitrary gap, take a block with inclusion-minimal first-to-last span, and note that every block meeting the inside of an internal gap between consecutive members of that block lies wholly in the gap, contradicting minimality. Deletion preserves noncrossing. Repeated interval splitting therefore leaves independent queried clusters. No unrestricted solver for a crossing-connected cluster is claimed.

If no two distinct colors alternate, every leaf problem has a connected exposed free graph. At hub length at least three any two distinct cap passages join via a simple path between distinct leaves, as shown in hub-tree.md. Length one is immediate. Length two is solved by the skew regular-path algorithm since the hub itself has deterministic degree two and the rest is free subcubic. Thus the noninterleaving class is polynomially recognizable and decidable with physical masks, regardless of component size, total hub degree or count. Its answer need not be YES when a two-leaf block occurs. The reduction also applies when larger crossing-connected leaf clusters are handled by separate valid algorithms.
