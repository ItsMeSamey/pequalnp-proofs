# Deterministic sparse-mask recovery

Suppose the explicit paired-Pfaffian construction in two-cycle-promise.md represents the successful physical masks by an F2 multilinear polynomial D in m variables with at most s distinct monomials. This applies under the physical promises in that note. When D is nonzero, choose any supported exponent vector, encoded as E=Σ 2^i bit_i. Among the first 1+m(s−1) primes, one prime p divides none of the nonzero differences from E to the other supported exponents: their product has absolute value less than 2^{m(s−1)} when there are other exponents. On substituting variable i by x^(2^i mod p), this term has a degree unique even modulo p and cannot cancel. The resulting polynomial has degree at most m(p−1). Evaluate at more than that many distinct points in a binary extension field. The stated primes are polynomially bounded in m and s; a sieve suffices. A binary extension of size exceeding that degree and polynomial in the degree can be constructed by enumerating irreducible polynomials of the corresponding logarithmic degree. A nonzero value gives deterministic coefficient-based recovery of a physical setting, verified by direct tracing. If s=1 the all-ones evaluation already suffices. With s supplied the algorithm takes polynomial time in the instance size and s. Failure to find a witness implies emptiness under the sparsity promise. The opposite sparsity promise (at most s unsuccessful masks) is handled by direct tracing on any s+1 distinct masks or all masks when fewer exist. Running both tests is exact when either side has size at most s.

In the hidden-word.md family the original cycles are always two and D has zero or one monomial, including after arbitrary rebasing of the physical controls. The explicit positive masks therefore admit deterministic polynomial reconstruction despite exponential lower bounds on the scalar minimum-orbit oracle.
