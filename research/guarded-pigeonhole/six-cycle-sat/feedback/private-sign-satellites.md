# Complementary signed bundles with a private false cycle

On three sites G,P,N use fixed return F=(G P) and one optional native r=(G N P). The off setting gives (G P)(N) and the on setting gives (G N)(P). Inflate P,N with arbitrarily long lists of positive and negative occurrences. Splice G sites into one ring and leave each remaining cycle private. Every switch setting is a complete truth assignment. Every true occurrence belongs to the common good ring, and all false occurrences of a given variable share its false-sign ring. Optional erasures from clause-satellites.md put removed occurrences on individual private cycles and preserve the truth of those retained.

Tap clauses through their satellites as in clause-satellites.md. For two clauses and fixed assignment and erasures the exact condition is that both retain a true literal, or they both retain an occurrence of the same false signed literal. This follows directly from their common-neighbor criterion on the orbit incidence graph. In particular overlapping variables of opposite polarity are harmless. For two clauses with a common signed literal the fixed-assignment interpretation can fail, though the two-clause formula itself is satisfiable by setting that shared literal true. The checker covers the contradictory unit pair, overlapping opposite polarities on two variables, repetition inside a clause, and the shared same-sign false positive. An extra isolated dummy site in each widget does not enter any query.

## Why every false occurrence cannot have a private orbit

Suppose a native network, possibly with hidden sockets and extra components, exposes G and three occurrences p,q,n. Require every setting to place only one sign on the G orbit, and each occurrence off G on an orbit containing no other of the four marks. Require some mask placing both positive copies p,q on G and some mask placing n on G.

Single-flip lemma: if a native gate meets the old orbit of an isolated mark n and n is still isolated after the flip, the projected permutation on the marks is unchanged. Rebase the current routing as the reference. The flip cyclically permutes three successor connections. If all three sites lie on n's orbit, no other marked orbit changes. If the sites belong to three distinct orbits, the flip merges them; both other orbits must be unmarked. If two lie on one orbit and one on a second, the flip cuts one interval from the twice-hit orbit and inserts it into the once-hit orbit. When the twice-hit orbit contains n, this can move n onto the other orbit only if the latter is unmarked. When the once-hit orbit contains n, the transferred interval must be unmarked. In either case the cyclic word of every other marked orbit is preserved. Both directions of the native rotation obey this description.

Choose a closest pair of masks A,B exhibiting the two required outcomes, respectively. Their first-return permutations on G,p,q,n differ on all four tails: a three-cycle on G,p,q with n fixed versus (G n) with p,q fixed. Every changed first-return tail needs a changed physical connection on its distinct old return segment. A single three-socket gate affects at most three such tails. Therefore no single flip at A can turn n good. For each switch where A and B differ, flipping it alone must also prevent p,q from both remaining good, by minimum distance to B. Each such flip changes the projection and leaves n isolated. The lemma shows that none of those gates meets the old physical orbit of n. Flipping them together therefore preserves that orbit, contradicting the required outcome at B.

This obstruction permits partial assignments and requires no fixed order inside the good bundle. It applies to a design promising a private orbit for each false occurrence on every setting. The positive three-site source instead gives one private orbit for the entire false bundle, allowing repeated occurrences to meet there.
