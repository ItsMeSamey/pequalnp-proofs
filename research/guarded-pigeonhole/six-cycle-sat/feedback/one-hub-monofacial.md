# A monofacial edge with one fixed loopless hub

Let J be a connected undirected multigraph, with one prescribed cyclic order at a LOOPLESS vertex H, otherwise free orientable rotations, J-H connected, and degree at most three at all free vertices. A graph without H is also allowed. At free u,v of degree at most two insert edge e=uv, requiring degree at most three after insertion (in particular u=v requires old degree at most one). A face means a boundary component of the orientable ribbon neighborhood. The opposite darts of e can lie in one face exactly when the bridge-block corridor between u and v in J meets a nontrivial block. Here the blocks are the connected components after deleting every graph bridge; a nontrivial block contains an edge, including a free loop or parallel cycle.

Inserting the new untwisted band between corners on distinct boundaries merges them, so e is monofacial; insertion on one boundary splits it into two. The terminal corners can be chosen freely. For coincident u=v of degree at most one there is only one incident boundary and its block is trivial.

Consider a degree-two vertex x in a nontrivial bridge block K. It can be incident to two distinct faces of K. If K does not contain H, a simple cycle through x can be made facial by setting its free rotations once at each vertex. A facial simple cycle has only one dart per edge, so its reverse dart at x belongs to another face. A free loop or a parallel digon counts as a simple cycle for this purpose. A fixed hub of degree at most one imposes no restriction. Otherwise all H spokes are nonbridges in J since J-H is connected and H has no loop. They are in one block K, and K-H is connected: any simple path in J-H between its vertices cannot leave K and then return across bridges.

If K contains H, first use any H-free simple cycle through x if available. If x has exactly one spoke, it is a leaf of K-H; pair its spoke with the next spoke at H and join their outer ends by a simple path in K-H. Two parallel spokes at x force K-H to be the single vertex x, so the digon works. In the remaining case x has no hub spoke and the two neighbors of x belong to distinct components of (K-H)-x (or there would be an H-free cycle through x). Both components receive hub spokes since the incident edges at x are nonbridges. Choose consecutive hub spokes switching sides in cyclic order. A simple free path between their outer ends passes through x. In each case one traversal direction of the resulting cycle respects the fixed hub rotation, and can be made a facial simple cycle while fixing every free vertex on it only once.

Suppose the corridor contains a nontrivial block K. The two corridor positions in K are the attachment vertices of the bridges toward u and v, or the respective terminal vertices themselves if in K. Both are free of retained degree two: H in such a block has no incident bridge, and each position needs one additional incidence for the bridge or for e. They are distinct because coinciding positions would need two additional incidences at a vertex already of retained degree two. Make one position incident with two different faces as above. Choose any corner at the other and then a corner on a different face at the first. These independently position the appropriate bridges or terminal ends. Glue the remaining bridge tree in arbitrary compatible rotations. Gluing a bridge to another component merges one boundary of each component and never identifies two prior boundaries in K. If a terminal further out does not belong to the face meeting its incoming bridge, then that terminal cannot meet the opposite terminal's face anyway. Thus u and v have available corners on distinct faces in the full J. Conversely, if all corridor blocks are singleton disks, gluing the bridge chain merges their sole boundaries into one containing every corner at u and v. Off-path components glued by bridges cannot separate that boundary. All steps are constructive in polynomial time.

For the routing application remove the two protected leaves from a connected free subcubic component and cap all remaining leaves with one fixed rotation. The cap creates no hub self-loop and deleting the hub leaves a connected graph of internal free vertices. Replacing the two protected leaves by e=uv cross-connects their sockets in both directions. Its two darts are cofacial exactly when the two-track open transfer was the identity. In the degenerate component consisting only of an edge with two terminal leaves the transfer is the transposition.

The assumption on H is material. A triangle H,u,v with a loop at H interleaved between its two spokes still has connected J-H. The extra uv edge is bifacial in all four endpoint rotations despite the absence of bridges. The checker check_monofacial_bridge.py includes the counterexample. The leaf-capping construction cannot create this type of loop.
