# A randomized rooted solver when every setting has two trajectories

There are m independently optional triple rotations, arbitrary fixed reversible feedback, and two marked arc occurrences. Assume **every** physical setting has exactly two closed trajectories. Then rooted common-trajectory decision and witness search are in RP. The promise concerns all settings, rather than just the optimum.

**Physical masks as monomials.** For any fixed reconnection of passive cut arcs let P(h) be the sum, in characteristic two, of the monomials ∏_{i:s_i=1} h_i for the settings s yielding a single trajectory. If the graph of untied transitions is disconnected, P is identically zero. Otherwise construct one untied reference tour, T its selected individual swaps, and A its binary interlace matrix as in cycle-parity.md. For each physical setting take X to be the symmetric difference of its chosen individual swaps with T. The binary Pfaffian of A[X] is precisely its single-tour indicator, including the empty principal matrix whose Pfaffian is one.

The following construction keeps physical mask labels the same even when different reconnections use different untied references. Assign gate i two scalar weights w0,w1, normally 1,h_i respectively. If T meets the gate's two indices evenly, one physical setting retains both and the other neither. On the ordinary matrix A scale one designated row and its column by the retained-both setting's weight. Perform all such scalings before adding the bypass edges. Then put an additional symmetric bypass contribution of the retained-neither setting's weight between those indices. If T meets the pair oddly, attach an auxiliary index adjacent to both ordinary indices, weighting the edge to each by the physical setting that retains the opposite index. A term in the augmented Pfaffian chooses a bypass or an auxiliary edge in exactly the way required by its retained principal set; ordinary matching edges cover all retained indices. This gives Σ_s Pf(A[X(s)])∏_i w_{s_i} in characteristic two. In particular the free weights produce P in the stated physical convention. An odd matrix dimension gives zero. Numerical evaluation takes polynomial time, either by alternating elimination or by taking the unique characteristic-two square root of the determinant. The field extension affects only the evaluation points; coefficients and trajectory semantics stay binary.

**Two named roots.** Reconnect their two outgoing continuations crosswise and form P for this fixed crossed network. Crossing merges two distinct trajectories and splits one shared trajectory. Under the two-trajectory promise P therefore indexes precisely the separated-root settings. The polynomial

    Q(h) = ∏_i(1+h_i) + P(h)

indexes exactly the original joined-root settings. If the crossed network is untied-disconnected, P=0 and every original setting passes. Use a direct trace for identical root positions, m=0, or a produced witness. Multiple requested marks on the same arc may also be placed at distinct points of a private subdivision before any reconnection, without altering the original trajectory memberships.

Choose independent uniform field values in a binary extension of size q≥4 max(1,m). A nonzero Q of total degree at most m is missed with probability at most m/q. One nonzero evaluation gives a witness deterministically: retain that numerical point for all remaining variables. Test the zero-mask-bit branch using weights (1,0) at that gate; use (0,1) for a forced one bit. In the universal-product term each forced gate contributes one. If the zero coefficient branch is nonzero keep it; otherwise the one branch is nonzero because the parent equals Q_zero+h_i Q_one. Keeping a branch means imposing its indicator weights on all subsequent evaluations. At the end trace the actual original permutation with those physical switches and verify that the named arcs agree in orbit. There are at most m+1 polynomial evaluations, with no fresh failure probability during recovery. Return no witness on a zero evaluation. Independent retries reduce false negatives. All positive answers have a directly validated witness.

## Protected landmarks with any number of trajectories

Suppose k≥2 supplied landmark arcs represent distinct trajectories and together exhaust the trajectories **for every physical setting**. The roots need not be landmarks and may lie on any trajectories. For each omitted pair i,j of landmarks, take the remaining k−2 landmarks together with both roots and reconnect the outgoing arcs at these k cuts by one fixed cyclic permutation. For a setting with exactly k trajectories, that cap produces one trajectory if and only if its k marks lie on distinct trajectories. If a trajectory has no mark it survives separately; if each has one mark the cap joins all k. When the roots are on different trajectories, exactly one omitted pair works, namely their two landmark classes. When the roots are together no pair works. Thus Q=∏(1+h_i)+Σ_{i<j} P_ij(h) indexes joined-root masks, with canonical physical coefficients for every cap. The same RP search applies with O(k²) Pfaffian evaluations per polynomial query. Separate repeated root/landmark positions by passive subdivision before capping.

There is a complementary fixed-k variant when a supplied set Z of positions intersects each trajectory oddly for every setting. Sum over all (k−2)-subsets of Z as the auxiliary marks beside the roots. A separated-root mask then occurs with multiplicity ∏ |C∩Z| over the remaining trajectories, an odd number. A joined-root mask still contributes zero. This takes O(|Z|^{k−2}) cap computations and requires the exactly-k promise. If instead k−2 specified landmarks are guaranteed on distinct trajectories avoiding both roots, successive transpositions joining those trajectories into the first root's trajectory leave exactly two trajectories and preserve original root equality.

**Checking the landmark promise.** This property itself has a coRP test, without assuming an initial cycle count. For k≥2 choose at most k(k−1) cyclic permutations of the k marked continuations so that each ordered distinct pair y,x occurs as an immediate cyclic step y→x in some cap. Fix any one original routing. Cutting the landmarks leaves some untouched closed trajectories and a permutation σ mapping each marked input to the next mark along its old trajectory (with consistent input/output naming). If each trajectory contains one landmark, there are no untouched cycles, σ is the identity, and every such cap gives one trajectory. If some original trajectory is untouched, every cap leaves it separate from the marked arcs. Otherwise, if σ is not the identity, take x with σ(x)=y≠x; a cap τ with τ(y)=x produces a fixed point of τσ, so its k>1 marks cannot form one cycle. Consequently the landmarks work for every routing exactly when each cap polynomial equals the universal mask polynomial. A nonzero difference yields a specific counterexample setting by the same recovery routine; directly trace that setting and check the landmark counts on all original trajectories. A supplied valid family is always accepted; an invalid one is detected with probability at least 1−m/q using one sampled field vector for all caps, since at least one fixed cap has a nonzero difference. For k=1 the single original cap must universally have one trajectory and the rooted answer on that promise is immediate.

The coRP recognition can erroneously accept an invalid certificate; its probabilistic acceptance is not a proof of the promise or of a negative rooted result on unrestricted inputs. The RP rooted solver verifies every returned witness in the original instance independently of the promise.

Run python3 check_promise_pfaffian.py for independent numerical checks in GF(256). It compares canonical mask coefficients and field evaluations with 1,017 enumerated masks, checks 70 two-orbit fixtures and 23 protected-landmark fixtures, and validates 50 recovered witnesses. Separate nonconstant examples exercise k=2,3,4,7 with root and landmark occurrences sharing original positions.

## Few lost cycles give a track bound

For an ordered word on n tracks with fixed final feedback, let d=n−min_s cycles(π_s). Every point moved by a permutation lies on a cycle of length l≥2, contributing l−1≥l/2 to that loss. Thus every setting moves at most 2d tracks. Under independent fair switch choices, each track in the union H of the triple supports has image point masses at most one half: it stays put up to its first potentially acting gate, which splits its location equally; later averages of permutations cannot increase the largest point mass. The fixed final feedback also preserves this bound. Any untouched point moved by the feedback has movement probability one. It follows by expectation that |H∪support(feedback)|≤4d. All other tracks are isolated fixed points. After handling named points on isolated tracks, enumerate binary initial words on the remaining at most 4d labels, and propagate their reachable binary words through the optional switches. The one-displacement feedback equality decides whether the roots can share an orbit; at intermediate passive layers one can first cyclically rebase at a common seam for seam roots, or record the marked bit constraints at their actual positions. For arbitrary arc positions, inserting an explicit bit flip on each of the two marked arcs and testing closed binary consistency gives the same common-orbit predicate: each cycle must see even flip parity. This yields deterministic 4^{4d} poly(n,m) time. On the exactly-k promise d=n−k, complementing the two-trajectory and landmark algorithms.
