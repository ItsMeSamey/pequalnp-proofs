# Three-orbit controls with unbounded whole-gate cut rank

For each L≥5 there are connected physical YES and NO switch networks of minimum orbit count three and whole-gate cut rank Ω(L) in every layout and reference. Neither admits an odd weight on all optimal trajectories, even if weights may be placed at all physical positions. The YES network has exponentially many accepting and rejecting masks in its gate count. Its roots are in different factors and each of three local choices can change their answer.

**Join operation.** In disjoint reversible networks cut one passive arc in each and exchange the successors of those arcs. Exactly the two orbits through those cuts join; all others remain intact. For a chain of three factors use two distinct passive cuts in its middle factor. The composite orbit count is the sum of the three separate counts minus two. Roots in opposite outer factors share a composite orbit exactly when (i) each outer root belongs to its local attachment orbit and (ii) both middle attachment arcs belong to a single middle orbit. Apply the two joins consecutively to obtain these statements even when the middle cuts lie on the same orbit.

**First factor.** Use the four-track five-gate alternating A4 example in check_optimum_odd_obstruction.py. Place the first root and first attachment on different seam tracks, say 0 and 1. Attainable permutations include the identity, which separates them, and (012), which joins them. This factor has minimum two. Its seven certified optimal cycles give a binary sum of zero of their incidence vectors, with odd cardinality.

**Middle factor.** Use the four-track two-gate examples in two-port-tropical-cap.md, with middle attachment tracks 2,3. In both twins the minimum is two. In the YES twin both gates on join the attachments; the other three masks separate them. In the NO twin all four masks separate them.

**Last factor.** Take the directed triangular torus network from physical-cutrank.md. Its underlying digraph is connected and balanced and has a directed Euler tour. Set each site's default bijection from input sockets to output sockets according to successive arcs of that tour. Thus all gates off produce a single orbit. At one designated site there are three distinct arrivals on this tour. Of the two possible orientations of a three-way rotation, one reconnects the three resulting directed tour segments individually to themselves. Order the gate's three tracks to allow this orientation. Switching that gate alone produces three orbits. Put the torus attachment and second root on original wire arcs located on two of these three segments. The torus root test then passes with all gates off and fails with just the designated gate on. This choice uses only fixed socket permutations, optional rotations, and passive arc cuts.

The composed minimum is 2+2+1−2=3, attained by independently optimizing each factor. Fix both external factors of the A4 obstruction at any optimal masks and perform the joins. Whenever one of the seven A4 trajectories traverses its attachment, it acquires the same foreign detour; otherwise it is unchanged. The attachment occurred in an even number of the seven supports. Every foreign position is consequently covered evenly, as are all original positions. Odd masses on these seven optimal composite trajectories would sum to one on a zero vector. Subdividing passive arcs inserts identical incidence columns and leaves this contradiction intact.

**Mask multiplicities.** Replace each torus gate by four successive independently optional copies of its rotation on the same triple. Their products realize each of the three rotation exponents modulo three at least five ways, by the binomial coefficients of order four. Thus every exhibited torus setting, including both the accepting and rejecting examples, lifts to at least 5^(L²) physical masks. The total gate count is 4L²+7. A single orbit remains possible and minimum one is absolute. No change to the A4 or middle switches is needed. This amplification uses equivalent physical encodings of aggregate rotations; it does not assert exponentially many distinct aggregate torus permutations.

**Width.** Start with an untied Euler reference in the original torus circuit. Leave the three extra copies at every site straight when extending this tour: their endpoints occur on the lifted single tour, but do not affect the cyclic order of the original transposition occurrences. Join that tour to one untied Euler reference in each of the other factors by the physical splices. On the torus original chords the composite interlacement matrix restricts to the torus interlacement matrix, by erasing the inserted excursions and extra chord occurrences. Restrict an arbitrary whole-gate layout tree to the original torus gate leaves and suppress degree-two nodes. Some cut of the restricted tree has rank Ω(L) by physical-cutrank.md. It lifts to an edge of the full tree, whose cut matrix contains the corresponding cut matrix as a submatrix. For any fixed physical whole-gate partition the cut-rank formula in that note is independent of reference, so the lower bound applies to every reference.

The positive control genuinely uses the wide factor and the obstructed factor, as changing the local setting of either can change the answer with the others fixed. The series joins and repeated copies are explicitly decomposable. The construction demonstrates simultaneous failure of the width and odd-mass parameters; it gives no computational hardness claim and does not rule out recognizing the decomposition.
