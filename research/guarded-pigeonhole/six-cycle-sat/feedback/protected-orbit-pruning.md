# Preserving separated orbits while joining two marks

Consider any finite reversible routing network in which each independent binary gate chooses between a fixed bijection of three input sockets to three output sockets and its cyclic rotation. Fixed wiring and private feedback are arbitrary. Marks designate physical wire arcs. A setting partitions the arcs into directed orbits.

Let a,b be marks that can share an orbit. Let D be any collection of protected arcs, each of which is separated from both a and b at every setting. Given any setting y, there exists a setting x joining a,b which preserves the entire directed y-orbit of every member of D, including its transitions and arcs.

Choose a joining setting x at minimum Hamming distance from y. Toggling any differing gate alone from x must split a from b, since otherwise the result is a closer joining setting. Such a gate has at least two input passages on the orbit C carrying a,b. Indeed, with at most one passage it cannot separate two points of C: cutting C at most once leaves its whole old circuit as one contiguous segment, including its marked arcs.

Fix d in D. Its x-orbit E is disjoint from C. If a differing gate touches E, it uses precisely two passages on C and one on E. Cutting these three passages leaves two C segments and one E segment. Either cyclic rotation of the three successors makes one C segment a cycle and joins the other C segment to the entire E segment. Since the toggle splits a from b, the rejoined segment contains one of them. Thus d shares an orbit with that mark in the toggled setting, contrary to protection. No differing gate touches E. Changing all differing gates therefore preserves E arc for arc and transition for transition. This holds simultaneously for every d, and finishes the proof.

Under the supplied universal-separation promise, trace the orbits of D at y and pin every gate they visit to its y setting. The theorem proves equivalence of the resulting and original joining queries for a,b. Fixed gates are replaced by their selected local bijections. The protected directed cycles can then be deleted. Partial fixed settings already present may be absorbed into the wiring before using the theorem. This is a polynomial-time reduction per application. It assumes the promise and supplies no general polynomial-time recognition of protected arcs.

## Exact graph of pair-only terminal behavior

Suppose every setting on a specified set of marks has at most one nonsingleton block, and any such block is a pair. Form a graph H whose edges are the attainable pairs. If ab is an edge and a vertex d is adjacent to neither a nor b and distinct from them, d is universally separated from a and b. If d had a neighbor c, take y joining c,d. Pruning gives an x joining a,b and simultaneously preserving c,d, contrary to the pair-only hypothesis. Thus d is isolated. Among nonisolated vertices, equality or nonadjacency is consequently an equivalence relation. For transitivity, two nonadjacencies with a common vertex forbid adjacency of their other endpoints. Hence H is a complete multipartite graph together with isolated vertices. In particular it cannot be an induced four-vertex path.

Every graph of this form is attainable. Begin with one permanent cycle per mark, keeping isolated marks separate. The following selector places a fresh pointer in the orbit of any one of a nonempty list of distinct target pointers t_i, with all choices attainable and no change to the orbit partition on old pointers. Process targets in order. Start x_i,b_i on their own fresh two-cycle. A fixed three-cycle on output labels (x_i,b_i,t_i) moves x_i from immediately before b_i to immediately before t_i. For i after the first, an optional three-cycle (x_i,t_i,x_{i-1}) moves x_i from there to immediately before the preceding pointer. Previous pointers can lie in the same orbit as t_i. In either case this operation only deletes and reinserts x_i, preserving the old circular orders. By induction x_i can designate any target up through i. The selector output is its final x_i. All fixed permutations between optional gates can be absorbed into the fixed wiring.

Use one selector for each nonempty part of the nonisolated graph, obtaining pointers p_i. Use two further selectors, with fresh private points, on the same list of p_i to obtain Q and R. Finish with a fixed transposition on Q,R. If they select different parts it merges two distinct cycles, containing exactly one original mark each. The two representatives can be chosen independently in their respective parts. If the two selectors choose the same part, their pointers lie in the same one-mark orbit and the transposition splits it, which joins no marks. Empty edge sets need only the permanent cycles. The construction admits the all-singleton partition and uses linearly many tracks and gates. The classification describes attainable marked pairs, with no condition on how many additional unmarked orbits are present.
