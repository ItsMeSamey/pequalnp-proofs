# Odd-port trees admit one face for every fixed hub order

Cut a single fixed loopless hub into distinct labeled port leaves. Suppose the exposed components are trees with freely reversible rotations at degree-three vertices and unique rotations at degrees one and two. Every component has a positive odd number of port leaves. Other unmarked twigs are allowed. Then some setting of the free rotations places all hub passages on the same face, for any prescribed global cyclic order of the hub ports, even when ports of different trees interleave. In fact the completed connected ribbon graph has precisely one boundary circle. A setting can be found in polynomial time.

Use the equivalent model in which each peripheral port stays at the end of its tree and an extra spoke connects it to the hub. Suppressing this degree-two subdivision after completion has no effect. With just one spoke present per tree, the entire graph is a tree and has one face.

To specify the restoration order, distinguish one permanent port in each tree. As long as the tree has at least three active ports, prune twigs absent from the minimal subtree spanning those ports and suppress degree-two vertices in that subtree. Root it at the permanent port. A deepest branching vertex has two terminal children other than the permanent port. Delete those two ports from the active list as a pair and repeat until just the permanent port remains. This construction depends only on the trees. In the unpruned tree, the two terminal paths from the branching vertex may have inert side twigs but contain no other active ports. When a pair is deleted, the whole pendant region of the tree containing those two paths has only one attachment toward the surviving active tree. For three active ports the surviving tree can be represented by its single permanent spoke and the path out to the branching vertex.

Now restore pairs in reverse deletion order, interleaving operations on different trees arbitrarily while respecting each tree's own order. Keep the single face of the partial completion. The two new spoke ends at the hub have fixed positions on its boundary according to the prescribed cyclic order; if they occupy one old corner, their relative order in it is fixed. At the tree, the excursion through the inactive pendant region meets no current hub spokes. Its two new port locations therefore occur consecutively relative to the hub ends. Reversing the cherry fork swaps their order without changing previously wired paths. Inert branches may be oriented arbitrarily and only subdivide this excursion. Choose the port order making the two pairs of band endpoints alternate on the single face. Adding the first untwisted spoke splits this face into two; the alternating order puts the ends of the second spoke on different faces, and adding it merges them back to one. This proves the induction and constructs rotations by tracing the two possibilities at each step. A fork used in an earlier restoration has active descendants; the currently pendant cherry contains no such earlier fork on its two inactive sides. Polynomial work by direct boundary tracing suffices.

For a connected completion of a forest of trees the cycle rank is the sum over trees of (number of its spokes minus one). Consequently an odd total cycle rank precludes one face in any orientable rotation. The odd-per-tree condition above is sufficient; this parity observation does not characterize mixtures of even-port components.

## A unicyclic five-port obstruction

Take a five-cycle with vertices labeled 0 through 4 in cycle order and put one port leaf at each vertex. Its thickening is an annulus, with free choice at each trivalent vertex of which side receives the pendant port. In one direction the external port permutation lists the occupied labels in increasing cyclic order; on the other side it lists them in decreasing cyclic order. An empty side gives an interior face, not a port. When all ports lie on one side, the only external five-cycles are (01234) and its inverse. For the prescribed hub order κ=(01243), their composites with κ have respective cycle partitions (02)(14)(3) and (034)(1)(2). When both sides are occupied, the external permutation has exactly two cycles on five labels and is odd. Its product with the even five-cycle κ is odd and cannot be a five-cycle. No setting places all hub passages on one face, despite the odd port count. This claim concerns hub passages; it does not mistakenly count a detached port-free face as an exposed passage.

Run python check_odd_tree_hub.py. It traces 1,000 seeded assemblies of odd-port trees with shuffled global hub words, and compares both possible cherry orders during constructive restoration. On all 32 rotations of the five-port cyclic example, 23 give two exposed hub cycles, two give three, and seven give four.
