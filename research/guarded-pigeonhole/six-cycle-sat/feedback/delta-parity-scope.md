# Which delta-parity optimization is being used?

For a twisted alternating representation D and a partition of its ground set into pairs, Geelen, Iwata and Murota deterministically minimize the number of pairs containing exactly one feasible-set element. In particular a minimum of zero asks for a feasible union of entire pairs, including the empty union when feasible. The representation may be over any field; a zero diagonal is required in characteristic two. Their Section 5.2 gives quartic time in field operations. This is the theorem used in deterministic-universality.md. [Geelen, Iwata and Murota](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~jfgeelen/Publications/deltamatroidparity.pdf)

The objectives “minimize the weight of split pairs” and “maximize the total weight of selected whole pairs among perfect solutions” are different. Koana and Wahlström discuss both before Theorem 5. They give the former a fast randomized solution via delta-sum representation. For the latter, their Corollary 6 supplies randomized pseudopolynomial search time O(W n^(ω+1)) for positive integer weights bounded by W. Their conclusion poses a strongly polynomial bound for weighted perfect delta-parity and a deterministic algorithm for maximum-cardinality linear delta-matroid intersection as open questions. The deterministic weighted *ordinary* linear matroid parity result of Iwata and Kobayashi concerns matroids rather than this weighted-perfect delta-matroid objective. [Koana and Wahlström](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol327-stacs2025/html/LIPIcs.STACS.2025.62/LIPIcs.STACS.2025.62.html) · [Iwata and Kobayashi](https://doi.org/10.1137/17M1141709)

**Specified split flags, with other flags free.** Twist one endpoint of each pair prescribed split so that the prescribed pairs must now all be unbroken. For every unconstrained endpoint introduce a separate fresh mate. Append to the representation on all fresh mates the delta-matroid of all subsets of the one required parity. That parity equals the parity of the transformed feasible sets, since the constrained pairs now contribute even size. The skew matrix with ones above the diagonal and minus ones below represents all even subsets: every even principal Pfaffian is one by expansion along its first row. A one-element twist represents all odd subsets. For zero fresh mates handle an incompatible parity directly. Keep the constrained pairs, and pair each unconstrained old endpoint with its new mate instead of its old partner. A zero-broken parity solution exists exactly when the original flag pattern extends. The old part of a returned solution, untwisted, supplies a witness.

**Deterministic minimum weight of split flags.** Let B consist of the sets of split-pair indices of feasible F. This family is a delta-matroid. Indeed form the direct sum of even-parity gadgets on the triples (old endpoints, new pair flag). Take its delta-sum with D padded by unused flag indices. Restrict all old coordinates to zero, so the two terms agree there and the surviving flag is exactly the XOR of the two old bits. This nonempty restriction is a delta-matroid minor. Only the abstract delta-sum closure is needed; its randomized representation is not computed. [Delta-sum closure](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol327-stacs2025/html/LIPIcs.STACS.2025.62/LIPIcs.STACS.2025.62.html)

Process flags in descending absolute penalty, using the deterministic extension test to choose the preferred bit whenever feasible. Relative to an optimum consistent with the fixed prefix, if its next bit disagrees with an extendible preferred bit, symmetric exchange toward a witnessing extension flips that bit and at most one later bit. The earlier absolute penalty dominates, so another optimum has the chosen prefix. This proves the greedy algorithm correct for arbitrary signed split penalties given ordered comparisons and exact arithmetic for the objective. It optimizes weights of broken flags only and supplies no deterministic weighted activation optimizer.
