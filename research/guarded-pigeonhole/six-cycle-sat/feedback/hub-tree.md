# Incidence-tree normal form for rooted routing

Use one-long-forbidden.md followed by the one-gate fixed-free cap of one-long-fixed-free.md. Skip optional preprocessing for raw selfloops. In the rotation graph each original switch has three parallel incidences with the long fixed hub H. Each added merge gate B has two incidences at H, as all subsequent splices avoid its private fixed wire. After the one-gate cap C replaces that wire, a degree-two white vertex joins B to C; another degree-two white vertex attaches twice to C. Hence the simple graph obtained by suppressing multiplicities is a tree, namely one hub, a leaf for each old gate, and one arm H--B--white--C--white per added merge. Maximum incidence multiplicity is three. Mark both original wires at their original input sockets, which remain incident to H. The degenerate case of one starting cycle needs no arm. The distinct preprocessing using larger loopless caps need not preserve this tree.

Read the cyclic order at H as a circular word in letters occurring either three times (old switch, allowing the two cyclic 3-rotations) or twice (merge branch, deterministically transposing its two leaves because its tail lies beyond a bridge). Thus even this representation retains the general independent-switch problem under an assignment-wise projection. Cyclic order at the unbounded hub is part of the data.

When opening the hub gives a connected free subcubic rotation network, two distinct marked hub passages always join for hub length at least three. If the passages are i to i+1 and j to j+1, at least one of the leaf pairs (i+1,j) and (j+1,i) has distinct entries. Choose a simple undirected path between those leaves and set each internal free cubic rotation along the path. Its physical boundary includes both marked hub passages after closure. The general canonical construction may have arbitrarily many open components.

Independent mask/structure checks on 826 networks cover all instances of one and two original gates and 100 sampled four-gate inputs. They include the one-cycle case and repeated use of the same splice representative.
