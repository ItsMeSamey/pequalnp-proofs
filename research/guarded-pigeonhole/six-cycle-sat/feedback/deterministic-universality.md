# Deterministic universality of a disjoint-tag binary Pfaffian

Let B be a binary alternating matrix. Put an independent variable h_i in the two symmetric positions of a distinct tagged edge i, with all tagged edges vertex disjoint. Write P(h) for the Pfaffian of this pencil. There is a deterministic polynomial algorithm to decide whether P(h)=∏(1+h_i). This is an identity of formal polynomials. The comparison applies directly to the raw one-tour construction in cycle-parity.md: the tagged mask may complement different individual physical bits depending on the reference, but universality over all masks is invariant under this relabeling.

Substitute 1+x_i for h_i. The right side becomes ∏x_i, and the new constant matrix B' is B plus all tagged edges. Denote the vertices not covered by a tag by U. The coefficient of the full x-monomial is Pf(B'[U]); if zero, identity fails. Otherwise eliminate the nonsingular U block. On the tagged endpoints this gives the alternating Schur complement C over GF(2). For every omitted set R of tags, the coefficient of the x-monomial containing all other tags is Pf(C[endpoints(R)]), because deleting the chosen tag endpoints leaves B' on U and the omitted pairs, and Pf(B'[U])=1. Hence identity holds precisely when no nonempty union of full pairs is nonsingular in C.

For each designated pair p={i,j}, first test whether the two columns C_i,C_j are independent. This is necessary for a nonsingular principal set containing p. If independent, extend them to a column basis T of C. The principal matrix C[T] is nonsingular: the alternating form on the quotient by its radical is nondegenerate, and these coordinate vectors descend to a basis of that quotient. Principal pivot on T represents the twist by T of the feasible principal sets. Delete i,j from the pivoted matrix, and give the remaining representation twist T minus p. For any remaining set K, feasibility now means exactly that K union p was nonsingular in C; this follows by symmetric differencing through the two twists. Apply deterministic linear delta-matroid parity to ask for a zero-broken-pair feasible set of the remaining pairs. Some designated pair succeeds exactly when identity fails. This uses at most one parity call per pair and requires neither weighted optimization nor enumeration of every mask.

The deterministic algorithm of Geelen, Iwata and Murota is for minimum number of split lines in a linearly represented delta-matroid, including twists. Its Section 5.2 gives O(n^4) field operations; characteristic two uses alternating matrices, with zero diagonal. Their Claim 5.18 also uses the column-basis principal-nonsingularity fact above. [Geelen, Iwata and Murota (2003)](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~jfgeelen/Publications/deltamatroidparity.pdf)

## Rooted and landmark consequences

On the promise of exactly two trajectories for every original mask, cross the roots and test universality of its one-tour polynomial. Joined-root settings exist exactly when universality fails. An untied-disconnected crossed graph has zero polynomial and immediately fails universality. Recover a mask by repeatedly fixing a physical gate off if the constrained crossed instance still fails universality and on otherwise, rebuilding the fixed wiring and the raw reference for each query. Trace the recovered original routing directly. This upgrades the RP result of two-cycle-promise.md to deterministic polynomial time.

The same test upgrades the universal supplied-landmark recognition there to deterministic polynomial time without a promise. Test each ordered-successor cap separately against its full envelope. A nonuniversal cap gives a directly recoverable violating mask. With one protected landmark per trajectory for general k, the earlier O(k²)-term sum of Pfaffians is still a randomized search algorithm; the single-pencil test does not directly compare a sum of distinct pencils.

## Three protected landmarks in one pencil

When k=3, its protected-landmark rooted search does admit a single pencil. The following formulation works at k=2 as well. Start by applying the fixed root transposition. Add k independent auxiliary individual swaps, one from each landmark to one of k fresh successive cuts along the same root carrier. Use distinct passive subdivisions wherever cuts coincide. Consider only settings in which exactly k−2 auxiliary swaps are taken. If the roots are separated, the root swap merges their two original cycles, and exactly one choice of omitted landmark pair leaves the other k−2 original cycles to be merged, one apiece, into the carrier. If the roots are joined the root swap splits a cycle and there are too few remaining swaps to obtain one tour. In either case k−1 total transpositions would have to merge on every operation to reach one tour. Thus summing exactly-k−2 auxiliary settings in characteristic two gives the separated-root indicator for each physical mask.

Build the untied reference tour using both the physical pairs and these individual auxiliary transpositions. Use the usual disjoint tags only on physical pairs. For each auxiliary index j outside the reference twist, designate its ordinary matrix index as a socket. For a twisted auxiliary index instead attach a new socket j' by a unit edge to its ordinary index. Add two mandatory selector vertices with no edge between them. Connect them to the k sockets by the columns (1,0),(0,1), and, when k=3, (1,1). A pair of sockets taken by these selectors has total matching coefficient equal to its two-by-two determinant, always one. Exactly these two auxiliary swaps are omitted. At a twisted socket, matching the socket externally retains the ordinary index, and leaving it free forces its unique edge to that ordinary index; this enforces X=S symmetric-difference T. Consequently the augmented matrix is one raw disjoint-tag pencil with the required coefficient support. Apply deterministic universality and physical self-reduction as above.

The two-selector construction here uses only k≤3. Beyond that, four columns with all two-by-two determinants equal to one violate the binary Plücker identity. The earlier randomized sum-of-caps remains available. Relatedly, if more than one protected point on a particular orbit I is supplied, collapse the other protected classes into a single orbit with representative j. A fixed cap (i r)(j a b), with i,r on I, yields one tour exactly when a,b lie on different arcs of I cut by i,r. This conditional result does not presume the extra interleaving in general.

## Verification

check_deterministic_universality.py independently compares the raw tagged coefficients against all masks, including the actual Schur complement, mandatory-pair pivot, deletion and twist construction. Its delta-parity subroutine enumerates feasible unions on small instances as a stand-in for the published polynomial-time algorithm. It verifies 960 arbitrary pencils, 869 physical cap identities, 364 landmark recognitions and 56 directly traced witnesses. check_three_landmark_selector.py verifies every physical coefficient in 144 protected fixtures. Neither checker implements the augmenting-path parity algorithm.

For any number k of protected landmarks, the individual omitted-pair cap tests also compute the graph of attainable separated class pairs by deterministic ordinary one-tour feasibility. If a proper coloring of this graph with at most three colors is available, merge all landmark trajectories within each color by fixed transpositions. Each merge connects distinct protected trajectories, so the color groups become protected superclasses and no originally separated root pair is identified. The three-class procedure is now exact. One simple sufficient condition is that the individual attainable class lists for the two roots overlap in at most one class. Give that class its own color, and give the two exclusive lists separate colors. The lists can be obtained by merging every class other than a tested i and comparing the root to i in the resulting two-cycle system. This is a conditional deterministic extension; arbitrary attainable-pair graphs are not asserted to be three-colorable.
