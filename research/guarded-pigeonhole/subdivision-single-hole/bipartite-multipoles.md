# Boundary signatures from one matching

Consider a bipartite multipole with shores A,B. Every internal vertex has total degree three counting dangling halfedges, and every dangling halfedge has a prescribed color in {1,2,3}. There are no other color prescriptions. Reject repeated colors at a vertex or any imbalance n_A(c)−n_B(c)≠|A|−|B|, where n_A(c) counts c-colored dangling halfedges on A. Indeed after using those terminals, the internal c-edges must perfectly match the remaining opposite shores.

Suppose a base color c contains all but at most two dangling halfedges. Find a perfect matching of the induced bipartite subgraph on vertices carrying no c-terminal; if none exists, the pattern cannot extend. Use that matching and the prescribed c-terminals for the c class. Remaining vertices have degree two counting dangling ends. Ordinary cycles are even, and paths pair remaining terminals. Two terminals on such a path need equal colors exactly when they are based on opposite shores; here same-vertex terminal pairs have zero internal edges and need different colors. The remaining total is even. If there are two terminals, the two other colors must have the same numerical A-minus-B imbalance. For both terminals of one color this implies opposite shores; for distinct colors it implies the same shore. Thus every matching found extends to a proper coloring by alternating on paths and cycles. The same argument holds per component even when the multipole is disconnected: no component can have exactly one residual terminal.

Whenever there are at most five prescribed terminals, one color meeting the base-color condition exists if the balances hold. The three color multiplicities have equal parity because their signed imbalances are equal. For 5 terminals they must be 3,1,1; for 4 they are 4,0,0 or 2,2,0; for 3 they are 1,1,1. At 2 or 0 the conclusion is immediate; 1 cannot meet the balance equations. Consequently all at most 3^5 boundary patterns of a five-terminal bipartite multipole have a polynomial-time feasibility and witness oracle. This does not count internal completions.

For arbitrarily many terminals given as distinct tips on a single disk boundary in a planar embedding of the multipole, the following sufficient condition on a base color likewise gives a complete decision for that prescribed pattern. After ignoring c-terminals, encode each remaining terminal by its shore bit XOR its prescribed color bit (the other two colors now labeled 0,1). Require that these bits alternate in cyclic boundary order (or there are no remaining terminals). For any candidate c-matching, the remaining paths yield a noncrossing terminal pairing; every chord in any noncrossing perfect pairing has endpoints of opposite cyclic index parity. Thus every path pairs opposite type bits, exactly the required alternation condition. Every available c-matching works. If this boundary test fails, the rule abstains rather than rejecting a possible coloring. General unbounded edge-precoloring extension is hard even for planar cubic bipartite graphs. [Marx](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.20085)

If a closed cubic instance is supplied as a partition into bipartite blocks each with at most five boundary edges, compute every Boolean boundary signature as above. Compose these finite factors along a supplied bounded-width incidence layout for the interface variables, storing witnesses for backtracking; a width-w primal tree decomposition gives 3^{w+1} poly(input) time after the local computations. The internal block graphs may have arbitrary width. This is a decision and witness algorithm; multiplying Boolean signatures would not recover the number of colorings. A one-block bipartite graph has no terminal colors and reduces to the usual matching followed by alternating cycles.

## Projected path decisions for an arbitrary boundary

Fix any base color c, with valid balances and a feasible internal c-matching on the required vertices. Use polynomial forced/excluded-edge matching tests to find edges absent from every such internal matching (red-impossible) and edges present in every such matching (red-forced). Start the possible residual graph from all internal edges except red-forced edges, attaching distinct terminal tip vertices for the non-c halfedges. Let R contain all internal edges lying on a simple path between any two distinct such tips. This set or a polynomially computed superset can be obtained from the usual block-cut forest: keep each biconnected block or bridge on paths between the tips and include its internal edges. If two tips meet one vertex the terminal path may contain no internal edges.

Compute one feasible red matching M0 and project onto R the ordinary binary cycle space of the allowed internal red graph (on red-matched vertices, omitting impossible edges). Differences of red matchings lie in that space, since each vertex has even difference degree. Thus all red patterns on R belong to the affine space M0|R plus this projected space, of rank r. Enumerate its at most 2^r elements. Pin presence or absence for every such edge, respecting additionally the predetermined forced and impossible edges, and find a red matching extending the pattern or skip. For each successful matching alternate the two other colors on residual paths and cycles, testing prescribed endpoint colors. All actual terminal paths lie wholly in R, and their remaining edges are determined by the R pattern; a different matching with identical R bits cannot change any pairing. Thus one completion per viable pattern suffices, even though exponentially many internal matchings might project onto it. This gives exact decision and a witness in 2^r poly(input), without a bound on terminal count or any embedding hypothesis; r can itself be unbounded. It counts neither red matchings nor all 3-colorings.

The plain one-matching extension theorem is sharp already on six ports: on a six-cycle with one terminal per vertex prescribe 3,3,2,2,1,1 cyclically. Every color individually has a unique valid internal matching and every shore balances, yet two of those matchings require the same edge, making the pattern impossible.
