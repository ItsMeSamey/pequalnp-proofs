# Six-end disk extension with opposite-shore color pairs

Consider an embedded bipartite cubic multipole with up to six boundary halfedges on its disk rim and prescribed colors from the three nonzero elements of the binary two-space. Test local color clashes. Work independently on each connected internal component with its induced cyclic boundary order. Terminal-free cubic bipartite components are three-edge-colorable by successive perfect matchings. A component with fewer than six ends is covered by the matching argument in matching-multipole.md. For a six-end component, each color's A-minus-B end count must equal |A|−|B|. Its three multiplicities are even, hence are 6, 4+2, or 2+2+2. In the first two cases choose the most frequent base color, leaving at most two terminal ends, and use the exact matching extension. In the last case either all ends lie on one shore (see same-shore-six.md) or every color occurs once on each shore. We handle this last case here.

**From edges to a hued patch.** Join all halfedges in rim order at one exterior apex, designating its dual face as outer. The boundary xor is zero, so an extending edge coloring would yield a nowhere-zero binary two-space flow including at the apex. A bridge in the capped primal immediately rules this out. Otherwise the dual is loopless, all inner faces triangular, and each internal dual vertex has even degree since its primal face walk avoids the apex and is bipartite. The six outer corners correspond to distinct dual vertices: in a connected bridgeless plane graph a repeated incidence of one face around the apex would make it a cut vertex, whereas deleting it leaves the connected component. Face differences of a proper dual four-coloring give the three edge colors; conversely a nowhere-zero flow integrates to such a coloring with one arbitrary starting value.

A proper three-hue assignment on the dual can be computed directly. Orient an internal edge from A to B in the primal, orient each apex incidence consistently at its ordinary holder, and give every arc the unit ternary flow. Ordinary divergences vanish modulo three. The apex divergence also vanishes by summation. Integrating this flow on faces gives dual hues with adjacent differences ±1. On a traversal of the outer dual face all terminal edges at A holders have the same signed hue increment, opposite to that at B holders. The prescribed boundary four-colors are the successive prefix xors of the edge labels. If the patch model requires simplicity, for any parallel dual edge pair discard the open digon on its nonouter side and one duplicate edge. No outer vertex lies strictly inside. The triangular faces of the retained side and its proper hues persist. Any different colors at the two attachment endpoints extend throughout the deleted region by the Four Color Theorem and a permutation of the colors; for reconstruction store each removed piece and reinsert in reverse order. This step does not claim to preserve counts.

**The lift.** In triangular grid coordinates use the six steps ±(1,0), ±(0,1), ±(1,1), with hue x+y modulo three and four-color (x mod 2,y mod 2). For each nonzero prescribed edge color, its two opposite grid steps have that color difference and opposite hue increments. Starting at a grid vertex matching the initial hue and four-color (or translating the hue/color origins), each boundary step is determined. Since the two occurrences of each color have opposite-shore holders, the lifted walk uses all six directions once and closes. This constructs the viable boundary homomorphism.

**Unit hexagon lemma.** Every closed walk using the six directions exactly once is contained in a center and its six neighbors. Write z=x−y. In each of the coordinates x,y,z only two steps can increase the coordinate, so its range is at most two. Translate the minima of x and y to zero. A center (X,Y) must satisfy

    xmax−1 ≤ X ≤ 1,   ymax−1 ≤ Y ≤ 1,   zmax−1 ≤ X−Y ≤ zmin+1.

These intervals admit an integer solution unless xmax−zmin>3 or ymax+zmax>3. Interchanging x,y exchanges the exceptions. In the first case necessarily xmax=2 and zmin=−2, so (0,2) occurs, ymax=2, and zmax≤0. Reaching xmax and ymin now forces (2,2) and (0,0). These three vertices are pairwise distance two along straight grid lines. A closed six-step walk through them must take three unique two-step straight segments. This repeats directions, a contradiction.

**Decision and reconstruction.** The single-hexagon hypothesis is now satisfied. Corollary 15 of Dvořák, Moore, Seifrtová and Šámal gives the fixed-boundary linear-time decision on the hued patch. For reconstruction, their Corollary 14 deletes vertices of the center hue, yielding the equivalent three-precolor extension on a plane bipartite graph. The at most six precolored vertices are on the original disk rim. Theorem 1.4 of Dvořák, Král’ and Thomas supplies a quadratic-time extension algorithm for triangle-free graphs on a fixed surface with boundedly many boundary vertices. Color the deleted hue class by the central color, undo any digon reductions, and difference adjacent dual colors to recover the original edge coloring. Bounds quoted for the source subroutines do not prescribe the complexity of any particular implementation of all preprocessing. [Single-hexagon definitions and Corollaries 14–15](https://research-explorer.ista.ac.at/download/19879/19887/2025_EuropJournCombinatorics_Dvorak.pdf) · [Three-coloring triangle-free graphs on surfaces VII, Theorems 1.3–1.4](https://thomas.math.gatech.edu/PAP/sedmy.pdf) · [Constructive Four Color Theorem](https://thomas.math.gatech.edu/FC/fourcolor.html)

The independent checker enumerates all 720 mixed 2+2+2 orders and tests all candidate centers explicitly. Exactly 540 orders admit one center, 144 admit two and 36 admit three. Comparison profiles also show why this geometric lemma is needed only on this branch: 12 of 90 same-shore 2+2+2 orders, 54 of 180 opposite-balanced 4+2 orders, and 6 of 20 balanced one-color orders have no such center. The checker verifies the lattice assertion, not an implementation of either published precoloring algorithm.
