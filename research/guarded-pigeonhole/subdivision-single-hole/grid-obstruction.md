# Bounded-occurrence obstruction to uniformly low flux and mixed leaf costs

On an L by L grid put one variable at each position. For every square add all four distinct positive ternary clauses on its three-corner subsets. There are n=L² variables and m=4(L−1)² clauses, no duplicated clauses and no repeated variable in a clause. Maximum occurrence count is twelve. Each grid edge's endpoints lie together in a clause.

For any occurrence partition of flux at most h delete its at most h middle vertices from the incidence graph. Mark each deleted variable's grid position and all four positions of any deleted clause's square. At most 4h positions are marked. If L>4h, every row and every column with no marked position is connected by surviving incidence paths; all those rows and columns lie in one component since they intersect. Outside their union are at most (4h)^2 grid positions. A surviving clause touching any surviving giant-component variable belongs to that same component. Since each position has bounded degree, only O((h+1)^2) occurrences can lie on the other shore (including deleted vertices and their incident edges). The component's occurrence edges all belong to the same shore. Thus every width-h cut has a small side.

Weight a coarse tree leaf by its number of grid occurrences. If every leaf has weight at most one third of the total, a weighted centroid vertex yields an incident edge with each side between one and two thirds (choose the largest incident weight at an internal centroid). A centroid leaf, if present at the equality endpoint, gives the same conclusion directly. For L sufficiently large relative to h this contradicts the small-side bound. Therefore some leaf has more than one third, hence all but O((h+1)^2) occurrences by its own cut, including the full-input single-leaf case.

Its triangle deficiency is at least the number of touched clauses minus the number of all grid variables, which grows quadratically. To lower bound stabilized quadratic rank choose the flux of an all-true assignment with one true witness per clause, chosen outside the leaf for every split clause. Every split variable in the leaf is fixed true, so its guard vanishes. Every full variable remains free. Full clauses have two independent witness directions. A partial clause of local size one has witness identically zero, and one of size two has one free direction. Every nonzero polar row of an unfixed variable lies in its own variable times clause tensor summand by strictness. Such rows are independent, and the remaining rows vanish identically. No affine absorption changes the rank. Except for O((h+1)^2) boundary occurrences and those incident to split variables, all rows at the giant leaf are of the full-variable/full-clause kind. Its residual rank therefore grows quadratically too. These are positive, easily satisfiable formulas; the claim concerns the layout certificate.

## Embedding the obstruction in an NP-hard input family

Take a connected bounded-occurrence strict SAT input F on separate variables. Attach the positive grid by a single strict clause (u OR v OR z), where u belongs to F, v to the grid and z is new. Grid-all-true satisfies the attachment, so satisfiability is equivalent to F. Then attach to every variable x a private satisfiable ternary parity gadget x+a_x+b_x=0, encoded by the four distinct strict clauses forbidding its four wrong assignments. Each original truth assignment extends to the gadgets, and all variables now have both polarities and bounded occurrence. For any nonempty variable set S, its members touch four private clauses per gadget represented, and each gadget contains at most three members of S. Hence |N(S)|≥4|S|/3>|S| and there is no nontrivial matching autarky. Standard bounded-occurrence strict SAT can be obtained from arbitrary ternary SAT by occurrence splitting, a directed cycle of binary implication clauses on clones, and replacing each binary clause C by the two strict clauses C OR z and C OR not-z with fresh z; start with distinct variables in each source clause.

Every satisfying output still lacks a uniformly bounded mixed layout as L grows. Weight only the grid occurrences in the centroid argument. A width-h cut in the full incidence graph also splits at most h grid incidence vertices inside the grid subgraph. Thus some leaf contains all but O((h+1)^2) grid occurrences regardless of the size or placement of F. The deficiency lower bound follows by Hall on the touched grid clauses and their grid neighbors alone.

For the rank bound, choose a global satisfying truth assignment with grid variables all true and choose a permissible witness assignment. Call a grid variable exceptional if it has any outside grid occurrence, is a split variable, is the attachment vertex v, or any of the variables or clauses of its private gadget is split across the leaf boundary. If a grid variable is not split and has a grid occurrence inside, all its other occurrences, including one in each private clause, are inside; therefore a nonexceptional private gadget is wholly inside the leaf. Only O((h+1)^2) grid variables are exceptional (each split vertex belongs to at most one private gadget). Work inside the leaf fiber of the global assignment. Further restrict all non-grid variables outside the nonexceptional private gadgets, exceptional grid variables and their gadget bits to the planted satisfying values. Fix witness choices of all nongrid clauses outside those intact gadgets to the planted choices. For every split grid clause choose a planted witness outside the leaf. All remaining guards are from positive grid clauses or untouched strict private clauses. The grid guards with fixed grid truth bits vanish since these are true; the other nonzero quadratic rows, including the intact private gadget clauses, have independent variable-times-clause polar coefficients. Zero-direction witnesses of singleton split clauses are zero by construction. No new affine rejection or absorption occurs in this restricted fiber, and quadratically many internal grid rows remain. By rank monotonicity under affinely nonempty restriction, the original giant-leaf fiber has at least that residual rank. This proves the obstruction on satisfiable members of a satisfiability-preserving NP-hard family. It does not assert equivalent widths on unsatisfiable outputs or forbid preprocessing that deletes the recognizable planted substructure.
