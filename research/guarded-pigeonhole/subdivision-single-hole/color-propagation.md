# Degree controls for two-bit graph coloring and binary direction maps

Use GF(2) and Boolean coordinates. To require a linear map into the binary plane to send every listed direction to a nonzero vector, take the directions e_v for graph vertices and e_u+e_v for graph edges. These are the reduced incidence columns of the cone on G, fixing the cone root's potential to zero. The e_v constraints exclude the zero color, and the edge constraints exclude equal colors. Every coloring is a map and conversely. If G has an edge the map necessarily has rank two. The cone root is a gauge choice, distinct from fixing a particular original vertex to a named color. The cone matrix has a row with no special role after the root row is deleted; direction weight is at most two even when the cone-root vertex has high graph degree.

After shifting both vertex bits by one, use x_v y_v=0 and the three indicators I_1=x_v, I_2=y_v, I_3=1+x_v+y_v. The strict one-hot condition is indispensable: over GF(2), three ones also sum to one. On legal assignments the three indicators are disjoint. The direction-map edge guard is
$$q_{uv}=(1+x_u+x_v)(1+y_u+y_v)=\sum_{c=1}^3 I_c(u)I_c(v)+x_u y_u+x_v y_v.$$
Thus the aggregate edge axiom is a degree-two linear combination of the standard three collision axioms and the endpoint vertex axioms. No reverse simulation is needed for a lower bound.

Lauria–Nordström, Theorem 1.1, gives bounded-degree non-3-colorable graphs on n vertices whose one-hot PC refutations over every field require degree Ω(n). Their three-indicator sum and exclusivity formulation allows affine elimination of I_3. In the other direction, substitute x_v=I_1(v), y_v=I_2(v) in any two-bit proof and use the linear sum equation to replace 1+I_1+I_2 by I_3 in quadratic axioms, one factor at a time. All axioms cost degree at most two and an affine multiplication is a linear combination of variable multiples. The proven lower bound therefore applies to either two-bit encoding (direct collisions or aggregate guard). If one starts with the traditional three-literal existence clause polynomial, expand it and cancel the pair and triple terms using the functionality axioms in degree three. [Lauria–Nordström](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.00125#page=2); [explicit one-hot equations and independent average-case result](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.17022#page=6).

For satisfiable hard implications take an edge-minimal non-3-colorable subgraph H of such a graph, retaining isolated vertices if convenient. Every refutation from H is also a refutation from the original larger axiom set, so the same degree lower bound d applies. Fix an edge uv and put G'=H−uv. It is 3-colorable; every proper coloring gives u and v the same color. Pin u red. Every model now forces v red, and a planted witness exists by renaming any proper coloring.

To quantify the cost of naming a color, suppose H with one color named at u has a refutation of degree D. Permuting colors gives all three pinned refutations in the named-color encoding at the same degree (affine bit substitutions and local Boolean identities cost at most two for vertex guards). Multiply the proof in branch c by I_c(u). The extra pin axiom is annihilated by Boolean reduction, and every original axiom has its product with I_c(u) derivable in degree at most three. Multiplications in proof lines raise the original maximum degree by at most one. Summing the three conclusions I_c(u)=0 yields 1=0. Consequently d≤max(3,D+1). From a degree-D proof that v is red in the SAT instance one obtains a refutation of pinned H in degree max(2,D) using the omitted red collision axiom and I_red(u)=1. The same transfer works for a proof in the aggregate-guard encoding: simulate it in the one-hot system and append the named-color collision there. Hence deriving the forced bit in the SAT instance also requires Ω(n) degree. Edge minimization and choosing a coloring are existence arguments; no efficient planted witness generator or average-case planted lower bound is implied.

For low-degree positive controls take the indicator of a zero image q(d)=(1+X_d)(1+Y_d), where X,Y are linear functionals on a three-dimensional binary direction space. As a function of d it has degree at most two. Thus its sum over the eight points vanishes. If all six directions other than 0 and m are constrained nonzero, q(0)=1 implies q(m)=1. Writing X=X_m and Y=Y_m gives X+Y+XY=0; multiplying by X or Y and applying Boolean identities gives X=Y=0 in degree three. This is sharp for an isolated generic gadget: the seven tensors d⊗d for nonzero d have the all-ones dependence, and any six are independent (the three unit and three pair-sum tensors are a basis of the symmetric 3×3 matrices, and a basis change sends any omitted nonzero d to 111). Thus no nonzero affine consequence arises in degree two in the independent six map coordinates. A semantic quotient by the forced direction must retain all remaining constraints; this local argument makes no claim that every quotient is planar graphic.

The executable check_color_propagation.py implements degree-bounded squarefree PC. In the named-color two-bit encoding it refutes unpinned K4 first in degree three and root-pinned K4 in degree two. On a diamond and on the tested edge-deleted odd wheels of rim lengths 3 and 5, it derives the equality implication with a red root in degree two. These small planted examples do not realize the asymptotic lower bound.

The hard graph family can have maximum degree four. Replace each high-degree source vertex by a tree that distributes its color to one terminal per incident edge. A distributor consists of an adjacent pair of hubs and three terminals, each adjacent to both hubs. In a proper 3-coloring its three terminals have the same color. Use one terminal as input and two as output; identify an output with the next distributor input. Hubs and identified terminals have degree four, and an external leaf terminal has degree at most three after reconnecting the original edge. A binary tree uses linear size in the original vertex degree. To simulate a target PC proof on the original graph, map all terminal indicators to their source vertex indicators. Assign hub colors by two fixed opposite cyclic permutations of the source color, which merely permute the three indicator variables. Every internal edge axiom pulls back to source same-vertex functionality, and each external edge axiom to an original edge axiom. Thus splitting costs no increase above degree two and preserves the linear lower bound because the source total degree is linear in n. Edge-minimize afterward for the satisfiable implication. For connected graphs of maximum degree three the only non-3-colorable case is K4, by Brooks' theorem.
