# Six boundary halfedges on one shore of a bipartite cubic disk

Let a connected loopless bipartite cubic multipole have six boundary halfedges, all with holders on shore A, with a specified disk embedding having all terminal ends on the rim. Internal parallel edges and repeated holders are allowed. Reject a repeated color at a holder. The bipartite incidence counts give |A|−|B|=2. In any proper three-edge-coloring each color must therefore occur exactly twice on the boundary. Reject every other multiplicity. Pair the two terminal ends prescribed each color and inspect the cyclic order of the six distinct terminal ends.

**Crossing pairs.** If two prescribed pairs interleave on the rim, use the omitted third color as base. Delete the holders of its two halfedges in the base-matching problem and find a perfect matching on the remaining internal vertices. A missing matching proves nonextension. Any such matching, together with the two base halfedges, covers every vertex once. Its complement has even cycles and two paths with the four remaining terminal ends. The paths are vertex-disjoint, so they induce a noncrossing pairing. Since the four colors alternate around the rim, each path connects opposite colors. Its holders are on the same shore, hence the internal path length is even. Alternation along its two terminal edges and internal edges works with precisely those opposite terminal colors. The same argument handles a zero-internal-edge path between two halfedges at one holder. Color the cycles alternately to complete a witness.

**Noncrossing pairs.** Join each equal-color pair of terminals outside the disk, suppressing the artificial terminal ends, to give three new edges between their holders. They can be drawn disjointly, producing a connected planar cubic loopless multigraph C. A coloring extending the targets colors the three caps. Conversely any three-edge-coloring of C, cut back to the multipole, must use each boundary color exactly twice by the shore identity. Each cap supplies two equal ends; hence the three cap colors are distinct. One global permutation matches all three prescriptions. A cubic graph with a bridge has no three-edge-coloring: each color must use the bridge by parity on its odd-order shore. If C is bridgeless, its plane dual is loopless; a four-coloring of its faces, treated as binary two-vectors, gives edge colors by differences of adjacent face colors. Around a cubic vertex the three face colors are pairwise distinct, so the three differences are exactly the three nonzero vectors. This gives a polynomial decision by planarity and a bridge test, and a witness using a constructive planar four-coloring algorithm. The companion implements the bridge decision, not the four-color constructor. [Robertson, Sanders, Seymour and Thomas](https://thomas.math.gatech.edu/FC/fourcolor.html)

**A topology-free sufficient test.** For a chosen base color, first find a feasible base matching. Construct a graph for the residual paths on every original internal vertex and append a distinct leaf for each of the four nonbase terminal ends. Delete internal edges forced into every feasible base matching (test whether forbidding the edge destroys all base matchings), and omit the two base terminal halfedges. Retain the base terminal *holders*, since residual paths may pass through them. If this residual overgraph has no pair of vertex-disjoint paths simultaneously joining the two terminals of each of the two equal-color pairs, every base matching extends. Indeed a failed base matching has exactly two paths, and if one joins equal colors then both do, giving the forbidden linkage. The presence of such a linkage in the overgraph decides neither failure nor success: it need not be the complement of a common matching, and an alternative matching may extend.

**Single-cycle exchange barrier.** Give the six distinct A vertices 0 through 5 boundary colors (0,0,1,1,2,2). Four B vertices 7,8,10,11 have neighborhoods {0,1,5}, {0,1,2}, {2,3,4}, {3,4,5}, respectively. Base-zero matching {2–8, 5–7, 3–10, 4–11} extends. Flipping its alternating four-cycle to use {3–11,4–10} instead makes the residual pairing incompatible. In general flipping a base-alternating cycle replaces one pairing of adjacent positions on the cycle by its shifted pairing, leaving its external strands fixed. Compatibility is consequently not exchange-invariant. This example asserts no cofacial embedding.

The regression compares every one of the 90 ordered balanced patterns on each of three connected disk multipoles with direct enumeration. Its 270 decisions include six rejections on each nonlocal branch. All 30 affirmative capping decisions and 138 affirmative crossing decisions agree with enumeration. The six-distinct-holder exchange barrier is checked separately. No unrestricted nondisk decision algorithm follows.
