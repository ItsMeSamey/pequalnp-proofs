# Bounded-dimensional affine projection has bounded polynomial-calculus degree

Consider a table rule over GF(2) that works in a space of at most s linear normals on the current affine domain. The rule filters evaluations by earlier forbidden flats and learned affine equations available in this space, and projects missing patterns or learns valid affine equations. Global row reduction of derived affine equations is allowed. A forbidden flat is encoded by its Boolean indicator set equal to zero. The result below allows arbitrary normal choices and arbitrarily many rounds.

A table inference has Boolean polynomial calculus degree at most 2s+2, uniformly in the ambient dimension. Choose coordinates for the table. For each rejected table point choose one premise violation that contains that point. Multiply its indicator equation by the point indicator (at most s affine factors). This derives a polynomial identical as a Boolean function to the point indicator alone. Booleanly identical polynomials of ordinary degree D can be interconverted in PC in degree at most max(D,2): multilinearize monomials using multiples of x_i²+x_i and use uniqueness of multilinear representative. Multiply by an affine form via linear combination of variable multiples. The sum of rejected point indicators in a projected forbidden flat is the indicator of that flat; the sum over the points violating an inferred affine equation is that affine equation. For an empty table sum over every point to derive 1=0. Every indicator and product has the asserted degree even after replacing table coordinates by arbitrary affine forms on the original cube.

After previous affine eliminations choose an affine retraction onto the current affine domain, obtained by solving its pivot equations. Substitution by its affine coordinates preserves degrees. Original and retracted versions of any earlier premise polynomial of degree D are interderivable in degree at most max(D,2) using the previously derived linear equations: telescope each monomial, replacing one factor at a time; each difference is a multiple of a linear equation in their span. Boolean multilinearize if necessary. Thus the simulation incurs no factor per round. It applies also to a finite original-normal catalog, which only limits the choice of tables. It does not apply to unrestricted branching on external decisions or a solver allowed a global oracle for missing projections.

For the native matching encoding of a strict ternary clause, use literal truth bits a,b,c. Substitute witnesses a,(1+a)b,(1+a)(1+b); they have odd parity. For false-literal times witness violations the first two vanish Booleanly and the last is exactly (1+a)(1+b)(1+c), the standard clause axiom. Native affine occurrence-difference and clause-parity constraints are identities after this substitution. Thus any degree-D native PC inference pulls back to the source CNF at degree at most 2D+O(1), including the Boolean axioms: multiply inferences by the degree-two substituted polynomials and multilinearize. This step is necessary to transfer a raw truth-coordinate obstruction to a native matching layout.

A direct strict ternary family is the subset-cardinality formula of Definition 4.6 and Theorem 4.8 of the cited paper. On a 4-regular bipartite expander with a single added edge, every constraint forbids one assignment on three distinct edge bits, every variable has bounded occurrence, and the PC degree is Ω(n). Thus the native projection-dimension lower bound already follows without a clause conversion.

Alternatively, for a bounded-degree expander graph functional pigeonhole principle, Mikša–Nordström Theorem 4.9 gives an Ω(n) degree bound over GF(2) on the source clause encoding, with n pigeons and constant degree. Its clauses have bounded width. To obtain a strict ternary source, introduce constant-size OR trees per clause with fresh auxiliaries, encode each gate by clauses of width at most three, and assert the root. Set gate variables to the actual OR of their descendant literals as Boolean polynomials: their degrees are bounded by the original constant width. All gate clauses pull back to Boolean identities and the asserted root pulls back to the source clause axiom. Pad each unary or binary clause to strict width three with opposite fresh literals; fixing padding variables recovers a source axiom or a tautology. This gives linear size and bounded occurrence. Consequently refuting the native strict matching encoding of these NOs requires projection dimension Ω(n), even if arbitrary normals are allowed.

For a nonvacuous satisfiable fixture, replace each strict hard clause (a OR b OR c) by (not s OR a OR w),(not w OR b OR c) with clause-private w. At s=0 all clauses have a joint model by setting w=0. At s=1 each pair projects to the original clause; hence every model entails s=0. Any proof of that unary projection restricts at s=1 to a refutation: substitute w=1+a to make the first clause tautological and the second clause the original. Use equality-linked selector clones with fresh strict binary padding to retain bounded occurrence. The same Ω(n) bound holds for learning this consequence in the native matching representation. This is a dimension lower bound for the stated calculus, not a general time or SAT-decision lower bound.

Source: [Mikša and Nordström, A Generalized Method for Proving Polynomial Calculus Degree Lower Bounds, Theorem 4.9](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.01358).
