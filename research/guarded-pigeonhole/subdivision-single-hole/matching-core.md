# Matching-autarky decomposition and irreducible instances

Work on the decoded fixed-target CNF after removing tautologies and simplifying repeated literals. In the clause-variable incidence graph, find a maximum matching. Start at unmatched clauses and follow alternating paths, using unmatched edges from clauses and matching edges from variables. Keep the reached clauses and variables. Every neighbor of a reached clause is reached: its matching partner, if any, was used to enter it. Every reached variable is matched by maximal cardinality. All unreached clauses are matched to distinct unreached variables. Assign each such variable to satisfy its matched clause. These assignments do not touch the reached core, so every satisfying assignment there extends to the entire formula. This removes matching autarkies of unbounded occurrence degree. It can be iterated with the degree-two component rule.

For every nonempty subset S of reached variables, its neighborhood inside the reached core is strictly larger than S. The matching gives distinct neighbors there. If these partners were the whole neighborhood, no alternating search could first enter S: any entrance through an unmatched edge would have to originate at a partner clause already reached via its unique matched variable in S. No unmatched starting clause is a partner. This excludes every further matching autarky, since each touched clause of such an autarky needs its own variable in the assigned set.

## Hard fixed points

Given a strict 3-CNF F, append, for each variable x, the four strict ternary clauses forbidding the odd local assignments to (x,a_x,b_x) for private a_x,b_x. This imposes x+a_x+b_x=0 and any original assignment extends. Every variable has at least four occurrences and both signs. A nonempty variable set S meets at least |S|/3 private triples and touches their four distinct clauses each, so its clause neighborhood has size at least 4|S|/3. Thus neither matching-autarky deletion nor the component, pure-variable, or repeated-literal rules change the formula. SAT on these fixed points is NP-complete by the explicit linear-size reduction from strict 3-SAT. The private parity relations can themselves be recognized and existentially deleted. The assertion concerns the specified preprocessing rules.

In fact an autarky touching any private triple must assign all its variables consistently: it touches all four clauses, and a partial triple with an unassigned variable has an extension of odd parity. Consequently nontrivial autarkies on the padded formula project to nontrivial autarkies on the original, and the converse follows by extending the private triples of assigned original variables.

## Minimal unsatisfiable fixed points

On a simple cubic graph G remaining connected after deleting any one vertex, put an edge variable on each edge, with a prescribed incident-edge parity at each vertex and odd total charge. Each parity relation is expressed by four strict ternary clauses. Every edge occurs eight times with balanced signs. The instance is unsatisfiable since summing the parity equations counts every edge twice. Its incidence graph has |N(S)|≥8|S|/3 for every nonempty variable set, since each endpoint meets at most three edges and contributes four clauses.

Deleting any single clause at vertex v permits precisely its formerly forbidden incident triple as a local violation. Fix that triple. The residual demand on G−v has even total: the incident bits have parity opposite the prescribed charge at v, canceling the original odd discrepancy. The connected graph G−v realizes any even demand vector by solving along a spanning tree from leaves to root. This proves minimal unsatisfiability. Any nontrivial autarky would satisfy some touched clauses independently and leave an unsatisfiable proper subformula, contradicting minimality. Circular ladders with cycle length at least three give an unbounded explicit graph family. These instances remain decidable by testing the total charge. They illustrate irreducibility and do not assert a one-hole syndrome property.
