# Common-interface recognition with affine-viable states

Let Az=b have disjoint pair exclusions, and fix a subspace W of the syndrome space of dimension h. Find a particular solution z0 or return zero. In A modulo W, find the finest pair-respecting direct-sum components by computing the fundamental-column graph and adding the excluded-pair links. Write these as I_1,...,I_l. For any actual solution in deviations d=z+z0, the contribution A_I d_I from each component belongs to W: projected contributions lie in a direct sum and total zero. Conversely local deviations with fluxes g_i in W combine to a solution of the parity system exactly when their sum is zero.

For each i,g∈W test consistency of local A_I d_I=g and perform the full affine absorption of quadratic guards, without evaluating a quadratic sum. Record its residual rank or affine contradiction. Call the set of noncontradictory fluxes V_i. Prefix and suffix sumsets over the finite group W determine precisely which g in V_i participate in at least one tuple g_j∈V_j with total zero. This is an affine viability test, not a satisfiability test. Discard the others; if no tuples remain, return zero.

Assume there exists any grouping of these canonical components into coarse bags such that each coarse-bag flux occurring in a global *linear* solution gives either affine contradiction or final quadratic rank ≤r. Then every individual retained state has rank ≤r. For a retained state take a zero-total tuple of affine-surviving states through it. The sum over one coarse bag is a globally linearly realizable flux. Inside that coarse fiber, impose the individual component fluxes by additional affine equations. Its domain factors as the product of the individual component domains, with independent guards. The stabilized affine closure after this restriction is the product of their nonempty closures, and its residual quadratic rank is the sum of the individual ranks. Restriction preserves every affine consequence of the coarser closure; if it remains affinely nonempty its stabilized residual rank cannot exceed that of the original fiber (see quadratic-refinement.md). Thus the original coarse fiber cannot affinely contradict and its rank bounds that sum. Apply to the bag containing the retained state.

One need neither find nor verify the unknown coarse grouping. Simply compute the retained ranks and check the threshold directly. Count each retained state by the rank-r Walsh method and convolve counts at total zero. Their backpointers recover a full witness. Runtime poly(N)(4^h+2^(h+r)) bit operations. If W is found by enumerating spans of at most q input columns, multiply recognition time by N^{O(q)} and apply counting once to a qualifying candidate. W need not be spanned by a bounded number of individual input columns in general. An apparent new projected dependency between bags modulo W need not lift to a true dependency unless W is available on the relevant sides. Therefore the guaranteed unknown-group criterion above explicitly requires their projected bag spaces to form a direct sum; containment of old boundary spaces alone is not used as a substitute.
