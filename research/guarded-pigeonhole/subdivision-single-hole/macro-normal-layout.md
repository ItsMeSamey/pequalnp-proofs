# Early marginalization of implicit quadratic leaves

Parametrize the shared affine domain by independent bits. The contracted private leaves give integer factors F_i(y)=α_i(1+β_i 1_Hi(y)(−1)^ψ_i(y)), per macro-fourier.md. For empty H_i take the constant factor. Compute K_i from the directions annihilating H_i normals, lying in the radical of the polar form of ψ_i, and satisfying ψ_i(u)+ψ_i(0)=0. The last condition is linear on the radical. These translations preserve F_i; hence it depends only on the evaluations on V_i=K_i^perp. This is a sufficient dual support, not necessarily minimal. Each linear function on ΣV_i has 2^{n−dim ΣV_i} lifts to the n-bit shared cube.

Find a tree for the subspace arrangement by Jeong, Kim and Oum, or use a supplied tree. Let a node's span U have boundary B=U∩U_out. At δ:B→F2 its table sums the local factor product over linear functions U→F2 extending δ. At a leaf impose δ as ordinary affine equations on y, sum F_i as one cardinality and one H_i-constrained quadratic Gauss sum, and divide by 2^{n−dim V_i} to remove padding. This is polynomial even on a large-dimensional leaf. [Primary layout algorithm](https://doi.org/10.1137/19M1285895)

For child spans L,R and external O, enumerate pairs of linear assignments on B_L=L∩(R+O), B_R=R∩(L+O) agreeing on L∩R. For b in the parent boundary (L+R)∩O write b=l+r; necessarily l,r lie in B_L,B_R. Parent value at b is the sum of the two child evaluations, independent of the decomposition because their overlap agrees. Add products of the two table entries to the resulting parent bucket. Child linear functions agreeing on overlap glue uniquely, so no normalization occurs at joins. Root scalar gets multiplied by 2^{n−dim ΣV_i}; for zero leaves it is 2^n directly. Full span makes the multiplier one. Time with a supplied width-h tree is 4^h poly(input) bit operations. Tree finding uses f(h)poly(input) at fixed h, with no claim that this is polynomial for logarithmic h. This is ordinary sum-product DP on an existing layout notion; the specialized ingredients here are the computed supports and the implicit polynomial-cost leaf contractions.

For an unsigned witness, backtrack positive child table entries. At each leaf recover an assignment with positive F_i satisfying its boundary by pinning any coordinates needed and reevaluating only its single polynomial-time Gaussian formula. Glue the resulting functions, solve for one shared y lifting their sum, then solve each private quadratic as in macro-fourier.md. This avoids any assumption of width monotonicity when globally pinning shared bits; projecting dual subspaces can increase their apparent intersections. Signed counts alone do not support positivity recovery.

For graph 3-colorings on a path, the expansion has 2^{n+3m} distinct phases at termination in all orders. Arrange vertex and edge equations in natural path order, grouped into constant-size blocks. At cuts between prefix and suffix blocks the underlying supports meet only on two bits of a shared vertex; cuts within blocks also have constant-dimensional boundary. Thus this method counts the path and produces witnesses with bounded width, while general graph 3-colorability remains NP-hard.

In fact the canonical encoding of a simple graph without isolated vertices has ordinary edge branchwidth bw(G)≤h≤max(2bw(G),4), where bw measures the number of middle vertices. Restrict the subspace arrangement to the first-color edge factors x_u x_v=0. Their normal subspaces are exactly span(x_u,x_v), and a cut overlap is the coordinate space of middle vertices. Deleting other leaves of a tree and suppressing degree-two nodes cannot increase cut width. This proves the lower bound. Conversely group the three edge factors together and assign each vertex factor to one incident edge. Each group spans precisely the four coordinate directions of its endpoints. Intergroup cuts on a graph branch tree have dimension twice the middle-set size; refining a single group cannot contribute more than four. Thus arbitrary high-width colorable grids and high-width noncolorable graphs (adjoin a K4 by a bridge to a grid) supply both-sign negative controls on width. The path is a positive small-width case, not a new graph coloring algorithm.
