# Two equal-color pins compile a mixed six-port rainbow prescription

Write the three halfedges on each shore of a bipartite cubic multipole as A1,A2,A3 and B1,B2,B3. The target is a rainbow with Ai and Bi equal for each index. Each coloring gives equal color histograms on the two shores by subtracting internal edges from the color incidences.

Join A1 and B1 by an edge e. Add p incident with A2 and B3, and q incident with A3 and B2. Join p and q by an edge f. Prescribe the same color at e and f. Every desired rainbow extends after permuting its colors. Conversely, each of the other four tips avoids the prescribed color. If A2=A3, shore balance forces both B2 and B3 to equal A2, a conflict at p. Thus both triples are rainbow; the constraints at p and q force B3=A3 and B2=A2. A single permutation recovers the original named target. This is a constructive reduction to two fixed disjoint edges of the same color on a cubic graph. Under p in B and q in A only p–B3 and q–A3 lie within a shore. If necessary eliminate parallel edges by replacing each vertex by a bipartite cube minus one vertex; its three distinct holders realize exactly all rainbow triples. In the target graph this preserves each marked edge and its incidence shore pattern.

The independent checker found four boundary words satisfying the cap with e=f=0; intersection with the 93 shore-balanced words leaves exactly 012012 and 021021. A single prescribed edge cannot alter feasibility of an otherwise unprescribed glued graph: a palette permutation makes the pin hold. The invisible twins for unrestricted caps in kempe-balanced-accessory.md and six-port-five-rainbows therefore apply also to algorithms using only one-pin cap decision answers. This sharpness concerns access by pinning edges of the capped graph, with the core left intact; it does not restrict general algorithms.
