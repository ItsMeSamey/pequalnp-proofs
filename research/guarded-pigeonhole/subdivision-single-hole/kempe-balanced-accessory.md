# No fixed cubic accessory compiles mixed six-port rainbow testing

A fixed cap glued by its six labeled halfedges cannot decide the prescribed rainbow matching for every mixed-shore bipartite cubic multipole. Even connected simple planar disk inputs with six distinct terminal holders suffice. The cap itself may be nonplanar, nonbipartite or disconnected.

Write the two ordered triples A,B. The target requires A and B each rainbow with A_i=B_i. All traces of a cubic six-pole have even color multiplicities (count each color at ordinary vertices). Every two-color subgraph pairs its terminals by paths; independently switching any subset of these disjoint paths preserves feasibility. This remains true for arbitrary internal topology and for terminal-to-terminal wires.

Take all even palette orbits subject to one condition: if A and B have identical color histograms, allow only the target or monochrome. Iteratively remove any orbit with a pair of colors such that no pairing of the two-color terminals permits every subset-switch within the current collection. Any realizable sublanguage of the initial language survives every round. Independent labeled and orbit enumerations produce 99,24,6,0 strings or 17,4,1,0 orbits. Thus every nonempty cap language admits a wrong balanced rainbow or a balanced four-plus-two word. Excluding monochrome at the outset gives 96,24,6,0. The independent checker check_six_decision_audit.py verifies the version permitting monochrome and exhausts the counterexamples below.

For each such bad word take the path a-b-c-d with dangling-tip counts 2,1,1,2 respectively. The A shore is a,c; B is b,d. Put the singleton tips at c and b at the same index i. Under the target the two tips at a force color A_i on ab, conflicting with B_i. For a wrong rainbow, select i at which the two shore triples differ. The two end vertices force the colors of the singleton A_i and B_i on ab and cd respectively, so the central edge receives the remaining color. For a four-plus-two word choose i carrying the majority color on both shores, possible by intersection of two size-two subsets of a three-set. Both endpoints force the third color on ab and cd, and the central edge receives the minority color. Consequently the bad word is accepted by the control, producing a false positive. An empty cap language gives a false negative when applied to a positive path control having distinct target colors on its two singleton tips.

Substitute a cube minus one vertex for every path vertex. Its three ports must be rainbow by parity and each rainbow pattern extends by a permutation of a proper cube coloring. Its three distinct holders lie on one shore and one face. This gives the claimed connected simple disk controls with distinct holders. An OR of fixed caps likewise cannot universally decide the predicate. More general reductions or combinations of queries are outside the scope of this theorem.
