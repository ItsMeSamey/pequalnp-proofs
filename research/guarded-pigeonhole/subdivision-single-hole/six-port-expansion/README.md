# Six ports after two vertex deletions in expanded K4

Let H arise from K4 by replacing vertices with triangles. Remove two nonadjacent vertices u,w, retaining their six incident half-edges as ordered triples U,W. There is always a proper three-edge coloring of the resulting graph with the same two-color multiset at both triples. This holds even when u,w have the same parity in all three bichromatic Hamilton cycles of H.

## Path of K4 pieces

Record each triangle expansion as a vertex 3-sum with a fresh K4, deleting one degree-three vertex in each piece and pairing the corresponding three edge ends. The pieces form a tree. A surviving vertex belongs to just one piece. A branch off the tree path between the pieces of u and w is a three-pole obtained by puncturing a triangle-expanded K4. Every proper coloring has three different colors at its three ports: for each color the number of those ports of that color is congruent to the odd number of internal vertices. Conversely every rainbow triple extends, since a coloring exists by extending the K4 coloring through each triangle expansion, and colors may be permuted. The branch therefore has exactly the signature of one cubic vertex. Branches can be collapsed individually even when joining placeholders are adjacent in the parent piece, since three-pole substitution preserves the complete labeled boundary relation. Collapsing also works when both deleted vertices belong to one piece. What remains is a sequence of one or more copies of K4 punctured at two distinct vertex positions, connected through triples of wires, with arbitrary ordered bijections at the joins. The deletion of two adjacent positions in a piece leaves their former connecting edge as a free through-wire, including when the final endpoints of the original graph were nonadjacent.

## Exact transition table

Each tile has one through-wire in position i and two adjacent internal vertices. Fix output coordinates by the permutation that agrees with the input on a rainbow coloring, absorbing all join permutations into the coordinates of successive triples. The through-wire preserves its color. If the other two inputs differ, they pass through unchanged in these coordinates. If they agree, both outputs agree and take either of the other two colors. Indeed, at each of the two internal vertices the two external colors must be distinct and their missing color is the common internal edge color. These rules give the full signature by Boolean relation composition along the path. In particular the rainbow permutation is unique.

Fix a color A. The seven triples with A as the only color of odd multiplicity are M=AAA, and B_j,C_j with A in slot j and B or C repeated in the other slots. A tile in position i permits exactly the three-vertex complete graph without loops on M,B_i,C_i and the identity on each of the other four states. A single tile preserves any B_j with j different from i. For a word of at least two tiles with first positions i,j, start at B_i, choose M after the first tile, and choose either B_j or C_j after the second. In the suffix keep the singleton at j by interchanging B_j and C_j at each tile j, and holding the state at every other tile. The choice after tile two can always be made so that the terminal state is B_j. Its color histogram equals that of B_i. Endpoint coordinate permutations preserve histograms.

## Direct check and first case

Run python check.py with NetworkX installed. The program independently enumerates graph isomorphism classes of expanded K4 through 14 vertices, computes the unique coloring up to permutation, and tests each nonadjacent pair of equal parity in every bichromatic cycle by directly coloring the six-pole. There are respectively 0,0,2,12,46,200 such pairs at orders 4,6,8,10,12,14, summed across representatives. The program displays the first table at each size and any putative counterexample to the theorem. replay.txt records the output.

The first example has edges 01,03,05,14,16,23,24,27,34,56,57,67 and deleted vertices 3 and 5. Order each triple by increasing neighboring vertex, concatenate U then W and rename colors by order of first appearance. The full table is 000110, 001100, 001122, 001212, 010111, 010122, 011101, 011202, 011220, 012120. The equal-histogram repeated-color states are 001100 and 011101.

For an arbitrary uniquely three-edge-colorable H, close U to W by matching the half-edges with a bijection. Existence of an equal-histogram nonrainbow state is equivalent to the existence of a properly colorable closure at one of the five bijections distinct from the unique rainbow bijection. Any equal nonrainbow histogram admits at least two matching bijections. Conversely any coloring at a different bijection cannot be rainbow by uniqueness. A loop in a closure prevents proper coloring. This criterion leaves open general uniquely colorable graphs outside the triangle-expansion family.
