# Full residual obstruction at the fixed SAT target

For each k≥1 take the implication chain (x1), (¬x1∨x2), …, (¬x_(k−1)∨xk), (¬xk). Convert it to strict three-literal CNF: at every node of length below three, introduce its own fresh variable z and replace C by the two children C∨z and C∨¬z, recursively continuing until width three. Every final clause uses three distinct variables. The two unit roots contribute eight leaves and six new variables; the k−1 binary roots contribute 2(k−1) leaves and k−1 new variables. Thus m=2k+6, n=2k+5, and d=4m−n=6k+19.

In the subdivision encoding each occurrence has an exclusion pair (f,w). Consecutive occurrences of a variable have prescribed f differences; each clause prescribes the parity of its three w bits. For the constructed formula call its usual target t. **Exactly t is absent** among the d-bit syndromes of all legal assignments.

Indeed, arbitrary path-difference syndromes describe arbitrary occurrence signs modulo per-variable complements: fix the first sign and propagate along the path. The f values in a fiber are precisely the literal-falsity vectors of assignments to variables under this signing. A clause with w parity zero can be dropped by choosing no witnesses. A parity-one clause needs at least one true literal, and choosing just that occurrence suffices. Thus the attainable syndromes correspond precisely to satisfiable signed subformulas.

For a split node, existentially eliminate all fresh variables in its descendant subtree. The result is either TRUE or one signed clause on exactly the variables at the node. A leaf gives TRUE if dropped, its specified clause otherwise. At an internal node first eliminate the disjoint descendant variables of its two children. If either child is TRUE, satisfy the other by the private splitting variable z. If both retain clauses and the two z signs agree, assigning z suffices. If the z signs differ, existential elimination gives the disjunction of both inherited parts. It is TRUE if any inherited variable has conflicting signs, and otherwise is exactly their common signed clause. Consequently the subtree retains a clause precisely when all leaves are active, each fresh split variable has consistent signs within either branch and opposite signs across branches, and every inherited occurrence sign agrees across copies. In that case the fresh-variable signs are unique up to independent complements.

After these eliminations, any missing chain clause breaks the signed path. Each surviving component has at most one endpoint unit; work outwards from it and choose each next variable to satisfy the intervening clause. Components without a unit can start at either end. In the intact path, if the two neighboring occurrences of some variable have the same sign, satisfy both by that variable and work inward from the two endpoints on the remaining segments. If all neighboring signs are opposite, the left unit propagates through the entire path to contradict the right unit. The unique unsatisfiable signing is therefore the original one up to per-variable complements, with every leaf active. Those complements do not change the consecutive-occurrence syndromes. This proves the claim including k=1.

For any linear projection L with nonzero kernel, pick nonzero h in that kernel. Some legal assignment attains t+h, and therefore meets the projected target Lt. This remains true if L is chosen after inspecting the instance and its fixed Karp target. In fact every target in the image of L is attained after projection. The obstruction applies when retaining the guard constraints and deciding solely by comparing projected syndromes. General Karp reductions may instead change the guards, change the representation, or replace these recognizable NO instances.

Alternatively pad the original chain by repeating literals within each clause. Any conflicting signs in a repeated block make that clause TRUE; otherwise the identical signed-path proof applies. This gives m=k+1, n=k, d=3k+4 and the same single missing target in the version allowing repeated literals.
