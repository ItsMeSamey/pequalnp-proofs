# Existential quadratic interfaces

Expose 2m binary ports x_i,y_i. The relation requires all x_i y_i=0. An exact representation with arbitrary existential auxiliaries, arbitrary affine equations and t equations of degree at most s over F2 requires st≥m.

The projection of the common affine solution space contains the origin and every port unit vector, since these are admissible. Hence projection is onto. Choose an accepting lift of zero. Choose linear preimages in the direction space for each basis vector of the port space; their linear combination plus the accepting lift defines an affine section satisfying all affine equations. Restrict this section to x=y. The product of the t zero-test indicators 1+q_j on the section equals one at zero. For every nonzero diagonal port input every lift fails some test, so the product vanishes there. By uniqueness of the multilinear representative it has Boolean degree m, the degree of ∏(1+x_i). Substitution of affine forms and Boolean reduction cannot increase degree, yielding st≥m. No bound on auxiliary count or on equation supports is needed.

For a concrete host use 2m disjoint four-cycles with one designated edge each, attached by individual bridges to distinct odd positions on a path on 4m vertices. Each even-order cycle forces its bridge absent by summing its demand bits. All-odd joins are alternating matchings on every cycle plus the unique path matching; all are forests. Designated edge bits are independent. Pair designated edges across successive cycles. The bound applies to a full relation interface even on these simple connected planar bipartite subcubic hosts of treewidth two. The instances have trivial decision. A general target-specific reduction, or unbounded intrinsic quadratic guard equations, is outside the statement.

For t factored guard rows L_j M_j=0 with affine factors, the exact bound strengthens to t≥m. On the diagonal section through the good lift of zero choose for each row any factor vanishing there. Their common zero space is linear and satisfies every guard. Thus it has dimension zero. In particular the coefficient vectors span m dimensions for every allowed factor choice through that lift. The m canonical pair products attain equality. If there are also arbitrary polynomial tests with sum of degrees D, the kernel of the chosen factors has dimension at least m−t; the other test indicators on that kernel isolate its origin, giving m≤t+D. Bounds count all internal nonlinear guards; a bounded number of linear markers leaves their count uncontrolled.

At the tight budget t=m, there is a triangular structure on the rows whose two factors vanish at the chosen origin. Use the chosen zero factors as a basis of the dual diagonal space indexed by rows. For the flexible row i write its other factor in that basis as row B_i. Every principal minor B[J,J] on flexible indices equals one: swapping those rows into the basis leaves a basis. Thus each flexible diagonal entry is one, and the directed graph on flexible indices having arc i→j for off-diagonal B_ij=1 is acyclic. A shortest directed cycle has no chord creating another shorter directed cycle. Its principal submatrix would be identity plus the cycle permutation, singular over F2. A topological order triangularizes the flexible square block. Coefficients on inflexible indices are unrestricted by this argument. Conversely these triangular conditions imply every permitted factor swap remains a basis. This describes tight-budget behavior on the chosen diagonal section; it does not constrain arbitrary off-section auxiliaries.

## Low residual quadratic rank

A tractable parameter is supplied by linearizing the join space, then taking the rank of only the squarefree degree-two coefficient rows of all pair violations. Eliminate the linear parity constraints to parametrize all D-joins, or detect inconsistency. Gaussian elimination on the quadratic coefficients, carrying along the affine terms, produces independent nonlinear rows and ordinary affine constraints. Absorb affine constraints by reparametrization, rejecting inconsistency. Repeat when a nontrivial affine restriction appears. Every such pass reduces dimension; rank cannot increase under affine substitution. Let r be the final nonlinear rank. Count compatible joins by summing 2^r quadratic Walsh sums, one per binary combination, and normalizing by 2^r. Every Walsh sum is evaluated by ordinary binary quadratic-form elimination. This costs 2^r times polynomial input size in bit operations. Pin edges successively to recover a join; extra pins do not increase the original final rank. Strip selected cycles to get a forest with the same demands still respecting all exclusions. Counting here is for joins; it counts forests directly only when all compatible joins are acyclic.

For example use just two cycles of length 2m (m≥2) joined by a bridge, with all-odd demand, pairing corresponding alternate edges across cycles. Bridge parity is forced zero. The m pair tests are identical in the two independent alternating state bits, so r=1 despite m disjoint pairs. The independent four-cycle construction instead has r=m despite its trivial decision. Thus r measures shared algebraic dependence of the pairs, not just their disjointness or count.
