# Projective color quotient

Represent the three colors by the nonzero vectors of F2². Take one formal basis vector per graph vertex. Put the vertex vectors and each edge's endpoint sum in P. Colorings are exactly linear maps L taking every point of P to a nonzero vector.

If all seven points of a binary 3-space are present, no such L exists. If exactly six are present, its seventh point must lie in the kernel. Contract that point and project P. Maps lift uniquely by composition, so the count and original vertex witnesses survive. Enumerate triples of present points to recognize relevant 3-spaces, stopping after at most the ambient rank many contractions. The zero-indicator q(d)=(1+X(d))(1+Y(d)) has degree at most two in the three coefficients of d, hence sums to zero over all eight directions. Since q(0)=1 and six terms vanish, q(w)=1 at the seventh; multiplication by X(w),Y(w) yields X(w)=Y(w)=0 modulo Boolean axioms at degree three. On graph triangles this forces the sum of their vertex colors to vanish.

If a,b,a+b are present, normalize L on R=span(a,b) to the standard basis. Every solution orbit under the six output-basis permutations has one normalized representative. In a nonroot coset p+R containing t present points, L(p) has a known list of 4−t values. t=4 rejects; t=2 or 3 supplies affine constraints; t=1 leaves one forbidden binary pattern. Solve these on common ambient coordinates, since coset representatives need not be independent. Multiply normalized counts by six; satisfying assignments lift through the original directions. There are at most quadratically many root lines to test.

A supplied labeled planar graphic representation is checkable by rank-preserving binary row-space equivalence and a planar embedding. With no zero columns, nonvanishing corresponds to differences of proper four-color vertex potentials. Obtain a coloring by the constructive Four Color Theorem, solve for the linear functional giving its edge differences, and lift to the original graph. No counting claim follows from this test. [Robertson, Sanders, Seymour and Thomas](https://thomas.math.gatech.edu/FC/fourcolor.html)

## Cubic line graphs

For simple triangle-free cubic G, the triangles of L(G) are the three incident edges at each vertex. Quotient by their forced sums: each component of the edge-color vector belongs to the binary cycle space. Residual point columns p_e are therefore the columns of a cycle-space generator matrix, representing M*(G). The edge sums consumed by L(G)'s adjacency constraints already equal third star points. A bridge yields p_e=0. For planar bridgeless G a plane dual represents P graphically, recovering the classical Tait/Four Color method.

For triangle-free cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic G, no six- or seven-point rank-three rule can fire on this residual representation. Regularity excludes Fano. Six rank-three points form Fano minus one point, the cycle matroid of K4. By duality contracting the complementary edges in G would give the cycle matroid of K4. The remaining graph is connected and simple of rank three and has six edges, hence is K4. The four contracted parts in G have three boundary edges apiece. Any nonsingleton such part contains a cycle by cubic edge count (3v−3)/2, and the other three parts contain a cycle through their pairwise interconnections. This contradicts cyclic four-edge connectivity; all singleton parts would give K4, which is not triangle-free. Three-edge cuts isolate vertices by the same cubic counting argument, so root lines are exactly stars. For a chosen root star, any two outer points in one coset form a star with their nonzero sum on the root line. Only the three stars at neighboring vertices qualify. Their outer pairs are disjoint by triangle-freeness and different pairs cannot occupy the same coset because they have different sums and would then furnish additional such stars. Thus there are three doubly occupied exterior cosets and |E|−9 singly occupied ones, before further affine elimination.

## Rank-four local affine hulls on high-girth snarks

Assume cyclic edge-connectivity at least five and girth greater than twelve in the simple cubic G. Each restriction of M*(G) of rank at most four is the dual cycle matroid of J obtained by contracting all edges outside the chosen subset. Delete any isolated graph components arising purely in the notation for contraction. J is connected, its cycle rank d≤4, and every nontrivial cut has size at least three. If J has a K4 graph minor there must be at least four vertices, each with degree at least three. The identity Σ(deg−2)=2d−2≤6 then gives at most six vertices and at most one vertex with degree at least five. Each other vertex represents a connected original part with boundary at most four and a complement containing at least three original vertices. The complement cannot be acyclic: for a cubic graph a nonempty induced forest of c components with boundary b has b−2c vertices, at most two if b≤4. By cyclic five-connectivity the part itself is therefore a tree, also of size at most two. Delete the single exceptional vertex from J, or any vertex if there is none. A cycle in the remainder would lift through the small connected parts to a cycle of length at most twelve in G; the bound includes loops and parallel-edge cycles by simplicity and acyclicity within small parts. Thus J is an apex forest and has treewidth at most two, contradicting a K4 minor. Smaller J cannot have that minor either.

J is planar, and its loopless plane dual D has no K4 minor either: the cycle matroid of the planar dual is the dual matroid and M(K4) is self-dual. A loop in D would come from a bridge of J, which contraction of the 3-edge-connected graph does not create. A loopless K4-minor-free graph is 3-colorable (indeed 2-degenerate after suppressing parallel duplicates). The full family of proper four-color maps on D has full affine hull on its vertex potentials. Fix a 3-coloring and recolor any one vertex to the unused fourth color. Applying invertible global affine permutations of F2² to both assignments realizes every nonzero local difference at that vertex, keeping the other vertices unchanged between assignments. Hence differences span all potential coordinates. Projecting onto edge differences and then parametrizing the spanning column space preserves the full affine hull. In a test subspace W not spanned by its occupied points, the remaining basis images are unrestricted as well.

It follows that after the star quotient, every affine-hull-only local test on a projective subspace of rank at most four has no nontrivial affine consequence, even if all such tests are run. This is a restriction on local nonzero-map hulls, not general degree-four polynomial calculus, not nonaffine projected clauses, and not a lower bound on arbitrary decision procedures. Cyclically five-edge-connected snarks of arbitrarily large girth exist, so the obstruction includes genuine NO inputs. [Kochol, Snarks without Small Cycles](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0095895696900325)

## Finite rank-four nonzero closure

The independent question whether a local table can mandate another nonzero direction has an exact rank-four census. check_rank4_points.py enumerates all 32768 labeled subsets of the 15 nonzero directions of F2^4, and all 256 output maps to F2². To test for an M(K4) minor, first test six occupied points in any rank-three subspace, then repeat after contracting one occupied point (clearing its leading bit in each vector) and simplifying. This suffices because a minor of rank three from rank at most four contracts at most one independent point. The union of kernels of the feasible maps identifies exactly the directions that can take value zero.

Of the 12784 K4-minor-free sets, 420 do force a further nonzero point. In contrast, among the 12364 such sets having at most two projective lines of three occupied points through each occupied point, none forces any additional point to be nonzero. In the cyclically five-connected cubic graphs above, each edge belongs to precisely two three-edge cuts, so the second condition holds hereditarily on all restricted point sets. Embedding a smaller-rank set into rank four adds only free directions. Consequently, starting with the star quotient of a snark in this family, even exhaustive unpinned projective hull AND mandatory-nonzero learning on all rank-at-most-four spaces stabilizes immediately. This statement still does not cover general nonaffine projected tuples, rooted pins, or branching. The example P={1,2,3,4,5,8,9} with the bitmask encoding forces 14 and 15; its point 1 is on three lines, demonstrating why the extra assumption matters.
