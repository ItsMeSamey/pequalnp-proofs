# A six-port same-shore accessory cannot isolate one crossing pairing

Partition six terminal ends into named triples E and F. Assume every terminal holder belongs to the same shore of a bipartite cubic multipole. A crossing pairing pairs the three E ends bijectively with the three F ends and requires three distinct pair colors. **At least two different crossing pairings are feasible.** No embedding or distinct-holder hypothesis is needed.

Cap E by a new star vertex u and F by a new star vertex w. The result G is cubic bipartite, with u,w distinct and on the same shore. Its proper three-edge-colorings give exactly the accessory colorings with crossing pairings: each star sees all three colors. At least one coloring exists by decomposing the cubic bipartite multigraph into perfect matchings. If u and w are in different components, independent color relabelings realize all six relative pairings.

Suppose the crossing pairing were unique, and write e_i,f_i for its corresponding incident edges at u,w. Every perfect matching M extends to a proper three-edge-coloring because its complement is a disjoint union of even cycles (parallel-edge digons included). Therefore e_i belongs to M exactly when f_i does, for each i and every perfect matching.

For completeness the binary differences of perfect matching incidence vectors span the binary cycle space of a regular bipartite multigraph. On any simple even cycle C, start with one copy per edge and change multiplicities on C alternately by plus one and minus one. The resulting cubic bipartite multigraph still has degree three everywhere; its three perfect matchings project to perfect matchings of G. Subtract, modulo two, the corresponding sum for any decomposition of the original graph. The difference is exactly the incidence vector of C and is a sum of differences of matchings. Conversely all matching differences have even degrees. This includes two-edge cycles of parallel edges.

Consequently {e_i,f_i} lies in the cut space, so it is a cut δ(S_i). Each such cut separates u from w. Otherwise complement S_i so that both u and w are inside. Both cut edges then have their inside endpoints on the same bipartite shore. Subtracting the sums of degrees of the two shores inside S_i gives a multiple of three, while the cut edges contribute plus or minus two, a contradiction. A simple u–w path must cross every one of the three cuts, yet among their six distinct edges it uses only its first and last edges. This contradiction proves the bound.

It is sharp. For K3,3 cap any two vertices on the same shore. Relative to their common neighbors, a crossing permutation cannot match any coordinate to itself, and the two derangements are both realized by the two cyclic Latin-square orientations. The corresponding accessory is a three-star with two terminal ends at each leaf. For six distinct holders, replace each leaf with a cube with one vertex removed, attaching its three terminal holders in place of the three old incidences. Their colors must be distinct by parity on the seven-vertex piece, and all permutations extend by permuting the colors of a cube coloring. The signature is unchanged.

Two different pairings can be extracted by testing the nine possible base-color edge pairs, forcing one edge at each star, and finding a perfect matching after deleting their endpoints. Complete a successful base matching by alternating the complementary even cycles. If only one pairing had been seen after all tests, either another feasible pairing would contain an untested off-pair (impossible), or the unique-pair contradiction above would apply. The checker verifies the actual edge colors before returning. Its finite tests recover the sharp K3,3 pairings and exercise 400 seeded cubic bipartite multigraphs.

This excludes the proposed single-accessory filter even if all nine noncrossing-in-the-triple-partition sense pairings are harmless to the core; it does not exclude an assembly using other target-dependent constraints.
