# Two unrestricted columns

Let k≥1 good columns of a bipartite graph have unique physical input-to-output paths in an exact-two positive circuit. Allow two additional arbitrary columns y,w with row neighborhoods A,B. Assume every column has at least two distinct rows and any two columns share at most one row. Write D for the total degree of the good columns. Then the circuit has at least D+|A|+|B|+k+1 gates. Count paths by operand incidences and trim unused gates.

Call the portion without good-column ancestors pure; its inputs may include rows and y,w. As in the single-exceptional-column proof, the good-bearing part is a tree over its k terminal leaves with pure side inputs. At y=w=0 and all rows high, its output is the OR of all good columns. Every two-bearing merger is OR. An OR with one bearing operand is an injector, whose pure operand is identically zero at y=w=0. For each good z there is a distinct one-bearing AND guard whose pure side restricted to y=w=0 equals OR of N(z). Indeed all path filters accept each neighbor singleton and at least one filter rejects the maximal row complement of N(z); no other good column can pass this particular guard since the neighborhoods are incomparable. The false-predecessor/true-singleton trace in that pure guard charges at least |N(z)|−1 physical pure ORs, each accepting two distinct singleton rows in N(z) at y=w=0 and rejecting all rows outside N(z) individually. The charges for different good columns are disjoint by the four-cycle condition. Including k−1 bearing mergers and k distinguished ANDs gives D+k−1 baseline gates. None of the one-bearing injectors are paid.

Let I consist of the internal edges from y to A and w to B, with no good columns. Join the pure operands of all t one-bearing injectors using t−1 auxiliary OR nodes, forming P. At zero good columns every true output must start at such an injector, so t≥1 and P accepts every internal edge. For an I-independent support X, if an injector accepts X then N(z) is contained in the open I-neighborhood of X for each good z below its bearing input. Indeed every one-bearing AND downstream accepts its pure operand at each singleton b in N(z), by the pivotality of z at singleton b on the y=w=0 slice. Keeping all good columns zero and enabling X plus b would transmit the injected one to the root. The resulting positivity requires an I edge, which must go between b and X. In particular P rejects all independent X for which the internal neighborhood contains no complete good neighborhood.

Set C1={y} union B and C2={w} union A. Both are minimal vertex covers of I: the opposite exceptional column witnesses necessity of each named row, and A minus B and B minus A are nonempty. Their complementary supports in all rows and the two exceptional columns are independent, and their internal open neighborhoods are exactly C1 and C2 respectively. Neither C contains the two rows of a good neighborhood. Thus P rejects both complements. It also rejects the assignment consisting of all rows and no exceptional columns, whose internal neighborhood is just {y,w}.

For each C separately, project the entire augmented pure DAG for P to OR gates and aliases. At each AND false on the complement of C choose a false operand, consistently across all uses; at other ANDs choose either operand. This replacement dominates the old function everywhere and preserves zero at that complement (every originally false gate stays false there). The projected output is therefore a disjunction of a subset of C. It still accepts every I edge, so its raw support must equal C by minimality. Every original gate in its reachable cone can pass its original singleton positives to the projected output: all gates downstream on the selected paths are ORs or aliases, and the substitutions only increased gate values. Consequently no pure OR charged in the baseline belongs to this cone. That would put two rows from a single good neighborhood inside C, impossible.

Take the union of these two reachable cones, retaining each selected AND as an actual vertex and each of its selected input incidences. This is a connected graph, rooted at P, with L=|A|+|B|+2−|A intersect B| distinct raw sinks, the union of C1 and C2. All other vertices are uncharged old pure gates or auxiliary ORs and have at most two input incidences. If their count is g then connectedness gives L+g−1 ≤ 2g, so g≥L−1. There are at most t−1 auxiliary gates; adding the distinct t real injectors gives at least L actual extra gates. This union count never adds the same injector separately for the two exceptional slices.

If A and B are disjoint this gives the required |A|+|B|+2 extras directly. Otherwise their only common row is x and one more is needed. Equality at g=L−1 forces every nonraw vertex to have both its physical input incidences in the union, and the underlying connected graph to be a tree (including incidences with multiplicity). The union is thus the full ancestral DAG for P, and is a read-once positive formula on exactly y,w and A union B. At y=w=0 its value is zero because P rejects all rows together. At y=1,w=0 its row function is exactly OR of A: the edges give the lower bound and the projected OR of C2 gives the upper bound. At y=0,w=1 it is likewise OR of B. At y=w=1 every nonempty row subset is positive by monotonicity; only the value on the empty row set remains free. Hence its function is either the edge predicate of I or that predicate with edge yw added. The first contains an induced P4 along a–y–x–w for a in A minus B, and the second has a triangle on y,w,x. Neither is read-once: on P4 the middle pair specializes to AND with the outer pair low and OR with it high; on the triangle any pair switches using the third vertex. Unique occurrences require an invariant least-common-ancestor operation on every genuinely bivariate restriction. Contradiction, so at least L+1 actual extras occur in the one-overlap case.

Total (D+k−1)+|A|+|B|+2 is the claimed bound. No step requires a least circuit or a separate refund for a shared injector. This result concerns two exceptional columns; larger families require another bound for overlapping row labels.
