# An absent exact plane in arbitrarily Tutte-connected nonregular binary hosts

Fix k≥5. Take a finite simple graph G of vertex connectivity at least max(k+15,112) and girth g≥k+15 (existence in high-connectivity-missing-plane.md). Choose six terminals a,b,c,d,e,x consisting of one star center and five neighbors. Delete the five star edges to form G₀. Its vertex connectivity is at least max(k+10,107), and all six terminal pairs have G₀-distance at least g−2 by the star-path argument. Let A₀ be the incidence columns of G₀. Let P={a,b,c,d,e}, let U be the four-dimensional even space on P, and choose four independent named columns T in U, e.g. four of the five vectors P minus one vertex. The ordinary matrix A consists of A₀ and one new ordinary column z={a,b,c,x}. Every column lives in the ambient even space on V(G); named columns and z are independent of one another as z has coordinate x.

The target H=span(P minus {a},P minus {b}) is absent. It has exactly one nonzero weight-two vector, {a,b}. A set of ordinary graph edges alone induces the terminal partition on P, whose corresponding trace consists exactly of vectors even on each block, generated by pairs inside blocks. It cannot produce H. If some graph-edge set together with z produced H, the graph-edge part's trace must be a line in H since adding one column increases the intersection dimension by at most one. This line must be {a,b}, by generation by pairs. Thus a,b are in a common graph-edge component and c,d,e are in three other distinct components. In the quotient by the chosen graph edges each even vector is detected by its vector of component parities. Each generator of H has four odd components (one for the common a,b block and one each for c,d,e). The extra z has at most two odd components as a,b cancel, leaving only c and x. Its quotient image therefore cannot equal that of a generator. This contradiction is independent of the position of x in this partition.

Both A and [A|T] have k-connected binary matroids. Their common spanning graphic restriction A₀ is k-connected by vertex connectivity and girth. Every dependence containing any new columns uses a nonempty independent subset of them and ordinary graph edges with nonzero boundary on the six terminals. Such a set contains a path between two terminals so has at least g−2 graph edges. Hence augmentation creates no circuit of size below k+10. For any partition of the full ground where both graphic sides have at least k−1 edges, monotonicity of both side ranks and unchanged total rank yield λ≥k−1. Otherwise a shore X with at most k−2 graphic edges has spanning complement, since deleting those graph edges cannot disconnect G₀. It has at most k+3 elements after counting up to five new columns, hence is independent by our girth margin. So λ(X)=|X|. The same argument applies to either augmentation. In particular the augmented matroid is simple; named linkage to every ≥4-element ordinary set is four by the definition in the companion. Also any nonzero named vector needs at least g−2 graph edges even when z is allowed: XOR with z, when used, leaves a nonzero vector supported on the six distant terminals by independence from U.

Even the ordinary matroid contains F₇ as a minor. Build a rooted topological K₅ on a,b,c,x,d using only G₀. For each of its ten demanded edges give each endpoint incidence a distinct neighbor outside these five vertices. G₀ minus these vertices is at least 100-connected, hence 10-linked by Thomas and Wollan (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2004.02.013). Link the neighbor pairs disjointly and reattach their terminals. Delete graph edges outside the subdivision, and contract each subdivided path down to its single surviving edge without merging terminals. These incidence contractions sum vertex coordinates, so z remains exactly the weight-four vector on a,b,c,x in the even space of the five surviving terminal vertices; all irrelevant isolated rows may be removed. Contracting z reduces this rank-four space to rank three. With d as root the four star columns are the four distinct images of coordinate units modulo 1111; the other six pair columns give three further distinct points by complementary pairs. The simple restriction on these seven points is F₇. This proves nonregularity of both matroids. The construction is a single-element extension of an ordinary graphic host. It rules out a richness theorem based on nonregularity, high Tutte connectivity, maximal four-name linkage, and large physical leaders. It makes no computational hardness assertion.
