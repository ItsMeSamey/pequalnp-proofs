# Isolated models cannot force clause-by-clause grants from square modules

Assume a formula is compiled to a closed switch network with one four-terminal square module per variable. For a prescribed input value, the module permits all subsets of that sign's two opposite edges, each as disjoint transpositions on its four terminals. Choices in distinct modules and in the remaining exterior are independent; the exterior may contain any number of nonmatching clause switches. Assume assignment-faithfulness: for each full input, some permitted global setting joins the two physical marks exactly when that input satisfies the formula. A grant is one selected square edge, attached to its specific literal occurrence.

Let x be an isolated satisfying assignment (all single flips fail). Take any joining mask. Fix all settings outside one square that has no interior mark. Treat the four square terminals and the two marks as observed cuts in the fixed wiring, retaining mark-only cycles. Its baseline cycles form vertices, and each square transposition is an edge between the incident baseline cycles, possibly a loop. If both sign edges are in use and neither a singleton nor the empty state still joins, the two transpositions necessarily form a simple path between distinct marked baseline cycles A,C. Indeed join implies connectivity, and an edge joining A directly to C alone would merge their cycles. The four endpoint incidences consequently have odd parity precisely at A and C. The other sign's perfect matching has exactly the same four incidences, so its edges also have odd degree exactly at A,C. The handshaking lemma puts A,C in one component. Select its simple A-to-C path using the opposite sign's grants. These transpositions act along a forest and therefore merge the marked cycles. The resulting setting is valid at the single flipped input, contradicting isolation.

Thus each unmarked square can be reduced to at most one grant while preserving the join and every other module setting. Repeating this operation yields one joining mask with at most n+2 grants, since at most two squares contain interior marks and either can use at most two grants.

A strict monotone 3-SAT-(2,2) instance has positive variable triples ade, bef, cfd, abc and negative triples dab, ebc, fca, def (each negative triple denotes a clause of all three negated variables). The assignment with a,b,c true and d,e,f false satisfies all eight clauses. Each true variable is the unique true literal in its indicated positive clause; each false variable is the unique true literal in its indicated negative clause. Therefore the assignment is isolated. Each of its six variables has exactly two occurrences per sign. Two vertex-disjoint copies have 12 variables and 16 clauses and the product assignment is isolated. The sparsified witness uses at most 14 grants. It cannot supply one actual literal occurrence grant to each of the 16 clauses.

This rules out simultaneous assignment-faithfulness and the requirement that every accepting mask provide each clause a selected occurrence through its square. It allows arbitrary exterior topology and arbitrary independent auxiliaries. It does not rule out different variable signatures, nonlocal decoding, or a decision-only reduction. The endpoint substitution argument is detailed in square-substitution.md.
