# Simultaneous one-tag traces at a private seam

Let physical columns k_i have binary tags t_i, and call site i active for a linear functional g if g(k_i)=t_i. A partial word uses active sites and its private column sum is its outgoing vector. Across a supplied decomposition require the real partial words x,y to use the SAME chart; the indexed alternating form Ψ pairs the actual original words. Let B be the linear intersection of the two private spans at a seam. In a region let V be its private span, and take B as a subspace of V. The fixed seam state is α in the dual of B and two outgoing vectors u,v in B.

Append the tag as a genuine extra coordinate. The boundary space is N=B⊕F₂ in V⊕F₂ with distinguished pure tag axis, and the lifted site is (k_i,t_i). Write Aα for the graph of α on B and Uα for its restriction to span(u,v). Partial x,y in a single extending chart exist iff some physical X has
Uα ⊆ span{(k_i,t_i):i∈X}∩N ⊆ Aα.
If the words exist use their union X. Every lifted column of X lies in the graph of g, and their sums include (u,α(u)),(v,α(v)). Conversely the upper containment excludes the pure tag vector, so projection of the chosen span onto its private coordinates is injective and defines a functional on that projection. Its agreement with α on the overlap with B follows from the upper containment (every lifted vector projecting into B belongs to N). Extend the two agreeing functionals jointly by linear algebra. Solve the two desired lifted sums on X separately; the same chart activates both supports. When dim B≤2 the complete table is found by the existing represented three-dimensional named trace algorithm (research/span-separation/three-dimensional-traces.md), enumerating its constant list of possible exact intersections. Dependent named directions and boundary points outside the ordinary span cause no problem: intersect N with the ordinary span first, rejecting a missing demanded direction. Each positive state gives two indexed witnesses and a single chart.

This table suffices for topology, not indexed Ψ. An exact weighted row representation for a fixed seam state consists of a linear basis of vectors (1,x,y,Ψ_region(x,y)) from physical witnesses. For two actual rows on disjoint indexed regions, joining gives the concatenated words and the scalar equal to the two local scalars plus Ψ(x_left,y_right)+Ψ(x_right,y_left). Homogenize constant/linear coordinates using their leading ones to get a bilinear product in the stored row coordinates. Boundary chart restrictions must agree and sums must compose to the requested outgoing vectors, tested on actual linear spaces. Thus enumerating pairs of basis rows and retaining actual independent products computes an exact physical spanning list at a binary join whenever its two child boundary spaces have bounded dimension. More precisely chart interface restrictions on both children and the requested parent boundary must jointly extend in the union span; this is tested independently of row contents by extending compatible local charts: write each child functional on its span, prescribed on its whole child boundary, then add them. The value on the parent boundary is determined by these prescribed child restrictions because a decomposition b=b1+b2 of any parent boundary vector has each bi in that child's boundary. No sum of incompatible physical rows is returned. Row length is O(number of physical sites), irrespective of rank of Ψ. This gives polynomial time on a supplied bounded-branchwidth layout. A high-rank basic torso in a 2/3-sum tree is not itself such a layout.

For an unbounded fanout around one torso there is a different sound reduction. For each child choose a fixed physical default pair and chart for each feasible tuple α,u,v as above. From any global positive witness keep its torso words and all these boundary tuples fixed and switch each child independently between its genuine witness and the stored default. Each child's chart agrees with the torso chart at the physical seam; distinct children are freely glued relative to the torso as in a proper star of represented sums. Each individual default gives the original sums u,v, so the global pair remains two dependencies. On switch bits the scalar Ψ has Boolean degree at most two, and a nonzero evaluation has an evaluation of support at most two relative to the defaults. This proves that at most two exceptional children suffice, even with arbitrary original cross coefficients. Computing which tuple to use simultaneously on every nonexceptional child is a separate torso problem. The result gives neither an unmarked oracle reduction for every rigid torso nor a generic polynomial unbounded-fanout algorithm.

At a one-dimensional interface a through vector is either zero or the single generator k. For each feasible α enabling through, store one physical through row rα and choose (u rα,v rα) as default with coefficients u,v in F₂. For a chart where through is impossible, the zero default handles all admissible tuples. These menus compile to one virtual column k per enabling α with tag α(k), with the stipulated physical rα lift. At a two-dimensional interface naive parallel use of all feasible directions fails, even on five distinct nonzero sites. Fix α(e1)=α(e2)=0, independent internal private coordinates s,h, and take tagged columns (s,0),(s+e1,0),(h,1),(s+h,0),(s+e2,1). If g(s)=0 the first two sites give direction e1 and no other nonzero boundary direction is possible. If g(s)=1 only the last three can possibly close the private coordinates, giving e2 for g(h)=1 and no nonzero direction otherwise. There is no chart supporting words realizing the two directions together. This example tests the simultaneous requirement separately from all topology-independent scalar span tests.
