# Exact classification of the failed five-terminal plane under arbitrary private shifts

Let P={a,b,c,d,e}, U its even vertex space, t={a,b}, and H=span(P minus {a},P minus {b}). A graph on these vertices and others supplies ordinary incidence columns. Add any collection of ordinary columns z_i=s_i+{u_i,v_i}, where s_i lies in U and u_i,v_i are distinct and outside P. The sufficient connectivity threshold below is independent of the number of grafts. On a 37-vertex-connected base graph the plane H is attainable if and only if at least one s_i is outside span(t). Endpoints of different grafts need not be distinct.

If all shifts belong to span(t), quotient by t, identifying a,b. Every graft then becomes a graphic edge. The image of H is the weight-four line on the four named blocks; a graphic trace on the full even space of these four cannot be that line since its traces are generated by pairs. Any original solution contains t in its span and would survive the projection.

Otherwise choose just one graft with shift s outside span(t). In the even space on the four blocks ab,c,d,e its image has weight either two or four. Connect a to b by a path. In the weight-four case also connect u to v by a separate path. In the weight-two case attach u and v separately by paths to a representative vertex of each missing block (use a when the block ab is missing). Arrange all path interiors disjoint and avoiding the seven terminals, with paths meeting only at any intentionally shared endpoint. There are at most three demands. At each endpoint incidence choose a distinct unmarked neighbor avoiding all other chosen neighbors, possible by minimum degree. Delete the seven terminals: the remaining graph is at least 30-connected and therefore 3-linked by Thomas and Wollan. Link distinct neighbor pairs, then restore their incident edges. The required witnesses can be found using the fixed-number disjoint-paths algorithm.

Take exactly the graph edges on these paths. Their component partition of P is ab,c,d,e, so their ordinary trace is span(t). In the weight-four case the private endpoints cancel in their common separate component. In the weight-two case they add one parity at each of the two missing named blocks. Thus z has the same component-boundary image as either h in H outside span(t), including zeros on all unnamed components. Consequently z+h is in the ordinary span while h is not. Adding z increases the trace dimension by at most one; it gives exactly H. This proves the sufficiency direction and constructs an original physical witness.

A general positive diagnostic for an arbitrary four-name host, independent of this graphical model, follows from Seymour's triples-in-circuits theorem as given in Geelen–Gerards–Whittle Theorem 2.1 (https://arxiv.org/html/2611.20960v1#S2). After contracting a physical subset C whose named trace is one line in H, suppose the remaining ordinary representation spans the residual names, is nongraphic and vertically 4-connected after simplification. Appending any three independent named test columns summing to the residual desired line preserves this vertical connectivity (restrict a candidate separation back to the spanning ordinary restriction) and preserves nongraphicness. Thus the three names are together on a circuit; contraction of its ordinary support gives that residual line exactly. Lifting together with C solves the original plane. One may first simplify loops and parallel ordinary elements and restore representative indices. If one contracts only a *virtual* t in H, the same argument guarantees an exact projected line but it may lift to a different line in H instead of the plane. Success after each of the three virtual contractions therefore by itself does not guarantee original feasibility.
