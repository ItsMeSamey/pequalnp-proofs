# Compressing exponential lift choices in rooted circuit sums

With V,U,H,W as in rooted-circuit-sum-plane.md, project by W. Discard projected loops and make a simple matroid M on the distinct nonzero types v of projected physical columns. Pick an original lift l_v. Each physical e above v has offset g_e=A_e−l_v in W. Set b_v=Σ_{e over v}x_e. For a basis h_i of H enumerate minimal TYPE supports C_i spanning the images of h_i in M.

For each tuple with independent union D, set t_i=h_i−Σ_{v in C_i}l_v in W. In the additive group algebra of W^k (k=dim H), take the product over v in D of Σ_{e over v}x_e[z(e,v)], where z(e,v) has i-coordinate g_e if v belongs to C_i and zero otherwise. Take its coefficient c at (t_1,…,t_k). Add c times the ordinary basis polynomial of M/D with free type weights b_v. The result is the exact original trace polynomial over any coefficient ring; a polynomial-time basis evaluator is available for characteristic-two weights.

Indeed, every projected physical basis uses distinct types. In its type basis each projected root has exactly one expression. These expressions lift to the respective actual h_i precisely when the k offset equations hold simultaneously. A single factor for a shared type uses the same chosen physical lift in all equations and counts its weight only once. Each free type contributes its unrestricted physical weight sum. Thus every desired physical basis monomial appears exactly once. For Boolean decision do offset reachability and traceback instead of field arithmetic, extend independent D to a type basis, and choose any physical lifts on additional types. In characteristic two use a weighted Gram determinant for the contraction. At fixed named dimension, runtime is polynomial in the physical input and total sizes of the chosen rooted type-support lists (use the incremental enumeration in the companion note); the offset table has 2^(k dim W) entries. Lists may still be exponential. Plane targets permit any two of three nonzero root directions.

Example: take independent p_1,…,p_(2m),w,z. Include physical columns p_i,p_i+w for all i and additionally p_1+z. Let h_1,h_2 be sums over the first and second halves respectively, W=span(w,z), H=span(h_1,h_2), U=H⊕W. The physical input is simple. The projection is free after simplification, so each of the two projected roots has a single expression. For m≥2 each original root has at least 2^(m−1) minimal physical representations, by choosing an even set of w shifts in its half. Each is independent by projection. Thus projection aggregation can save exponential time compared with listing rooted physical representations even without physical parallel elements.
