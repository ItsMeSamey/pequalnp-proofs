# Arbitrary dense four-space with a vertex-pair direction in the target

Assume even vertex coordinates, arbitrary physical columns including a supplied spanning 600-vertex-connected simple graph G, and dim U=4. Let a target plane H contain a vertex pair t={a,b}. Test graph-only feasibility by the fixed-dimensional connected-partition algorithm. If it fails, H is attainable exactly when some ordinary z has weight ≥4 after identifying a,b. In that case graph edges plus this SINGLE z suffice. To get a witness, enumerate candidate graph-only traces on U+span(z), rank at most five, and test those for which adjoining z gives the required trace on U. Graph partitions are computed with the algorithm in research/span-separation/high-connectivity.md; no further nonconstructive test is needed.

We use that, for r-bit zero-total vertex labels, any abstract ordered partition into h+1 parts can be realized by connected parts with the same sums if connectivity is at least h+2+21hr: the original assignment satisfies the exception congruence criterion of that theorem. To preserve just the span of sums, retain parts giving an independent basis and merge the rest into a background. There are at most r+1 parts and their span is unchanged. Total zero ensures that the basis cannot use every original part unless the collection was empty. Rank zero needs one part. All uses here have h,r≤5, thus threshold ≤532. For incidence columns the kernel of component sums on named coordinates is exactly the graphic trace.

Quotient by t identifies a,b. If every projected physical column has weight zero or two, any original solution descends to a graphic witness of H/t. Split the merged vertex back into its two preimages in the SAME part of this abstract vertex partition. The new component kernel on U is exactly H as its value on a vector is the old value on its image modulo t. Normalize the span and use the preceding high-connectivity theorem on G; this contradicts the failure of the graph-only test.

Otherwise take a projected column z of weight at least four. The simple graph G' after vertex identification and simplification remains at least 599-connected (restore the two preimages of any fused vertex in a separator). Its graphic matroid is vertically 4-connected by the standard graph-to-vertical connectivity implication. The indexed extra column makes it nongraphic: a 3-connected simple graphic restriction determines its graph representation up to isomorphism by Whitney's theorem. A spanned nonloop edge of its extension must be an edge on its vertices, hence must have weight two. The indexed incidence columns fix the ambient row transformation on their span. Appending spanned elements preserves vertical 4-connectivity: restricting a nonspanning separation to the original spanning restriction cannot increase λ or make either side spanning.

Append three independent names forming a basis of U/t with sum the generator of H/t. The matroid still has the nongraphic restriction, and is vertically 4-connected. By Seymour's coupled-triple theorem (GGW Theorem 2.1, https://arxiv.org/html/2611.20960v1#S2), a circuit through these three names exists. Its ordinary support has trace exactly H/t: a proper additional terminal relation would give a proper dependent subset of the circuit. Its support uses graph edges and perhaps z. On the graph-edge component partition only, undo the identification keeping a,b in the same part. The abstract graph-only kernel contains t and its evaluation on every vector in U+span(z) coincides with quotient evaluation at the projected vector. Hence adding z if selected gives exactly H in original U. Normalize these part sums and realize their span with physically connected parts in G. The resulting graphic span has identical intersection with U+span(z); this suffices to preserve the trace on U also after adding z. This proves the characterization and justifies the candidate-trace search. No assumption of sparse remaining names or bounded number of extra columns enters.
