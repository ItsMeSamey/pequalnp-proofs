# Mixed-prime group charges from a bounded parent skeleton

Fix a finite abelian group G and p ordered colors on the vertices of an undirected graph. Prescribe each color's sum and check the whole-graph sum. Use a vertex order and t guessed stopping roots colored by p colors. Every other vertex must have a later physical neighbor. Fix any available choice f(v) of one such neighbor per nonroot. A physical parent map chooses one later neighbor per nonroot; endpoints at roots determine colors. Allow a bounded set of prescribed physical site-to-specific-root demands, including endpoints of connecting edges as in ordered-charged-partitions.md. This proof works in the actual group without choosing a characteristic.

## Existence of a bounded-leaf witness

Given a successful parent map g, retain a set S closed under g, containing all roots and demanded sites; start with the full graph. Fill the omitted sites by following f until their first hit of S, then g to a root. This always terminates because every move increases the order and all roots are retained. Among such S preserving all sums pick one of minimum size. The retained g-arcs are a forest. For each unprotected leaf v (a site with no retained children, neither a root nor demanded), define s(v) to be the first retained site reached strictly after v along f. Its current basin consists of v and all omitted sites whose first hit of S is v. Distinct leaf basins are disjoint. If v is removed from S, this basin goes to s(v)'s color. The change in the first p−1 color sums is a vector in G^{p−1}.

On the unprotected leaves join v to s(v) when the latter is also an unprotected leaf. This is a forest: each arc goes to a later site and there is at most one outgoing arc, excluding undirected cycles. Take an independent set of at least half the leaves. For simultaneous removals from this set all their successors remain, so exactly the specified disjoint basins change to those fixed colors and the changes add. Removing leaves preserves g-closure; protected demands retain their specific roots. Any sequence of |G|^{p−1} group changes has a nonempty zero-sum subsequence by the partial sums argument. Hence at most 2(|G|^{p−1}−1) unprotected leaves were necessary. The last score follows from the conserved total. This bound is on leaves, not on retained vertices or on individual changed arcs.

## Physical construction

Enumerate this bounded list of leaf sources and assign each a specific root. Add every demand as a source, and every root as a source demanding itself. The union of retained paths from these sources to their roots contains the whole forest in a witness above. Sweep sites in order, maintaining pending (site,root) walkers on those paths. At a site with a pending walker coalesce equal demands and reject contradictions. It is retained: either verify its stopping root or move that demand to a guessed physical later parent. Sites with no walker are omitted and use f. These rules share one actual parent decision at each site.

Also maintain an exact group buffer on future f-heads. At v add its accumulated inflow to its original charge. If retained credit this amount to its declared root color; otherwise pass the amount along f(v). This accounts exactly for first-hit basins, including sites carrying zero sums; no charge rides along the guessed retained paths after crediting. At a cut each nonzero buffer position is among the distinct future heads of f from earlier sites. If at most b such heads cross any cut, there are at most |G|^b buffer configurations, plus at most |G|^{p−1} partial score tuples. Use ordinary dynamic programming, storing one physical prefix per identical full state with traceback. At the end check totals and stopping demands. Running time is polynomial for fixed G,p,t,pin count and b. No restriction on the individual path lengths is used. If the order has hereditary sink bound r, t≤pr roots and at most 2(t−p) pins for physical intertree edges suffice, by the earlier ordered partition theorem.

## Circular-arc construction

Use the checked order from circular-parent-charges.md: noncovering intervals in increasing right-end order, then the chosen point's covering clique. For an interval with a later intersecting interval, its default is the later interval with smallest left endpoint (fixed deterministic tie-break). This choice does intersect: among later right endpoints the intersection condition is exactly having left endpoint at most the current right endpoint. All such defaults crossing a cut in the interval section go to the same minimum-left vertex of the remaining interval suffix. Otherwise if the interval touches the covering clique it may go to one of two fixed clique vertices, chosen to maximize their reach on the initial and terminal arms of the cut line. Their union contains the full union of the clique arcs. A preprocessing root with prescribed stop ignores its default; it may still be used as parent. In the clique section choose the last clique site as common parent for its other nonroots. Abort a root guess with a nonroot that cannot move. During interval processing the number of buffer heads is at most three. During clique processing the two fixed landing sites for earlier intervals plus the clique's last site also give at most three. If the covering clique is empty the pure interval construction has one buffer head. Use closed arcs after combinatorial discretization of the intersection representation. Together with the two-sink bound this gives deterministic physical spanning partitions with arbitrary fixed finite abelian labels on recognized circular-arc graphs.
