# A quadratic cofactor statistic for the mod-eight binary basis correction

Let A be a binary r-row matrix of full row rank with its 0/1 integer lift. For an r-set S let M=A_S. Cauchy–Binet counts odd-det bases correctly modulo 8 unless det(M)=2 modulo 4, in which case it contributes an unwanted 4. For such even minors only binary corank one matters: an integer Smith form has at least d even invariant factors for binary corank d. In corank one let x,y be the unique nonzero left/right nullvectors, lifted as 0/1. Then det(M)/2 mod 2 equals xᵀMy/2 mod 2. To see this, choose unimodular changes of row and column coordinates with last row xᵀ and last column y (primitive vectors). Both last row and last column of the changed matrix are even, and the top left minor is odd since binary corank is one. In the determinant expansion all terms except that diagonal block product vanish modulo four. Signs have no effect after division and reduction mod two.

Write c_ij for the cofactor modulo two and let t=Σ_ij M_ij c_ij as an INTEGER. In corank one c_ij=x_i y_j and hence the desired bit equals binom(t,2) mod2. In higher corank t=0. On nonsingular minors the same statistic may be nonzero. Its value is not even constant at fixed rank: for rank three the identity has t=3, whereas the matrix with binary row masks 011,101,111 has t=5, producing opposite bits. Thus the unrestricted quadratic surrogate cannot correct the mod-eight count by itself, or by adding a rank-dependent parity multiple of the bases. Both examples have odd determinant.

The sum of this surrogate over ALL r-sets is nevertheless polynomial-time computable even with weights in any characteristic-two commutative algebra. Expand binom(t,2) over pairs of distinct nonzero positions in M. Fix the two physical column indices j,k and the two row indices i,l. If j=k, the sum of the two cofactor products factors as weight(j) times a mixed Cauchy–Binet determinant on all ordinary columns except j, with rows i and l deleted respectively. If j≠k, require both in S and factor their weights. Each cofactor then includes the OTHER specified column; expand along these forced columns in both determinants. A mixed Cauchy–Binet determinant on common remaining physical columns excluding j,k sums the products of their (r−2)-minors. Signs disappear. This also handles repeated rows i=l; omit zero-order negative-sized cases. The result is a polynomial-time evaluable surrogate plus a residual supported exclusively on actual binary bases. No general mod-eight or mod-32 evaluator follows.
