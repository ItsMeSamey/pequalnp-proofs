# Physical nonclique adhesions and torsos of bounded independence

Fix the number p of connected spanning colors, a finite abelian charge group Gamma, degree D for an optional nonzero indexed polynomial over one effective field, and constants a,s. Supply a polynomial-size tree decomposition in which each actual induced bag has independence number at most a and each adhesion has cardinality at most s. No physical or virtual clique condition is needed. These conditions are directly checkable for fixed a,s. The algorithm reconstructs original witnesses, including for joint group sums and a cross-piece polynomial.

Guess one distinct physical anchor per color, fixing their colors; add all p anchors to every bag and put a synthetic bag consisting of the anchors above the old root. Run the algorithm on this enlarged decomposition of the ORIGINAL graph: no edges are added. The new independence and adhesion bounds are a+p and s+p. For a child define its strict interior by excluding its parent adhesion B. Every connected component of its strict interior has an actual neighbor in B, by connectedness and separation from the synthetic root. Choose a fixed such owner for each component.

For every subtree tabulate a boundary coloring, an equivalence relation on its boundary refining colors, and the interior group totals per color (boundary charges excluded). A successful assignment realizes all specified same-block boundary connections and attaches every interior site to a boundary site of its color inside that physical subtree. Additional same-color connections are allowed. Store actual interior degree-D evaluation rows forming a basis of all feasible rows per state, including the constant coordinate. As usual learn such a basis with polynomially many separator-polynomial queries at the node. These queries use only already stored child tables, with no recursive calls to rebuild them.

Fix a true assignment accepted by one such query at a node with torso A. Each physical monochrome induced subgraph on A has at most a+p components, because taking one vertex from each gives an independent set. There are therefore at most h=p(a+p) torso pieces. Each child interior attaches to its boundary in this assignment. Its same-color paths can join distinct torso pieces. Form the graph of all these joins on the at most h pieces and keep children realizing a spanning forest of its joins, at most h children. This suffices to preserve every connection among torso vertices previously made through any child, since any physical path decomposes into torso edges and single-child excursions. In particular all torso pieces can still reach the parent boundary and realize its required connections.

Default a remaining child by assigning every strict-interior component entirely to its fixed boundary owner's color. This attaches all its original sites, possibly adding helpful connections. Conditional on the torso colors, switching one whole child between its old assignment and default changes the first p−1 group totals by one vector in Gamma^{p−1}, and affects site indicators only in that child's interior. Among the remaining nondefault children, if there are at least (D+1)|Gamma|^{p−1}, select D+1 disjoint zero-sum packets each of size at most |Gamma|^{p−1}. Simultaneously resetting any selection of these packets preserves all totals and connections protected by the forest. Every site indicator is affine in at most one packet bit, so a degree-D polynomial nonzero at the original coloring cannot vanish at every nonempty toggle. Repeat to retain at most h+(D+1)|Gamma|^{p−1} exceptional children. Any incoming physical pins can be included as indicator factors in the predicate, or made boundary anchors; the degree or boundary parameters then include them.

To answer the local polynomial query enumerate these exceptional children, their full states and actual representatives, and at most h distinct connected fragment labels on the torso mapped to original colors. Pin the parent boundary sites and every exceptional child adhesion site to guessed torso fragment labels. Use the stored child relations to merge those fragments, requiring every torso fragment to reach a fragment containing a parent boundary site, and requiring all parent connections. This test uses promised connectivity and may disregard extra physical edges between different fragment labels of the same original color. It is complete by choosing the actual torso monochrome components and the children of the preceding spanning forest; it is sound because all claimed joins have real paths. Child interiors attach to torso sites by their stored records or their default assignments.

Subtract the selected children’s actual group totals. Fold all default child contributions into their actual owner sites, with torso original charges zero on the parent adhesion. Enumerate fragment totals consistent with the remaining original-color totals. In the local predicate substitute each default interior indicator by its owner's color indicator, and the selected child interior indicators by their stored physical assignments; each torso color indicator sums the indicators of its chosen fragment labels. Degree cannot increase. At the original true torso coloring, some tuple of stored child rows yields nonzero: the polynomial after fixing every other site is linear in each selected child's degree-D evaluation row, so replace children successively within their true state. Enumeration is polynomial at fixed parameters because the number of exceptional children is bounded.

The torso query for a connected partition into those fragment labels with group totals, all pins, and the single polynomial is effective. For an induced bag of independence number q≤a+p, take any maximal independent set M, put its vertices last in any order, and default all other sites to their actual M neighbor. All induced sink sets are independent, so each connected fragment has at most q increasing roots; link those rooted trees by boundedly many pinned physical edges. Default heads lie in M, all of which are global sinks and must be stopping roots. The exact joint group and polynomial skeleton algorithm in joint-parent-predicate.md applies. The parent-map search and bridge test work equally on a disconnected torso: every required individual fragment must be connected, and every site must reach one of its actual roots. Empty torsos have the trivial empty assignment only.

At the synthetic root every actual color has a unique anchor; the real root's attachment condition now guarantees global connectivity. Include the anchors' original scores exactly once. The resulting positive assignment or any learned evaluation row is physical. Polynomial exponents may depend on all fixed parameters but do not grow with tree height or branching. This is a theorem on a supplied decomposition; the bounded independence of a bag is used for its number of actual color components and for its local partition oracle.
