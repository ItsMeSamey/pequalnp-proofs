# Exact shared selectors for simultaneous marked-connection constraints

Guard j is a closed physical permutation routing with a specified pair of marks. At each site g a logical selector i chooses a physical rotation through a supplied lookup table; occurrences of the same i, even in other guards, take the same value a. Give each logical option positive rational probability (uniform in the reference). Quantification is existential over ONE simultaneous assignment. Coincident-mark guards can be discarded for decisions. No aggregation into one physical two-root routing is assumed.

Apply the rational marked-cofactor common-row quotient separately in each remaining guard. Write V_j for its dimension twice the site count, and w_{g,a} for the ordered bivector of a physical option in that quotient. Each site's full option support has dimension at most four. Embed all these spaces into V=⊕_j V_j. Define the local even form
F_i=E_a ∏_{g reading i}(1+w_{g,a}).
Its constant is one. Expanding ∏_i F_i chooses the selector once for the whole product, and its top coefficient, up to fixed orientation, is the expected product of the absolute projected guard determinants. To reach the total ambient degree every site must contribute its bivector in every guard. Each product is nonnegative up to the same sign because each guard has one cofactor orientation independent of settings. Thus nonvanishing is exactly simultaneous satisfiability. Products of independently averaged guard cofactors would in general answer a different question.

Write log F_i=A_i+D_i with A_i quadratic and D_i of degrees at least four. If selector i has at most q site occurrences, all local powers are supported in at most 4q dimensions, so the logarithm of 1+u is the finite sum through power 2q. Compute A=Σ_i A_i and D=Σ_i D_i in N^{O(q)} rational bit operations up to polynomial factors in the explicit input sizes. Set t=dim supp D, using the spans of all (k−1)-fold coordinate contractions of its degree-k parts. The product above is exp(A+D). Compute exp(D) in its t-dimensional support and use complementary principal Pfaffians of A to evaluate its top coefficient in 2^{O(t)}N^{O(q)}. Rational denominators have polynomial bit length for fixed q; exterior products and Pfaffians use polynomial degrees. See native-fermionic-attribution.md for the matchgate/Gaussian-nullity interpretation of this compression.

For arbitrary overlapping selectors this net-t bound is a decision bound. Original choices can always be recovered at the stronger parameter dim(sum supp D_i), since conditioning factors out all chosen pairs and then discards the fixed local logarithmic corrections after projection. The following condition yields actual witnesses at the original net parameter.

Suppose all shared selectors are Boolean, each occurs at most once per guard, and any two guards share at most one selector. Guard-specific selectors may follow the single-site setting. Every local form and D have even degree in each V_j. Independent sign flips on the individual V_j preserve D, so its minimal support S is invariant and splits into the direct sum of its intersections S_j with V_j. Projecting to each multihomogeneous degree therefore leaves forms supported in S. For distinct guards j,k sharing only Boolean i, the bidegree (2,2) projection of D with every other guard degree zero is p(1−p)Δ_j∧Δ_k, where Δ_j=w_{j,1}−w_{j,0} and p is the probability of option one. This follows by subtracting the product of means in the degree-four logarithm. If both differences are nonzero this pure tensor exposes both their supports inside S: contract the other guard against a functional with nonzero pairing, then take single contractions in the first.

Fix a choice of a selector. In each guard where its chosen pair is independent of the pairs already fixed, quotient by these actual vectors; a dependent fixed choice cannot succeed. The chosen w projects to zero, and the alternate now projects to the signed image of Δ. If i has at least two nonzero differences, every such difference is exposed by a unique overlapping guard pair, and all the projected D_i lives in the image of S. If at most one difference remains nonzero in projection, the single surviving alternate bivector is decomposable, so the projected F_i has quadratic logarithm and its projected nonlinear correction vanishes. A selector with a single occurrence has the same property by the local four-space bound. The new nonlinear sum after deleting that selector is the image of D minus its projected D_i, hence is supported in the image of S. Induction preserves the initial t along witness self-reduction. Flattening a homogeneous component and projecting after already chosen pairs use only the actual rational vectors. The code tests complete physical assignments at the original lookup tables.

The algorithm is for a correlated family of native constraints. A polynomial-time reduction to this family or a list of separate native queries is not a construction joining all constraints in one physical circuit.
