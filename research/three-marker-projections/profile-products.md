# A recognizable finite separator profile and Cartesian products

Replace expansion in expander-low-separators.md and expander-binary-bank.md by the following polynomially recognizable criterion. Fix bounded Δ and a computable nondecreasing function g on the nonnegative integers (enlarge to at least 1 if desired), as well as the original predicate parameters. There are effective constants K,h,N such that it suffices for a connected graph on n>N sites to have maximum degree ≤Δ, contain a K_h minor, and have for every deletion X of ≤K sites one remaining component containing all but at most g(|X|) of the surviving sites. Test all such X by component search, and test fixed clique minor containment constructively. This is a sufficient test. For n≤N solve by enumeration directly.

At a splitting stage of order s≤3 the old argument gives a center C, every fringe of size at most g(s), degree growth at most a factor s, and |C|≥n/(1+Δ_current g(s)). Every remaining separator profile value up to K transfers WITHOUT INCREASE. Indeed choose X⊆C, |X|≤K, and project paths in the original surviving largest component onto the filled torso via its fringe cliques; all vertices of that component in C belong to one torso component. At most g(|X|) surviving bag vertices lie outside it. For large n it is the unique giant by the size bound.

A clique minor of order h>g(s)+s transfers undiminished to C. A branch wholly outside C would be inside one strict fringe. Every other branch not wholly in that fringe must then meet its adhesion to be adjacent to the first branch. At most s branches meet the adhesion, so at most g(s)+s branches altogether. Thus all branches meet C. Their intersections with C are disjoint and connected after shortcutting; every interbranch edge in a fringe projects to two distinct adhesion vertices in the corresponding branches, adjacent in the filled clique. Actual edges in C remain edges. This is only a torso-model argument; the physical owner and exceptional-path expansions for partition witnesses are supplied separately by the low-separator proof.

Choose K after the finite pin, bank, short-path, small-color and branching bounds are computed, with g(v+2d)+2d replacing the old damaged-graph nongiant bound for v lifted deletions (including contractions). Include orders needed to remove all modified sites and nonsingleton preimages, and s≤3. No circular choice occurs: the finite recurrence evaluates the fixed total function g at finitely many bounded arguments before selecting K. Let h exceed all required rooted-linkage clique sizes by the maximum number of sites discarded to remove damaged endpoints, small regions, test sites and contracted classes (at most twice the number of contracting edges for the nonsingleton classes). Physical site removal hits at most that many branch sets, leaving a clique minor large enough in the routing graph. Choose N to accommodate all giant, distant-sink, and size requirements through three linear-size centers. These choices remove any appeal to a growing balanced-separator estimate. Thus the finite profile yields actual connected charged partitions for the full quadratic predicate, with deterministic traceback and a checkable host certificate.

For a Cartesian product of three connected bounded-degree graphs with each factor order >k, deleting k sites dirties at most k values of each coordinate. All slices at a clean coordinate value are connected and undamaged; slices belonging to two distinct coordinate directions intersect. Their union is connected and all omitted sites use three dirty values, at most k³ sites. Hence g(k)=k³ uniformly for k below every factor order. The product contains every fixed clique minor when the three factor orders are sufficiently large. Each sufficiently large connected graph of bounded maximum degree contains a sufficiently long simple path by a distance-ball bound. Inside their Cartesian product reserve columns along the third axis with first two coordinates (i,i). At a dedicated third-coordinate layer for each i<j, join the i and j columns by the two coordinate segments through (i,j). This meets no third column, and different routes have different layers. Assign every interior point of each route to the i branch. This is an indexed physical K_h model. In particular large cubic grid boxes [n]³ satisfy the checkable criterion and admit the quadratic charged-partition algorithm although their vertex expansion tends to zero (bisect by a coordinate plane). The same holds for sufficiently large three-factor products without a supplied Cartesian factorization, since the finite graph tests themselves suffice. The guarantee given here requires all three factor orders beyond the fixed thresholds; no assertion for arbitrary narrow products is needed.
