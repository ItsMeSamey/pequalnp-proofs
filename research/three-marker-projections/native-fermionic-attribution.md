# Relation to fermionic Gaussian compression

For an even exterior form D with no terms of degree below four and a quadratic form A, set Ψ=exp(A+D) in the complex exterior Fock space. Its constant coordinate is one. A linear fermionic annihilator (contraction by α plus exterior multiplication by z) annihilates Ψ precisely when z=−ι_α A and ι_α D=0: differentiate the exponential and multiply by its invertible exterior inverse. Consequently the dimension of the linear annihilator space is dim(V) minus the support dimension of D. After normalizing Ψ this identifies the computed support dimension with Gaussian nullity; independent annihilators can be orthonormalized in the usual canonical anticommutation inner product. This applies both to the single-guard form D=−Ω/2 and to the higher-degree joint defect.

The evaluation as a sum of Gaussian/Pfaffian coefficients belongs to Bravyi's matchgate and Grassmann framework, and Proposition 5 of Mele–Herasymenko relates Gaussian nullity of a pure state to its minimum number of uncompressed modes. See Sergey Bravyi, [Contraction of matchgate tensor networks on non-planar graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/0801.2989), and Antonio Anna Mele and Yaroslav Herasymenko, [Efficient Learning of Quantum States Prepared with Few Fermionic Non-Gaussian Gates](https://doi.org/10.1103/PRXQuantum.6.010319).

The specific routing steps here construct the logarithmic defect directly over the rationals from indexed physical menu options, evaluate marked-connection cofactors with a uniform sign, and recover original shared selectors under the stated conditioning hypotheses. No priority claim is made for the general Gaussian compression or the complementary Pfaffian method.
