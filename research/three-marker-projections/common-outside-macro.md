# Shared character coordinates for common-exterior tests

Use p,G,B,E from common-outside-joint.md. Set L=(p−1)(exp(B)−1) and k=E+(2E+1)L. The universally extensible site bound improves to |G|k+E.

Fix every whole-weight type occurring at most k times to its witness colors. Reserve a maximum-degree monomial support U of the remaining Q, of size ≤E. For each common type w reserve 2E+1 disjoint packets outside U. Each needs (p−1)(ord(w_B)−1) copies. Their total per type together with its U sites fits the available multiplicity. All rare sites and packets together use at most |G|k; add U. One packet encodes an arbitrary y in J_w=⟨w_B⟩^(p−1). Allocate ord(w_B)−1 sites per nonbaseline color, of which the first j receive that color to encode jw_B. All others receive the baseline color. The score contribution is y. Ignore trivial J_w.

For any exterior fixing, consider the product domain of independent colors on U and independent packet coordinates. Fixing every packet to zero still preserves the chosen maximum monomial of Q on U. Thus Q remains nonzero. Each original site in a packet depends on only one coordinate, so every term of Q has tensor degree ≤E in the product domain. Use the full character basis on each J_w over the splitting field, simultaneously for all characters. Use a constant-first basis on U. The residual target lies in H generated by the J_w. Every nontrivial character of H is nontrivial on some J_w. Its term in the target-indicator Fourier expansion carries an explicit nonconstant basis factor on all 2E+1 packets of this type. Every elementary term of Q leaves ≥E+1 untouched, so the projection onto support degree ≤E contains only the nonzero trivial-character contribution Q/|H|. An accepting assignment exists on the product domain. This bounds the physical sites independently of |B|^(p−1); all graph algorithm conclusions carry over without constructing characters.
