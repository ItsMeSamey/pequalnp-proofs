# Constructing physical connected-partition moments on bounded mim-width

Let G have a supplied binary decomposition tree on its indexed vertices. For every shore A of a tree edge, the bipartite graph of physical edges between A and its complement has induced matching number at most c. For fixed c this can be verified by enumeration. Fix p named nonempty colors, arbitrary site charges in a fixed finite abelian group Gamma, bounded-degree D, and work over F₂. Pins on site colors are permitted. The algorithm constructs a family of actual connected spanning partitions whose evaluation rows on all indicator monomials through degree D span all such rows, separately at each group-total tuple. In particular, any explicit high-rank alternating form on indexed physical cuts can be imposed through its degree-two expansion. No restriction applies to which pairs of sites occur in that form.

Guess distinct physical anchors, one per color, and pin these choices. For a particular actual coloring x on A meeting its local pins, form the connectivity feature K_A(x). For every assignment of an auxiliary subcolor 0 or 1 to each *connected component* of each color inside G[A], forcing each anchor in A to have subcolor 0, take its signature: for each original color i and subcolor j, record the physical set of neighbors OUTSIDE A of all sites with colors (i,j). The feature coordinate at this tuple of sets is the parity of partial assignments with the signature. All possible single neighborhood unions across this cut number at most sum_{h=0}^c binom(n,h), because each inclusion-minimal subset producing a union has private neighbors forming an induced matching. Thus all signatures across all actual x fit in n^{O(cp)} coordinates. Their parity vector for any fixed x is computed by a DP over its monochromatic components, adding each component's external neighbor set by union to its two alternative or one pinned subcolor slot. This construction uses original neighborhoods, even when many vertices have identical neighborhoods.

For a fixed exterior coloring y satisfying its anchors and pins, connectivity of all colors in x union y is *linear* in K_A(x). To see this, for each signature count the exterior auxiliary component assignments compatible with it: whenever an exterior site of original color i has subcolor j, it cannot lie in the recorded neighboring set for (i,1−j). Exterior anchors have subcolor zero. A compatible combined assignment is exactly a subcolor constant on each global monochromatic component. There are 2^{sum_i(cc_i−1)} such choices since every color has its anchor. Modulo two this is one precisely when all p global colors are connected. This linear functional is used only in the proof; no exterior enumeration is required by the algorithm.

Let M_A(x) be all values of monomials in local site-to-color indicators through degree D, including the constant 1. Combine repeated indicators using their Boolean identities. For each local group-score tuple retain linearly independent ORIGINAL features K_A(x) tensor M_A(x), with physical assignments. At fixed y any global polynomial P of degree at most D specializes to a linear function of M_A(x). The product of the connectivity bit and P(x,y) is accordingly a linear function of this tensor. Group labels need not have characteristic two: their exact totals are the separate buckets.

Compute tables from leaves to root. At a join enumerate all pairs of actual retained assignments in the two child tables and extend them by union. Recompute the parent's component-parity and monomial features from these actual sites and reduce each group bucket by Gaussian elimination. Universal representative invariant: for any actual assignment x in the subtree and exterior y and any degree-D P giving a global connected nonzero solution, some stored assignment of equal partial group score works with that SAME exterior and P. Before parent reduction this holds by replacing the first child while fixing the second child and exterior, then replacing the second while fixing the first replacement and exterior. Parent reduction preserves it by the explicit tensor functional. At the root the only outside-neighborhood signature is empty, and its connectivity bit vanishes on disconnected partitions; thus every nonzero retained row is an actual connected spanning partition's moment row. Every linear functional nonzero on any actual connected moment row is nonzero on a retained row, yielding the claimed full span. Unite the tables across all anchor guesses and reduce actual moment rows once more. The final family has size at most the number of degree-D monomials per desired group tuple, independent of anchors. This also constructs an unweighted graph-only family simultaneously testing every degree-D score polynomial.

At fixed p,D,c,Gamma all tables and joins are polynomial, with running time n^{O(cp+D+p+1)} and fixed-parameter factors for Gamma. Traceback may store the whole physical assignment. Conversely any reported row can be checked in G. This is a constructive moment extension of the connectivity and neighborhood-equivalence framework of Bergougnoux and Kanté, including their corrected partition theorem (https://doi.org/10.1137/20M1350571 and https://doi.org/10.1137/23M157644X). The proof above gives the needed factorization and arbitrary-site polynomial preservation explicitly. It does not find an optimal graph layout or assert a polynomial construction of such families on all graphs.
