# Modulo-eight binary basis evaluation and parity of exact three-root lines

For an explicit binary matrix of rank r, evaluating the basis polynomial modulo 8 is polynomial-time Turing equivalent to evaluating, in characteristic two, the exact-line polynomial for three independent named columns. The latter sums weights of independent physical (d−2)-sets S in a d-dimensional ambient space whose span intersects the three-dimensional named space U in the specified line. This statement holds for scalar weights and in matched ring pairs R=(Z/8)[z]/f, k=R/2R with f monic lifting a binary irreducible; conversion reduces or lifts coefficients as appropriate. It gives neither unconditional evaluator. If a representation does not span the ambient space physically, basis queries requiring ambient rank are returned zero when physical columns do not span.

Use the 0/1 lift of the binary matrix. For an r-set S with submatrix M write c_ij for its unsigned cofactor reduced modulo two, as a bit. Define integers t_i=Σ_j M_ij c_ij. Laplace expansion modulo two makes all t_i congruent to det M. In binary corank one, with binary left/right kernels x,y, the cofactor c_ij=x_i y_j and det(M)/2 agrees mod two with xᵀMy/2 (see the unimodular argument in binary-basis-mod8-defect.md). Each t_i is even, so the sum of their integer halves modulo two equals Σ_i binom(t_i,2) mod two. In corank at least two all the cofactors vanish. On a basis every t_i is odd, so binom(t_i,2) and binom(t_i,3) have the same parity, by the binary digit rule. On a singular matrix binom(t_i,3) is even. Consequently the bit [det M ≡ 2 mod 4] for every S equals Σ_i [binom(t_i,2)+binom(t_i,3)] mod two.

Sum against products of reduced weights in k. The quadratic sums are polynomially computable by mixed Cauchy–Binet after enumerating two forced columns and one common omitted row, as in the companion defect note. For the cubic sums fix row i and three distinct columns a,b,c meeting row i, chosen in a fixed order. Factor their three weights and remove these columns from ordinary choices; delete row i throughout. The new ambient dimension is d=r−1. Regard a,b,c as names in this ambient space. For an ordinary (d−2)-set S′ the coefficient product c_ia c_ib c_ic is one exactly if every pair of names extends S′ to an ambient basis. If the three names have rank below two or a pair is dependent the value is zero. If the three have rank two and every pair is independent, all complement tests agree and one ordinary projected basis evaluation in characteristic two suffices. Otherwise the names are independent. Then S′ plus each pair being a basis means S′ is independent, S′+U spans the ambient space, and its two-dimensional quotient gives three distinct nonzero name images. Their sole relation is a+b+c, so the trace is exactly its line. Conversely a (d−2)-dimensional independent S′ with this trace satisfies all three pair tests. Use the stated three-root oracle, allowing separate indexed names (or supply them as virtual copies). The number of queries is O(r n³). When r<3 the cubic sum is empty. Integer Cauchy–Binet evaluated in R gives the weighted sum of det(M)². Subtract four times the lift of the computed weighted correction, since odd squares are 1 modulo 8 and even squares are 0 or 4. This returns the binary basis polynomial in R.

For the reverse, for each of seven named planes Q query the binary basis polynomial of the physical columns projected modulo Q, at lifts of the given weights in R. A contributing (d−2)-set is independent and spans together with U, so its trace is a line. For the seven possible lines v and seven nonzero covectors n on U, the disjointness coefficient for the plane ker n is the bit n(v). Let K denote this seven by seven matrix with rows indexed by lines and columns by covectors. Every row sum is four, different rows have two common ones, and KKᵀ=2I+2J. Hence K(2Kᵀ−J)=4I. The appropriate column gives a signed integer combination of the queried basis polynomials equal to four times the desired exact-line polynomial. Reduce the combination modulo eight, divide each canonical residue coefficient by four and reduce modulo two. The resulting value in k is independent of lift choices. No inverse of four in R is used. In particular the seven-probe identity entails no promotion from a scalar mod-eight oracle to arbitrary extension weights.
