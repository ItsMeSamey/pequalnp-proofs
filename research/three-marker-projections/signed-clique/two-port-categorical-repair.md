# Arbitrary projected rank on two-port trees of highly connected torsos

The scalar saturation hypothesis from two-port-signed-torso-closure.md can be removed for arbitrary fixed original k,r when the local signed torso oracle handles bounded-arity moment functions. This addendum gives such an oracle on sufficiently highly vertex-connected simple skeletons. It does not claim an arbitrary-degree expansion bound for bounded-connectivity hosts.

**Conditional forest.** For each two-port descendant and fixed projected group, determine attainable projected gains of simple u-v paths by the fixed finite-group prescribed path solver. For each attainable δ take a path of that gain, extend to a spanning tree, split at one edge on that path to get F_δ. Its two rooted trees and that crossing edge realize δ. For unattainable values use any fixed two-root spanning forest. Given the endpoint states always use F_δ for δ=x_u+x_v (also if parts differ). Build ONE fixed virtual bundle: for each δ keep precisely those crossing full gains of F_δ whose projection equals δ. At least one edge exists as the child is connected. For any actual endpoint assignment, the resulting inactive bundle matches the crossing options of its chosen default and preserves any previously possible same-part connection. Ignore all conditional DEFAULT internal cycle credits. In the sparse exception proof choose empty replacements for zero-boundary chains, and choose forest-tree plus single crossing for nonzero-boundary chains. The discrepancy argument keeps enough exceptional children to restore every required full gain; it never needs the omitted internal credits. Every virtual chain still lifts and additional physical cycles cannot harm a lower bound.

For each nonexceptional child, tabulate its complete nonport moment for every pair of possible endpoint states using these actual rooted assignments. This is now an arbitrary finite function of the two port states. Sum all such functions when testing torso moments, adding actual exceptional contributions separately. These tables are computed on original descendant vertices, hence their arity remains two at every depth. The parent-boundary auxiliary marker and the global child tables are unchanged. If a local gauge normalization changes the meaning of a switch bit, precompose each unary or binary moment table with the inverse state translations at its actual vertices, including externally pinned values. Virtual edge gains change by the new coboundary too.

**Categorical repair lemma.** Let each free vertex have a fixed finite list of states. For K prescribed bits, each the XOR of local functions of arity at most d, the Boolean indicator of joint satisfaction equals the product of K factors 1+bit+target over F2. Choose a baseline state at each vertex. The categorical multilinear basis consists of products of nonbaseline-state indicators, using at most one indicator per vertex. It is a basis of all functions by the one-vertex basis and tensor multiplication; distinct indicators on one vertex multiply to zero, equal indicators are idempotent. Consequently the indicator has degree ≤dK. Compute its coefficients through that degree by Möbius inversion over the support of each assignment differing from baseline on ≤dK vertices. Identically zero is precisely infeasible. For any nonzero coefficient of maximum degree let R be its ≤dK vertices and select the specific nonbaseline alternative in that monomial for each. After fixing ANY states outside R and restricting each R vertex to baseline or its one chosen alternative, the coefficient of the product of all repair bits remains one: any additional outside variables would require a strictly higher-degree term. Thus SOME choice of the two states per R vertex repairs the bits, regardless of all other free baseline choices. This holds also for varying vertex lists and degree zero.

**Local torso oracle.** In signed-high-connectivity-traces.md use a bounded given pinned set and permit arbitrary unary/binary tables in K target bits instead of tensor moments. Keep the same fixed T, prescribed used parts, and desired lower gain credit H rather than exact span. Guess holes P, their states and all needed real portals as there. After these pins are fixed, each outside vertex can take any selected bin state; form the categorical indicator with these lists and the fixed bits. No part-count condition need appear in the polynomial: an actual witness gives a nonzero assignment, and one root per inhabited bin forces nonempty bins later. Apply the lemma with d=2. In place of charge exceptions and bases use the ≤2K repair vertices; each receives just two candidate entries. Use the complete L-model linkage to attach each fixed portal/pin once and each repair vertex to both eligible bin/port states by independent rooted requests, plus the usual cycles spanning L∩T-perp. All fixed or repair sites are bounded irrespective of n and edge density. Grow the forest outside the bounded choice-dependent sites. The bridge-free multi-source construction of the high-connectivity proof fixes every nonrepair vertex to an allowed menu value without consulting the repair choices. Now use the categorical lemma, or enumerate its bounded two-state cube, to satisfy all K bits. The finite skeleton only needs to contain the image of H modulo L; the inserted internal cycles still cover L∩T-perp. Connectivity and lower gain claims hold exactly as before.

For uniform constants enlarge n(b) in the high-connectivity proof to cover bin roots, bounded external pins, bJ2^k portals and 2K repair vertices, and use the same backward subgroup-size recurrence. In the two-port application the group may be k+1 for a marked parent edge, numbers of pins and K=r(p+t) are fixed. Require the original torso connectivity to exceed this constant by e+2, where e bounds exceptional children. Losing ≤e underlying adjacencies decreases vertex connectivity by at most e (for n larger than parameter bounds); one way to see this is that any separating vertex cut after deletions can be augmented by one endpoint of each restored crossing edge, or use the standard mixed vertex/edge connectivity bound). Labelled virtual duplicates can be retained as parallel gain options with fixed multiplicity; the ambient ordinary minor, connectivity and distinct-stub arguments use the simple adjacency. No torso interface increases with recursive depth. Thus arbitrary fixed-rank signed exact trace is polynomial on supplied two-port trees of sufficiently highly connected torso skeletons. The counterexample cd347236 still excludes a single unconditional forest but does not apply to these conditional defaults.
