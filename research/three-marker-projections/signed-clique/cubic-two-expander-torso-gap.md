# A cubic obstruction to the present fixed-adhesion torso catalogue

Fix any adhesion a≥1. Use the canonical filled torso on bag B: take G[B] and complete each actual N(C) for a connected component C of G−B. These are the virtual adjacencies witnessed by physical paths through C. An adhesion-a tree decomposition implies |N(C)|≤a. Filling unrelated vertices that occur in a nonminimal separator without a physical connector is not this torso.

Take two copies of an n-vertex simple 3-connected cubic vertex expander with expansion α uniform. Such families exist by Wormald (https://doi.org/10.1016/S0095-8956(81)80021-4) and Kolesnik–Wormald (https://doi.org/10.1137/120891265); equivalently fix the first suitable labelled graph by finite exhaustive test, without asserting efficiency of that choice. Choose m=floor(sqrt(n)) edges forming a matching in each half (greedy gives linear size) and subdivide each once. Match the m new vertices across the two halves in a bijection. The resulting G is simple cubic and 3-connected. Indeed after deletion of ≤2 vertices all remaining ORIGINAL vertices in each half stay connected: deleting a subdivision vertex removes at most one old edge, and a 3-connected graph stays connected after any two vertex/edge deletions (after one vertex it is 2-connected, and with no vertex deletion it is 3-edge-connected). A surviving subdivision vertex missing both old endpoints can occur only when both deletions were in that half, and it is attached across its matching edge. For large m some crossing path joins the two original cores despite the deletions.

Each modified half has uniform expansion α'>0. Assign every new vertex to a different old endpoint, making connected fibers of size ≤2. For any vertex cut X let b be its number of crossing edges in the half. At most b fibers split, and old edges crossing Y=X intersect old vertices use edge-disjoint paths crossing the new cut. Thus min(|Y|,n−|Y|)≤b/α. Both sides have size at most twice their old side size plus b, giving min(|X|,|half−X|)≤(2/α+1)b. Degree three yields α'=1/[3(2/α+1)].

Delete ≤a arbitrary vertices. In each half some component exceeds half its original order when n is large (otherwise group components into a set between one quarter and one half, contradicting expansion). All other surviving components in that half have total size ≤a/α'. At most 2a+2a/α' matching endpoints fail to be in their respective giants, so at least one crossing joins the giants. Across the whole graph at most s=ceil(2a/α') surviving vertices lie outside that unique giant.

Orient every edge of an arbitrary adhesion-a decomposition toward the strict side containing this giant after deleting the adhesion. The opposite strict side has size ≤s. At a sink bag B every incident fringe has size ≤s outside the bag; hence every component C of G−B has ≤s vertices and ≤a actual neighbors. Write B_L,B_R for B's portions in the two halves. Any fringe component meeting the left half either touches B_L or uses an original matching edge: all its connections to the bag cannot lie exclusively on the right without crossing the interface. At most 3|B_L| components can touch B_L, and at most m other such components use distinct matching edges. Therefore n+m≤(1+3s)|B_L|+sm, and likewise for B_R. Both have linear size.

The canonical filled torso on B has degree ≤3a, as each of ≤3 original incident choices either joins another bag vertex or reaches a component with at most a attachments. Every filled cross-edge between B_L and B_R is either an original matching edge or comes from a component with both-side attachments, whose physical through-path uses a matching edge incident to that component. At most m components have this property, hence at most (1+a²)m cross-edges in total. The smaller of B_L,B_R sees only O(sqrt(n)) exterior neighbors despite its linear size. Thus this torso fails every fixed positive expansion bound, has unbounded diameter by bounded degree and order, and cannot have connectivity greater than 3a.

Finally each original expander half has K_h minors for every fixed h once large by Alon–Seymour–Thomas's balanced separator theorem; subdivisions preserve them. If h>s+a, all branch sets of a K_h model must meet B: a branch set entirely in one component C of order ≤s can touch distinct branch sets only in C∪N(C), of size ≤s+a. Inside the filled torso, each branch's nonempty intersection with B is connected by replacing excursions into one C by edges within N(C). Adjacencies wholly outside B transfer by the same two-neighbor argument using the adjacent branches' paths back to B. Thus the canonical central torso has K_h minors of unbounded order, and so unbounded genus, treewidth and size.

Consequently no fixed-adhesion decomposition theorem can promise at every canonical torso one of bounded genus, bounded order/width, fixed positive expansion, bounded robust diameter or connectivity >3a. Native highly connected torsos on cubic hosts are even more restricted. This host-only counterexample is not a hardness reduction for named trace and does not exclude effective algorithms for additional torso classes. In particular an induced native subgraph may look planar while virtual child connections encode the unbounded-genus canonical torso; existing bounded-genus algorithms cannot silently process those unverified virtual relations.
