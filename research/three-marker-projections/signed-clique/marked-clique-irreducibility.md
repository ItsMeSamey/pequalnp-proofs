# Irreducible nongraphic rank-one marked examples

In the marked-cut-defect-triangles construction take a complete graph K_n partitioned into three disjoint vertex sets. On six reserved vertices set z=1 on all pairs; on five further vertices set z=0 on all pairs; on the remaining arbitrarily many vertices let z encode any simple graph. Cross-set defect bits are unrestricted. Let Q have kernel D=cut(K_n)+<z>. The dual of M(Q) has representation [incidence; z]. Its rank on A is r(A)+δ(A), where r is the graphic rank and δ(A) indicates an odd-z cycle in A. This is the single appended row rank test.

For every edge bipartition A,B at least one side contains the three edges of a triangle on the six reserved vertices. Indeed some vertex has at least three edges of the same color; unless two of its endpoints are joined by that color, the three endpoints form a triangle of the other color. This gives δ(A)+δ(B)≥1; δ(E)=1. Thus the dual connectivity function dominates that of M(K_n) on every edge bipartition. The private matroid remains 3-connected and has at least the branchwidth of M(K_n). On the five clean vertices the dual restricts to M(K_5), so M(Q) has M*(K_5) as a minor and is nongraphic. The induced defect graph on the remaining vertices can have arbitrarily large vertex rank-width. The colored presentation consequently has unbounded width even under arbitrary vertex switches: toggling all adjacency bits crossing one vertex bipartition adds at most two to each GF(2) cut-matrix rank.

One elementary triangle row on the six reserved vertices specifies a named marker that vanishes on cuts and evaluates to one on z. A target with this coordinate zero, with arbitrary other named coordinates, satisfies the exact algorithm's hypothesis. This is an explicit infinite family for literal five/six named trace; no assertion is made that every member arises from the separate physical Omega-to-dual-quotient compiler.
