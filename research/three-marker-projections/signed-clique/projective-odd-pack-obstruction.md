# An all-negative projective obstruction with an indexed graphic solution

For m≥4 take the m projective lines y=jx+j²z with 1≤j≤m in the real projective plane. No two are parallel in the affine chart and their pairwise intersections are (−i−j,−ij), so no three meet. Vertices are the pairwise intersections. Consecutive vertices on each projective line bound arcs. Subdivide each finite arc once and keep the unique wraparound arc on each line as one edge. Label every physical edge by one. The resulting embedded graph has m(m−1)/2+m(m−2) vertices and m(2m−3) edges.

The parity of every cycle equals its mod-two intersection number with the projective line at infinity. Each wraparound arc crosses it once, and the finite arcs have even physical length. Thus every odd simple cycle is one-sided. Two such curves cannot be disjoint: cutting the projective plane along a one-sided simple closed curve leaves a disk. Every designated line cycle has odd length 2m−3, so the maximum vertex-disjoint odd-cycle packing is exactly one. Each vertex is on at most two designated cycles, forcing every odd-cycle transversal to have at least ceil(m/2) vertices.

This lower bound is exact. The vertical projective line x=−(m+1)z meets each pair of lines i,j with i+j=m+1 at their shared vertex. For odd m it also meets the unpaired middle line in the finite arc between its crossings with the two neighboring slopes; put that arc's subdivision at this crossing. These ceil(m/2) vertices are all the intersections with the vertical line, including at infinity since its vertical direction differs from all m chosen directions. Delete them: every remaining cycle avoids a noncontractible projective line and thus has even parity. A switch can zero the all-one signing off P exactly when the graph with P deleted is bipartite. Consequently integral packing one cannot imply bounded P even for a single defect. This explicit arrangement illustrates the obstruction behind Bruce Reed's Escher walls; see [Mangoes and Blueberries, Combinatorica 19 (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s004930050056).

There is an exact positive algorithm on the same family. The line arrangement is cellular (its lifted great circles on the sphere cut it into convex chambers); subdivision preserves cellularity. Write C for the primal cut space. Every physical facial boundary has even length by contractibility, so the all-one vector is a cycle of the geometric dual on the same indexed edges. The line cycles show it is outside C. Euler's formula on the projective plane implies that the dual cycle space exceeds C by exactly one dimension. Thus C+span(1) is precisely the dual graph's binary cycle space, on original edges. The fixed-name graphic trace algorithm applies directly. No conclusion follows for arbitrary graph signings from the packing number alone.

More generally, let a connected cellularly embedded graph on a closed surface of Euler genus γ have k independent-mod-cuts defect vectors. If b faces have nonzero boundary vector in any defect coordinate, identify their dual vertices to one (leave the dual unchanged when b≤1). Keep every original edge ID. Every original cut and every defect then has zero dual boundary, including at the merged vertex by total parity. The new dual cycle space has codimension surplus γ+max(b−1,0)−k over the desired space.
