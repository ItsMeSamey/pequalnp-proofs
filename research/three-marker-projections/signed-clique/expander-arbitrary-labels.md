# Connected ordered binary sums on bounded-degree vertex expanders

Fix maximum degree Δ, vertex expansion lower bound α in (0,1], label dimension r, number b of nonempty ordered parts, and number p of prescribed vertex colours. An α-expander here has at least α|X| external neighbours for every nonempty X of size ≤n/2. There is a deterministic polynomial-time decision and witness algorithm on these promised graphs for arbitrary binary labels and prescribed part sums. Cographic trace queries reduce to these tuples by the h+1 part theorem. Connected and unrestricted catalogues can differ.

**Initialization.** First suppose G is biconnected. Select a correction subspace S of dimension d spanned by sufficiently frequent labels. Pin every vertex labelled outside S and the prescribed vertices; enumerate their allowed colours. A frequency-gap construction below bounds their number P and ensures abundant basis copies. If d=0, all nonzero labels are pinned. For each compatible assignment test the rooted minor of an edgeless b-vertex pattern, each branch containing the corresponding pins, with possibly empty sets of prescribed terminals. The disjoint nonempty connected branch sets grow to cover connected G.

**State.** Maintain holes H already coloured, targets on remaining vertices, pins R with fixed colours, and added edges only between pins of identical colour witnessing their connection through holes. Every surviving physical neighbour of a hole is pinned. Every active colour must be connected and nonempty in the augmented remainder. Completed colours cannot recur. Initially holes and added edges are empty. Check conservation and part-sum congruences modulo S: all unpinned labels belong to S. A one-colour remainder is checked directly for connectedness, nonemptiness, target and pins; with no colours require empty remainder.

**Routing test.** For b'≥2 active colours choose t=(b'−1)d singleton toggles, a private full basis for each nonlast colour. Each toggle demands two paths, to its owner and the last anchor. Every pin demands one path to its assigned anchor, so m=2t+|R|. Use a connected anchor bag of size ceil(d_i/α) for colour i of demand degree d_i. Keep bags and toggles pairwise at physical distance >2q+4 and that far from holes and pins; pins can be arbitrarily close to each other. Here B counts physical vertices in all branches including pins; take q≥(|H|+B+2m)/α. Constants below avoid circularity.

Remove holes and branches from G, discard clique-model bags touching them and add a separate clone for each demand end adjacent to its branch's remaining ordinary neighbours. All virtual edges had pinned endpoints and disappear. Use fixed-size disjoint paths. The original clique model is chosen large enough to leave at least max(3m+1,q+2m+1) intact bags. Robertson–Seymour Graph Minors XIII Theorem 5.4 implies linkage unless some separator of size <2m has all terminals on one side and a whole clique-model bag exclusively opposite. See Theorem 4.1 at https://research.nii.ac.jp/~k_keniti/quaddp1.pdf.

**Success.** Restore physically connected anchor bags and their paths to assigned pins and toggles, stopping just before toggles. This gives one disjoint connected core per colour. In G minus holes and toggles there is a physical giant containing the intact model, since a component of size >q is larger than n/2 by expansion. The union of the remaining components has ≤q vertices by expansion. Every anchor is in the giant: any small component containing a bag would need a physical boundary in holes or deleted toggles, too far from the bag. If another component meets a hole, it contains a pin and hence has a physical path to an anchor via the routing, contradiction. Otherwise its entire original boundary consists of toggles. It needs at least two distinct such vertices by biconnectivity and is too small to meet two. Thus grow the cores in the connected residual graph to cover it. Provisionally give all toggles to the last core, and use their private bases to repair part sums. This works because all outside-S labels are pinned and target congruences hold. Virtual edges only improve connectivity.

**Failure.** In the original graph delete holes, branch vertices and the ordinary separator vertices Y. The untouched clique model bags still form a component of size >q, hence a giant of size >n/2. The remaining physical components total ≤q. The giant contains the exclusively opposite model bag. A branch with any surviving clone has no ordinary neighbour in the giant. A separator can be found without recovering a minor model by enumerating cuts of fewer than 2m vertices, computing the physical giant and testing that no surviving clone reaches it. Existence follows from the failed linkage theorem.

For any nonpin branch with a surviving clone, take its original vertices and all off-giant components adjoining it. All exterior neighbours belong to Y: other branches, pins and holes are too far. For an anchor this set has size at most its bag size plus q≤n/2, so expansion charges at least its demand degree in distinct Y vertices. For a toggle biconnectivity charges at least two. These charges lie within distance q+1 of their respective branches, and are disjoint. Fully deleted nonpin clone groups and killed pin clones pay their own clone costs. Let R' be the pins with surviving clones. The uncharged ordinary cut budget is strictly less than |R'| since the total cut is smaller than 2m.

Take U to be all pins R' plus every small ordinary component adjacent to one of them. It has at most |R|+q vertices. Its exterior neighbours in the augmented remainder comprise previously pinned vertices and ordinary cut vertices within q+1 of R'. These ordinary vertices are disjoint from all nonpin branch charges. Thus there are strictly fewer *new* neighbours than |R'|. Guess the colours on U and its whole boundary, respecting existing prescriptions. Pin the surviving boundary. Remove U, subtract its sums, and use actual and pre-existing virtual edges to record every same-colour connection it creates among boundary vertices, as edges between pinned vertices. Precisely, for each monochromatic component meeting U, use the graph induced on U plus its boundary, with true edges and existing certified edges. If the component reaches the surviving boundary, connect its same-colour boundary vertices. If it has no surviving contact, it must be the unique completed component of that colour, with no other surviving pins or local occurrences of that colour. Check its residual target and forbid that colour henceforth. Repeat for each admissible guess. This is the exact connected-part gluing rule; a path in the larger region projects to the added connections and every added edge expands through fixed vertices of the correct colour. The surviving physical boundary of every hole is pinned. The pin count strictly decreases since all R' were internal and fewer new pins were added. This proves termination and completeness relative to each initial choice.

**Uniform constants and label gap.** Put K=2^r and T_0=0. For j=0,…,K−1 set P=p+KT_j, t_*=br, m_*=2t_*+P, A=max(1,ceil((t_*+P)/α)), B_*=t_*+P+bA. Set H_0=0. For i=0,…,P let Q_i=ceil((H_i+B_*+2m_*)/α), H_(i+1)=H_i+P+Q_i. Set H_*=H_(P+1), Q=ceil((H_*+B_*+2m_*)/α). These dominate every hole and off-giant size in at most P reductions. Set M=H_*+B_*+max(3m_*+1,Q+2m_*+1). For this level n>max(2(Q+A+1),36M³/α²) ensures the size tests and original K_M model by the excluded-minor balanced separator theorem, as in expander-dense-bases.md.

Let V(l)=(Δ+1)^(l+1) bound a radius-l ball. Take T_(j+1)>T_j+(H_*+P+t_*+b+1)V(2Q+2A+6). There are at most K−1 distinct nonzero types, so some interval (T_j,T_(j+1)] contains no nonzero type frequency. Let S be the span of types occurring above T_(j+1). At most KT_j vertices have labels outside S. Every abundant basis label supplies enough copies to greedily choose toggles and then extra anchor centres, avoiding balls around every old hole, pin and chosen centre. Grow anchor bags within distance A of their centres. All thresholds depend only on the fixed parameters. If n is below the size cutoff for this level simply enumerate colourings. All procedures, including small-cut enumeration and bounded-region colourings, take polynomial time for fixed parameters.

**General connected host.** Take the block-cut tree with unit weight on articulation nodes and the number of nonarticulation vertices as each block weight. A weighted centroid has no side of weight >n/2. If it is an articulation vertex, every component off it has size ≤1/α by expansion, and n≤1+Δ/α. Otherwise the centroid is a block K. Each component off K attaches at one vertex and has size ≤n/2, hence ≤1/α. If K is just a bridge, n≤2+2Δ/α. Handle bounded cases directly. A biconnected centroid block has vertex expansion at least α/Δ. Lift any core bipartition by annexing the hanging components to their attachments. Both lifted sides have at least the size of the smaller core side. Expansion of the smaller lifted side gives at least α times this size in crossing edges, all actual core edges; divide by Δ to bound the smaller core side's exterior neighbourhood.

In a valid colouring every pendant component containing vertices of a colour different from its attachment holds a whole colour absent elsewhere. Thus at most b−1 components are exceptional. Enumerate them and their internal colourings; require every nonattachment colour locally connected and closed globally, and every vertex of the attachment colour connected to that attachment. Fold all nonexceptional interior labels into the attachment, propagating any prescribed colours as pins. Subtract exceptional interior sums, forbid completed colours, and pin exceptional attachment colours. Enforce original prescriptions locally. There are at most p+b−1 core pins. Apply the biconnected solver with expansion α/Δ and the adjusted labels and targets. Each acceptance lifts; the branch of every existing solution is examined.
