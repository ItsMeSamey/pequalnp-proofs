# Recognizing balanced physical rooted clique models with dense charges

Use the anchored charge fold. In a connected proper region C with boundary W take U=span q(C), V=span q(outside C), D=U intersect (H+V), and a linear projection L of U onto D fixing D. Let K=ker L, let R be the sites of C with nonzero projected charge and let P=W union R have size p. Work in the physical graph induced by C union W. The desired bag totals are the original charge at W roots and L(q(r)) at R roots.

## Constructive certificate

Choose at most m=(p−1)dim K distinct switch vertices outside P. Delete them. All P must remain in a common component A. Each other component is assigned whole to any incident switch (one exists by connectedness), producing disjoint connected packets: a switch together with its assigned components. Every packet has gain in K as it contains only interior sites outside R. Compute the possible lists of packet gains and recover a realizing assignment by dynamic programming over the components, adding each total to one of its incident switches. The table has at most |K|^m entries.

For each switch choose two distinct roots i,j and two of its physical neighbors in A. Require one neighbor to be in the i bag and the other in the j bag. Shared contacts must have compatible requirements, including when themselves retained roots. Ask if a clique on P is a rooted minor of A with each branch set containing its indexed root and every contact assigned to it. Korhonen, Pilipczuk and Stamoulis, Theorem 1.1, provides a witnessing model: https://arxiv.org/html/2404.03958v1 . Give each bag one of the model vertices, retaining its actual branch set. Attach uncovered vertices of A to adjacent bags successively, possible since A is connected. This arbitrary completion need not compute or optimize any charges.

Place each packet in its first owner i. Each completed bag's discrepancy from its desired sum belongs to K: among its interior sites only its own R root (if any) has nonzero projection, so all projected contributions agree. The discrepancies sum to zero, because total q(C) lies in D and equals the sum over R of L(q(r)). Thus the discrepancy tuple belongs to K_0={(x_i) in K^p: sum_i x_i=0}. Toggling packet h to its second bag moves its entire charge g_h, adding g_h in precisely the two owner positions. Require these m or fewer vectors to span K_0. Then a binary linear solve gives simultaneous toggles with exactly the required final bag totals. Endpoints retained in the two core bags ensure every reassignment stays connected, and touching between all core bags remains. No omitted vertex is left unassigned. If the specific computed discrepancy lies in a smaller switch span the same solve is also sufficient, though arbitrary model selection need not recognize all such successes.

Enumerate switches, their contacts and their owners, checking all feasible gain lists. For fixed p and d the cost is polynomial, with n^{O(pd+1)} guesses and parameter-only dynamic-program factors and rooted minor pattern size. This recognizes exactly the displayed full-rank certificate with the specified cap on selector size. Failure does not preclude other balanced models, including those needing many switches for connectivity. For bounded boundary size enumerate W and components of its complement; all choices of L are bounded in terms of d. Apply any strict successful fold, saving branch memberships, and repeat. Each successful contraction replaces original charges by actual bag sums in the original fixed group. All projections, kernels and bounds must be recomputed on the current graph. Connectivity and charge-preserving reverse witness lifts compose without accumulating named dimensions; at most n strict steps are possible. This claim concerns feasibility for the fixed target H, and makes no promise about a minimum reduced graph or order-independence.
