# Auxiliary witness distance for equality of many literal transposition controls

Take n≥3 separately named binary input sites acting as literal transpositions on disjoint pairs of sockets. Fixed wiring and additional independent optional native three-cycle sites are arbitrary. Marks may occur anywhere on wires of this effective model, with passive subdivisions separating them from controlled departures. For a physical two-port implementation of an input swap, its third feedback channel must be private and contain neither a mark nor an auxiliary site. Suppose the accepted input language, allowing existential settings of the additional sites, consists exactly of 0^n and 1^n. Any respective auxiliary witnesses differ at at least ceiling(n/2) sites.

Freeze a joining witness at zero and take its physical permutation as the reference. Flipping any one input must separate the marks. Both of the input transposition's cuts therefore lie on the common marked cycle, with one on either marked interval. Flipping any two inputs must also reject at this witness because their word is neither endpoint for n≥3. The two chords cannot alternate around the marked cycle: after applying one transposition (which splits the cycle), the endpoints of an alternating other chord are on the two different cycles, so its transposition merges them and retains every original point together.

The n chords on that cycle are consequently pairwise noncrossing. Applying all of them splits it into n+1 cycles that each contain at least one data cut. For a direct induction choose a chord with consecutive endpoints after restriction to the remaining cyclic list of cut sites. One endpoint becomes a singleton of the induced return after that transposition; the other stays on the large cycle. Further chords do not touch the separated cycle, and the remaining endpoint of the removed chord remains an ordinary passive site. Equivalently insert noncrossing chords one by one: each joins two cut sites on the same current cycle and splits it into two cycles, each retaining one of those two cut sites. The first-return count agrees with the physical count of cycles containing a specified cut, regardless of other untouched wires.

At the joining all-ones witness, each single data flip there must reject. Thus every one of the 2n data cuts lies on its one marked cycle. Holding all inputs at one, move between the auxiliary witnesses one differing site at a time. A three-cycle changes at most three old cycles and cannot reduce the count of data-bearing cycles by more than two: if any altered old cycle bears data, at least one of the altered new cycles still does. Reducing n+1 to one needs at least ceiling(n/2) differing sites. No assumption on intermediate auxiliary states or on interblock feedback is required.

This lower bound does not exclude linear-overhead gadgets. It applies to literal two-port swap controls, not a public three-socket readout whose third contact may carry an independent mark or checking channel.
