# Two-root native detectors for complements of low-dimensional ternary affine flats

Let an affine family of ternary words be given by a constant vector plus the image of a full-column-rank ternary matrix with one or two columns. A single native existential two-root test recognizes precisely the words outside the family in the cases below. Each input trit is read once, with a freely routable balanced feedback graph. A full trit can be an affine score of a disjoint group of original binary switches.

Use sheet indices in F₃. A signed edge implements a bijection x↦sx+g with s∈{1,−1} and g an affine function of just its own trit with nonzero slope (or a fixed constant for an auxiliary edge). This requires one native rotation by the input, along with fixed label permutations; reversing an edge uses its inverse and preserves the one-read count. Give the base a special vertex O with a fixed loop x↦−x. Give each base vertex even degree and orient its edges in an Euler tour. Each sheet vertex consequently has the same number of inputs as outputs. Its independently programmable port-permutation router may be built from fixed sorting schedules of optional two-track swaps; each swap uses a native binary three-cycle with third socket fed back. Mark the sheet-zero and sheet-one entrances of the special loop.

An assignment of one sheet to each connected base vertex agreeing with the edge maps exists precisely when the group of closed-walk bijections fixes a common sheet at O. Because the fixed special loop acts by negation, that sheet must be zero. If the common sheet exists, zero and one belong to different covering components. Otherwise the group contains a transposition fixing zero and another element moving zero, hence acts transitively on all three sheets. As the directed cover is balanced, the routers can follow an Euler circuit in a component and join the two marks exactly in the latter case. Thus the single rooted query tests inconsistency. A fixed coordinate can be checked by an extra positive loop at O translating by its input minus the required value. This loop is harmless for the prescribed value and makes the group transitive for either other value.

For a line, normalize each nonconstant input by its row's affine bijection so that all k such inputs must equal one hidden value u. For even k>0 use k links from O to a new vertex P, each requiring the sheet at P to equal its input when O is zero. For odd k≥3 use k−1 such links and a negative loop x↦2t−x at P for the remaining input t. The loop fixes the sheet at P to t. Degrees are even and the base is connected in both cases. If k=1 its only nonconstant coordinate is unrestricted, so accept only a mismatch at a constant coordinate. An unused unrestricted trit can be put on a detached component.

For a plane, choose two independent nonzero row directions as coordinates u,v of the hidden parameter and normalize input slopes and offsets individually. Each nonzero row then reads one of u,v,u+v,u−v. A simple u or v reading can either be a link from O to its corresponding vertex P or Q, or a negative loop at that vertex fixing its sheet to the input. Every mixed reading is a link between P and Q: x↦t−x implements t=u+v, and x↦x−t implements t=u−v when directed P to Q. Inverse maps are used whenever an edge is oriented the other way.

If only two row directions occur, they give two independent line tests. Their complement is the OR of line mismatches. Cut the two fixed marked arcs of each such detector to give a two-port permutation that swaps exactly when its marks joined. Attach each module between the same pair of free hubs, with a marked loop at each hub. Any attainable swap connects the hubs, and if no module can swap then their loops stay separate. All detector auxiliaries are independent. Each original coordinate is used once. Constants may be checked in either module.

If at least three directions occur, let T>0 be the number of mixed inputs. When T is odd use one O–P link and one O–Q link and read the remaining simple inputs by negative loops. When T is even and one simple direction occurs at least twice, use two anchor links for that direction and none for the other; all remaining simple inputs are again loops. These choices give even degrees at O,P,Q; the positive number of mixed links connects both chart vertices to the anchor component. Edge consistency then gives exactly the original plane. If any direction is repeated, choose it as the first simple direction and choose any other occurring direction as the second. When all directions occur only once, three occurring directions give T=1 and are covered. The only multiplicity profile left by the construction is four projective directions with one nonconstant input each. Constants do not change that profile.

On that exceptional four-input profile the separate balanced unanchored signed cover still gives an exact two-root test if any one input is promised to take at most two ternary values. Choose it as the negative-loop coordinate at P. Under consistency the P fiber partition consists of its assigned sheet as a singleton and the other two together. Put the marks on the two possible values of that input (or any two sheets including the sole possible value). They are separated on every consistent word in the promise and joinable on every inconsistent word. Without that domain restriction, the known unanchored construction tests all four directions exactly with three common roots. The plane theorem concerns dimension two and does not assert a general theorem for codimension two.
