# Exact completion of a partial physical clique model

Suppose connected disjoint indexed bags B_i form a clique model on p roots in the graph induced by a region and its contacts. Each bag has one root. Bags can be arbitrarily large and carry arbitrary known charge sums. Prescribe final totals for the bags and require every site to belong to one bag. For each connected component C of the uncovered graph build a local graph from C and p index vertices, one per bag, copying the physical adjacencies from C to those bags. Add a clique between index vertices. These index vertices have charge zero and are pinned to their distinct colors. Ask for p connected spanning color classes, each containing its own pinned index, and tabulate their p charge sums. The fixed-index clique edges cannot assist a monochromatic path since their endpoints have different required colors.

All these local problems are independent: in a connected global completion every path in a bag from a C vertex to the original B_i first leaves C at B_i. Thus the local i-class connects to its own index. Conversely all local i-classes attach to B_i by actual same-color contacts. The possible full totals are exactly the Minkowski sum of the local tables and the prescribed core totals; propagate by finite additive dynamic programming and traceback. This is exact for the supplied partial model and never requires a different local model for different bits of the same score.

For fixed p,d,k each local table is constructible in polynomial time if its nonzero-charge support is dominated by k sites in its local graph. The bounded-domination proof works verbatim with p anchored colors: enumerate owners, guess their colors subject to all p pins; protect all owners and pins. All other charged sites have baseline owner colors. The joint deviation for the first p−1 totals has d(p−1) binary bits; the last total follows from the fixed component sum. In a feasible witness minimize the total vertices of disjoint retained trees per color that contain its pins/owners, after counting deviations and putting all omitted charged sites back with their owners. Each color has a pin. More than d(p−1) unprotected leaves yield a nonempty zero-sum subset of leaf deviations. Remove them simultaneously to contradict minimality. Enumerate the bounded topologies, their physical branch images, and all branch charge subtotals, routing vertex-disjoint branch interiors by the finite-group labeled paths algorithm exactly as in dominating-charged-partitions.md (use p copies of the vertex-to-edge Z4 scoring rule). Complete omitted charged sites first by their physical owner adjacencies, then neutral sites by flood fill. This also applies to one color and components with empty charged support. Arbitrary local totals are allowed, including zero; anchors enforce nonempty local parts. The local graph is connected via its pinned clique.

Cores of at most b sites in total are recognizable by brute force assignment to p bags, with all rootedness, connectivity, pair contacts and component domination tests verified, in n^{O(b+k+1)} time for those certificate checks. The full solver is polynomial for fixed b,k,d,p. It accepts exactly existence of such a core with a balanced completion under the stated component-domination bound. For a supplied large core the same exact completion and indexed lifting work without bounding its cardinality. The number of uncovered components and the global domination number are unrestricted. This routine may supply the balanced model in anchored-charge-fold.md. Contractions of multiple accepted regions need only retain the current target and the original d-dimensional bag sums. If different feasible completions exist the algorithm recovers one; no completeness claim is made for graphs with no recognized or supplied core satisfying the stated bound.
