# Independent affine pivots for the two-row AND encoding

Work over F₂. On three independent local coordinates x,y,o, the four points violating o=xy are E={001,011,101,110}. The ordinary two-row encoding is o(1+x)=0 and x(o+y)=0. The four invalid points form an affine basis and sum to the o-coordinate unit vector. A nonzero product of two affine functions which vanishes on every valid assignment must be supported on a two-point affine line inside E: its support is an affine flat of codimension at most two, and E contains no plane. Two rows must therefore partition E into disjoint pairs. A pair can be specified by a product with given nonzero pivot normal a iff a is constant on it: offset that form to one, and inside its plane choose a second affine factor isolating the line.

A nonzero normal is balanced (2+2 on E) iff its o coefficient vanishes. Otherwise it has one singleton side. Distinct independent balanced normals have different pair partitions, so a pair monochromatic for one never leaves its complement monochromatic for the other. With one balanced normal and one unbalanced normal, choose the balanced pair containing the unbalanced singleton. With two independent unbalanced normals, the singleton positions are distinct; choose a pair excluding the first singleton and including the second. Hence two independent local normals can serve as row pivots exactly when at least one contains o.

At gate i take one slot with candidate vectors o,o+x,o+y,o+x+y and a second with all seven nonzero local normals. Both lists span the full local wire space V_i. By Rado's independent transversal theorem a globally independent choice exists iff dim(sum of V_i over i in S)≥2|S| for every subset of gates S: an arbitrary subset of slots supported on S has at most 2|S| elements and spans the same sum. A linear matroid/partition matroid intersection constructs the representatives. Extend these independent affine coordinates, including each chosen offset, to an invertible affine basis; assign these gate rows to distinct activation bits and let extra rows vanish. The output wire target becomes one affine readout; inverse substitution recovers original coordinates.

For ordinary circuits with three distinct wire names at each AND and the distinct names as the original coordinate basis, dim of the sum counts incident wire names. Each connected incidence component on g gates and w wires has 3g edges, so w≥2g iff it has at most one cycle. Every subgraph of a pseudoforest is a pseudoforest. Conversely the gate set of any component with more than one cycle fails the rank test. Thus the all-subsets condition is exactly gate–wire incidence pseudoforest. Affine wire aliases still admit the linear rank sufficiency test, but locally degenerate gate relations may have other encodings. The necessity statement is for universal local two-product encodings on three independent coordinates, using local pivots and a single global affine basis change, not for arbitrary auxiliaries or nonlocal encodings.

As a total-rank counterexample, take outputs c,d,e,f for AND(a,b), AND(a,c), AND(b,d), AND(c,e). Six actual wire names cover eight slots. Append two disjoint gates on six fresh actual wires. Total rank is now twelve for twelve slots, but the first four gates still fail. Unused inputs cannot affect the deficiency either. This concerns the affine pin elimination step of single-unequal-p3.md, rather than a claimed unrestricted SAT transfer.

For the strict linear nonzero target convention, append a fresh activation h, use h=1 as sole target, and place h(1+O)=0 on its row, where O is the affine output in the new coordinates. Every original accepting assignment has a unique extension h=1. This handles either affine constant of O, including a constant output.
