# Exact reduction to the central torso of a quaternary height uplift

Let B be the represented binary private matroid, with two height rows h1,h2 and two tag rows. The target is the full height plane with zero tags. For each connected component of B and each nonzero functional chi on the two heights, use the three-name exact-line algorithm on the two tags and chi(height), targeting (0,0,1). From a positive output extract a private fundamental circuit with this nonzero projected gain and retain its true height. These actual circuits span all achievable tag-zero circuit directions in that component: if any direction lies outside their span, a functional vanishing on that span detects it, contradicting that functional's query. Independent circuits in distinct components immediately solve the problem. If no such pair exists then either all component spans together have dimension at most one (reject), or a unique component supplies dimension two and all others zero (reduce to it). Every compatible witness has a fundamental circuit basis of zero individual tag. We may thus assume B connected and the height rows independent modulo B.

Over K=GF(4) take a outside GF(2), append the row h1+a*h2 to B, and adjoin the special bottom unit e. Call the matroid M. It is the same matroid as the common-height uplift with its special point over GF(16): on any column subset the extra row increases the binary private rank by one iff at least one of the height rows is nonzero on a private dependency there. Its contraction at e is B. The connectedness and nonbinarity proof of uplift-graphic-scope.md requires only the hypotheses just listed, no deletion promise. Compute its standard tree of 2-sums. Let R be the piece containing physical e. It cannot be a parallel class, since contraction of e in such a piece splits its at least two remaining nonempty sides, contradicting connectedness of B. In every other case R/e is connected and leaves all other ports nonloop/noncoloop (a 3-connected piece has both single-element reductions connected; for a circuit of size at least three contraction gives a shorter circuit). Therefore the other pieces survive as minors of B. Justification: on the other sides of a proper two-sum one can reduce the marker and a chosen physical representative to a parallel pair using a common circuit. Hence all distal pieces are binary. As binary matroids are closed under two-sums and M is nonbinary, R is a nonbinary 3-connected piece. The decomposition and linear port vectors are obtainable in polynomial time. For example a represented proper 2-separation can be found by submodular connectivity minimization, pinning two elements on each side over polynomially many choices, then introducing a marker on the rank-one intersection. Circuit/cocircuit pieces can be merged as in the standard decomposition.

Let E_i be the nonempty physical branch at each central port other than e, including a singleton when the port is itself physical. In M its K-span meets the rest including e in the single port line, distinct from the line e since R is simple. For a singleton, use its own line, which lies in the span of the other central elements because R remains connected. Thus no branch spans e. Projecting modulo e carries this intersection to a line in the private space: the rest contains e, so adding span(e) to the branch before intersecting adds only that same span(e). Since B is rational, compute by binary elimination the unique nonzero generator b_i of span B[E_i] intersect span B[E outside E_i]. Height is a linear function of the private projection on E_i because a nonzero h1+a*h2 on a private dependency would span e there. Compute its value v_i on b_i.

For every i compute all tag words u attainable by a private independent expression of b_i in E_i. With u fixed, add a virtual column of private vector b_i and tag zero; give it one extra named marker bit. The three-name target line (u,1) is achieved exactly by a private circuit through this virtual column with tag u, equivalently an independent physical expression on deleting the marker. Store its physical witness.

Form a binary central instance with one column (b_i,u,v_i) for each attainable u at port i. Its private vectors may be compressed to a basis of their span by binary elimination. All four named bits are retained. In any global dependency the private contributions x_i of a branch lie in its interface and are 0 or b_i; writing x_i=c_i*b_i leaves exactly the relation sum c_i*b_i=0. In a compatible solution every local private dependency has tag zero, so if its selected branch expresses b_i, all such expressions have the same tag u_i and height v_i. Local dependencies have height zero regardless. Ignore branches that cannot express b_i. Selecting the corresponding central columns produces exactly the original trace because all central relations glue independently through the selected local expressions and every physical relation has the same image as its central port relation. Conversely a compatible central subset cannot take two different tag choices for the same port (their two-element relation has zero height and nonzero tag). Replace each selected central column by its stored independent physical expression. Branches are disjoint, local kernels vanish, and the private relations project onto exactly the central relation space with the same named gains. This gives a witness in original column IDs.

Consequently only a single 3-connected nonbinary torso of the height uplift remains, regardless of size and branchwidth of the peripheral matroids. A bounded-size central torso gives a deterministic polynomial-time decision, including witness recovery. For central rank two, all b_i coincide and heights v_i are pairwise distinct because the central lines are simple. For each tag u test whether at least three distinct heights are attainable; three such heights suffice and are necessary for affine span dimension two. In this case there can be at most four non-e ports. More generally a bounded central rank d or corank d bounds its size by (4^d-1)/3, using simplicity and cosimplicity over GF(4). No theorem here bounds the central torso for arbitrary input.
