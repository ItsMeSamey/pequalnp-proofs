# Five-point chart reconstruction beyond triangle cores

Let E be the dictionary of distinct nonzero ordinary columns over F₂ in their own span V, with original identities retained. Enumerate every four independent members e₁,…,e₄. Regard P={0,e₁,…,e₄} as potential vertices of a graphic representation, and optionally discard it unless all its pair differences are physical. Compute
                 Q = P union {x in E: x+p in E for every p in P}.
If Q is an affine basis of V, send the column joining potential vertices u,v to u+v in V. This identifies the even vertex space on Q with V (take a star basis at 0); in particular every column which is the difference of two Q-points is an indexed graphic edge. Scan all columns, test the resulting graph for vertex connectivity at least 600, and run dense-graphic-four-space.md on success. All candidates and verification steps take polynomial time, without a supplied restriction or supplied row coordinates. One may run this test together with the earlier triangle-core selector. It may abstain when no tested basis qualifies. A recovered witness always belongs to the original indexed input.

Here is a strict family separation from graphic-chart-triangle-cores.md. For n=2^m≥1024 label a hidden vertex set by F₂^m. Include every pair (the edges of K_n), and every four-set whose vertex labels sum to zero (called a block below). Regard these even characteristic vectors in the rank-(n−1) space and apply an arbitrary invertible row transformation to hide their coordinates. The blocks form a Steiner quadruple system: any three distinct labels determine a unique distinct fourth. The full physical restriction is nongraphic since even a single block together with the K₅ on its four vertices and a fifth gives F₇ after contracting the block.

Pick as e₁,…,e₄ the four edges of a hidden star with five distinct vertices. After an affine translation the potential P consists of five hidden unit vectors and any candidate x is an odd vertex vector y in that coordinate model (all even ambient vectors become odd under the same translation). For any hidden vertex outside P all five differences are edges, so the filter accepts it. For y of weight three, toggling a member of its support gives three possible edges, while toggling a vertex outside its support gives a block for at most one choice by the triple-completion property. For y of weight five, toggling a support member gives a block only when that member equals the XOR of the five vertex labels, hence at most once. Any other nonunit odd weight gives neither an edge nor a block. Therefore no spurious y meets all five tests and Q is exactly the full hidden affine vertex basis. This iteration finds a 600-connected chart.

By contrast each physical point has exactly 3(n−2)/2 triangles in the binary triple system. An edge belongs to n−2 graph-only triangles and to one mixed triangle for each of the (n−2)/2 blocks through its endpoints, with the complementary pair as the third point. It cannot be the sum of two blocks, since blocks cannot share three vertices. A block lies in three mixed triangles from pairings of its four vertices. Each of its six pairs occurs in (n−2)/2−1 additional blocks, which meet it exactly in that pair. The symmetric difference is then another weight-four XOR-zero block. Each all-block triangle is counted twice by choosing the other block, giving 3((n−2)/2−1) of these triangles per block. This matches the edge degree. The full triple system is connected through the clique triangles and every block's mixed triangles. Consequently every threshold core in the earlier selector is either the entire nonregular component or empty. The five-point scan succeeds where that complete selector cannot.
