# Rank two suffices for a hard zero determinant over growing characteristic-two fields

Given a matrix B over an explicitly represented finite field of characteristic two, asking whether a principal submatrix is singular is NP-complete even if rank(B−I)≤2. The fields used here have polynomial degree over GF₂. With rank(B−I)≤1 the question is binary linear feasibility.

Take a simple graph with vertices 0,…,n−1 and at least one edge. Removing edgeless inputs from the source poses no difficulty: an edgeless input is a known YES and can instead be mapped to a single edge. Let d be the least power of two at least n² and K=GF(2ᵈ). Obtain α of degree d over GF₂ by a deterministic tower. Start with GF₂ and θ=1. At each step adjoin s with s²+s=θ, and replace θ by sθ. Absolute trace of θ stays one because the relative trace of s is one. The image of x↦x²+x is the absolute-trace-zero hyperplane, so each quadratic is irreducible. The final s lies outside the unique index-two subfield of K. As d is a power of two, every proper subfield lies inside that subfield; the final s therefore has degree d and serves as α. Each tower multiplication takes a constant number of half-size multiplications per level over log₂d levels, and inversion by repeated squaring takes polynomial bit time.

Use independent binary choices pᵤ and qᵥ. Put A=Σ pᵤα^(n·u) and D=Σ qᵥα^v. The coefficient of α^(n·u+v) in AD is pᵤqᵥ: the n² exponents are distinct and below d. Define W as the affine binary space of scalars C in the span of the first n² power-basis vectors for which the coefficient bits at n·u+v and n·v+u sum to one on every edge {u,v}. These coordinate pairs are disjoint. For each pair take z and 1+z, for one independent Boolean selector z. Give every other coefficient below n² an independent Boolean selector.

Then AD∈W iff pᵤqᵥ+pᵥqᵤ=1 on every edge. Over GF₂ this determinant is one precisely when the endpoint vectors (pᵤ,qᵤ) and (pᵥ,qᵥ) are different and nonzero. The three nonzero binary vectors serve as the three colors. An isolated vertex can be given any proper color on recovery. Consequently the graph is colorable exactly when the 2×2 matrix with rows (A,C) and (1,D) is singular at some Boolean assignment: its determinant is AD+C. Each selector changes exactly one field entry by a fixed scalar, a rank-one update. At all-zero selectors, A=D=0 and C=C₀ is nonzero because an edge forces one coefficient to be one. Graph 3-colorability is NP-complete. [Garey, Johnson and Stockmeyer](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975%2876%2990059-1)

Let T be the nonsingular baseline matrix. Factor the normalized pencil as I₂+U diag(x) Vᵀ; each selector gets one column of U and one row of Vᵀ by left multiplying its single-entry update by T⁻¹. Place the p bits first, next the q bits, and then all the slack bits, using this order for the h=2n+n²−|E| selectors. Output B=Iₕ+VᵀU. It differs from the identity by rank at most two. For any original toggle mask x and S=supp(x), Sylvester's identity and expansion along the unselected identity rows give det(B[S])=det(I₂+U diag(x) Vᵀ). A singular index set yields the original p,q by membership in its first two n-blocks. A coloring supplies p,q and the unique slack for C=AD. The construction and output have polynomial bit size, and field elimination verifies a guessed principal support.

For rank one write B=I+uvᵀ. The principal determinant on S equals 1+Σᵢ∈S uᵢvᵢ. A zero exists exactly when 1 belongs to the GF₂-linear span of the field products, decidable in polynomial time by binary elimination. Rank zero has no singular support. The hardness result uses growing field degree and generally nonsymmetric matrices. It leaves the fixed-field cases separate.
