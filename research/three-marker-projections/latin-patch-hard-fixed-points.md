# NP-hard fixed points for every fixed aligned patch dimension

Fix h >= 0 and B=3·2^h. There is a deterministic polynomial many-one reduction from hypergraph 2-colourability on 3-uniform hypergraphs to the signed-star Latin language for which every aligned patch of dimension at most h touching any live site fails the exact binary-interface test of latin-subgroup-projection.md. This applies on both verdicts, with empty binary background after star identification. It allows projecting private names. It concerns the individual patch rule; it puts no lower bound on other SAT algorithms.

Start with monotone NAE3SAT (hypergraph 2-colourability), an NP-complete Boolean relation by Schaefer's dichotomy (https://doi.org/10.1145/800133.804350). To allow repeated input positions, use fresh occurrence vertices even within each constraint and tie copies of each variable as below. Remove unused names or record them separately. The following construction uses constant-size devices depending only on h.

Choose g>B+3. A simple 3-uniform hypergraph of Berge girth at least g and without a 2-colouring exists. For completeness take independent triples on N points with probability N^(-2+epsilon), epsilon=1/(2g). With probability tending to one there is no independent set of size ceiling(N/4), by a union bound of 2^N exp(-Omega(N^(1+epsilon))). A Berge cycle of length 2<=l<g specifies l distinct alternating base vertices and l distinct triples; the triples' extra positions have at most N^l choices. Thus its expected count is at most N^(2l) p^l=N^(epsilon l). The expected total is o(N). Delete one vertex from each short cycle, with fewer than N/4 vertices deleted in a suitable realization. No new independent set of size ceiling(N/4) appears, and more than N/2 vertices remain, precluding a 2-colouring. Take an edge-minimal non-2-colourable subhypergraph and delete one edge e. The result is colourable, but all colourings agree on the three terminal vertices of e (otherwise e could be restored). Both common colours are realizable by complementation. Every path between different terminals uses at least g-1 hyperedges, since it would otherwise close with e to a short Berge cycle. The gadget can be found by finite exhaustive search independent of the input. Isolated vertices can be discarded, except designated terminals.

Replace each original constraint by its own hyperedge on private occurrence vertices. For each variable connect successive occurrences by copies of the gadget identifying two of its terminals with that pair, leaving the third terminal private. All other vertices are fresh. This preserves satisfiability with extension/recovery. Any incidence cycle contained in a gadget has Berge length at least g; one that enters and exits a gadget must traverse between different terminals and uses at least g-1 gadget hyperedges; cycles avoiding gadgets cannot occur among the disjoint occurrence edges. Thus every family of at most B distinct hyperedges in the resulting hypergraph has an incidence forest. Drop isolated vertices.

Replace every vertex v by t clones, t²>B, and every hyperedge by all t³ clone triples. A valid old colouring extends by giving each clone its parent's colour. A valid new colouring restricts to a valid old one by choosing the first clone at every vertex, because all triples of choices were required. Do not identify the clone variables. Replace each NAE by two ordinary clauses, its all-positive and all-negative triple, and apply the isolated-clause embedding of rank-one-latin-sat.md. Each clone representative occurs at >B distinct live occurrence sites, and each live occurrence belongs to exactly one nontrivial Latin row triple after pins. Source size grows only by a fixed factor before the polynomially sized Latin embedding.

A patch includes at most B sites in total, so every representative it reads has an occurrence outside the patch. No source variable disappears under existential projection. The exact patch relation on all exterior names is the conjunction of the original signed clauses whose triples meet the patch. All other mixed or internal physical triples are pinned true. At most B distinct relevant clauses remain; unblow their edges and deduplicate to obtain an incidence forest.

For any forest of 3-edges in this pre-blowup graph, on its full clone blowup every assignment to any two distinct clone coordinates extends to a colouring. If their parent vertices differ, give the two parents the specified colours. When they are in the same tree assign colours arbitrarily on the unique connecting incidence path respecting the pins, and for each path edge use its third vertex to avoid monochromaticity; the third vertices are distinct off-path by acyclicity. Extend down branches from the path by colouring child vertices differently. Disconnected components and unpinned subtrees work by rooting at one vertex. If two distinct clones of one parent have opposite prescribed colours, assign its clones as desired and colour the two other parents of every incident edge oppositely. Extend from those parents down the forest. This makes every clone triple on all these edges nonmonochromatic. The equal-pin same-parent case is immediate. Unused names are arbitrary.

Consequently the patch conjunction has no nontautological binary or unary consequence and is satisfiable. When it touches a live site it contains at least one actual, individually falsifiable three-literal clause with distinct clone names. Its binary hull is universal but the relation is proper, so no exact 2CNF representation exists. Thus even the first nontrivial patch cannot be removed. Redistributing clauses among rails or renumbering isolated clause slots preserves the argument so long as every live physical occurrence has a distinct site and every nontrivial Latin triple is an original source clause.
