# Dense charged partitions on a recognizable bounded-height separator hierarchy

Fix p,d,k,s,h. In a connected graph G with vertex charges in F₂^d prescribe totals for p nonempty connected spanning parts. Recursively cut a connected interior C with its actual boundary N_G(C): either the nonzero sites of C are dominated by k sites in C union N_G(C), or delete a splitter of at most s sites of C and recurse independently on every remaining component. The maximum number of splitters on a root-to-leaf path is h. The initial interior is the whole graph. Branching, leaf size, treewidth and the number of owners across different leaves are unrestricted. A decomposition and the local owners can be found in polynomial time at fixed parameters: enumerate possible dominators or top splitter, testing its children independently. At each level the sum of the child sizes is at most the parent size; with global size n and at most h nested choices the work is n^{O(sh+k+1)} for recognition. Empty splitters are unnecessary.

Guess one distinct physical anchor per color and prescribe its color. Remove all anchors at the top. Restrict the supplied or found hierarchy independently to each remaining component: intersect its current splitter with the component and recurse. Upon deletion of arbitrary anchors, surviving pieces reaching an old leaf lie inside that leaf. Every charged site still has an old owner either in its own component or in its actual boundary (an owner outside with a physical adjacency is a boundary vertex). Thus its class property persists at no cost in hierarchy depth. Retain the anchors in the physical graph as boundary vertices. All later boundaries have size at most p+hs.

For a connected interior C with boundary B, enumerate its boundary coloring, its exact interior charge tuple by color, and a partition of B refining colors. A record consists of an actual assignment on C, every interior vertex connected within C union B to a boundary vertex of its color, and all vertices in each recorded block mutually connected there in that color. The record promises these connections, allowing additional same-color connections. For completeness keep enough records to include the actual connected-component partition on B of every feasible assignment (or a coarser certified relation, which must be the actual partition when exact for that assignment). B is nonempty below the top since G is connected and has anchors.

At a leaf enumerate all partitions of B refining its coloring. Regard each block as a distinct local color, pin every member of B to its own block, give boundary vertices charge zero, and tabulate all achievable local totals using the multi-color pinned support-domination lemma in charged-clique-model-completion.md. The connected local graph uses only actual edges of G[C union B], with **no** artificial clique added. Distinct locally named colors may later merge into a single original color. Convert the local totals to the original p totals by summing. Genuine assignments whose same-color components all meet B are included by splitting each color according to its actual components. A leaf may have many different original colors absent locally; none needs an additional local part. Every enumerated local block is pinned, so zero totals cause no empty-part issue.

At a splitter S guess its colors. Start on B union S with connections from all actual equal-color edges between its vertices. Each independent child supplies a table record on its own actual boundary inside B union S. Add the record's partition connections by transitive closure and its full interior score tuple by addition. Process children sequentially with a finite-state table on B union S, retaining traceback. Add the original S charges once. Require each surviving block on B union S to reach B, then record its restriction to B. This ensures every site, including each splitter vertex and all sites in children, reaches the parent boundary. To see completeness use each genuine assignment's actual child boundary components: any same-color path decomposes into visited sites of B union S, equal-color edges within that set, and excursions in a single child. Its true relation therefore survives precisely these updates. For soundness each recorded connection is realized in the chosen physical coloring; if a record understates actual connectivity the additional edges can only help. Alternatively store all refinements of every certified output relation, retaining actual generating witnesses.

At the top combine children around the colored anchors by the same closure and add the anchor scores. Require the given total tuple. Each color has one anchor; each site can reach a same-color anchor by the attachment invariant, giving the three (or p) actual spanning parts. Traceback recovers all indexed colors. All local tables and per-child combination states have parameter-only cardinality; the pinned routing solver is polynomial for each fixed parameter tuple. No temporal or separation restrictions are imposed on the interface colors.

Even height one already handles a single 2-connected block with unbounded global domination of its charged support. Use two splitter vertices a,b and arbitrarily many disjoint cliques, joining a to one designated vertex and b to another distinct designated vertex of each clique. With at least two cliques of size at least three this is two-connected, but no vertex dominates interior nonport charged sites in two cliques. Each residual clique has one owner. Using unbounded clique sizes also gives unbounded treewidth.
