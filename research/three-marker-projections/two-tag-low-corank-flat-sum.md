# Indexed arbitrary-form detection in polynomial time in the number of private cocircuits, with one-sided error

Let K be full-row-rank r-by-n over F2 with two arbitrary tag rows g1,g2. Let N be the number of cocircuits of its column matroid. For every explicitly indexed alternating form Ω, there is a randomized polynomial-time witness algorithm in n and N with one-sided error. No restriction on the rank of Ω or the number of private circuits is needed. A prescribed exact height plane is the query Ω=h1∧h2. Repeating a polynomial number of amplified adaptive queries gives an actual basis of the entire paired space with high probability; the recorded pairs always are genuine.

Precompute all complementary hyperplanes of the N cocircuits by incremental polynomial-time matroid hyperplane enumeration, using the represented independence oracle. See Khachiyan, Boros, Elbassioni, Gurvich and Makino, On the Complexity of Some Enumeration Problems for Matroids (DOI 10.1137/S0895480103428338). Enumerate, with deduplication, all intersections of at most four of these flats, including the whole ground set. Every flat H of corank d≤4 occurs: extend any basis of H to a matroid basis using d columns, then intersect the d fundamental hyperplanes obtained by deleting each added column in turn. Their linear span intersections recover the original flat exactly. There are O((N+1)^4) candidates. With a numerical budget, incremental enumeration can be stopped as soon as it exceeds the claimed budget; doing so returns an unresolved instance, not a negative answer.

For S of cardinality r+2 define D_ab(S)=det([K;a;b]_S), with physical subscripts standing for coordinate row units. Use the normalization and full-rank completion in four-port-saddle.md: a detected compatible pair can always be witnessed on such a support. For K_S of full rank the column pair in a fixed kernel basis is detected by Ω with zero tags precisely when the following binary coefficient is one:

    f(S) = Σ_{i<j} Ω_ij D_ij ∏_{a=1,2}(1+D_{i,g_a})(1+D_{j,g_a}).

Indeed if D_ij=1, i,j distinguish the two kernel coordinates, so the four factors require that both tag evaluations on that kernel vanish. If K_S is rank-deficient all factors containing D_ij vanish. Evaluating Σ_S f(S)∏_{i in S}w_i gives a multilinear polynomial whose nonzero monomials are exactly the desired physical supports.

Expand each tag determinant linearly into coordinate determinants. There are O(n^6) terms and each is a product of one to five coordinate minors. Discard terms containing a D_ii=0. The site set P appearing among these pairs has size 2 through 6. A nonzero product requires P⊆S. Write F=S minus P and d=|P|−2. If r<d the term contributes nothing. In a contributing S, F is an independent (r−d)-set, since deleting each indicated pair must leave a basis of K. Its span H is a flat of corank d. For each precomputed flat of this corank, check that for every indicated pair ab the |P|−2 columns P minus {a,b} extend H to full rank in the represented matroid. In other words they form a quotient basis modulo span(H). This test is independent of which basis F spans H.

If all checks pass, the contribution from that flat is the product of weights on P times the weighted basis polynomial of the rank-(r−d) restriction on H minus P. Use coordinates in a basis of span(H); insufficient rank after removing P gives zero, including the usual empty determinant one when r−d=0. A single Gram determinant over the characteristic-two weight field gives the basis polynomial by Cauchy–Binet. This preserves original indices, repeated columns and loops. Every contributing F belongs to exactly one H, and every monomial in the resulting determinant has exactly the prescribed coefficient for this term. Add all term/flat contributions in the weight field. This is polynomial in n and N (fixed two tag bits).

Choose independent random weights in a binary extension of order >2(r+2). The polynomial degree is r+2, so one evaluation detects nonempty support with probability greater than one half. From a nonzero point extract S deterministically as follows. Keep a set A forced present, zero previously deleted weights, and preserve the nonzero coefficient of z^|A| obtained by scaling the weights on A by z. This coefficient is the sum of the weights of all surviving terms containing A. For the next site zero its weight and interpolate the coefficient at |A|+1 distinct field points; if nonzero retain the deletion. Otherwise restore the weight and force that site, since its present contribution is the prior nonzero conditional total. Only r+3 points per site are needed and at termination all remaining sites form the recovered monomial. Recompute the original private kernel on S and verify each tag, nullity two, and Ω before returning its physical pair. Bit sizes and field arithmetic are polynomial. Zero at one point does not prove absence. N can be exponential on unrestricted binary matroids.

This parameter differs from listing private circuits directly. For the cographic matroid of K_{2,m}, its cocircuits are graph cycles, of which there are binom(m,2), while the bonds separating the two large-degree graph vertices include exponentially many distinct private circuits. No unconditional unrestricted polynomial-time or SAT conclusion follows.
