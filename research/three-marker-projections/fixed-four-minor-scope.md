# Fixed-minor theorem scope for exact four-name traces

Append a basis of four indexed names and rebase so the target plane is spanned by names 1,2. After ordinary contraction X and deletion of all other ordinary sites, the kernel of the four surviving columns consists exactly of named combinations in span(X). Thus the target minor has loops at sites 1,2 and two independent coloops at 3,4, with all four sites fixed. A corresponding decision oracle could recover physical witnesses by processing ordinary sites and testing deletion then contraction, retaining all names throughout and permitting dependent residual names or eliminating their dependencies.

Geelen–Gerards–Whittle prove that high branch-width over a fixed finite field forces an ISOMORPHIC cycle matroid of any fixed planar graph as a minor. Their tangle theorem controls grid minors but still does not prescribe four arbitrary survivors. A direct sum of a high-width graphic grid and four named/ordinary parallel pairs has only coordinate traces on the four names, although the grid contains the unrooted planar target. For an omitted noncoordinate plane rebase the four names to put that plane into the standard two-loop/two-coloop coordinates; the ordinary grid restriction is unaffected and the transported target remains absent. Sources: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jctb.2007.02.005 and https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jctb.2007.10.008 .

For fixed bounded branch-width an explicit finite-field representation admits a constructive branch decomposition (Hliněný–Oum, https://doi.org/10.1137/070685920). The four fixed named labels may be carried in a finite-field interface DP or as unary labels in the minor MSO test. Self-reduction remains in bounded width. Geelen sets out the broad unrooted finite-field minor-testing objective in https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aam.2008.05.002 . Seymour's three-element circuit test covers the three-dimensional line case but imposes no such four-survivor placement. No primary proof located in this audit supplies the unrestricted-width rooted test. This is not a hardness result or a claim that no other algorithm exists.
