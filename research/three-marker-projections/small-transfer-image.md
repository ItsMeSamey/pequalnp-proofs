# Exact five- and six-point images of connected hard components

At any attained T in a connected hard binary component, for any proper subspaces W0 and V0 of the two binary factor planes, one can produce a nonzero neighboring transfer difference g h for which either both factors lie inside their respective subspaces or both lie outside. Choose a shortest fundamental path from an h outside V0 to a g outside W0. At distance one its exchange gives the outside case; at greater distance the interior b1/e1 has both factors inside. If its gain is zero pivot there at the same T, shortening the path and preserving the endpoint conditions as in three-hard-exact.md. Separately, all attained states are connected via rank-one steps, and they affinely span four dimensions by connected-basis-transfer.md.

Enumerate subsets of the 16 matrices of size five or six under these necessary conditions. The checker gives 144 candidates of size five and 304 of size six. Enumerate the physical rank-two class with directions 1,2,3 and any nonempty subset of two-bit column offsets at each direction. Every projection is connected. Evaluating all pairs of independent projected columns realizes all 144 five-point candidates and 288 six-point candidates. The five-point sets are translations and left/right changes of basis of {0,1,2,4,8}; at a realized point there must be at least three rank-one neighbors by the two-ended test. The 16 extra six-point candidates are exactly the translations of the six invertible two-by-two matrices.

None of those translations can contain the entire local image. For a proposed shift L let N' be the quotient of the lifted ordinary columns by the graph of L over the embedded target plane, in dimension rank(N). Inclusion of all local transfers in the translated invertibles would make every N-basis an N'-basis. A connected binary represented matroid N cannot be strictly below another binary represented matroid of equal rank with this inclusion of bases. To check this, normalize both on any N-basis to [I|D] and [I|D']. Every 1 in D must be present in D', by a single basis exchange. If there is an extra edge in D', take a shortest D-path b0,e1,b1,...,ek between endpoints of any extra edge. Its length is at least three. Pivot b1/e1 in both charts. In the pivot rectangle formula the entries b0/e1 coincide, and b1/ek coincide because their distance was smaller. The discrepancy at b0/ek persists. If k=2 it is now an edge present only in D, a contradiction. Otherwise the shortened path permits repetition; select a shortest new discrepancy each time. This proves normalized equality and hence equal kernels. It also gives an explicit basis violating inclusion if the kernels differ (or the initial basis already fails). But any N-cycle has lifted sum (0,z), which is zero in N' only if z=0, since the graph used for quotienting is transverse to the entire vertical plane. Hardness here means the residual z span has dimension two, contradicting equal kernels. This completes classification of the two sizes for arbitrary connected components. It classifies image sets, without assuming a size bound can be decided on arbitrary inputs.

Two hard components can exhibit a physical ambiguity invisible to all static queries from three-transfer-simplices.md, even with the same witness per positive reply. Write a block as its three sets of offsets on projected directions 1,2,3. Take X=({0,2},{0,1},{0,3}) and add one actual site to obtain X'=({0,2},{0,1},{0,2,3}). The two images are respectively {0,2,3,4,5,6,10,12} and that same set plus 7,8. Every three-name row or column fiber and translated invertibility query has the same Boolean result on the two; witnesses in X can be reused physically in X'. Both affine hulls are full. The fixed second hard block Y=({1},{2},{1,2,3}) attains {1,8,9,11,13}. Therefore zero is absent from X+Y and occurs in X'+Y. The extra physical column and queries after conditioning on it remain available to stronger algorithms.

If the known nonhard sum table contains a four-point affine plane, two hard components suffice for full global attainment: the sum of one witnessed affine basis from each hard block has size at least thirteen, except when they are translates. For translated standard simplices their missing points have Hamming weight at least three, a five-point affine simplex containing no affine plane. In either case each translate of the four-point plane meets the sum. Enumerating the physical choices gives witnesses.
