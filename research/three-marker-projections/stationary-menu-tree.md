# Actual paired-word tables on trees with stationary through spaces

Fix a supplied rooted represented substitution tree. At each node an explicit binary private center V contains its outgoing boundary B0, all true local site keys and each child's contact Bi; all contacts, including B0, have dimension at most two. Each child span intersects the remaining graph only on Bi, and its private space modulo the center is independent of all other child spaces modulo the center. Thus partial words glue iff their sums compose in V, and child charts glue iff their restrictions on Bi agree with a common central functional. Keep original physical indices and one arbitrary indexed binary alternating form Ψ. For a represented 2/3-sum decomposition use these actual coordinates, with virtual sites excluded from real sites. An arbitrary edge partition lacking these properties does not suffice.

Compute for every child its table from one-tag-joint-seam.md, indexed by a chart restriction α on Bi and two exact sums u,v in Bi. Each entry is an actual linear basis of (1,x,y,Ψ_i(x,y)) for compatible supported child pairs with their chart witnesses; Ψ_i is the restriction of the original form. Empty states are detected by an empty table.

A child is stationary if there exists a fixed subspace U_i of Bi such that for EVERY α all feasible individual sums lie in U_i, and one chart extending α simultaneously realizes a basis of U_i by supported words. Test the (u,0) entries for all u and α; their lists must be the same subspace U_i. Test an ordered basis tuple (p,q) in each α (append zero entries at smaller rank). A retained actual row of that tuple gives a physical linear section J_iα:U_i→words sharing its chart. Individual attainable sums cannot be spanned without this test, as the five-site example of one-tag-joint-seam.md shows.

Let E be the local span in V of all nonstationary child interfaces and all true local site keys, and d the maximum of its dimension across nodes. The algorithm runs in 2^{O(d)}poly(input) at absolute polynomial exponent. For a supplied bound on d, inspect the computed child tables and stop outside the class if a node exceeds it. It needs no center paired oracle and no bound on depth, fanout, center rank or form rank. It does require the supplied private-space layout; an undecomposed rigid leaf with many independent true sites has large d.

Fix the desired parent state (α0,u0,v0) and any linear functional of (1,x,y,Ψ_region(x,y)). This is a constant plus physical linear forms on x and y and either zero or Ψ_region. Enumerate g|E. All true sites have known activation. Their two bits are free if active and forced zero otherwise. Nonstationary children have fixed chart restrictions; retain any of their sums and actual rows. Combine these bad children and true sites in a table indexed by two aggregate sums in E. Each join concatenates words, adds the two restricted Ψ values and adds both physical cross pairings. Homogenizing each linear term by the other leading one makes the entire row product bilinear. Choices from different children coexist with g|E. Keep actual independent product rows (dimension at most 2n+2) in each of at most 4^d sum cells; combination costs 2^{O(d)}poly(n). Fix one such aggregate row when searching; under any fixed exterior the separating test is linear in it.

In a positive physical evaluation freeze the boundary restrictions and sums and change each stationary child independently between its actual pair and the default section J_iα applied to its two fixed sums. Each switch preserves admissibility, both sums and the center chart. The separating test has switch degree at most two. A nonzero Boolean polynomial of degree at most two is positive at a vector of weight at most two, by Möbius inversion. Thus at most two nondefault stationary children suffice. Guess their identities, exact joint tuples and actual rows. Within their fixed states the separating test is separately row-linear for every fixed outside assignment, so actual basis rows suffice. This is an existence argument; the sparse-default pair need not equal every original positive pair.

Now solve the affine equations on the center chart g from E, B0 and the exceptional children. Solve, independently of g, two linear systems in the vectors p_i,q_i∈U_i for all default children: their sums in V give u0,v0 after subtracting the known aggregate and exception sums. Parametrize the three affine solution spaces by free binary bits; reject inconsistency. Each original x site on a default child is a coordinate of J_i,g|Bi(p_i), and y similarly uses q_i. Its section coefficients are Boolean lookups on ≤2 bits, therefore degree ≤2 in free chart bits, and p_i,q_i are affine. Each physical coordinate has degree ≤3. Fixed physical coordinates are constants. The separating predicate has degree ≤6. Evaluate the *original* predicate on parameter vectors of weight at most six. If it is ever one, return the actual expanded pair and a glued chart. If never one, the Boolean polynomial vanishes identically by Möbius inversion. The sparsity bound is on local free parameters, not on site support.

Use repeated queries on annihilator basis functionals to construct all state-table bases. Leading one detects every nonempty state. All stored rows are actual original indexed physical words sharing a chart at that row. Each parent still queries a linear functional of these same coordinates; the degree-six calculation is local and never recurses in its degree. Each node is solved once, and all table rows and exception choices, free-parameter evaluations and separation iterations have an absolute polynomial bound times 2^{O(d)}. Root with zero seam recovers and verifies the original Ψ witness. The method covers arbitrary-depth trees under this measurable bad-span condition, not arbitrary unconditioned 2/3-sum trees.

For unary contacts, replacing each private site of any center by a pair of parallel sites with opposite tags gives stationary gadgets even at unbounded nonregular center rank; the physical lift depends on g. This single-center example illustrates the test and is not a novelty claim about previous policy-rank algorithms. The extension here is recursive retention of complete weighted joint rows, including rank-two simultaneous sections, cross terms and original witnesses at every level.
