# Robust binary tag menus for the physical exact plane

Group physical columns by identical private vector v and identical two-bit height h. Their two-bit tags form the group's menu, and retain an original representative of each tag. Call a group thick if its menu has at least three entries. An acceptable witness has compatible tags (one linear chart on its selected private vectors), and private-kernel heights spanning the two-dimensional target.

For all-thick inputs remove private-zero groups missing the zero tag. Every remaining private-zero group can use the zero tag. Compute a private basis from the remaining groups. For every off-basis group compute the height of its fundamental dependence including the loop case. If those heights do not span the target, no subset can succeed. Otherwise take two groups with independent fundamental heights together with the private basis. Independently assign the chart value at each basis group uniformly from its menu. Each selected nonzero off-basis vector is a nonempty XOR of basis vectors. Conditional on all but one contributor, the probability of any forbidden tag is at most one third. At most two off-basis groups have a forbidden tag. Their union has probability at most two thirds, giving a physical choice whose heights span the target. One can choose the chart deterministically: process the basis groups consecutively and minimize the conditional expected number of forbidden outcomes at each step. Each conditional probability is computed by four-state XOR convolution of the remaining independent choices; integer numerators and denominators have linear bit lengths. Return actual column representatives and verify their binary span. The same proof applies for target dimension zero or one by choosing the appropriate number of off-basis groups.

More generally let d be the private rank of the remaining groups with at most two tag options. In time 4^d times a polynomial enumerate every linear chart on their joint private span. In each branch take all compatible thin options, with their complete physical span L. Their zero-private trace has tag zero and its height spans some H0 in the target H. Write W for the private projection of L, and choose a linear section of L over W. Split the private space as W plus a complement. For a thick group subtract the section at its W component and reduce its height modulo H0. The private column is now in the complement and every tag in the menu receives the same shift; all options still share one height. Run the thick-only algorithm in the quotient for the remaining target H/H0. Expand every chosen indexed option and include the compatible thin sites. Projection and elimination by the section identify its zero-private relation gains precisely modulo H0, proving soundness. For completeness take the chart of any successful witness; tags compatible on a subspace always extend to the ambient space. Its restriction to the thin span is enumerated. Adding all thin options compatible with it preserves that common chart and original height witnesses. Only W is contracted. The quotient solver may change chart values on private directions outside W even if a tentative value had been guessed for an unused thin group; those unused groups can remain omitted.

In particular the menu of all three nonzero tags for each purported graph-coloring edge cannot enforce proper coloring of every source edge in this exact-plane model, even if private stage coordinates are appended. The theorem assumes one height and private vector per menu and fully optional physical columns. Arbitrary singleton-menu instances remain open.
