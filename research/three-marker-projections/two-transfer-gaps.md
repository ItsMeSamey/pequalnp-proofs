# Two hard components and the remaining simplex filters

For exactly two hard components use the actual five witnessed affine-basis points in each. The sum proves at least eleven transfers and gives physical bases. If these points are not translated copies, it proves at least thirteen. Finite certificates may add actual local points via a positive three-name or ordinary matroid-intersection query and may exclude an entire set by a negative query. More precisely maintain lower and upper attainable sets per component; apply every available predicate, record its physical witness or remove its whole support, and repeat while this learns a point or excludes any candidate. Outputs in the sum of the lower sets are YES; targets outside the sum of the upper sets are NO. This rule uses no common-witness inference from individually attained scalar values. The five-point minimum is physically sharp: for the two families of connected rank-two projections with directions 1,2,3 and nonempty subsets of offsets per direction, exhaustive enumeration gives 144 distinct full-dimensional five-point local tables. For each such table, pairing all 144 possibilities yields sixteen sums of size 11, sixty-four of size 13 and sixty-four of size 14. Every one of the 144 local tables can be certified using the prior probes. These are results for the physically realized five-point tables in this particular family.

Of the prior 816 simplex cases with one possible untested sixth point, 144 automatically realize an extra point if the five affine-basis points have already been witnessed in the same projection component. Their induced rank-one-difference graph is disconnected. Connecting physical bases of two separate pieces by ordinary basis exchange crosses an additional point because every step of the transfer has rank at most one. Compute that extra basis by explicitly walking using standard exchanges. In the remaining 576 cases the graph has degrees (1,1,2,3,3) and the untested point is the sum of three simplex points; in 96 it has degrees (2,2,2,3,3) and that point is the sum of all five. A deterministic extra-point test by walking can still succeed if a chosen path leaves the simplex in a connected case, but is not complete there.

In all 816 cases the determinant at the possible sixth point agrees with its unique affine interpolant on the simplex. Thus no binary linear combination of the constant, four transfer entries and determinant can vanish on all five yet detect that point. This is an obstruction for this feature space on the six abstract transfer points. It neither constructs a physical instance for the remaining 672 types nor excludes stronger physical predicates. Exhaustive checks use two-transfer-gaps.py with its three-transfer-simplices.py dependency.
