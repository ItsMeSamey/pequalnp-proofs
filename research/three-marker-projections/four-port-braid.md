# An unbounded-order four-port electrical correction for a genuine (3,3,1) grove

Work in characteristic two. Write D for the four-port grove signature with weight one on all seven partitions into two blocks and zero elsewhere. It is physically the difference between a star and its normalized electrical clique divided by one nonzero scalar (see four-port-response-defect.md). For example use star conductances (1,1,1,a) with a neither zero nor one in GF(4). Consequently a network of d formal D insertions computes, up to a nonzero scalar, the mixed difference of actual forest evaluations with each site independently replaced by these two ordinary weighted choices.

Use three strands with left ends L1,L2,L3 and right ends R1,R2,R3. At alternating steps introduce a D cell on the first two, then last two strands, with its ports being the two incoming and two outgoing strand positions. The third strand continues unchanged. Consecutive positions along an unchanged strand are identical physical sites; successive incidences at interior sites are bivalent. Terminal sites are distinct once the strip contains both types of cell. The ordinary realizations have bounded pathwidth independent of d.

The mod-two coefficient at end partition {L1} | {L2,L3,R1} | {R2,R3} equals one for every odd d≥3. The finite certificate is check_four_port_braid.py. Its state is a subset of partitions of three left terminal labels and the three current boundary labels, representing odd multiplicity. Initially each left/current pair denotes the same site. For a cell put fresh labels at its two outgoing positions. Enumerate the seven nonempty choices for the block not containing the first input port; its complement is the other block. Adjoin each local block as a tree: if two positions of this block were already in the same previously joined class, discard the cyclic choice. Merge otherwise and process the other block in the updated partition. Forget the two incoming boundary labels (keeping any left marker). If an entire class disappears it is unmarked and cannot be a component of a final grove; discard. Relabel the new positions with their strand names and XOR equal states. This is precisely acyclic gluing of physical forest signatures. Through lengths 0,1,2,3,4,5 it gives state cardinalities printed by the checker, and the ENTIRE state at 3 equals that at 5. The next generator is also the same; induction now proves period two. The displayed target lies in the state at 3. Attaching one extra marked pendant adjacent to R2 and prescribing it to lie with R2 forces that edge in every accepted grove, so the terminal shape is exactly (3,3,1).

Thus no constant bound on the number of simultaneous four-port Schur defects survives for this actual marked-grove task, even in a strip of bounded pathwidth. This does not imply difficulty of deciding the grove on the strip: running the exact finite-state transfer there is polynomial. Nor does it by itself establish an obstruction to other algorithms on bounded-width vortices attached to surfaces.
