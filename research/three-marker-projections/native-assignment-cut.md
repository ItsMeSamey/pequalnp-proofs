# Linear wiring cuts for pointwise native compilers of shared Boolean clauses

A finite native network consists of fixed directed wires between sites with equally many inputs and outputs. Each site selects a permutation. The data bits are assigned to their respective physical sites, and all auxiliary selectors are independent existential choices local to their own sites. The Boolean function of the named data asks whether one simultaneous auxiliary mask puts two fixed marked wires on one orbit. One may allow any finite local menu, arbitrary feedback, and as many auxiliary sites as desired. Consider a split of sites into two sides such that each original input is read only on its own side. With k crossing wires in one direction there are exactly k in the reverse direction by balance.

Cut at a point on every crossing wire and on each marked wire. A mark lying on a crossing wire can use its existing cutpoint. This gives d≤2k+2 points, including the two distinguished ones r,s (coincident marks have constant verdict). Fix a local setting on each side. The permutation of cutpoints obtained by following each until the next cutpoint has the same marked orbit test as the original. Every row of its permutation matrix P with P[i,j]=1 for a transition i to j depends only on the side entered just after point i. Closed components with no cutpoints play no role. Whether the two marks lie in the same orbit is the positivity of the (r,s) entry of (I−P/2)^{-1}, by its convergent nonnegative geometric series. Its determinant denominator is positive, as each cycle contributes a factor 1−2^{-length}. Consequently the corresponding signed cofactor of I−P/2 gives a nonnegative real number whose support is exactly the required test.

Make a real matrix K of these cofactors, indexed by every local data/auxiliary choice on the left and every such choice on the right. Expand the cofactor determinant along all remaining left-controlled rows. Each term is a product of a left-only and a right-only minor, with fixed sign. There are at most 2^{d−1} terms, so rank K≤2^{2k+1}, irrespective of internal network size. Given a family of t globally accepting data pairs whose two crossed pairs cannot both be accepting, choose an actual local witness pair per diagonal entry. The submatrix N on these local witnesses has positive diagonal and N_ij N_ji=0 for i≠j. Its entrywise product with its transpose is diagonal of rank t, while its rank is at most (rank N)^2 by multiplying rank-one decompositions. Thus log₂ t≤4k+2. If every off-diagonal crossed data combination is false then N itself is diagonal and log₂ t≤2k+1.

For the graph independent-set CNF put one Boolean variable at each vertex and a clause (¬x_u∨¬x_v) at each edge. At a given data cut take an induced matching of m crossing edges, pin all other variables false, and enumerate subsets S of the matching indices having size floor(m/2). At the left endpoints set exactly S and at the right endpoints exactly its complement. These are independent sets. Pairing S with the complementary right assignment of a different equal-size T violates an edge in S minus T. The selected witness submatrix is diagonal of size binomial(m,floor(m/2)); thus 2k+1≥m−log₂(m+1). All pins simply restrict the original pointwise function. A 2-clause can be padded by a repeated literal in a 3CNF or evaluated as a three-input clause with one false input. The signed/pinned Latin representation transfers this obstruction when the named Boolean witness is preserved across the representation.

On a simple bounded-degree graph of maximum degree D with edge expansion h>0, each cut with at least n/3 data vertices on each side has ≥hn/3 crossing edges. Greedily take a crossing edge, then remove every candidate with an endpoint in the closed neighborhood of either chosen endpoint. At most 2D(D+1) candidates are removed per iteration, and the resulting matching is induced in the entire graph. Thus m=Ω(n), and every such balanced wiring cut must have k=Ω(n). Explicit constant-degree expander families are available, for example Reingold, Vadhan and Wigderson, Annals of Mathematics 155 (2002), https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2002/155-1/p04 . Loops can be discarded and bounded parallel multiplicity erased with only a constant-factor change to edge expansion.

This barrier is for pointwise representation of the original named data with local selectors. The independent-set example itself has trivial existential satisfiability. The result permits polynomial-size layouts with linear balanced cuts, and permits decision reductions that change the original witness representation.
