# Connected charged partitions through clique adhesions

Fix p,d,s. Use a polynomial-size rooted tree decomposition of a connected charged graph into connected induced bags whose adhesions are actual cliques of size at most s. Each component of a child's subtree interior has an actual neighbor in its adhesion (by tree separation and connectedness). Choose one owner from these neighbors. Each subtree interior excludes its parent adhesion. Charge vectors are binary, d-dimensional.

Table states specify the parent adhesion coloring, set of occurring colors, and interior charge tuple by color. Each occurring color must be connected within the subtree including its parent adhesion. A color present and absent on the adhesion is called closed, meaning present only in the interior. A genuine connected global color restricts to a connected set whenever nonempty: shortcut exterior excursions via same-color contacts on the adhesion clique. The same argument shortcuts each child excursion to see connectivity of that color's restriction to the torso. Conversely connected assignments glue provided a child's closed color occurs nowhere else and all other child colors meet the torso.

Default every child, under any adhesion coloring, by assigning each interior component entirely to its owner's color. This closes no color. Fold its total charge into that owner in the parent torso. The baseline torso weights are original charges (set originals on the parent adhesion to zero), plus all such default additions including at parent-adhesion vertices. They are independent of the selected colors. Empty interiors contribute zero.

In a genuine subtree solution mark all children with closed colors; their number is at most p. For every other child compare its actual interior tuple with the default under the actual boundary colors. The deviations lie in the zero-sum subspace of (F₂^d)^p of dimension d(p−1), so their sum is realized by a subset of at most d(p−1) actual deviations. Keep these and all marked children unchanged, default the others. All local scores, occurring colors and connectivity requirements persist. In particular no color occurring wholly inside one child disappears.

To compute a table entry enumerate the at most p+d(p−1) exceptional children and their complete stored entries, along with the precise set of nonempty torso colors. Enforce all adhesion pins and the parent coloring, reject any closed child color occurring elsewhere, and require the union of colors used to be correct. Remove the chosen children's default charge additions from the baseline, then subtract their actual interior tuples from the desired total. Solve the resulting connected partition on the actual induced torso, omitting its absent colors (their residual scores must vanish). Recover original indexed witnesses by gluing the torso, exceptional children and explicit default component assignments. At root require all p colors. There are finitely many table states in the parameters, polynomially many exceptional choices for fixed p,d, and at most J=s(1+p+d(p−1)) physical torso pins. Bottom-up memoization keeps the polynomial exponent independent of height.

Two checkable torso conditions suffice in any mixture. If the baseline nonzero support on the induced torso has domination number at most k (meaning k torso vertices dominate that support), all altered weights differ only on exceptional adhesions, adding at most J potential owners. The pinned multi-color domination algorithm applies, with a guessed local anchor for every required color lacking pins. Alternatively if p≥2 and the physical torso has vertex connectivity at least (p−1)+2+21(p−1)(d+J), use high-connectivity.md: append one indicator coordinate for each pinned site and put a one in only its assigned color's target. One occurring torso color is trivial. Local dimensions d+J are never inherited by child tables. Running time is polynomial for fixed p,d,k,s.

A clique-minimal-separator atom tree is available in polynomial time (Berry, Pogorelcnik and Simonet, Organizing the atoms of the clique separator decomposition into an atom tree, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0166218X14002455). Build one such tree, choose a root and owners and test the conditions. This recognizes the stated checks on the selected tree. Another tree or choice of owners may pass if the selected one fails. No bound is required on the number or depth of branches, and no shortcut is licensed for a nonclique separator in this theorem.
