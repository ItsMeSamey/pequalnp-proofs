# Constructive product with arbitrary complementary orbits

Observe a cycle anchored at G which contains every distinct permanent guard g_i. In every setting g_i is immediately followed on the observed cycle by its probe p_i if that probe is good, and otherwise by a guard or G. A good p_i is followed by a guard or G. Bad probes lie off this cycle with no restrictions on their orbit sizes, count or cyclic order. The order of guard slots on the good cycle itself may vary. The native switches are independent optional rotations at disjoint physical triples.

Suppose a switch touching an actually changed physical departure on the old segment from g_i changes the status of u≠i. Then the old segments from g_u and p_u must also contain changed departures: g_u gains or loses p_u, and p_u's successor changes between a guard/G and a complementary site. Return segments have disjoint departures. Exactly one changed departure lies in each of these three segments. Thus the first-return permutation is changed by a genuine three-cycle rearrangement of their three suffixes.

If u was good, all three old segments start on the G circle. Rejoining at three distinct cuts by a three-cycle either keeps the whole circle connected, preserving u, or produces three circles, with the two cut guards g_i and g_u in different ones. Both outcomes contradict the hypotheses for a changed u. If u was bad, two distinct cuts g_i and g_u are on the G circle, and the third cut p_u is outside it. Either three-cycle orientation splits the G circle into two portions, each ending at its respective cut guard, and attaches the old complement circle to one of them. The other portion remains separate, so the two guards no longer lie together. This is impossible. Hence this switch cannot alter any u≠i.

Given an arbitrary current mask and a target mask attaining a different desired status for i, some actually differing departure on the current g_i return segment must change toward the target, since the target assigns that segment a different observed endpoint. Switch its gate toward the target, retrace, and continue until the desired status holds. Each step decreases Hamming distance and preserves all other statuses. It follows that the status table is precisely the Cartesian product of the individual possibilities, and unary witness masks can be merged in polynomial time. Obtaining unary witnesses for a general source remains a separate problem.
