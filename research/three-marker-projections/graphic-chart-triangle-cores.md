# Finding a spanning graphic chart from intrinsic triangle cores

Input is a represented binary matrix in arbitrary row coordinates with four independent named columns. No graph coordinates or edge subset need be supplied for this sufficient recognition procedure. Reduce to the span of the ordinary columns (in applying the four-independent-name theorem assume the names are spanned), ignore loops, and simplify ordinary parallel classes for chart discovery only, keeping original site indices for the solver.

A spanning ordinary restriction that is graphic and 3-connected fixes a coordinate chart. Test graphicness and recover an indexed graph by a constructive graphic-matroid realization algorithm, e.g. Bixby and Wagner, https://doi.org/10.1287/moor.13.1.99. Require its graph to be simple 3-vertex-connected on at least four vertices; discard redundant isolated vertices. Map the columns of a chosen basis to the even incidence vectors of the recovered labelled tree edges. Fundamental circuits of each other anchor edge verify its image, with unique binary coefficients in that basis. Extend this isomorphism to all original ordinary and named columns by their original basis coordinates. Gather the entire simple graph formed by the weight-two ordinary columns, and test its vertex connectivity, for example by vertex-capacitated max flows. If it is at least 600, apply dense-graphic-four-space.md to the full original labelled input. Whitney uniqueness means this check is exact for charts in which this anchor consists entirely of incidence edges; the anchor itself need only be 3-connected. The witness map uses only reversible row coordinates and retains all physical identities.

Here is one polynomial family of intrinsic anchor candidates. On the distinct nonzero ordinary vectors form the triple system with hyperedges x,y,x+y, enumerated by looking up every pair sum. For each integer threshold j from zero up to the maximum triple degree, repeatedly remove any point incident to fewer than j surviving triples. Test each connected component of the resulting triple system as the candidate restriction above. Empty cores may be skipped. The surviving core is independent of peeling order since any subset of minimum triple-degree ≥j is preserved throughout, and the final set has that minimum degree. There are polynomially many candidates and all steps have polynomial time on the explicitly represented input. The procedure may abstain: failing all candidates makes no assertion about other possible coordinate charts or plane feasibility. The threshold-zero case alone suffices for a triangle component isolated from unrecognizable ordinary extras.

For an example not found by testing only the full component, take all incidence edges of K_n, n≥601, and add arbitrary many vectors of weight four supported on pairwise disjoint four-vertex sets. Precompose the representation with any unknown invertible row map. Each extra is in exactly three triangles, the pairings of its own four support vertices. There are no triple edges involving two extras since their sum has weight eight. Every clique edge belongs to at least n−2 clique-edge triangles. Every extra attaches to the single clique triangle component at threshold zero, but threshold four removes all extras and no clique edge, recovering the spanning K_n chart. The original component with any one extra is nonregular: restrict to a K₅ on its four vertices and a fifth, then contract the four-vector. Modulo 1111 the four units and three complementary pairs form F₇. Thus simply asking if the full ordinary matroid is graphic fails. The number of extras is unbounded with n. The method also handles exponentially many distinct extra vectors when they form other components: take weight at least six and pairwise Hamming distances at least four, so none is in a triple with a clique edge. Large such families exist by greedy packing (each chosen even vector excludes only polynomially many even vectors at distance below four).
