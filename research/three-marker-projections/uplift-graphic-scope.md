# Graphicness of the one-row common uplift does not enlarge the private graphic class

Let B be the binary private matroid, and h1,h2 binary height rows independent modulo row(B), as required by a full anchor. Over a characteristic-two extension field with a outside GF(2), form Q=[B;h1+a*h2]. If Q is graphic as a labeled matroid, B is graphic.

First separate the matroid components of B and call one active if its circuits have some nonzero height word. A nonactive component's Q restriction is just its original matroid. If two or more are active, for each active component i choose a circuit D of another active component with nonzero height. The sum of its columns in Q is a nonzero multiple of the bottom unit e. Contract all of D, then restrict to i. In the private projection the two components' spans are independent, so the resulting minor represents precisely B on i. Every component is therefore graphic. If there is only one active component, the two height forms retain independence modulo its row span and it suffices to work on that connected component.

Append the bottom unit e to Q to obtain M. Its deletion M backslash e is graphic, and contraction M/e is B. Because B is connected, and e is neither loop nor coloop, M is connected. It is nonbinary. In fact use a private basis plus e as basis; its fundamental bipartite graph on the private basic and nonbasic physical columns is connected. Its private block has entries zero/one. Any row and column scalings to a binary standard representation must multiply all its private row factors by a common scalar and all its physical nonbasis column factors by the inverse. The bottom row on nonbasis columns consists of residual h1+a*h2 values after killing the private basis. At least two nonzero such values differ, because the binary residual forms h1,h2 are independent. Thus those values cannot all be scaled to one. Over characteristic two a standard matrix represents a binary matroid exactly when its support can be so scaled, by the chordless-cycle proof in binary-weak-map.md.

Take the standard 2-sum tree decomposition of connected M into circuits, cocircuits and 3-connected pieces. Only the piece containing physical e can be nonbinary. Indeed, any other piece can be realized as a minor avoiding e: for each incident 2-sum choose one physical representative from that side, avoiding e (a proper side has at least two physical elements), and contract/delete there to a parallel pair consisting of the virtual marker and that representative. This uses connectivity, since both chosen elements lie on a common circuit. The resulting minor is a minor of one of M backslash e, M/e, both binary. Some piece must be nonbinary, since binary matroids are closed under two-sums. The central piece R containing e is therefore a 3-connected nonbinary matroid; it is a minor of M preserving e. Both R backslash e and R/e are binary. Oxley's single-element theorem gives R=U(2,4). Its deletion and contraction of e are connected U(2,3) and U(1,3). Consequently both reductions of M decompose using the same neighboring 2-sum pieces with the central piece replaced by the corresponding connected three-element piece. Every neighboring piece is a minor of graphic M backslash e, hence graphic. Replacing the central triangle by the graphic three-parallel class and using closure under two-sums proves M/e graphic.

The 3-connected theorem is stated in James Oxley, *Matroid Theory*, second edition, Section 12.3 exercise 13(a), attributing the result to *On nonbinary 3-connected matroids*, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 300 (1987), 663–679. https://www.math.ens.psl.eu/~benoist/refs/Oxley.pdf . The same decomposition argument applies to a minor-closed, direct- and two-sum-closed class containing both connected three-element pieces, provided the deletion matroid belongs to that class and is binary. No such restriction on Q is imposed by the general one-row base-containment reduction.
