# A public three-socket data control with independent nullable reads

Take external rails G,P,N and private m. Use fixed F=(P m), data rotation R=(P m N), and an independent optional transposition (G m) made by one native rotation with private third rail z. In forward order apply the optional enable, the data gate, then F; hide m and z by feedback. Since F R=(N m), disabling the enable gives identity on all three exterior tracks. Enabling it gives (G P) for data 0 and (G N) for data 1. Thus one genuine native input and one independent native enable realize exactly the four requested cases. The self-contained checker check_public_nullable.py traces both private lanes. This construction makes actual use of the third data socket.

Inflate the m segment by inserting k ordered passive probe sites m_i. With every enable off these sites lie on the P track for input zero and on the N track for input one. At each m_i independently offer a transposition to its own external track G_i, using a private third channel. On cutting and rejoining the chosen path, the new first return consists of an ordered cycle through the chosen hub and the enabled G_i, with all other tracks fixed. Equivalently it is the ordered product of (h,G_i) over enabled probes, for h=P or N according to the one original data switch. Reversing the product convention only reverses the order assigned to the taps. This exports arbitrarily many readable sites on a common selected hub.

For a single three-socket flip that preserves the entire exposed return permutation in both settings, a transfer of a passive site between different exterior paths forces a specific local geometry. Three distinct touched exterior paths would all change output. If there is one cut on each of two exterior paths and the third lies on a closed circuit, the projected flip interchanges their outputs. Thus two cuts lie on one exterior path and one on the other. Of the two possible cyclic orientations exactly one retains the old external pairs. It removes the segment between the two cuts and inserts it at the cut on the other path. All passive sites transferred between distinct exterior paths therefore move together between the same ordered pair of tracks. Attached-to-detached moves when only one exterior track is involved are outside this description.

## Whole-network decision with fixed feedback

Use any collection of these independent broadcasts, with a different named data switch in each, and any fixed directed permutation feedback on their terminal rails. Additional ordinary optional transpositions are allowed. For fixed data values take the cycles of the reference exterior permutation with all taps off as vertices. For each broadcaster insert the available edges from its chosen hub's baseline cycle to its leaf cycles. Ordinary optional transpositions contribute unconditional edges. A joining mask exists exactly when the marked baseline cycles are connected: necessity follows from components; sufficiency follows by enabling only edges on a simple path. Products of transpositions on a forest merge entire baseline cycles, regardless of their physical ordering, yielding the original marked join.

For unassigned inputs, first contract all unconditional edges and all the leaf vertices of each nonempty broadcaster together. Its leaves are always connected in the available graph through whichever hub was chosen. Ignore empty broadcasters. The remaining choice for each variable is one of two edges sharing the consolidated leaf endpoint. Give both the same unique variable color and drop loops; parallel edges can be kept with their own incidences. Two different marked quotient vertices can be connected under some settings precisely when they have a simple path whose incident selected edges at each internal vertex have different colors. Indeed a simple path can use both alternatives of one variable only consecutively at their shared endpoint (parallel edges cannot both lie in a simple path). A compatible path therefore determines mutually consistent original data values. If the roots coincide after contraction any data choice suffices.

Here is a constructive perfect-matching test for the compatible path on a loopless multigraph. For every incidence of e at v make a pair p_ve,q_ve matched by default. Each edge e=uv allows p_ue to match p_ve. For every color at v make a default pair c, cprime, and connect q_ve to the c of its color. Introduce two slack vertices at each internal v, adjacent to each other and to every cprime there. Use only one slack at each root, adjacent to its cprimes. In a perfect matching every externally selected graph edge consumes both incidence p's; the two exposed q's each consume their own color's c. This allows at most one use of a color at that vertex. The freed cprimes must match slack vertices. Thus each internal vertex has zero or two distinct selected colors and each root has one. Conversely any such subgraph extends to a perfect matching by filling the default pairs and slack edges. Its path component between the roots is the requested simple compatible path; discard detached cycles. Pick the indicated data values, fill unforced choices, rebuild the uncontracted available graph and enable only the taps on a simple physical quotient path. This last reconstruction avoids any assumption that the contracted connections are themselves fixed wires.

If a mark is inside a broadcaster, enumerate the data bit for that broadcaster. At most two data bits need this treatment. After fixing them, absorb the broadcaster's transport wiring into the reference and expose its independent optional tap transpositions. If the private third path of a tap contains a mark, enumerate that tap as well, at most two extra bits. Retain passive mark sites while compressing fixed paths and detached circuits. The remaining problem has external marks as above. The constant at most sixteen branches permits all interior marks even for unbounded fanout. This result assumes a supplied assembly and independent controls. Permitting external nonmatching clause cells or tying different broadcast data switches adds behavior not covered here.
