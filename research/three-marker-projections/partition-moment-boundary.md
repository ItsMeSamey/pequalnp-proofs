# Rooted forest responses and partition moments

For each ordered connected spanning partition define x_v and y_v to indicate membership in its first two parts. The indicator of any four-bit prescribed pair of part sums is a binary polynomial of bidegree at most (4,4), being the product of eight affine parity checks. A linear basis of the vectors of all evaluations of squarefree monomials of that bidegree on actual partitions is a universal witness list of size at most (sum_{i<=4} binom(n,i))^2. The zero vector of polynomial evaluations on this list implies zero on every feasible partition. This proves existence; enumerating partitions constructs it only offline. The third part is the complementary vertex set, and nonempty connected parts are required in every basis witness.

For fixed roots r1,r2,r3 in the parts, the affine hull of degree-one moment vectors DOES admit a deterministic polynomial construction. Merge the three roots, taking the reduced graphic incidence matrix N of the resulting graph. Regard each physical column as carrying two extra bits Z, its original incidences at r1,r2. A physical N-basis is precisely a rooted spanning three-forest: lifting a spanning tree of the root identification separates the three roots, and each original nonroot is still joined to its assigned root. Conversely a rooted three-forest gives such a tree. For each nonroot v the unique representation of its coordinate unit in the basis is its path to the identified root; Z summed on that path is 10,01,00 according to its original component (in coordinate notation). Thus the entire two-row matrix Z_B N_B^-1 is the desired degree-one response, with trivial fixed entries added at the roots. Apply connected-basis-transfer.md with input equal to the identity; if N decomposes into components only one changes at a time. Annihilator search yields an affine basis of the output with actual trees in polynomial time even though input rank grows. Union over distinct roots gives degree-one witnesses for unrooted ordered partitions as well. The K_n example with more than k unpinned vertices shows why any one arbitrary witness for each placement of k terminals does not suffice: every such placement consistent with the roots can be completed with even size of the first part by toggling one unpinned vertex, although odd-sized parts also occur.

For each ordered pair i,j of different nonroots, the alternating cross moment x_i y_j+x_j y_i is the determinant of the two columns of this transfer. Its same-forest generating polynomial over F2 is given by Cauchy–Binet: use N on the first side of the product, and on the other quotient the physical (N,Z) columns by the span of the two input coordinate vectors (with zero Z entries); this leaves rank-N ambient dimension. The second determinant is one exactly for bases where those two transfer outputs are independent. Independent variables for physical edges distinguish forests. Formal sums of such quantities and the affine moments have random evaluation and deletion-based witness extraction; mixed individual moments are not implied by their alternating sums.

Over characteristic zero a generic evaluation routine for all rooted moments would include bidegree (2,2) on fixed roots with four specified extra terminals. This is precisely the 3,3,1 weighted grove partition, covered by the separate reviewed permanent-to-grove reduction. No analogous counting lower bound over characteristic two follows. In characteristic two the 25 five-terminal three-block partition types give coefficient space rank 24 for all two-root-triple transversal products plus the ten individually accessible 3,1,1 groves. On all 41 patterns into at most three classes, append every pair of two-link separation tests and unfiltered total; the rank is 35 and none of the 15 individual 2,2,1 patterns lies in the span. These are limitations on these designated coefficient features. All 41 types physically occur among spanning three-forests of K7 with two unused vertices for singleton components if needed.
