# Recognizing an unknown simultaneous plane quotient in parameter k alone

Given binary block subspaces R_i, find an original quotient kernel U of dimension at most k such that every block image is a coordinate subspace of dimension at most two in one common basis of the image span. The following deterministic search takes 2^{O(k²)} times polynomial input length.

At a search node perform all linear algebra in the current quotient with budget k. If the supplied-quotient recognizer with zero additional kernel succeeds, return. At budget zero reject otherwise. For a block of rank at least three choose any three independent vectors within it. One of their seven nonzero sums must vanish in a valid quotient. Branch on these directions, quotient by the chosen nonzero direction, and decrease k. We can now assume all remaining blocks have rank at most two; discard zeros and duplicate spaces.

Use the polynomial simultaneous-coordinate test to find a coordinate prefix with span W and its first failing extra block R. Fix a coordinate basis B of W returned for that prefix. Let F consist of all singleton generators and all nonzero intersections of pairs of distinct prefix planes. Every element of F belongs to B: two distinct coordinate planes meeting in a line meet on a coordinate axis. For each nonsingleton f in F record two witnessing planes through it. Every B-axis outside F belongs to exactly one prefix plane. Within each plane independently replace its non-F axes by any completion of its fixed F axes to a local basis. This is a valid coordinate basis change: modulo span(F), the spaces of private axes of the different owners are independent direct summands. No other block uses these private axes.

Fix hypothetically a valid unknown quotient map Q for the entire instance, with kernel of dimension at most k and a witnessing quotient basis. There is a choice of the preceding changes giving an old basis B' in which every non-F axis has image either zero or a single quotient basis element. Indeed an owner whose image has dimension ≤1 already has this property on all vectors. For a rank-two image its three nonzero vectors consist of two quotient axes and their sum. With no fixed F axis take the two axes' preimages; with one fixed axis choose a quotient axis with independent preimage; with two fixed axes no change is needed. We need not identify Q to enumerate the relevant portion of this choice.

For each nonzero x in R∩W (at most three vectors) let S be its support in B'. The image Qx has coordinate weight at most two. Every member of S has image of weight at most two because it belongs to some prefix block. The rank of these images is ≥|S|−k, so at least |S|−k distinct quotient coordinates occur. At most two coordinates occur an odd number of times. Total multiplicity is therefore ≥2(|S|−k)−2. It follows that at most 2k+2 support members have image weight ≤1. In particular at most 2k+2 different private owners have nonzero contribution to x modulo F. These owners can be read from the initial basis B; they do not depend on private basis changes since each owner's coordinate block changes invertibly modulo F. Reject if this bound fails. Across all x there are at most 6k+6 active owners. Enumerate all their local basis choices (at most 6 choices per plane), fixing inactive owners arbitrarily. One guess gives the same actual supports of every x as B'. Fix a guess and compute those supports by elimination.

**Large support.** If some support has size m>4k+4, use any such x. In the good guess at least m−2k−2≥m/2 of its supporting axes have image of weight two. All of these lie in F and none comes from a singleton. For such f, its two distinct witnessing planes must both map onto the unique coordinate plane containing that weight-two image. Their original joint dimension is three, so their joint span contains a nonzero kernel direction. Count each of the seven nonzero directions of the recorded joint span once for this support occurrence; skip singleton forced axes and unforced axes. Since the kernel contains at most 2^k−1 nonzero directions, at least one of them occurs at least m/[2(2^k−1)] times in these lists. The total number of listed occurrences is at most 7m, so there are at most 14(2^k−1) directions at that threshold. Propose only those for contraction.

**Small supports.** If every support has size ≤4k+4, form V_* from R, all the actual supporting axes, and both recorded planes for every supporting forced axis without a singleton witness. This space has dimension O(k+1). In the good guess suppose Q is injective on V_*. Each supporting forced axis has a unit image: singleton images are units when nonzero, and a weight-two image of a witnessed intersection would force a nonzero kernel direction in its recorded three-space. The supporting non-F axes also have unit images by the good choice and injectivity. All supporting axes thus map to distinct units.

If R⊆W, the span of its images is a coordinate subspace in the images of those supporting axes. Pulling back by the injective restriction shows that R itself is spanned by old axes. If R∩W is a line smaller than R, its generator x has weight one or two in that same system. In the former case x is an old axis, so any one new independent direction of R outside W extends the old chart. In the latter case the only coordinate plane containing Qx is the plane of the two supporting units. Injectivity on V_*, containing both their old axes and R, would force R to equal that old two-axis plane, contrary to R not being contained in W. If R∩W=0, its basis extends the old chart independently. In every case the failing prefix would be coordinate, a contradiction. Therefore propose every nonzero vector of V_* for contraction.

Take the union of directions proposed by all guesses. Any genuine kernel contains one direction in the union; a proposed direction outside every true kernel can only make its recursive branch fail. Each contraction lowers the budget, and each node proposes at most 2^{O(k+1)} actual directions. Return original kernel generators by lifting the contracted coordinates; a recursive success verifies by the ordinary simultaneous-coordinate algorithm on the original input. Multiplication over the k levels gives the claimed bound. This is structural recognition, separate from the subsequent 2-SAT profile computation.
