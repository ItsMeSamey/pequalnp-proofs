# Exact quotient dimension and joins in literal clause charts

On the actual clause compiler with distinct surviving literal coordinates (and blocks of width ≤3), the minimum quotient dimension k is precisely the transversal number of the family of ternary scopes. In any feasible quotient U, each triple span must meet U nontrivially. Fix a global echelon basis of U. The leading position of each nonzero vector of U is a pivot position; for a vector supported on a triple this hits that triple. All pivots together have size dim U. Conversely kill the coordinate forms of a minimum hitting set: all surviving blocks are coordinate and have dimension ≤2. This covers duplicate scopes, smaller blocks and any invertible change of ambient coordinates. It need not hold after arbitrary noninjective affine substitutions. The compiler's bounded-search-tree already finds a literal hitting set.

Disjoint literal instances therefore add k exactly. In a Fano-plane NAE chart on seven independent bits with seven line scopes (each two opposite clauses, six allowed patterns, nonaffine), every line meets every other. Two points cover at most five lines, hence k=3, still 3 after any one line deletion. Whole Fano is NO and a line-deleted Fano YES; structural k alone does not distinguish them. Do not transfer these values to expanded SAT gadgets without verifying their emitted row spaces.

For any simple cubic bipartite vertex expander place an edge variable at each edge and a NAE clause pair at each vertex on its three incident edges. Each block survives the nonaffine table check. A transversal is an edge cover; covering all n vertices costs at least n/2 and a perfect matching attains it. The original formula is satisfiable by setting precisely the matching edges true. This gives linear quotient dimension in the actual connected clause chart, without any SAT running-time lower bound.

Given a tree decomposition of the literal variable scopes with adjacent bag intersections ≤2, choose a home bag for every original clause block. For a translated profile-membership query, first compute one GLOBAL lift Hy=t after subtracting the translation. The resulting local missing-bit constraints and shrinking clauses have their original scopes. Each bag enumerates the ≤4 assignments on its parent separator; each child sends its exact feasible boundary table, each forbidden tuple giving a clause of width at most two (an empty forbidden tuple signals impossibility). Condition on a local ternary transversal, and solve the remaining local constraints including child tables by 2-SAT. Save reconstructing assignments for feasible separator states. Running intersection ensures that child witnesses join on all common variables. Decode menus from y+Wx, then split copies in translated two-profile queries and replay the physical checks. Original whole-instance decoding uses its original translation. Time polynomial(input) times sum over bags of 4(3^k_b+2^k_b), with k_b the local literal transversal of owned blocks, available by width-three branching. Assigned boundary values do not change physical multiplicities. This improves arbitrary-profile handling when many locally small charts join on one or two bits; no existence theorem for such a decomposition on arbitrary formulas is asserted.

For dense charts on a tree partition of block IDs, compute for each edge the intersection P of the two original shores' row spans. Suppose dim P≤2. Put P as a synthetic block into both incident local charts along with each node's owned physical blocks. This ensures that after a chosen quotient evaluation every incident seam truth table depends on at most two local basis bits. Give P a fixed linear basis, enumerate its at most four evaluations, and express each forbidden local basis tuple by a width-at-most-two clause. Apply the verified k-only recognizer to the augmented local chart. Use the same table propagation and physical reconstruction as above, with no literal requirement. To see sufficiency of the gluing test, inside every subtree all synthetic rows there belong to its original row span: an internal seam lies in the span of its interior shore, and the parent seam also lies in this subtree's span. The complementary assertion holds on the other side. Their augmented intersection is exactly P. Any two linear functionals agreeing on that intersection extend linearly to their sum. When combining children successively, the current combined space lies in the complement of the next child except for interface forms already in its complement, and contains that interface from the parent node. Thus local agreement checks suffice and the final linear solve recovers one global x. Local k includes every incident synthetic block; there is no accumulating ancestor budget. With supplied tree this costs sum of 2^O(k_b^2) poly(L), including FPT recognition, and gives actual indexed menu witnesses.
