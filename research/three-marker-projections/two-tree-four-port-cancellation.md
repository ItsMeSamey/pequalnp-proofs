# Four-mark two-tree correction is first order in four-port defects in characteristic two

Take four distinct marked sites, with any fixed partition into two nonempty trees. In an otherwise arbitrary indexed exterior with unmarked, edge-disjoint four-port regions, write each four-port signature as a Schur clique times its normalization plus a scalar multiple of D, when its interior pivot is invertible. D is supported with coefficient one on all seven two-block partitions and zero elsewhere; the signature identity is the earlier four-port-response-defect.md. The following statement is about evaluating D insertions and also applies to formal networks with D prescribed, with no invertibility requirement on the other physical edges.

In the expansion by D insertions EVERY term with ≥2 insertions vanishes over characteristic two. The coefficient of a single D at ports T is exactly the unsigned mixed rooted four-forest sum on the remaining ordinary graph: forests with four components, each containing one of the four marks and one of the four ports. This equals the single all-minors matrix-tree determinant deleting the four named rows and the four port columns, since bijection signs are trivial in characteristic two. Intersections between marks and ports cause no problem. Consequently for four-mark two-tree queries the response of arbitrarily many four-seams is additive (up to the standard common Schur scalar factors), with one ordinary minor per residual. This statement does not cover three global trees.

Fix one ordinary exterior forest and contract its components; an exterior edge cycle contributes nothing. Let d>0 be the number of D cells and m the number of external component-sites including isolates. A two-tree gluing requires m=2d+2, as every D state contributes exactly two independent joins. Count incidences with multiplicity, four per cell. An unmarked degree-zero site cannot join a marked component; an unmarked degree-one site annihilates the sum by integrating that occurrence of D down to three ports: for any restriction to the other three ports and no detached singleton, the two admissible 4-port refinements cancel, or none exists. This holds before identifying any of the remaining three positions, so includes coincidences. If a marked site is isolated, its required tree already occupies a whole component, and all other cells would have to lie in a single marked tree. A monochrome D cell contributes zero to any final single-tree gluing: its terminal minors are zero, equivalently the sum of its seven 2-plane Plücker vectors is zero. More explicitly, after fixing every other state and contracting its ordinary component forests, single-tree completion is the determinant of the two extra joins projected to the rank-two remaining quotient; this determinant is linear in the local plane Plücker coordinates. If only one global color occurs among the four ports, no other color interacts with this test. Thus in any nonzero term every external site has positive degree and unmarked sites have degree at least two.

Now Σ(deg−2)=4d−2m=−4. There are at most four marked sites after contracting the exterior, so there must be exactly four distinct degree-one sites, all marked separately, and every other site must have degree exactly two. For each fixed globally feasible red/blue coloring of the contracted external sites, sum local D states. A cell of one color cancels by the preceding fixed-others argument. A cell of two colors has unique local state, the partition into the two color classes. Suppress each unmarked degree-two external site, leaving an edge joining its two incident cells (possibly a loop), and leave the four marked degree-one occurrences as prescribed halfedges. Each color must now give a spanning tree on the same d cell vertices: every polychrome cell occurs in both layers, and the four fixed halfedges add pendant leaves without joins between cells. Conversely each ordered complementary pair of spanning trees gives exactly one valid coloring provided all cells see both colors; for d≥2 each tree touches every cell, so this is automatic. Swapping the tree colors pairs these decompositions freely when d≥2 (there are d−1≥1 edges per tree). The fixed halfedge colors remain fixed in the swap, and both trees still connect all their prescribed leaves. Hence the contribution is even, for EVERY exterior forest individually. At d=1 the four distinct degree-one marked exterior components touch distinct physical ports; their two required colors determine exactly one of D's seven states. This gives the claimed mixed determinant.

The electrical Schur formula in the original field may have singular pivots. For arithmetic reduction purposes perturb the underlying physical edge weights and specialize by the earlier interpolation method rather than treating division by zero as legitimate. The vanishing statement requires only characteristic two. No unrestricted evaluator for the zero-insertion base term or for the intrinsic four-port mismatch of an arbitrary child follows from this identity alone.
