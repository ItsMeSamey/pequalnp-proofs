# Deterministic arbitrary alternating queries from a recognized large one-bit chart

Take indexed binary private columns K_i and one tag bit t_i. Write R=ker K, with physical coordinate restrictions e_i on R. For a private chart g, its active site set A consists of indices i with g(K_i)=t_i and its relation space R_g is R intersect the coordinate subspace supported on A. Let L be the span of these R_g over all g. Use the previously established certifying scalar first-space routine to compute L in polynomial time. Alternatively replace L in this sufficient test by ker[K;t] if a simpler but potentially weaker parameter is preferred.

The hypothesis that some R_g has codimension at most one in L is deterministically recognizable. In coordinates on a supplied L basis, form ℓ_i=e_i restricted to L. For V=0 or the span of one nonzero ℓ_i, solve g(K_j)=t_j at all sites with ℓ_j outside V. No equation is needed at sites with zero signature. If solvable, return g. Indeed R_g is exactly the subspace of L annihilated by the signatures of all inactive sites (by L⊆R and containing every R_g). Its codimension is the rank of those signatures. Conversely when that rank is at most one the corresponding V appears in this list. Construct an actual basis of R_g by elimination on active physical columns. There is no requirement that this chart stay fixed in the second stage.

Given an arbitrary indexed alternating Ω, test it on a basis of this R_g. A nonzero pairing gives two actual physical matched relations immediately. If Ω vanishes on R_g then its restriction to L has rank ≤2: in a basis extending the isotropic subspace of codimension at most one, only the last row/column can contain nonzero entries. If zero return NO. Otherwise factor Ω|L as h1 wedge h2 by alternating elimination, and extend these two binary linear forms from L to all physical words (any extension). Every genuinely matched pair lies in L and its Ω evaluation equals this determinant. This step also works independently of the large-chart hypothesis if rank(Ω|L)≤2.

A deterministic three-name oracle suffices for the remaining rank-two one-tag query, via an explicit duality that preserves original indices. Let A:R→F2³ have coordinates (t,h1,h2) and let H={(0,a,b)}. The goal on a physical support S is A(R_S)=H, where R_S consists of R words supported on S. If rank A<3, H not contained in im A gives NO; otherwise im A=H and all sites already attain it. Suppose rank A=3. In the space R* use physical columns e_i|R and independent names t|R,h1|R,h2|R spanning U. On a complement S' accepting exactly the named line spanned by t|R, we have

    A(R_S)^perp = {v : A^T v belongs to span{e_i|R : i in S'}},   S=E minus S'.

This is the annihilator of an intersection of coordinate kernels. Thus the exact three-name line query is equivalent in both directions. A positive answer supplies S'; find the two original words on S mapping to (0,1,0),(0,0,1) by solving linear systems. Every dependency on S has zero tag, giving one common private chart on S. The three-name line solver uses the represented binary circuit-through-three procedure of Geelen, Gerards and Whittle, Triples in matroid circuits, section 2 (arXiv:2611.20960v1); it is never asked to find two simultaneously constrained circuits.

Consequently the recognized class admits deterministic polynomial original-index arbitrary-Ω search, and ordinary separating-form accumulation recovers W with actual generators. The class includes arbitrary binary hosts with at most two nonzero sites in any gauge of the tag row: every matched word has even parity over those exceptional sites, so their two coordinate restrictions on L are equal (or the single one is zero). The gauge need not be supplied. It also allows many exceptional sites provided their inactive signatures span a line. There is no assertion every connected binary matroid admits such a chart. The same rank test applies to a supplied parameter b: enumerate spans of at most b signatures in n^{O(b)} time, and any alternating Ω|L of rank >2b necessarily detects on the returned R_g. For two independent tag bits even rank-two residual Ω ordinarily leaves four names and is not resolved by this dual reduction.
