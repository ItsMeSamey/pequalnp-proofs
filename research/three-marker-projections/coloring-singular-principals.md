# A characteristic-two singular principal-minor problem with growing field degree

The following decision problem is NP-complete. Input is a square matrix over an explicitly represented characteristic-two field, including its polynomial-size field representation. Ask whether some principal submatrix is singular. The reduction uses fields K containing F₄ with [K:F₄] at most twice the order of the output matrix. The empty principal determinant equals one.

Take a connected graph with n vertices and m≥n edges, with at least one cycle, and orient the edges arbitrarily. One may use connected four-regular 3-colorability as source, as in the accompanying routing construction. Alternatively, join the components of a general 3-colorability instance by a chain of bridges and attach a triangle at one vertex to ensure a cycle. Both operations preserve colorability by permuting colors separately. Classical source hardness is given by [Garey, Johnson and Stockmeyer](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975%2876%2990059-1).

Write F₄={0,1,ω,ω²}, with ω²=ω+1. For a directed edge e=(u,v), the row of M(z) has coefficient 1 at v and coefficient ω+zₑ at u. All zₑ are independent Boolean bits. A nonzero kernel vector has every vertex entry nonzero, by connectedness. Along each edge the ratio is then ω or ω², so the three nonzero entries serve as proper colors. Conversely any proper coloring by F₄* has one of these two ratios at each edge and supplies a mask with a kernel vector. For any fixed mask the entries on a spanning tree are determined by one root entry, so rank is n or n−1. A full-rank baseline z⁰ is explicit. Assign arbitrary nonzero gains on a spanning tree and propagate a nonzero root value. On a chord choose one of the two gains that disagrees with that assignment.

Let d be the least power of two ≥m and construct K of degree d over F₄. A deterministic tower starts at θ=ω, which has absolute trace 1. At each step extend by s²+s=θ. This polynomial is irreducible since the image of x↦x²+x is the absolute-trace-zero hyperplane. The new θ'=sθ again has absolute trace 1 since the relative trace of s is 1. The usual tower basis and field operations have polynomial bit cost: a multiplication recursively uses a constant number of half-size multiplications at each of log₂d levels. Inversion uses repeated squaring to exponent |K|−2 of bit length 2d.

Use m distinct tower basis elements b₁,…,bₘ. Form the n×m matrix P with Pᵢⱼ=bⱼ^(4ⁱ) for i=0,…,n−1. For any GF₄ matrix M of the stated dimensions, PM is nonsingular over K precisely when the columns of M are independent over F₄. Indeed, define yⱼ as the GF₄-linear encoding of column j in the b basis; column j of PM consists of yⱼ^(4ⁱ). A GF₄ dependence gives a K dependence. Conversely, if the yⱼ are independent but PM singular, a nonzero left kernel gives a nonzero linearized polynomial of degree at most 4^(n−1) vanishing on their 4ⁿ-element GF₄ span, impossible. This simultaneous rank preservation uses no random weights or per-mask choice.

Set T=PM(z⁰), U=T⁻¹P, and let Vᵀ be the m×n matrix whose edge row e is the coordinate row for its tail u. Toggling an edge bit changes its one entry by 1. Thus with D(x) diagonal on the Boolean toggle mask, the normalized square pencil is Iₙ+UD(x)Vᵀ. By Sylvester's determinant identity its determinant is det(Iₘ+D(x)VᵀU). Off the selected rows S=supp(x) this matrix agrees with the identity, so its determinant is det(B[S]) for the single m×m matrix B=Iₘ+VᵀU. Consequently some principal submatrix is singular exactly when the graph is 3-colorable. A singular support recovers the original gains by xoring with z⁰, then the colors by spanning-tree propagation. Matrix entries and construction time are polynomial in m. A support certifies membership in NP by field elimination.

Here rank(B−I)≤n. With an Euler orientation of a connected four-regular source graph, each of the n rows of U is repeated twice in B−I, corresponding to the two edges leaving each vertex. The argument does not provide a reduction to any fixed finite field, to principal-minor equivalence, or to finding a nonsingular rank-one-pencil assignment. The input matrix is generally nonsymmetric.
