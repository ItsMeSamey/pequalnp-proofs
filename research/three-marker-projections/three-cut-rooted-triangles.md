# Physical triangle certificates in recursive three-cut decompositions

Suppose a simple 3-connected graph G separates into nonempty interiors A,B with no edges between them and an interface T of three vertices. In G[A∪T] delete all edges within T. A rooted triangle minor on the three physical roots exists exactly when |A|≥2.

Every component of A touches all three roots. Two components give a triangle by assigning one to each of the first two roots, leaving the third root alone. If A is connected, all three roots are nonarticulations. In the block-cut tree attach each root as a marked leaf to its unique block. If the median of the three leaves is a cutvertex x, each component after deleting x contains at most one root. Any such component with interior sites would separate those sites from B in G after deleting x and its sole root (if any), impossible. Thus A={x}. Otherwise the three arms meet the median block at distinct attachment vertices, taking the root itself for a zero-length arm. A bridge cannot have three such vertices. Every 2-connected block contains a rooted triangle on any three vertices: take a cycle through the first two and, when the third is outside, two internally disjoint paths from it to distinct first-arrival sites of that cycle. Partition the cycle into two consecutive arcs containing the first two roots separately and the two arrival sites separately. To see such a partition exists, place one cut on each of the two arcs between the roots; if both arrival sites lie on the same arc cut between them, and if one is in the interior of each arc assign them opposite ownership. Arrival sites at the roots already have fixed ownership. The paths without the arrival sites give the third branch. If the third root is on the cycle, split it into three arcs instead. Extend branches along their three disjoint outside arms, then flood remaining vertices. The one-interior-vertex graph is a claw. All operations are constructive.

Each piece stays 3-connected: detours of paths through discarded sites can be shortcut with interface edges even after deleting up to two vertices.
