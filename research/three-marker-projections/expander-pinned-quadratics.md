# Arbitrary bounded pins and quadratic site-color tests in four-connected expanders

Fix p, a maximum degree Δ, vertex expansion ε>0 up to n/2, and a bound k on the number of pins. On sufficiently large simple 4-connected graphs with these promises, a polynomial algorithm decides and constructs a connected spanning ordered p-color partition respecting the pins and making ANY given degree-at-most-two site-color polynomial over one effective field nonzero. In particular boundedly many charged sites of arbitrary placement and arbitrary indexed wedge Ω are handled at connectivity four: enumerate their colors and enforce the original neutrality before the quadratic test. Polynomial counts below are field operations (ordinary polynomial bit complexity for GF₂). The expander promise is not tested. Bounded small input sizes can be exhaustively handled.

Use the giant and clique facts of expander-distant-moments.md. For bounded numbers of deletions X and contractions of at most m vertices (decrease in order), separators in the minor lift to deletions of at most |X|+m additional vertices beyond the separator itself. Every unaffected connected set with more vertices than this total deletion size divided by ε, including the separator, is therefore on the original giant side. The minor after bounded deletions/contractions has arbitrarily large fixed clique minors for n sufficiently large: in the original graph delete the bounded modified sites including all preimages of nonsingleton contraction classes (≤2m); a balanced excluded-clique-minor separator in the remainder would contradict expansion. No algorithmic recognition of expansion or clique model is needed below.

Suppose x is a valid original partition on which P is nonzero. Declare some colors small and remove their ENTIRE sets B (s=|B|), pinning them permanently as in x and forbidding their colors off B. All surviving colors are large on a scale specified at the end. Their union H=G−B is connected: after deleting B all nongiant components total ≤s/ε and every component of H contains at least one entire remaining color. Add one guessed actual representative per remaining color to the original pins. Use F=B together with these representatives and all original pins as initial sites.

Introduce four successive distance buffers about F in the ORIGINAL graph, with radii ρ₀=0<ρ₁<ρ₂<ρ₃. At each radius condition P on x on all sites in the buffer (and on B), restricting the alphabet elsewhere to the surviving colors. Each polynomial remains nonzero and degree weakly decreases. Some consecutive radii i,i+1 have the same degree e≤2. Use the one-site basis consisting of 1 and all but one color indicator, designating one common baseline surviving color. A leading monomial of the outer restricted polynomial has sites U, |U|=e, outside the outer radius; it already has the same nonzero coefficient in the inner restricted polynomial. Indeed no higher-degree strict extension exists after inner conditioning. Further fixing any other sites likewise preserves that coefficient. We will simultaneously allow each u to choose its monomial's designated color or baseline, admitting a physical cube that must contain an accepting assignment. With a one-color surviving alphabet the polynomial is constant, and the argument uses U empty.

Let A be every site of the inner buffer in H, with its actual color pinned, a=|A|. We prove that H−U admits a partition into the surviving colors respecting all pins A and assigning specified physical neighbors of each u to its two requested colors. The neighbors can be chosen. To prove existence use x as follows, without assuming x accommodates all independent toggles.

In H contract short paths of the actual x-color subgraphs between pins of the same color. Keep every vertex of A as a distinguished pin of its contraction class. Starting at m=0, put L(m)=2+ceil((s+a+10+m)/ε). If two distinct same-color pin classes have distance ≤2L(m) inside their true-color connected subgraph of the current minor, contract a shortest path between them. Thus m increases by at most 2L(m), and the number of distinct pin classes drops. No contractions join different colors. There are at most a such steps. Write m_* for the bound from iterating m←m+2L(m)+1 a times and L_*=L(m_*). Every modified site lies within original distance m_*+1 of A. Require the next buffer gap >4(m_*+L_*+s+10). Each surviving color originally has more than m_*+L_* sites. In the final minor each distinguished class has a connected L(m)-site private piece of its true color within color-distance L(m). These pieces are disjoint: different colors are disjoint and same-color pins now have color-distance >2L(m). Their preimages are near A (any path through contracted classes gains at most m_* in total length).

Give each site in U two distinct neighbor contacts and private outward arms of L(m) sites. If |U|=2, a 4-unit flow from the two sources of capacity two each to four distant distinct sinks in the original 4-connected graph exists even for adjacent sources: allow no arcs entering either source; any cheaper separating cut blocks fewer than four physical vertices, at least one source and sink survive, and a connecting path after its last surviving source contradicts the cut. For |U|=1 use a two-path fan. Large n and bounded Δ give sinks at distance >L_* from all sources. The truncated arms and contacts are disjoint from A, B, all contractions and pin private pieces by the buffer gap. Their first sites get the requested two color names. In H−U after contractions the combined contact and pin roots Z have disjoint private pieces of size L(m), with |Z|≤a+4.

H−U is connected. A nongiant component after deleting B∪U has at most (s+2)/ε vertices and must neighbor U because H was connected. It cannot also touch B by the buffer gap. But its neighbors in U number at most two, contradicting original 4-connectivity. (With U empty use H directly.) Bounded contractions retain connectivity. In any separation of the resulting graph of order <|Z| with all roots on one side, one whole private piece is untouched and therefore on the giant side after lifting the separator; the lifted deletion size is at most s+2+m+|Z|. Pick a constant clique minor of order >max(3|Z|/2,L(m)+|Z|+1). If one complete branch lay wholly beyond this separation, every branch missing the separator lies together there, and their number forces that component onto the giant side also, a contradiction. The rooted partition lemma of Robertson–Seymour (Grohe et al. Lemma 4.1) supplies disjoint connected sets grouping Z by prescribed colors. Expand contractions and flood H−U from these sets. Every named A site has been assigned its actual x color, and each u can now toggle independently between its two colors. The mixed finite difference of P on this cube is the nonzero leading coefficient, including in characteristic two. Restore the small color sets B exactly.

All scales depend only on the promises. For any bound s on removed sites, |F|≤s+k+p and bounded Δ bound the size of A in each buffer. Iterate the three displayed gap choices using these worst-case sizes, obtaining a bound M(s) on every m_*+L_* and on s/ε. Start T₀=0 and choose T_{j+1}>max(T_j,M(pT_j)). Among the p+1 intervals (T_j,T_{j+1}] some interval contains no size of any of the p colors. Choose its index, remove all colors of size ≤T_j and the rest exceed every required threshold. Larger n than all the finitely many giant, distance and excluded-minor thresholds suffices.

Enumerate the small connected color sets, representatives, the inner buffer and its complete coloring, and at most two remote monomial sites and their neighbor contacts. The number is polynomial at fixed parameters. On the original H−U use rooted minor containment with edgeless target and prescribed connected groups for the A sites and contacts; this directly recovers the sets, so the x-dependent contractions need never be guessed or executed. Flood, restore B, test all toggles and verify every pin and P physically. On promised graphs exhaustion is exact NO; elsewhere the algorithm may abstain. The rare-pins corollary here covers bounded total nonzero tag support. It does not alone treat an unbounded plentiful tag direction together with a missing rare direction.
