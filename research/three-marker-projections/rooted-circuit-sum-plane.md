# Rooted circuit sums for compatible exact traces

Let the ordinary binary columns span the ambient space V of rank r. Let U be the named space, H a target of dimension k, and choose a complement W to H in U. Put N=(ordinary columns modulo W). Its bases have size r−dim W. An ordinary subset F of that size has trace H and is independent exactly when F is a basis of N and contains H in its original span. Indeed independence in N means real independence and vanishing intersection with W. Containing H then forces trace H. Conversely the dimension count makes every independent maximal-sized witness a basis of N. Every arbitrary-size successful witness extends to that size by adding ordinary columns outside its span plus W and choosing an independent basis.

Take any fixed basis h₁,…,h_k of H. For each i let R_i consist of inclusion-minimal subsets of physical column indices spanning h_i in the ORIGINAL space. Their members are independent; their union with one freshly appended named copy of h_i is a circuit. For each tuple C_i in R_i form D=union_i C_i. Discard it if D is dependent in N. The exact maximal-sized trace polynomial equals the sum, over the remaining tuples, of
                    (product over e in D of x_e) times B_(N/D)(x).
The remaining variables exclude D. Any surviving basis of N containing D physically spans H. Conversely in every desired F, independence gives a unique physical expression of each h_i in F, whose support is its unique minimal spanning subset. Thus every monomial occurs exactly once even before reduction in characteristic two; overlaps of the C_i receive only one weight. No cancellation or oracle for parity of a witness family enters this identity.

An incremental polynomial-time algorithm generates all minimal sets spanning a specified fixed column of a represented matroid (Khachiyan et al., SIAM Journal on Discrete Mathematics, https://doi.org/10.1137/S0895480103428338). It is applied with S the physical sites and A a single fresh named h_i. Alternatively enumerate rooted circuits as in the same result. Consequently for fixed k the characteristic-two evaluation is deterministic polynomial in the explicit input and the sizes of the lists R_i, since weighted binary basis polynomials in characteristic two are mixed Gram determinants by Cauchy–Binet. The field weights may be arbitrary. The parity procedure also works if only one minimal-spanning list is accessible and every residual trace evaluation is tractable: fix its C, discard dependent choices in N, and use the remaining k−1 named directions in the quotient by span C, adjusting dimensional rank and dependencies, or use the unchanged tuple formula above. For this last refinement, if other h_i enter span(C)+W one must impose their precise lifts, not just drop the equation on the basis of their dependence in N.

For deterministic four-name plane DECISION one list suffices with no assumption on the other. Choose any nonzero h in H, enumerate R_h. If span C meets U outside H discard C. If that trace is H, C itself realizes the target. Otherwise the trace is precisely ⟨h⟩. Contract C in the original matrix, keeping the image of U; it now has dimension three, with target H/⟨h⟩ a line. The exact three-name decision algorithm tests a mate and recovers it by deletion self-reduction. Lifting it together with C is exact by the ordinary quotient correspondence. In a successful F the unique h-support occurs on the list. Dovetail the three separate enumerations if desired: failure upon exhaustion of ANY one complete list is sound and success in any is sound. This gives polynomial time in the input and the smallest of the three rooted-support list sizes, without a bound on their lengths. Enumeration parameters are actual indexed minimal representations; the algorithm is not polynomial in the number of target directions alone. For weighted parity two lists suffice, and dovetailing all basis choices permits the two smallest lists. No generic complexity separation or equivalence between existence and parity is implied.
