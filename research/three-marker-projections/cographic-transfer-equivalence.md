# Charged partitions and physical basis transfer

Write a full-row-rank binary N in coordinates complementary to the two-dimensional im H and along H, as N_e=(P_e,h_e). With two lift rows Z, the four named coordinates are (h,Z) and the target plane J is graph(L). A physical X has trace J if and only if an N-basis B has transfer Z_B N_B^-1 H=L. J misses the vertical plane, so a basis of the successful original span projects independently; extend it by indexed sites to an N-basis. The lifted map on H is already fixed. Conversely the lifted physical basis is a graph, with intersection J in the four named coordinates.

When P is cographic, construct a graph G whose cut vectors are ker P on the original IDs. Glue graph components at a common vertex if necessary, preserving cut space, and discard unused isolated vertices. Give each edge gain (h_e,Z_e) and each vertex the incident gain sum with loops contributing twice. The gain of any cut is the sum of its shore's charges. Cuts supported in X are exactly those constant in potential on components of G−X; their gain image is the span of component sums. This includes all vertices.

Starting from a successful basis X=B, contract components of G−X to blocks. If m>3 blocks remain with charge span J of rank two, merge along an interblock edge preserving rank. An edge ij losing rank gives a nonzero functional whose evaluation on the old block charges equals the pair vector e_i+e_j. Only a two-dimensional row space contains such vectors, while the m−1 vectors of a spanning tree are independent. Thus a safe edge exists. At three blocks total sum zero and rank two force the three distinct nonzero vectors of J. Conversely from a connected partition with those three sums choose X to contain all crossing edges. The complementary components are exactly the three parts, and the first paragraph extends its support to a physical N-basis.

Conversely, for four-bit vertex charges totaling zero on a connected graph, solve the four incidence systems for edge gains. Split the named space as J plus a complementary W and call the two coordinate pairs h,Z. Let P be a full-row-rank cographic representation. If h has rank less than two modulo row(P), the image of all cuts cannot project onto J and the answer is NO. Otherwise N=(P;h) has full row rank, H is the two new coordinate directions, and L=0. Thus the charged partition and this cographic-private transfer subclass are polynomially witness-equivalent. For general N the private quotient P=N/im H need not be cographic; sufficiently large projective geometry after quotient by a plane still has an F7 restriction on its simplification. The canonical representation does not give a graph in those cases.
