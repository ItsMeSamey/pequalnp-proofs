# Explicit acceptance polynomials over a prime field

The ordered and cyclic coalescing-demand algorithms work over any effectively presented field: a satisfied indicator has value one, a contradicted one zero, duplicate demands coalesce, and repeated coefficients are added in that field. Gaussian elimination is used solely for spans of reachable vectors per summary. Any given polynomial of bounded degree in vertex-to-root or vertex-to-color indicators can be evaluated for *nonzero existence* on the same physical parent map, including bounded products with the pin indicators. Expansion size and coefficient arithmetic are part of the input/runtime. This includes the alternating-form query in three-part-wedge.md over F₂.

For a fixed prime l and finite group G equal to a product of cyclic groups of orders q_j=l^{a_j}, allow arbitrary site labels in G, with ordinary additive target sums in the p parts. For one component of modulus q and one color, write weights w_v and target b as integers in [0,q−1], and let X_v indicate that vertex v has that color. Over F_l take the coefficient of z^{q−1} in
 (1+z)^{q−1−b} times product_v [1+X_v((1+z)^{w_v}−1)].
On any Boolean assignment this is binomial(q−1−b+sum_v w_v X_v,q−1) modulo l. In F_l, (1+z)^q=1+z^q, so mod z^q this expression depends only on the exponent modulo q; its coefficient at q−1 is one precisely when that residue is q−1, zero otherwise. Thus it is the exact target indicator. Each selected X_v factor supplies at least one z, so degree in root demands is at most q−1. Truncate the product in z and expand the bounded-degree site-indicator coefficients to construct it.

After checking the fixed graph total, multiply this score test for p−1 colors and each cyclic component. The total degree is at most (p−1) sum_j(q_j−1), plus endpoint-to-root pins and any other requested bounded-degree tests. Substitute by the same physical transitions using F_l throughout. This yields deterministic polynomial indexed witnesses for fixed p and G on all independently established parent-chart graph classes. The argument proves same-prime groups only. Individual acceptors over incompatible characteristics cannot be multiplied within a common field by this construction.
