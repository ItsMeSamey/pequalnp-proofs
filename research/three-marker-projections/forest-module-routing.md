# Exact elimination on a forest of physically connected modules

Take explicit bounded modules, each with up to p input ports and p output ports. A setting gives a deterministic bijection of all its wires, possibly with private circuits. The settings of different modules are independent. Fixed directed wires between modules match outputs to inputs. Keep the two marked positions as passive cuts, inserting identities on their wires. The undirected support joins distinct modules sharing at least one physical wire in either direction; self-wires belong to their own module. Suppose this support is a forest. In particular multiple wires between adjacent modules need not individually reverse one another.

For a rooted subtree there is only one adjacent exterior module. Its bundle contains k incoming and k outgoing wires, with equal counts by balance summed over the subtree. Choose any fixed bijection between the two ordered boundary lists to name the outputs by the same k labels as the inputs. Temporarily return each named output to its correspondingly named input. Retain the induced first-return permutation on these k names and on all t marked cuts inside the subtree. The names let one undo this artificial closure during composition, and a cycle of only marked names records marks detached from every boundary route. Unobserved private cycles have no effect.

At a vertex enumerate its own settings and one attainable child permutation per child. Undo each child's artificial closure and splice the resulting walks and detached mark cycles into the local wires. Follow each remaining incoming or marked cut to the next named event, recording the parent response and one choice tuple yielding it. Conversely every physical global setting restricts to one such tuple; independent recorded choices can be realized simultaneously. At the top there are no boundary labels. Accept if the two named marks lie in the same cycle of an attainable permutation, and recover original switches by backtracking. Distinct connected components cannot join. With no extra local constraints, components disjoint from both marks may be dropped.

For each parent, the sum of the child bundle sizes is at most p and the sum of their mark counts is at most two. Thus at most (p+2)! tuples of child permutations occur, since the product of the individual factorials is at most the factorial of the sum. For squares and rotations p≤4 and at most 16 original local settings suffice, even with interior marks. Each tuple can be traced in constant time, yielding a linear bound in module count for these fixed modules. This algorithm requires the forest property of actual wire adjacency; a tree of named source constraints with other cross wires has a different interface.

**A source layout obstruction.** For a strict monotone (2,2) formula with n variables each occurring exactly twice per sign, there are 4n/3 clauses and 4n source incidences. Its incidence graph has cycle rank 5n/3 plus its number of components. A minor-preserving physical realization of this graph cannot be a forest. For unbounded width, choose the k by k periodic square grid with k≥3, replace each grid vertex v by L_v,R_v, and include undirected edges R_v L_v, R_v L_(v+east), R_v L_(v+north). The result is loopless simple cubic bipartite. Each edge is a Boolean variable; at each vertex impose both a positive and a negative three-literal clause on its three edges. This is exact monotone (2,2) and assigning precisely all self pairs R_v L_v true satisfies every clause. Keeping only the positive clauses gives the graph obtained by subdividing every cubic edge. Contract subdivisions and then the self pairs to obtain the toroidal grid as a minor. Its ordinary square grid subgraph has treewidth at least k−1, e.g. by its connected row-plus-column bramble with hitting number k. Hence literal-faithful incidence-minor layouts have unbounded treewidth on this YES family despite elementary solutions. Disjoint union with a fixed strict monotone (2,2) unsatisfiable formula preserves this width while producing NO instances. No claim applies to arbitrary decision-preserving encodings which do not retain incidence as a minor.
