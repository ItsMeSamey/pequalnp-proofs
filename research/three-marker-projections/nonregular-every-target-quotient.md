# A missing plane even when all three target contractions remain highly connected and nonregular

Fix k≥5. Use a simple graph of girth ≥k+20 and vertex connectivity ≥k+130; explicit finite existence is proved in high-connectivity-missing-plane.md. Choose seven terminals as the center and six leaves of a star, then delete these six spokes. The resulting graph G remains at least (k+124)-vertex-connected, has girth ≥k+20, and all seven terminal pairs are at distance ≥k+18 in G. Let D be its binary incidence matrix. Choose four of the terminals A={a,b,c,d} and denote the other three by u,v,w. Let p=A and q={a,u,v,w} be binary even vertex vectors; use indicator notation. Let U=Even(A)+span(q), H=span(p,q). Take the four columns T to be a basis of U in which p,q are last, and let M=[D|T]. For example take ab,ac,p,q. In particular the two parts of the construction share the terminal a: U contains no nonzero vector supported on {u,v,w}, since every vector using q has odd parity on A.

The instance to be tested is a binary representation of the entire DUAL M*, obtainable by a basis of the nullspace of [D|T]; keep exactly the same four indexed names. Its target is H* = span(1000,0100) in dual name coordinates, the annihilator of the primal target coefficient space. It is absent. Indeed, for any graph edge subset whose span contains p, its component partition places the four vertices of A in blocks of even sizes, so some A pair belongs to that same edge span. This pair is in U but not H. Thus H is absent in the primal. For any set of contracted ordinary edges X, deleting the others gives a minor on the four named elements whose kernel is the exact named trace in coefficient coordinates. The dual minor uses complementary ordinary contractions and its kernel is the orthogonal complement of the first kernel. This proves absence in M* without combining separate witnesses.

The augmented primal and dual matroids are k-connected in the Tutte sense. The spanning graph matroid is (k+10)-connected by the vertex-connectivity and girth bounds. Adding any independent subset of U as new names gives no short circuit: a nontrivial combination has nonzero even boundary on the seven distant terminals, and every physical representation uses ≥k+18 ordinary graph edges. For partitions where both ordinary sides have ≥k−1 edges, λ cannot fall on adding these spanned names. Otherwise the smaller ordinary side has at most k−2 graph edges, and including at most four names leaves it independent by the girth bound; its complement spans. Apply duality. This argument also works for any independently rebased list of at most four names from U.

For any subspace K⊆U, the ordinary projected matroid D/K is k-connected. Its λ for any fixed partition falls by at most dim K≤4: each side rank drops by at most dim K and the total rank drops by exactly dim K. If both sides have ≥k+3 edges, the original λ is at least k+3. Otherwise a shore with at most k+2 edges is independent in the projection (no short ordinary cycle, and no nonzero boundary in K can occur with this few edges); its complement spans. This proves the connectivity of each D/K and its dual, with loops and parallel pairs already excluded by k≥5.

The dual ordinary restriction is (D/U)*. For each of the three nonzero dual target directions s, projecting dual ordinary columns by its virtual named vector gives exactly (D/K_s)*, where K_s consists of named primal combinations annihilated by s. To check this, replace T by T B for an invertible 4-by-4 B whose last three columns span ker s and whose first column pairs to one with s. The corresponding dual representation fixes the ordinary column representation up to row basis and sends the list of named columns to T* B^(−transpose). Its first named column is now the desired original combination s. Contract that first dual name and delete the other three: dually this deletes the first primal name and contracts the last three, yielding D/K_s. If the three other independent residual names are retained, the rebased augmented dual quotient is the dual of the graph representation with those three primal names appended, hence likewise k-connected by the previous argument. For s=e₁+e₂ the images of e₁,e₂ coincide; dropping one and retaining e₃,e₄ yields a basis of residual names. For s=e₁ or e₂ simply drop the vanishing name. These exact residual bases may be chosen as the other three dual columns when rebasing.

For K=U and each K_s above, one has q∈H⊆K and K=span(q)⊕K_A with K_A⊆Even(A). Choose a fresh graph vertex r outside the seven terminals. Route a rooted K₅ subdivision on a,u,v,w,r in G avoiding b,c,d. As in high-connectivity-nonregular-plane.md, there are ten paths with distinct external endpoint proxies, routed after deleting the five roots and the forbidden vertices using the Thomas–Wollan 10-linkage bound. In the coordinate space supported on this subdivision, K intersects precisely in span(q): after subtracting any q term, a vector of K_A could have at most a single coordinate a and must be even. Consequently the restriction survives projection modulo K exactly as it does modulo q. Contract the subdivided K₅ to K₅, preserving the four vertices supporting q; contracting q gives a rank-three matroid whose simplification is F₇ (four coordinate units and three complementary pairs modulo 1111). These independent incidence contractions commute with quotienting by q, or equivalently with taking the indicated named extension minor. Thus D/K contains F₇ and each (D/K)* contains F₇*. In particular the augmented test instance, its ordinary restriction, and EACH ordinary target-vector quotient are nonregular, with arbitrarily high Tutte connectivity. For the original augmented instance named linkage to every ordinary set of size at least four is four as before. Every nonzero dual named combination requires ≥k−4 ordinary columns by the girth of M* and independence of its names (ordinary restriction spans M*, since the primal names were independent).

This is an obstruction to an all-planes richness theorem, not a hardness result. There is no low-order separation attaching a disconnected Fano summand. The dual construction instead embeds an explicit seven-terminal algebraic obstruction in a single highly connected host. Primary sources for graph connectivity and linkage are in high-connectivity-missing-plane.md and high-connectivity-star-spectrum.md.
