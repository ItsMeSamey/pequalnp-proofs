# One hard family for bounded patches and cached flag projections

Fix h, B=3·2^h. Use the high-girth NAE core before its clone blowup in latin-patch-hard-fixed-points.md. Let N be the number of vertices plus edges in that core. Take a deterministically constructed constant-degree edge expander with n=Theta(N) vertices, with fixed expansion for all sets of size at most n/2. Explicit constant-degree expanders are available from Reingold, Vadhan and Wigderson (https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0406038); alternatively generate the square-order Margulis–Gabber–Galil family, rounding N up to the next square. Loops can be removed and parallel edges merged: edge expansion loses at most the original constant maximum degree factor. Subdivide every edge B+1 times to form G. For every edge uv of G add a distinct private parent p_e and the hyperedge NAE(x_u,x_v,p_e); identify one x vertex with one hard core vertex. No other vertex is shared between the sections. The graph of NAE hyperedges on the attachment has Berge girth greater than B, and the one shared vertex creates no cycle crossing between the two sections. Perform the same full clone blowup and isolated Latin embedding. The proof excluding every nontrivial h-patch on both verdicts therefore applies verbatim. For fixed h the number of source variables, clauses, and live sites is Theta(n), before the polynomial Latin padding.

For any 2-colouring of the old hard core and ANY assignment to all vertices x of G agreeing at the shared vertex, assign each p_e to avoid a monochromatic triple. Give all clones their parent values. This is a genuine globally feasible assignment of the combined Latin instance. Conversely fixing the first clone of each parent always recovers a valid old core colouring. If the hard core has no colouring the new instance has none.

Consider any rooted tree of arity at most four assigning each actual ternary clause to a distinct leaf, in particular any native cached coordinate flag after any physical address relabeling which retains distinct native clauses at distinct leaves. The tree can have additional leaves. For every edge e select only its positive clause on first clones of its two graph endpoints and its p_e. At each node let S consist of graph vertices whose selected edge leaves occur both inside and outside the node. Its actual table boundary includes their first clone names. If |S|<=b everywhere, put the union of the at most four child S sets at each internal node; at each selected leaf put its two graph endpoints and at other leaves the empty bag. This gives a tree decomposition of G with maximum bag size max(2,4b): every edge is covered, and for each vertex its bags are exactly the spanning subtree of its incident leaves (or just the unique incident leaf for degree one). G contains the original expander as a minor by contracting its subdivided edges.

For completeness any width-w tree decomposition of the expander yields a separator of at most w+1 vertices with every remaining component of size at most n/2: assign each vertex to one bag containing it and take a weighted centroid of the tree. Since the components outside this bag have edge expansion at least c times their sizes, their combined edge boundary is at least c(n-w-1) and all these edges meet the separator, whose degree is <=d(w+1). Thus w+1 >= cn/(c+d). Minor monotonicity follows by replacing both endpoints of a contracted edge in all bags by their single new name (their subtrees meet). Some cell has |S|=Omega(n).

On every satisfiable image these first-clone names alone have at least 2^(|S|-1) distinct projections of complete feasible assignments. Any 2CNF background valid on all models preserves every such projection. Consequently the explicit projected-key cache cannot have polynomially many globally realizable keys at all nodes on that family, regardless of address charts, flags, or pair schedule. On unsatisfiable images the number of globally feasible keys is necessarily zero. The actual explicit background after signed-star substitution is empty, so pruning with that background alone retains all binary patterns on either verdict. This is not a conclusion for arbitrary affine changes of the Boolean source variables or decision-only solvers.

The separated attachment IS removable for existential decision by cutting its shared vertex and using the extension property. In fact fixing all other x parents to zero and all p_e to one extends every possible bit pattern even on clones of the shared vertex. Thus preprocessing permitted to discard this whole section escapes the large-cache construction; even when original names are protected, compressed symbolic fibers can encode their possibilities. The joint obstructions apply to the two specified mechanisms on the exhibited images, not to recursive preprocessing in general.
