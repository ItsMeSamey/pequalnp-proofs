# Full four-name plane catalogue with a highly connected spanning incidence matrix

Under the hypotheses of five-terminal-dense-column.md, all 35 planes of the four-dimensional even space on P can be classified by a scan of all physical columns in the original vertex coordinates. First, 25 planes are always attained: each partition of five vertices into three blocks is present via the two routed connecting pairs from high-connectivity-star-spectrum.md. Different partitions have different pair vectors. There are ten partitions of type 3+1+1 and fifteen of type 2+2+1.

For any pair t={a,b} of P let H_t=span(P minus {a},P minus {b}). It has precisely one nonzero pair vector t, so is distinct both from the 25 pair-generated planes and from the nine H indexed by different pairs. These exhaust the remaining planes since the binary four-space has 35 planes. The dense-column criterion now applies to each relabelling.

If any extra physical column has weight at least six, after merging any two terminals its weight falls by at most two. Therefore all 35 planes occur. Otherwise ignore columns of weight zero or two, and let J be the subset of P common to the supports of every weight-four column, taking J=P if there are none. Merging t drops such a column from weight four to two if and only if it contains t. Thus exactly the planes H_t with t⊆J are absent. The number attained is 35 minus binom(|J|,2), one of 25,29,32,34,35. The constructive linkage proofs in the companions return an indexed physical witness for any attained plane. The supplied named basis can be any basis of the even space on these five sites, by ordinary Gaussian change of coordinates.
