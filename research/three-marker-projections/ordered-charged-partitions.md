# Deterministic charged connected partitions from a vertex order

Let G be a finite connected undirected graph, its vertices labeled by vectors in F₂^d. Prescribe p ordered nonempty connected spanning parts and their charge sums. Reject if the prescribed total does not equal the graph total. The following deterministic polynomial-time witness algorithm for each fixed d,p applies to ALL chordal graphs, independent of leafage or adhesion size.

## Acyclic choices and coalescing demands

First fix any vertex order, distinct physical roots r_1,...,r_t, and an assignment of one of the p colors to each root. A root is forced to stop; each nonroot v must choose one actual neighboring vertex w later in the order as its parent. Reject this root tuple if a nonroot has no possible parent. A parent map ends at roots and determines colors of all vertices. Forests of a single given root are connected. The following procedure tests score conditions on these rooted forests, together with a bounded list of constraints specifying the actual root reached by particular sites.

Express acceptance of the first p−1 prescribed charge sums by multiplying d(p−1) affine bit tests over F₂. A bit test for color i has form 1+target_bit+sum_v q_bit(v) [v ends at a root of color i]. The omitted color's total then follows. Replace a color indicator by the sum of the root indicators of that color. Multiply any required individual physical site-to-root pins as further indicator factors. The expanded polynomial has at most (1+nt)^{k} terms before consolidation, where k=d(p−1) plus the number of pins. Terms are conjunctions of at most k demands (v,r). Duplicate equal demands coalesce and conflicting demands on one vertex make the term zero. Keep coefficients modulo two. The empty demand list represents one.

A physical parent decision at site v induces a linear map on the vector space with basis the consistent demand lists of cardinality at most k. Process all vertices in increasing order. For a root discard terms demanding another root at that site and remove its satisfied demand. For a nonroot with chosen parent w, move its demand if any from v to w, merging equal demands and killing contradictory ones. Terms with no demand at v pass through. This substitution is correct for every future completion since v and w will end at the same root. After all sites only the empty-state coordinate remains: for a fixed sequence of parent choices its value is exactly the acceptance bit of that sequence. In particular no cancellation occurs between different physical parent maps.

The state dimension is at most (1+nt)^{O(k)}. Initialize with the one polynomial coefficient vector, or the zero space if it vanishes. At each physical site, apply every allowed linear map to every current retained basis vector, selecting a linearly independent subset of the resulting actual vectors by Gaussian elimination (store selected input vectors, not their reduced row sums). The resulting basis spans all vectors reachable by physical choices on the processed prefix, by linearity and induction. Store backpointers to the chosen previous physical vector and operation. A nonzero empty coordinate at the final layer occurs on an actual retained vector if it occurs on any reachable vector. Recover the complete parent map by traceback and verify its indexed colors directly. This step searches for acceptance even if many accepted forests have even parity; it never sums over parent choices. Time and space are polynomial in n for fixed k,t. Each parent operation is common to every coefficient in its whole vector.

## One sink on chordal graphs

Use a perfect elimination ordering, in which the later neighbors of each vertex form a clique. On any connected induced subgraph, removing its smallest vertex preserves connectivity of the rest by shortcutting between its remaining neighbors. Hence all nonmaximum vertices in the subgraph have an edge to a later vertex of the same subgraph (apply this removal repeatedly). For any genuine connected partition each color can therefore choose increasing parent edges to its unique maximum. Enumerate one distinct physical root per color and run the acyclic-choice test. Conversely the resulting trees give the required nonempty connected spanning parts. A perfect elimination order can be found and verified in polynomial time. One-site and zero-charge parts work unchanged.

## A recognized broader promise on a supplied order

Say its sink bound is r if every nonempty connected induced vertex subset has at most r vertices with no later neighbor inside the subset. Test a proposed bound by enumerating r+1 sites: forbid every later neighbor of every listed site; if no listed site is forbidden and they are in one component of the remaining graph, that component certifies failure. Conversely the sinks of a violating connected set provide such a tuple.

Given this bound, a true partition admits parent edges within each color except at its local sinks. There are at most t≤pr roots. Choose such tuples and colors, each color represented. Within each color contract its individual rooted trees: connectedness supplies a spanning tree of this quotient. Guess the at most t−p actual edges connecting those trees, including their endpoint-to-specific-root assignments, enforcing them as at most 2(t−p) additional demand factors in the same acyclic-choice test. Require the guessed links connect all roots of each color and only link roots of the same color. A recovered map yields a connected spanning partition by these actual links. Every true partition is found from its sink roots and a physical connecting tree per color. This gives deterministic n^{O(dp+pr)} time with a constant-factor exponent from linear algebra, for fixed d,p,r. The order can always be tested for this property; no method for finding an optimal arbitrary order is assumed.
