# A native two-root test excluding two ternary words

Given two specified length-n ternary words, there is a linear-size open sequence of fixed permutations and one full rotation per differing coordinate when their Hamming distance d is at least two. It preserves a specified subset X of its rails precisely on those two restricted words. On closing with polynomial-size native auxiliary routers, the two-root predicate accepts exactly the complement of the two words. Every input is used at one site. The existential auxiliaries select physical routing at closure; this is a complement predicate, not an automatic positive two-word test.

Local affine permutations of each input trit (offset and choice of orientation) put the differing positions into the form 0ᵈ,1ᵈ. For the construction below call a rail marked as a member of X a rail carrying a one; the other rails carry zeros. The open map preserves X exactly when all bits return to their initial rails. This explanation tracks a fixed subset of rails, not new choices of input bits.

For d=2 take the rotations (0 1 2)^a followed by (1 0 3)^b, with X={0}. The initial one either stays at 0 or moves to 1 or the private rail 2. It reaches 0 at the end precisely at 00 and 11. For d=3 start with ones on rails 0 and 3, and apply (0 1 2)^a,(0 4 3)^b,(1 3 5)^c. Direct enumeration of the 27 choices gives preservation only at 000 and 111. Equivalently, a=2 leaves a one on private rail 2. At a=0 the occupied pair is {0,3} and the only restoring choices are b=c=0; at a=1 the pair is {1,3}, and b=1 moves 3 to 0 before c=1 moves 1 to 3.

An odd-length path gadget permits replacement of the first rotation in either seed by k=1,3,5,… independent trits. Its endpoints are the old rails 0,1. The initial bits along the path alternate, starting at one on rail 0 and ending at zero on rail 1. Each path edge has a private zero rail. For edges with odd index, starting at index one, first apply their disjoint trit rotations. Orient each so choice 1 moves the one across the edge while leaving its private zero fixed. Choice 2 moves that one to the private rail, irreversibly spoiling the test. Thus a clean odd edge either stays or flips. Then process all even-index edges; their endpoints are internal to the path. If the two neighboring first-layer flags differ, the two endpoints now contain zero or two ones, and no rotation on them and their zero private rail can restore the initial endpoint values and private zero simultaneously. If the neighbors' flags agree at zero, choice 0 is uniquely restoring. If they agree at one, the endpoint values have exchanged, and orient the even-edge cycle so 1 uniquely restores the original values. Therefore restoration of all internal and private bits requires all k trits equal 0 or all equal 1. The old endpoints respectively retain or exchange their initial bits. No later operation touches the added rails. This is exactly the needed action of the old first rotation on its successful settings; its former private rail 2 remains unchanged. Starting from the d=2 seed covers even d, and from d=3 covers odd d.

For a coordinate common to both words, take a disjoint triple with one initial one and apply its rotation after subtracting the common value. That triple preserves its bit subset iff the coordinate matches. Take the union of all the prescribed subsets. Its preservation now means exactly that the full input belongs to the specified two-word family.

For closure use a freely programmable permutation hub on X and a separate such hub on its complement. Independently optional two-track swaps in a fixed sorting schedule can realize every permutation at a hub. Each swap uses one native optional three-cycle with its third socket feeding itself. Feed the open register outputs back to their hub inputs, and add a fixed marked loop at each hub (including its ports in the permutation). For a fixed word, if the open permutation preserves X then the two hubs lie in different weak components, for all settings. Otherwise some arc crosses the partition, and the balanced two-vertex graph has an Euler circuit containing both marked loops. Its transitions are realized by one common physical hub setting. Only the separating verdict is universal here; existence of a join gives the requested complement.

For distance one, the differing coordinate has one excluded-from-the-pair symbol: a three-track register closed by a transposition can recognize that singleton. Combine this singleton detector with the independent common-coordinate mismatch detectors by an OR: cut each two-root detector at its two marked arcs to get a two-port permutation that swaps iff they joined. Plug every module between the same pair of free hubs as above, one port at each. The marked hub loops can join precisely when at least one module swaps. A concrete singleton detector on trit t has fixed feedback (0 1), input rotation (0 1 2)^t and roots 0,1. The three feedback permutations are three distinct transpositions, and only the one at t=0 joins that pair. Offsetting t gives any desired singleton. Distance zero reduces to the OR of the independent singleton-coordinate mismatches.
