# Local rank and full-menu replacement in the paired-row cofactor algorithm

Work over the rationals. Delete row s and column r from 2I-P for the chosen routing permutation. In each site not containing row s replace the third row by its sum with the first and second. This determinant-preserving operation makes the third row independent of the selected rotation. The n-1 common rows are independent: a dependency gives weights y_j constant on each complete site and zero at the marked site, satisfying 2 y_j=y_pred(j) at every surviving column j≠r. A nonzero maximum absolute value appears at all three sockets of its site, so choose one different from r to get a contradiction. The quotient dimension is exactly 2n. Reordering common rows and passing to a quotient basis changes all determinants by the same nonzero scalar. The signed cofactor of 2I-P is nonnegative by the walk expansion and is positive precisely when the marks join.

For cyclically indexed surviving row choices write u,v for the base rows, and d=f0−f1,e=f1−f2 for cyclic differences of the three projected destination units. The ordered row pairs are (u,v), (u+d,v+e), (u+d+e,v−d). If the marked site's surviving rows occur in reverse cyclic sequence, cyclically number or reverse socket names and reverse the rotations' indexing; alternating every pair changes only the same fixed sign. The three bivectors are supported on span(u,v,d,e). The wedge of any two distinct ones equals u∧v∧d∧e, because all three determinants of the corresponding difference pairs relative to d,e equal +1. Thus a menu sum over at least two distinct options has rank four exactly when the four vectors are independent. Otherwise its rank is zero or two. A test on a repeated menu entry should count its multiplicity; positive multiplicities give the same rank-four criterion.

Already for two sites, feedback [3,4,5,0,1,2], r=0, s=3, the first site's four vectors span e1,e3,e4,e5 after deleting column zero. The common row has nonzero e2, so rank four survives the quotient. More generally with n sites on a cycle and feedback (g,i)→(g+1,i), any site g for which neither g nor g+1 contains r has four independent vectors after contraction. Project separately to the two-dimensional quotients by the coordinate sums at those two sites, sending every other coordinate to zero. All common rows and e_r disappear, the destination differences span the second quotient, and the two source units span the first. This yields at least n-2 rank-four sites for two-choice menus even on connected wiring.

## Freely expanding a full ternary menu

Fix all other gates, cut one full ternary site and trace the exterior. Marks on detached components give a constant verdict, and marks on the same passage are necessarily joined. For marks on distinct exterior passages, the induced return on the three cut labels ranges over one coset of A3 as the site takes its three cyclic choices. In either coset some member joins any specified two labels: use a three-cycle in the even coset, or their transposition in the odd one. Hence a full ternary site and an arbitrary S3-permutation site are equivalent for existential two-root joining against every fixed exterior. Passive subdivisions handle marks on original site seams. Expand full menus one at a time; any expanded successful witness can likewise be restored to cyclic choices one gate at a time with all other choices fixed.

The common row sum survives this expansion. For the selected pair of source units e_i,e_j and mean destination M=(f0+f1+f2)/3, the six-option bivector sum is exactly 6(2e_i−M)∧(2e_j−M). The pure destination terms cancel pairwise by order reversal, and the remaining formula follows by bilinearity. Thus full menus always admit a zero or simple aggregate, irrespective of the original three-option rank. Genuine two-option rank-four menus are the only branching sites required on this variant.

For any simple sum C with nonzero uv entry, the pair a_i=C_iv/C_uv, b_j=C_uj realizes C; this can be checked at all entries. The upper cofactor criterion is unchanged by a common ordering scalar. Each branch determinant after aggregating equals the sum of corresponding physical determinants up to this fixed scalar, so actual successful assignments exist precisely at nonzero branch totals. Sequentially replace simple pairs by actual choices preserving a nonzero determinant. Rational projection and final elimination require only polynomial bit complexity per branch: projected coefficients are ratios of common-row minors, synthetic pair construction uses such coefficients and a pivot, and all matrices have polynomial dimensions and polynomial-bit entries.
