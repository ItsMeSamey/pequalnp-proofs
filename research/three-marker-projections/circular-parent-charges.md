# Orders with two sinks on circular-arc graphs

In a circular-arc model select a point avoiding all arc endpoints. Put all arcs missing that point first, by increasing right endpoint after cutting the circle at the point. Put the covering arcs last in arbitrary order; they form a clique. The first group is an interval graph and this order is a PEO there. Ties are arbitrary. One may discretize the model retaining cyclic endpoint order and additional intervening representatives so physical intersections are preserved.

For a nonempty connected induced vertex subset U avoiding the last clique there is just one sink. Otherwise let M be the union of selected covering arcs. On the cut line it occupies a prefix and suffix, possibly all sites. All selected covering arcs except their last have a later neighbor. Consider any component J of selected noncovering intervals. Its interval union is contiguous, its distinct component unions are disjoint, and it touches M because U is connected. Only its maximum-right-end interval could be a sink by the PEO. If that interval touches M it has a later covering neighbor. Otherwise its right endpoint lies in the gap outside M. Thus no interval of J reaches the right-hand suffix, yet J touches the left-hand prefix and also goes outside it. Its contiguous union therefore crosses the unique right boundary of the left prefix. Two disjoint component unions cannot do this. There is at most one noncovering sink, hence at most two sinks in U. McConnell's certifying algorithm constructs a circular-arc model in polynomial time: https://www.cs.colostate.edu/~rmm/circArcMcConnell.pdf . This yields the exact indexed charged partition and bounded-degree membership polynomial algorithms by ordered-charged-partitions.md. Circular-arc graphs here are intersection graphs of arcs, not circle graphs from chord crossings.

More generally, suppose a model as an intersection graph of connected vertex sets of a subdivision of a fixed graph H is supplied. Take X to comprise all modeled vertices whose sets contain some original vertex of H and put them last. X is covered by |V(H)| cliques; any set of sinks among X is independent, so there are at most this many. Each other model is a single interval on the open subdivided path of one original edge; use increasing right endpoints on each, choosing arbitrary directions and any order of the original edges. Within a connected U meeting X each non-X component has its model union on one edge and touches the union M of selected X models. As each X model is connected and contains an original vertex, its occupied sites on each open subdivided edge extend from at least one endpoint (each interval component must connect through a path endpoint). Thus on each edge M occupies at most a prefix and a suffix. The preceding right-end argument shows at most one non-X sink per edge. If U misses X it consists of one interval component. For nonempty connected H the bound is |V(H)|+|E(H)|, allowing arbitrary physical charges. This argument uses the supplied H representation; graph-only recognition for general H is not required. Existing H-graph width results and model terminology: https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.09737 .
