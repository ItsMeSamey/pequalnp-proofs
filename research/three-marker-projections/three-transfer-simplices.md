# Three connected components with four-dimensional transfer span

Use connected-basis-transfer.md to compute a five-point affine basis S_i of physically witnessed transfers in each of three hard components. Fix any first basis, query annihilators of known anchored differences, and when an exchange has nonzero pairing take its endpoint escaping the old anchored span. If fewer than three hard components remain this particular result need not apply.

For three subsets of the 16-element transfer group each affinely spanning, failure of the sum to cover all sixteen points forces all three sets to be translates of exactly the same five-point affine simplex. Indeed an affine change normalizes any chosen five-point affine basis of the first set to 0,1,2,4,8. Enumerating the 2688 affine bases of size five shows their sum with that normalized simplex always has at least eleven points, with equality only for its sixteen translations. If any of the three sets has a sixth point the other two have sum of size at least eleven and the pigeonhole principle gives every target. If the three are translated simplices, their sum has size fifteen (normalized, the vectors of Hamming weight at most three). Nonhard contributions can subsequently be added via their exact tables.

To test for a sixth actual point, query every fiber T h=v and d T=v for nonzero h or d, and every set det(T+L)=1 disjoint from S_i. The fiber queries are exact three-name line/plane problems: a single projected input with a requested two-bit lift, or the projection to a single output bit and the graph of a map on two inputs. For each invertibility test form an extension of the original lifted ordinary space by quotienting out the graph of L over the two target inputs (use their actual embeddings as the two columns of H); this quotient has dimension rank N. A physical N-basis is also independent in this quotient exactly when the two graphs are transverse on the two inputs. Ordinary linear matroid intersection enforces both constraints on the same basis. Each positive query recovers a sixth point, proving full sum and allowing all witnesses by finite enumeration of the known local choices. If all such queries fail, all but at most one of the eleven possible extra points are excluded; for 1872 of the 2688 simplices every extra point is excluded and for the other 816 precisely one is untested. These claims use the physical binary 2-by-2 coordinate coding, not arbitrary changes of the four transfer coordinates. See three-transfer-simplices.py for exhaustive verification. If the three individual simplices are translated copies and lie in the fully covered types, this is an exact decision and witness algorithm for their sum. Otherwise if all queries fail and one untested point is possible, only the single missing sum remains undecided.

A physical attainable five-point example uses connected rank-two projection with nonzero column directions 1,2,3. For direction 1 take offsets 1,2,3; for direction 2 take offset 3; for direction 3 take offset 2. With H=I, encode a transfer as first output plus four times second. Enumerating ordinary pairs of distinct directions gives exactly 2,7,13,14,15. Three independent copies with diagonally embedded inputs miss one prescribed transfer.
