# P9-free private matroids reduce to regular private matroids

Ding and Wu, Theorems 1.2 and 1.3 in https://arxiv.org/pdf/1410.0954 , classify the 3-connected binary P9-free matroids and their direct/2-sum closure. Besides regular matroids the 3-connected pieces are minors of Y16, the named binary spike variants, and starfishes. All these nonregular pieces have bounded branchwidth independently of their size.

For the spike Z_r write e_i and e_i+t on each leg, where t=sum_i e_i is the tip. A side consisting of complete legs is spanned by the coordinate units on those legs and t (possibly fewer), as is its complement using the complementary units and t. Their intersection has dimension at most two: both spans have excess dimension at most one over disjoint spaces of total dimension r. Separate the tip as its own leaf and use a branch tree grouping complete legs before subdividing each pair. Deletion and duality do not increase branchwidth, so this covers the listed variants. The remaining finite Y16 cases have size at most sixteen.

A starfish starts with M*(G), where G=K3,n plus at most three links among the three hub vertices, and attaches disjoint Fano copies by triangle sums at triangles of the center. For n≥4 a three-edge cut cannot split the three hubs: each of the n nonhubs contributes a crossing edge if some hubs lie on either side. If the hubs stay together, a three-edge cut must isolate one nonhub. Hence each Fano copy replaces one nonhub star by four physical sites, the Fano points off its interface line W. In the ordinary vector-space amalgam their span is exactly W plus a new private dimension exclusive to that Fano copy. Thus connectivity across any partition into entire replaced/unreplaced nonhub bundles and any hub-edge singletons is unchanged: the fresh basis dimensions occur on one side only. For G's graphic matroid, the only vertices possibly shared by such a cut are the three hubs, bounding the span intersection by three; duality preserves this connectivity value. Give each bundle a branch subtree with its at most four individual leaves and take any binary tree on bundles outside. Every internal micro-cut has one side of size at most four. This bounds the resulting ordinary binary branchwidth independently of n. For n≤3 the entire center and the number of disjoint attachments are bounded.

To solve the supported-image problem on the nonregular pieces, use fixed-width binary branch-decomposition recognition and construction (Hliněný–Oum https://doi.org/10.1137/070685920) and the standard indexed interface DP with the fixed named score space; stacking or adjoining fixed-dimensional names changes every cut rank by at most a fixed constant. The existence proof supplies a uniform width threshold, so no starfish/spike isomorphism recognition is needed. Assemble exact gain images on the canonical proper unary sum tree as in regular-excluded-private.md; when its local code is regular use the assumed regular oracle. Minors formed for unary constraints stay regular or bounded-width as appropriate. Every regular matroid is P9-free since P9 is nonregular. Thus the two oracle families over fixed gain dimensions are polynomial-time Turing equivalent, with actual physical supports recoverable by DP or site-deletion self-reduction. If the P9-free input ALSO excludes M*(K3,3), every regular torso has that exclusion as a minor of the input, and regular-excluded-private.md removes the oracle assumption. There is no connectivity or globally bounded-width assumption in this last result.
