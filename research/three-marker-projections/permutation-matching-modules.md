# Root joining for matching modules under arbitrary fixed permutation feedback

Let R be any fixed permutation of the exposed sockets. Independent modules have disjoint socket sets. Each realizes exactly the permutations which exchange endpoints of an arbitrary matching in its given undirected socket graph and fix unmatched sockets. A physical decoder for each matching is supplied. The two marks lie on external wires, each identified with its socket on the same side of the return seam. Let U,V be their cycles in the baseline R. If U=V, empty local matchings suffice.

For distinct U,V, a joining setting exists precisely when a matching of allowed socket pairs connects U and V after contracting the cycles of R. Necessity follows because every orbit of the composed permutation stays inside a component of the graph with R-edges and the local matching. Conversely extract a simple U–V path in the contracted graph and retain only its selected local edges. These edges are still a matching. Multiplying R by the transpositions along a forest merges the complete baseline cycles in each component. Thus the returned physical mask joins the original marks, regardless of the cyclic orders of their other sockets.

To find such a path, split every socket s into vertices L_s and T_s joined by a default matching edge. Add L_s–L_t for every allowed local pair. Add T_s–T_t for any two distinct sockets in one cycle of R. Add two exposed vertices a,b adjacent respectively to all T_s in U and in V. Find a perfect matching. Its symmetric difference with the default matching has an augmenting a–b path. Traversing the path alternates between local L-pairs and same-cycle T-pairs, separated by default edges. Each visited socket contributes its entire default edge exactly once, so the L-pairs are a matching in the original socket graph. They connect U,V after contracting R. Conversely any matching path in the quotient gives such an augmenting path by using its local edges on the L side and switching between its distinct arrival and departure sockets on the T side. Extract a simple quotient path to obtain the promised joining physical witness.

This is a deterministic polynomial algorithm in the explicit socket-graph size plus local decoding times. It applies to arbitrary fixed clause wiring around the native four-grant square and does not require bounded width. Interior marks, globally coupled matchings, and local actions other than the specified matching family remain outside the proof.
