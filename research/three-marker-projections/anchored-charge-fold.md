# Clique contraction retaining charged interior sites

Let C be connected and proper, W its exterior neighborhood, U the span of its charges, V the span outside, and H the target plane. Let L be a linear endomorphism of U fixing D=U intersect (H+V) pointwise. Put R={r in C: L(q(r)) is nonzero}. On C union W take a supplied rooted clique model with disjoint connected bags covering all sites, one vertex from P=W union R per bag. Require the bag total at each r in R to equal L(q(r)), and the bag total at each w in W to equal its original q(w).

Contracting all bags to their indexed roots preserves the existence of the three connected spanning parts with sums the nonzero points of H. Indeed in any genuine solution each part's C charge belongs to D, hence equals the sum of L(q(r)) on its R vertices. Each part has a retained vertex since its target is nonzero. Excursions of its paths through discarded sites can be shortcutted between clique vertices, since W contains all external contacts. Thus its exterior and retained sites stay connected, even if the original part was entirely inside C. The stipulated bag totals yield exactly the required reduced sums. Conversely color each bag monochromatically by its root; this preserves all sums and connectivity.

With at most k charged sites in C choose L the identity. Any clique model rooted at W and these sites has the required totals once completed: attach leftover neutral vertices to adjacent bags until all are assigned. Thus for bounded |W|+k the fixed rooted graph minor problem and model recovery suffice to recognize this physical fold on a supplied C. With dense residual charges, an actual model satisfying its original-charge identities is an additional hypothesis.
