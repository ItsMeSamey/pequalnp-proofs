# Sharp five-terminal criterion for arbitrary extra even columns

Let the ordinary binary matrix contain the incidence columns of a spanning 80-vertex-connected simple graph on V and any additional even vertex vectors. Let U be the entire even space on P={a,b,c,d,e}. Take t={a,b}, and H spanned by P minus {a} and P minus {b}. A physical subset has intersection H with U if and only if some ordinary column has weight at least four AFTER identifying a and b, i.e. projecting modulo t. No restrictions on supports, counts or overlaps of the extra columns are required. The vertex coordinates and the highly connected spanning graph are supplied.

If all projected ordinary columns have weight zero or two, every subset in the quotient is graphic in these coordinates. The quotient U/t is the even space on four sites, H/t its weight-four line. Every graphic trace is generated by pairs, so any solution containing t is impossible.

Otherwise take a single extra column z of projected weight at least four. Seek four vertex parts with connected induced subgraphs and P-partition ab,c,d,e. It suffices to arrange that the sum of the vertex bits of z in EACH part is odd. The edge set of their spanning trees has trace span(t); modulo its span z has the same component parities as each generator of H. Adding z therefore gives exactly H.

If q=|supp(z) outside P|≤2, let d₀ be the number of even P-blocks under z. The projected weight equals 4−d₀+q, so q≥d₀. Total parity yields q−d₀ even. Assign one outside support to each deficient block and all remaining pairs to any one block. There are at most seven pinned vertices, in four prescribed groups, and a tree per group asks for at most three pair connections. Choose distinct unpinned neighbors for every endpoint incidence. Delete all pins, route the pairs via disjoint paths, and restore their endpoint incidences. Thomas and Wollan's 10l bound applies for l≤3. The cores are disjoint and connected. Extend them to a partition by growing into unassigned vertices in the connected graph. Every charge of z was pinned, so the desired parities persist.

For q≥3 take three charged vertices outside P as toggles. Use a as the background root and c,d,e as the three other roots. Demand an a–b path and, for each toggle, a path to its own nonbackground root and another to a. There are eight distinct endpoints and seven demands. Choose distinct external neighbors for all endpoint incidences, delete the eight endpoints, then route the seven pairs using 70-connectivity of the remainder and restore the incidence edges. All paths have disjoint interiors and avoid other endpoints. Exclude the toggle vertices temporarily. Assign each remaining path segment to the part of its root (and the full a–b path to the background). These disjoint connected cores contain all named vertices. The graph minus the three toggles is connected, so grow the cores to partition it. Each toggle now has a neighbor in each of its two intended parts. Start them all in the background and move whichever ones are necessary to make each nonbackground group odd. Connectivity persists in the background because the original background core without toggles is connected. The background becomes odd automatically since z has even total weight. This gives an original physical witness; the disjoint path instances have fixed size.

In particular, in this explicit spanning-graphic class nonregularity of the ordinary quotient by t suffices for YES. Failure of the test gives a graphic representation of that quotient. This does not identify the quotient of every unrestricted NO instance as graphic. Primary linkage bound: Thomas–Wollan, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2004.02.013 .
