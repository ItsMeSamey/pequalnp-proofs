# Completing a specified physical word, with an anchor-basis obstruction

Let K be an arbitrary represented binary private matrix with one physical tag vector t. For a supplied dependency x and arbitrary indexed alternating Ω, decide whether there exists y sharing one matching chart with x and Ω(x,y)=1. Write F=supp x and ℓ_i=Ω(x,e_i). Test that t restricted to F factors through K_F, equivalently a chart matches every site of F. If not reject. If ℓ is nonzero on ker K_F, recover a detecting word supported in F and use any such chart. Otherwise ℓ also factors through K_F. Extend the two factors to ambient linear functionals g0 and h0. Subtract their evaluations on each physical key from its tag and ℓ respectively; both residual scores vanish on all of F.

Project outside private columns modulo span K_F. Any common matching chart equals g0 plus a quotient functional on these outside sites. An actual mate projects to a quotient dependency: private residue lies in span K_F, the outside residual ℓ sum is one, and the outside sites used share this quotient chart on their residual tags. Decompose that dependency into circuits on its support. Every circuit there has residual tag zero and one of them has residual ℓ one. Conversely a quotient circuit with binary gain (0,1) has just one dependence and so its residual tags extend to a matching quotient chart. Complete its private sum with a linear combination of physical F columns. Chart and score both lift since residual tags and ℓ vanish on F. This also handles quotient loops. Thus the specified-word test is the rank-two prescribed circuit-gain test on the quotient. Componentwise, a connected binary component with rank-two circuit score map realizes all three nonzero gains by the zero-gain pivot shortest-path lemma; rank one is a fundamental-cycle test. Alternatively use the existing certifying one-tag linear first-space query on the quotient with residual test ℓ. If a circuit mate specifically is desired, decompose the lifted y in the original active support and retain a circuit giving value one.

Any positive unrestricted pairing expands in a physical matched chart into pairs of ordinary balanced circuits, at least one positive. But an arbitrary balanced-circuit basis of the aggregate need not supply a usable anchor, even with rank-four Ω. On the nine physical keys [10,8,12,1,5,2,3,15,14] in F₂⁴, take bit tag mask 232. In the ordered physical-word basis [61,75,143,263] of their aggregate, define Ω by mask 23 in lexicographic order of off-diagonal entries. Extend to the whole indexed word space by any linear coordinate extension. This Ω has rank four on the aggregate. The balanced physical circuits [61,118,143,314] form another basis of that same space; none has a compatible mate, even allowing arbitrary words as mates. Chart bit mask 1 nevertheless gives the positive physical circuit pair (75,263). check-fixed-circuit-anchors.py exhausts all 16 charts, all 31 nonzero raw dependencies and all 12 balanced circuits. This disproves universal correctness of trying any arbitrary spanning circuit basis; it does not preclude selecting anchors intelligently by inspecting Ω and the host.
