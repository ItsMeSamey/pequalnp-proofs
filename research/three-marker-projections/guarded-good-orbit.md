# One guarded orbit prevents opposite selectable literals

In a closed permutation network of independent optional three-cycles acting on disjoint physical departure triples, observe G, permanent guards before clauses, and literal probes. At every mask the G orbit traverses all guards and selected probes in prescribed guarded clause order. All other orbits are arbitrary. Opposite literals may never be simultaneously selected. Then opposite probes p,n in distinct clauses cannot both be individually selectable.

Trace out every other probe, keeping G, guards, p,n. The permutations P,N selecting p alone and n alone are fixed: the sole complementary site is necessarily fixed under first return. They differ at the preceding guards gp,gn and at p,n, four distinct tails. The neither state E can have either permutation on the complementary pair. P to E changes at least gp and p, and fixes gn.

One native flip alters at most three first-return tails, since each changed tail uses an old segment containing a changed departure and these segments are disjoint. If two distinct tails change, no fixed tail's old segment can contain a changed departure. Otherwise the three physical cuts lie on three distinct old segments exactly once each. The flip cyclically reattaches the three old prefixes to the three suffixes, whose endpoints are distinct. All three projected tails would change.

Choose closest masks A,D realizing P,N. Toggling any differing gate at A cannot give N by the four-tail bound, or P by minimality. It must give E. Hence no physical departure of any such gate lies on the old gn segment. Toggling all those disjoint native gates preserves every successor along that original segment, whereas N requires its endpoint to be n. This contradiction proves the theorem.

The argument imposes no conditions on the count, membership or order of complementary orbits, including hidden and detached orbits. Good-guard order is still prescribed at every mask. Joint decision reductions admitting other good orders or coupled interior tests remain outside its scope.
