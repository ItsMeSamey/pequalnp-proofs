# Large clique adhesions with bounded cross induced matching

Use charged-clique-tree.md with no bound on physical clique adhesion sizes. For an original parent-to-child edge write B for the adhesion and I for the strict child subtree interior. Require the bipartite graph of cross edges between B and I to have induced matching number at most c. At fixed c test this by trying c+1 edges. For any subset of B, an inclusion-minimal subset with the same neighborhood union into I has size at most c: each retained vertex has a private neighbor, yielding a cross induced matching.

Write b=p(c+1). Each child entry is indexed by a physical subset R of its original B of size at most b whose neighborhood union into I equals that of B, a coloring of R, the occurring color set, and the exact interior scores. It describes a coloring of G[I union R] connected in each occurring color. No other B vertex belongs to this local problem. The root uses empty B and R. Empty interiors contribute nothing new.

In a current context remove the parent adhesion except R from the current induced torso, leaving A. For a child j put S_j=B_j intersect A. Its neighbors in I_j are exactly those of the original B_j. Indeed a deleted parent adhesion vertex adjacent to x in I_j has a replacement r in R adjacent to x by total coverage into the current interior. Tree separation implies r is in B_j and A. Each effective S_j remains a clique. A is connected if nonempty: neighbors of any deleted clique sites on the torso interior have contacts in the surviving clique, and all surviving contacts are adjacent. Empty redundant bags can equivalently be suppressed. Choose a minimal neighborhood cover from S_j. It contains at most c vertices and supplies an actual adjacent owner to every component of I_j by total coverage and connectedness of the original graph. Thus a child's default charge additions use at most c owner sites, even for a disconnected interior.

Default assignments and the proof of at most p+d(p−1) exceptional children proceed on this effective torso and its S_j as in charged-clique-tree.md. For completeness, for each exceptional child and each color on S_j select at most c contacts of that color covering all its neighbors into I_j, selecting one representative when there are no neighbors. The union R_j is a legal stored context. Restrict the original solution to I_j and S_j by clique shortcuts, then discard S_j except R_j: any interior site touching its own color on the lost boundary still touches its own color on R_j. All retained contacts of a color form a clique, so every occurring color stays connected, including colors wholly inside I_j. Precisely the same colors are present on R_j as on S_j in this completeness construction.

Enumerate exception sets, their R_j subsets of S_j and table states, and the exact torso colors. Pin only R_j, besides the parent pins. Enforce that each closed child color occurs nowhere else, and check total color occurrence and scores. Additional vertices of S_j may take other permitted torso colors. Soundness follows because each nonclosed child color attaches to its own torso color through an actual retained contact; unpinned boundary sites only supply additional edges. Closed colors remain confined to one child. Traceback uses every original site. Parent adhesion scores are excluded while actual default charge additions at current retained adhesion sites are counted as before.

There are n^{O(b)} contexts per tree node. With z=p+d(p−1) exceptions the number of pins is at most (z+1)b and changes to baseline charges touch at most cz owner sites. Check bounded domination of the baseline support on each reduced context, or sufficient physical connectivity there for the pinned high-connectivity solver. These checks and the full table computation are polynomial for fixed p,d,c,k and polynomial-size tree, without a height-dependent exponent. They may reject a decomposition on an unsupported context. For clique bags every nonempty reduced torso automatically has domination number one. This gives a recognizable condition on any computed clique tree of a chordal graph, allowing unbounded physical adhesions. No recognition of an optimal tree or solver for unbounded cross induced matching is claimed.
