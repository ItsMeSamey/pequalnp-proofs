# Domination of folded charge support

Let G be a connected finite graph with vertex charges in F₂^d of sum zero. A part is nonempty and connected. In the three-part problem prescribe the three distinct nonzero elements of a two-dimensional subspace H as the part sums.

In the dominating-set algorithm of dominating-charged-partitions.md it suffices for D to dominate the nonzero-charge vertices. Choose owners just for charged vertices outside D; all neutral outside vertices have baseline and deviation zero. Keep the anchors in D and an anchor in each missing color, exactly as in the original construction. Route the retained trees. Attach each omitted charged vertex to its owner's tree by their edge. While any vertex remains, attach a vertex adjacent to a current part to that part. Connectedness guarantees progress and every such vertex is neutral. Leaf removal in a minimal true retained subtree still preserves the computed sum because the restoration procedure works for every retained subtree. The argument also applies to two prescribed parts by using their first sum (the second is its complement). D need not be supplied: enumerate subsets of size at most k and check domination of the support.

There is no requirement for a common D across edge blocks. Choose edge vectors g with incident sum q, for example by accumulating subtree sums on a spanning tree. For each bridge or maximal two-connected edge block B, set q_B(v) to the sum of incident g on B. These charges are independent of the choice of g, since every change decomposes into labeled simple cycles, each confined to one block. In particular the folded charge includes the total of every component attached through its vertex.

For a physical edge set X let I(X) consist of the gains of cuts supported in X (gain is the sum of g on the cut). This is the span of component totals of G−X: shores are exactly unions of components. The cut space is the orthogonal of the cycle space, and cycles decompose by edge block, so I(X) is the sum of independently chosen I_B(X intersection E(B)). Thus equality to H requires either one local plane H, or two different local lines in H in different blocks. Conversely either certificate works, with all other X_B empty.

An image of dimension s can be realized with exactly s+1 remaining connected components while retaining the image. In the component quotient choose a spanning tree. Loss of an image direction upon merging adjacent components i,j implies some functional of the vector of component sums equals the incidence vector of this pair (the functional vanishes on all other components and is one on both). These evaluations span a space of dimension s; tree edges are independent, so a safe merge exists whenever there are more than s+1 components. Select the corresponding physical edges for restoration and repeat. For s=2 the final three totals are precisely the three nonzero target vectors since their sum is zero and rank two. For s=1 both totals equal the nonzero generator.

Hence if each block's folded nonzero support is dominated by k vertices, compute its H and three line tests using the preceding algorithm, combine their actual cuts and restore edges as above. This yields a deterministic n^{O(k+d+1)} algorithm with the original spanning partition as witness. The total number of owners used in different blocks is unrestricted. Single vertices and loops supply zero cut image. There is no polynomial time guarantee here when the local support domination parameter grows.
