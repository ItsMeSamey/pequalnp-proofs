# Removing the connectivity assumption on expanders

Fix Δ,ε>0 (Δ integer), binary tag dimension r, color count p and pin bound. On connected simple graphs of maximum degree Δ and vertex expansion ε up to n/2, construct connected spanning ordered nonempty parts with prescribed binary tags and nonzero GF₂ quadratic site-color predicate, or decide that none exist. This is polynomial for fixed parameters. Use the companion expander-binary-bank.md and its dependencies. No test of ε-expansion is claimed.

Maintain undamaged skeleton J, actual routing graph J minus boundedly many damaged edges, and guessed true colors pinned at all damaged endpoints. At three successive stages s=1,2,3 the undamaged J is s-connected. Recursively split its current bags at size-s separators with both exclusive sides nonempty; for s=3 require both exclusive sides to contain at least TWO vertices. Complete the interface on both sides and attach old clique interfaces to a side containing them. This preserves running intersections and s-connectivity by shortcutting opposite-side excursions after <s deletions. The sum of (bag size minus s) is unchanged. Final large bags at stages 1 and 2 are (s+1)-connected; after stage 3 they are quasi-4-connected, meaning 3-connected with no three-separation having two or more exclusive vertices on both sides. Fixed-size separator enumeration constructs the tree. Each final tree edge has original sides at least as large as its creating split's exclusive sides. Every nonphysical final bag edge is covered by an incident interface, as proved in three-cut-rooted-triangles.md.

Write η,δ for current expansion and degree bounds. Removing s vertices leaves a giant and all nongiant vertices together ≤s/η for sufficiently large order. Orient every tree edge toward its strict side of size >|J|/2 (the side containing the giant); the opposite strict side has size ≤s/η. Outdegree is at most one since distinct outward strict sides at a bag are disjoint. The unique sink bag C has bounded-size strict fringes, each a whole physical subtree. Every component of a fringe touches all its contacts by s-connectivity in undamaged J. Thus every C site occurs in at most δ incident fringe interfaces, |J|≤(1+δs/η)|C|, and the completed central skeleton J' has degree ≤sδ. Small cases can be brute-forced. J' has expansion at least η'=min(1/4,η/4). For Y⊆C, |Y|≤|C|/2, let T denote its external neighbors in J'. If |T|<η'|Y| then |T|<η|J|/8 and |T|<|J|/8. The giant of J−T misses at most |T|/η surviving sites. Both Y and C minus (Y union T) exceed that number, but paths in J−T between bag sites project to walks of J'−T by shortcutting fringe excursions, a contradiction.

Every clean fringe has a fixed partition into connected owners covering its whole interior and physically realizing the clique on its contacts. For s=1 use the sole owner. For s=2 use an interior path between the contacts and flood. For s=3 apply three-cut-rooted-triangles.md since J at this stage is 3-connected and the strict side has at least two original J vertices. No owner uses contact-contact edges. Mark a fringe mandatory if it contains an original pin or damaged endpoint in its strict interior. There are boundedly many mandatory fringes.

In an accepting coloring retain also every fringe containing a color closed away from C (at most p). With bag colors fixed, reset any other fringe to its default owners. Every equal-color connection across the fringe remains physically realizable because its full clique is realized. No new color appears and every new strict vertex reaches its owner. The predicate P times the requested binary-score indicator has degree ≤2+pr on any family of binary reset switches: each vertex depends on at most one switch. Minimize nondefaults with this product nonzero; at most 2+pr nonmandatory, nonclosed exceptions remain, by the Boolean point indicator degree. Enumerate exceptions and their entire bounded-size interior colorings, pin their interface colors, keep the center palette and one representative per surviving color. Verify closed-color uniqueness and that all other fringe components reach their contacts. Form the actual new central graph from undamaged physical bag edges, default full cliques, and exceptional boundary joins only where physical equal-color paths exist. Such joins have pinned endpoints. It differs from J' in at most old damage plus binom(s,2) edges per exception. Guess and pin all missing-edge endpoints. Original normalized coloring induces connected bag parts; conversely every bag witness expands using separate full owners and true exceptional joins. Default sites copy one fixed owner, exceptional sites are fixed, so scores stay binary linear and P stays quadratic. Only three stages of branching are used. Re-expand all fringes and verify the indexed original witness.

The final large quasi-4-connected bounded-degree expander with bounded pinned edge damage is solved by expander-binary-bank.md with these modifications. Each pair of distant or nearby free sites needs only 2+2 arms: use at least eight distinct distant sinks. A flow cut of cost ≤3 blocking one source (cost two) deletes it and at most one ordinary blocker; 3-connectivity connects the other to a surviving sink. If neither source is blocked, the at most three blocked sites would separate both sources from at least five sinks, impossible by quasi-4-connectivity. Take any surviving path after its last surviving source when arcs entering sources are forbidden. Singleton requests require only two arms. Truncation to bounded lengths keeps all arms clear of damage, since every damaged endpoint is pinned in the inner protected region.

Use the actual damaged graph for all witness-dependent contractions and the undamaged skeleton for distant arms. With d damaged edges and v deleted sites, the damaged graph's nongiant mass is at most (v+2d)/η'+2d for all fixed v and sufficiently large order. Indeed first delete all damaged endpoints in the undamaged skeleton, locate the undamaged giant, then restore the surviving endpoints with the forbidden edges still absent. The giant remains connected. Use this revised mass bound plus one for every private size threshold, including m extra sites for contractions. Thus a private set remains large even when it contains a damaged endpoint. Obtain a large clique minor by discarding the bounded set of all damaged endpoints and modified sites before the balanced-separator argument (or from a larger clique minor in the undamaged original). The damaged graph after bounded small-color closures B is connected if every remaining actual color is larger than the mass bound. After omitting the remote test sites S, any nongiant component must touch S; it cannot also touch B or a damaged endpoint by the buffer gap and mass bound. Its true external neighborhood in the undamaged graph would then have at most the two sites in one test cluster, contradicting 3-connectivity. Increase all buffer and small-color scales as required by the modified bounds. The rest of the affine bank and original-witness proof is unchanged.
