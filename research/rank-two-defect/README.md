# The rank-two defect and three parity markers

Write K=A+Aᵀ over GF(2), and assume it has rank two. Its row space W has three nonzero elements. Any ordered basis a,b of W gives K=abᵀ+baᵀ. Indeed a rank-two alternating form is represented on its two-dimensional nondegenerate quotient by the unique nonzero alternating matrix. Conversely any decomposition K=uvᵀ+vuᵀ uses an ordered basis of W. The six choices can therefore be tested directly from the rows of K. Here a and b are column vectors and abᵀ denotes their outer product.

Write q(x)=xᵀAx. Every solution of the guards has q(x)=0. For any basis a,b the residual q(x)+(aᵀx)(bᵀx) is the linear form with coefficient diag(A)+a⊙b, where ⊙ means coordinatewise product. If the residual is in W, q descends to the two coordinates aᵀx,bᵀx. It has either three zeros or just the origin. The former case occurs exactly when some ordered basis gives diag(A)=a⊙b. To see this, the product of any two independent forms on the plane is the indicator of the unique nonzero point where both equal one, and all three points are available by changing basis.

**Three zeros give a polynomial algorithm for every marker h.** Choose factors q(x)=(aᵀx)(bᵀx). The zero set is a union of two hyperplanes. On bᵀx=0, the alternating matrix T=A+abᵀ agrees with A. Test T with the two markers bᵀx=0 and hᵀx=1. Exchange a,b to test the other hyperplane. A solution to either alternating problem is an original witness. When h itself belongs to W and is nonzero, a separate reduction covers arbitrary diagonals. Choose b=h, add abᵀ to A to make it symmetric, and add diag(a) to that matrix. On bᵀx=1 its guard products equal the original guard products. The symmetric one-marker algorithm then applies.

**One zero gives an exact encoding of three alternating markers.** In this case q(x)=aᵀx+bᵀx+(aᵀx)(bᵀx). Every feasible assignment therefore lies in the common kernel of aᵀ and bᵀ. The alternating matrix T=A+aaᵀ+bbᵀ+abᵀ agrees with A there. Conversely, from an arbitrary alternating T and three independent linear markers with nonzero joint target, row-reduce the markers so two targets are zero and one is one. Name their normals a,b,h respectively. The original solutions are exactly the solutions of A=T+aaᵀ+bbᵀ+abᵀ with the single marker hᵀx=1. The defect of A has rank two. A zero target has the zero witness, and dependent markers either conflict or reduce the number of independent markers.

Even with residual outside W, any rank-two instance reduces to two alternating three-marker decisions. Fix bᵀx=y in each branch. S=A+abᵀ+diag(ya) is symmetric and has the original guard products on the branch. Add an apex adjacent to the vertices of diagonal one in S, remove the diagonal, and impose the three markers bᵀx=y, hᵀx=1, and apex=1. The new apex guard holds because summing the symmetric guard equations gives diag(S)ᵀx=0. Thus rank-at-most-two defect with one marker and alternating matrices with three markers are polynomial-time Turing equivalent. This does not decide the general three-marker problem.

The checker classifies all rank-two-defect matrices through dimension three (174 with three zeros, 58 with one zero, 224 with residual outside W). It verifies every marker on the first family and the exact equality of witness sets on the second. It loads the alternating witness finder from the sibling two-markers directory.
