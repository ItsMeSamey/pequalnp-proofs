# Common printable SAT error batches

Use CNF encoded as an outer bracketed sequence of bracketed clauses, with signed binary variable names separated by commas inside each clause. Empty conjunctions and empty clauses have their usual meanings. Trailing spaces are legal and count toward length. Malformed words are rejected. This finite-alphabet language is polynomial-time parsable and NP-complete.

One fixed deterministic printer G on unary input of length n prints in polynomial time a framed list B_n of valid formulas with no answer labels. For every deterministic polynomial-time Boolean heuristic A, infinitely many n must have some F in B_n on which A errs. The printer is common to all machines and exponents. Honesty means n ≤ p(|F|+1) for all printed F, with one fixed polynomial p.

## Honest batches exist exactly when E differs from NE

E and NE use deterministic and nondeterministic time 2^{O(m)} on binary inputs of length m. Suppose E = NE. The predicate on binary pairs (n,j) stating that the j-th printed formula exists and is satisfiable belongs to NE. Generate the batch in poly(n) time and guess and verify its assignment. Use a self-delimiting encoding of length O(log(n+2)+log(j+2)) that contains the binary expansion of n. Under the assumed equality this predicate has a deterministic 2^{O(m)} decider. On a formula F of length L, enumerate all n up to a fixed integer polynomial dominating p(L+1), regenerate each batch and look for an exact match. On finding a match with index j, run the pair decider and return its answer. Both n and j are polynomial in L+1, so the encoding length is O(log(L+2)) and this takes polynomial time. Return zero after an unsuccessful search. By honesty this heuristic is correct on every printed input.

Conversely take K in NE outside E. For binary x of length m let t be the integer with binary expansion 1 followed by x. Applying the uniform Cook–Levin construction to the fixed NE machine for K gives a CNF F_x in deterministic time and output length bounded by 2^{a(m+1)} for some fixed positive integer a. It is satisfiable exactly when x belongs to K. Use the injective length n(x)=(2t)^a, between 2^{a(m+1)} and 2^{O(m+1)}. On unary n, test whether n is the a-th power of an even positive integer 2t, and if so recover x, construct F_x and pad it with spaces to exactly n symbols. Otherwise print a fixed true formula padded to length n. Begin indexing after the finite prefix too short for the fixed formula, or allow empty batches there. The printer runs in polynomial time in n and prints one formula at every sufficiently large length.

If A made only finitely many errors at the special lengths n(x), constructing the padded formula and running A would decide K in 2^{O(m+1)} time after hardwiring finitely many exceptional answers. This contradicts the choice of K. Thus common exact-length singletons suffice.

### A single output-linear printer suffices

Measure time on a deterministic multitape Turing machine with a two-way read-only unary input, work tapes, and a one-way write-only output. A singleton can be emitted as its raw formula with the end of output delimiting it. Choose a large enough in the preceding construction to dominate the Cook–Levin generation time on this model, including serialization in the specified CNF alphabet. Scan the unary input once, recording n with a least-significant-bit-first binary counter. Each increment scans through its trailing ones and returns to the units position. The total number of such ones over n increments is at most n, so the scan costs O(n). Perfect-power testing, integer root extraction for fixed a, and recovery of x take polynomial time in log(n+2), hence O(n) overall. Construct the formula on a work tape in time O(n) and reset its head to the start. On nonspecial lengths use the constant true formula instead. Rewind the unary input. For each of its n symbols emit the next formula character, switching to spaces when the stored formula ends. This last pass emits exactly n characters in O(n) steps and requires no counter decrements. Finitely many undersized lengths may still have empty batches. Consequently E differs from NE exactly when one deterministic O(n)-time unary printer produces a length-n singleton at every sufficiently large n and hits every polynomial-time SAT candidate infinitely often. Writing the n output symbols itself requires linear time on this model.

## Without honesty the equivalence is with P different from NP


An exact polynomial-time decider defeats all printers. Conversely, enumerate words over the formula alphabet in length-lexicographic order starting at index one. On unary n recover word n by positional arithmetic and print it if valid, or print a fixed true formula otherwise. The printer takes polynomial time in n. Every valid formula appears and the indexed word has length O(log(n+1)). If a polynomial heuristic had only finitely many errors on valid words, a parser rejecting malformed words and a lookup table correcting its finitely many valid exceptions would decide SAT in P. Thus if P differs from NP, infinitely many distinct valid errors for each heuristic occur at distinct printer indices.

## Bounded clocks for candidate-specific refuters

Use deterministic exact-length singleton refuters: for each candidate A a unary printer R_A outputs a valid length-n error on infinitely many n. It may print arbitrary words or nothing at other lengths. If there is a common polynomial degree d such that each A admits some such printer taking O_A(n^d) time, set k=d+1 (round up to an integer if necessary). On 1^n enumerate all binary programs of length ≤ floor(log₂ n), and simulate each for n^k of its own steps on 1^n. Keep valid length-n outputs of completed programs. There are fewer than 2n programs, and standard universal simulation, parsing and copying take polynomial total time in n for this fixed k. Each R_A is eventually present and fits its allotted clock, including its machine-dependent constant. Infinitely many of its successful outputs therefore enter this common honest batch. The construction needs neither a compiler from A to R_A nor knowledge of its description. Conversely E different from NE supplies one common polynomial-clock singleton printer by the preceding construction, hence a common degree bound. Thus the bounded-degree candidate-specific statement is equivalent to E different from NE. Under the joint hypothesis P different from NP and E = NE, no fixed degree suffices across these refuters. Quantitatively, for each d some candidate admits no O(n^d) exact-length refuter. This does not impose a single lower bound on all refuters for every candidate.

## Literature and local comparison

Hartmanis, Immerman and Sewelson already give the printable hard SAT restriction in Corollaries 3–4. Their exponential classes on page 162 are today's E and NE. In batch terminology the range S of an honest printer is P-printable: to list all members through length L, run every index through p(L+1), filter by length and remove duplicates. Conversely a P-printable restriction supplies exact-length batches by filtering its list through length n. Membership in S is polynomial-time decidable by printing through the input length. Thus a heuristic correct on S exists exactly when S ∩ SAT belongs to P. Finitely many exceptional inputs can be corrected by a finite table. This identifies the honest batch equivalence with the classical result. [Hartmanis, Immerman and Sewelson, pp. 162, 165–166](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~immerman/HIS85.pdf)

The same paper records Wilson's oracle A with P^A different from NP^A and E^A equal to NE^A, and Kurtz's oracle with no sparse language in NP^A outside P^A despite polynomial-time separation. In particular all tally NP^A languages are in P^A in those examples. Use an NP^A-complete verifier satisfiability problem, for example satisfiability of a polynomial-size circuit with gates querying A on their input strings, encoded with legal trailing padding. The binary-pair label predicate belongs to NE^A even for a printer with oracle access. Wilson's collapse gives an E^A label decider, and the range lookup above supplies one P^A heuristic correct on every emitted formula. The universal enumeration also relativizes by forwarding each simulated oracle query. Consequently the relativized polynomial-time separation alone cannot force a common degree bound for exact-length candidate-specific refuters. Ordinary CNF satisfiability need not be NP^A-complete, so the verifier choice is material. [Hartmanis, Immerman and Sewelson, pp. 159–160](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~immerman/HIS85.pdf)

Chen, Jin, Santhanam and Williams formalize constructive separations with candidate-specific refuters and discuss the SAT result of Gutfreund, Shaltiel and Ta-Shma. The latter also distinguish samplable distributions tailored to each candidate from a common distribution. The common batch here receives no heuristic input, and its success is existential among unlabelled entries. [Chen et al.](https://theoretics.episciences.org/12881) · [Gutfreund et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00037-007-0235-8)
