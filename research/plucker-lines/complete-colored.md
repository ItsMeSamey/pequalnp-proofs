# Complete colored block and arbitrary shared graph

Use the labels and even induced degree target of [the two-sided problem](two-sided-shared.md). Assume every colored left vertex is adjacent to every colored right vertex. The shared graph and the incidences between colored and shared vertices are arbitrary. There is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm finding a witness or certifying infeasibility.

For each colored pair i,j on opposite shores, look for a path through shared vertices after deleting ij. A shortest path closes via ij to an induced cycle with evenly many shared vertices. Matching endpoint colors give a target immediately. For opposite colors, search additionally over shared spectators h missing i,j, deleting h and its neighbors before the path search. A cycle found there, together with the isolated h, is a target. The [rooted-edge theorem and application](rooted-cycle/README.md) prove that these searches succeed whenever there is a target with odd shared-only count.

Suppose the searches failed and there is a target with even shared-only count. Each selected colored vertex has odd degree into selected shared vertices, because the opposite colored selection count is odd and the colored block is complete. Summing the selected left shared guards shows the total number of selected shared-to-shared edges is odd. There is a component C of the shared graph with an odd number of such edges. Summing its selected guards on each shore shows that C contacts colored vertices on both shores. Without such a component no target is possible.

For a component touching colored vertices on both shores, its left and right colored contacts must have opposite colors, since matching contacts would yield a target cycle. For any such endpoints i,j, a shared right vertex outside C missing i would be an isolated spectator on the cycle, and likewise for a shared left vertex outside C missing j. Thus i and j see every opposite shared vertex outside C. In particular if another nonisolated component exists it too contacts both i and j. Consequently every shared component containing an edge is bi-contact, and all colored vertices touching any such component have a common color on their respective shores; these two colors are opposite. To check consistency across two components, use i and j touching the first, who now also touch the second. Every such colored vertex sees every opposite shared isolate, by the same spectator argument. Here an isolate means a vertex with no shared-to-shared neighbor.

Call these colored vertices participants, and the remaining colored vertices outsiders. A selected right outsider has odd degree into selected left isolates, since it has no nonisolated shared neighbor. A selected left isolate sees every right participant, so its degree parity into the selected right outsiders equals the selected right participant parity. Write u,v,e for the respective parities of selected right participants, right outsiders, and left isolates. Summing their shared incidence yields v=eu. Since u+v=1 we get u=1 and v=e=0. The mirrored statement gives odd left participant parity and even left outsider and right isolate parities.

The two participant shores therefore contribute opposite colors, while the total shared count is even. To meet the target third coordinate, at least one outsider shore must have odd selection counts in both ordinary colors (its total count is even). Assume this is the right shore. Every selected right outsider and selected left isolate has odd degree in their induced incidence graph by the previous equations. Some opposite-color pair of selected outsiders then has incomparable isolate neighborhoods. Otherwise every opposite-color pair has an odd number of common selected isolate neighbors, since comparable neighborhoods have odd sizes. The number of pairs is odd. Yet at each selected isolate its two outsider color-degree parities sum to one, so their product vanishes. This counts the same pairs with multiplicity by common neighbors and gives an even total. This contradiction proves incomparability.

Take outsiders q0,q1 of opposite colors with distinguishing isolates t0,t1 whose four incidences form a matching. Take any opposite-color participant cycle found by the first path search. Add q0,q1,t0,t1. Each q sees only the cycle's colored left vertex and its matched isolate. Each isolate sees only the colored right endpoint and its matched q. The two colored endpoints each gain two neighbors; no internal shared vertex gains one. All degrees are even. The opposite colors of the two added outsiders correct the opposite endpoint colors, and the two isolates cancel in the label sum. The mirror construction handles a left outsider pair. The check is polynomial and does not have to know the hypothetical target. The proof covers any number of nonisolated components and arbitrary further isolates.

The [solver](complete_solver.py) implements the path, spectator and isolate-matching searches. The [checker](check_complete.py) compares with direct support enumeration and verifies the returned witnesses, including an example that requires the isolate matching. Together with the [noncomplete-block algorithm](noncomplete-colored.md), this resolves both cases for arbitrary mutual shared adjacency when every vertex is ordinarily colored or shared-only.
