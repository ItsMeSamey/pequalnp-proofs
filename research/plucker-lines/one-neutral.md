# One zero-label vertex in a mixed three-marker bipartite instance

Let the two left label classes be P0,P1 with vectors (1,0,r), and the right classes Q0,Q1 have vectors (0,1,r). Require label sum (1,1,0) and even degrees at every selected vertex in the induced graph. Allow one additional left vertex z of label zero. The same result holds with sides exchanged. Every feasible instance has a witness of size two or five.

A missing edge in either matched block Pr × Qr gives a two-vertex witness. Assume both matched blocks complete. For any solution there is a common index r such that its selected counts in Pr and Qr are odd and both complementary counts are even. If z is absent, the cut Pr ∪ Q(1−r) against its complement crosses just the matched blocks, in an odd total of selected edges. Even degrees rule this out.

Write e(j) for the indicator of zj. In the selected cross-block Pr × Q(1−r), each selected left degree is odd, and each selected right degree is e(j) modulo two. Consequently the selected vertices of Q(1−r) with e=1 are odd in number, as are those with e=0. Complement each column of this cross-block with e=1. Within the resulting selected cross-block all degrees are even. There must be a left vertex i having a nonneighbor in each right subtype. To see this, for each selected i write d0,d1 for its two degrees. Their parities agree; the number of selected i with odd d0 is even, by summing the even degrees on the first right subtype. Oddly many selected i therefore have both degrees even. Each such i misses a vertex in each of the two odd-sized right subtypes.

Take those missed vertices j,k with e(j)=0, e(k)=1. In the original graph ij is absent and ik present. The even degree of z forces a selected neighbor l in Qr because z has odd degree into the other right class. The set {i,z,j,k,l} induces a four-cycle on i,z,k,l and the isolated vertex j. Its label sum is the target. Enumeration of pairs and these certificates is polynomial. Identical-neighborhood zero-label twins on the same side may first be replaced by a single vertex, since an even number of selected copies can be removed and an odd number reduced to one without changing degree parities of their neighbors.

The independent script check_neutral.py compares brute-force support enumeration with the two- or five-vertex characterization.

## Several neutral vertices

Restrict to a feasible induced graph. Rename classes so P0,Q0 have odd size and assume both matched blocks complete. Each neutral row z has the same parity t_z on both right classes. Let O and E be the XORs of the neutral rows with odd and even t_z respectively. The parity of the number of five-vertex certificates above, summed over both possible sole colored classes, is 1+O·E.

Fix i in Pr and neutral z. Let d be the parity of their common neighbors in the opposite right class. There are t_z choices modulo two for l, d choices for k and 1+t_z+d choices for j. The last formula uses the fact that the size of the opposite right class plus the degree of i there is odd, by even row degree. The product reduces to t_z d. The sum of all opposite-class colored rows, taking their restrictions on each opposite class, is O+E+1_Q0 by even column degrees. The certificate count is therefore O·(O+E+1_Q0). Here O·O=0 since each neutral row is even. Also O·1_Q0=1 since the sum of all neutral degrees on Q1 equals the sum of the opposite-block degrees of P0 modulo two, which is odd. Each choice corresponds to a distinct certificate.

Thus O·E=0 suffices to find a witness omitting an entire colored left class. In the absence of all five-vertex certificates, for every neutral z touching Qr and every i in Pr, the neighborhood of i on the opposite right block is inclusion-comparable with the complement of the neighborhood of z on that block. Otherwise both a shared neighbor and a shared nonneighbor are available. The script check_many_neutral.py verifies the identity on 2,000 planted instances.

## Degree-two neutral vertices

If every neutral vertex has degree at most two, every feasible instance admits a witness with just one colored left vertex. Restrict to a feasible induced graph where P0,Q0 are odd and both matched blocks are complete. Regard its nonisolated neutral vertices as edges on the right set. The number of mixed neutral edges (joining the two right classes) is odd by the previous argument. Some component of the neutral-edge graph restricted to Q1 thus has an odd number of mixed edges incident to it. Summing the full column parities there shows some i in P0 has a neighbor in that component. Take a mixed edge from l in Q0 into this component and a shortest path from its Q1 endpoint to an i-neighbor inside the component. Stop at the first i-neighbor. Choose i, all right path vertices and their connecting neutral edges. These induce a cycle because i sees l and the final vertex only and neutral vertices have degree two. If the path uses an odd number of Q1 vertices, it uses an even number of Q1 nonneighbors of i. The total number of Q1 nonneighbors of i is odd by its even degree and |Q0| odd. Add one such nonneighbor outside the path as an isolated selected vertex. This gives the target.

The resulting witnesses can be arbitrarily large. Take a single even bipartite cycle with an odd number of right vertices, one colored left vertex i, one right neighbor of i in Q0 and the remaining right vertices in Q1. All remaining left vertices are neutral. Selecting i and its unique Q0 neighbor forces their entire cycle. To add P1, take two twins adjacent to Q1 only. A witness using only P1 would give its selected P1 vertices odd degree. Adding both twins changes no degree parity at original right vertices, so any witness still contains the cycle.

## Finite certificate for small right supports

Every feasible support using at most five right vertices contains a witness using just one colored left vertex, with unrestricted neutral degrees. The program check_five_right.cpp checks the finite obstruction below. Restrict to the original feasible support and discard duplicate pairs of neutral row vectors, which preserves all degrees modulo two. Each neutral row is even. For each neutral system, let E be its row XOR. For any possible even colored row a with a complete matched block, check every right vector y of the requisite label. A witness with sole colored row a and right support y exists precisely when a restricted to y lies in the span of the restrictions of neutral rows z with z dot y = 0. Their individual guards hold, and the representation gives the required column parities and guarantees a dot y = 0. If the claimed theorem failed, augment each bad colored row by its two-bit class indicator. Their span must contain (E,1,0), using the actual selected colored rows and full-graph column parities. Binary elimination excludes this possibility in all 98,321 neutral type systems for normalized right cardinalities 1,3,5. Existence for these cardinalities does not give a uniform bound on witness size.
