# Recognizing uniform filling is coNP-complete

For a spanning binary point set E⊂V, failure of the strong-blocking property is equivalent to a surjective linear map φ:V→F₂² whose image on the listed points avoids the value 11. To see this, a missed direction in some hyperplane gives independent linear functionals separating that direction from the points of E within the hyperplane. Their two-bit images miss one nonzero point; an invertible change of output coordinates carries it to 11. Conversely, on the hyperplane φ⁻¹({00,11}) the listed points all lie in ker φ, a proper subspace. The map is a short NP witness for failure.

Reduce graph 4-colorability. Write V=A⊕B, with the two blocks separately based by an anchor and 2n variable coordinates X_i,Y_i for an n-vertex graph. Include in E all basis columns and all pair sums within each block. Such points strongly block their block, by the kernel-generator argument in the main note. In a surjection missing 11, neither block can map onto both output dimensions; neither can map to the 11 line because its points span it. The two block images therefore are the two distinct lines 10 and 01. After exchanging the output bits the map is specified by two nonzero linear forms α on A and β on B. A mixed point (a,b) is allowed precisely when α(a)β(b)=0.

Write a₀,b₀ for the anchor basis columns. For each sum w of exactly one or two nonanchor basis vectors, let w_A,w_B denote the corresponding vectors in each block and add the two points

    (w_A, b₀+w_B),    (a₀+w_A, w_B).

If both anchor values are zero, this forces α(w_A)β(w_B)=0 for all tested w. Two nonzero linear forms on the variable coordinates have a common-one test among the units and pairs. If they have no common-one unit, their coordinate supports are disjoint and nonempty; use any pair with one unit from each support. Thus both anchors cannot vanish. If α(a₀)=0 and β(b₀)=1, the two tests for a unit force both α(w)(1+β(w))=0 and α(w)β(w)=0, so α vanishes, impossible. The exchanged case rules out β(b₀)=0 as well. With both anchors one, the tests on units force equality of the two variable assignments; every equal assignment also satisfies all the pair tests.

For each graph edge ij add the mixed point

    (a₀+X_i^A+X_j^A, b₀+Y_i^B+Y_j^B).

Under the common assignment, its two image bits are simultaneously one exactly when i and j receive the same color (X_i,Y_i). All edge points avoid 11 exactly for a proper 4-coloring. This constructs a failure from every coloring and recovers a coloring from every failure. There are O(n²) points with at most six nonzero coordinates. They include two explicitly supplied strong blocking sets in complementary subspaces spanning V. Hence testing whether E itself is a strong blocking set is coNP-complete even under that promise. Equivalently, nonintersection of the supports of two nonzero codewords is NP-complete to detect in the code with generator columns E. The existential guarded model algorithm under the two-set cover uses the supplied subspaces directly.

## A fixed target also has hard universal coverage

For a graph with basis z,e_v take a_uv=z+e_u+e_v for each edge. The existence of d such that d(z)=0 yet z is outside the span of its d-zero listed columns is equivalent to the existence of f(z)=1 annihilating all those columns. Assign colors (d(e_v),f(e_v)). Two adjacent vertices with matching first bits require different second bits, exactly the proper coloring condition. Conversely a coloring defines d,f. Add a disjoint triangle if it is necessary to guarantee that z lies in the original column span. Universal coverage of this prescribed target is therefore coNP-complete for weight-three columns. This predicate has universal quantification over d; the model-finding problem instead asks for a dual that spans its target.

## Exact dual-count separation for two certified filling blocks

Use the two-block construction and introduce named root occurrences t=a₀+b₀ and s=a₀. The ordinary occurrence of a₀ stays available; s is a separate named occurrence with the same vector. Require d(s)=1,d(t)=0, and use no primal labels. Every proper coloring gives a unique defective base covector d=(α,β), with the same anchored bit string on both blocks. Its eligible columns are annihilated by the covector (0,β), which takes value one on t, so t is outside their span. Conversely take a defective base d and a separator f that evaluates to one on t and vanishes on all eligible ordinary points. The two-row map (d+f,f) is surjective because d(t)=0,f(t)=1 and d(s)=1. Its image misses 11 because equality of its two bits implies d=0 at that column and eligibility then forces f=0. The synchronization proof forces its rows to be (α,0),(0,β) in some order, with the same assignment encoded by α,β. Their sum d determines this coloring uniquely, regardless of the order. Thus the number of distinct defective base duals equals the number of labeled proper four-colorings, and the number of viable base duals is exactly 2^(dim V−2) minus that number. Exact viable-dual counting is therefore #P-hard under a single query and subtraction, even with two certified complementary filling blocks spanning the entire ambient space and zero primal labels. A fixed dual is checkable by Gaussian elimination, so the counting problem is in #P. Decision and witness extraction stay polynomial on this class by the covering theorem. This differs from the single-core model, on which the viable-dual count is fixed-parameter tractable in its codimension. [Linial, Hard Enumeration Problems in Geometry and Combinatorics](https://doi.org/10.1137/0607036).

In matroid terminology strong blocking of spanning binary points is roundness: failure partitions the ground points among two proper hyperplanes. In the dual binary matroid the disjoint-nonzero-word problem is the existence of two disjoint circuits. Earlier NP-completeness of two disjoint circuits for rational linear matroids used a different reduction and a different representation field. [Bernáth and Király, Theorem 10](https://real.mtak.hu/20689/1/1407.4999v1.pdf). No priority in the binary case is asserted.

## Recognition with one supplied strong core

Suppose a strong blocking subset of E spans W with codimension h in V. For any pair of nonzero disjoint codewords from covectors α,β, the map (α,β) misses 11, hence on W has image zero or one of the two allowed lines. Therefore at least one form vanishes on W. Enumerate each nonzero f∈W⊥, at most 2^h−1. All nonzero partners are precisely the nonzero covectors vanishing on B_f=span{v∈E:f(v)=1}, of which there are 2^(dim V−dim B_f)−1. Since E spans V, f is not its own partner. This decides global strong blocking in 2^h poly(input). It also counts all unordered pairs exactly in that time: sum the displayed counts, then subtract half the sum counting partners additionally vanishing on W, using B_f+W instead of B_f. Every pair with two forms vanishing on W had been counted twice, while all other pairs were counted once. In contrast, the construction above proves coNP-completeness and #P-hardness under two complementary supplied cores with zero uncovered codimension.

## Two skew circuits and rooted theta restrictions

Take a represented binary matroid N whose cycle space is the code generated by the point matrix E; a parity-check matrix obtained by Gaussian elimination represents N. In a disjoint pair x,y from a proper coloring, the underlying generating covectors are (α,0) and (0,β). Any codeword contained in supp(x)∪supp(y) has generating form (g,h) with support on the pure A block contained in the support of α, and similarly on the B block for β. A strong blocking point set generates a binary minimal code: if nonzero g has support properly contained in the support of α then g and α+g have nonempty disjoint supports, contradicting strong blocking. Thus g=0 or α, and h=0 or β. All four choices are indeed present and supported in the union. It follows that x,y are circuits, and their union has nullity exactly two. Since they are disjoint this says their spans intersect trivially: they are skew circuits. Conversely any disjoint circuits are a disjoint pair of codewords and so give the coloring already proved.

Consequently even an unmarked test for two skew circuits of a represented binary matroid is NP-complete. Each of the two circuits in every pair contains its own prescribed anchor occurrence (a₀ or b₀). Assign gain one to both anchors and zero to all other elements. Then the two skew circuits each have the same nonzero gain and one passes through the distinguished element a₀. Membership in NP follows from direct rank and gain checks. The input matroid is connected when the graph has at least one vertex: the pure sides are connected via the triangles formed by units and pair sums, and a mixed synchronizer has a fundamental circuit joining the sides. Duality preserves connectedness.

Append this gain row to a representation of N and add a new q whose original projection is zero and whose gain is one. A pair of skew gain-one circuits K,J gives a theta restriction on K∪J∪{q}: its three circuits are exactly K∪{q}, J∪{q}, K∪J, since the cycles of the contraction on K∪J form exactly span(K,J). Conversely, contracting the named singleton arm of such a theta restriction gives exactly two skew gain-one circuits of N. This proves NP-completeness of deciding existence of such a restriction with a named singleton arm and another named element on one remaining arm. Arbitrary contraction of unrelated ordinary elements is disallowed in this restriction problem. A polynomial three-terminal parallel-minor oracle has a different acceptance criterion. The full guarded existence problem can additionally accept other circuit patterns, so neither skew-packing nor theta-restriction hardness alone implies hardness for three mixed guarded markers.
