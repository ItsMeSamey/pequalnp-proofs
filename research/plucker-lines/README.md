# A four-terminal obstruction to a quadratic Plücker test

Consider a binary fundamental graph whose two blocks give disjoint cycles and cocycles of a represented binary matroid. Let the three-bit marker input be fully mixed. If the target lies in either pure image, one side alone supplies a witness. Otherwise each pure image has rank at most two. If the two images fail to span the target space, check consistency and reduce their dimension. The unresolved new case has two distinct planes in the three-dimensional marker codomain. Use coordinates (p,q,r) so the cycle side contributes (p,0,r_C), the cocycle side (0,q,r_D), and the target is (1,1,s).

The natural terminal space for this reduction is the four-dimensional binary space indexed by cycle-label coordinates followed by cocycle-parameter coordinates. For a collection of ordinary columns, let W be the intersection of the span of their lifts with this terminal space. The attainable cycle labels on the selected elements form A=W∩V, where V is the low (cycle-label) coordinate plane, and attainable cocycle labels supported on the complement form Q=(proj_high W)ᵖᵉʳᵖ. This follows by writing a lifted ordinary column as (v_e,p_e,r_e); to be a cycle its original v_e sum must vanish, while a cocycle disjoint from all selected elements is a functional annihilating their v_e span. Its prescribed values on the two high coordinate directions extend to such a functional precisely when they annihilate the intersection with that span. Thus the target is feasible on this specific support rectangle iff A contains (1,c) and Q contains (1,d) with c+d=s.

Even on the slice where W is a two-plane, every characteristic-two polynomial of degree at most two in its Plücker coordinates that vanishes on every infeasible rectangle also vanishes on every feasible rectangle. There are 35 planes. Exactly five are feasible for either fixed s: the whole low plane, and four lines with a one-dimensional cycle image and a one-dimensional cocycle image of compatible slopes. The six standard Plücker coordinates on W are the 2-by-2 minors of any basis matrix for W. Over GF(2) the basis choice does not affect them. Evaluate all six coordinates and their pairwise products (their squares equal themselves). Their binary span has rank 20 on all 35 planes and rank 20 after restricting to the 30 infeasible planes. Adjoining the constant one raises both ranks to 21. These exact finite computations are reproduced by check.py, enumerating the planes as the triples of nonzero vectors closed under XOR. The same ranks persist over any characteristic-two extension because the evaluation matrices are binary.

Disjointness of W from a fixed two-plane is a linear Plücker form, via the 4-by-4 determinant of the stacked bases. Thus even linear combinations of products of two such complementary-plane tests cannot yield a pointwise positive certificate supported on the five wanted states. The finite checker also verifies that products of three fixed-plane disjointness tests can have support contained in the good states, and a collection of these tests covers all five. This does not give a polynomial algorithm to enforce three simultaneous independence conditions on the same ordinary column set. It rules out a universal quadratic local simplification; global restrictions on the family of possible W in one instance remain available.
