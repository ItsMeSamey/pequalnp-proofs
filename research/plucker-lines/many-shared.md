# Any number of shared-only labels on one side

In a bipartite graph the left vertices are colored Pr with label (1,0,r), or shared-only with label (0,0,1). Right vertices Qr have label (0,1,r). A selected induced subgraph must have all degrees even and label sum (1,1,0), over GF(2). The statement is symmetric under swapping sides and the first two coordinates.

**Theorem.** Every feasible instance has an explicit certificate of size two, three, or six. These certificates can be found in polynomial time. When none exists, the standard vertex-selection equations admit a polynomial-calculus refutation over GF(2) of degree at most three.

A missing matched edge between Pr and Qr gives a two-vertex certificate. Assume both matched blocks are complete. Write U for the right vertices adjacent to every colored left vertex, and D for the remaining right vertices. Each j in D misses a colored neighbor i from its opposite class. If j also misses a shared-only vertex t, the independent triple i,j,t is a certificate. Hence assume each vertex in D sees every shared-only vertex.

If D is nonempty, fix such i,j. Write Ur = U ∩ Qr. Suppose a in U0 and b in U1 have incomparable neighborhoods among shared-only vertices. Choose t adjacent to a but not b and s adjacent to b but not a. Then i,a,t,j,s,b in cyclic order induce a six-cycle. Both shared-only labels cancel. On the right the odd color count is opposite to j's color and therefore matches i. This gives a target. After excluding these certificates, every pair in U0 × U1 has comparable shared-only neighborhoods.

## Degree-three refutation

Use Boolean variables x_i for colored left vertices, z_t for shared-only left vertices and y_j for right vertices. The guard equation for each vertex is its variable times the sum of its neighbors' variables, equated to zero. The three label equations assert that the sums of the respective label coordinates equal the target coordinates. Polynomial calculus uses linear combinations and multiplication by a variable. Multiplication by a linear form abbreviates a sum of variable multiples. All equations below are over GF(2); reducing a square v² to v uses the Boolean axiom of v. Squares of linear sums reduce termwise.

Let p_r, u_r and d_r be the selected sums in Pr, Ur and D ∩ Qr, and put c=Σ_t z_t, u=u0+u1, d=d0+d1. The target gives p0+p1=1 and u+d=1. If D is empty, every colored vertex sees every right vertex. Its guard and the right-label equation imply x_i=0, contrary to p0+p1=1. This already gives a degree-two refutation.

Otherwise for j in U define Z_j as the sum of its shared-only neighbors' variables. Its guard simplifies to y_j Z_j = y_j using p0+p1=1. For each shared-only vertex t let A_t and B_t denote the sums of its neighbors in U0 and U1 respectively. Its guard reads z_t(d+A_t+B_t)=0. Summing these guards and using the U equations gives cd+u=0. Substitute d=1+u and multiply by c. Using c²=c, the resulting cubic equation reduces to c=0. The same equations yield u=0 and d=1.

The t guard now gives z_t(1+A_t+B_t)=0. Multiplying by A_t and reducing A_t²=A_t gives z_t A_t B_t=0. For each a in U0 and b in U1 let Z_ab denote the selected sum of their common shared-only neighbors. By comparability it equals Z_a or Z_b. Multiplying the corresponding U equation by the other right variable gives y_a y_b Z_ab = y_a y_b. Sum over all such pairs. The sum of the left sides is Σ_t z_t A_t B_t=0. Hence u0 u1=0. Multiplying u0+u1=0 by u0 and reducing its square gives u0=u1=0.

For each colored vertex the U contribution to its guard is x_i u=0. For j in D the shared-only contribution is y_j c=0. Thus the restricted graph on the colored left vertices and D satisfies every degree equation. Summing those equations for P0 together with D ∩ Q1 cancels internal edges twice and leaves crossing terms p0 d0+p1 d1=0, using completeness of the matched blocks. The three label equations now give p0+p1=d0+d1=1 and p1=d1. Thus p0=d0 as well. Replacing d0,d1 by p0,p1 in the crossing equation and reducing squares gives p0+p1=0, a contradiction. Substitutions in these steps multiply prior equations by at most enough variables to reach degree three. All sums and products displayed can be expanded using polynomially many elementary inferences.

The six-vertex bound is sharp. Take just the six-cycle above with i in P0, j in Q1, a in Q0, b in Q1. Since i is the only colored left vertex, a target must contain i. With no shared-only vertex the unique Q0 vertex a must be selected, but it would then have degree one. With one selected shared-only vertex, every selected right column has equal incidence into i and that shared vertex. The common nonneighbors form the empty set, while any selected common neighbors must be even in number for i to have even degree, contradicting the odd total of selected right vertices. With both shared vertices active, every selected right column has degree two, and the left parity equations on the three columns force all three right choices equal. The triangle of two-element columns thus requires all six vertices.

The independent checker enumerates all feasible supports on the test graphs and validates every returned certificate. On rejected samples it separately closes the guard and label equations under degree-three multilinear polynomial calculus and checks that the constant one is derivable. Run `python -B check_many_shared.py` in this directory.
