# Two or more nonisolated shared components

Use the labels, target and even induced degree conditions of [the two-sided shared-only problem](two-sided-shared.md). The graph on the shared-only vertices can now have arbitrary edges. A nonisolated shared component means a connected component containing at least one shared-to-shared edge. Shared vertices incident to no such edge are called isolates; they can still have colored neighbors. Assume there are at least two nonisolated shared components.

**Theorem.** Feasibility and witness search are deterministic polynomial time. The algorithm checks explicitly bounded certificates and induced cycles through two colored endpoints, optionally with one isolated vertex in the selected subgraph. The path certificates can have arbitrary length. Isolates of the shared graph are retained in the argument.

First check for matched nonedges and independent target triples consisting of one colored vertex on each side and one shared-only vertex. Thus both colored matched blocks are complete. Write D_R for the colored right vertices having a colored nonneighbor, and D_L symmetrically. Every vertex in D_R sees every shared-only left vertex: otherwise its colored nonneighbor (opposite color), a shared nonneighbor and the vertex itself form an independent target triple. Mirror this observation for D_L. Either both D sets are empty or neither is.

If a shared component contacts a colored left vertex i and colored right vertex j of the same color, i,j are adjacent. In the graph induced by i,j and the component, delete their edge and find a shortest i-to-j path. Restoring the edge gives an induced target cycle. The interior has an even number of shared vertices by bipartiteness. The same construction for adjacent i,j of *opposite* colors gives a cycle that becomes a target upon adjoining a shared vertex from another component or an isolate with no neighbor on the cycle. For a shared right vertex outside that component it suffices that it miss i, and for a left vertex that it miss j. Search over endpoint pairs and components by breadth-first search. Assume no such certificates from now on.

## Uniform colored neighborhoods on the nonisolated components

If D_R is nonempty, every nonisolated component contacts every vertex of D_L and D_R. The absence of same-color contact cycles means that all D_L have a common color a and all D_R the other color b. In every nonisolated component the colored left contacts all have color a, and the colored right contacts all have color b.

Consider a colored left vertex i with a shared right neighbor s in a nonisolated component C. If i belongs to D_L it already sees every shared right vertex. Otherwise i is adjacent to all colored right vertices. Fix j in D_R and a shared neighbor t of s in C. The four vertices i,j,t,s induce a square, and i,j have opposite colors. If any shared right vertex h outside C misses i, it is isolated on this square, yielding a target of size five. Thus i sees every shared right vertex outside C, including all shared isolates. Take a neighbor of i in a second nonisolated component and repeat to see that i is also adjacent to every shared right vertex in C. The mirrored argument applies on the right.

If D_R is empty, the colored block is complete. In any feasible support every selected colored vertex has odd degree into opposite shared vertices. The total number of selected colored-right to shared-left incidences is odd because the selected colored-right count is odd. Summing guards on the selected shared-left vertices shows that the total number of selected shared-to-shared edges is odd. Hence some component C has odd selected shared-edge count. Summing the even degree equations separately on its selected left and right vertices shows that C contacts colored vertices on both shores. If no such bi-contact component exists the instance is infeasible.

Choose colored endpoints i,j touching this bi-contact component. They have opposite colors by the previous cycle test, and are adjacent. Without a cycle plus an isolated vertex, i must see all shared right vertices outside C and j all shared left vertices outside C. Thus every other nonisolated component now contacts both shores. In particular every nonisolated component is bi-contact. Given any colored left i' touching any such component C', choose a colored right contact in C'. The same argument shows i' sees all shared right vertices outside C'. Repeat with another nonisolated component to get every shared right vertex in C' as well. Mirror this proof on the right. All colored contacts on each side have a common color, opposite to those on the other side, since every such contact now touches all nonisolated components and matching colors would give a cycle.

In both cases let P⁺ and Q⁺ be the colored vertices touching any nonisolated shared vertex. They have respective opposite colors a and b, and each sees every opposite shared vertex, including isolates. Their complements P⁻ and Q⁻ among colored vertices have no nonisolated shared neighbors. Every minus vertex sees all opposite colored vertices since the D sets are contained in the plus sets.

## Compressing the nonisolated part of a witness

Fix the selected colored vertices and isolates from any witness. All shared left vertices in nonisolated components have the same external degree parity α (selected Q⁺ parity). The analogous right parity β is the selected P⁺ parity. A replacement selected subgraph in the nonisolated part must have internal degree α on each selected left vertex and β on each selected right vertex. It need only preserve left and right selection parities there, denoted L,R: colored vertices either see the entire appropriate nonisolated shore or none, and isolates do not see any of it.

* For α=β=0 any pair (L,R) is realized by taking at most one vertex per shore, chosen from different nonisolated components.
* For α=1,β=0 summing edge counts gives L=0. Take the empty selection or a single right vertex to realize R. Mirror for α=0,β=1.
* For α=β=1 edge counts give L=R. Use the empty selection for parity zero and the two endpoints of one shared edge for parity one.

Unless the replacement uses one edge, all remaining selected shared vertices, including the selected isolates, have an empty mutual block. Apply the [empty-block theorem](two-sided-shared.md) to the induced graph obtained by keeping all colored vertices and just these shared vertices. Its positive certificate (size two, three or six) is also valid in the original graph. This inference establishes the certificate bound without searching for the hypothetical support: the algorithm can directly enumerate these small certificates in the full graph.

Suppose the replacement uses one shared edge. Both plus classes have odd selected parity, and the minus classes have even selected parity. Every selected vertex of Q⁻ needs odd degree into selected shared-left isolates because the colored-left selection parity is odd and Q⁻ has no nonisolated shared neighbors. Every selected shared-left isolate needs odd degree into selected Q⁻ because it sees every vertex of the odd selected Q⁺. Summing these two sets of equations shows that the selected shared-left isolate count equals the selected Q⁻ parity, hence is even. The analogous right isolate count is even. The total shared-only parity is therefore even.

Here is a separate fact about this bipartite graph between selected Q⁻ vertices and selected isolates, in which all degrees are odd. If the two Q⁻ color parities are both odd, some opposite-color pair of its vertices has incomparable isolate neighborhoods. To prove this, for a selected isolate t let A_t,B_t be its two colored degree parities. Their sum is one, so A_t B_t=0. If all opposite-color pairs have comparable isolate neighborhoods, the common-neighbor count of each pair is odd because it equals the degree of the smaller neighborhood. Summing over selected opposite-color pairs, their number is even, since the parity of the same sum of common-neighbor counts is Σ_t A_t B_t=0. This contradicts both color counts being odd. Incomparability on the selected isolates implies incomparability in the full graph on shared isolates. The same holds with shores exchanged.

Only the two plus classes give odd color contributions if both minus classes have even counts in each color. Since the plus colors are opposite and the total shared-only parity is even, this would violate the third target coordinate. Thus at least one minus shore has odd counts in both colors, and supplies an incomparable opposite-color pair by the preceding argument.

When D_R is nonempty such a pair on Q⁻ already gives the [one-sided six-cycle](many-shared.md) by using its two distinguishing isolates, a D_R vertex and a colored nonneighbor of that D_R vertex. Mirror the construction on P⁻. When D_R is empty, take q0,q1 in Q⁻ of opposite colors and shared-left isolates t0,t1 giving a matching to q0,q1. Choose i in P⁺, j in Q⁺, and two endpoints of a shared edge. These four form a square. Add q0,q1,t0,t1. Each added q sees i and its own t; each added t sees j and its own q. The original i,j each gain two neighbors; the shared edge endpoints gain none. The four shared labels cancel, and the opposite colors q0,q1 correct the opposite colors of i,j. These eight vertices form a target. The mirror case gives the same bound.

Testing all small vertex sets up to size eight, together with the polynomial path checks above, therefore suffices. Alternatively the explicit configurations can be generated directly. This proof gives no classification of a single connected nonisolated shared component with arbitrary adjacency.
