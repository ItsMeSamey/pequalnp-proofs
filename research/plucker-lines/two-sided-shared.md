# Shared-only vertices on both sides with an empty or complete mutual block

Consider a bipartite graph with colored left classes Pr carrying (1,0,r), colored right classes Qr carrying (0,1,r), and additional vertices on either side carrying (0,0,1). The target label sum is (1,1,0), and every selected vertex must have even induced degree. Assume the block between the two sets of shared-only vertices is either empty or complete.

**Theorem.** Every feasible instance has a witness of size two, three, four, or six, discoverable in polynomial time. Infeasible instances have polynomial-calculus refutations over GF(2) of degree at most three for the empty block and at most four for the complete block. The six-vertex one-sided example still applies.

Use the Boolean guard and label equations of [the one-sided theorem](many-shared.md). Write l and r for the parity sums of the left and right shared-only selection variables. Colored left and colored right selection parities are both one. A missing matched edge gives a size-two target, so assume both matched blocks complete. Define U_R to be the right colored vertices adjacent to all left colored vertices, and D_R its complement among right colored vertices. Define U_L and D_L symmetrically. D_R and D_L are simultaneously empty or nonempty. If j in D_R misses a shared-only left vertex t, choose a colored left nonneighbor i of j. Completeness makes i's color opposite to j's, so i,j,t is an independent target triple. Without triples every D_R column sees all shared-only left vertices, and symmetrically on the other side.

When D_R is nonempty, exclude size-six targets exactly as in the one-sided theorem. If two U_R vertices of opposite colors have incomparable neighborhoods among shared-only left vertices, take these two vertices, two distinguishing shared-only left vertices, a vertex j in D_R, and a colored left nonneighbor i of j. This gives an induced target six-cycle. Therefore opposite-color pairs in U_R have comparable shared-only neighborhoods. The same holds on the left.

## The empty shared-only block

Let u_R and d_R be the selected sums on U_R and D_R. For each j in U_R the guard reduces to y_j Z_j=y_j, where Z_j sums its shared-only left neighbors. Summing the guards of the shared-only left vertices and using those equations gives u_R+l d_R=0. Since d_R=1+u_R, multiplication by l and Boolean square reduction give l=0, hence u_R=0 and d_R=1. If D_R is empty, the original identity already reads 1=0. On the other side the same calculation gives r=u_L=0 and d_L=1.

For nonempty D_R, apply the per-color part of the one-sided proof on both sides. Explicitly, for shared-only left vertex t let A_t,B_t be the sums of its neighbors of colors zero and one in U_R. Its guard is z_t(1+A_t+B_t)=0, as d_R=1. Multiplication by A_t gives z_t A_t B_t=0. For opposite-color a,b in U_R, comparability identifies their shared-neighbor intersection sum Z_ab with either Z_a or Z_b. Thus y_a y_b Z_ab=y_a y_b. Summing over all such pairs gives u_R0 u_R1=Σ_t z_t A_t B_t=0, where u_Rs is the selected sum of color s in U_R. Since u_R0+u_R1=0, both sums vanish. Likewise u_L0=u_L1=0.

Restrict to D_L and D_R. Each D_L vertex loses selected neighbor parity r+u_R=0, since it sees all right shared-only vertices and every U_R vertex. Symmetrically each D_R vertex loses parity l+u_L=0. Both colors retain their selection parities on both sides and the shared-only contribution to the third label vanishes. Thus the resulting degree equations have a pure target with complete matched blocks. Summing the equations on color-zero left vertices and color-one right vertices gives the crossing parity p0 q0+p1 q1=0, where p_s,q_s are their respective color sums on the restricted sides. The target gives p0=q0, p1=q1 and p0+p1=1. Substitution and Boolean square reduction give the contradiction 1=0. These are polynomial-size derivations of degree at most three.

## The complete shared-only block

In the summed left shared-only equations the complete block contributes lr. Consequently u_R+l(1+u_R+r)=0. Multiplication by l and Boolean square reduction give l+lr=0. The mirrored equations give r+rl=0, so l=r in degree at most three.

In the branch l=0, also r=0. In each guard the extra complete-block term vanishes because it is the variable of a shared-only vertex times the sum on the opposite shared-only side. All empty-block derivations now apply, including the D_R empty case.

In the branch l=1, also r=1. There must be a graph edge from a shared-only left vertex to a colored right vertex: without any such edge every left shared-only guard reduces to z_t r=z_t=0, contradicting l=1. The same argument forces some edge from a shared-only right vertex to a colored left vertex. If a colored right endpoint and a colored left endpoint of these two types have the same color, take the two edges and their endpoints. Completeness of the matched block and shared-only block gives a target four-cycle. Thus in the absence of this certificate the two nonempty sets of available endpoint colors are disjoint. There are only two colors, so all right colored endpoints have one color b and all left colored endpoints have color 1−b.

Every Q(1−b) vertex therefore has no shared-only left neighbor and belongs to U_R, since each D_R vertex sees all shared-only left vertices and that side is nonempty. Its guard becomes y_j times the full colored-left sum, forcing y_j=0. Likewise every P_b selection variable vanishes. All selected colored vertices on the left then have color 1−b, and all on the right have color b. Each side has odd colored size, so their third-coordinate sum is one. The shared-only vertices contribute l+r=0. This contradicts the third target coordinate.

The l=0 branch uses degree three, and the l=1 branch uses degree at most three (including the preliminary derivation of l=r). To combine them in polynomial calculus, multiply the derivation in the first branch by 1+l and that in the second branch by l. The branch hypotheses become l(1+l)=0 in either case, a Boolean consequence of degree two. For an original axiom its multiple by the linear factor is derivable by variable multiplication and addition. Each original inference can be replayed on these multiples, costing at most one degree. The two conclusions 1+l=0 and l=0 sum to 1=0. This yields degree at most four and polynomial size.

The certificate search checks matched nonedges, independent triples, the two mirrored comparability failures, and, in the complete-block case, the matched four-cycle. Each positive case explicitly supplies its selected vertices.
