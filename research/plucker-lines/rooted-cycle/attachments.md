# Weighted attachments at a single vertex

Let B be the biconnected block containing a root edge st in a feasible graph. Each region attached at one vertex v of B can be treated independently, counting only its vertices other than v in its binary weight. With v excluded, the zero and singleton choices give both regional parities precisely when there is a marked outside vertex. With v selected, start from {v}. A marked nonneighbor can be added alone. Otherwise all regional marks lie among its neighbors. In each component of the region minus v, an even support requires evenly many selected neighbors of v, by summing the vertex degree equations. If those neighbors all have one weight per component, the regional parity is zero. If a component has two differently marked neighbors, take a shortest path between any such pair there. Its interior contains no other neighbor of v by minimality, so adding v yields an induced cycle whose non-v weight is one.

Thus a marked region is either flexible in both states of v or flexible only with v absent. The former yields both weights as soon as the root fiber is feasible. The latter also yields both if the root edge lies on a cycle after deleting v. If v is compulsory and the region is inflexible in that state, erase the region without changing attainable parities. These connectivity tests reduce weighted feasibility to the root block. Any induced even region contributes even degree at its own attachment by the degree sum. A disconnected component is handled by a marked singleton if any.

## A biconnected obstruction

Take bipartite row masks [29,18,17,30] on four left and five right vertices indexed from zero. Root L0–R0 and mark only L1. This graph is biconnected. The full support is even and has weight one. Every induced root cycle and each of its spectators avoids L1: R0 has precisely two neighbors L0,L2 and L2 has precisely two neighbors R0,R4. All must lie on the root cycle. Since R4 neighbors L0, these four vertices form the entire induced root cycle. L1 neighbors R4 and cannot be a spectator. The subcubic hypothesis in the cycle-plus-spectator generating theorem is essential.
