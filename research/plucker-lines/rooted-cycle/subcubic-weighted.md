# Arbitrary weights at a rooted edge of a subcubic graph

Given a simple graph of maximum degree three, an edge st and a binary vertex weight λ, one can decide in deterministic polynomial time which parities are attained on induced even vertex sets containing s,t, and recover witnesses. Bipartiteness is unnecessary here.

In an induced even subgraph of a subcubic graph every nontrivial component is a chordless cycle. If the component of st is C, each selected vertex outside C is individually selectable as a spectator of C. Thus the pinned affine hull is generated by induced st-cycles and additions of individual nonneighbors to them. Put H=G−st. If s,t disconnect the pinned fiber is empty. Test for each marked h whether s,t connect in H−N_G[h]. If so, a shortest path gives a root cycle and toggling h realizes both weights. Otherwise there is no marked spectator, so it suffices to find an induced s–t path of prescribed weight in H.

Use Marx, O'Sullivan and Razgon, [Finding small separators in linear time via treewidth reduction, Lemma 2.11](https://www.cs.bme.hu/~dmarx/papers/marx-tw-reduction-talg.pdf), to find a set S containing all vertices of inclusion-minimal s–t separators in H of size at most three, with bounded treewidth of torso(H,S). Include s,t in S, adding at most two vertices to all bags if needed. Here the torso retains original edges within the set and completes the boundary of each complementary component into a clique. For each marked h distinct from and nonadjacent to the roots, N_H(h) separates s,t: deleting its neighbors isolates h and gives the same terminal connectivity as deleting N_H[h]. Thus a nonempty minimal separator inside N_H(h) lies in S. Every marked vertex is within distance one of S.

Put K equal to the closed radius-three neighborhood of S in H. Its torso still has bounded treewidth. For each complementary component D of the old set, its boundary B is a clique of the old torso and belongs to one bag. To expand by one step attach there a bag containing B and all vertices of D neighboring B. Its size is at most 4|B|. All new torso edges in D or from its added vertices to B are covered there. Repeat three times. Every virtual edge of the final torso has endpoints at distance three from S, and a connecting path with interior outside K. Thus both endpoints and connector avoid the closed neighborhoods of all marked vertices.

Take an induced terminal path P in H. Its ordered vertices in K form a path in the torso. If a torso edge connects two nonconsecutive projected vertices a,b, it is virtual: a real edge would be a chord of P. No marked h can appear between them in P, since replacing this original subpath by the outside connector gives an s–t walk avoiding N_H[h]. The retained prefix and suffix cannot touch h by inducedness and a,b are not its neighbors. This would yield a marked spectator. Shortening the projected path to an induced torso path on an ordered subsequence therefore preserves all marked vertices: apply the argument to each interval skipped by the final subsequence.

Conversely each induced terminal path in the torso lifts to one of equal weight in H. Keep its real edges and replace virtual edges by shortest routes through complementary components. Nonconsecutive path vertices cannot touch the same component, whose boundary is a clique. Thus the routes for different edges use different components, introduce no chord, and contain no marks.

Parity of selected marked vertices on an induced terminal path is expressible in counting monadic second-order logic, or by a bounded-treewidth path dynamic program with a parity bit. The torso width and formula are fixed; witness construction follows by reconstruction. To compute the affine hull, start from one witness and query a basis of the annihilator of the differences found so far. Each witnessed discrepancy increases the dimension, and when every basis query is constant the obtained hull is exact. The torso argument also solves the path-plus-spectator subproblem at every fixed degree bound Δ by taking separators of size Δ and increasing each enlarged bag size by at most factor Δ+1. Identifying this subproblem with the entire rooted induced-even fiber uses the degree-three assumption.
