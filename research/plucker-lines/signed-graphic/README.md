# Mixed anchored weights when contraction of one root is graphic

Let M be a represented binary matroid. Seek a cycle containing t and a disjoint cocycle containing s with a prescribed binary sum of additive weights on the two sets. Given an indexed graphic representation H of M/s, where s is a nonloop and t≠s, there is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm to decide either weight and recover witnesses. No assumption of constancy within fibers is needed. Duality gives the same conclusion when M*/t is graphic. Under constancy on both kinds of fibers, occurrence of both weights detects a weight-changing opposite-edge switch by the compatible-chart criterion. To recover the switch, reduce each returned pair to an anchored circuit and cocircuit inside its supports by Gaussian elimination and support minimization. These two individual-fiber reductions preserve the weight. Extend each resulting pair to its common fundamental basis, extend the basis path by the monotone single/double exchange procedure, and inspect every change. A weight change on that path yields the required double exchange, in polynomial time.

## Signs and fibers

Choose a linear functional a with a(s)=1 and put σ_e=a(v_e) on each ordinary edge of H. Any cocycle containing s has bit σ_e+p_u+p_v on an edge e=uv for some vertex potential p; differences from a annihilate s and hence are cuts of H. Call e consistent when σ_e=p_u+p_v. For fixed p the allowable primal cycles are precisely the even edge sets of the consistent graph containing t. Their sign sums vanish by telescoping, so their sums also vanish in the original representation. The dual weight m equals the known constant m_s+Σ_e m_eσ_e plus Σ_v w_v p_v, where w is the binary boundary of the ordinary edges with m_e=1. A loop contributes two ends and cancels in w. Write ℓ for the primal weights.

First test whether a single potential permits two different primal weights. If t belongs to a cycle in the consistent graph, take a spanning forest avoiding t and its fundamental cycle Q through t. Every cycle difference avoiding t decomposes into other fundamental cycles, so weight variation implies the existence of another fundamental cycle P of odd ℓ-weight. Their tree paths intersect in a path, one vertex or the empty set. Their two nonforest edges are distinct, with t only in Q. Their union is a theta, a figure-eight or two disjoint cycles.

Conversely, seek any such subdivision with t only in Q, σ(Q)=σ(P)=0, and ℓ(P)=1. In the theta the two distinct cycles are independent and the entire cycle space has dimension two; this is also true in the other two patterns. Thus both zero signs imply that the *whole union* is balanced, enabling a consistent potential on every edge of both cycles. Extend that potential arbitrarily to H. The two cycles Q and Q XOR P contain t and have opposite weights at the same potential.

This bounded-pattern test is polynomial. Treat the actual t edge as a named segment, splitting its pattern path and enumerating its orientation. Enumerate the constant number of resulting branch/anchor placements, segment labels in GF(2)², and which two cycles are Q,P. The labels record σ and ℓ. Find a subdivision with those labels by constant-size, parity-constrained, vertex-disjoint linkage. To handle loops and parallel edges, stretch every edge into a path of length three (a loop becomes a triangle) with its label on just one segment. Designate an internal segment of the t-chain, which necessarily lies on Q exactly when t does. The core can be made simple by enumerating two further distinct interior anchors per core segment after a further constant stretch. For a shared pattern vertex use a different terminal port for each link, copying incident host edges to its ports. Since all ports are terminals none can serve as another link's internal vertex. Direct host edges joining placed vertices cannot be reused by two links because the pattern is simple. Reserve the designated t segment before testing the other links. Equivalently use the topological-containment formulation with these rooted placements. [Liu and Yoo, Theorem 1.3 and Corollary 1.4](https://people.tamu.edu/~yyoo/stoc25.pdf#page=3)

If the test succeeds both global weights are attainable. If it fails, each fixed-potential fiber has one primal weight, so replacing its cycle by the fundamental t-cycle preserves the objective. It suffices to search balanced simple t-cycles and choose potentials on their vertices and freely elsewhere. A loop t with σ_t=1 is inconsistent for every potential. If t is a loop of sign zero the simple-cycle stage uses just t with arbitrary potentials. The two-cycle test still applies, with Q the loop and P an odd-weight balanced cycle avoiding t.

## A signed path with free off-path potentials

For a nonloop t=xy, delete t and seek a simple x–y path, prescribing p_x,p_y with p_x+p_y=σ_t. Every traversed edge must be consistent; costs are the ℓ-weights on its edges plus Σ_v w_v p_v on *all* vertices. Add ℓ_t and the constant dual contribution at the end. Test initial path feasibility by a single prescribed-sign path query. Restrict to the biconnected block containing t; if a positive vertex outside that block exists and there is a path, both costs occur by flipping its potential. A bridge t has no cycle.

Use the SPQR decomposition of this block rooted at t. A network with poles a,b stores all attainable binary costs on a simple a–b traversal for each fixed (p_a,p_b). Internal vertex costs include free off-path potentials, and exclude the pole charges. Also record whether the child contains any positive internal vertex. If a child is unused, its possible contributions are zero or both values according to this flag. For a given endpoint difference traversal feasibility is invariant under simultaneous complementation of all potentials. A real edge checks its sign and charges its ℓ. At a series node combine children along the path and charge each intervening pole once. At a parallel node take one traversal and the unused contributions of the rest. This is the usual decomposition into polygons, bonds and rigid skeletons. [Gutwenger and Mutzel](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-44541-2_8)

## Rigid skeleton computation

Include its parent reference edge ab when discussing skeleton connectivity, and exclude this edge in every traversal. Each other edge represents a (nonempty) two-terminal network. For a sign-only query give it parallel copies for each possible sign, ignoring costs. Fix pole potentials and β=p_a+p_b. If there is no simple a–b path of sign β return the empty table entry.

A feature is a positive internal skeleton vertex or an edge whose child has a positive internal vertex. If deleting any one feature leaves a path of sign β, both costs are realized by flipping the potential of an unused positive vertex. Edge deletion removes all copies. With no features, traversal costs depend only on the endpoint difference, by simultaneous complementation, and all vertex weights are zero except possibly at the excluded poles. One simple-path query with sign and cost coordinates suffices, using parallel edges for feasible sign/cost pairs.

Otherwise fix one feature f. All β-paths use it. Removing f from the simple 3-connected skeleton, *with* its reference edge, leaves a 2-connected graph. This holds for deletion of a single edge as well as a nonterminal vertex. After removing the reference edge, all a–b paths have the same sign, opposite to β. Every block in this graph lies on the entry–exit block-cut route: an off-route branch would remain separated at its attachment vertex even after restoring ab. In a 2-connected unbalanced signed block, any two distinct entry and exit vertices can be connected with both signs. Link them disjointly to distinct points of an unbalanced cycle using vertex Menger, truncate at first contact, and use the two arcs. A pair of opposite-signed parallel edges gives both signs in a two-vertex block. Hence every block on the route is balanced, since choices in different blocks are independent. A spanning-tree switch therefore makes all residual edge signs zero. This includes sign-option copies, so every surviving skeleton edge has only one allowed sign.

Enumerate the orientation of a traversed feature edge, or the two distinct incident edges in order for a feature vertex. Guess their terminal potentials and local costs. After removing the reserved feature traversal, there are two disjoint arcs connecting the poles to its attachments. On each arc the switched potential is a known constant. Undo the switch vertex by vertex to compute charges and permitted child-edge costs for that arc. These are additive and do not depend on traversal direction. All unused child interiors and unvisited skeleton vertices now have zero weight, since every sign-β path meets every feature.

Test these two disjoint paths and the desired sum by enumerating their individual binary cost targets. Strict-interior vertex charges c_v can be recorded by giving each edge uv the unoriented length c_u+c_v modulo four. The sum on a path with ends i,j equals c_i+c_j+2Σ_internal c_v modulo four. Charge prescribed vertices directly, once per vertex. Arcs can have different allowable costs on the same skeleton edge. Put a parallel copy for each pair in the Cartesian product of the two sets of allowed costs, and give the two arcs separate label coordinates. Both sets are nonempty because the fixed switched potential on either arc satisfies the unique residual sign. A path constrains just its own coordinates. If its endpoints coincide it is an empty arc; reserve that vertex from use by the other arc. Distinct arcs cannot share a vertex. Remove all reserved feature vertices from arc interiors. With both arcs empty, simply check the reserved traversal. Child completions then glue independently at the chosen potentials.

Liu–Yoo Theorem 1.3 uses a fixed number of disjoint paths, finite abelian groups with unoriented edge sums, and a fixed bound on the number of forbidden values per coordinate. All calls here are of this form: demand a residue by excluding all other group elements; the groups are GF(2) and Z/4. Parallel options can each be replaced by a distinct two-edge path with the label on one edge, so the linkage input may be simple; internal subdivision vertices cannot be used by a different option. Backpointers recover paths, potentials and cycles. All table sizes are constant, the number of skeletons is linear, and each has only polynomially many attachment guesses. This establishes the claimed polynomial algorithm for the supplied graphic contraction.

## Finite replay

Run `python3 check.py`. On 300 seeded loopless signed graphs the checker compared direct enumeration of potentials and even sets against the rank-two union test and simple-cycle reduction. On 240 rigid skeletons it compared all four pole-potential entries against direct path enumeration, using child tables with sign and cost alternatives. All 960 entries agreed, including 240 with a mandatory feature and a nonempty path family. The small checker enumerates linkages and starts from supplied rigid skeletons; it does not implement the general SPQR decomposition or the large-instance Liu–Yoo algorithm.
