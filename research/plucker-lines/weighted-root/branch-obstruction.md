# A rooted singleton weight can force unbounded branching in one block

For every k≥1 there is a 2-connected simple bipartite graph of maximum degree four with an edge st and a single marked vertex z such that the only even induced support containing s,t,z is the entire graph. It has 2k+1 degree-four vertices, all in one two-connected block. Thus even allowing an arbitrary bounded number of branching vertices in the root component does not generate every rooted affine hull.

A cell has entry triple (A,x,z), exit triple (D,w,y), and three interior vertices C,v,B. Give it three vertex-disjoint rail paths A–w, x–C–v–B–y, z–D, and two additional edges A–v, v–D. Identify the exit triple of each cell coordinatewise with the next entry triple. Only at the two ends add cap edges: A–x and A–z at the very first entry, D–w and D–y at the last exit. The prescribed edge is the first A–x, and only the first z has positive weight.

The graph is bipartite with every triple center A,D and every interior B,C on one shore, and all x,z,w,y,v on the other. The 3(k+1) interface vertices and 3k interior vertices are distinct except for the prescribed identifications. The k+1 interface centers and k vertices v have degree four; all remaining vertices have degree two. The rail paths concatenate, with a permutation at each interface, into three disjoint paths through all vertices. Both ends join the three rails by a star on their endpoints. After deleting an internal rail vertex the two remaining rails connect the two end stars, and the remaining pieces of the damaged rail reach an end. After deleting a rail endpoint, every remaining rail piece reaches the opposite intact end star. This proves two-connectivity already on the rail-and-cap subgraph.

Suppose an induced even support contains the three entry vertices of the first cell. Whenever a selected vertex has exactly one neighbor whose membership is unknown and all its other neighbors are selected, the remaining neighbor must be selected since the full degree is even. In each cell perform the forces x→C, z→D, C→v, v→B, B→y, A→w. The other neighbor of x or z is already known from the preceding cell or the initial cap. When v forces B, its other neighbors A,C,D are known. When A forces w, its neighbors behind the interface and v are known. None of the exit vertices needs to force within that cell. Thus the property propagates to the next entry triple and selects every vertex. Conversely the entire graph is an even support.

All rooted supports whose root component has fewer than 2k+1 branch vertices must therefore have zero at the marked coordinate. Their entire affine hull has zero there, whereas the full graph has one. The k=1 cell is the four-by-five obstruction with row masks 29,18,17,30, rooted at the first row and first column and marked at the third column.
