# Weighted induced cycles through a fixed edge, with an optional spectator

Given a finite simple graph G, edge st and binary vertex weights, one can find in deterministic polynomial time each attainable weight parity of an induced cycle through st, optionally augmented by one isolated vertex. This holds in arbitrary graphs. In graphs of maximum degree three it gives the full parity answer for every even induced support containing s and t, and constructs an affine basis from actual witnesses. Two-connectivity is unnecessary.

Delete st to form H. The cycles under consideration correspond exactly to induced s–t paths in H. First test for an s–t path. If none exists the family is empty. For each positive-weight vertex h, test for a path outside its closed neighborhood in G. If one exists, its shortest such path gives a cycle and adjoining h flips its parity. Otherwise every positive vertex separates the terminals by its closed neighborhood. Spectators of weight zero can be omitted.

Attach new paths of length five to s and t, ending at external terminals S and T. The induced S–T paths in this padded H contain the whole tails and correspond to the original induced s–t paths. Give the new vertices weight zero. On each tail designate its middle vertex as an artificial anchor, three steps from the corresponding original terminal, with its preceding and following neighbors prescribed. For every real positive-weight anchor u and both artificial anchors, let L(u) and R(u) be the respective components of S,T after deleting N[u]. They are distinct, including when u=s or u=t. A state is a triple (a,u,b) of consecutive possible path vertices through an anchor; a and b are distinct nonadjacent neighbors of u. At artificial anchors use the prescribed triples.

Consider any induced S–T path. Starting at the initial artificial anchor, take the next positive vertex at least three steps beyond the last anchor, repeating until there is none; finish at the final artificial anchor. The end padding ensures the last separation is at least three. Internal vertices between consecutive anchors have positive weight only possibly at offsets one and two after the earlier anchor. On a path through (a,u,b), the vertices strictly before a belong to L(u), and those strictly after b to R(u), since none can be adjacent to u. Thus for consecutive triples (a,u,b), (c,v,d), the first triple lies in L(v) and the second in R(u).

Use these two containment tests before constructing any transition. They imply L(u) is a strict subset of L(v): it is connected to S, disjoint and anticomplete to R(u), hence misses N[v], while u itself lies in L(v) only. Likewise R(v) is contained in R(u). This makes the transition graph acyclic. To build an arc enumerate the vertex r immediately after b (the second step after u). Allow r=c when the anchor separation is three. Require the prescribed local sequence a,u,b,r,...,c,v,d to be induced, and every vertex strictly between b and c to lie in R(u) ∩ L(v). All gap vertices after r must have weight zero, including c when distinct from r. For r=c check the six-vertex chain directly. Otherwise reject collisions and chords among the prescribed vertices apart from the permitted r–c edge. Delete all forbidden vertices, in particular other marked vertices and neighbors of a,u,b,v,d, from the open gap. Find a shortest r–c path through the remaining allowed interior. The resulting arc is an induced local path. Its parity contribution counts u,b,r and excludes v, so each weight on the final path is counted once.

Concatenation preserves inducedness. Suppose the existing path ends in (a,u,b) and its vertices strictly before a belong to L(u). The new vertices after b all lie in R(u) and have no edges to this earlier portion. Chords involving a,u,b are excluded by the local path test. In the resulting path the portion before c lies in L(v), by nesting, the containment condition for a,u,b and the open-gap test. This maintains the induction invariant. Conversely, the greedy anchors of any actual induced path pass all of these checks, and replacing an open gap by a shortest admissible path leaves its parity contribution unchanged. Dynamic programming records a representative for each attainable prefix parity in each state. There are polynomially many triples, pairs of triples and second-step choices; each gap requires only a graph search.

For any subcubic graph, the component of any nonempty induced even support containing st is, at st, a connected component of degree exactly two throughout. It is therefore an induced cycle. Every other selected vertex individually lies outside its closed neighborhood. Relative to that cycle, the support is the sum of the indicator of the cycle and the unit vectors of its selected spectators over GF(2). Its weight parity can be realized by keeping just the cycle if its other selected vertices have even total weight, or one positive spectator otherwise. Equivalently its indicator lies in the affine hull of the rooted cycles and cycles plus one spectator. The reverse containment is immediate. The same argument applies whenever the selected root component has maximum degree three, regardless of other selected components.

To construct an affine basis of the cycle/spectator family in any graph, choose a baseline support, start with an empty difference basis, and compute a basis of the orthogonal complement of the known differences. For each orthogonal basis weight, run the parity solver. If a returned witness differs from the baseline on that weight, add its independent difference and repeat. If none does, every true difference belongs to the known span. This takes O(n²) weighted calls and returns actual affinely independent supports. Claims about full even-support hulls in higher degree require separate generators.

The [solver](solver.py) implements the parity and affine-basis procedures. The [checker](check.py) compares both parity results and actual witnesses against brute force on all five-vertex graphs containing the fixed edge and all binary weights, plus seeded and long-cycle examples.
