# Rank-four trace and short ordinary contractions

Given boundary T of rank four with nonzero λ∈T* and nonzero p∈ker λ, put K=ker λ and project ordinary vectors to V/K, obtaining binary matroid N. The sum of the original vectors of each projected dependency is its gain in K.

For any ordinary set C whose span meets K nontrivially, impose d(C)=0 by contracting its actual ordinary members. If λ is nonzero on T∩span(C), reject this branch. Otherwise it descends to the projected boundary, now of rank at most three. Apply the rank-three trace solver. Its witness lifts by filling the residual with the contracted members, all killed by d. Conversely any genuine solution whose covector kills C descends.

For a true solution, its selected subset X sums to p and gives a dependency in N. Every ordinary column in the projected flat spanned by X is d-zero, since d annihilates K. If this flat's dependency space is generated by circuits of length at most L, some such circuit has nonzero gain; otherwise their sum X would have gain zero. Enumerate all ordinary projected circuits up to size L with nonzero gain and use the preceding contraction tests. Under the stated property on every projected flat this is a deterministic n^{O(L)}poly(input) solver. In particular in a chordal binary projected matroid chords recursively express circuits as sums of triangles in the same flat; include loops and parallel pairs in a nonsimple representation.

General preprocessing uses any pair of distinct ordinary vectors projecting to the same point modulo K. They have the same evaluation for every admissible d. Every true selection using either is covered by contracting both in an alternative branch. Once all these branches reject, delete all such projected parallel classes. Identical ordinary vectors may first be identified in the unweighted trace problem. A nonzero ordinary vector lying in K itself can always be contracted.

For a projection obstruction in any dimension ≥2, take T=V and let ordinary columns be E={v:δ₀(v)=1}, δ₀ nonzero. On EVERY proper subspace W⊂T all orthogonal (p,λ) are attainable. Extend λ to some d≠δ₀. If δ₀(p)=1 use the ordinary column p; otherwise for nonzero p choose v with d(v)=0 and δ₀(v)=1 and use v,v+p. Such v exists both when d=0 and when the two forms are independent. The empty selection handles p=0. The complete collection of proper-subspace tables matches the full projective point set. Yet at full T, δ₀ misses every nonzero target p in its kernel. In dimension four exactly seven of the eight duals allowed by each such p are viable.

## Cographic projected configuration

For fixed boundary rank k this pure trace is also polynomial when the neutral quotient N is given cographically. After testing p in the ordinary span, choose a splitting of that ambient span along K to label each ordinary projected edge with its K gain. If a representative s of λ(s)=1 projects outside the ordinary span, its value is free and the gain problem is linear. Otherwise encode it by an edge subset L whose projected vector is s mod K; then the evaluation of s by a circulation is its edge inner product with L.

In a cographic representation, projected dependencies are the graph cuts, and projected functionals are graph circulations. Precompute at each vertex the sum of its incident edge gains (loops count twice). Then the K gain of a cut is the sum of the vertex weights on one shore. The existence of d with d(s)=1 disjoint from that cut means an L-odd simple cycle can be selected, since a binary circulation decomposes into circuits. All vertices of this cycle must lie on one shore. Flip the cut-color convention if necessary to put that cycle outside the chosen shore; the vertex gain sum is unaffected. Shrink the selected shore to at most dim K vertices while preserving its gain p; the same cycle still avoids them. Conversely such a vertex set defines a cut giving the target, and any odd cycle in its complement supplies d. Enumerate all vertex subsets of size ≤dim K with weights summing p and test signed-cycle unbalance by a graph search. This gives deterministic n^{O(k)}poly(input).

If the projected graph representation is graphic instead, projected dependencies are Eulerian edge sets with K gains; the residual d(s)=1 is a single vertex-weight test on a cut. A true Eulerian support can be pruned within its fixed monochromatic graph until cyclomatic rank ≤dim K: otherwise a nonempty subcycle has zero gain and may be removed. Its compressed topological core has O(k) branch vertices, pure cycles and edges. Any witnessing cut can retain its component colors on the support and at most one positive-color off-support vertex to preserve its parity. The known fixed-size group-labeled disjoint-paths construction for a graphic matroid handles edge gains, and vertex contributions via the endpoint-corrected Z₄ path sum. Thus this is another deterministic fixed-k case even if the original represented matroid before projection is nongraphic.
