# Four arbitrary mixed markers reduce to biconnected blocks

Take a bipartite homogeneous guard graph with four-bit vertex labels and target p. Split at v into lobes A,B sharing only v. Assign v's label s to A and label zero to the B copy. Denote the local score sets at pin h by A_h,B_h and their open label spans by U_A,U_B. The global supports correspond exactly to pairs of feasible local supports agreeing at v. Summing guards over each open lobe gives zero local contribution to v's guard when it is selected, proving necessity; sufficiency follows by adding the two local contributions. If an open lobe has zero label span, remove it and keep the original label of v on the retained lobe: the empty set or singleton v extends any local model.

With both open lobes marker-active, first test p in A_0+B_0. Membership of any pair of syndromes in an open lobe is queried by three markers, projecting along the difference. A set with at least two missing points is recovered exactly, with witnesses for every member; if no pair misses, the obtained witnesses omit at most one point. Both open sets have at least two points; hence dense plus active gives every target, and otherwise exact tables suffice. A negative answer implies p+B_0 disjoint from A_0, and p belongs to neither set since zero belongs to both.

The pin-one meeting points belong to V=(s+U_A) intersect (p+U_B). Every pair in V is queryable in both A_1 and p+B_1. Indeed every h in U_B can be written as the sum of one label combination from each open bipartite shore. Choosing just one shore attains each combination at pin zero. Thus B_0 contains b,b+h, so A_0 misses c=p+b and c+h. For a nonzero difference h in V, to test whether A_1 meets {a,a+h}, query the four joint (pin,score) outputs (1,a),(1,a+h),(0,c),(0,c+h). They form an affine plane in five dimensions specified by three equations, and both pin-zero outputs are impossible. The reverse argument provides every needed pair test in B after translation by p.

If V is empty, reject. If it has size at least four, pair recovery now computes each restricted pinned set exactly or produces witnessed points missing at most one. Exact versus exact, dense versus two or more witnessed partners, and empty sets are settled immediately. The sole possible ambiguity is exact singleton versus one possibly missing dense point: this needs one membership query on one lobe, while holding the witness for the other lobe. For size one, ask membership of the point in both sides (AND). For size two, query the whole pair on both sides. Reject if either side is empty, accept if witnessed points agree, and otherwise test whether either side also contains the opposite point (OR). Each case asks at most one point membership on each lobe.

Implement the unresolved local query a in A_1 by replacing v's local label by its old label plus p+a and solving ordinary four-marker target p on A alone. At pin zero the target was already impossible, while at pin one the new test asks for precisely a. Do the same for B with its local score untranslated. The copies of v need no explicit fifth marker and accept arbitrary subsequent changes to their labels. Retrieved local witnesses glue through the stored partner. Recursing asks at most one hard question per child lobe. The children partition the parent's edges, and each split partitions its blocks into two nonempty subfamilies. Thus there are linearly many recursive nodes, with polynomially many three-marker computations at each, and each biconnected block receives at most one ordinary four-marker query. A class supporting arbitrary four-marker rows on every block therefore supports arbitrary four-marker rows on its block trees.

If both open label spans are the full four-space, the query always terminates at this split. Here V is the full group. A singleton pinned image would make every open vertex on v's shore have label zero, because it can be selected alongside v. The entire open label span then lies on the opposite shore and is attained at pin zero, contradicting failure of the pin-zero branch. Both pinned images have at least two points and the pair tests decide unconditionally.

## Articulations after changing the fundamental basis

In a connected binary matroid's fundamental graph, deleting a graph vertex e gives a fundamental graph for the matroid contraction M/e when e is basic, and for the deletion M minus e when it is nonbasic. Consequently a basis exposing a cut at e exists exactly when one of these two elementary minors is disconnected. Each candidate and a suitable basis can be found by Gaussian elimination. Recompute the four score rows under the chosen basis by expressing the same primal cycles and dual covectors in its coordinates. Repeat on actual child instances with their changed labels. The potential number of vertices minus one adds across the two lobes and decreases under a one-sided removal; thus the number of recursive hard calls is linear even if each child first changes basis.

As an actual residual, check_residual_pivots.py tests the 11-vertex split-plane candidate obtained independently by the proofsystems group. All 22 single-element minor statuses remain connected. The group's subsequent direct signed circuit/cocircuit enumeration covers this NO candidate at cutoff four. The articulation criterion itself does not resolve it.
