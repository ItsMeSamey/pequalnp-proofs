# Rectangular limb elimination for a protected four-space

Let ordinary columns partition into A and B. Write V_A=span(A,T), V_B=span(B), W=V_A∩V_B. Assume dim T=4 and dim W≤3. The prescribed trace is (p,λ) on T. The rank-three trace algorithm computes the table L of attainable pairs (w,δ) on W for B. A global witness is exactly an exterior subset with sum p+w killed by an exterior functional restricting to λ on T and δ on W, for some (w,δ) in L. This follows by gluing the functionals on their intersection and taking the union of the subsets.

Enumerate all products (a+H)×{δ:δ|J=η} contained in L, where H,J are linear subspaces of W. Each nonempty product satisfies H⊆J and a∈J, since varying δ arbitrarily on W/J must preserve orthogonality to every member of a+H. Also η(H)=0 and η(a)=0. Discard cells whose δ disagree with λ on T∩W (alternatively restrict attention to that subset of L before enumeration). For a surviving product require λ and η agree on T∩J. In V_A/H seek an exterior subset with flux (p+a)+H and functional equal on (T+J)/H to the descend of λ and η. An exterior solution lifts precisely when its flux belongs to p+a+H. Its functional can be glued to the B functional for the matching local state; restricting the lifted functional to W automatically gives a member of the allowed dual coset. Conversely an original solution using the product passes the quotient test. The new protected rank is dim(T+J)−dim H.

Thus if rectangles with this rank at most three cover all locally viable cells consistent with λ, polynomially many rank-three queries (indeed boundedly many for a given cut) decide the rank-four instance. Positive rectangle tests are always sound, even for an incomplete cover. H need not be spanned by ordinary elements: filling the flux error in H uses the table property. Quotienting arbitrary directions without this property could give spurious solutions.

For W⊆T, the only relevant δ is λ|W, and the feasible fluxes S contain zero. If S={0}, delete B. Otherwise cover S by {0,w} for nonzero w∈S with fixed δ; each quotient reduces rank to three. Any number of such limbs may be processed. Every nonzero w solves the rank-three test against the original exterior including the other limbs. The singleton case simply erases a limb and preserves the original four-space.

## Connected component algorithm without an adhesion bound

Compute the connected components E_i of the vector matroid represented by E modulo T (loops each form their own component). Their spans modulo T are a direct sum. Let S_i be the set of fluxes in T achievable by a subset of E_i killed by a functional extending the fixed λ. The full flux image is the sum of the S_i: any global subset summing into T splits into local subsets summing into T, and any local functionals agreeing on T extend jointly because the ordinary spans are in direct sum modulo T. Each S_i contains zero and is contained in K=ker λ.

For every pair of distinct a,b in K, test whether S_i meets {a,b} by projecting along the line through a+b and querying the rank-three trace with target a modulo that line, ordinary block E_i alone. Its flux lifts to either a or b, both acceptable. The union of endpoints of all negative pairs is exactly K minus S_i whenever that complement has at least two elements. If there is no negative pair, at most one point is missing and the block is dense. This distinguishes inactive blocks S_i={0} from active blocks and recovers every nondense block exactly.

If all blocks are nondense, add their exact tables on the eight-element group. If a dense block and another active block both occur, their sum equals K, since the latter supplies two distinct translates of the former. If a single dense block occurs and every other is inactive, discard the inactive blocks and retain just that connected block. Thus every instance with two active components in the quotient has polynomial-time decision, and disconnected instances with a single active component reduce to it. The reasoning generalizes to protected rank k≥3 given the rank-(k−1) oracle.

The same rule applies dually. First restrict T to T∩span E if necessary (reject p outside the ordinary span). Give T a named basis B with both independent and coindependent columns in M=E∪B. Local feasibility is existence of a cycle and cocycle in M with terminal coordinates respectively p and λ, whose ordinary parts are disjoint. Duality exchanges these terminals and roles. The quotient components in the dual are those of (M delete B)*, the same component partition as the original ordinary matroid. Reapplying the two rules when they shrink the ground set leaves an irreducible case where both E and E modulo T are connected, or terminates at rank at most three.

Witnesses are constructive. From each positive pair query, lift the actual returned subset and record its original flux and functional. For a nondense block every true point is recorded since one can pair it with an absent point. For a dense block the recorded points meet every pair, hence omit at most one point. The recorded dense set plus any other recorded active set therefore already covers K. Combine stored local witnesses for the requested flux.

Both connectivity tests can leave a genuine one-hole obstruction. Set T to the low four bits in binary seven-space, λ=bit zero, p=2, and take E=[53,70,79,91,94,99,100,118,125]. Both the raw and projected matroids are connected. At fixed λ the achievable points are exactly 0,4,6,8,10,12,14. For a direct NO certificate, for d=1+16j in increasing order use the separating covectors 123,107,38,66,94,66,63,98, respectively. Each separator evaluates to one on p and zero on all ordinary columns killed by its d.

A general NO certificate is a vector-valued function c(x) on affine parameters x for d=d₀+Lx, satisfying c(x)(p)=1 and c(x)(e)=0 whenever d(e)=0. These equations are necessary by linear separation and sufficient even for arbitrary functions c. For any fixed degree k, fitting a multilinear polynomial c is deterministic polynomial time. Write its unknown coefficients and restrict each constraint to an affine parametrization of the relevant hyperplane (or whole parameter space, or the empty set). Multilinearize and equate coefficients. All equations are linear in the unknowns; construction takes input-size to the O(k+1) power. On the connected example, no constant or affine selector exists, but a quadratic selector does, as checked by separator_degree.py. There is no general bounded-degree guarantee here.

## Four fully mixed markers on disconnected guard graphs

The component addition argument also applies directly to four arbitrary mixed markers H on a bipartite homogeneous guard graph. For each connected component i let S_i⊂F₂⁴ be its attainable syndromes. Disjointness of the components gives Minkowski addition, and each component admits the zero support. For two distinct syndromes a,b, project H along a+b and test the projected target by the three-marker theorem. The same pair recovery constructs every attainable syndrome of a nondense component and at least fifteen syndromes of a dense one. If all components are nondense or at least two components are active, witness reconstruction by adding local tables is complete. Otherwise the sole dense active component is an equivalent connected four-marker instance. Here marker-active means that the component contains a vertex of nonzero label, since each singleton support is feasible. All statements also hold for any product of solution sets with an additive syndrome and an oracle for its three-dimensional linear projections.
