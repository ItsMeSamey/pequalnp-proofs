# Two-score rigidity in graphic and cographic matroids

For fixed score dimension four, any set of ordinary positions necessarily primal at attainable q and necessarily dual at attainable r has universally bounded size in a graphic or cographic raw matroid.

Reduce by ordinary deletion (primal zero) or contraction (dual zero) while retaining both scores. Every specified position survives. At termination each operation at every remaining position loses a score. They must lose opposite scores by disjointness. Thus every q witness has the same ordinary support pair (A,B), partitioning all positions, and every r witness the reverse. The projected constrained primal and dual codes are the raw minor's cycle and cocycle spaces. Take linear sections of each projection; independent kernel shifts are allowed because on each eliminated edge one side was fixed zero. The total score is the sum of the residual linear cycle and cut scores and an independent hidden summand, which we freeze to its witness value.

Represent the graphic minor by G without isolated vertices. Both A and B are Eulerian. Cycles supported on A inject into four score bits: toggling a nonzero zero-gain cycle preserves q against B. Similarly B has cycle rank at most four using r. Hence G has at most eight components. The cut variations vanishing on A inject into four score bits, by the same q uniqueness. Their dimension is the component excess of the spanning subgraph (V,A) over G. Thus at most twelve vertices are unused by A.

A is a bounded number of trails with internally disjoint degree-two vertices: anchor every used vertex of degree at least four plus one anchor on every isolated cycle, by the cycle-rank bound. Parallel classes lie entirely on the same side because A is also a cut; freeze their endpoints as extra anchors (a class of size m contributes m−1 independent two-cycles). Neither side contains a loop. Freeze the first and last edges of every trail. Freeze length-at-most-three trails entirely. Remove the unused vertices, anchors and interiors of entirely frozen trails, keeping on the other trails distinct prescribed path endpoints. The resulting finite simple graph has a spanning linkage of a bounded number of paths.

Extend the four primal binary gains linearly to edges. Represent cut gains by vertex moments (the gains of vertex stars). Give each undirected edge its primal gain and the sum of the four endpoint moment components, each modulo four. Exact path gains fix primal score and moment parity of internal vertices, since each internal vertex contributes twice. For any new linkage with the original individual path gains, color its paths by their original constant q colors; restore frozen colors and give other vertices color zero. The recombined Eulerian support is disjoint from this cut and has unchanged q score, including the frozen hidden summand. By uniqueness it uses every remaining vertex. The group-labeled irrelevant-vertex theorem for fixed path pattern and fixed forbidden-gain budget therefore bounds residual graph treewidth (exact gains use a finite group). Reinserting boundedly many removed vertices leaves bounded treewidth. See Liu–Yoo Theorem 2.2, https://people.tamu.edu/~yyoo/stoc25.pdf .

Use the eight individually named terminal augmentation in forcing-rank.md. The raw minor arises by contracting the four primal basis elements and deleting the four dual moment elements, since these operations commute with ordinary minors. Thus the augmented minor has width at most the raw branch-width plus a constant. The labeled bounded-width represented-matroid minor WQO of Oum (Theorem 6.1 and Section 8, https://arxiv.org/pdf/1007.3807) implies a finite size bound: otherwise a sequence of strict increasing sizes at fixed q,r yields a comparable pair, although the larger reduced core has no proper ordinary minor retaining both scores. Duality exchanges the argument for cographic inputs. In a minimal solitary-hole instance every position has one of at most 210 ordered forcing types, so its entire size is also bounded. No explicit bound or arbitrary binary extension is established here.
