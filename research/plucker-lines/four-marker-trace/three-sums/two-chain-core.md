# Bounded width of a two-chain forced core

Consider a two-score-rigid core as in joint-forcing-rank.md. Choose a basis maximizing its A intersection. Let only the ordinary (clean) rows and columns remain in its fundamental matrix, temporarily setting aside the bounded exceptional vertices. A row signature records its fundamental dual gain and its incidence on the exceptional columns; a column signature records its fundamental primal gain and its incidence on the exceptional rows. Assume all clean columns have one signature, and rows have at most two signatures. Then the original raw matroid has bounded path-width.

Within one row class two incomparable column neighborhoods would have a two-by-two permutation submatrix. Toggling the two fundamental cycles and two fundamental cocycles cancels their gains and exceptional effects, while each toggled clean row and column sees an odd number of toggles on the opposite shore. All other clean coordinates are allowed to leave their original side. The switch preserves q and changes its raw support, impossible. Hence each row class weakly orders columns by increasing inclusion.

On any residual set of at least four columns, the union of the minimum and maximum equivalence classes of the first order cannot be disjoint from the analogous union in the second. Otherwise choose one minimum and one maximum representative in each order. Each chosen representative is strictly internal in the opposite order; neither order can be constant under this assumption. Let C be the four representatives. In each row class some rows have a singleton and a triple of ones on C, since the two other representatives lie strictly between its extremes. Their xor on C is the middle pair of the associated order. The two middle pairs are complementary. Toggling the four cycles indexed by C and the four cocycles of these two row pairs again cancels gains and exceptional effects. Each toggled row has degree one or three, each toggled column odd degree. This is a second q witness. On sets of size at most three the endpoint sets intersect automatically. Thus repeatedly remove a column that is an endpoint in both remaining orders; one row class alone works as well.

In the fundamental bipartite graph, order the deleted columns in this sequence. Move rows to the processed side as soon as they become constant on remaining columns. An already processed row is constant on future columns, so these contribute cross rank at most one. For each still-unprocessed row class, every processed column was an endpoint when removed. All remaining nonconstant rows in that class have the same value on that column: a lower endpoint has value zero on every row still having some zero on the future columns, and an upper endpoint has value one on every row still having some one. Thus the second crossing rectangle has rank at most two. During a batch of row transfers, the newly constant rows waiting for transfer need not see the most recently removed column uniformly with the earlier nonconstant rows, but that single column increases the bound by at most one. Initialize by transferring globally constant rows. The resulting linear cut-rank is at most four throughout. Restoring the exceptional vertices costs at most their number, at most three. The fundamental-graph cut-rank equals binary matroid connectivity, proving bounded matroid path-width (up to convention on the additive one). Complementary uniqueness for r is used in securing the bounded number of exceptions.

The eight-terminal augmentation and labeled bounded-width WQO of graphic-forcing.md now bound the sizes of all such pair-minimal cores. No graphic hypothesis is needed. Ties are allowed. Three or more row signatures are outside the unconditional two-order argument.
