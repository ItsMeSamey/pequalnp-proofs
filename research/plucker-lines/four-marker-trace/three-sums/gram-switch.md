# Three weak row orders force a core on at most four clean rows

Consider the complementary unique raw witnesses (A,B) and (B,A). Choose a basis maximizing its intersection with A. Write X for the clean A basis rows, Y for the clean B nonbasis columns, and N for their fundamental submatrix. Assume all Y have one augmented signature. A row signature records its fundamental cocycle score and incidence on exceptional columns. A column signature records its fundamental cycle score and incidence on exceptional rows. All vectors and matrices below are binary.

The B cycle equation gives N1=0 on X. The A cocycle equation gives Nᵀ1=0 on Y. The matrix G=NNᵀ is the adjacency matrix of a simple Eulerian graph on X. Any two rows of one signature have comparable clean neighborhoods by the four-toggle permutation-submatrix argument. Their dot product equals the size modulo two of the smaller neighborhood, hence is zero. Each signature class is therefore independent in G.

Suppose T⊆X has the same signature XOR as X and induces only even degrees in G. Set x=1_X+1_T and y=Nᵀ1_T. Modify the dual basis variables at x and the primal nonbasis variables at y. The exceptional-column changes and dual score vanish by signature cancellation. Since y has even weight and all Y have one signature, the exceptional-row changes and primal score vanish as well. At the reverse witness (B,A) we have Nᵀx=y, so every newly selected dual clean column loses its primal selection. The changed clean primal rows are Ny=G1_T, which is supported outside T and hence inside x. Every newly selected primal clean row thus loses its dual selection. This is another raw witness unless T=X. Consequently the unique induced-even set with this aggregate signature is X.

Now assume at most three row signatures. It suffices to find an induced-even set T matching all three class-size parities. Empty or singleton T works if zero or one class has odd size.

If all three classes are odd, write E_ij for the parity of the number of edges between classes i,j. Since G is Eulerian and each class independent, E_12=E_13=E_23=e. A triple, one vertex per class, induces even degrees precisely when its three edge bits a,b,c are equal. Its indicator over the binary field is 1+a+b+c+ab+bc+ca. Summing the linear terms over the triples yields e, since all classes are odd. At a vertex the two cross-degrees have equal parity, so their product has that same parity. Summing such products counts the quadratic terms and yields e from each of the three classes, hence e in total. The constant sums to one. There is an odd number of successful rainbow triples.

If exactly two classes A',B' are odd, let C' be the even class, possibly empty. Any nonadjacent A',B' pair works. Otherwise A' and B' are completely joined, so each of their vertices has odd degree into C'. For every pair a,b, their common-neighbor count in C' has the parity of their common-nonneighbor count, by inclusion–exclusion. The total of the common-neighbor counts over a,b is odd: at c in C' the two degrees into A',B' agree modulo two, so their product has the parity of the degree into A'; the sum of these degrees is odd. Some pair has both a common neighbor and a common nonneighbor. The pair and these two vertices induce a triangle plus an isolated vertex.

Thus |X|≤4 by uniqueness. In the clean one-column-signature setting rank(N)=|Y|−1, as follows directly when B is a circuit: a vector y in ker N of even weight cannot be nonzero since its exceptional coordinates also vanish, giving a pure cycle strictly supported in B (|Y| odd). Then |Y|≤5. The bound on X itself does not need that extra rank assertion.
