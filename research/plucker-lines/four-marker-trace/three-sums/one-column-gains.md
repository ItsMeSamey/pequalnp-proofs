# Opposite-root gain exclusions in a one-column-signature core

Use the maximal-A fundamental chart of gram-switch.md. Let X=A∩I be the clean basis rows, Y=B outside I the clean nonbasis columns, N the clean rectangle, P,Q the pure primal/dual gain spaces, and q,r the two scores attained uniquely at (A,B),(B,A). Suppose a score p is absent. Write b=L(B) and g_i=D(d_i) for the fundamental dual cocycle of clean row i. The statements below concern |Y|>1.

Every B-supported cycle is determined by a subset of Y and has gain equal to the parity of that subset times the common clean-column gain. Joint injectivity inside B hence implies B is a circuit. Since the exceptional basis part of B is nonempty (its complement A is contained in the kernel of the nonzero cocycle B), the common exceptional restriction on each column must sum to its all-one vector, so |Y| is odd and the pattern all ones. In particular each individual fundamental c_j has gain b. Modding out by the exceptional B-basis columns gives rank N=|Y|−1.

For a fixed j, every sum of fundamental dual cocycles from rows with N_ij=0 is disjoint from c_j: it has no exceptional B-basis coordinate, vanishes at j, and uses only clean basis rows where c_j is absent. Thus the score coset b+W_j with W_j=span{g_i:N_ij=0} is attainable. Its primal support is neither A nor B, so this entire coset omits q,r,p. For the last conclusion q,r are understood as uniquely attained among all supports and p as missing. In particular D(A) is separated from each W_j by a linear functional on Q. For rankQ=1 the same unique nonzero functional would require an all-one clean row, contradicting even clean row sums on odd |Y|.

If Q has rank three, scores are four-dimensional, and hidden freedom is zero, the translated forbidden gains q+b,r+b,p+b all lie in Q. Indeed p,q,r are outside Q since all pure-Q scores are attainable, and b is outside Q since r=b+D(A). None of the three translated gains can be a clean-row gain: every clean row has a zero somewhere by even degree. If one of the translated values is zero there is immediately a contradiction. Otherwise for an independent forbidden triple the four remaining nonzero elements of Q consist of three pair sums in one plane plus a single gain outside it. Any W_j lies in that plane or the span of the single gain, since the sum of any cross-pair is forbidden. If the triple is dependent, it comprises the nonzeros of a plane, and each W_j has rank at most one. Consequently outside zero-gain rows there are sharply restricted possible column-zero patterns.

When the exceptional A-nonbasis part has size one, all clean rows have entry one on that exceptional coordinate because A is a cycle. For columns j,k use primal A+c_j+c_k, with unchanged gain L(A). To form a disjoint dual, start from B and toggle odd subsets from each of the two groups of clean rows of pattern 10 and 01 at (j,k). Both columns then have dual zero; selected clean dual entries occur only where the primal was toggled off, and the dual is zero on the unique A exception because the number of rows toggled is even. For two nonempty groups the resulting gain coset is q+aff(g_10)+aff(g_01), which therefore omits q,r,p. These conditions constrain candidates; they do not prove bounded width for arbitrary row signatures.
