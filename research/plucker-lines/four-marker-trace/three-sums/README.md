# Three-marker queries across a genuine rank-two separator

Suppose ordinary vectors partition between two binary subspaces whose intersection W has dimension two. A limb state consists of its ordinary primal subset sum w∈W and ordinary dual restriction δ∈W*. Orthogonality of a disjoint assignment implies δ(w)=0. For four mixed scores let S(w,δ) be the attainable ordinary scores in one limb. They can be translated statewise by any affine-linear function of (w,δ) by moving those terms to the other side. Write all tables after a common affine-linear normalization.

Every feasible state is in at least one of five state planes R_J=J×ann(J), one for each subspace J≤W. The opposite limb restricted to R_J is an ordinary binary matroid on its own ordinary elements projected modulo J: its cycles lift precisely to subsets summing into J and its covectors to those killing J. In this minor, original flux w and original trace δ are linear functions of the cycle and cocycle. Mixed-marker weights transfer by evaluating these linear functions. Thus affine score adjustments on R_J are available without consuming marker rows.

On a plane R_J, pick a nonzero score direction h, and one pair {t_m,t_m+h} of actual scores in S(m) at EVERY state of the plane. If t_m modulo h is affine on R_J, query the opposite minor with its scores shifted accordingly, and with the four-bit target projected mod h. Every success glues to a stored same-state witness on the first side. If each attainable (mode,score) on the first side appears in at least one such plane-and-pair query, the reduction decides global feasibility and constructs a witness on an otherwise arbitrary opposite side. This finite covering condition requires an exact table or another certificate of coverage.

An explicit sufficient class has a two-dimensional effective score space K on the known limb. All four primal-axis states (w,0) have full normalized table K, and each other attainable state has at least three of the four normalized scores. Every pure state is attainable since W lies in the ordinary span on either side. For a pure primal state use the primal-axis plane, for a pure dual state use the dual-axis plane, and for a mixed state use J=span(w). In the last case the three other states are zero and the two associated pure states, hence attainable. To cover a particular score b at state m choose h≠0 with b+h also attainable. Any table of size at least three in K contains an h-pair. On a two-dimensional mode plane, pair offsets modulo h are affine exactly when their four quotient values sum to zero. For m≠0 adjust the pair at the zero state, whose table is full, to satisfy that identity. To cover a zero-state score use the fully available primal-axis plane. This proves the sufficient class. The dual variant holds as well.

The affine requirement is real. Any deterministic rigid ordinary-score gadget on the full boundary has additive values on pure-mode axes because its cycle and dual weights are separately linear; evaluating an arbitrary model's cycle and covector against the zero opposite shows that its rigid score is the sum of those two pure scores. In particular generic four-state mode offsets cannot be independently specified even on one pure axis, unlike the three affinely independent states at a one-dimensional port.

## A nonregular three-connected one-hole example

Each limb consists of seven columns (4,5,6,7,8,9,10) in binary four-space. The low two coordinates define W, and the upper two coordinates of the two limbs are private. The left primal labels are (3,3,3,0,3,1,2), the dual labels (2,0,1,2,0,3,1). All pure tables are full K={0,1,2,3}. The three mixed states, indexed by m=w+4δ, have tables S(6)={0,1,3}, S(9)={0,1,2}, S(15)={0,2,3}. Thus the state tables lack a common nonzero translation period.

On the right, assign primal labels (0,4,0,4,0,4,0) and dual labels (8,8,0,0,0,0,0). The resulting high score bits record the first coordinate of the boundary flux and trace. Direct enumeration gives every four-bit whole score except 13. Testing all cuts certifies 3-connectivity of the full 14-element represented matroid. Its seven-element ordinary restriction is 3-connected of rank four, and hence is the dual Fano matroid (its dual is simple binary of rank three on all seven nonzero points). The example is nonregular. The executable example.py reproduces the local tables, full raw image and connectivity checks.

This result is conditional on knowing the indicated local score table or certifying its coverage. A constant-size side as in the example is enough; no restriction on the opposite side is required. General rank-two-separator four-marker feasibility is left open.
