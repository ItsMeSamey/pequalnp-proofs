# Exponentially many certified affine separator cells

Fix n≥2. Use ordinary graphic incidence columns, with the protected four-space and covector given by five terminals L,R,O (red) and U,D (blue). Let h₀=U and hₙ₊₁=D. Introduce free intermediate connectors h₁,…,hₙ. Between h₀ and h₁ place a special pair y,z; join both connectors to both vertices and add Ly,yO,Oz,zR. For each i from 1 to n place a pair aᵢ,bᵢ between hᵢ and hᵢ₊₁. Join each connector to each pair vertex and add aᵢbᵢ, Laᵢ, bᵢR. Add Lhᵢ,Rhᵢ for every intermediate connector.

Blue U,D are connected exactly when all intermediate connectors are blue, some special pair member is blue, and each ordinary pair contains a blue vertex. All red terminals are unavailable as shortcuts for blue paths. A red intermediate connector joins L to R. The special all-red pair joins L to R via O, and any ordinary all-red pair joins L to R directly. Conversely if blue wins, no red intermediate connector can connect stages and no individual stage connects L to R. Exactly one of the red LR and blue UD connections holds in every coloring. The missing flux is p=LR+UD. All red-only fluxes occur with free vertices red. For fluxes involving UD take all connectors and ordinary pairs blue, and set the special pair both blue, y red only, or z red only to obtain UD, LO+UD, or RO+UD.

For every valid separator the scalar c(L)+c(R) equals the blue win function, by monochromatic connectivity and c(p)=1. Restrict all free hᵢ and y,z to blue. On the remaining 2n independent bits the forced scalar is F=∏ᵢ(aᵢ∨bᵢ). Its support is Aⁿ, A={01,10,11}. An affine space contained in Aⁿ has at most 2ⁿ points: the projection onto each pair is an affine space contained in A, hence contains at most two points. Intersect any affine separator domain with the stated slice and the equation c(L)+c(R)=1. For an affine selector this intersection is affine and lies in Aⁿ. Since |Aⁿ|=3ⁿ, covering all dual parameters needs at least (3/2)ⁿ such domains. The domains may overlap, and their affine selectors may differ. The same bound applies to the leaves of trees querying one affine parity per node. General priority lists whose effective regions exclude all previous tests can have nonaffine domains. In particular the forced scalar alone permits a short ordered list of zero-pair tests.

Both matroids are connected. In the ordinary graph, deleting any vertex other than L,R leaves an intact ordinary stage joining L and R, and every remaining vertex has a path to an anchor (for O,U,D use their two neighbors). Deleting L or R leaves the opposite anchor, all ordinary rungs, and paths to that anchor from the special pair. After identifying all terminals into a hub, deleting it leaves a connected chain of stage pairs and connectors. Every free vertex is adjacent to the hub, so deleting any free vertex also preserves connectivity. This proves biconnectivity for the ordinary and quotient graphs.

## A short priority list for the complete selector

Use one early test for each intermediate connector being red, the special pair both red, and each ordinary pair both red. On such a failed gate, cut the stage chain at that connector or pair. Set the potential on free vertices strictly on the U side to their input color, and on the failed gate and D side to zero. Set L,R,O,D to zero and U to one. This affine assignment respects all monochromatic edges on the failure flat. Edges to red terminal anchors do not cross two blue endpoints, and every connection across the barrier uses a red vertex with zero potential.

If all failure tests miss, assign L potential one, R,U,D potential zero, c(aᵢ)=1+aᵢ, c(bᵢ)=c(hᵢ)=0, c(y)=c(O)=1+y, and c(z)=0. This affine default respects all monochromatic edges when each pair contains a blue vertex and all intermediate connectors are blue. For example a red-red ordinary rung would be the only way that rung could violate the test, and would activate an earlier failure clause. All branches satisfy c(p)=1. Thus the same family admits O(n) affine tests and affine outputs under priority semantics. It separates the size of globally valid affine covers from the size of ordered lists with differences of affine cells as effective domains.
