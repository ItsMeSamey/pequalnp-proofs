# A soundness test for a projected opposite-edge switch

Let the binary columns of M span V. A model consists of a set X of column sum zero containing t and a covector d taking value one on s and vanishing on X. Its weight is ℓ(X)+d(u). Choose q=u+as+bt nonzero and form Q by projecting all columns to V/〈q〉. For a cycle Y of Q, define g(Y) by sum_M(Y)=g(Y)q. This is linear on the cycle space. Every candidate quotient covector d pulls back to a genuine original covector with d(q)=0.

If a circuit C of Q contains t, spans no s in Q, and has g(C)=0, both original weights occur. In fact its rank in M is its rank in Q: its unique quotient relation already holds in M. Also s increases the quotient rank. Thus q is independent of span_M(C,s). Linear extension gives two covectors taking the same prescribed values zero on C and one on s, with opposite values on q. Their values on u differ because t belongs to C. This is a sufficient condition which does not require any stationarity assumption.

Now inspect a compatible opposite-root K4 chart switch in Q. Let B include s, exclude t, and have zero (s,t) entry. The two exchanged basis elements i,k both occur in the fundamental circuit C1 of t. The end chart has root circuit C2. Write d1,d2 for the two fundamental s-cocircuit covectors. In B coordinates they agree except on i,k, where they differ. Assume the two fundamental rooted circuits have opposite projected primal weights with the chosen lift convention; the projected primal weight can have added a common multiple of g.

If the original cycle fiber is nonempty at either of d1,d2, both original objective weights occur. To see this orient the switch so that d1 has a nonempty original fiber, and assume the previous sufficient condition does not hold. In the restriction H of Q to the columns annihilated by d1, every circuit through t has g=1. On the connected component of H containing t, the circuits through t span its cycle space (the usual rooted circuit spanning lemma for connected binary matroids). Hence g equals the t-coordinate throughout that component's cycle space. Any genuine original rooted cycle killed by d1 has t-coordinate one and g zero. Decomposing it over the components of H therefore gives a circuit J from a different component with g(J)=1. In H, B minus s is a basis; both i and k lie in the component of C1. No column in J has any i or k coordinate relative to B. Thus J is killed by d2 as well. Both C1 and C2 have g=1 by the preceding sufficient condition applied contrapositively. Consequently C1 symmetric-difference J and C2 symmetric-difference J are genuine original cycles through t, killed respectively by d1 and d2. Their primal weights differ, and their dual weights agree since each covector kills q,t and takes value one at s.

Testing whether the endpoint fiber is nonempty takes a span computation: t must be in the original span of the remaining d-zero columns. Empty original fibers at both endpoints leave this criterion inconclusive. The lemma does not supply an algorithm to find the relevant quotient minor or prove that all positive quotient switches lift.

When M has rank four and an opposite-edge K4 survives in Q, q,s,t are independent. There are exactly two covectors satisfying d(s)=1 and d(t)=d(q)=0; the two distinct endpoint covectors exhaust these. If the original dual marginal d(u) takes both values over genuine models, at least one genuine model has d(q)=0. Thus in rank four the quotient switch is sound on every instance with varying dual marginal. More generally, suppose Z is an ordinary set contracted along with q to leave ambient rank three. Existence of a genuine d killing q and Z forces one endpoint active, by the same dimension count.

For a remaining ghost switch the uncontracted local obstruction is particularly simple. Lift an independent ordinary contraction set from Q to M, perform that ordinary contraction only, and restrict to the six K4 elements. The q coordinate gives an extra relation test g on the three-dimensional graphic K4 cycle space. Both end circuits have g=1. They differ by C_j+C_l, so g(C_j)=g(C_l). In the fundamental cycle basis (C_t,C_j,C_l) its signature is therefore (1,a,a), exactly the coordinate function x_t+a x_s on K4 cycles. Locally the old cycle space is the kernel of that coordinate function. When a=0, t is a coloop; when a=1, s and t form a series pair. In either case any original anchored model must use elements outside the local minor.
