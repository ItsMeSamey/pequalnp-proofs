# Asymmetric true witnesses in the projected matroid

Keep the setup of the [virtual-contraction criterion](../virtual-contraction.md). Contract q freely to obtain Q and let g be the binary lift defect on its cycles. Assume s and t project to distinct nonzero vectors, as in any surviving opposite-edge chart. The earlier easy test gives both objective values whenever a quotient circuit through t of defect zero does not span s. Assume this case is absent below. Supports never use s. An original dual functional satisfies d(s)=1 and kills the selected primal cycle, so d(t)=0. The difference between evaluating the dual weight at u and at q is a common constant.

**Dual value zero.** The marginal d(q)=0 is nonempty if and only if there are disjoint quotient circuits K,J with t in K, both of defect one, whose union does not span the projected s. For sufficiency, their union has zero lift defect. A quotient functional killing their span and taking one on s pulls back to a true original dual. Conversely a true cycle in this branch projects to a cycle in a hyperplane missing s. Decompose it into disjoint circuits. Its rooted circuit must have defect one by the easy-test exclusion; some other circuit must also have defect one because the total defect vanishes.

To preserve an individual requested primal weight ℓ(X), allow one additional circuit, for a total of at most three. Keep the rooted circuit and choose at most two of the others to reproduce their combined pair of gains (g,ℓ). The exact test on the union remains that its defect is zero, it has the requested weight, and it does not span s in Q.

**Dual value one.** The marginal d(q)=1 is nonempty if and only if there is a quotient circuit C through t, avoiding s, such that s lies in its span, g(C)=0, and the two circuits using s in C∪{s} both have defect one. More explicitly, partition C into its two nonempty complementary subsets each summing in Q to s. Adding s to either yields a circuit; together these are a theta with singleton branch s. For necessity, restrict a true model in this branch to any constituent quotient circuit through t. Every quotient subcycle has zero defect, since its lifted sum is g times q and the functional d kills every selected column but takes one on q. The rooted circuit must span s by exclusion of the easy case. A half together with s has defect one by the same evaluation, now with d(s)=1. For sufficiency the lifted C has the same rank as its projection. Thus q is outside its span, and original s differs from either lifted half-sum by q. A functional zero on C and one on q therefore takes one on s.

To preserve a prescribed ℓ-weight in this branch, use C alone or append a disjoint ℓ-odd circuit J avoiding s. Require the lift defect to vanish on the whole cycle space of the union. This can be checked by comparing the rank on the union before and after adjoining the defect-coordinate row. It holds on every subset of the actual original witness. If the target primal parity differs from ℓ(C), a disjoint circuit decomposition of the rest of that witness supplies an ℓ-odd J. Conversely the rank condition keeps q outside the original span of the union, and the theta forces the extension with d(q)=1 to satisfy d(s)=1.

In particular a theta C and a pair K,J from the two marginal descriptions with ℓ(C)=ℓ(K)+ℓ(J) directly certify different true total weights. These witnesses can use elements deleted by the proposed chart and need not annihilate the contracted ordinary basis. The [checker](check_theta.py) compares both full weight tables with brute force on 957 small instances after excluding the easy case.

**Common contractions outside the named basis.** Let Z be the ordinary basis contracted by a given positive quotient chart. If both true marginal covectors kill some z∈Z, contract it first in the original instance, gauging its primal label to zero. The chosen true witnesses project, stationarity is inherited because every feasible minor pair lifts, and the chosen quotient chart is unchanged. More generally, let U be the span of all labeled lifts (z,ℓ_z) for z in Z. Fix the lift convention (q,η) used in the quotient. Consider any ordinary columns killed by the two marginal covectors whose labeled lifts belong to U+span(q,η). Contract a subset with independent projections modulo q. Its ordinary span avoids q because one marginal takes one on q. In the labeled quotient the contracted vectors lie in the prior ordinary contraction flat, so the surviving Z complete precisely the same labeled contraction and the switch persists. If the resulting ambient rank lies in the proven finite range, soundness follows there. The two covectors and the candidate common contraction flat can all be checked by binary elimination.

## Activating a switch by spanning three specified tails

This test needs just one original model with d(q)=0 and has no ambient-rank cutoff. In Q write the relative-to-chart residuals

    r_t = t+i+k,   r_j = j+s+i,   r_l = l+s+k.

They lie in the span A of the independent projected ordinary contraction columns Z. Fix the projected weight convention used to score the switch. The labels on Z induce a linear form σ on A. Find all ordinary columns outside the six named positions killed by d whose projected vector lies in A and whose projected label is σ of that vector. If their span contains r_t,r_j,r_l, the positive switch certifies genuine mixed variation.

Choose independent generators of that available span and contract their original elements, first gauging their labels to zero. They are independent modulo q, so q survives. The original d kills them, and its rooted cycle survives by projection. In the quotient by q after these ordinary contractions the three residuals vanish. Since A is disjoint from the old chart span of s,i,k, the six named elements are now a K₄ restriction. The labels used to eliminate each of the three tails agree with the old chart labels by construction. Thus the switch keeps its nonzero primal difference. This argument uses the label associated to the tail by Z; the full circuit label can differ from that value.

On the six elements, d(s)=1,d(t)=0 implies d(i)=d(k). If they are both zero, start an independent set with i,k; otherwise start with j,l, both of which are zero. Extend to a basis of the span of all d-zero ordinary columns. The rooted t is already spanned by the initial two. The element s is outside this space. Add s and complete to a basis. The fundamental s-cocircuit now vanishes on all the old d-zero columns, including the genuine rooted cycle. The named K₄ is a positive chart switch at this basis because its three circuit relations hold on the named restriction. The [active endpoint lemma](../virtual-contraction.md) gives actual mixed variation in the ordinary minor, and both models lift through the ordinary contractions. In particular, if all three tails are zero from the beginning, every genuinely populated d(q)=0 marginal activates some chart displaying that same switch, even if the originally supplied basis has both endpoint fibers empty.
