# Rooted selectors for the weighted opposite-edge switch

In a compatible binary basis chart, let s,i,k be basis and t,j,l nonbasis. The six vectors reduce to the graphic K4 with opposite edges s,t. Let C_t,C_j,C_l be its fundamental circuits. A nonzero switch changes exactly one of its two additive weights; pass to the dual matroid if necessary so that the primal weight changes. In the one-dimensional primal lift the fundamental parities of j and l are then distinct. The four possibilities for the signs on (C_t,C_j,C_l) coincide exactly with the signatures of a single peripheral edge: i has (1,1,0), j has (0,1,0), k has (1,0,1) and l has (0,0,1). These circuits form a cycle basis, so additive labels with the same signatures differ only by a row-space gauge. The seven-element lifted target including its named extra vector h is therefore the graphic matroid of K4 with one nonroot edge subdivided, h being one of its two series edges. Direct verification: after choosing a gauge so just that original edge carries the additional row bit, every cycle using it must use h as well, and all other cycles are unchanged.

The dual moment u has equal i,k coordinates in this orientation. Modulo the ordinary chart contraction its underlying projection belongs to span(s,t). Guess two binary coefficients, shifting the virtual lifted moment by that combination of the lifted s,t. Its residual is zero or h. One extra guessed h shift converts these two cases into the single condition that a named virtual element be parallel to h. The ordered target is planar graphic, with opposite roots s,t, a peripheral series extension h, and a named parallel copy of h. Contracting a virtual sum for free would not, by itself, certify that ordinary columns can contract its span with all named incidences kept. The normalization establishes a target-minor equivalence and does not establish a rooted-minor algorithm.

## Why replacing named roots by constant unrooted tags needs additional structure

In F7 use columns 1 to 7 as three-bit vectors and number them from zero. Let cocycle root s be position 0 and cycle root t be position 1. The two feasible anchored pairs, in (cycle,cocycle) masks, are (42,85) and (82,45). Give the primal weight to bit 3 and the dual weight to bit 4. Each marginal varies, but the mixed sum is constantly zero. The supplied checker directly enumerates the words.

Take a binary matroid 2-sum along an unlabeled element distinct from both roots with an arbitrarily large binary projective geometry, giving every added ordinary element weight zero. A cycle and cocycle of a 2-sum lift to cycles and cocycles of each summand with matching port bits across summands. A feasible pair is disjoint at every actual ground element. It is also disjoint at the virtual port within each summand: that port alone cannot be the odd intersection of a binary cycle and cocycle. Thus the objective of every anchored pair is unchanged. Conversely all occurring single-port states 00,10,01 extend into the other summand by the empty pair, an ordinary circuit through its port, or a cocircuit through the port. The resulting connected example still has both active marginal weights and no mixed sum one. It has every preassigned finite unrooted binary minor entirely away from the roots for sufficiently large projective rank. Indeed restriction to a hyperplane of the projective geometry away from the port already gives a large projective geometry; arbitrary binary parallel classes and loops can be generated by contracting extra directions.

Consequently a strategy that attaches fixed binary tags only around the marked elements and asks only about existence of a fixed unrooted binary minor is vulnerable to a root-free hit even on connected nontrivial inputs. This statement concerns local attachments that leave remote minors available; it does not exclude reductions that inspect the 2-sum tree, preprocess distant torsos, or use a genuine rooted oracle.

## Paired matrix and parity

Use the paired columns (A[:,i],e_i) in [A | I]. The kernel word associated to x is (x,Ax) for symmetric A. A true guard word uses at most one element from each pair. Any such word decomposes into disjoint binary matroid circuits; if it attains a nonzero marker target of dimension d, choose a basis of the marker contributions of these disjoint circuits and retain the subfamily representing the target. At most d circuits remain and their union is still admissible. This is an exact compatible-circuit formulation. Ordinary matroid parity asks for independent unions of whole pairs.

The latter data, even given for every index subset, are insufficient. The union of the two columns for all i in S is independent precisely when the cut matrix A[complement S,S] has column rank |S|. On four fixed vertices a,s,t,b take the path a–s–t–b. Pivot on the middle edge to obtain the cycle with edges st,ta,sb,ab. Both graphs have the same whole-pair independent sets on every subset (in fact the same cut ranks). Pin s,t. The path has no solution containing the pair, whereas the four-cycle's full support works and includes a. The full delta-matroid of feasible transversals, whose indicator is det A[S], does distinguish these inputs at S={a,b}; the negative example is deliberately limited to whole-pair independence data.
