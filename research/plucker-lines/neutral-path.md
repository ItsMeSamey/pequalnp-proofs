# A neutral path and an extension test

Let every full degree be even in a bipartite graph with left classes P0,P1,Z and right classes Q0,Q1. The two blocks Pr × Qr are complete. Assume |P0| and |Q0| are odd while |P1| and |Q1| are even. Z is the left zero-label class. A target has odd selected counts in Pr and Qr for some common r, even counts in the other classes, and even degree at every selected vertex.

**Path lemma for arbitrary neutral degrees.** There exist i in P0 and an induced path in the neutral/right incidence graph from l in Q0 to an i-neighbor in Q1, which uses only Q1 as internal right vertices and has no internal right neighbor of i. Closing the path through i gives an induced even-degree cycle.

Every P0 row has an odd number of neighbors in Q1 because it has even total degree and is complete on Q0. Since |P0| is odd, the total P0 incidence on Q1 is odd. On Q1 the total P1 incidence is even, so the total neutral incidence on Q1 is odd as well.

Form the bipartite subgraph on Q1 and all neutral vertices. For each of its connected components sum its neutral incidences on Q1. At least one component C has an odd sum. In that same component, the total number of incidences from its neutral vertices to Q0 is odd, by the even degree of every neutral. Moreover the total number of incidences from its Q1 vertices to P0 is odd, by the even degrees of those Q1 vertices and the even size of P1. Thus choose i in P0 having a neighbor in C, and a vertex l in Q0 having a neutral neighbor in C. In the subgraph induced on C together with l, choose a shortest path from l to the set of neighbors of i in C. The component is connected, so this exists. Shortestness removes all chords (including from l to later neutral vertices) and excludes internal Q1 neighbors of i. Only one Q0 vertex was admitted. Since i also sees l by completeness, adding i closes an induced cycle. The cycle can be found by traversing components and a breadth-first search.

Write k for the number of Q1 vertices of the path. When k is even, the cycle itself is a target with one labeled left vertex. Suppose k is odd. There are k+1 left vertices in the cycle. Color them alternately around the cycle, regarding each right vertex on it as an edge between consecutive left vertices. This coloring exists because k+1 is even, including the two-left-vertex case with two parallel edges. Let L be the full left support of this cycle (i and the neutral path vertices).

**Exact extension test for this fixed L.** A target with this left support exists if and only if some right vertex has even incidence into each of the two alternating left color classes. For the forward direction, each selected right vertex must have even total incidence into L. Without an even/even vertex, every such admissible column has an odd number of neighbors in either color class. Any selected right set giving even degrees on L then has even cardinality by summing the equations over one color class. Every target has odd right cardinality.

Conversely, represent right vertices by their adjacency columns on L. The cycle columns, each with ones at its two left endpoints, span the whole even-weight subspace on L: the columns of a spanning path already generate every pair by summing path segments. Let j be an even/even column, necessarily off the cycle. It has even weight and therefore is the sum of a subset of cycle columns. That subset has even cardinality, because the functional summing one alternating color class takes one on each cycle column and zero on j. Adding j to this subset gives an odd-cardinality right set summing to zero. Every used column has even weight, so all induced degrees are even. If this set contains even Q0 parity, take its symmetric difference with the entire right cycle support (one Q0 vertex and k odd Q1 vertices). The right cardinality remains odd, and the Q0 parity becomes odd, as required.

**Arbitrarily many degree-two neutrals.** If every neutral has degree zero or two, the path lemma and extension test always give a target with a single labeled left vertex. For odd k, the path uses k−1 Q1 nonneighbors of i, an even number. The total number of Q1 nonneighbors of i is odd, so one lies outside the path. This vertex has no neighbor in L: it does not see i and each selected neutral already has its two neighbors on the path. Adding this isolated vertex to the original cycle corrects the Q1 parity. This also gives a direct polynomial algorithm.

**Unbounded minimum witness size.** Take a bipartite cycle with 2m+1 right vertices, m at least one. Make one left vertex i the only P0. Put one of i's right neighbors in Q0, and put the other 2m right vertices in Q1. The other cycle left vertices are neutral. Add two P1 twins adjacent to every Q1 and no Q0. The full graph satisfies all hypotheses. Type 1 is impossible because each selected P1 vertex would have odd degree. In a type 0 target the two P1 twins are absent or both present, so removing them does not change parity on the right. Both i and its unique Q0 neighbor must be present. The latter forces its neutral neighbor, then the next right vertex, continuing around the entire cycle. Every target therefore uses at least 2m+1 right vertices.

For unrestricted neutral degrees it remains open here whether a path with even k, or an odd-k path with an even/even column, must exist, or whether another type of target always supplies the desired omission of one left class.
