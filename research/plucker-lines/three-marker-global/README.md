# Polynomial feasibility for an anchored mixed binary cycle–cocycle

Ordinary occurrences e have binary vectors v_e and label bits ℓ_e. The protected data are s,t,u and a bit b. Seek a set X and covector d with sum_X v_e=t, d(v_e)=0 for selected e, d(s)=1, and ℓ(X)+d(u)=b. A fixed root label is absorbed in b, and d(t)=0 follows. Protected vectors need not be selectable. Ordinary occurrences parallel to protected points remain selectable.

Use the [unweighted rank-three trace primitive](../../three-dimensional-trace/README.md). Given boundary T with dim T≤3, p∈T, and functional λ on T, this decides whether an ordinary selection sums to p and is annihilated by an extension of λ. Reject inconsistent values on boundary generators. In particular p=0 succeeds via the empty selection, and λ=0 requires only ordinary span. The proof uses the represented binary three-element circuit algorithm and the vertically 4-connected coupled-star theorem. [Geelen, Gerards and Whittle, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.20960v1#S2)

Assume first that s,t,u are independent. Introduce three unselectable structural columns U={s,u,t+s+u}, and also their three pair sums as unselectable padding. These six columns form a K₄ restriction, and U sums to t. A circuit through all three U cannot use a padding occurrence, since that would place a proper triangle inside the circuit. Its ordinary part X sums to t and its span intersects span U only in the line of t, by the ranks of the circuit and its independent parts. Hence d(s)=1 and either value of d(u) are compatible with this same X, yielding both objective values.

If the triple is coupled and the carrier is vertically 4-connected, use the final graphic step below. Otherwise take a vertical separation with connectivity k≤2. One side spans all six protected points because two proper planes of the three-dimensional span U cover at most five points. Move all six protected occurrences to that side A. The rank of A stays fixed and the connectivity drops by the same amount as any drop in rank of B. Both sides still have rank greater than the connectivity. Such a cut can be found by trying k=0,1,2, forcing k+1 independent ordinary columns in B and the six protected occurrences in A, minimizing the submodular connectivity under these constraints, and testing whether its minimum is at most k. Thus B contains ordinary occurrences only, the entire dual moment u belongs to the exterior span, and H=span(A)∩span(B) has dimension ≤2. A includes its protected vectors when computing spans, but all primal representations in A use only its ordinary occurrences.

## Local weights and flexible states

For w∈H and δ∈H* compute the set Λ(w,δ) of B label sums from selections of flux w killed by an extension of δ. Query each bit c with the unweighted primitive on lifted ordinary columns (v_e,ℓ_e), target (w,c), and boundary H⊕F₂ with trace (δ,0). The dual is zero on the label axis and can be extended as a raw-column covector. If the lifted ordinary span is smaller than this ambient space, reject targets outside it, restrict the boundary to its intersection, and extend afterwards by linear algebra. Orthogonality δ(w)=0 is necessary.

Exterior and interior models glue when the exterior ordinary sum is t+w and their duals agree on H. Gluing does not change d(s), d(u), or any exterior label. Therefore a globally accessible cell with both label values yields both global weights.

Check by Gaussian elimination whether ℓ on B is a linear functional of its columns. If so every feasible state has the unique label given by that functional on w. Otherwise there exists a zero-sum ordinary B word of odd label. With the zero local dual, every w∈H is achievable with BOTH label values: B spans H and that odd word can be added.

In the nonlinear case first test global reachability of ANY zero-trace state regardless of flux. In the quotient of the exterior by H request ordinary sum t mod H with dual taking one at s mod H and zero at t mod H. This is a rank≤2 unweighted trace. The equivalence uses the fact that B can supply EVERY residual in H at zero trace. This test is not a test for zero primal flux. If it succeeds, either weight is possible. It handles projected t=0 (empty exterior selection suffices if the trace is consistent) and projected s=0 (inconsistent).

After a negative zero-trace test, every globally attainable trace on H is nonzero. For every remaining flexible cell (w,δ), choose p spanning ker δ in dimension two, or omit p if H has dimension one. In the exterior alone ask for ordinary sum t+w and prescribe d(s)=1, d(t+w)=0 and d(p)=0 when present. This boundary has rank ≤3. A positive d restricts on H either to δ or to zero. A zero restriction would pass the previous any-flux quotient test; hence the restriction must equal δ. Since δ(w)=0, gluing gives d(t)=0. Conversely every global model using the cell passes this exterior test. Thus both objective values are achieved whenever this test succeeds. In dimension zero there are no nonzero traces.

## Signed interface replacement

If no reachable flexible state terminates the algorithm, substitute signed ordinary directions in H for B. In dimension two retain the set S of nonzero p with nonempty Λ(p,δ_p), where δ_p is the unique nonzero functional killing p. S spans H. In any basis of B, at most one of the three nonzero directions p has support containing the supports of all of H. For either remaining p another direction lies outside the span of the basis entries of p, so extending δ_p to kill that representation proves p∈S. Assign p any label in Λ(p,δ_p). In dimension one retain the nonzero direction p with any label in Λ(p,0); dimension zero uses no column.

These ordinary directions reproduce exactly the unweighted state table. For nonzero δ, at most the unique nonzero point of its kernel can be used. The empty selection has true label zero. For zero δ all fluxes can be realized since S spans H. Every surrogate sign is a possible native sign: in the linear case the chosen labels agree with the representing functional, while in the nonlinear case both signs occur at every zero-trace flux. Thus each unweighted state has at least one surrogate choice, every baseline choice is native, and all *globally accessible* native states are rigid at this point. Replacing B preserves exactly the attainable global objectives. The new carrier consists of A's ordinary data, these new ordinary signed directions, and just the same six protected columns. Do not insert t itself as a structural occurrence: the sum of U must not create an artificial rooted circuit. Retest the triple on the new carrier. Semantic equivalence makes this valid without any promise that a structural property survived, even if later cuts split earlier signed substitutes. The new ambient rank equals rank(A), strictly smaller than the old rank.

## Final graph and dependent vectors

At a vertically 4-connected coupled carrier, the three edges s,u,t'=t+s+u are incident with a common vertex in a graphic representation. The padding K₄ forces pairwise incidence of the three independent roots, which excludes a triangle among them. Contract s; the sum u+t' mod s is now t mod s, a graphic edge between the far ends of u,t' (possibly a loop). Keep ordinary edges plus this new distinguished t, delete structural auxiliaries, and keep the appropriate primal signs. This produces a graphic representation for the original matroid contracted by s, so the [signed-graphic mixed-weight solver](../signed-graphic/README.md) applies. If u lies outside span(ordinary,s,t) after deleting auxiliaries, first test ordinary anchored feasibility; a genuine rooted model then permits either d(u) by linear extension.

The same final step admits a direct graphic argument. On the carrier fix the center color and the two possible leaf-color patterns obeying d(s)=1,d(t)=0, hence d(u) is fixed. Using real ordinary edges, the primal is a monochromatic four-terminal T-join of specified label parity. Given a witness in its coloring, remove zero-label cycles until support nullity ≤1 (a cycle space of dimension ≥2 always has a nonzero word of label zero). Thus the support consists of two disjoint terminal paths and at most one cycle; a cycle may meet its same-color path in one vertex. Enumerate its constant-size suppressed pattern, branch vertices, terminal incidences and segment parities. Fixed disjoint paths with individual finite-group length conditions test it. Loops and parallel edges may instead be subdivided, preserving binary length labels, before enumerating. [Liu and Yoo, STOC 2025](https://people.tamu.edu/~yyoo/stoc25.pdf)

Before choosing an independent U handle dependent protected vectors. s=0 or s=t≠0 is inconsistent. If t=0 and u is independent of s, the empty primal permits either objective. In remaining dependent cases u belongs to span(s,t), so d(u) is fixed by d(s)=1,d(t)=0. Lift the ordinary columns by their label and use one unweighted trace of rank ≤3 on span{(s,0),(t,0),(0,1)}, target (t,b+d(u)), and prescribed values 1,0,0. Intersect with available span and check target as above if desired. This also covers t=0 when d(u) is fixed.

The polynomial number of rank-decreasing iterations and the independent polynomial primitive give polynomial time. One may recover a primal witness by deleting ordinary occurrences greedily while decision stays positive; a positive model in the final inclusion-minimal ground selects every survivor. Solve the remaining dual requirements and the mixed bit linearly. [Independent elimination checker](check_elimination.py) passed 1,140 tests including all interface dimensions and signed baseline cases.

**Padding invariant.** In both primitive U and the weighted solver the recommended carrier is the true currently available ordinary set plus precisely the original six protected occurrences. A deleted limb represented by only two directions never inserts its missing third direction into the circuit-search matroid. Retesting after each selective substitution makes the proof independent of whether completing any other boundary preserves triple coupledness. The input target is carried as the sum of U, never as another structural occurrence.
