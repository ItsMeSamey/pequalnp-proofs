# A finite profile criterion gives bounded-degree ordinary PC

In the mixed three-marker bipartite system of neutral-signatures-pc.md let r be the rank of all left-neutral neighborhood vectors. This result allows pure neutral rows and arbitrary colored cross blocks. For r=3 the checked finite criterion gives degree at most **32**; for r=2 it gives at most **14**. For general r the theorem below is conditional on a specific finite rank-r table. It does not assert that every table passes.

**Finite condition for k directions.** There are right groups g=(d,u), d∈{0,1}, u∈F₂^k. Give each group size zero, three (odd), or four (positive even). Normalize by swapping color indices so side Q0 has odd total and Q1 even. Assume the XOR of the odd groups' signatures on each side is the same vector, with odd popcount. An abstract colored row from P_c is all ones on Q_c and arbitrary on the opposite groups. Retain it if its total weight is even and no solution using this single colored row, any subset of the k neutral coordinates, and any subset of abstract right vertices attains the target. Such a retained row is called bad. The linear test for badness depends only on which pairs (group,row-entry bit) occur: duplicate eligible columns of the same class and restricted neutral signature do not enlarge a span.

For each c call an opposite group flexible if some bad row varies on it. The record of a bad row has count coordinate one, an entry parity at each flexible group, and a common entry bit on each positive-size nonflexible group. Empty positions are zero. The target record has count 1+c and constant desired entry t_g=parity(u)+c in each opposite-group column, collapsed by the same rule. Equivalently one may use entry parity on every odd group, including nonflexible groups since their fixed bit then equals that parity. The test requires **at least one class for which the target lies outside the binary span of its bad row records**, in each admitted profile. Tables allowing all multiplicities are equivalent here: pair-differences generated within flexible groups are eliminated and positive sizes three/four suffice for all parity and flexibility options. For the present transfer it suffices to check just the displayed multiplicities directly. The rank-two C++ enumeration and its independent checker cover 11,232 admitted profiles; the rank-three pair covers 656,000. They are in the guard packages plucker-lines/two-neutral and plucker-lines/three-neutral. Rank-three tables also cover fewer actual directions by padding isolated formal neutral rows.

**Neutral branching without enumerating subsets of actual pivots.** Assume the original instance is negative, so both same-color blocks are complete. Fix a binary basis for the original neutral rows. Guess the r evaluations of its vectors on the selected right bits y and the r coefficients of the aggregate neutral-neighbor row w=Σ_z x_z row_z. Both lists consist of affine forms in the input bits. Each original neutral row whose now-fixed evaluation is one has its pivot forced zero by its guard in degree two. If w lies outside the span of the individually allowed rows, a separating functional on row space, applied to the pinned aggregate coefficients and the forbidden units, derives 1 linearly. Otherwise choose a subset H of at most r linearly independent actual allowed rows whose XOR is w. Each selected representative in H has zero neighbor parity under the branch; the argument does not require its original pivot bit to have been one. The original neutral effect on all right column guards is exactly that of selecting H, and the representatives themselves are available in the unconditioned input when lifting a synthetic singleton model.

Alternatively guess all parities n_a of selected pivots of each distinct nonzero neutral row type, at most 2^r−1 bits. From n_a=1 the sum of its guards gives a·y=0. Delete XOR-zero dependencies among the active types to select H with the same w and individual even degrees. Either strategy is valid, at cost B=min(2r,2^r−1) initial linear guesses. Write k=|H|. If k=0, cut P0∪Q1 and the fixed w=0 refute immediately.

**Right profiles with bounded branching degree.** Form the m≤2^(k+1) physically nonempty groups by right color and signature on H. Let Y_g denote each group's selected parity. The k equations from H are independent on group sums, since the original rows in H are independent on all physical columns. The equation Σ Y_g=1 is independent of them or immediately contradictory. Summing guards on the cut P0∪Q1 gives the further equality Σ_(g in Q1) parity(u)Y_g=1, since p0q0+p1q1=1 using the marker. Swapping colors gives the analogous Q0 equality. All these derivations have degree two at the branch. If m=2^(k+1), the Q1 equation is independent of the previous k+1: a constant plus a linear function of u cannot depend on the color for fixed u of odd weight. If m is smaller, m≤2^(k+1)−1 is already sufficient. Thus at most 2^(k+1)−k−2 independent parity guesses fix every Y_g, after rejecting inconsistent branches by elimination. No viable branch exists when k=1 and the resulting constraints already conflict; otherwise the displayed nonnegative count applies.

Both color cuts force some odd group on each side, and on the even-total side there are at least two. Thus number of even Y_g groups is at most 2^(k+1)−3. For each such group use a presence split: either all its selected bits are zero, or fix one named bit y_j=1. The cost in PC degree is only one per group. Inductively refute every positive subcase and lift its proof to derive y_j=0 separately for each j; then run the all-zero branch using these already derived units. In a positive group keep its representative j_g. Odd groups can be treated as nonempty without naming any representative. Use size 3 there, size 4 on a positively represented even group, and size zero on an all-zero group. The preceding linear equations guarantee an admitted abstract profile, after interchanging color names if needed.

**Lift the failed span test at degree two.** Fix a class c failing the finite test and a separating record functional λ, zero on all its bad rows and one on its target. For any actual colored vertex i in that class, write M_ig=Σ_(j∈g) A_ij y_j on its opposing groups. Form an affine expression L_i from λ and the augmented count bit 1. On flexible groups and on every odd group use M_ig. On positive even nonflexible groups use the actual bit A_i,jg at the pinned representative, and use zero on empty groups.

To see x_i L_i=0 algebraically, temporarily regard the numbers τ_g=M_ig as unknown binary parameters. On empty groups force zero; on groups where the original row is globally constant force that constant times Y_g. The full even row degree imposes q_c+Σ τ_g=0. These restrictions define an affine space. For every point of it construct a synthetic even row: on each nonempty group use only entry values available on this row's original physical group and meet parity τ_g. On positive even groups prescribe the representative bit A_i,jg in one position. If both global values occur, the three or four abstract positions admit either required parity, even with that one prescription. For a globally constant group the fixed parity already ensures consistency. Left matched-group entries are one. This row must be bad: a synthetic singleton solution projects to an original solution by retaining one actual right column of each (group,entry) having odd selected multiplicity. The originals are distinct across these types; all right eligibility, selected left-degree parities and marker values survive. Its chosen neutral rows are physical representatives from H (padding zeros can be omitted). Unsatisfiability of the unconditioned graph suffices; this witness need not obey the branch guesses. Thus λ vanishes on the synthetic record, proving L_i(τ)=0 throughout the affine space.

If this space is nonempty, its vanishing affine forms are the span of its defining affine equations. Substitute M_ig for τ_g. The empty-group and uniform-group equations are already linear combinations of the fixed profile and units; only the full even row constraint is conditional, and multiplying this by x_i is exactly the original guard after using the known matched-group parity. Multiplying the linear representation by x_i therefore derives x_i L_i at degree two. If the parameter space is empty, its defining affine equations span one; the same argument first gives x_i=0, then its product with L_i. No guesses per colored row are needed, even if there are arbitrarily many distinct rows.

Sum those consequences over the class. The marker fixes its count bit. On every flexible or odd group, summing its column guards gives Σ_i x_i M_ig=t_g Y_g, after the known matched left parity and the aggregate w replace the other column contributions. On every positive even nonflexible group the single pinned representative column gives Σ_i A_i,jg x_i=t_g. Empty-group coordinates have value zero. Hence the sum of the x_i L_i equals λ(target)=1 and also equals zero. These steps use degree at most two within the current branch.

Discharging each binary linear guess costs at most one degree by the indicator simulation in neutral-signatures-pc.md. The total for k≥1 is at most

    B + [2^(k+1)−k−2] + [2^(k+1)−3] + 2
      ≤ min(2r,2^r−1) + 2^(r+2) − r − 3.

For rank two this is 3+4+5+2=14; for rank three it is 6+11+13+2=32. For rank one the direct degree-three proof is stronger. This transfer uses no spanning-by-mixed promise and imposes no rank bound on the colored cross blocks. Its only unproved premise for higher ranks is the success of the explicitly described finite profile test.

**A failed table produces an actual small obstruction to the singleton conjecture.** Conversely, suppose some admitted profile has both class targets in their projected bad-row spans. Consider the unprojected bad rows on its three/four abstract columns per active group. In a flexible group, transposing two positions of one mixed bad row and adding the old row generates their pair difference in that group alone, with count coordinate zero; permuting arbitrary group positions yields the full even subspace. Hence membership in the projected span lifts to actual full-column membership for the required constant target. At most one plus the number of opposite columns many bad rows suffice in each class by taking a linearly independent generating subset. Make them the actual colored rows, add the k actual neutral signature rows, and select the entire graph. Every colored row has even degree by construction, every neutral has even degree by the profile, and the full-column target and counts give even degree at every right vertex and the desired markers. No singleton-colored-left witness exists, since every actual colored row was chosen bad against all available columns and neutral subsets. There are at most 4·2^(k+1) right vertices and at most that many plus two colored left vertices, for a total of at most 16·2^k+k+2 vertices. Thus for each fixed rank the finite obstruction has an exact alternative: it proves the singleton reduction (and the PC bound above), or it constructively gives a bounded-size counterexample to the singleton reduction. The latter conclusion alone would not refute a general PC degree bound.
