# A singleton colored vertex with three neutral dimensions

Use the bipartite classes P₀,P₁,Z and Q₀,Q₁ from the [rank-two theorem](../two-neutral/README.md). If some feasible support has neutral-to-right incidence rank at most three, there is a witness with one colored left vertex and at most three neutral vertices. In particular this holds under the corresponding rank bound on the input. A missing edge in a matched block gives an immediate two-vertex witness. Otherwise restrict to a feasible support, remove dependent neutral rows, pad with isolated neutral vertices to reach three, and swap both subscripts if necessary. All full degrees are even; P₀ and Q₀ have odd size, P₁ and Q₁ even size.

A right vertex's signature u is its incidence on the three neutrals. Record for each right class its present signatures and those with odd multiplicity. In a hypothetical counterexample, duplicate any column in pairs until every positive even size is at least four and every odd size at least three. All full parities survive. A singleton witness in the padded graph would also be a singleton witness before padding because duplicate columns in the right-side linear test give the same vector.

Choose a colored row of class c. Its entries in Q_c are all one. A neutral subset H admits an opposite column of signature u and entry e exactly when e equals the parity of u∩H. Each eligible column contributes its masked signature u∩H and a unit bit for its right class. The required sum is zero on the neutral coordinates and is the class unit c on the two class coordinates. This enforces the selected neutral degrees and marker. Eligibility enforces right degrees; the colored row degree is even by summing degrees. H empty cannot work because every own entry is one. Call a row bad if all seven other tests fail.

For each nonempty own presence mask, enumerate all subsets of the 16 opposite occurrence types (u,e). Badness is downward closed under removing types. There are 2,345 inclusion-maximal bad masks across the 255 own masks, at most 12 per own mask. Their subsets describe all bad rows. For a given opposite support a maximal mask is discarded if any nonempty group has no available bit. In every other group a single available bit fixes all entries; when both bits are available each entry is free. Impose the opposite weight parity 1−c required by the full row degree. This discards a wholly fixed cube of the wrong parity.

The XOR of all bad rows of class c, augmented by a count bit, must have count 1−c and entry parity(u) XOR c at every opposite column. These follow from full right degrees and the complete matched blocks. To test membership in the span, a group free in any surviving cube needs just its entry parity in the quotient: flipping any pair of bits within the group preserves row parity and supplies their difference with zero count bit. A group never free has a single common bit. Therefore uniform ones map to the group-size parity in flexible groups, and to one elsewhere. For each cube, start with the fixed ones and flip one designated free coordinate if necessary for full row parity. Add this projected row with count one. All choices in free-group parities differ by even total weight, generated by pairing the designated free group with each other free group. Add these differences with count zero. This produces the exact span in at most nine binary coordinates.

Write o₀,o₁ for the masks of odd multiplicities. Their cardinalities are odd and even respectively. Their signature XORs agree by neutral even degree. This common signature has odd Hamming weight: total P₀ incidence into Q₁ is odd, total P₁ incidence there is even, and all Q₁ degrees are even. The [checker](check.cpp) enumerates all 656,000 support and parity profiles satisfying these conditions. Exactly 32,729 pass the P₁ span test, and none passes both. The [independent checker](audit.cpp) obtains the same counts. It can cap each group at one or two columns: replace three same-class columns of the same signature by their bitwise XOR. Full parities and matched blocks persist. A singleton witness using the merged column lifts by taking an original column with the same bit in its sole colored row, which exists for the XOR of three bits.

Under the rank promise enumerate each colored row and every subset of at most three neutral rows; the right-side linear test recovers an actual support. Both exhaustive checks use C++17. Compile either with `c++ -std=c++17 -O3 check.cpp -o /tmp/check-three-neutral` (substitute `audit.cpp` for the independent check), then run the executable. The [antichain theorem](antichain.md) gives a separate unbounded-rank class.
