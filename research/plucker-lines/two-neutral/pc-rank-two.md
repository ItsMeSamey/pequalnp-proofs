# Bounded ordinary polynomial-calculus degree for neutral rank at most two

Consider a NO instance in the normalized one-sided zero-label model of the accompanying finite theorem. Assume the binary neutral-to-right adjacency matrix has rank at most two. Every such NO has an ordinary Boolean PC refutation over GF(2) of degree at most 14. We first give a degree-21 bound, then refine its branching costs. All colored-to-colored blocks can have unrestricted rank. A nonsaturated matched block would give a witness, so both matched blocks are complete.

We use two elementary branching rules. A split on the bit of an affine linear form costs at most one degree: multiply the two branch proofs by their respective branch indicators and sum the resulting indicators. Boolean reductions for the indicator of an affine linear equation cost at most this overhead, since its square reduces to itself using the ordinary Boolean axioms. A split on presence of a selected right vertex in a group also costs one degree, independently of group size. For each vertex j, the proof assuming y_j=1 derives y_j=0 in the unconditioned system after multiplying through by y_j. Use all these equations to run the proof for the all-zero branch. In the positive branch retain a named vertex with y_j=1. The rules hold in the presence of earlier assumptions.

Branch on the parity of selected neutral twins for each nonzero row type. There are at most three types. Summing the guards within a type whose parity is one gives its right linear degree equation L_z(y)=0. Let E be the XOR of the active row types. Choose a subset of the active types summing to E of minimum size; its rows are independent and thus number k at most two. Use these k rows as the synthetic neutral system. The actual right guards have the same neutral contribution as this system after the parity branches. Each of the k individual synthetic rows still satisfies its even-degree equation.

Partition right columns by their color and their k-bit neutral pattern, at most eight groups. Branch on the presence and parity p_h of each group h. Denote a selected representative by j_h in each nonempty group. Inconsistent branches are refuted linearly; the right primary equation forces exactly one class to have odd size. By the mixed marker and the left primary marker, the colored left counts have the same class parities. Swap the two indices simultaneously if needed to normalize the odd classes to index zero.

Make a synthetic right multiset with n_h=0 for an empty group, n_h=2 for a nonempty group of even parity, and n_h=3 for a group of odd parity. Give a designated position in each nonempty group the name of j_h. All synthetic neutral rows are even by their right linear equations on the branch. Pad the k rows by zero until there are two. This profile occurs in the independently checked finite theorem (all multiplicities at most four). Its bad colored rows have even total degree, complete matched block, and allow no singleton-colored witness. By that theorem at least one colored class r fails the augmented span criterion: the necessary XOR of its opposite-block rows and class-count bit is outside the span of the augmented bad rows. Let D be a separating binary linear functional with value one on the necessary vector and zero on every bad generator. Within any opposite group admitting a mixed bad generator, transpositions show D has equal coefficients at every index there. In a group whose entries are uniform in every bad generator, replace all coefficients by their sum at the designated position. This preserves D's values on all bad rows and on the necessary vector (which is also constant in each group).

Fix a real colored row i of this class. For each opposite nonempty group write T_h=Σ_{j in h} a_ij y_j. The only constraints we need on these bits are (i) T_h=b p_h if the original row has the same bit b on the whole original group, and (ii) Σ_h T_h equals the parity of its complete matched class, if i is selected. For every binary vector of values satisfying (i) and (ii), build a synthetic colored row on the profile. On each opposite group its row has parity T_h and at the designated position takes the original bit a_i,j_h. On a uniform original group assign its constant. On a mixed original group at least two synthetic positions are available, so either parity is possible with the prescribed bit. The row is even and complete on its matched group. It must be bad: all (color,neutral pattern,colored bit) types present in it are present for the original row. Any synthetic singleton witness uses some subset of the k neutral representatives; compress identical column types modulo two and lift each remaining type to a distinct original column. Then every selected guard and marker is preserved, contradicting NO.

Accordingly the substituted linear functional D vanishes on every vector of T_h satisfying (i),(ii), taking T_h in a symmetric group and the fixed bit a_i,j_h in a uniform bad group. By linear algebra its affine identity follows from (i),(ii), or those equations are inconsistent. After multiplying by x_i, these identities are ordinary PC consequences: the matched-group parity and (i) are linear branch facts; (ii) multiplied by x_i is exactly the colored guard after substituting the matched-group parity. If the local linear system is inconsistent the same operation derives x_i=0. Thus each selected row contributes zero to this functional as encoded by PC.

Sum these weighted identities over class r. Its class bit has the prescribed parity. On a symmetric group, sum the right guards over that group: the selected contribution from class r is the XOR of the shared neutral column effect and the opposite colored class count, multiplied by p_h. This is the value of that group's part of the synthetic necessary vector under D. On a uniform bad group, use just the right guard for its designated vertex, which is selected on this branch. Its value is that same constant necessary column entry. Therefore the weighted identity has value one by the separator, a contradiction. All branch-local calculations use degree at most two (the fixed-bit guards and substitutions only require multiplying linear facts). Three neutral-type plus at most sixteen presence/parity splits give the initial degree-21 bound. For a fixed rank bound, the number of representative choices is polynomial in the input size.

## Refined degree accounting

The bound improves to 14. Branch on group parities before presence. The k active representative rows are independent, so their k even-degree equations in group parities are independent on the groups present in the input. The right primary equation gives one further independent equation or yields immediate inconsistency. In addition, summing the guards of colored P0 and Q1 produces the matched product plus the selected neutral incidence into Q1. The matched product equals one from the three marker equations. The parity of selected neutral incidence into Q1 is therefore one, and the other cut gives the same for Q0. These become affine parity equations after the neutral-type branches. When k=2 and m=8, the Q1 cut gives an independent constraint beyond the two neutral rows and the right primary: all four patterns occur separately on each shore, and the cut coefficients are zero on Q0 and nonconstant on Q1. Hence the number of independent parity splits is at most four for k=2 (m≤7 already gives four), and at most two for k≤1. Use Gaussian elimination to derive the remaining group parities from these splits and the fixed equations.

Both right color classes have at least one odd-parity group by the two cut equations. One class has even total size, so it has at least two odd-parity groups. In particular at most m−3≤5 groups have even parity. Only these groups need presence splits: an odd group is nonempty algebraically; on its three synthetic clones, a symmetric separator is always available even when every bad row is uniform there, because summing three equal bits gives that bit. No named representative or selected individual y_j is used for such a group. In the row construction, prescribe the synthetic parity using only bit types available for i in that original group. The actual proof thus costs at most three neutral-type splits, four parity splits, five group-presence splits and two degrees in the innermost calculation.
