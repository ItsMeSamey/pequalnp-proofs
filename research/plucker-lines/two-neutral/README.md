# A singleton colored left vertex with at most two neutral left vertices

In a bipartite graph the selected induced subgraph must have even degrees. The left color classes P0,P1 have labels (1,0,i), the right classes Q0,Q1 have labels (0,1,i), and up to two additional left vertices have label zero. The target is (1,1,0). Every feasible instance has a witness using exactly one colored left vertex.

A nonedge in a same-index block gives a two-vertex witness. Otherwise restrict the graph to the support of any witness. Every full degree is now even and the same-index blocks are complete. Swap the two class indices simultaneously if necessary so that P0 and Q0 have odd size, P1 and Q1 even size. Pad with isolated neutral vertices so there are exactly two.

For each right class, partition vertices by their two incidence bits u to the neutrals. Write k[d,u] for each of the eight group sizes. For a single colored vertex of index c, its row on Qc is all ones. On each opposite group record one of four states: all zeros, all ones, mixed with an odd number of ones, or mixed with an even number of ones. On an empty group use the all-zero state. A mixed odd state is possible for size at least two, and mixed even for size at least three. Discard rows whose full degree is odd.

For each remaining row try all four masks H of selected neutral vertices. A right vertex with colored row entry e is available precisely when e equals the parity of u intersect H. Each available vertex supplies its masked incidence u intersect H together with a unit bit for its right class. The required sum of these four-bit columns is zero on neutral coordinates and the unit bit for class c on class coordinates. This linear test enforces right degrees, selected neutral degrees, and the marker. The colored vertex also has even degree because the sum of degrees on all selected vertices is even. A row failing this test for every H is called bad. The test depends only on which entry values occur in each group.

A counterexample must supply only bad colored rows. Their XOR on each opposite right class is prescribed by full right-degree parity. For P0 the prescribed bit at signature u is parity(u) and the number of chosen rows must be odd. For P1 it is 1 XOR parity(u), with even row count. Test these goals independently in the binary spans of the bad rows augmented by a count-parity bit. An empty row subset is allowed for the even goal, and repeated equal rows cancel in pairs.

For any group mixed in at least one bad row, permuting two unequal entries of that row and subtracting yields their pair difference in the span. Symmetry gives all pair differences in that group, independently of every other group. The quotient therefore retains only the parity of entries on a mixed-capable group. On a nonempty group uniform in every bad row, retain the common bit. The prescribed target is constant on each group, hence belongs to this ambient space too. The quotient test is exact.

All tests now use only presence of the four entry states and parity of group size. Every odd size at least three can be represented by three, and every even size at least four by four. This reduces the search to 5^8 profiles. Full feasibility requires odd Q0 and even Q1 sizes. The XOR of all neutral signatures in Q0 must equal that in Q1 because the neutral rows have even degree. This common signature has odd popcount because the XOR of the odd-degree opposite portions of the odd number of P0 rows is the total neutral incidence in Q1. The exhaustive integer checker retains 11,232 profiles. In each profile at least one required span test fails, ruling out a counterexample.

Compile and run with `c++ -O2 -std=c++17 check.cpp -o /tmp/check-two-neutral && /tmp/check-two-neutral`. The [independent checker](../check_two_zero.cpp) uses a larger profile set. The compression proof works for any fixed number of neutral rows by grouping vertices by their full neutral signature. The calculation here covers up to two.

## Rank-two form

The theorem also holds if the neutral-to-right incidence matrix has binary rank at most two, or more generally whenever some feasible support has this rank bound on its selected rows and columns. In any such support, a nonempty linear dependency among selected neutral rows restricted to the selected columns can be removed in full. Every right degree keeps its parity, all remaining left degrees stay fixed, and the marker sum stays fixed. Iterate to obtain at most two selected neutral rows. Apply the theorem to the induced support.

For the rank-two promise, the algorithm tries each colored left vertex i and each subset of at most two neutral indices as the exact left support. Keep all right columns that have even degree into this support. A binary linear system imposes the marker target and even degree for each selected left vertex, and recovers a right support when feasible. A missing edge in a matched block is an immediate two-vertex witness.
