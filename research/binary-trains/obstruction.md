# A universal grouping obstruction on an easy dense formula

Let n=q² with q a power of two greater than 9. Index coordinates by (u,t) in GF(q)². For each polynomial p of degree at most 3 over GF(q), take the n-bit word with entries Tr(p(u)t), using the field trace to GF(2). Distinct polynomials differ in at least q−3 evaluations. At each such evaluation u their two binary words differ in exactly q/2 positions t because a nonzero trace functional is balanced. Hence the n² words have mutual distances at least q(q−3)/2>n/3. For each word add its full-width maxterm, false only at that word. The formula has 2^n−n² models.

Suppose this truth function is factored as the conjunction of arbitrary local predicates evaluated on subsets of its existing variables. Every zero of any predicate is a zero of the formula. A nonconstant predicate omitting a variable would therefore exclude two global assignments at Hamming distance one; the code has no such pair. Consequently every nontrivial predicate has full scope. In particular a constant-width CNF core can impose no nontrivial restriction. Under a total-scope bound of Cn there can be at most C nontrivial predicates. The union of their zero sets is the entire code, so one excludes at least n²/C distinct words.

Split the n coordinates into equal halves, in any order. Three codewords agreeing on one half have sum of their three pairwise distances at most n, since a binary coordinate in the other half contributes at most two. This contradicts the strict distance bound. Thus the 0/1 flattening of the chosen predicate's zero set has at least n²/C ones and at most two per row or column. It is an edge-incidence matrix between left and right half-assignments; as a bipartite graph its maximum degree is at most two. Greedily choose edges for an induced matching, removing constantly many conflicting edges after each choice. The matched submatrix is an identity of size Ω(n²/C). Therefore the zero matrix indicator has rank Ω(n²/C) over *every* field, and its complement, the truth table, has rank at least that value minus one. This argument works at the balanced cut of any variable order. Nearby checkpoints differing by O(log n) coordinates can be addressed with a slackened projection bound: if a common half is shortened by d and 3 words agree there, the sum of distances is at most n+2d, still less than the explicit code's 3q(q−3)/2 for d=o(n).

This excludes universally sufficient low-rank, linear-total-scope multiplicative groupings over the original variables, even if arbitrary orders, predicates and constant-width cores may be chosen. It gives no SAT running-time lower bound. With an arbitrary bounded-width core H conjoined to this same wide portion, the exact count is #H minus the number of listed codewords satisfying H, directly computable by one core query and polynomial work. Thus additive corrections and multiplicative low-rank structure cover different inputs.
