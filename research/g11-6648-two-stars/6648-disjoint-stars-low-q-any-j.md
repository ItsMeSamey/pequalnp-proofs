# Two disjoint stars, c=0 and q≤7 for arbitrary j≥3

We exclude failure of U≤j+q for the original least size-21 G11 TopForm circuit with two disjoint starred edges. Thus U=14, j+q≤13, 2t+l=22−j−q≥9. Use the physical endpoint import ledger in 6648-two-disjoint-stars-three-junk.md. The sum m of its positive-raw/empty-raw paid OR imports must be ≥4: m≤3 gives 2t+l≤8 by the all-concentrated, 2+1 and three-single partitions independently of j. The seven vertices outside the four star endpoints are called exterior. The exterior induced graph has vertex cover number ≥3 since G11 has cover number seven. A dirty AND cannot have two raw variable pins (clean on an edge, never active on a graph minterm on a nonedge, duplicate pin redundant).

Let X be the set of exterior variables directly read by any OR. It covers the exterior graph. Indeed, on an edge pair avoiding all four endpoints and X, no true raw input enters an OR. At the first active dirty AND, both true pins would have to be raw: earlier ORs cannot be true without a true raw source or an earlier true dirty AND, and clean AND ancestry is forbidden. Such an AND does not exist. Hence no ordinary top accepts this pair. Consequently |X|≥3, and at least two ORs outside the m endpoint imports directly read exterior raw labels. They are paid, since a Lambda reader would star its exterior input. In particular q≥6, disposing of q≤5.

When m=4 the endpoints split into a repeated group at ab with ALL imports there and a single other raw top c∧d. To see this, if each group has an unimported endpoint then the two such raw variables cover all graph edges. The all-four-positive import partition yields l=0,t≤4, hence 2t+l≤8. The 2+1+1 partition yields l≤1 and t≤3 (one group has unimported endpoint, the other has at most one on at least one side). All other possibilities with a fully imported group and a third or fourth imported endpoint have already been considered. A group having neither endpoint imported can contain only the pure physical product, since shared clean pins fuse. This reasoning does not require P=13.

**Two exterior ORs.** If at most two ORs directly read exterior variables, the required three distinct X labels force at least six distinct exterior variables to have direct dirty AND gates. For two pure exterior pairs, disjoint pairs leave every exterior vertex needing an AND, since a sole raw-raw OR is impossible by its C4 singleton masking; overlapping pairs can exempt only the shared label. For one pair and a third exterior variable on the other OR, only that third variable can avoid an AND. These exhaust two gates including ≥3 distinct exterior labels. At q=6, m=4 and there are exactly two such ORs. If neither reads c,d, both c,d require additional dirty AND readers to detect a neighbor other than their mate. If an OR reads c or d, both exterior ORs must have raw pins, with three different exterior labels appearing once in them; all seven exterior variables then require dirty ANDs, and the other single-group endpoint also needs one. Either way j≥8 contradicts j≤7. If q=7 and m≥5 there are again at most two exterior ORs and j≤6. Exactly six exterior variables could require ANDs and all junk gates would be on different such variables. The one remaining exterior vertex would have no incident exterior edge: such an edge requires two distinct active dirty donors, but just one of its two endpoints has a donor. Then X without that vertex must still cover the exterior graph and have size ≥3, forcing |X|≥4, which is possible only with two disjoint raw pairs and therefore seven different mandatory AND labels. This excludes q=7,m≥5.

**q=7, m=4.** There are three other paid ORs and at least four dirty ANDs directly reading exterior variables, by the seven-reader count. Let their number be r. If r=4, all seven exterior reader gates are exactly those four and the three ORs. Equality in the reader capacity count makes each multiply-read exterior variable have exactly two readers, each with two multiply-read exterior raw pins. Every privately read exterior OR has singleton-negative nonraw sibling. In particular no remaining OR directly reads c or d. Both need distinct dirty raw readers besides c∧d. This exhausts j≤6. On an exterior graph pair the c,d-gated donors are false, and acceptance requires two among the four exterior-gated donors. Their labels are distinct by capacity equality. The exterior graph is then supported on four vertices, C4-free and hence covered by at most two vertices, contradiction.

If every junk gate is exterior raw-gated, including r=j=5 or 6, the four star endpoints induce only their matching: with no exterior raw input all junk outputs are false. At least two distinct exterior vertices are neighbors of c or d, since there are no two adjacent degree-two vertices in G11. Each such neighbor v requires at least two gates with its own raw pin: on cv or dv the single cd top is off, the repeated ab group has both raw labels absent, thus at least two different junk donors are active. Those exterior vertices are repeated labels among the at most six raw-gated donors. On every graph edge avoiding a,b,c, both activated donors are still required. All such edges can be covered by at most three vertices: choose all the repeated raw labels and a cover of edges between singleton labels. For two repeats at most two singleton labels remain, needing ≤1 additional vertex; three or more repeats leave no singleton labels. This gives a cover of G11 of size at most 6, impossible.

The remaining possibility r=5,j=6 has just one other junk AND. If that gate directly reads c or d, say c, then on every graph edge avoiding a,b,c at least two of the five exterior-raw donors must activate. The resulting subgraph again has cover ≤3. For all five distinct labels it is supported on five vertices and is not complete by C4-freeness. With one repeat choose it plus a cover on the remaining ≤3 singleton labels (≤2 vertices). With two repeats at most one singleton remains. This contradicts the required cover ≥7−3=4. Finally if neither c nor d has any dirty raw reader, each must directly feed an OR besides cd. All three nonendpoint ORs directly read exterior variables: two such ORs would force ≥6 exterior-raw gates by the two-OR argument. Thus two of them are c∨u,d∨v with exterior u,v. If u=v, the third OR must involve two new exterior raw labels to have |X|≥3. Those labels each occur on a single two-raw OR, hence both require AND readers; only u can avoid an AND. If u≠v, both could avoid AND only if the third gate is u∨v, which leaves |X|=2. More explicitly, a third gate with two raw inputs can exempt only previously used labels that it reads again; a third gate with a nonraw input can exempt only its own singleton raw input. In every permitted case at most one of seven exterior variables avoids an AND, contrary to r=5. This finishes q=7.

No mask enumeration or projected leastness is involved. The matching condition matters; the adjacent-star case has a different raw-input ledger.

## The last q=8 case, j=5

With the earlier all-q j=3 and j=4 results, the only matching case left at q≥8 is q=8,j=5: above q=8 the deficit condition forces j≤4. Here P=13. Two exterior ORs would require at least six different dirty ANDs by the two-OR argument. Hence m is 4 or 5. Let r count exterior-raw-reading dirty ANDs; no dirty AND directly reads two raw variables. A useful slot bound is that n nongated exterior vertices (no direct dirty raw AND) together consume at least 2n slots among the binary nonendpoint ORs. A vertex occurring at two or more raw OR pins consumes at least two slots. A vertex with just one reader has a private OR whose other pin is singleton-negative, hence nonraw, so reserve both its slots. These charges are disjoint across the n vertices. This permits repeated raw-gated labels, sharing through nonraw wires, and other raw labels outside the exterior.

### Five endpoint imports

There are three other ORs and r≥4. If r=4, the seven exterior readers saturate. The four corresponding exterior literals V are distinct and private; the remaining three U are the only exterior raw OR labels, since the four V have sole AND readership. Every exterior edge meets U. At most the fifth donor can activate on a U-U pair, so all exterior edges meet V also. U's three ORs must either be three private sole gates or the three raw-pair edges of a cycle on U: equality forces every nonprivate raw OR to read two nonprivate U labels and each such label to occur twice, with duplicate gates excluded. Before the earliest dirty AND the only available nontrivial ORs are these pure pair gates on U, because each private sole OR needs nonraw singleton-negative input and endpoint gates require imports. A private V-AND cannot be earliest: its other operand must accept each of its at least two neighbors and a pure support of at most two then makes that AND clean or fails. Thus the remaining donor F is earliest. If F directly reads another exterior variable that would contradict r=4; if it reads a star endpoint F vanishes on exterior pairs. Otherwise it is the product of two distinct pair supports on U, with at most one common singleton p. For an exterior edge uv with u in U and v in V, only F and the v donor can be active; F depends only on U. Hence u=p and the exterior graph has a cover of size at most one, impossible.

If r=5 all junk ANDs have exterior raw literals. With five distinct literals there are two nongated exterior labels consuming four of the six nonendpoint OR slots. Only two gated labels can enter the ORs. But every exterior edge lies on two gated labels and must meet the raw OR label cover, requiring three or more gated OR labels. If there are repeated literals, at least three nongated vertices consume all six slots. No gated exterior label then occurs in an OR. Every exterior edge meets a nongated vertex, so its other vertex must carry at least two donor labels to activate two different dirty gates. At most two labels have this multiplicity, again failing the exterior cover lower bound three.

### Four endpoint imports

As above cd is the pure singleton terminal and ab the repeated group. The remaining four ORs must include all exterior and all raw c,d OR readers. Thus r≥3. If r=3, exterior readership is saturated, c,d have no OR readers and must each consume a new direct dirty raw AND. On an exterior edge these two donors vanish; the remaining three, each raw-gated at a distinct exterior variable, force every exterior edge to be supported on those three variables, whose graph has cover at most two. If r=5, all junk gates are exterior-gated. The matching of the four star endpoints is induced, and at least two different exterior neighbors of c,d must have double dirty-AND multiplicity, exactly as in the q=7 proof. With five donors this makes every exterior edge meet one of at most two repeated labels, covering the exterior graph.

If r=4 the fifth dirty gate cannot directly read c or d. Were it c-gated, on G11 minus a,b,c every edge needs two true exterior-raw donors because cd is off, and only four such donors remain. Distinct labels give a C4-free four-vertex support with a two-vertex cover; with repeated labels include all repeats and cover the at most two residual singleton labels, still using at most two. This contradicts the at-least-four vertex-cover lower bound after deleting three vertices. The d case is symmetric (delete a,b,d).

Both c,d must therefore enter OR gates to sense their other neighbors. At least three exterior vertices have no raw AND. Four nonendpoint ORs have eight pins, and c,d spend at least two. By the slot bound, exactly three exterior vertices U are nongated, all four others V gate one distinct dirty AND each, and there is no slot left for any other raw label or uncharged gate operand after accounting for U,c,d. Every V label is thus privately read by its AND, and every exterior edge meets U by the raw-OR cover. U-U pairs cannot activate two dirty gates. Before the earliest dirty AND, all available nontrivial pure ORs are distinct direct raw pairs from U∪{c,d}: if an OR slot is not one of those raw labels it is the singleton-negative sibling of a private U reader and hence cannot be a pure disjunction. Endpoint imports and Lambda traces still require an earlier dirty source. As above a private V-AND cannot be earliest. The fifth dirty gate F therefore is earliest; it cannot read any raw exterior variable by r=4, and a directly read interior raw would make F false on exterior pairs. In the remaining case F is a product of two distinct pair supports in U∪{c,d}, hence has at most one positive singleton. An exterior pair with u in U and v in V must activate both F and the unique v-gate, forcing u to be that singleton. This gives a one-vertex exterior cover, impossible.

This eliminates the last q=8,j=5 case, and the two earlier three- and four-junk proofs now give the matching s=2,c=0 surplus inequality for every q and every j≥3.
