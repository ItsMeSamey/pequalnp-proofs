# Arbitrary original star mask: one surviving dirty AND

Let C be any vertex cover of the ORIGINAL starred edges in a monotone TopForm circuit computing a simple graph G. Set C together with any raw variables R false. Suppose this makes all but at most one original dirty AND identically false on the entire remaining cube. There is no requirement that C or R be independent, that R be disjoint from C, or that every killed wire have a private raw input. Let G' be the induced graph after these false assignments. Then G' is empty or its edge function has a monotone CNF of at most three clauses.

Indeed write D for the one possibly nonzero dirty AND. Every AND-operand wire up to the first clean top equals an OR of its uncut raw support plus some earlier dirty ANDs, by following only ORs to their frontier. TopForm excludes clean AND ancestry. In particular the two operands of D have only raw terms after the other dirty ANDs vanish. Thus D is the conjunction of two raw disjunctions. In any clean top A, before the cut a centreless top already has an empty raw pin, and at an ordinary top every cross between its two raw supports is an original star. Covering all such edges leaves a whole raw side empty (if both retained a label their cross would evade C). Hence after the cut one side is either zero or D. Its product with the other side is zero, D, or D times a raw disjunction. Taking the disjunction over ALL clean tops gives zero, D, or D times ONE global raw disjunction. All algebra is exact on the restricted cube and uses the actual original topology. It also applies if no dirty AND survives.

For a nonempty monotone graph function written as the product of ≤3 raw clauses there are ≤3 maximal independent sets: each maximal false assignment must be the complement of an entire clause, by maximality. We prove that a simple graph without any four-cycle and with ≤3 maximal independent sets has vertex-cover number ≤2. List its maximal independent sets using three indices, duplicating sets if necessary. Label each vertex v by the nonempty index subset I(v) of sets containing v. Distinct vertices are adjacent exactly when their index subsets are disjoint, since every independent pair extends to a maximal independent set. Vertices carrying all three indices are isolated; any two carrying at least two indices are nonadjacent.

If there is no singleton index class the graph is edgeless. If there are at most two singleton classes and each has size one, their vertices cover the graph. If some singleton class has ≥2 vertices, they have the same open neighborhood of size ≤1, since two common neighbors would make a four-cycle. At most one other singleton class can be present, and if present it consists of just one vertex u, the entire common neighborhood. All remaining edges must touch u, as size-two labels never meet one another by edges. Without that other singleton class the single common neighbor suffices.

If all three singleton classes occur they each have size one by the same four-cycle argument. An additional neighbor of singleton i beyond the other singleton vertices must carry the complementary index pair. The three complementary-pair types cannot all occur: choosing one vertex of each would give an independent triple (pairwise overlapping index pairs) which does not lie in any one indexed maximal independent set. Thus some singleton has no such extra neighbor. The other two singleton vertices then cover every edge. This proves τ(G')≤2.

As each false vertex can be put in a cover of the original graph together with a cover of G', the issued G11 with τ(G)=7 satisfies |C∪R|≥5 under the one-surviving-donor hypothesis. If every dirty donor is killed the cofactor is edgeless, so |C∪R|≥7. These conclusions hold for any q,j,c and any original star mask.

## Consequences across the q=4 through 9 table

Write h=τ(stars), and let C vary over all minimum star covers. Every C of size ≤4 must itself leave at least two dirty donors alive. More generally every set of ≤4−h additional labels outside that C must leave at least two. A convenient exact filter: for each donor record all witnessed mandatory labels v with D_i⇒v. Kill every donor meeting the proposed C∪R via one of these labels; if at most one remains and |C∪R|≤4, exclude the placement. Cofactor-implied kills detectable without single mandatory labels only strengthen this test. For each h=1,2,3,4, the respective extra-raw budgets are 3,2,1,0. This depends on graph mask and original donor placement, not on q. For instance:

- h=1 excludes any three raw-mandatory dirty gates at j=4 and any two at j=3 (labels may repeat). At j=5 four mandatory gates require four distinct labels outside each singleton star cover if only these certificates are used.
- h=2 excludes any two raw-mandatory dirty gates at j=3. At j=4, if three are mandatory then their three witness labels must be distinct and outside every minimum two-vertex cover. This applies to any size-two cover, beyond disjoint two-star masks.
- h=3,j=3: the two raw-mandatory gates, if present, must have distinct labels both lying outside every minimum three-vertex cover. More generally a single outside label cannot eliminate all but one after the cover is fixed.
- h=4: two dirty donors must survive every four-vertex original star cover. For h≥5 this cardinal test alone does not rule out a placement.

A complementary exact graph-side filter is available even when |C∪R|≥5. If the induced graph G' has three independent MATCHING edges (an induced matching of size three), a triangle plus a disjoint induced edge, or any induced subgraph with ≥4 maximal independent sets, the one-survivor placement is impossible by the CNF bound. In particular an induced pair of independent matching edges already has FOUR maximal independent sets and suffices. These are tests on original masks and chosen raw placements; no blanket closure of the remaining scalar signatures is asserted. No numerical search was used.
