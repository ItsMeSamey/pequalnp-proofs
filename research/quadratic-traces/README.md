# Exact affine-chart costs for quadratic traces

Let Q=(Q₁,…,Q_e) be Boolean polynomials of degree at most two on n input bits. Let B_i(x,y)=Q_i(x+y)+Q_i(x)+Q_i(y)+Q_i(0) be their alternating polar forms. Write d for the maximum dimension of a binary subspace W on which every B_i vanishes identically.

**Trace theorem.** The graph {(x,Q(x))} has maximum affine-flat dimension d. Its minimum number of flats in an exact cover and its minimum number in a partition are both 2^{n−d}. Projection onto the input coordinates is injective on the trace. Consequently any trace-flat projects to an input coset on which every Q_i is affine. The mixed second differences on that coset equal B_i(u,v) and are independent of its base point. Conversely, for any maximizing W, every coset parallel to W has all Q_i affine on it, and its graph is a flat. Counting 2^n points gives the lower bound, and the cosets attain it. Invertible affine changes of ambient coordinates preserve these numbers; projecting a union of flats from auxiliary coordinates also projects each flat to a flat.

This characterizes graphs of quadratic maps. For a general multilayer Boolean circuit the values of intermediate gates as functions of primary inputs can have degree higher than two. Its local gate equations *on trace variables* nevertheless have degree at most two. Finding a common isotropic direction for their polar forms makes all residual equations affine on each coset of that direction, allowing exact counting by linear algebra per coset. This gives an upper bound for solution counting; the minimum cover theorem concerns the graph of the residual map on **all** assignments, rather than its zero set alone.

## Computing d

For one alternating form of rank 2k, symplectic elimination gives d=n−k. For two forms A,B let R be the normal rank of A+tB over the rational function field F₂(t). Then d=n−R/2. The following construction yields an optimal basis with polynomially many binary-field operations. The normal rank gives an upper bound because a common isotropic subspace extends to one for the pencil over the rational function field, where a rank-R alternating form admits dimension at most n−R/2.

If A is singular choose a nonzero v in its radical. If v is also in the radical of B, quotient by v, recurse and lift, adding v to the result. The dimension drops by one and R does not change. Otherwise restrict to the hyperplane B(v,·)=0 and quotient by v. These are operations over F₂. Over F₂(t) this hyperplane is exactly the perpendicular to v for A+tB. For an alternating form with v nonradical this symplectic reduction removes one nondegenerate plane, dropping both ambient dimension and rank by two. Lifting the recursive isotropic space and adding v therefore attains the upper bound.

When A is nonsingular, let T be the unique operator with A(Tu,v)=B(u,v). It is self-adjoint for A. The cyclic subspace C generated by any nonzero x under T is common isotropic. Indeed A(x,T^{2j}x)=A(T^jx,T^jx)=0 and A(x,T^{2j+1}x)=B(T^jx,T^jx)=0. Self-adjointness gives the result for all pairs of powers and both forms. Its A-perpendicular is T-invariant; both forms descend on C-perpendicular modulo C and the first remains nonsingular. Induction and lifting give a common Lagrangian of dimension n/2. All steps use kernels, basis choices, restrictions, and quotients from polynomial-sized binary matrices. This reproduces consequences of the classical theory of alternating pencils. [Gow and Laffey on pairs of alternating forms](https://doi.org/10.1016/0024-3795(84)90139-3)

Normal rank can differ from every rank evaluated over the base field. For instance take three two-dimensional symplectic blocks scaled respectively by 1, t and 1+t. Its normal rank is six, whereas A, B and A+B each have rank four. The optimum is three, and the minimum trace cover has eight flats.

For an unbounded list of forms, deciding whether d is at least a supplied value is NP-complete. The certificate is a basis; test every pair of basis vectors on each form. Hardness already holds for graphical forms, one per edge, with Q_{ij}=x_i x_j. In that case d equals the independence number α(G), a known graphical alternating-matrix-space theorem. A short proof chooses d independent coordinate functionals on W. Any adjacent two would have a nonzero alternating product on W. Conversely a coordinate span on any independent set is common isotropic. As a result the graph-product trace has exact affine-cover cost 2^{n−α(G)}, attained simply by fixing all bits in a minimum vertex cover. On a bipartite gate-interaction graph, matching and a minimum vertex cover therefore compute an optimum partition implicitly in polynomial time. [Bei, Chen, Guan, Qiao and Sun](https://doi.org/10.1137/19M1299128)

For arbitrary affine operand forms L_i,M_i of binary product gates, a syndrome subspace D in the dual linearizes all product residuals on every coset of its kernel exactly when it meets each two-dimensional span of the independent operand covectors. This follows because their restrictions to the kernel must be linearly dependent. Dependent original covectors give affine products already. Given such D, its 2^{dim D} branches leave linear equations to solve. More generally, if after Gaussian row operations the span of quadratic homogeneous parts has dimension h, retain h quadratic equations and eliminate the affine ones first. Character orthogonality on the h residuals gives 2^h quadratic Gauss sums; each is evaluable by binary symplectic elimination with affine terms. This last counting method can aggregate many affine charts. Exact chart cost alone does not lower-bound it.

## Threshold example and uniform witness cost

Partition n=tk primary inputs into t cliques of size k and compute all edge products. Add a bounded-fan-in OR tree on the product bits. At least 2^{n−2} inputs accept, by fixing a single tested pair to one. Projection of any affine flat of accepting gate traces onto the inputs and edge outputs gives a flat in the graphical trace. Hence each contains at most 2^t points (the full deterministic trace is unique per input). Every exact affine-flat cover of the accepting full trace needs at least 2^{n−t−2} terms. For k of order √log n the circuit has Θ(n√log n) gates, the size scale in the quantitative witness-circuit reduction, while the explicit affine-branch count exceeds 2^{n/2}. The circuit itself is easy to count: exactly (k+1)^t assignments reject. Clever aggregation and other representations remain possible.

In the OpenAI appendix the unary hierarchy language has index decoder length n+O(log n), canonical-variable address length dn, and winning circuit size threshold c n√log n. Infinitely often the produced formula is satisfiable and **every** satisfying assignment has circuit complexity above threshold. If all sufficiently large satisfiable formulas had even one small assignment circuit, guessing it and testing the three-copy violation circuit would decide the hierarchy language too fast. Any globally consistent assignment selector inherits this bound. Taking the bitwise OR of all possible witnesses typically fails consistency. The explicit canonical selector is computed by exponential-length adaptive NP queries. Padding the address length n to N=2^{Θ(n)} puts the selector in P with an NP oracle by that algorithm, while the inherited lower bound becomes only Ω(log N √log log N). Padding of length n f(n) preserves superlinear strength from this transfer only if √log n/f(n) is unbounded on the hard lengths; it does not turn exponential witness recovery into polynomial time. Even a selector in NP on the original inputs would give only a superlinear circuit lower bound, compatible with polynomial time. [Source quantitative proof](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/quantitative-circuits.tex)

## Finding a small affine branching space for actual gates

Over the two-element field a two-dimensional operand plane P has precisely three nonzero vectors. To decide whether a dual space of dimension at most k hits every gate plane, start with the zero space D. If all planes meet D, return D. Otherwise, if its dimension is below k, select a plane disjoint from D. Every feasible extension of D contains at least one of its three nonzero vectors. Recurse on the three spans obtained by adjoining one vector. Each child increases dimension by one. Gaussian elimination tests intersection and span membership, so the search uses O*(3^k) deterministic time and polynomial space. This finds a branching space on traces of ordinary two-input nonlinear gates, including after substitution of solved affine equations. Once found, enumerating its at most 2^k syndromes leaves linear systems. The branching argument concerns rank-two polar forms and need not apply to more general quadratic pencils.

For a pair of alternating forms with nonsingular first form, see also Gow and Laffey. The singular reduction is proved above. The finite checks compare optimum dimensions and ordinary ranks. [Gow and Laffey](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0024379584901393)
