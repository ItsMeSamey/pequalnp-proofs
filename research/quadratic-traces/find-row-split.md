# Finding a shared quadratic row space

Given polar matrices M_i of binary quadratics, a b-dimensional direction Y supports a common pure-y quadratic carrier exactly when Y is totally isotropic for every difference M_i−M_1. Indeed equality of polar forms means the restricted quadratics differ only by an affine function; translation to any row coset adds only linear and constant terms. A supplied basis is checked by restricting matrices. The best exact split can be NP-hard even for rank-two differences: use one zero matrix and differences e_u e_vᵀ+e_v e_uᵀ for graph edges. For an isotropic Y the restrictions of endpoint coordinate functionals to Y must be dependent. Choose dim(Y) independent coordinate restrictions (they span Y*); the corresponding vertices are independent. Conversely every independent vertex set spans an isotropic coordinate subspace. Hence the maximum dimension is exactly the graph independence number, even if all affine address coordinate changes are allowed. This graph reduction is in the literature [Bei et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.03950). It is an obstruction to optimal split discovery, not to exact score evaluation by another algorithm. If approximate rank 2 is allowed for every form, the edge example is vacuous because all its matrices already have rank 2.

**Two differences: deterministic exact theorem, including degenerate pencils in characteristic two.** For two alternating forms A,B on an n-dimensional GF(2) space, the maximum common isotropic dimension is n−r/2, where r is the rank of A+zB over GF(2)(z). Extending scalars gives the upper bound by ordinary alternating form theory. The following recursion attains it using only binary linear algebra, avoiding any root choice in the field of rational functions.

* If A is singular, take any nonzero v in its radical. Put W=ker B(v,·). Both restricted forms descend to W/<v>. Recurse there and take the inverse image, gaining one dimension. If v is in both radicals, over the rational function field the dimension drops by one and the rank does not change. Otherwise v is not in the radical of A+zB, and W is its generic perpendicular, since (A+zB)(v,·)=z B(v,·). Removing the hyperbolic pair by passing to W/<v> lowers the dimension and rank by two each. In either case the lifted answer meets the original upper bound.
* If A is nonsingular, n is even and r=n. Let T satisfy A(Tu,v)=B(u,v); it is self-adjoint for A. For any nonzero v compute its nonempty cyclic subspace L=span(v,Tv,T²v,...) by elimination. The scalar A(v,T^iv) vanishes for every i. For i=2j use alternation of A on T^jv, and for i=2j+1 use alternation of B on T^jv. Self-adjointness then shows A and B both vanish on L. Because T(L)⊂L, the perpendicular L^⊥ for A is perpendicular to L also for B. Descend both forms to L^⊥/L, where A remains nonsingular. A recursively constructed common half-dimensional subspace lifts to dimension dim L+(n−2dim L)/2=n/2.
* The zero-dimensional space terminates. Each nontrivial step decreases dimension. Computing cyclic bases, restriction matrices, radicals, quotients and lifts takes polynomial time.

A matrix space generated by two differences can be found by elimination on matrix entries. Its maximum row dimension is at least ceil(n/2), so balancing the input coordinates is always possible in that case. This is a split-construction result; at most four distinct difference polars also permit grouping by each whole-space carrier, independently of the split construction.

**Controlled residuals.** Start with any exact Y₀ for a chosen pair, including the maximum space above. Let additional forms C_i have ranks r_i on Y₀, and prescribe allowable even residual ranks τ_i. Process them on the current subspace. If the current rank is 2h>τ_i, choose a symplectic basis for its nonsingular part and remove one member from each of h−τ_i/2 pairs. The new restriction has rank τ_i, and prior restrictions cannot gain rank. The returned space therefore has dimension at least dim Y₀−sum_i max(0,(r_i−τ_i)/2), possibly larger by recalculating ranks. Restrict further to a desired dimension if available. A witness basis for any approximate solution is checked deterministically by Gaussian elimination on restrictions. On full-support terms, a row space of dimension b with restricted residual ranks r'_i gives row enumeration poly(n)(N+2^(n−b)sum_i 2^r'_i) for one carrier, as in row-structured-phase.md. These guarantees are conditional on the measured ranks, not converses.

**Fixed-parameter exact recovery.** Intersect the radicals of all polar differences to get R. All differences descend to the c-dimensional quotient V/R. An optimal common isotropic space may include R, since adjoining it cannot affect any restriction. Enumerating reduced-echelon subspace bases of the quotient and lifting the best solution finds the exact optimum in 2^(c²/4+O(c))poly(input) time. The same enumeration works for prescribed residual rank caps: adjoining R preserves rank. This uses joint effective dimension c, not the maximum individual matrix rank (which is two in the hard graph family).

**Balanced decision is already hard, and two generators are a real boundary.** For a graph instance with n vertices and target k, add t=max(0,n−2k) isolated vertices and a disjoint clique on c=2k−n+t+2≥2 vertices. The new vertex count is 2(k+t+1), its independence number is α(G)+t+1, and having a half-dimensional isotropic split is equivalent to α(G)≥k. Thus the decision problem for *balanced* exact splits is NP-complete; bases are short certificates. Separately, a span of three alternating differences need not have a balanced isotropic space. For n=2b sample three independent uniformly random alternating matrices. Each fixed b-space is isotropic for all with probability 2^(-3 binom(b,2)); the number of candidates is at most 4·2^(b²). For b≥5 the expected count is <1. More quantitatively, for integer τ≥0 the probability a given restriction has rank ≤τ is at most 4·2^(-binom(b−τ,2)) by the radical-subspace count. Consequently whenever 4^4·2^(b²−3 binom(b−τ,2))<1 there exist three generators for which every b-space has at least one restricted rank >τ. This holds asymptotically for τ≤(1−sqrt(2/3)−δ)b, any fixed δ>0 below the positive threshold. It is a residual-rank limitation relative to a fixed baseline, not a lower bound on the cost of evaluating three quadratic terms.
