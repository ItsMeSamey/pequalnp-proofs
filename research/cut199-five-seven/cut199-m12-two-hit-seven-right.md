# Two hits in the six-left, seven-right tree

Suppose the only paid left producer is AND(6,7) or OR(6,7), and two right ports accept singleton 3. Pin 3 true and both 6,7 false. Three left inputs (0,1,2) remain, each occurring only once. At most five right ports vary and at most seven right-pure gates remain from a budget of eight: at least one of the two hits was a paid producer which becomes constant. The restricted function is the induced right edge function F_R OR the three terms x_i AND Q_i, where the Q_i are neighborhood ORs over {9,10}, {4,8}, {5,10}. The graph left after pinning contains eleven edges on eight vertices.

Trim constants. Pure-right subtrees attached to a mixed gate give effective cuts. At least four right cuts are needed since the eight left settings distinguish nine upper-face profiles. A four-cut realization can absorb at most one pure-right merge of the original five varying right leaves. Even charging that merge to the free upper bank would leave at most eight paid gates, whereas the four-cut tree replay requires nine. Therefore five effective right cuts are necessary, all originating from individual surviving ports. A pure-left subtree on two raw variables would collapse two distinct left neighborhood queries into one local bit; this is impossible by testing their distinct singleton neighborhoods. Every remaining mixed gate is binary and the cone is a read-once tree.

All pairwise left LCAs are ORs: choose an independent right assignment hitting both respective neighborhoods, set the third left variable to zero, and the induced function of the pair is OR. Each AND on a left leaf's path thus gates it by a pure-right port. On independent inputs, with x_i the only selected left bit, the query Q_i is exactly the conjunction of the gates on its path. Each query needs a private multiplicative port. For i=0,1,2 respectively the independent right sets {4,5}, {10}, {4,9} make that query false and both others true, so the rejecting factor cannot also gate either other leaf. A separate additive right port must supply the nonzero interior when all left bits are zero. With five right leaves the split is either three multiplicative and two additive, or four multiplicative and one additive.

In the first split every query must itself be an individual port on independent inputs. Each additive term can lie under at most one of the three private multiplicative guards. cut199-m12-face-three-guards.cjs 7 enumerates 1,569,329 shared producer-bank states; 512,001 realize all three queries, and none covers the interior by the two permitted additive terms. In the second split, with a single additive port, the interior is a conjunction of that port and any multiplicative guards on its path. The 1,615 producer banks in cut199-m12-seven-meet-banks.json cover all such free conjunctions at seven or fewer physical gates. cut199-m12-face-four-guards-seven.cjs checks every unordered five-port multiset from those banks, permitting repeated signal functions. Twenty-seven pass the interior conjunction and the 22 directed upper-face comparisons; none passes the read-once wedge test.

This eliminates the two-hit branch at total cost at most 21. Three or more hits leave at most four variable right ports and are already excluded by the upper-face four-port bound. The one-hit case with AND(6,7) is excluded because the equal completed left rows for edges (6,3) and (7,3) would need the same nonempty left port mask, while their eligible singleton left masks are disjoint. Thus no genuine one-paid AND(6,7) tree reaches total 21. The corresponding one-hit OR(6,7) case is separate.
