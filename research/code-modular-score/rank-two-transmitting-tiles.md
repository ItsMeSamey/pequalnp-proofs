# A physical transmitting-interval lemma for binary rank-width two

Let G be a connected charged graph with a supplied subcubic cut-rank decomposition of width at most r (in particular r=2). Fix desired dense named-kernel dimension, port count and conserved finite group Γ. Follow an oriented leaf-to-leaf spine, indexing its edge cuts by prefixes P_0⊂...⊂P_m and suffixes R_h=V−P_h; U_h=P_{h+1}−P_h is the nonempty hanging tile. Suppose each transverse matrix G[P_h,R_{h+1}] has rank r over F₂. Every such stretch of computably bounded sufficient length contains a nonempty interval removable by a conservative spanning contraction and induced edges deletion, preserving the full fixed catalogue. No contact multiplicity or graph size bound is assumed.

For every intermediate cut the rows of P_0 span G[P_h,R_h]. Inductively this survives restricting columns to R_{h+1}, where the transverse matrix has rank r; adding rows of U_h cannot increase cut-rank past r. Similarly R_m spans all respective suffix column spaces by reverse induction. In particular G[P_0,R_m] has rank r. Pick r rows of P_0 and r columns of R_m with invertible minor. These same physical left and right anchors are respectively row and column bases at every intervening cut (the fixed invertible minor persists). If a connected component of G[U_h] has a neighbor in P_h, the corresponding nonzero column on P_h has a 1 at some physical left anchor. If it has a neighbor in R_{h+1}, the symmetric assertion gives a physical right anchor. Every tile component contacts at least one side by connectedness. Attach the component wholesale to one such anchor and assign its charge accordingly, fixing the choices once for the stretch. A subinterval with equal prefix vectors in Γ^{2r} therefore contracts entirely into the exterior anchors leaving their charges and port identities unchanged. Ignoring surplus quotient edges gives the induced graph on surviving vertices.

For catalogue preservation, label each suffix vertex by the binary r-vector of its original adjacencies to the fixed left anchors. When adjoining U_h to R_{h+1} to recover R_h, each x in U_h sees y in R_{h+1} by a scalar product c_x·label(y), since the same fixed rows span this cut. Give the tile its own internal graph, charges, permanent suffix labels and these temporary coefficient labels. The gluing operation takes disjoint union, adds exactly this definable bipartite relation, and retains the permanent suffix labels. It induces a map f_h on a common finite state set Q of labelled graph CMSO types sufficient for the prescribed catalogue queries. Closure follows from the disjoint-union composition of fixed quantifier-rank types followed by the quantifier-free edge and label interpretation. In this frame skipping any tile does exactly nothing to the suffix state or its permanent labels. The fixed prefix graph P_0 also attaches by coefficient vectors against these same labels, so the final suffix state determines the desired catalogue of the full graph.

Tag cut h by its cumulative anchor charge vector and the function f_0∘...∘f_{h−1}:Q→Q (identity at h=0). More than |Γ|^{2r}|Q|^{|Q|} cuts force equal tags i<j. Delete U_i through U_{j−1} using the explicit connected anchor branches. Induced adjacency on all retained vertices is exactly the originally composed graph with those tiles omitted: earlier retained tile rows use unchanged suffix labels for every later vertex. Equal functions give equal final CMSO types for every suffix continuation. The induced graph stays connected: P_0 and R_m have fixed anchors with invertible crossing, but this need not connect all surviving components; if connectedness is required as part of the reduction, include the connectedness sentence in Q, as we do here. Induced deletion preserves the supplied width bound by pruning the leaf set and suppressing degree-two nodes. Thus the lemma really constructs a width-preserving physical minor.

The proof needs the transverse ranks, not merely the ranks of the spine cuts. For example an induced six-cycle on ordered vertices l1,l2,u,v,r1,r2 has edges l1l2,l1u,l2v,ur2,vr1,r1r2. All three cuts after l2, u and v have rank two, and both endpoint interfaces are two independent matching edges. Keeping the four exterior vertices individually protected, a straight splice requiring l1r1 and l2r2 is impossible: no interior path joins l1 to r1 without another protected exterior vertex. Swapping r1,r2 in the two interior incidences makes that prescribed splice feasible with identical unlabelled counts. This obstruction concerns specified splices and gives no antichain. Nor does the positive lemma yield a finite kernel for every rank-width-two graph: deficient transverse stretches remain.
