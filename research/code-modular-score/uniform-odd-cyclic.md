# Uniform deterministic FPT for any odd cyclic score with binary markers

For any cyclic odd-order group O and elementary binary group B, unsigned O×B even-support image membership and witness recovery on arbitrary graphs take deterministic f(|O|,|B|) n^C time with a universal exponent C independent of the number of distinct odd primes. The earlier fixed-number-of-primes proof in two-odd-factors.md gives the group lifting step. Two refinements below ensure uniformity.

## Constructing a correction in the large-packing case without a recursive call

Fix one nontrivial cyclic p-primary factor P of O and let Q be the product of the other primary factors and B. To correct any target in H×{0}, where H is the order-p subgroup of P, take k large enough that k edge-disjoint P-nonzero even supports have enough identical full P×Q gains to generate H×{0}. Let F denote the edge Erdős–Pósa threshold for k P-nonzero cycles, as given by the port argument. Apply quiet-recognition.md with budget F to the translated P-labels. Suppose it certifies that more than F edges are required. In this high case k disjoint nonzero cycles exist. Delete edges while the remaining graph is still high. Stop at the first edge e whose trial deletion gives a quiet modulator S of size at most F: this happens by the time all edges would be deleted. Before this last deletion the graph was still high, and now has a known edge transversal K=S∪{e} of size at most F+1. All earlier removals were on the correction graph; their absence is compatible with XOR traceback at the current base.

Each of k disjoint cycles in this graph uses an individual, distinct edge in K. In the port graph enumerate one chosen K wire per cycle, delete those wires and retain their endpoint ports as distinct prescribed terminal pairs. Use Liu–Yoo's rooted vertex-disjoint paths solver to require that each path's P-score plus its chosen wire weight is nonzero. A successful family gives edge-disjoint even supports of nonzero P-score upon closing its wires; disjoint simple cycles guarantee at least one successful trial. Compute their full scores and select a subfamily attaining the required increment, using the coprimality and unique-minimal-subgroup argument. No Q oracle is invoked in this high branch, even for witness recovery. The number of quiet-recognition calls is at most n, with a fixed polynomial exponent, and the bounded-root routing exponent is also fixed.

## Restrict recursive graphic calls to simple 3-connected torsos

Use an outermost block/SPQR dynamic program on an arbitrary graph, convolving independent block tables. For polygon and parallel torsos use the two-state or parity-sum routines. For an R torso with arbitrarily many unary child interfaces apply child-hashing.md: the occurrence predicates at singleton ports use only elementary binary coordinates. The generated queries are on a simple 3-connected graphic torso, with its physical and virtual edges carrying O and enlarged binary labels, but with no pending child tables. The input-size polynomial overhead of the outer hashing is paid once.

For a query on such a simple 3-connected graph G, induct on the total number of prime factors of |O|, counted with multiplicity. The trivial odd part needs only binary elimination. First recursively recover a base at the quotient by H. Translate physical weights and target. In the high P case use the preceding explicit packing algorithm. Otherwise obtain a P-quiet set K of size ≤F. Apply the quiet-ports.md algorithm in G−K, with the following stronger accounting.

For every R torso in the block/SPQR trees of G−K the number of its nontrivial SPQR child pieces is at most 2|K|. Each such pertinent subgraph has only the two poles as attachment vertices within its block. It has strict interior vertices: in a simple graph a portion containing only pole-to-pole edges is at most a single real edge, which needs no child table. If an interior vertex is an articulation of G−K, attach to that interior all the components away from the current block. The expanded portions for different children of this R torso have disjoint interiors, by the block-cut tree and the separating-pair construction. Each expanded portion attaches to the rest of G−K only through the same two poles. The R torso has other vertices outside, since it is simple 3-connected on at least four vertices. If no endpoint of a removed edge is in the expanded interior, removal of the two poles would disconnect G itself. Hence every nontrivial child accounts for a distinct endpoint occurrence among at most 2|K|. This is a bound per R torso and does not restrict degrees of polygon or parallel bags.

At an R torso enumerate the trace and full translated score of every nontrivial child using its table; these are parameter-many choices. Subtract the selected contributions, zero their virtual-edge weights and pin virtual bits using binary marker coordinates, including the parent pin when present. Real P-weights in a 3-connected torso vanish identically by the odd Walsh argument in quiet-ports.md. Thus after verifying the specified local P target, the remaining query has only the strictly smaller odd group and boundedly many binary pins. It is again on a simple 3-connected torso. No occurrence hash or input-dependent vertex/edge enumeration is needed inside this recursion. Direct treatment of block articulation syndromes, F-bit assignments, translation and witness assembly follows quiet-ports.md. An empty graph, a single edge, initial loops, initial repeated physical edges and cut vertices are handled in the outer elementary bag/block routines.

For each fixed parameter choice, the sum of sizes of the SPQR torsos of all blocks is O(n); hence the sum of their Cth powers is O(n)^C for C≥1. Each has at most a parameter-dependent multiplicity of recursive queries (table entries, K-patterns, child scores and trace choices), and all calls return actual witnesses. The initial quotient call is on the same 3-connected graph at a smaller odd factor count. Choosing C at least the fixed exponent for quiet recognition, high packing recovery, purely local scans and decomposition ensures the induction keeps the same C. Factors such as the linear-size constant to the Cth power enter only the parameter function. Binary-group sizes can increase by a computable function of the current parameters but the odd factor count strictly falls on every recursive call. Outer hashing may add a single constant to the final exponent. No global self-reduction adds an input-size factor at each prime.

This argument uses cyclic Sylow subgroups. It does not claim the same result for e.g. F_p² at odd p.
