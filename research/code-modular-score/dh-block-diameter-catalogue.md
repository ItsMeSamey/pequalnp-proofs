# Physical catalogue kernels at bounded biconnected-block diameter

For fixed D, fixed named dimension and protected port count, connected distance-hereditary graphs all of whose blocks have diameter at most D admit computably bounded physical representatives of their full catalogue. Bridges count as blocks. This permits unbounded graph diameter and arbitrarily branching block-cut trees.

Use the ternary presentation, transmitting rewrite and block-count distance identity in bounded-diameter-dh-catalogue.md. Orient a leaf-to-leaf spine from x to y. A blocked star is called singleton on this spine if its hanging center side has exactly one active leaf z. Cut just after this star toward y. The left side's only accessible physical leaf at that cut is z (enter the star at the outgoing arm, leave only by its center). For a second singleton farther along the same spine call its leaf w. Let L be all leaves on the left of the first cut, I the leaves between the cuts, and R the right of the second cut. We have z in L and w in I. Only z in L can contact I or R; only w in L union I can contact R. Hence there are no L--R edges. Every connected-graph excursion out of the induced region I union {z} can be shortened: left-side excursions return at z and right-side excursions at w. Thus this region is connected. Contract every vertex of I into z. The resulting graph on L union R has exactly the two induced side graphs and joins z to precisely the right neighbors formerly incident to w. This is the split tree formed by splicing the two cuts (retaining the left and right incidence marks). Its new graph is the one-vertex sum of G[L] and G[R union {w}] with w renamed z. Each of the two side graphs is connected by the analogous excursion argument.

For each such cut record the total charge of its prefix leaf set and the finite full-catalogue CMSO automaton state mapping the remaining right continuation to the full output, as in accessible-split-run-pumping.md. Use a common orientation by rooting at x; the right argument's own incidence mark stays with the right subtree. Equal records for two cuts give total charge zero on I and identical output after splicing, for every fixed query in the catalogue. Thus this contraction preserves charges, ports, and the full catalogue. In a path with more than M such cuts, a pair exists, where M is the computable number of records. Each move strictly decreases leaf count.

Interleave this move with transmitting-run pumping. The graph after a transmitting splice is exactly the induced graph on the surviving leaves: the removed path introduces no blocked turn between the exterior faces. After the singleton splice it is a one-vertex sum of two connected induced subgraphs with their designated articulation identified. The block-diameter hypothesis persists in either case. Indeed any block of a connected induced subgraph H of a DH graph is contained in an original block; distances within the smaller block equal distances within H (a shortest path cannot profit from leaving and reentering a block), and within H equal original distances by distance heredity. A one-vertex sum creates no block across the sum vertex. The graph represented by the remaining tree is again DH.

At termination an arbitrary spine has at most M singleton blocked stars. Its blocked active sets are disjoint, and every choice of one leaf in each yields an x-to-y geodesic, as in the distance identity. Any internal articulation separating x and y must be one of these singleton leaves: every such geodesic visits it, and independent choices in nonsingleton sets can avoid it. Conversely each singleton separates x and y. Consecutive separating articulations, with x and y as endpoint sentinels, lie in a common block by the block-cut tree, so their geodesic segments have length at most D. Thus the graph distance of the two leaves is at most D(M+1). Since no transmitting run reaches its fixed threshold C, the same counting as in bounded-diameter-dh-catalogue.md bounds the presentation-tree diameter by CD(M+1), hence its size by 1+3(2^{CD(M+1)}−1). The algorithm searches for either rewrite and composes its explicit contraction models. No WQO or bound for general unbounded-diameter biconnected DH blocks follows.
