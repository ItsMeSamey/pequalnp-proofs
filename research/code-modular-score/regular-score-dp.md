# Unsigned group scores on regular matroid cycles

Given a represented regular matroid with weights in a finite abelian group G of order g, a deterministic XP algorithm in g decides and finds a cycle with prescribed unsigned weight sum.

Use a proper unordered sum tree with graphic, cographic and R10 bags. In the cographic graphs each three-port separator is the star at a degree-three vertex. R10 has only zero- and one-port separators. Streit's Proposition 2.2 and Appendix A give this refinement of the constructive Seymour and Aprile–Fiorini decompositions. Root at any bag. A child subtree matroid, including its parent separator, can be represented by elimination of the internal virtual coordinates in the simultaneous bag cycle constraints. Its other elements are real. Adjacent pieces impose agreement on virtual bits and forget them. For a proper three-port T the virtual triangle is itself a cycle with zero real projection. Thus the relevant trace is in F₂³/span(111); representatives differing by 111 can always be aligned. Projection of the subtree's cycle space onto raw F₂^T is surjective since the proper separator contains no subtree cocircuit. The analogous unary statement holds for proper two-sums.

A child table records all attainable pairs (trace, real score). Choose by Gaussian elimination a linear section of the trace projection into the child's entire subtree cycle space. Each real coordinate is a linear function of the trace. For three ports the three nonzero functions are exactly the pairwise differences of raw virtual bits. Sum physical weights by coordinate function, giving three default weights, whose unsigned sum on the active functions is an attainable score. For one port obtain a unary weight, for zero ports the default score is zero. This section only needs to match the trace modulo 111.

Fix an attaining solution for a bag table entry. Hold its central cycle and child traces fixed. Subtract each child's default score from its actual score. When at least g differences are nonzero, take any g of them. Two of their g+1 prefix sums coincide. Reset the intervening children to defaults, preserving the total score. Thus at most g−1 exceptional children suffice. Enumerate their identities, traces and actual scores from their tables. Set their default weights to zero, require their specified traces, and add their tabulated scores separately. For all other children use the defaults.

In a graphic torso replace each child virtual triangle by a new star at its vertices. The star bits are precisely the pairwise differences of former triangle bits, repairing the same degree parities at the vertices; their sum is even. Conversely each such star determines the quotient trace and has two preimage triangle assignments. Replacements on edge-disjoint triangles remain valid even at shared vertices. Put the three default weights on the respective spokes. In a cographic torso replace each child virtual degree-three center by a triangle among its three neighbors, allowing repeated neighbors and loops. Cut bits on the new triangle are the pairwise differences of neighbor colors, hence of former virtual star bits; either color can be restored at the center. Different star centers cannot be adjacent since port sets are edge-disjoint. Put default weights on new triangle edges. For singleton children put the unary weight directly on the port. The parent separator remains intact with zero physical weight.

Pin all exceptional traces by the replacement bits, and pin the parent separator to an arbitrary representative of the queried trace. Each pin is one binary score coordinate, weighted one solely on that edge. This enlarges the group to H=G × F₂^b for b≤3g. Keep each physical edge weight once. A cographic bag now asks for the image of the group-valued Boolean quadratic with edge indicator x_u+x_v−2x_ux_v. A graphic bag asks for an even subgraph with one unsigned H-score: the bounded topological type of a minimal witness has cycle rank O(|H| log(|H|+1)), and the unsigned group-labelled paths algorithm of Liu and Yoo tests its subdivisions as in mixed-cycles.md. R10 is constant size and is enumerated directly. Each central solution lifts by restoring its virtual bits, gluing default sections and stored exceptional witnesses, adjusting 111 on the child port where needed. Conversely every attaining solution is admitted by the exception argument.

There are at most 4g entries per bag. Guessing at most g−1 exceptional children takes |T|^{g−1} times a function of g. Each bag query has fixed-parameter-dependent polynomial running time, giving deterministic XP overall. For witness recovery a bag decision oracle suffices: greedily force edges to zero whenever feasible, deleting in the graphic graph and contracting in the cographic graph (cut loops are always zero). The remaining unforced edges form the unique possible successful support after these restrictions. Traceback yields original physical bits.
