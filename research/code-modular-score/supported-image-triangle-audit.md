# Degenerate gauges and cover preservation

Every physical support permits all pure child traces (0,g), with
g in a fixed subspace G. A child with no nonzero physical zero-trace
word gives a unique linear real response f on its trace domain Q.
Its kernel is exactly G. Every genuine kernel-free baseline at the
same Q also kills G. The discrepancy therefore factors through Q/G,
which is determined by the old real word. Thus discrepancy maps
factor through the assembled physical row space even when raw
compatible tuples have unbounded virtual gauge freedom. The p²
argument applies without a coindependence assumption. With G nonzero,
all projected outside column types on the rank-two triangle plane are
annihilated by G, leaving at most one nonzero type. A one-wire
parallel representative or its empty version suffices.

Cover preservation does fail. The triangular prism has a nonstar
three-edge bond formed by its longitudinal matching. Its planar dual
is the triangular bipyramid K5 minus one edge; the slot is its
equatorial triangle. Replacing that triangle by a star introduces a
third apex, giving K3,3. The resulting row cycle matroid is M*(K3,3),
nongraphic. Wedge m such prisms at one vertex and replace each
matching slot. The original code is graphic; the resulting code is
the direct sum of m copies of the nongraphic code.

In every graphic supercode its shortening to each physical block
is graphic, contains that nongraphic summand, and exceeds it by at
least one dimension. These shortened spaces add directly, so global
excess is at least m. This tests simultaneous preservation of the
cover, not the existence of an alternate efficient sum tree.
