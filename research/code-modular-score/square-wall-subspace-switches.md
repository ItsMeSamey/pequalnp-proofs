# Bounded physical carriers from partial charge switches on a square wall

Consider a rectangular connected square grid with its h=q+b−1 augmented demands for a b-port patch, as in planar-physical-patch.md. Work in a fixed splitting of score space Q⊕L. Every Q label and every exterior port occurs at designated marker vertices along the top side. Include the two top corners as markers even if their projected labels are zero. Interior labels in L may be arbitrary. Assume at least h+1 interior routing rows above the reserved sites below. For each consecutive pair of markers, the intervening top gap has only L labels and its prefix sums (including the empty prefix) cover L. For every nonzero v∈L designate h+1 pairwise nonadjacent singleton sites of label v in the deep part of the grid, disjoint from the top routing rows, with the grid still connected on deleting all these sites and the top interiors of all marker gaps. All designated sites across different values are distinct and nonadjacent. These are checkable sufficient conditions, and bounded degree holds in the grid. The full augmented table has a connected physical carrier of size bounded computably in h,b, valid under any exterior.

Normalize a witness to k≤h+1 connected parts. Those containing markers induce a noncrossing partition in their top order; the others have sums in L and, for k>1, are nonzero. Each of the latter can instead use a different reserved singleton of its prescribed L label. Thus nothing requires reproducing the old geometry of an interior-only part. Each marker block is connected by linking successive occurrences with vertical segments and horizontal intervals at nesting depths, which need at most h+1 grid rows. For any partition the graph of transitions between colors of successive markers in linear order is connected. With each chosen spanning-tree transition use the intervening top gap as a switch: assign a contiguous prefix to its left color and the remaining suffix to its right. The possible prefix sums cover L, so one may transfer an arbitrary L vector across that edge between the two colors. Such switches on a spanning tree generate every tuple of changes summing to zero on the marker colors. This is one simultaneous connected coloring, with scores assigned jointly.

For a physical bound, take one normalized witness of each of the N full table states, available by planar dual routing. Protect every gap in which successive markers change color in any of these witnesses. At most 2hN such gaps are required by the facial run bound. Add a gap between every pair of successive distinct exterior ports if necessary (≤b more) so that no two ports are merged. Selected gaps cut the linear word into nonempty sectors. A sector is monochromatic for every chosen witness. Contract its whole top interval to a single macro vertex, carrying its original charge sum and at most one port. Each survivor corresponds to a consecutive group of markers. Delete downward edges from the interiors of the protected gaps. Their top paths now have degree-two internal vertices. On each path retain a bounded set of cuts at prefix positions representing every element of L, together with its beginning and end; contract the nonempty intervals between successive cuts to single vertices. The set of possible prefix transfers remains all of L. Empty gap paths are harmless for L zero. Port boundary labels and any charge now carried by a macro vertex are kept.

Temporarily remove the internal vertices of the surviving gap paths and the designated deep singleton sites. The connected remainder contains every macro vertex. Indeed before contracting the sectors it contains the connected residual grid from the hypothesis, and the sector contractions can only join it further. Mark each macro vertex and all neighbors of the deep singleton sites. Their number is bounded by 2hN+b+1+4(h+1)(|L|−1). Compute a connected ordinary physical minor of the remainder preserving its entire uncharged marked partition family on these vertices, as in planar-few-face-physical-minor.md. All of its branch sets are spanning, and its actual charges push forward by XOR. Reinsert the bounded path interiors and named singleton sites with their original physical edges and charges, attached to the retained respective neighbors. These latter vertices acquired no extraneous charge. Every step represents actual contractions/deletions in the original graph.

For each chosen witness its macro colors form a noncrossing partition. Using one original column per macro, the nested interval routes avoid deleted gap interiors and deep sites. Extend to a spanning partition of the connected remainder. Its induced partition on the marked macro and neighbor vertices survives the uncharged minor; extend the resulting connected pieces to a spanning partition there. The Q sums are the originals, because all quotient mass lies in the appropriately colored macros. Use a reserved singleton for each former marker-free part. Attach unused reserved sites through any retained neighbor. The unused non-transition gaps are filled with their common endpoint color or with an arbitrary prefix assignment if endpoints differ. The reserved spanning-tree gaps now correct *all* remaining L discrepancies: their total over marker colors is zero since the total charge is fixed and the singleton sums were restored exactly. Connectivity is preserved by gap prefix/suffix attachments. For k=1 just use the connected minor. This recovers every original table state, while minor monotonicity prevents new ones. The simplified grid, switch paths, ports and bounded uncharged minor are a bounded physical carrier. There is no restriction that the original table be the whole subspace lattice.
