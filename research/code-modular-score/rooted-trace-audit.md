# What the finite opposite-unique bound buys

The sharp-opposite-core.md full-selector proof supersedes the earlier 34-element bound. Its reviewed SHA-256 is 7810d99e0ca03e099ef08f7af645af99e92ab5bb78fc67452a2ae46aac1bd561. Two distinct opposite unique raw witnesses imply at most 2^(t−1) ordinary elements, eight at four bits, and no missing target is needed. For two already supplied witnesses there is a constructive pair-preserving reduction even without uniqueness.

In the [I|R] chart write selector s=(dual basis bits, primal nonbasis bits) and J with off-diagonal blocks R and R transpose. The two conflicting physical bits at each site are s_i and (Js)_i; overall conflict parity vanishes by binary orthogonality. Opposite partition witnesses have selectors a,a+1 with Ja=1+a and J1=1. Project their linear score modulo q+r; its kernel K has codimension h≤t−1 and contains 1. For z∈K the conflicts of a+z and a+1+z partition supp((I+J)z) and are each even. If ker((I+J)|K) contains z outside span(1), then z gives feasible replacements. Otherwise the image has dimension n−h−1 and is an even code (J is symmetric and J1=1). At n>2^h, two columns of a parity check with first row all ones coincide, so elimination finds z∈K with (I+J)z of weight two. One of the two conflict sets must be empty. Either way the new selector differs from both originals and has score q or r in the quotient. Keep it as the witness of its own target, pairing it with the old witness of the other target. The pair is no longer opposite at every site, so one deletion imposing zero primal bits or contraction imposing zero dual bits preserves both. For arbitrarily supplied nonopposite starting witnesses use such a site immediately. Repeat until n≤2^(t−1). Each step takes a polynomial number of binary matrix operations; there are at most the initial n steps.

Exact lifting is essential. For a recorded set of deletions and contractions, shorten the physical cycle space on deleted sites and the cocycle space on contracted sites. Their independent restrictions to the surviving elements have linear sections. The score shifts from their two section kernels together with any preexisting freely available shift space form a single subspace H, independent of the surviving raw pair: lifts cannot conflict on removed elements because one physical role was forced zero at each. Project scores modulo H and use the resulting actual dimension when computing h. For any chosen target lift, solve for the two section-kernel corrections and original free offset by elimination, recording or verifying the physical support pair. If q,r coincide in the quotient, a single raw pair can realize each using its own offset and all ordinary sites can be removed compatibly. This only keeps the supplied targets; finding a witness for an unknown target is separate.

The existing augmentation in proofsystems/rooted-core/README.md preserves both channels. If H is a physical representation and L,D give t primal and dual score forms on original bits, use columns (H_e,L_e), terminals h=(0,I), and z=(H Dᵀ,0), fixing z-primal and h-dual to zero. The h-cycle bits record Lc and z-cocycle bits record Dd. Encode the target by their XOR in the original score space (including the stated common offsets where present). Taking a physical minor while preserving the named terminals leaves exact linear lifts: cycles are zero on deleted sites and cocycles are zero on contracted sites, so separately lifted witnesses are automatically disjoint there. This verifies terminal data but does not determine a root-preserving contraction set without a search.

## Exact algebra for fixed roots

Let S be fixed roots, H_S their columns and U all other column vectors. Let V_o=span U and W=span(H_S)∩V_o, of dimension λ. Define Cmin=ker H_S and Cmax={z:H_S z∈W}. Given a specified terminal cycle space T, first require Cmin⊆T⊆Cmax. Contracting K of the ordinary elements and deleting the rest yields exactly the space {z:H_S z∈span K}. Thus T is achievable iff span K∩W equals D=H_S(T). Choices D=0,W are immediate. Under duality Cmin,Cmax exchange with Cmax^⊥,Cmin^⊥ and T becomes T^⊥, so the new relative dimension is λ−dim D.

If D is the line spanned by t≠0, choose λ independent synthetic columns forming a basis of W whose sum is t. Insert these columns alongside the ordinary columns, and ask if a single circuit contains all synthetic columns. A witnessing K yields an inclusion-minimal subset of K summing to t. This subset is independent and intersects W in precisely the line of t, so with the synthetic basis it forms a circuit. Conversely a circuit containing that basis gives an independent ordinary subset with precisely this intersection. For λ≤3 use the binary three-elements procedure (for two, test common circuit via connected components; for one, test spanning). With duality this handles every terminal trace of connectivity ≤3. Recover a circuit by deleting ordinary elements while the oracle remains positive; verify the represented trace after reconstructing real K. In the dual construction swap the ordinary deletion and contraction sets on return. [Geelen–Gerards–Whittle, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.20960v1).

At λ=4 and desired relative dimension one, this is the binary four-specified-elements-in-one-circuit problem on independent terminals whose span lies in the ordinary span. Conversely in an arbitrary independent four-terminal circuit problem a circuit requires its terminal sum t in the ordinary span. If so the relevant trace is precisely the line of t in W. Cases of lower λ are solved above, so the unrestricted four-independent-terminal problem reduces to this λ≤4 trace test. The cited 2026 paper proves a theorem about at least three elements, not four specified elements in one circuit. No generic fixed-root polynomial bound follows.

Constant root size does not bound contraction rank: take independent ordinary g_1,...,g_m,u, with two roots t=sum g_i and u. The requested trace line is t. Every realizing set contracts all m ordinary g_i and cannot contract the ordinary u. There is an elementary solution on this family; its purpose is to disprove bounded-rank guessing. The nearby-regular finite-template algorithm assumes the contracted set is already specified for each template and enumerates only a bounded number of generator directions. Neither its dimensions nor its affine/quadratic count quantifies an unknown ordinary contraction subspace of arbitrary rank. No hardness follows from this distinction.
