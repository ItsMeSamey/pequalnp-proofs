# Rank-width two outside single clique flips of distance-hereditary graphs

Take two 5-cycles numbered consecutively 0,...,4 and 5,...,9, joined by bridge 4--5. Ordering the vertices as numbered gives a linear rank decomposition of width at most two: each internal cut of a single cyclic block crosses at most its two rim edges, the bridge contributes only at the between-block cut, and all other adjacency across the cut is zero. Rank-width one fails because an induced 5-cycle is not distance-hereditary.

No choice of a single toggled clique on any subset of the ten vertices makes the graph even componentwise distance-hereditary. This is a finite exhaustively verifiable scope control. The accompanying test-flip-scope.py enumerates all 1,024 masks; for each it repeatedly deletes any isolated or pendant vertex, or one of two vertices having equal neighborhoods outside the pair. A graph is componentwise distance-hereditary exactly when this succeeds down to at most one vertex, by the Bandelt–Mulder pruning characterization and heredity. Its result for cycle lengths [5,5] is zero passing masks. This example only addresses one symmetric binary rank-one flip. The graph itself has treewidth two, so the sparse physical-catalogue algorithm still applies.
