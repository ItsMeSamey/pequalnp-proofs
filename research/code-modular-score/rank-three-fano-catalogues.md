# The six Fano catalogue types of all two-connected three-bit blocks

Let G be a simple two-vertex-connected graph, with binary three-bit vertex charges of full rank and total zero. Its catalogue contains 0 and the full space. Call the seven nonzero one-dimensional subspaces points and the seven two-dimensional subspaces planes. Up to an invertible relabelling of the three coordinates, precisely six possible catalogues occur:

* One point is missing; any number 0,1,2,3 of the three planes containing it are missing, with no other omissions.
* No point is missing; at most one plane is missing.

**Keeping a specified point in a simplex core.** Any attained nonzero point q is the sum on either shore of a bond: its witnessing connected partition reduces to at most two parts by the merger lemma, and total zero forces the sums q,q. Fix such connected shores A,B. We show that some physical two-connected four-vertex minor has full charge rank and still witnesses q by the images of these same shores.

At n>4 consider edges internal to A or B for which deletion of both endpoints leaves a connected graph. Contracting such an edge, simplifying loops and duplicates, preserves two-connectivity: deleting the contracted vertex leaves that connected complement, and deleting any other vertex followed by contraction preserves connectivity. These allowable internal edges span each shore. To prove this, start with the original graph and delete an internal edge uv whenever its two endpoints separate the current graph. Every component after removing u,v attaches to both u and v by two-connectivity. Hence there are two internally disjoint alternative u--v paths, and removing edge uv preserves two-connectivity (after removing any third vertex at least one alternative path still exists). If uv lies in A, the whole connected B lies in one component, so a different component supplies an alternative path entirely inside A, preserving A's connectivity as well. The argument for B is symmetric. In the terminal spanning subgraph both shores are connected and every remaining internal edge has connected endpoint complement there, hence also in the original graph. The temporary edge deletions only certify availability.

Write L:F₂^n→F₂³ for the charge map. Merging u,v retains its rank iff ker L contains some vector with different u,v coordinates, since the merged span is the image of the equal-coordinate hyperplane. If all allowable internal mergers lowered rank, every kernel vector would be constant on A and constant on B. Among such vectors only the two global constants are in ker L because the two shore sums equal q≠0. This contradicts dim ker L=n−3>1. Contract a preserving edge, retain the labelled shores, and repeat. At four vertices the charges form a binary simplex: the only nontrivial dependence is their total. This procedure is polynomial given the bond witness. For a core without a specified point, first choose any vertex with nonzero charge: its singleton is a bond shore in a two-connected graph. For distinct physical pins no assertion about keeping them separated in this core is made.

**Simplex catalogues.** Every simple two-connected graph on four vertices has a spanning four-cycle, so is a square, a diamond or K4. Singleton shores give four distinct points. Each complementary pair of two-element shores provides the common pair sum iff both pair edges exist; the three complementary pairs give the other three points. Plane witnesses reduce to three parts, since rank two at zero total needs at least three; with four simplex vertices this means merging the endpoints of one actual edge. Each such pair ij gives a distinct plane, with its unique nonzero annihilating functional taking value 1 exactly on the two merged vertices. The seventh possible normal, taking value 1 on all four vertices, is never obtained. In the square, the lone missing point is the sum of either opposite pair and the three missing planes all contain it. In the diamond, again one point is missing, and its two missing planes contain it. In K4 all seven points occur and only the plane annihilated by the all-ones functional is absent.

**Classification.** Catalogue entries lift along every connected conservative spanning minor. The first core implies G has at least six points. If G lacks a point x, every four-core must lack x; hence all missing planes in G belong to its pencil. If G has all points, take a first core. A K4 already leaves at most one possible missing plane. Otherwise its unique missing point x occurs in G. Apply the specified-point construction to x: a second core is either K4 or misses a different point x'. In the latter case every missing plane in G belongs to both pencils, whose intersection consists of the single plane spanned by x and x'. This proves the exhaustive alternatives. The stabilizer of a point permutes its three incident planes arbitrarily (via GL of the dimension-two quotient), and GL is transitive on planes, proving uniqueness within the stated orbit types.

**Examples.** Number vertices from zero and identify charge masks with integers 0 through 7. Square in cyclic order, diamond containing that square, and K4 on charges (1,2,4,7) give respectively the one-point/three-plane, one-point/two-plane, and zero-point/one-plane omissions. The five-cycle in cyclic order on (1,2,4,1,6) realizes everything. Edges 01,04,12,13,14,23,34 with charges (1,0,2,4,7) miss one point and one plane. Edges 01,05,12,23,24,25,34,45 with charges (1,2,2,1,4,4) miss one point only. These finite checks use direct connected-partition enumeration (rank-three-fano-examples.py). Therefore every large G has a signature equivalent to an example on at most six vertices. This alone does not exhibit one of those examples as a physical spanning minor of G and does not supply a general recognition algorithm to choose its signature. The specified-point construction assumes a witness, and different core witnesses can be different minors.
