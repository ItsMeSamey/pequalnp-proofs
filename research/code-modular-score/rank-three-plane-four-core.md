# Four vertices suffice for one attained plane

Let G be a finite simple 2-connected graph with vertex labels in F₂³ of total zero and span three. The score space of a connected partition is the span of its part sums. If a plane W occurs then G has a 2-connected four-vertex spanning contraction minor with full label rank on which W still occurs. Edge deletion is allowed. Contraction sums labels. Given the witnessing partition the reduction is polynomial. It does not preserve prescribed individual vertices.

Reduce a witnessing partition to three parts by merging adjacent parts whenever the score span remains two. To justify the bound, write the part sums as columns. A merge drops rank only if the indicator of its endpoint pair belongs to the row space of this matrix (equivalently the equal-coordinate hyperplane contains the kernel). The edge indicators of a spanning tree of the connected quotient are independent. If no merge works there are at most two tree edges. Conversely a zero-total rank-two partition has at least three parts. Its three sums are distinct nonzero elements of W.

Keep these three connected parts and full label rank. Call an edge good if deleting its two endpoints leaves a connected graph. At order at least five its contraction preserves 2-connectivity precisely in that case. If an edge is bad, deleting the edge preserves 2-connectivity: every component after deleting its endpoints attaches to both endpoints, so two internally disjoint replacement paths exist. Delete any bad internal edge that is not a bridge inside its part, or contract any good internal edge whose contraction preserves rank, until four vertices remain or neither operation applies. Suppress loops and duplicates.

At a stalled state, any good internal endpoint-pair indicator lies in the three-dimensional label row space. Its evaluations on the three part indicators vanish. The latter evaluation map on that row space has rank two, so at most one such pair exists. If it exists, its indicator is the unique nonzero row obtained from the functional annihilating W. Call its two marked vertices odd. Every other vertex has label in W. All bad internal edges are bridges of their parts. Thus each part is an induced tree, since an internal cycle would have at least three distinct good edges.

A part with connected complement has no bad internal edge: a component of the endpoint deletion disjoint from the complement would give a replacement path inside the part, contrary to bridge status. If the part quotient is a triangle every internal edge is good, giving at most four vertices. Otherwise name the path of parts A–B–C. Only B can have bad internal edges. Each such endpoint pair separates A from C, by the same replacement-path argument. Contract A and C each to one vertex. The resulting graph is 2-connected: removing either terminal leaves connected parts, and removing a B vertex preserves connectivity by the original hypothesis. Two internally vertex-disjoint terminal paths have nonempty disjoint connected interiors in the tree B. Every bad pair intersects both interiors, so its edge joins them. A tree has at most one edge between two disjoint connected subtrees. The number of internal edges is at most two, giving at most five vertices.

At five vertices there is exactly one good and one bad internal edge. If the good edge xy lies in A (or symmetrically C), put B={u,v}, C={c}. The bad pair u,v separates A and c; both u,v see c and both see A. Each of x,y sees B by minimum degree. The bipartite graph from A to B has no isolates and hence a perfect matching. Relabel B so that the label of v is independent of that of c, possible since the sum on B is independent of that on C. Orient the names x,y along a matching xu,yv. Contract xu. The remaining y,v,c are connected, so 2-connectivity persists. The even singletons v,c are independent and the two odd vertices are adjacent via xy. Those singletons and the odd pair exhibit W, and an odd label gives rank three.

Otherwise the middle tree is x–y–z with xy good, yz bad. Write A={a}, C={c}. The vertex x sees precisely one of a,c, since it has degree at least two and deleting y,z separates a,c. Suppose it sees a. The component {c} after deleting y,z attaches to both; the component {a,x} attaches to z via a. If z has label independent of c, contract ax (the remaining y,z,c are connected). If z has label independent of a, contract cy (remaining x,a,z connected). Otherwise z has zero label by independence of a,c, and contract az (remaining x,y,c connected). In every case the two even singleton labels are independent and the odd pair is adjacent. This completes the reduction.

At four vertices full rank and zero total force the unique linear dependence to be the sum of all four labels. The underlying 2-connected simple graph is a square, a diamond or K4. The theorem concerns one plane at a time. It gives no unseeded polynomial search for the initial witness and no common minor for all planes.
