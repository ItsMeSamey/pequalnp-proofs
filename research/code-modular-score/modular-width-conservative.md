# Conservative minors on connected graphs of bounded modular width or lettericity

Fix a finite abelian charge group. A spanning model partitions every physical vertex into nonempty connected sets indexed by target vertices. The sum of charges on each set must equal its target charge. Each required target edge has a physical edge between its sets. Optional finite labels must occur on a designated representative in each set. A fixed list of ports can be protected by adding one separate binary charge coordinate per port, initially nonzero exactly there. This forces the correct port into its own set. Edges beyond the required set may be deleted.

**Transfer lemma.** Suppose a connected graph family has bounded domination number and is well-quasi-ordered by induced embeddings respecting any finite alphabet of vertex labels. Then its conservatively charged, finitely labelled members are well-quasi-ordered under spanning models.

Choose in every member of an infinite sequence a dominating set of size at most the bound. Stabilize the size, order the chosen vertices, and assign every other vertex to an adjacent chosen vertex. Refine the finite labels by actual charge and either the chosen vertex's unique index or its assigned owner's index, keeping these two cases distinct. Stabilize the total charge of nonchosen vertices of each owner. A forward induced embedding for the refined labels matches every chosen vertex. Unused vertices assigned to any one owner now total zero, since all used charges in that owner class match. Add all unused vertices to their matched owner's singleton. Physical adjacency makes these sets connected, the original embedded edges certify all target edges, and all vertices have been assigned. Singletons are covered as well.

**Modular width.** Use substitution terms of maximum arity k (at least two), binarizing union and complete join. An operation is labelled by an ordered graph on its argument positions. Every nontrivial connected represented graph has connected root quotient, with at least two nonempty arguments. Selecting one representative per argument dominates, through a neighboring argument. The finite-alphabet induced WQO is the familiar substitution-tree application of Kruskal's theorem (see Atminas, *Well-quasi-ordering of combinatorial structures*, Theorem 3.1.2 in section 3.1.4, https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/67023/1/WRAP_THESIS_Atminas_2015.pdf). Explicitly use ordered term embedding, with diving and positionwise coupling of equal operation symbols. At a matched branching operation all child paths start in distinct argument positions in order. Skipped levels along a path therefore do not change adjacency between any two matched leaves: their least common ancestor is matched and the two departing argument positions determine adjacency. This proves the induced hypothesis independently of matching the two roots. The transfer lemma proves conservative WQO.

**Lettericity.** Fix a finite alphabet and pass to a common decoder. A word describes the graph by declaring the vertices at positions i<j adjacent exactly when their ordered letters belong to the decoder. Colour-respecting subwords yield induced embeddings by Higman's lemma. In a connected nonsingleton graph every vertex has a neighbor. An earlier neighbor can be replaced by the first occurrence of its letter and a later neighbor by the last. Thus at most twice the alphabet size dominates. The same transfer lemma applies. For the word model see Petkovšek, *Letter graphs and well-quasi-order*, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012365X01000942. The half-graphs with adjacency a_i b_j iff i≤j are two-letter graphs, with unbounded prime order. Indeed a module meeting both shores in a connected bipartite graph can have no outside neighbor, and two vertices on the same shore have distinct neighborhoods in this family.

**Effective catalogue representatives.** Now consider finitely many binary named coordinates. For a selected edge set the named kernel is the orthogonal complement of the span of its component charge sums, since an incidence image consists of vectors summing to zero in each component. For any attainable span of rank d, if there are more than d+1 components their charge map has nullity at least two. An adjacent pair can be merged without reducing rank: otherwise for every adjacent pair the difference of the two coordinate evaluations is zero on the nullspace (merging restricts the domain to equal coefficients, reducing image rank exactly when this equality already holds throughout the nullspace). Connectivity of the component quotient then forces the nullspace into the constants, contradiction. Hence every span has a witness partition into at most d+1 connected sets. The complete list of attainable kernels is finite at fixed name dimension. Each entry is CMSO1-definable by quantifying those sets, requiring connectedness, and computing each coordinate by parity. A fixed conservative minor model is also CMSO1-definable by one connected set per target vertex and finite modular counts, labelled representatives, and edges. Protecting ports physically is separate from these query coordinates.

For each candidate catalogue, decide whether some connected source with this catalogue avoids all already listed representatives for that catalogue. On ranked substitution terms, the vertex universe consists of leaves and adjacency is MSO-definable by the least common ancestor and the two first argument positions. On words use the order and decoder. Finite-tree or finite-word automata with modular counts decide emptiness and return witnesses. Add the witness graph and repeat. Nontermination would give a bad sequence for the conservative WQO. Thus the procedure computes a finite bound and a physical library. Given a term or word one can obtain a model by selecting the corresponding accepting branch-set assignments. The proof gives no numerical small bound.

For a limb with protected ports P, split each external named column additively between limb and exterior. Include the unit vector at every shared port as an extra local query column. Given a choice of physical edges on each side, a global coefficient x belongs to the global kernel iff there exists the same port vector y with (x,y) belonging to both local kernels. This is cancellation of the two equal boundary residuals in characteristic two. Thus equality of the entire augmented local catalogue preserves every exterior query under a common physical gluing. This statement does not require the exterior to have bounded width.

**Limit of the proof.** Paths with the two ends individually distinguished form a labelled induced antichain, although their rank-width is one. The transfer argument therefore cannot establish a conservative WQO for all bounded rank-width graphs. This example is only an obstruction to the induced-embedding premise.
