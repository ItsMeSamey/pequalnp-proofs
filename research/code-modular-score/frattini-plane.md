# Rank-two odd primary scores

Let A be an abelian odd p-group with dim(A/pA)=2. Use the high packing thresholds of plane-graphic-fpt.md for characters of A/pA, with homogeneous families large enough for pigeonholing in the entire group A. If all characters are high then every target of A is attained. In that proof choose row and column gains a,b constant in A and independent modulo p, and intersection gain c constant in A. The generators a,b span A (by induction on p-power digits). If c is in pA, write c=pαa+pβb. The map (s,t) ↦ (s−2pαst,t−2pβst) on pairs modulo exp(A) is bijective: modulo p it is the identity, and altering the input by p^i times a digit alters the output by exactly that increment modulo p^(i+1). Sufficiently many rows and columns therefore attain the entire group. Otherwise interchange the two families if necessary so that c lies outside the span of b modulo p. For any baseline with many increments outside every single residue line, pigeonhole the full gains to obtain two independent residue directions each represented by exp(A) equal full increments. Disjoint switches then attain A. Under a missing-target assumption all remaining universal-collinearity and block-cut arguments in plane-graphic-fpt.md apply in the residue plane verbatim, since they only compare residue directions.

When some character χ is low, use its bounded physical quiet cover and the port transport of quiet-ports.md with χ as the projected score. There is no use of a splitting of χ in that argument: enumerate exceptional child contributions in A and subtract them in A. Every remaining real weight and every attainable remaining child score belongs to ker χ, since the projected weights were proved individually zero on the simple torso. Guard the residual target by membership in ker χ and recurse there with the inherited binary pins. The rank of this smaller abelian p-group is at most two. In elementary polygon or parallel torsos convolve full scores directly.

For binary side constraints first classify each binary character by a physical edge modulator (binary group feedback edge set). Absence of a cover at the half-integral port-graph packing threshold yields a nonzero binary period detected by that character, with zero odd component, by the Ramsey switch argument of prime-graphic-fpt.md. The span L of low characters has L-perpendicular contained in the period group, since each character annihilating all periods must be low. For a basis of L take the union F of its physical covers. On the cycle space, each basis character is a linear combination of the bits on F because it vanishes on their joint kernel. Enumerate those bits, verify the low characters directly, translate the odd score by an affine T-join, and solve the remaining pure odd instance. High binary periods correct the other coordinates in the unrestricted graph. This reduction is decision-only.

For recursive complexity begin each call on a simple 3-connected torso with binary pins. Keep the physical edges fixed for binary feasibility and the later low odd character in one combined bounded modulator relative to that original torso. After removing these edges each resulting simple torso has boundedly many virtual children by the original 3-connectivity charging bound of uniform-odd-cyclic.md. Enumerate all child-table choices in that torso, so every recursive call uses just the smaller group on a simple torso and artificial binary pins. The number of parameter-dependent branches per torso does not depend on the number of vertices. Total recursive input torso sizes per branch have linear total up to a constant, giving a uniform polynomial exponent across p-adic levels (use a size measure including edges). All recursive tables query decision only. One outer physical-edge deletion self-reduction suffices for witnesses. The initial arbitrary graph uses child-hashing.md once before entering this recursion.
