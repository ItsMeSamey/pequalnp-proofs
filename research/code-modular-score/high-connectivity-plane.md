# Four-port plane decision on highly connected graphic hosts

Let G be an undirected simple graph with vertex connectivity at least 7535. Let b_v in F₂⁴ be its named vertex rows with XOR zero and rank four. Fix a two-dimensional U and distinct nonzero elements u,v of U. There is a contraction/deletion minor on the four named columns with kernel U⊥ exactly when disjoint vertex subsets, with no connectivity requirement, can realize u and v respectively.

For an ordinary contraction set X, the named kernel annihilator is the span of the row sums of its connected components. If that span is U, merge components along graph edges while preserving it. On c components let M have their sums as columns. Merging components i,j lowers the span dimension exactly when e_i+e_j belongs to row(M). A spanning tree of the quotient graph has c−1 independent such differences. Thus when no merge preserves the span c−1≤2. Since total XOR is zero, dimension two needs at least three components; their scores are u,v,u+v. Conversely three connected blocks with those sums give the exact named kernel by taking component trees. Necessity therefore holds without the connectivity promise.

For sufficiency shrink each disjoint algebraic witness for u or v to an independent basis subset of at most four vertices with the desired sum. Temporarily delete whatever singleton witnesses occur, at most two vertices. For every larger witness choose a tree on its anchors and replace each edge by five parallel edges. The resulting abstract loopless multigraph has no isolated vertices and at most 30 edges. If nonempty, Wollan Theorem 2.2 gives a batch non-separating rooted subdivision in the residual graph, of connectivity at least 7533=243(30+1). For each five-path parallel bundle compute the four-bit sums on its path interiors. Five vectors have a nonempty zero-sum subcollection. Keep those paths in each bundle. The interiors are disjoint and avoid all branch vertices, so each selected tree union is connected and has the desired sum. Singleton witnesses are already connected. Removing the union of all the chosen paths in the residual graph leaves a connected complement by batch nonseparation; it is nonempty since its sum u+v is nonzero. If both witnesses were singletons, use high connectivity directly.

Track both sums in 16² states, assigning each vertex to either witness or neither. This yields deterministic linear-time decision at the stated connectivity promise. The argument uses existential batch nonseparation and does not furnish an implemented connectivity-preserving witness-construction algorithm. Large complete graphs can still have algebraic NO instances: on labels e1,e2,e3,e4,e1+e2+e3+e4 and any number of zeros, the two nonzero targets e1+e2 and e2+e3 each have two complementary representations on the five nonzero vertices, and every choice for one intersects every choice for the other. For comparison, on a star the connected partitions into three blocks isolate two leaves, so two leaves carrying distinct nonzero labels in U are necessary and sufficient regardless of unrestricted algebraic representability.

Primary source: Paul Wollan, Bridges in Highly Connected Graphs, Theorem 2.2, https://preprint.math.uni-hamburg.de/public/papers/hbm/hbm299.pdf .
