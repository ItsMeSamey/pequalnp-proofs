# Testing bounded collections of minor templates for nearest regular codes

Let G be a fixed injective generator map F₂^d→F₂^n with image C. For the two directional budgets a and b, possible references are precisely the spaces R = {Gx + Tc : Px=0, c∈F₂^b}, for an unknown binary a-by-d matrix P and n-by-b matrix T. Indeed the intersection of C and R is obtained by cutting C by at most a binary functionals and the remaining generators of R can be chosen in at most b slots. Conversely every such parametrization contains G(ker P), so both directed distances meet the respective caps. Zero and redundant slots are allowed.

A minor template fixes seven survivor coordinates in a specified order and a disjoint contracted set K, deleting the rest. Let J=E outside K. To test if a given pattern z on the seven coordinates survives as a cycle, extend it by zeros to J. There must be a c and x such that G_J x=w=z+(Tc)_J and Px=0. Enumerate the 2^b fixed choices for c. For each, w∈im G_J is a single affine-subspace predicate in the unknown entries of T. Choose a linear generalized inverse L on the full target space, with G_JL acting as identity on im G_J, and let N=ker G_J. Subject to that first test the preimages of w are Lw+N. Some preimage satisfies Px=0 exactly when PLw∈P(N). This is equivalent to the conjunction over all 2^a vectors μ of:

* if μP kills N, then μP Lw=0.

The premise is one linear-subspace predicate on the unknown entries of P, even if dim N is large. The conclusion is one Boolean polynomial of degree at most two in the unknown entries of (P,T). OR over c and compute occurrence for all 128 z. Test this table against all labeled F7 and F7* cycle tables. Inputs can use all 7! orders, a constant. The test is exact for the candidate minor; no assertion about other minors is made.

For t templates, there are at most O(128·2^b(1+2^a)t) scalar quadratic and affine-subspace predicates. Enumerate their truth assignments, retaining those passing all templates. To count realizations, use ordinary inclusion-exclusion on subspace predicates prescribed false; all remaining true subspace constraints combine to a single linear system. Parametrize its solution affinely. To impose the prescribed values of q binary quadratic polynomials on that system, Fourier-expand the q-bit equality indicator. Each of its 2^q character sums is the signed bias of a binary polynomial of degree at most two on independent free bits, computable exactly by quadratic elimination. For clarity, if a cross term xy appears, write the phase as xy+xA+yB+D with A,B affine functions of the remaining bits. Summing over x,y gives 2 times the phase (−1)^(D+AB), still quadratic. An affine remainder sums to zero or to a signed power of two. The combined enumeration costs f(a,b,t) times a polynomial in n,d and the input template size, with absolute exponent independent of those parameters. The number of unknown bits is ad+nb; signed intermediate bit lengths are polynomial in this and the number of predicates. Matrix parametrizations can repeat a code, which is harmless for existence.

Test candidate positivity and recover P,T via successive bit pins on the same procedure. Build its full represented R and check regularity. If nonregular, greedily delete or contract elements while maintaining nonregularity until a labeled F7 or F7* minor is obtained (binary excluded-minor criterion). Add its template. It excludes the current parameter matrices. Zero surviving candidates certifies infeasibility of the two budgets; a regular surviving candidate is a true witness. This method terminates, but the stated FPT running time is parameterized by t actually used before termination. A supplied list of t templates excluding every candidate is an independently checkable negative certificate in f(a,b,t)poly(input). No bound on that t in terms of a,b alone or the minimum possible certificate size is asserted. It uses no structural theorem on unrestricted binary matroids beyond ordinary regularity recognition and its established excluded minors.

## Additive flag lower and attainable upper

For fixed ordered blocks the flag-and-rank-one.md proof gives a simultaneous directional sum lower. If Csplit is the direct sum of the graded block images C_j then dim Csplit=dim C. Let g=dim C−dim(C∩Csplit). The direct sum of optimum regular references to the C_j is a valid physical reference with total distance at most their summed optima plus 2g. For one bipartition X,Y, g is the connectivity λ since C∩Csplit=C[X]⊕C[Y]. Choose a linear lift F of rank λ from π_X C to the boundary map into F₂^Y/C[Y], extended by zero on a complement. The shear (x,y)↦(x,y+Fx) sends Csplit to C, since the correct y for x is arbitrary modulo C[Y]. If the sheared sum of local regular references passes an explicit regularity test, it attains the flag lower exactly. For a partial shear using PF with P a projection on im F, the distance from the partially sheared Csplit to C is at most 2 rank((I−P)F); the same regularity test is needed. Across a proper 2-sum λ=1 the plain direct sum has additive loss ≤2, a strict ratio below 3/2 only when OPT>4. No universal factor improvement is claimed.
