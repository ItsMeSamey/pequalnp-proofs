# Contextual pruning in trees of physical clique sums

Let a connected piece G have charges in V=F₂ᵖ and b distinct named exterior vertices. Its boundary meets an exterior only in these vertices. Charge contributions of the two sides are added at identified vertices. Edge choices on the exterior can be prescribed. We consider all attainable spans of component sums, allowing arbitrary edge choices on the piece. To record its response, give the b ports additional independent one-hot flags and write B=V⊕F₂ᵇ. For any edge choice let L≤B be the span of the resulting flagged component sums. Every L contains the same total τ. Project the initial charge span modulo ⟨τ⟩ and let d be the dimension of the image. Store τ and the initial ambient span. Equality of projected subspaces recovers equality of the original L by inverse image in that ambient span. This remains valid for later physical minors conserving τ, even if their individual vertex charges span a smaller subspace. If b>0 the total is nonzero, giving d≤p+b−1. If b=0 use the actual d, with no automatic subtraction when τ=0.

For each L, its projection to the flag coordinates is spanned by the disjoint indicator vectors of the nonempty port blocks. The atoms of this Boolean algebra of subsets recover the port partition. The subspace D={x:(x,0)∈L} is precisely the span of the port-free component sums, because the port-block indicators are independent. A lift of one block indicator has first coordinate equal to the corresponding component charge modulo D. Gluing any fixed exterior edge choice joins components according to the two port partitions and adds their charges. The detached components already generate D independently, so using charge cosets modulo D suffices. Consequently preserving the list of these L is sufficient for all such exterior responses. No correspondence between individual edge configurations is required. For b=0 the same statement reduces to combining detached subspaces.

Suppose a rooted tree decomposition of G is supplied, with nonempty bags and every parent-child adhesion of size at most a inducing a clique in the actual graph (in particular a=2 or 3 is allowed). A child interior is the union of its subtree bags less its parent adhesion. Distinct child interiors at the same node are disjoint with no edges between them. Every connected private component there contacts the adhesion, by connectedness, unless the interior is empty. Redundant empty interiors may be dropped. Maintain the actual adhesion clique edges at the pruning stage: deleting edges incident to a private vertex never deletes an edge with both ends on the adhesion.

Assume d≤3, or more generally that complete global projected witnesses can be computed anew on every current piece (or are carried by explicit witness updates). No enlarged local port score is required for pruning. For d≤3 use the binary three-elements reduction of separator-component-pruning.md, SHA-256 6516a3de4e0bf81cb159b3fbe244a3232becab93f3fb099e59a08f6de54c5a00. For each global projected subspace W of rank r, choose a connected-partition witness with k≤r+1 parts by adjacent rank-preserving mergers. At the current node freeze the entire exterior of its child interiors, including the whole current bag X. Assign every connected private component a default destination at any of its neighboring adhesion vertices. To default a child, contract each such component physically to its destination, deleting all its other incidences. No two vertices of X thereby merge.

For this witness mark children containing an entire part disjoint from X. Such a part, if meeting a child interior, lies wholly in that interior by the separation. At most k−1 children are marked because X is nonempty. In a defaulted child all old excursions of a part from the frozen graph had ends at its same-part adhesion vertices; replace each excursion by their actual clique edge or the trivial walk. In any kept child every connected piece of a part that reaches the exterior can attach to its own part on the adhesion by following a path to the first exit. Thus defaulting any remaining children keeps the old exterior restrictions of parts connected after accounting for their retained child pieces. This also applies to a part lying wholly on the parent side, and a marked port-free part is unchanged.

For each unmarked child compute the difference vector of its original and default assignments, aggregating all its components in the direct sum of k copies of the d-bit charge space. Its coordinates sum to zero. The sum over these children can be represented using at most d(k−1) of their vectors, by selecting a basis from the list. Also mark those children and retain their original assignments. Defaulting all others preserves the k projected part sums. Take the union of marks over W. Each extra child kept for a different witness may realize its default assignment inside the surviving graph. Thus all earlier projected subspaces still occur. The physical branch lift gives the opposite inclusion. The graph remains connected since paths through a removed child can always be replaced by actual adhesion edges. The independently flagged exterior ports remain distinct: before pruning, the singleton partition attains a subspace projecting onto all b flag dimensions; equality of projected catalogues, followed by unprojection, preserves this entry. If two ports were merged then all subspaces would have equal bits on that pair. Track each name at the unique surviving branch containing its original vertex, including when a port in a removed child has moved into its destination. This uses physical charge conservation (including flags), not a choice of new query bits.

At most C=(d+1)Σ_W dim W children with private interiors remain at a processed torso. For d≤3 this is at most 96 (in F₂³ the dimension sum over all subspaces is 24). Process the selected children top down in the current graph. Absorption elsewhere changes charges on shared vertices but neither removes their adhesion clique edges nor adds new edges inside unprocessed interiors. Rerun the same global projected solver or use the transferred witnesses for the new step. A child still meeting its parent has the same physical named adhesion; trivial branches are discarded. The result is one spanning minor with the original exterior response. The bound on child count does not by itself bound the order or treewidth of any one bag.

For a further, conditional composition suppose each surviving physical torso, with all its interface names, admits a constructively supplied connected conservative contextual representative of order ≤F (a uniform bound in the declared parameters). The hypothesis must apply to the actual remaining torso and its current charges, including any transferred charges. Its boundary consists of its parent adhesion, up to C child adhesions and any named exterior vertices present in it, so there are at most a(C+1)+b distinct ports per torso. Split charge ownership across bags once (for instance assign each vertex's full charge to its highest bag and zero elsewhere). Shared clique edges may be given parallel copies in incident bags for the composition argument. Duplicate selectable copies of an edge produce exactly the same connected partitions as one original copy. Likewise zero or duplicate output edges may be suppressed. Local representatives must separate their boundary vertices, retain their names and conserve their local charge contributions; their complete response is taken with respect to these named vertices. This local hypothesis does not follow simply by applying the global d≤3 algorithm at a larger interface.

Starting from the leaves, substitute these representatives using their contextual guarantee, gluing their named vertices and adding charge contributions. A shared edge can remain available to the exterior when doing one substitution. In the eventual physical realization, each local branch containing a named shared vertex is joined to the branches with that same name in the other bags. Connected occurrences in the decomposition and local port separation imply the resulting branches partition the original vertices, are physically connected, and do not identify distinct ports: only the branches bearing a common original boundary vertex are identified. Every retained quotient edge has an actual original edge as witness; coincident copies require only one such edge. Thus all substitutions together are conservative spanning-minor operations. The images of the torso representatives are bags of a decomposition of the resulting graph with bag size at most F, regardless of tree height. Connectedness follows because the torso representatives are themselves connected and adjacent torsos meet in nonempty adhesion (empty-adhesion branches of a connected input can be discarded). Apply the empty-template bounded-treewidth theorem in bounded-treewidth-additive-catalogue.md, SHA-256 be0ff664287ff5acb5025674a911e554d22f5db696d30885c42b4e6e103b8350, preserving the projected-subspace catalogue, connectedness and b distinct physical names, conserving the full original flagged charges. Fixed catalogue tests use CMSO1 and bounded witnesses. Unprojecting again yields the complete exterior response. The final bound depends on F and the fixed charge and port parameters; no dependence on the number of torsos or their tree height is needed.

A useful unconditional local case is a single scalar binary charge with at most two named ports in any connected graph. When at least one port is present d≤1+b−1≤2; without ports the rank is at most one. Over these projected bits, two-bit-unicyclic-support.md supplies a connected support of cycle rank at most one, keeping all vertices, with the same catalogue. The bounded-treewidth stage then compresses it while protecting the named vertices and conserving the original flagged charges. This gives a uniform contextual representative for that two-port case. It does not supply the full high-port torso hypothesis above.
