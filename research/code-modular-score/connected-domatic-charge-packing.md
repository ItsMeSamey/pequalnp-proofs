# Disjoint connected dominating sets for prescribed binary charge sums

Let each vertex of a graph carry a charge in F₂^r. For k≥2 ordered nonempty parts suppose a family of k+(k−1)r pairwise vertex-disjoint nonempty connected dominating sets is supplied. An abstract partition attaining prescribed sums exists if and only if a connected spanning partition attaining the same ordered sums exists. This is a polynomial constructive criterion for fixed k,r given the sets. There is no bound on the order of any dominating set and no spanning or edge-disjoint linkage oracle. An abstract partition can be obtained by dynamic programming recording the first k−1 sums and a k-bit nonemptiness mask. Recognizing a maximum packing of connected dominating sets is not assumed.

Here is the charge-reserve lemma (also used by the existing highly connected host theorem). Write l=k−1. Choose a subspace D≤F₂^r minimizing t+l dim D, with t the number of vertices whose charge is outside D. Comparison with the whole space yields t+l dim D≤lr. For every subspace L≤D, comparison with L gives at least l(dim D−dim L) vertices whose charge lies in D outside L. Thus, among the occurrences of vectors in D, there are l pairwise disjoint bases of D by the matroid base packing theorem. Indeed for any subset of occurrences use the dimension of its linear span for L; the requisite inequalities for the complement hold. All spaces, exceptional vertices, and bases can be found in polynomial time at fixed k,r (even enumeration of O(kr) retained vertices suffices to find the bases). D=0 permits empty bases. Denote them by B₁,…,B_l.

All exceptions and all bases collectively use at most lr vertices. At most lr supplied dominating sets intersect them. Choose k disjoint untouched dominating sets R₁,…,R_k and assign one whole R_i to each part i. All charges in these sets lie in D. Assign each exception to its part in the abstract witness. Assign all other vertices, including the B_i, initially to part k unless already assigned. Current charges in each part agree with its prescribed value modulo D. For i≤l move a suitable subset of B_i from part k to i to correct its remaining D-valued discrepancy. Such a subset always exists by the basis property. The total sum fixes part k. No part ever loses any vertex of its R_i, and every assigned vertex outside R_i has an actual neighbor in R_i. Thus all parts are nonempty and connected, including all originally unassigned vertices.

For k=3,r=4 this gives 11 sets. The four-connected octahedron with charges (0,4,8,1,6,11) and missing edges 03,14,25 has three disjoint connected dominating sets: for example take the pairs 01,24,35 (each is an edge, and every other vertex is adjacent to at least one endpoint). Its prescribed sums 1,2,3 are abstractly attainable but not attainable by connected spanning parts: the two forced pairs 14 and 25 are nonedges and both need the sole available connector 0, as in cographic-plane-bond-extension.md. This proves a lower bound of four on any general packing threshold for this parameter pair; it does not prove optimality of eleven.

A family outside the direct bounded-domination and connectivity thresholds is the Cartesian product of any connected bounded-degree graphs B of growing order with K11. The 11 layers induce copies of B and each dominates every other layer through its same-base-vertex clique. For cubic B the product has degree 13 and domination number at least 11|V(B)|/14. Cubic expander bases can be used to obtain growing treewidth and genus as well (B is a subgraph). The statement does not require the layers to be small. For context on connected dominating sets for prescribed cardinalities see Niklanovits, Simonov, Verma and Zeif, Connected Partitions via Connected Dominating Sets, https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13112 ; our proof uses binary charge bases and no cardinality prescription.
