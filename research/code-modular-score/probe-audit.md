# Audit of the robust-probe condition

The rank growth in robust-probe-packets.md is valid for the stated sufficient certificate. When an even baseline change avoids a second packet, all full gains of that packet are fixed. They supply the old ranks in every primary quotient. A change on any constituent of the first packet detected by an annihilating scalar raises the combined rank in that quotient. Disjoint matched packet pairs need only a common successful baseline; uniform graphic fiber density and expectation give it without independence. Pigeonholing full gain tuples ensures coherent multiplicities. The contracted restrictions of physical probes are edge-disjoint even C-sets, and each nonzero restriction decomposes into simple cycles with at least one nonzero member. The packet-arcs bound therefore applies. Full rank on every primary quotient means full generation by the prime-index maximal-subgroup criterion. The proof is a saturation certificate with parameter-only thresholds. A supplied exact one-edge probe family can be checked directly in time polynomial in the witness and graph sizes. The existential robust condition itself is not turned into an unconditional input algorithm here.

The count obstruction in three-direction-count-obstruction.md is valid: the four family counts produce (s,t,(1−s)v+(1−t)u), missing (1,1,1) on their generated subspace. Each actual component has full image in its own plane by the earlier plane saturation theorem when the supply is sufficiently large. Their independent sum attains the target. Three universal zero-weight vertices preserve the original switches and produce a simple 3-connected augmentation when private subdivisions have removed repeated edges.

## Why high scalar packing does not imply probes

Take the simple complete bipartite graph K_(3,n), whose three-vertex side has distinct vertices a,b,c. For each generator g of a fixed nontrivial odd abelian group A choose arbitrarily many distinct pairs of vertices u,v in the large side. Put weight g on au and zero on all other edges. The cycles a-u-b-v-a for distinct pairs are edge-disjoint. Each has full score g. Every nonzero scalar on any A/pA detects at least one generator, so it admits arbitrarily large edge-disjoint nonzero packings. These switches also give the entire image A when each generator occurs at least exp(A) times. Moreover, arbitrary physical sign changes preserve this high-packing property, since each named cycle has just one nonzero edge, irrespective of its sign.

Nevertheless every simple cycle of K_(3,n) has at most six edges, alternating between the two sides. For a distinguished simple cycle C and a weight supported on C, each nonzero probe must use at least one edge of C. Pairwise edge-disjoint probes therefore number at most six (indeed at most three possibly labeled edges). This holds after any translation and for every candidate C. Thus not even all-translations high scalar packing implies the proposed robust probe supply, on a 3-connected simple graph. This refutes the deduction of that specific hypothesis, not a universal saturation conclusion or an alternative dichotomy allowing already-compatible generator packets.

## A constructive local consequence when rank growth fails

Keep a packet with three other universally non-bad packets in the small-matching case of robust-probe-packets.md. For a constituent C and a scalar χ annihilating the packet span at baseline y, the signed χ-weight η restricted to C vanishes on every original even support avoiding any of the three other packets. The packet-arcs lemma therefore bounds the packing number of nonzero simple cycles in the multigraph on C obtained by contracting G−C. Its labels lie in F_p with p odd, so the unsigned edge Erdős–Pósa theorem (Thomas–Yoo with the standard half-edge port reduction) yields a parameter-bounded transversal F among the edges of C itself. An even support of the original graph avoiding F projects to an even edge set on the contracted graph avoiding F, and hence has η-score zero by decomposition. The quiet-recognition algorithm on the contracted graph can find such a transversal given a parameter-only bound, and the edges are identified directly with physical members of C. Distinct disjoint constituents have disjoint local modulators. This supplies constructive localization at the stalled baseline without a global modulator bound.
