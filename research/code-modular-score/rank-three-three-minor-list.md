# A deterministic list of at most three physical four-vertex minors

Assume a 2-connected simple graph with vertex labels spanning F₂³ and of total zero. The full catalogue of score spaces of connected partitions is the union of the catalogues of at most three explicitly obtainable four-vertex 2-connected physical minors. The bound three is sharp. Different list entries may use incompatible contractions.

For an unseeded query use the incidence matrix A and the vertex-by-three matrix B of labels; B is injective as a map on coefficient vectors. For any ordinary edge set X, the component sums of (V,X) span the orthogonal complement of {u: Bu belongs to span A_X}, by the even-component characterization of a graphic column span. For a specified score plane W write its orthogonal as <u>, and choose a basis (u,a,b). Append Ba,Bb,B(u+a+b) as three named columns to A. The desired kernel occurs precisely when the three names form a triangle after contracting X and deleting other ordinary edges. This is equivalent to one original circuit containing all the names. Indeed lift the triangle dependence using a basis of X; any smaller dependency would either avoid names and contradict independence, or would use one or two names contrary to the triangle. Conversely contract the ordinary members of a circuit and delete all other ordinary columns. The deterministic binary-matrix three-elements algorithm is described by [Geelen, Gerards and Whittle, §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.20960v1#S2). Greedily deleting ordinary columns whenever the answer remains positive leaves exactly the circuit. For a point of score, the required kernel is a plane <a,b>. Append Bc,B(c+a),B(c+b) for c outside the plane; the desired terminal minor is three parallel nonloops. Apply the same triangle test in the binary dual and choose the complement of its contracted ordinary set for primal contraction. These queries recover witnesses for every entry deterministically in polynomial time. No four-vertex minor is assumed supplied.

Use the point-preserving contraction from rank-three-fano-catalogues.md on a bond (merge adjacent parts preserving rank until there are two), and the plane-preserving reduction in rank-three-plane-four-core.md. Each preserves full charge rank. A square with simplex labels lacks one point q and all three incident planes, a diamond lacks one point q and two incident planes, and a K4 contains all points and lacks exactly one plane. Every minor catalogue is contained in the host catalogue by lifting the partition.

If q is absent in the host and at least one plane through q occurs, take a four-core for each attained plane through q. It must be a diamond missing q. All such diamonds cover every plane avoiding q and all points except q. If no plane through q occurs, take a four-core preserving any attained point; it must be a square missing q. Thus at most three suffice. If all points occur, begin with any point core. A K4 core misses only one plane; include a core for that plane when attainable. Otherwise take another core preserving the point omitted by the first. If the second is a K4 apply the previous rule. In the remaining case the two cores omit distinct points, so their missing plane pencils have at most one common plane. Retrieve a third core for that plane if attained. Every core carries the full space and zero, completing the catalogue.

The six-signature classification in rank-three-fano-catalogues.md includes a host missing a point q and attaining all three planes through it. Every four-core of this host contributes at most one of those planes. It therefore requires all three list entries. The proof does not give a bounded single simultaneous minor for this signature.
