# Counts and decision at exponents four and eight

For a binary affine code, take an injective affine parametrization x∈F₂^d by Gaussian elimination. Replace each complemented physical linear bit by a constant offset and a negative coefficient. For A=(Z/4)^k × (Z/2)^h each character phase is i^(c+q(x)) with q(0)=0 and q=Σ a_e ℓ_e(x) modulo four, including doubled coefficients for binary characters. Its defect is 2β(x,y) where β=Σ(a_e mod 2)ℓ_e(x)ℓ_e(y) is symmetric bilinear: minus two equals plus two modulo four. In particular β(v,v)=q(v) mod 2. Split nondegenerate odd-norm lines orthogonally, each contributing 1+i^q(v). In an alternating remainder with a nonzero pairing, split off a hyperbolic plane u,v. Its factor 1+i^q(u)+i^q(v)−i^(q(u)+q(v)) is −2 if both q-values are 2 and otherwise 2. The remaining radical contributes zero if q there is nonzero, otherwise 2 to its dimension. Multiply by i^c, invert with conjugate target character over all |A| characters, and divide exactly by |A|. Each Gaussian integer during inversion has O(d+log|A|) bits in each component. Time |A|poly(input) for one target, with all constants absolute. Fix each independent bit to a branch with positive count to recover a witness; consistency and original weights can be checked directly.

For exponent eight the identity 4xyz=x+y+z−(x⊕y)−(x⊕z)−(y⊕z)+(x⊕y⊕z) makes an unrestricted cubic sign the eighth-root phase of an explicit parity score; 4xy=2x+2y−2(x⊕y) handles pairs. Appending zero-weight identity coordinates ensures injectivity. The restricted score takes only values zero and four up to a known constant. The hardness of computing the cubic gap thus gives hardness of exact bin counts on arbitrary binary subspaces. [Montanaro](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.08473).

The graphic cycle case is already hard with unit weights. At ζ=exp(iπ/4), set W=Σ_even ζ^|X|. Spin expansion gives 2^|V| W=(1−ζ)^|E| Z_RC(G;2,2ζ/(1−ζ)), hence W differs from the Tutte value at (1/ζ,(1+ζ)/(1−ζ)) by an explicit nonzero prefactor. The second coordinate is i(1+sqrt(2)), and the q=2 point lies outside [Jaeger–Vertigan–Welsh's exceptional list](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100068936). One fixed zero-bin oracle recovers all eight bins: attach a disjoint simple cycle of length congruent to −j modulo eight and at least three; the new zero count minus the old equals bin j. Each edge can be replaced by a private length-nine path (or nine-cycle for a loop) to ensure simplicity while preserving residues. Thus single zero-bin exact counting is #P-complete under polynomial-time Turing reductions already for unweighted simple graphs. This is a counting barrier only.

For pure (Z/8)^k scores, decision and witness recovery take deterministic 2^O(k) poly(input) time on arbitrary affine binary codes. Expand a coefficient into at most seven copies of its bit. By Lucas the three output digits are elementary symmetric polynomials of degrees 1,2,4 over F₂, evaluated on the affine physical bits. The target indicator is a Boolean polynomial of degree ≤7k; every nonzero Boolean polynomial of degree D is one on at least a 2^(−D) fraction of inputs, by induction on a variable occurring in a maximum-degree monomial. Expand the equality indicator in the Walsh characters on its 3k output bits: their normalized coefficients have absolute sum one. Each character becomes a sign of a degree-at-most-four polynomial on message bits. Use [Viola's explicit generator, Theorem 2](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~viola/papers/d.pdf) at error less than 2^(−7k−2) (allowing the factor two for conversion from distributional to sign error). Its seed length is O(log(d+2)+k) for degree four. Enumerate seeds and evaluate the original weighted code to find a witness. Empty instances always reject. This provides decision and witness, never exact counts. The sharp density example uses k independent length-seven weight blocks with joint target zero.

For cyclic factors of exponent at most 2^a with a FIXED, use maximum phase degree D=2^(a−1) and density exponent Σ_j(2^(a_j)−1) for factors of orders 2^(a_j). Viola enumeration has an input exponent O(D); this does not establish an exponent independent of a when a varies. For a fixed ceiling the input exponent is independent of the number of factors and of the odd-group sizes in the conditional assembly below.

For joint odd A and coprime 2-primary T, first recover an actual T-target base, translate all A and T weights, and subtract its full joint value. High odd induction aimed at A×{0} applies to A/⟨a⟩×{0} on graphs avoiding a reserved packet of exp(A)exp(T) disjoint equal full gains (a,b). Correct missing a by a number of untouched switches divisible by ord(b), a unit on ⟨a⟩. The zero T component is preserved. Low odd χ maps into the smaller kerχ retaining unrestricted T. On each refined simple torso enumerate every bounded nontrivial child in the full joint group, subtract its full contribution, check odd residual belongs to kerχ, and retain T and binary trace pins. Each new smaller-group instance recomputes its actual T-base on its own graph. No assertion of independent marginal scores is needed. Original low-odd SPQR accounting gives a uniform graph-size exponent since each level has boundedly replicated linear-total-size torsos, with one outer witness deletion pass. The T-only base can use exact Gauss for exponent four or Viola decision for exponent eight (or any fixed ceiling). For unbounded exponent, the graph exponent from this particular base is not uniform.
