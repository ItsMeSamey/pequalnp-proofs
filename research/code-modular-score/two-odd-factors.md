# Deterministic FPT for two odd cyclic prime-power factors and binary markers

For distinct odd primes p,q, one can decide and recover any prescribed unsigned graphic even-support score in C_(p^a) × C_(q^b) × F₂^t in deterministic f(p^a,q^b,2^t)poly(input) time. This includes unrestricted Z/15. The argument combines the edge form of odd-group nonzero-cycle Erdős–Pósa with quiet-edge modulator recognition and the quiet-ports solver.

For P=C_(p^a), expand the graph into half-edge port cliques and length-three wires, carrying each P label on one wire segment and zero elsewhere. Distinct original cycles need not be vertex disjoint. If their edges are disjoint, their canonical lifts are vertex-disjoint simple port cycles: each port is reserved to its own physical edge. Conversely vertex-disjoint nonzero port cycles project to edge-disjoint nonzero original even supports, and each supplies an original nonzero simple cycle. An integral transversal of port vertices gives an original edge transversal of no greater size: map each port to its own original edge and each private wire interior vertex to its original edge. Every original nonzero simple cycle has a canonical lift, and any vertex on its lift projects to an edge on the original cycle. Hence the integral odd-group vertex Erdős–Pósa theorem gives a computable bound F(k,P) for an EDGE transversal unless k pairwise edge-disjoint nonzero cycles exist. Use quiet-recognition.md to find such a transversal of size ≤F or certify none. The high-packing case needs no construction of the packed cycles.

Put Q=C_(q^b) × F₂^t, with |Q| coprime to p, and H=p^(a−1)P. First solve any target in H×{0} on P×Q. A modulator for P of size ≤F lets quiet-ports.md solve that exact target using only the unrestricted Q-plus-additional-binary-marker graphic oracle. In its absence choose the packing threshold at least |P||Q| times the number of possible joint labels. Among the disjoint P-nonzero cycles there are enough identical joint increments (δ,y) to attain their whole cyclic subgroup. The element δ≠0 has subgroup containing H, and multiplying by the order of y leaves the same p-primary subgroup, so the full joint subgroup contains H×{0}. Accept every target of this kind. The standalone H×{0} decision remains exact after deletion of any edges; greedy deletion obtains a witness using only that decision and its Q oracle, without any recursion on the p exponent.

For a general target, solve modulo H×{0} recursively, recovering an even base x₀. The quotient reduces a by one, and the base case is the known unrestricted Q oracle. Translate all physical weights by the signs of x₀, subtract its original joint score and use the standalone oracle on the resulting residual target in H×{0}. XOR the returned correction with x₀. There are only a additive lifting stages and O(am) standalone calls including witness recovery; no recursive chain of m-way self-reductions in a. All subordinate Q queries are to the established single-odd-factor plus elementary-binary algorithm with a fixed polynomial exponent.

The same argument iterates for each fixed upper bound on the number of distinct odd prime factors, with a polynomial exponent allowed to depend on that fixed bound. The construction as stated does not give a uniform FPT exponent for unboundedly many distinct primes, nor for a noncyclic odd Sylow subgroup.

Primary sources and prerequisites: https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.11266 for integral odd-group packing/cover; quiet-recognition.md, quiet-ports.md and prime-graphic-fpt.md in this directory for the constructive pieces.
