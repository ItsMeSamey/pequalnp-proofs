# Finding a bounded-genus embedding from an algebraic dual promise

The polygon construction, surface normalization and accounting for pinches are due to Lowell Abrams and Dan Slilaty, Construction 3.1 and Propositions 3.2 and 4.2 of [Algebraic Characterizations of Graph Imbeddability in Surfaces and Pseudosurfaces](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0218216506004683). The factor-two bound below follows as a corollary by adding handles when restoring split vertices.

Let G be a finite connected multigraph with at least one edge. Suppose an edge-identified graph H satisfies cut(G) contained in Z(H) over GF(2), with dimension difference d. The Euler genus of G is at most 2d. This promise suffices to construct a bounded-genus embedding without the graph H.

Discard isolated vertices of H and join edge-containing components at single vertices so that H becomes connected. Neither operation changes its edge cycle space. Orthogonal complements give cut(H) contained in Z(G). At each vertex y of H collect a copy of the corresponding G edge for each incident edge end. An H loop supplies two copies. The resulting multigraph on G vertices has even degrees, because its mod-two incidence is the cut of y. In each edge-bearing component choose a closed Euler walk. Attach a polygon for each walk, labelling corners by G vertices and sides by traversed G edges. For each edge of G glue its two copies, matching corresponding ends. Distinguish the two ends even for a G loop. Self-gluings and monogons can equivalently be performed after subdividing every side into three segments.

In the polygon quotient every edge interior has a disk neighborhood. Around vertices the corner arcs link up in circles, since every incident side-end is paired with exactly one other. Keep distinct circles as distinct vertices even when their G labels coincide. The result is a union of c closed connected surfaces with cellularly embedded graphs G'. Every original edge occurs exactly once, and identifying all vertices with equal G label recovers G. Each surface component has a connected cellular dual. Its vertices are polygons, labelled by H vertices, and every dual edge has its two original H endpoint labels. Identifying equal labels of these dual vertices recovers H, including loops. Write s=|V(G')|−|V(G)| and u=(number of polygons)−|V(H)|. Since G and H are both connected, each of the two identifications joins c components and hence s,u≥c−1.

With m=|E|, the dimension hypothesis gives d=m−|V(H)|−|V(G)|+2. Euler characteristic summed over the surfaces now gives total Euler genus
g=d−(s−c+1)−(u−c+1).
Choose c−1 of the primal vertex identifications that join distinct components according to a spanning tree. For two surfaces use their connected sum, removing small disks in sectors at the two specified vertices. Route an extra edge between those vertices through the joining tube, then contract it in the surface. This makes the vertex identification and preserves the sum of Euler genera. Each of the remaining s−c+1 identifications can similarly be carried out in the same surface using one added handle, at cost at most two in Euler genus. The resulting embedding of G has Euler genus at most g+2(s−c+1)≤2d.

For a disconnected G, one-point join its edge-bearing components first, preserving its cutspace. After embedding the joined graph, each original component separately inherits an embedding of Euler genus at most 2d, by restriction and capping a regular neighborhood. Run the named-kernel component algorithm on the original components. Isolated primal vertices keep their constant named row charges. This argument asserts individual component genus bounds.

For fixed d, try Mohar's embedding algorithm on each of the finitely many surfaces of Euler genus at most 2d. On a successful embedding cap the boundary circles of a regular neighborhood of the embedded graph to obtain a cellular embedding of no higher Euler genus. This supplies an edge-identified cellular dual for the exact-kernel routing algorithm. The algorithm answers the kernel question on every promised graphic host. Computing the minimum algebraic deficiency or recognizing that it is at most a specified d is a separate problem.

Reference: Bojan Mohar, A Linear Time Algorithm for Embedding Graphs in an Arbitrary Surface, SIAM J. Discrete Math. 12 (1999), 6–26. https://doi.org/10.1137/S089548019529248X
