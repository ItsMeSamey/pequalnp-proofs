# Physical named-kernel representatives for bounded-diameter distance-hereditary graphs

Fix diameter D≥1, binary named dimension and protected port count. Every connected distance-hereditary graph of diameter at most D, with arbitrary dense named columns, has a computably bounded physical conservative spanning minor with exactly its full named-kernel catalogue. The bound concerns the catalogue; we do not assert a WQO from this pumping proof.

Use the ternary clique/star accessibility-tree presentation of connected DH graphs (Gioan and Paul, https://arxiv.org/abs/0810.1823). Graph vertices are its leaves. For distinct leaves x,y a turn on their tree path is blocked exactly if a star is traversed between two arms. At such a star the hanging subtree is attached on its center. It contains a leaf accessible from this center interface: starting at the interface choose any allowed continuing turn at each node until reaching a leaf. The three incident sectors partition all leaves. There are no graph edges from the left sector to the right sector, and every edge from the third sector to either side must meet its active leaves (those accessible from its interface). Consequently any graph path x to y visits this active set. For the b blocked turns, these sets lie in pairwise disjoint off-path subtrees, so distance is at least b+1. Conversely choose one active leaf per blocked turn. The sequence from x through the chosen leaves in path order to y has adjacent successive vertices: inside either hanging subtree its path is accessible, at its blocked star it uses a center–arm turn, and between consecutive blocked turns all spine transitions are allowed. Hence distance equals b+1. This applies to every ternary presentation, including noncanonical intermediate trees and the two-leaf case.

Let C be the positive computable transmitting-run pumping threshold from accessible-split-run-pumping.md (SHA 97809e0ca86f0c672adda49e93868d5f66b665884a389ee1b43d386abb43b36e), using the fixed charge group including all port bits and the fixed full-catalogue automaton states. It suffices to use a larger integer if endpoint conventions require reserving a site on each end. While any leaf-to-leaf path contains at least C consecutive transmitting internal nodes, apply its certified rewrite. Both exterior faces contain the original end leaves or a subtree; physical recipients are chosen from active leaves in each retained face. A nonempty interval and all its off-spine subtrees are deleted and its two exterior stubs joined. Every vertex in deleted subtrees is assigned, and total charge per surviving receiver is unchanged. The rewrite preserves the complete catalogue, including protected port query columns. The residual tree is ternary or a single edge. For any pair of surviving leaves its new tree path is its old path with zero or more removed nodes; the orientation at every surviving node is unchanged on corresponding incident stubs. The count of blocked turns never increases. Thus the diameter hypothesis remains true. Each step strictly decreases the number of graph vertices.

At termination, each leaf-to-leaf path contains b≤D−1 blocked sites and at most (b+1)(C−1) transmitting sites. Its number of tree edges is at most DC. A tree of maximum degree three and diameter at most DC has at most 1+3(2^{DC}−1) vertices by a breadth-first count. This bounds the graph's physical leaves and hence its catalogue representative. Singleton inputs are immediate. The algorithm may enumerate leaf pairs on the supplied or computed split tree, compute finite automaton records and recover physical branch assignments by composing the local contraction witnesses. The finite bound can be extremely large. Appending the finite local port columns before invoking this result preserves every exterior test under the usual shared-residual gluing rule. General connected DH graphs need not have bounded diameter.
