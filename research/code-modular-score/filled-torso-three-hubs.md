# A filled torso above the four-bit connectivity threshold can give a false plane

Use a connected simple graph with a central bag X=A∪B∪C of three independent sets of order 85. No two vertices in X are physically adjacent. Outside X there are three independent vertices s_AB,s_BC,s_CA, each adjacent precisely to the two classes indicated by its subscript. Give the decomposition one child bag per hub, consisting of that hub and all its 170 neighbors. Every edge is covered and the vertex occurrences satisfy running intersection. Deleting X really does produce these three components with the stated neighborhoods.

The actual-component torso of X, formed by completing every neighborhood to a clique, is K255, with vertex connectivity 254. The adhesions have size 170. The numerical threshold for prescribed three-part sums of four-bit charges on a physical graph in high-connectivity.md is 172.

Assign six different vertices of A the charges 4,5,8,10,4,7 respectively (decimal binary masks); all remaining vertices are zero. The total is 0, and the rank is four since 4+5=1 and 8+10=2 under XOR. Let H={0,1,2,3}. The three displayed pairs give sums 1,2,3. In the filled torso they extend to a connected three-part partition, assigning all zero vertices freely. In the physical graph every part with nonzero sum in H must contain at least two of the charged A vertices: no single nonzero supported charge belongs to H. A connected part meeting two A vertices must contain s_AB or s_CA, since these are the only neighbors of an A vertex. Three disjoint such parts cannot exist. If a partition into more parts gave score image H, it could be merged to a three-part witness by the adjacent-parts rank argument; alternatively the nonzero part sums belong to H and total zero, so a rank-two image needs at least three nonzero parts. Thus the physical answer is NO.

The instance has genuine cographic private edge gains: route the charge assignment along a spanning tree to obtain the given incidence divergence. The obstruction concerns evaluation by the connectivity theorem on the filled torso without child compatibility information. It does not contradict high connectivity of a physical graph or algorithms retaining complete joint boundary states. This particular physical graph even has ordinary domination number at most three, since the three hubs together dominate it.
