# Three directions can defeat the count polynomial of fixed packs

Fix any odd prime p and any pack size n. In the first graph component place n simple row cycles of gain e1 and n simple column cycles of gain e3. Make each row intersect each column in exactly one edge, distinct for every cell, and ensure internal edge disjointness of each family. Construct this by creating two vertex ends per cell edge, then joining consecutive cell edges cyclically with private connector paths in each row and column. Give each cell edge gain e3/2 and choose a private connector weight on each cycle to give its required total. In the second disjoint component use singleton gains e2 and e3 by the same construction. All other edges have zero gain. Private subdivisions avoid parallel edges if desired.

Choose s rows and v columns in the first component, t rows and u columns in the second. Their XOR has score (s,t,(1−s)v+(1−t)u). Even arbitrarily large families miss (1,1,1) when restricted to these switches, while every nonzero scalar character has an arbitrarily large edge-disjoint nonzero packing among the four families. In fact the entire binary span of the four designated families misses the target, since every such XOR has the displayed form. The actual graph's entire score image fills F_p³ for n sufficiently large: each component fills its two-dimensional score plane by plane-graphic-fpt.md, and the sum of those planes is the full space. This isolates the need for physical recombination, without claiming hardness or a global counterexample.
The same obstruction is possible on a simple 3-connected host. Add three new adjacent vertices, join each to every old vertex and give every new edge zero score. Removing any two vertices preserves connectivity. These edges do not affect the designated switches or their intersections. Every old edge subset now extends to an even support by pairing its odd vertices through one surviving new hub (use its spoke once per odd vertex); the unrestricted image in this augmented graph contains the full plane sum as well.
