# Exact projective contact allocation at a binary rank-two interface

A rank-r interface represents adjacency of a physical contact of row type x to an exterior column of type y by x·y over F₂. Suppose a connected receiver branch has actual exposed contact types P and needs all edges of a single target row a. Surplus edges may be deleted. Coverage for every possible exterior column holds exactly when a belongs to span(P). Indeed the uncovered columns are precisely those y in P-perp with a·y=1. If only specified exterior types Y are to be kept, the exact condition is that Y intersect P-perp only in a-perp. Include the receiver's own exposed row among P if it already provides eligible edges. This tests coverage for an already constructed branch, not connectivity or simultaneous disjointness.

At rank two there are three nonzero types. Suppose n_t distinct physical contacts of each type t are freely assignable to individual lanes, and d_t lanes demand type t. For now each contact is used at most once, unused contacts are allowed, and no internal connectivity or charge requirement is imposed. Then disjoint assignments providing universal column coverage exist if and only if

**nₜ + nᵤ ≥ dₜ + dᵤ**

for every pair of distinct nonzero types t,u. To prove necessity take the column that is one on t,u and zero on the third type. Each of those d_t+d_u lanes must receive its own contact hitting this column. For sufficiency, first assign exact types wherever available. At most one type t can be deficient by the pair inequalities. If its deficit is δ, each other type has at least δ surplus contacts. Assign one of each other type to every remaining t-lane; their sum is t. Zero-type demands need no coverage contacts. Disjoint equal-type matching alone is too restrictive: types 01 and 10 together serve one 11-lane against every exterior column. Total cardinality alone is insufficient: two 01 contacts cannot serve two 10 demands.

This allocation has a physical realization when distinct seeded lanes are each directly adjacent to every assignable contact, with all unused contacts and other vertices treated separately. With finite additive charges, fixed seeded lanes and independent allowed choices, a direct finite-state test can instead require every item to be assigned once. An item is a connected physical bag with a prescribed set of physically adjacent seeds, a total charge and an exposed subspace. Starting with existing seed charges and exposed spans, for each bag choose an allowed seed, add its charge there and take the sum of the two subspaces. At the end test prescribed charge increments and containment of the required rows. Backtracking gives real branches since every bag touches its receiving seed. This is exact for whole-bag attachments to these specified seeds. It does not reduce a general disjoint-path routing question to vector arithmetic.

For a routing obstruction take a cycle of length four, five or six with four terminals in cyclic order in_A, in_B, out_A, out_B. The remaining vertices, if any, subdivide the arcs and carry zero charge. Give out_A and out_B the unique independent outgoing types A and B, fixing private exterior neighbors testing their respective coordinates. The two input receivers have no existing outgoing edges. Supply counts meet every inequality, each receiver individually reaches each outgoing contact, and an unpaired disjoint linkage exists (pair in_A with out_B, and in_B with out_A). Yet universal typed coverage requires the A-lane to contain out_A and the B-lane out_B. Paths joining these alternating pairs cannot be vertex-disjoint in the cycle. The private exterior vertices remain singletons and do not serve as routing vertices. These graphs have linear rank-width at most two: in a cyclic order with each private leaf placed immediately after its parent, a prefix has at most two crossing rows on the cycle; if it separates a parent from its private leaf, the parent is the last cycle vertex in the prefix and already one of the two possible crossing rows. This disproves use of the inequalities alone as a physical linkage criterion; it gives no minor antichain.
