## Physical pair constraints on an unknown four-root target

Let E be indexed ordinary binary columns, S a four-dimensional named span, and H the desired plane. For each D⊆E of size at most two, test whether H is contained in span(E∖D). If containment fails, some column of D must be chosen. Independently test whether span(D)∩S is a subspace of H. If this test fails, some column of D must be omitted. These tests give a polynomial-size 2-CNF on physical selection bits. Unsatisfiability proves infeasibility. A variable fixed in all satisfying assignments can be pinned to this value without knowing whether the target is achievable. Delete a forced omitted column. For forced selections P check span(P)∩S⊆H, solve using the images of S and H modulo span(P), and retain P for traceback. Any satisfying Boolean assignment returned by 2-SAT still needs its actual trace checked. This method alone may abstain.

In the binary rank-eight example E={19,39,33,32,30,49,52,64,67,128,138}, S is the low four bits and H={0,4,8,12}. Start with the rank-six 24-plane fixture in port-four-real-mask-check.py. Replace its point 3 by the pair (64,67) and its point 10 by (128,138). In a sum lying in S the unique new high bit for each pair requires both or neither of its columns. Thus every selected set has the old trace obtained by ignoring incomplete pairs; conversely every original choice lifts. All 24 attainable planes are preserved, and all 160 named-plane tests can still return rivals. The ten positive and seven negative two-index clauses have no common assignment, although no singleton gives a clause. Direct enumeration checked all 2,048 selections and all two-plane traces. This comparison does not require the instance to resist other structural reductions. The growing dense disk controls reported by matroids block fixed-width extensions: if no nonzero named vector has a representation using at most k ordinary columns there are no negative clauses of width k. If no two forbidden omissions disconnect the graph, all positive nontrivial clauses have width at least three, and every individual selection bit can take either value while keeping all other bits selected.

## Root-fixing orbit simplification

Suppose E spans S and r=rank E. Any attained plane has an independent witness of size r−2. If an orbit O of a target-preserving linear automorphism group is dependent, has size greater than r−2, or has span intersecting S outside H, every such witness omits some orbit member. Moving that member to a chosen e proves deletion of e preserves the target. If rank(E∖O)<r−2 or H is not contained in span(E∖O), every witness meets O. Moving a selected member to e proves contraction of e preserves the target, with named coefficients tracked in the quotient. The size and rank tests work simultaneously for all 35 planes when the automorphisms fix S pointwise. So do dimensions greater than two for span(O)∩S (delete) and less than two for span(E∖O)∩S (contract). These are sufficient tests.

All transvection actions fixing S pointwise can be found in polynomial time. For each difference v≠0 between two ordinary column types, find a basis for the forms l vanishing on v, on S, and on each present x whose multiplicity differs from that of x+v (zero for absent types). Then x↦x+l(x)v preserves the multiset and fixes S. Conversely every nonidentity transvection on the ordinary types has this form. Its terms are necessary because an unequal-multiplicity pair cannot be swapped, and checking present types suffices as l(v)=0. Composition adds forms at fixed v. Compute the orbits generated by those basis actions and by swaps of equal copies. Rigid representations may give no progress: the rank-four, -five and -six small masking fixtures have trivial ambient linear group fixing S pointwise. This last statement follows by exhausting the possible images of the zero, one or two additional basis vectors extending S while preserving invertibility and the physical point set.
