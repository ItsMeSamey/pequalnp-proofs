# Shortening a fixed-width charged strip by physical minors

Fix width h, color count q and binary label dimension d. A supplied strip has disjoint ordered layers V_i=(v_i1,...,v_ih). Vertices with the same index in adjacent layers have longitudinal rail edges. Other strip edges are within or between consecutive layers, and its only contacts with the exterior occur at the two end layers. A long strip has a strictly shorter physical minor obtained by contracting rail edges with label addition and deleting some cross edges, preserving all ordered connected nonempty q-part sums in every exterior.

For i=2,...,L−1 compute the full table of the prefix through V_i, treating V_1 and V_i as 2h ordered terminals. For each achievable coloring record terminal colors, the equivalence of terminals joined by a monochromatic path, numbers of components of each color not touching any terminal (capped at two), and every color's total prefix label. Standard frontier DP introduces the next layer and its edges, merges equally colored adjacent components, and closes a component on forgetting its last terminal. Prefix vertices are counted only upon introduction. Equal tables are interchangeable in every continuation: terminal equivalences merge by equivalence closure, and closed components can never be touched subsequently. Counts capped at two suffice to test whether each color is connected and nonempty.

Append the h cumulative per-rail XORs through layer i to the table state. There are boundedly many such states for fixed h,q,d. If internal i<j have the same state, retain the entire prefix through i and all suffix edges incident to any layer strictly beyond j. Inside the skipped region delete all other edges except the h rail chains between V_i and V_j. Contract each such chain onto its vertex in V_i. Equality of cumulative XORs makes the net absorbed charge zero on each chain, preserving the prefix vertex's label. The resulting labeled graph is exactly the old prefix i glued to the old suffix after j, using the ordered identification V_j→V_i; edges formerly internal to V_j were accounted for by the prefix j table. The equal prefix tables give identical feasibility and scores for arbitrary external attachments to the original end layers. A solution can be reconstructed via a compatible old-prefix table witness. Iteration leaves at most the state bound plus two layers. End layers retain all their vertices and labels. All operations are polynomial time for a supplied fixed-width strip.

This contextual reduction supports arbitrary internal labels on width-two or greater ladder sections, even if all their internal vertices have degree three and the outside graph has unbounded width. It is a batch of graph-minor operations, not a theorem that every individual operation in the batch preserves the spectra on its own.
