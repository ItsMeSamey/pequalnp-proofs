# Face transitions and physical reduction on an orientable surface

Let a connected graph be embedded in an orientable surface of genus g and let F be a face homeomorphic to a disk with simple boundary. In a spanning partition into k connected parts, let k' be the number appearing on its boundary. If k'≥2 the number m of cyclic runs there is at most 2k'−2+4g. With one color there are no transition edges.

Choose one distinct boundary vertex per run. Add a central vertex x in the open face and draw m mutually internally disjoint spokes to the chosen vertices in order. Outside the open face take a tree for each appearing color connecting its chosen boundary vertices. The trees of different colors are disjoint. Retain just these trees and the spokes, then contract each tree. The remaining embedded bipartite multistar has k'+1 vertices and m edges; the sequence of color neighbors at x has consecutive terms distinct, cyclically. Cap the boundary curves of its regular neighborhood by disks; the resulting cellular orientable surface has genus g'≤g. No face walk has length one or three by bipartiteness. A length-two face on distinct edges would use the same color at two consecutive positions around x. A length-two face traversing a single edge twice would require that edge to be the only edge at both ends, impossible as m≥2. Therefore the number of faces is at most m/2. Euler gives 2−2g'≤k'+1−m/2, proving the bound.

For a patch with h augmented score bits and N exact states, use the bounded-genus labelled dual-routing solver to obtain a normalized forest for each state. Apply the equal-record physical contractions of planar-face-contraction.md. On each supplied simple disk face at most N(2h+4g) original edges have endpoints with different records. All others have their endpoints physically identified. If all nonzero local labels and exterior ports lie on f such faces, there are at most b+f(1+N(2h+4g)) surviving distinguished charged vertices and ports. The ordinary rooted 0-folio argument of planar-few-face-physical-minor.md applies to fixed-genus graphs too: the marked graph-minor well-quasi-ordering is for a fixed surface, and the bounded-width argument uses strong irrelevance for ordinary rooted minors, not group-labelled minors. Connected fixed-genus minors can then be greedily reduced to computably bounded order while preserving all augmented kernels against arbitrary exteriors. Outside labels need not lie on these faces; the hypothesis is on the patch and its interface.
