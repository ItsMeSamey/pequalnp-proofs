# Graphic packing saturation fails in cographic binary codes

Fix an odd prime p. In the cut space of K_(n,4), with n a multiple of p, give every edge of its first column weight 1 and every other edge zero. The n row stars are disjoint bonds of nonzero scalar score. The four column stars are disjoint bonds. Avoiding even one full column star forces all row vertex potentials equal to that column's potential; every selected edge set is then a union of whole column stars. Each column star has score zero, so each of these four singleton-circuit packets separately hits every nonzero bond. This refutes the graphic four-packet packing bound even in the cographic subclass of regular binary matroids.

For a full-spectrum obstruction take K_(n,m) with n,m≥p, add a new vertex adjacent to every original vertex, and use weights in F_p². Give each cell edge c=e1/2; choose the root spokes to give each row star total e1 and each column star total e2. A star is a bond since deleting its vertex leaves a connected graph. All row stars are disjoint from one another, and all column stars are disjoint from one another. Fix the root potential zero. A choice of s row and t column potentials equal to one has total cut score s e1+t e2−2st c=(s(1−t),t). Thus (1,1) is impossible for all n,m. Yet every nonzero scalar has a packing of at least min(n,m) disjoint nonzero bonds. This concerns failure of saturation; cut-score feasibility itself has a different algorithm. Direct enumeration on (p,n,m)=(3,3,4),(5,5,5),(7,7,7) gave respectively 7,21,43 attainable values, as predicted.

For arbitrary binary codes even the scalar odd packing-cover alternative fails. In the simplex code with coordinates a·y for all nonzero a∈F₂^d, every nonzero word is a support-minimal circuit of weight 2^(d−1), nonzero modulo every odd prime. Two distinct nonzero words meet on 2^(d−2) positions when d≥2. A set hitting all such circuits must span the d-dimensional binary mask space, and a basis suffices. The nonzero packing number is one while the minimum cover size is d.

In contrast, universal saturation from large primitive packings alone holds for every cyclic group on any binary code. For each Sylow prime p choose many disjoint codewords with primitive p-coordinate; pigeonhole their full gain a_p, and homogenize all intersections across the finitely many families by multipartite Ramsey. If choosing n_p words from each family, XOR inclusion-exclusion gives the singleton terms n_p a_p and higher intersection terms whose coefficients are multiples of the products of the relevant n_p. Choose n_p divisible by the complete order of every other Sylow factor. All cross terms vanish, and each coordinate p receives only n_p a_p. The Chinese remainder theorem sets that value arbitrarily. This structural assertion requires sufficiently many disjoint words within each family; the simplex example precludes a general bounded physical blocker alternative.
