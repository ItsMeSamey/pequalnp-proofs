# Exact named kernels with bounded graphic boundary rank support

Let A be the supplied full vertex-edge incidence matrix of an undirected graph, with loops permitted, and B an arbitrary binary named-column map into the same vertex coordinates. Put k=dim R and W=B(K) for a prescribed K⊆R. Suppose the union D of the vertex supports of W has size d. There is a deterministic witness algorithm polynomial for each fixed k,d. Named columns outside W may be dense. This is an XP statement; no FPT or arbitrary-perturbation result follows.

For each basis z of K choose an ordinary chain C_z with A C_z=Bz. For each basis b of K⊥ choose a vertex potential f_b with Bᵀ f_b=b. Require every C_z to avoid every cut Aᵀ f_b. Necessity follows by extending true contractions to all primal lifts and extending every functional of the named minor to the vertex space while vanishing on contracted columns. Conversely contracting precisely the union of the chains and deleting all other edges gives a named kernel containing K, and the dual inclusions force equality. This direct incidence formulation includes freedoms in f on disconnected components.

Fix a solution. In the graph of edges monochromatic in ALL the potentials choose one spanning forest. Each Bz has even weight in each component because a feasible C_z exists there. The forest contains its unique join with boundary Bz. Use these joins in all lanes. Their union is a forest; any leaf has a nonzero edge mask and must belong to D, and at an unmarked degree-two vertex the lane masks agree. Keep every used vertex in D and all branching vertices. The number of branching vertices is ≤d and the number of segments is O(d), since every nonempty tree has at least two leaves. Distinct D vertices not on the forest are guessed absent and forbidden as interiors. Loops are never required and parallel copies with the same ends have identical influence. Isolated host vertices may retain arbitrary potentials.

Enumerate the forest skeleton, nonzero lane masks per segment, placements of its O(d) special vertices and one potential vector per component. Check lane-wise boundary equations including all D vertices. The dual equations Bᵀ f_b=b are just linear vertex-score tests (at most k² total bits). Preserve all colors on the forest. Positive vertex/lane contributions off the forest with the prescribed joint score can be reduced to at most k² such contributions by taking a basis within the previously selected contributions. Enumerate these positions, allowing a single vertex in multiple lanes; all other outside bits are zero. Forbid all outside guessed vertices as linkage interiors. Enumerate the sums of interior B-row vectors modulo two on each segment. The standard disjoint-path linkage routine returns a joint subdivision subject to these sums, using unoriented Z4 edge coordinates B_i(v)+B_i(w) to recover each strict-interior parity after subtracting endpoints. Clone placed vertices by incidence, reserving prescribed terminals and pinned direct edges as in the graphic portfolio proof. The guessed forest is acyclic (no parallel skeleton links), hence no duplication of direct host edges is possible. Verify the resulting component colors, score sums, and cuts against the explicit chains.

The target-dependent quotient need not be regular, even up to bounded strong deletion/contraction backdoor, when d=4. Use K₅ with center 0, four noncentral vertices 1,..,4, and at least N parallel copies of every edge. Name b supported on vertices 1,..,4 and c on vertices 0,1. For K=span{(1,0)}, W=span b. In reduced incidence coordinates b=1111. Modulo b, the four spoke types and the three complementary rim-pair types are precisely the seven nonzero points of a three-dimensional binary space: the quotient contains the Fano matroid. Under deletion of fewer than N edges every type survives, ruling out a strong regularity backdoor of size <N. The target is attained by contracting edges 12 and 34 and deleting the others. Their span contains b and excludes c. Larger connected hosts can be made by appending new vertices. This is a class separation for these two sufficient regularity conditions.
