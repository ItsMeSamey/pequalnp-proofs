# Globalizing the scalar obstruction and locating its residual graph

At a fixed even baseline y suppose at least four packets have pairwise disjoint constituent simple cycles, disjoint packets, and the same full gain tuple. Let k be that tuple's total rank across all primary quotients. Assume for each two retained packets at EVERY even baseline their concatenation has total rank at most k, as in the stalled matching case of robust-probe-packets.md. Choose a prime p and scalar χ annihilating the common span in A/pA. Write U for all retained constituent edges and w' for the physical weights after signing at y. There is a bounded set F⊆U such that, on every even support avoiding F, the unsigned χ(w') contribution of the entire union U is zero. The bound depends on packet width and the fixed odd group, independently of the number of packets or sizes of their cycles.

Exclude three anchor packets and take C to be the union of all remaining packets. If an even change z avoids one anchor, its gain tuple at y remains fixed. At y XOR z its rank already supplies all k old dimensions across the primary quotients. For every other individual packet, the concatenation rank cap forces every updated constituent gain in the old span in its respective quotient. Thus both old and new χ-gain vanish; their difference on a constituent J is minus twice the sum of χ(w') on z∩J. Since p is odd that sum vanishes. Adding over disjoint constituents gives zero χ(w')-score on C for any z avoiding any of the three anchors. Apply packet-arcs.md to this single distinguished physical set C, which need not be connected. In the graph of C after contracting all components of G−C the packing of nonzero cycles is bounded in terms of the three anchors' maximum number of connected members. Odd-group edge Erdős–Pósa gives a bounded transversal using only physical edges of C. It is a quiet modulator for the original graph's C-only score by projection. For each of the three excluded packets repeat on its edge set with three other packets as anchors. Take the union of these four bounded modulators. The argument uses the pointwise rank cap and does not require that a target be absent.

Given the named family, y and χ satisfying these hypotheses, construct each transversal by applying the existing deterministic quiet-recognition algorithm to the appropriate contracted graph, with the parameter bound supplied by edge Erdős–Pósa. This takes f(A,s)poly(n+m) for s the maximum packet width. The packet hypotheses themselves have not been recognized by this algorithm.

## Exact residual scalar graph

Delete F. The score of every remaining even support in the chosen signed scalar now equals the contribution on edges exterior to U. Contract connected components of the surviving U edges and keep all outside edges, including loops. Every even outside edge set of this quotient can be extended inside those components by matching its odd physical boundary, and every original even support projects to an even set. Thus the quotient has exactly the same scalar image, with witnesses liftable in polynomial time. This deals with one scalar and the stratum avoiding F; it does not lift the complete vector score. In particular, if all χ-active edges lie in U, the global signed scalar is quiet after deleting F.

There is quantitative equivalence for bounded scalar modulators in this residual reduction. In any graph with an odd abelian score supported on C, let Q be its C-edge graph after contracting components of G−C. A quiet cover using only C edges works in the physical graph precisely when it works in Q, by the same lift. If any k-edge quiet cover K exists in the physical graph, delete K∩C in Q. Removing the other at most k physical edges refines the contracted components by at most k additional components in total. The binary boundary vectors on refined components for Q-even supports lie in a space of dimension at most k, since the parities sum to zero on each coarse component. Among more than 2·2^k disjoint nonzero simple cycles, three would have the same refined boundary. Each pair would then extend avoiding all K and have zero group score, forcing three nonzero group elements pairwise opposite, impossible in odd order. Apply the odd-group edge Erdős–Pósa bound to the remaining Q, and restore K∩C to the cover. This yields a C-only quiet cover of size at most k+EP(2^(k+1)+1). The result is effective with the same quiet-recognition solver; no minimum-cover equality is asserted.

## A maximum-packing counterexample

In K_(3,2m) let the three hubs be a,b,c and pair the leaves u_i,v_i. Give au_i gain e1, cu_i gain e2 in F_p², and all other edges zero. The named cycles a-u_i-b-v_i-a are edge-disjoint and have only gain e1; at every even baseline their gains lie in {±e1}. All named packet pairs are universally nonbad. Each leaf of degree three lies on at most one cycle in any edge-disjoint packing, and every cycle needs at least two leaves, so these m cycles form a maximum packing. Their complement is the star centered at c. Yet the m cycles c-u_i-b-v_i-c each have a single e2-weighted edge, and remain e2-nonzero under arbitrary physical signs. Thus the scalar χ selecting e2 needs at least m deleted edges, attained by deleting all cu_i. The host is simple and 3-connected for m≥2. Every projection has unbounded packing as m grows; rank saturation in the whole graph follows by choosing e1 or e2 cycles on distinct pairs. No missing-target or hardness claim follows. Even maximum packing and an acyclic exterior do not replace the coverage condition.

## Detecting a supplied finite cover in score space

For each of several supplied stalled families obtain its signed scalar function f_j on its named union and its bounded physical quieting set F_j. Regions may overlap and even baselines may differ; the functions under comparison must take values in the same prime field. For a desired global signed scalar f, form canonical parity coefficients on binary cycle parameters by aggregating weights at identical nonzero edge evaluation forms. Zero forms never contribute. The remaining parity functions are linearly independent over the odd prime field, since a Walsh inner product with one associated character isolates its coefficient times a nonzero power of two. Test f∈span{f_j} by deterministic Gaussian elimination. If successful choose a representation with at most rank many nonzero coefficients. The union of these F_j quiets f on all even supports by linearity. This recognizes a bounded cover when the supplied functions span f with bounded total rank, without incorrectly counting overlapping regions twice. A scalar-preserving identity in the supplied functions exists exactly when this test succeeds. Other physical modulators can exist even if the span test fails.
