# Random star surrogates on a highly connected torso

Let B be n bag vertices, E0 actual bag edges, and let each credited child of a supplied tree decomposition have adhesion A⊆B of size at most a. Assume the child's interior is connected and touches every vertex of A; interiors of distinct children are disjoint. For a child with empty interior a direct physical realization may instead be handled as an actual edge. Children with at most one port supply no fill. The simple torso T has E0 and every edge of each credited adhesion clique; it excludes parent-only or unsupported fill. Overlap among the adhesions is arbitrary.

Choose one root uniformly and independently in each credited adhesion of size at least two. Assign the entire connected child interior to its root for the default construction, producing simultaneously all edges of the corresponding star as genuine contracted edges. Let J be the simple union of these stars and E0. The default assignment folds the interior label sum into the root. Uncredited children whose interior components merely have neighbors on the adhesion can be defaulted by attaching each to one fixed neighbor, giving no credited edges.

Fix K≥1, a≥2, C=binom(a,2), n≥2. The sufficient condition
κ(T) ≥ K + a C (K+6) ln(2n)
implies Pr[κ(J)≥K]≥1/2. In particular the degree of a bag vertex in the child incidence system is unrestricted.

For Z⊂B with |Z|<K let λ be the ordinary minimum edge cut of T−Z. The hypothesis implies it has enough vertices, and λ≥κ(T)−|Z|. Consider a nontrivial bipartition after deleting Z, crossed by j edges of T−Z. If an actual edge crosses, this cut cannot fail in J−Z. Otherwise assign each crossing torso edge to one credited child containing it. Each child can own at most C such edges, so at least j/C distinct children cross. For each child crossing the cut after deletion, its selected star certainly crosses if its root is anywhere in A outside Z: both cut shores contain a port. This happens with probability at least 2/a. Independent roots bound the cut failure probability by exp(−2j/(aC)).

Group cut sizes in [hλ,(h+1)λ), h=1,2,... . Karger's cut counting bound gives at most n^{2(h+1)} cuts of size at most (h+1)λ. The proof only needs integer approximation ratios. There are at most n^K choices of Z (for instance by encoding a length-<K sorted list with a distinguished padding symbol or using the binomial sum). For n≥2 and K≥1, Σ_{i<K} binom(n,i)≤n^K. Thus the total failure probability is at most the sum over h≥1 of
n^{K+2(h+1)} (2n)^{−2(K+6)h}.
This geometric sum is smaller than one half. Check κ(J)≥K by ordinary max-flow computations, retrying independent choices if needed; expected attempts are at most two. The certificate can be verified deterministically. [Karger, cut counting, Theorem A.1](https://people.csail.mit.edu/karger/Papers/skeleton-journal.pdf).

For use in the existing bounded-adhesion binary connected-partition algorithm, take K at least that algorithm's pinned physical-bag connectivity requirement plus F C, where F bounds the number of nondefault children in any table witness. When a child becomes exceptional, remove all of its credited star edges and omit its default folded contribution, then use its actual child record and pin its adhesion colors. At most FC edges are removed and vertex connectivity drops by at most that count. The remaining graph still meets the pinned physical routing bound. In a default child's rooted branch region, color the entire interior by its root's color: every selected star edge whose two ports have equal color is realized, and the resulting interior is connected to its root. The same chosen stars therefore work for every table row and every exceptional set in that bag. Verify the surrogates once per large bag, before the deterministic table calculation. Expected total work remains polynomial for fixed parameters, with arbitrary tree depth, since this step is additive across the supplied bags.
