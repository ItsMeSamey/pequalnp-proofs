# A constant connectivity threshold for arbitrary binary charges on actual-component torsos

Fix an adhesion bound a≥2, small bag size s≥a, number of ordered nonempty parts b≥1, and binary label space W of dimension r. For a connected graph G and a bag X, form the simple torso T_X from G[X] by adding a clique on the exact bag neighbors of each connected component of G−X. Suppose G admits a tree decomposition of adhesion at most a whose every bag X either has size at most s or has κ(T_X)≥f. For a sufficiently large constant f=f(a,b,r), one can recognize this class and, on it, decide and construct a partition into b connected parts having prescribed sums in W, deterministically in polynomial time for fixed parameters. Port overlap and the sum of a complementary component are unrestricted.

Use the recognizer in recognizable-component-torsos.md, including its exact small-bag/adhesion assembly, and the weak boundary records in research/span-separation/cographic-separators.md. Every component off X lies on one side of the tree node and has all its ports in that side's one adhesion. Omit the parent side, which costs at most C=binom(a,2) distinct clique edges. The remaining components D are disjoint child interiors with nonempty port sets A_D⊆X of size at most a. Their individual charges g_D are the sums of their actual labels. All sums of labels below are binary.

## Predetermining default pivots

Put F=b+r(b−1), p=a(F+1)+b and M=p+4br+b+3. For each nonzero g in W greedily take distinct interiors of charge g, marking two distinct unused ports of each chosen interior, until M interiors have been taken or no choice remains. The marked pairs within each family are pairwise disjoint. Call types reaching M abundant and let H be their linear span. Let P be the union of the entire port sets of the selected interiors of all remaining nonzero types. Then |P|≤L=a(2^r−1)(M−1).

For a nonabundant nonzero type, every interior has at most one port outside P. This holds for selected interiors by construction. Any unselected counterexample could have been appended to its greedy family since two ports outside P avoid all marks. Choose the unique outside port as pivot when one exists, or any port otherwise. Replace each such interior's clique by its star about that pivot. Every lost edge has both ends in P, so all these replacements together lose at most binom(L,2) distinct edges. The star is simultaneously valid for each bag coloring when the entire interior takes the pivot's color. Its true charge folds into that pivot. P need never be avoided during routing. All other components (abundant types and zero types) get an arbitrary baseline pivot used during boundary normalization. Their actual routing assignment may subsequently change.

## Weak boundary normalization

For a feasible record at X start with an actual witness. Retain each child with a color closed in its interior and absent from its own adhesion. Such a closed color cannot occur anywhere else in the witness: otherwise that color would either have a component missing the parent interface while present there, or have two components while absent there. Hence at most b children are retained this way. For each remaining child compare its actual vector of ordered interior sums with defaulting each of its components to its baseline pivot, preserving the current bag coloring. Each difference has total zero across the b colors. Their total is a sum of at most r(b−1) of those differences, by choosing a basis among them. Retain these children as well, and default all others. The resulting assignment has the required ordered sums. Any color in a nonretained child occurred at one of its adhesion vertices, so this also preserves the palette. Connectivity will be rebuilt.

Enumerate up to F retained children, their records and compatible boundary colors, the parent record, the set of q bag colors and a representative for each. Initial bag pins include all retained child adhesions, the parent adhesion and the representatives, at most p total. A color absent from the bag occurs only as closed in one retained child, with final score checked there. A closed child color cannot occur elsewhere. For a bag color, subtract retained interior contributions and restore parent-interface vertex labels omitted from the requested parent interior score. The adjusted targets count all bag vertices and all default interiors, once each. Omitting all credits of retained children costs at most FC distinct torso edges.

## First correct the quotient scores

Let Q=W/H and l=q−1. Fold charges of nonretained nonabundant nonzero interiors into their respective fixed pivots. Give each bag vertex its resulting label in Q. Other nonretained interiors have zero charge in Q. The normalized assignment shows that the adjusted projected targets are achievable by an unrestricted assignment of these bag labels respecting the initial pins. Check this elementary condition by ordered-sum DP and recover one such assignment. Also require total equality of adjusted true targets with actual remaining labels. For q=1 assign all remaining material to that color and check its exact sum; the surviving torso after omitted parent and retained fills is connected, its paths lift through default interiors, and every such interior has a port. In what follows q≥2.

Among the unpinned bag vertices choose S≤Q minimizing the number of projected labels outside S plus l dim S. Write d=dim S. Comparison with Q bounds the number of unpinned vertices outside S by l(dim Q−d). Comparison with all T≤S yields at least l(d−dim T) in-S vertices labeled outside T. By linear matroid union, the in-S vertices contain l disjoint bases of S: for any subset U of them, the number outside U plus l rank(U) is at least ld using T=span(U). Reserve one vertex basis per owner color (the first l colors) as bag toggles. Pin each initially unpinned vertex outside S to its color in the unrestricted projected witness. These extra pins and reserved bag vertices together number at most lr.

Choose a basis of H from the abundant types. For each of its types and each owner color select one nonretained interior from its recorded M-family as a second kind of toggle. Its marked pair must avoid all initial pins, rare vertices, bag toggles and previously selected pairs. Within each family any forbidden port disqualifies at most one candidate. At most p+lr+2lr ports are forbidden before each choice. A retained child contains no eligible candidate because its adhesion is pinned. Distinct pairs imply distinct interiors within each family, and different families have different charges. Pin the first port of each new pair to the owner and the second to the last color, and withhold the selected interior entirely from routing. These at most lr deleted centers cost at most lr C further torso edges. Every new port had projected label in S. Prescribing its new color changes each projected per-color score only by an element of S.

Use the initial representative in each bag color as its anchor. Join every other fixed pin (including new pairs and the rare vertices) to its prescribed anchor by a template edge, and join each reserved bag toggle to both its own and the last anchor. The branch count is at most p+3br and edge count at most p+4br. In the routing graph include actual bag edges, the fixed stars above as edges on the bag, and one independent center per other nonretained nonwithheld interior, adjacent to its exact ports. Each free center has label zero in Q. Apply the bag-terminal linkage and pinned-template proof of zero-charge-constant.md. It yields internally disjoint routed template edges in this incidence graph, with their prescribed branch vertices. The paths to fixed pins and the portions of toggle paths with their toggles removed give disjoint connected color cores. After removing bag toggles all surviving bag vertices are in a single component, and all centers with a surviving port lie there too. Grow the cores to cover this component, then provisionally assign all bag toggles to the last color. Centers with only bag-toggle ports can wait until the toggles' final assignment. Every vertex outside S has the color used for computing the projected target, and all new port pins had labels in S. Thus projected deficits belong to S. Each owner's reserved bag basis realizes its deficit by moving some of its toggles from last to owner. Both attachments already exist, and prior cores stay connected. By total equality the last projected score follows. Color any delayed free center like one of its final ports. Every free center now has a same-colored neighbor port since its connected part contains its bag anchor and centers have no center neighbors.

## Lift to true scores

Expand each fixed star's interior entirely in its pivot color. A monochromatic used star edge lifts through that physical connected interior, and every such interior attaches to its pivot. Expand each free center entirely in its assigned color. Each resulting connected bag-color core includes all its prescribed ports and excludes every withheld interior. Provisionally assign all withheld interiors to the last color, to which each has a designated port. Add retained child witnesses. Every open component there meets an adhesion vertex in the already connected bag color. Closed colors and sums were checked separately. Hence the parent boundary connections required by the weak record are met, allowing stronger same-color connections.

Every bag-color deficit in the true space now belongs to H, and their sum is zero. For each owner independently express its deficit in the chosen basis of H. For every basis type used give that owner's dedicated withheld interior to the owner instead of last. Its true charge is that basis element, its owner port is in the owner's connected core, and the last core is connected without any withheld interior. Targets hold for every owner, hence also for last by conservation. Every constructed record has a real witness, so the weak boundary DP remains sound. The normalization and the two corrections show completeness for every feasible record.

## One explicit constant

Write B=p+3br+b+2 and m=p+4br+b+2. For integer f it suffices to take
```
f = 2 + a + a*C + (1+F+br)*C + binom(L,2)
    + B + 2*m*(a+C+1) + C*(2*m+br+1) + a*2^(3*m−1).
```
After omitting parent credits, retained children, scarce-type internal losses and withheld toggles, the remaining torso exceeds the minimum degree needed for all distinct private spokes, B+2m+2ma. After deleting at most B branch vertices and 2m spoke centers it exceeds both C(2m−1) and a*2^(3m−1), as required by the relative bag linkage lemma. The bag stays connected after its at most br bag toggles are removed. Recognition uses f>a+aC. All enumeration bounds are fixed; recovery of disjoint paths, component expansions and witness backtracking are deterministic polynomial-time procedures. At a high node a default subtree may be colored directly by its physical components, and retained subtrees are recovered through their records.

For four-dimensional binary scores and a specified plane, enumerate the ordered nonzero component sums for three connected parts. This gives an algorithm on the stated recognizable class. The unrestricted host problem remains open. The earlier logarithmic-threshold theorem separately constructs a single globally highly connected bag-star minor. The present construction uses the requested sums and pins when choosing withheld interiors and performing the routing.
