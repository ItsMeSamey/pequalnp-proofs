# A constant torso threshold for freely routed zero-charge components

Let B be a bag. Form R from the actual bag edges and one independent auxiliary vertex for each credited child-interior component. Join that vertex to its at most a exact neighboring bag vertices. Each such component is connected and touches every listed port. Also allow fixed physical default stars or other simultaneously certified edges; their label contributions are folded into known bag vertices as in the existing boundary algorithm. The auxiliary vertices whose arbitrary turns are credited here must have zero total binary label, so they can follow any routed color. Let T be the simple clique completion of these stars on B, including the actual and fixed certified edges. This note proves a deterministic bag compiler at a sufficiently large threshold on κ(T) depending only on the fixed parameters, with no log |B| or bound on port overlap.

## Linking bag vertices in the incidence graph

Write C=binom(a,2) and assume a≥2. For k≥1 put t=3k+1. It suffices to have κ(T)>C(2k−1) and κ(T)≥a 2^{t−2} to route every pairing of 2k distinct bag vertices by vertex-disjoint paths in R. These inequalities are deliberately coarse. If up to b_0 bag vertices and c_0 free centers must first be deleted, adding b_0+c_0 C to this threshold suffices.

First, any set S of fewer than 2k vertices in R leaves all surviving bag vertices connected. Deleting S∩B costs its size in vertex connectivity in T. Suppressing all clique contributions of deleted nonbag vertices costs at most C edges per such vertex, so the remaining torso stays connected and its paths lift to walks in R−S. This also proves the preliminary damage bound, since each individual edge deletion decreases vertex connectivity by at most one in this range.

Next, contract each auxiliary center to one uniformly chosen surviving neighboring bag vertex, omitting centers with no neighbors. Each such contraction creates a genuine minor on the bag. Every edge of the remaining torso survives with probability ≥2/a since either it was already an actual edge or its two endpoints lie in one credited adhesion. Thus some minor J on the bag has at least (2/a)|E(T)|≥κ(T)|B|/a edges; use the damaged torso and its surviving B in this argument if there were preliminary deletions. This expectation is only an existence bound. A graph with at least 2^{t−2}n edges has a K_t minor: induct on t≥2; take a vertex-minimal minor still meeting the stated density, remove any isolated vertices, and note that contracting an edge loses one plus the number of common neighbors of its endpoints. Consequently every edge has at least 2^{t−2} common neighbors, and the neighborhood of any vertex has density at least 2^{t−3}, giving a K_{t−1} minor there. The extra vertex is adjacent to all its branch sets. The case t=2 is immediate.

Lift the K_t model of J to R. Every lifted branch set contains a bag vertex. No separation of order <2k can contain all designated terminals on one side and a whole branch set on the other side outside the separator: a terminal off the separator and a surviving bag vertex in that branch would lie in different components. Robertson–Seymour's rooted clique linkage theorem therefore gives the required disjoint paths. They can be found deterministically by a fixed-pairs linkage algorithm, without searching for the dense minor or using random bits. [Robertson and Seymour, Graph Minors XIII, (5.4)](https://doi.org/10.1006/jctb.1995.1006) and [Kawarabayashi, Kobayashi and Reed, explicit formulation of the clique linkage criterion](https://research.nii.ac.jp/~k_keniti/quaddp1.pdf).

## Applying a pinned connected-sum template

Use the packed bases, rare vertices, anchors, and boundary pins of the pinned physical-bag lemma in research/span-separation/cographic-separators.md. All nonzero movable labels are at bag vertices; fixed default components are represented by the labels at their fixed pivots. Each template branch vertex is therefore a bag vertex. Let b_0 be a bound on the number of branch vertices and k on the number of prescribed template edges. Both depend only on the fixed dimensions and pin count. For each template edge-end choose a distinct fresh bag proxy adjacent in T to its branch vertex. Realize this adjacency by an actual/fixed edge or by a two-edge spoke through a free center different from all previously used spoke centers. Greedy choice works with minimum degree exceeding b_0+2k+2ka: avoid all branch/proxy vertices and the at most a ports per previously used center. Delete the branch vertices and chosen spoke centers before solving the k paired paths between the distinct proxy endpoints. The preceding lemma applies after adding b_0+2kC to the sufficient connectivity. Restore all spokes to obtain the required internally disjoint rooted subdivision. If k=0 there are no attachment paths to find.

The resulting connected color cores contain the required prescribed vertices and give each reserved basis vertex an attachment to each of its two allowed parts. With all toggles removed, the surviving bag points are still mutually connected by the connectivity argument. Every remaining free center with a surviving port lies in their component. Grow the color cores over that component, and correct the binary sums by assigning the disjoint designated basis toggles in the usual manner. Free centers with only toggles as neighbors, omitted during growth, can now be assigned to any of their now-colored neighbors. They have zero charge. Every free center is adjacent to a same-colored port in the resulting partition: it belongs to a part containing an anchor on the bag and has no center neighbors. Expand it to the entire corresponding real interior, retaining actual connections for that color and preserving sums. When an arbitrary number of zero-charge interior components lie in one child, their omitted fill on making that child exceptional still costs at most C distinct torso edges in total. Parent-only credit is omitted. Add these constant edge costs (including FC for F exceptional children) to the original connectivity threshold as usual.

For a plain actual-component-torso class one can require every credited complementary component at every large bag to have binary sum zero. This is computable from the graph and labels. Then the exact recognizer of recognizable-component-torsos.md still works with this extra label test and a sufficiently large constant f > a+aC. Its reconstruction uses the original graph and label data to verify each retained bag. The more general fixed-star option above is useful with supplied certificates as well. This result concerns zero-charge freely routable components; replacing them by arbitrary nonzero charges would change the packed-basis corrections and is not justified by structural linkage alone.
