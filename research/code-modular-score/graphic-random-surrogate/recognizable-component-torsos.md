# Recognizable high-connectivity torsos from actual complementary components

Let G be a finite connected graph. Fix a≥2, s≥a, and C=binom(a,2). For X⊆V(G), define T_X on X from the actual edges of G[X], plus a clique on the exact neighbors in X of each connected component D of G−X. Define a large permissible bag X by |X|>s, |N_G(D)|≤a for every such D, and κ(T_X)≥f(|X|), with a fixed polynomial-time test for the integer threshold f and f(n)>a+aC. Small permissible bags have size ≤s.

There is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm, for fixed parameters, deciding whether G admits a tree decomposition of adhesion ≤a into permissible bags, and returning one when it exists. This permits sparse or edgeless G[X].

For a large permissible X, deleting any set S of at most a original vertices cannot separate two untouched vertices of X. Remove S∩X from T_X; this drops vertex connectivity by at most a and leaves edge connectivity greater than aC. Ignore the clique edges of every complementary component containing a deleted nonbag vertex; there are at most a such components, each costing at most C edges. The surviving torso is connected, and each of its edges can be followed in G−S because every credited complementary component is untouched and connected. Thus X is pairwise a-inseparable in the original graph.

For every pairwise a-inseparable seed R of size a+1 form all vertices inseparable from every seed, including the seeds themselves. Retain those sets X passing the independently computable permissibility test. Under a promised decomposition this lists precisely its large bags. For R inside such a bag H, every vertex of H is included by the result above; an outside vertex x is separated from some seed by the first tree-edge adhesion toward its occurrence subtree, which misses x and has at most a vertices. Conversely any retained X is pairwise inseparable. Its vertex occurrence subtrees must meet pairwise, by the adhesion bound, hence by subtree Helly they share a bag. This bag must be large and contains R, so equals X by the first direction. Complementary-component neighborhood sizes at a decomposition bag are in any case bounded by its adhesion: a component lies in one branch away from that bag, and all its neighbors in the bag lie in the first edge adhesion.

In detail, fill every retained X to a clique in the auxiliary recognition graph, split recursively at clique separators of size ≤a, and keep a terminal region equal to one retained X as a single bag. Under the promise, any terminal region containing a whole retained X must equal it, since the first promised adhesion toward any outside vertex would give a clique separator inside X. A terminal region without a whole retained X meets each one in at most a vertices; the restricted promised decomposition witnesses small bags of size ≤s and adhesion ≤a. Recognize those small regions exactly by the boundary DP of the cited source, then glue across clique interfaces using subtree Helly. Verify the final decomposition, including every large bag's T_X test in the original graph. Auxiliary recognition cliques are never supplied as physical routing edges.

## Ordered binary vertex sums

For fixed label dimension r and b ordered colors, let K0 be the pinned physical-bag connectivity threshold required by research/span-separation/cographic-separators.md; let F=b+r(b−1) be its bound on retained exceptional children. Put K=K0+FC. It suffices to use any integer threshold f(n) that is at least
C + K + a C (K+6) ln(2n)
and strictly greater than a+aC. For example replace ln(2n) by the computable integer ceil(log_2(2n)). With this recognized decomposition the binary connected-partition decision and construction run in Las Vegas expected polynomial time at the fixed parameters, with arbitrary depth, degree, and repeated port overlaps.

To see how to use the global torso after rooting, fix a node with bag X. Every actual component of G−X belongs to exactly one incident decomposition-tree branch. The child interiors off their adhesion consist precisely of the components in the respective branches. The components in the parent branch, taken together, have neighbors only in its single adhesion of size ≤a. Omitting all of their clique contributions therefore costs at most C *distinct* edges. The torso left from actual edges and the other component cliques has vertex connectivity at least f(|X|)−C. Independently root each actual child-interior component at a uniform one of its exact neighboring bag vertices. Assigning that whole component to its root realizes the resulting star whenever the endpoints have equal color and folds its labels into that root. The random-star theorem README.md SHA256 a9608b6a01e3896b75af39975ae2b6af13ebf7e21969132fee88056bd5c902f4 gives a verifiable simultaneous surrogate of connectivity at least K after expected at most two samples. A child subtree may contain many independent components, but all of their roots' neighbors belong to the same adhesion. Making that entire child exceptional therefore removes at most C distinct star edges, independently of its component count. Removing up to F such families still meets K0. The boundary DP then applies without treating parent fill, recognition fill, or any unsupported clique edge as a physical edge. The small bags use their exact ordinary physical edges and child tables.

This class concerns torsos formed using actual complementary components of the original graph. It does not assert a high-connectivity surrogate from arbitrary unsupported clique fills.
