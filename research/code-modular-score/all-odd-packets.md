# Full spectrum for arbitrary finite odd abelian scores

Write Q(z) for the scalar unsigned score and Q_C(z)=Q(z∩C). A packet is the disjoint union of at most s simple cycles and at most s additional physical single edges. Suppose four pairwise edge-disjoint packets each intersect every nonzero simple cycle, with odd scalar scores. Then the edge-disjoint packing number of all nonzero simple cycles is bounded solely by s and the scalar group.

Every even support avoiding a given packet has zero score by cycle decomposition. For a constituent cycle C of one packet and an even support z avoiding a different packet, Q(z), Q(C), Q(z XOR C) all vanish, so 2Q_C(z)=0. Apply packet-separators.md to C and the three other packets. In the multigraph obtained by contracting the components of G−C the packing number of Q_C-nonzero simple cycles on the C edges is bounded. The odd unsigned edge Erdős–Pósa theorem there gives a bounded physical cover F_C inside C. Every original even support avoiding F_C has Q_C zero by projection and decomposition. Loops and parallel edges are covered by the half-edge port reduction in two-odd-factors.md.

In just three anchor packets delete F_C for all constituent cycles, and delete all their additional single edges. Denote these boundedly many deletions by F. On G−F the scalar sum equals the score supported solely OUTSIDE these three anchors, for every even support. Each surviving anchor is a union of boundedly many connected sets: deleting d edges from a cycle leaves at most d+1 nonempty edge pieces. Avoiding one surviving anchor still gives zero score since deleted edges cannot occur. Apply packet-separators.md again, using as distinguished edge set ALL edges outside the three surviving anchors. Every nonzero simple cycle projects to a nonzero even support there, hence contains a nonzero simple cycle after projection. Disjoint original cycles give disjoint edge subsets there. Thus the global packing number in G−F is bounded, and adding |F| bounds the original packing. If an anchor disappears all surviving scores already vanish.

For each finite odd abelian A there is a computable k(A) such that the full graphic even-support spectrum is A whenever every nonzero scalar χ:A/pA→F_p, for every prime divisor p, has at least k(A) edge-disjoint χ-nonzero simple cycles. Induct on |A|; the trivial case is immediate. From one high scalar packing pigeonhole equal full gain a with nonzero scalar. Make many disjoint packets P_i of exp(A) such cycles. If G−P_i satisfies the inductive packing condition over A/⟨a⟩ for any i, use its full quotient spectrum and toggle a suitable number of untapped P_i cycles to lift any desired target in A.

Otherwise pigeonhole the same deficient scalar ψ on this quotient for many bundles. It lifts to a nonzero scalar of A/pA. Odd unsigned edge Erdős–Pósa gives bounded K_i in G−P_i covering all ψ-nonzero cycles there. Thin K_i to a large sunflower (if a set repeats sufficiently many times it is a sunflower with empty petals). Delete the bounded common core K. Discard any bundles P_i meeting the core, at most |K| indices. The remaining petals are disjoint. Each meets at most |K_i| other P_j, so their directed conflict graph has bounded outdegree; every induced graph then has bounded average undirected degree and a large independent subset exists. Choose four independent indices. In G−K the P_i plus their petals are disjoint packets of boundedly many cycles and additional edges, and each separately hits every ψ-nonzero cycle. Our first theorem bounds the global ψ-packing in G−K, and adding |K| bounds it in G. This contradicts the high hypothesis. The thresholds are effective by taking maxima over proper quotient thresholds and their odd EP covers; these bound the packet width, common core, thinning and final packing contradiction before k(A) is chosen.

For exact decision search using quiet-recognition.md for a physical quiet cover in each scalar up to the odd edge EP bound for k(A). Absence of all such covers implies saturation. With an actual low cover reduce to ker χ by quiet-ports.md and the nonsplit transport and uniform-exponent recursion of frattini-plane.md. Only bounded removal, individual projected weights zero in simple torsos, and strict decrease of odd group order are used there, without a rank bound. Binary side constraints are removed by the period/physical-cover procedure first, concatenating the bounded binary and odd removals at each original simple 3-connected torso. Elementary torsos use direct tables, and outer hashing is incurred once. Internal recursion only uses decisions. One outer deletion self-reduction recovers physical witnesses. This yields deterministic f(|A|,|B|)poly(n+m) for arbitrary odd abelian A and elementary binary B. The high case uses no explicit construction of the packets, covers in quotient proof or sunflowers.

The underlying integral vertex Erdős–Pósa result for odd groups is Thomas and Yoo, Theorem 1.1. The port construction above supplies the physical edge version. Independent proof reviews covered the four-packet bound, quotient induction, binary-period reduction, recursion on simple torsos and witness recovery. The polynomial exponent is independent of both group orders. [Thomas and Yoo](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jctb.2023.02.011)
