# A conservative named-kernel catalogue on graphs of bounded rank-width

Fix binary name dimension p, rank-width bound r, and a bound on the number of individually protected vertices. Give each vertex of a connected finite simple graph G a p-bit charge. Contracting a connected partition and deleting surplus edges yields the row space spanned by its block charge sums, or equivalently its orthogonal named kernel. The catalogue ranges over all connected partitions. Given a rank decomposition of width at most r, a deterministic algorithm polynomial in the input size for fixed parameters produces a connected conservative spanning minor of computably bounded order with exactly this catalogue. All vertices belong to physical connected branches, every resulting charge is the sum on its branch, and designated ports survive in distinct prescribed branches. The final rank-width need not equal the supplied bound.

**Bounded witnesses.** Write s(p) for the number of subspaces of the binary p-space. Every attainable row space U of dimension d has a connected partition with at most d+1 parts. In a witnessing partition with k>d+1 parts let L map the k coefficient bits to the combination of the part sums. Merging adjacent parts i,j replaces its domain by the hyperplane of equal i,j coordinates. The image persists if the kernel contains a vector whose i,j coordinates differ. If no quotient edge allows such a merger, every kernel vector is constant on the connected quotient, contradicting nullity k-d>1. Repeat. For each space enumerate its spanning lists of at most p+1 vectors, quantify a partition into that many nonempty connected sets, and test its charges by parity in CMSO1. At fixed parameters these tests are effective on the supplied rank decomposition by conversion to bounded clique-width and finite-state evaluation. Witnesses are retrievable by fixing one vertex at a time to one of the parts and retesting existence with unary constraints. This takes O_p(n) tests per space. No port restriction is needed on these catalogue witnesses. If the desired catalogue includes port-indicator coordinates, include them in p from the start.

**Physical spanning subgraph.** Collect all the chosen witness blocks and the whole vertex set. Their number B is at most 1+(p+1)s(p). Give vertices distinct priorities from 1 through n. For every collected block take a minimum spanning tree of its induced graph with edge weights equal to the sum of endpoint priorities. Let H on the original vertex set be the union of these trees, including singleton blocks as vertices. H is connected by the whole-graph tree. Each chosen partition is still connected block by block, with unchanged charges. Every connected partition of H remains connected in G. Consequently H has exactly the same catalogue and is obtained by physical edge deletions preserving all vertices and ports.

**An explicit tree decomposition of H.** On each shore of each layout cut group vertices having identical neighborhoods in the entire opposite shore of G. There are at most 2^r classes per shore by binary cut-rank, applied also to the transpose. For each collected block mark the minimum-priority vertex in each nonempty intersection of a class with that block. At most 2^(r+1)B vertices are marked per cut. If xy is an edge of a block tree across the cut, its two endpoint classes form a complete bipartite rectangle in G. If neither endpoint is marked for this block, let x0 and y0 be their respective class minima in the block. The three physical edges x-y0, y0-x0, x0-y all belong to the induced block, and all have strictly lower weight than xy. This excludes xy from every minimum spanning tree by the cycle property. Thus every crossing edge of H touches a mark.

For a vertex v form a subtree T_v of the layout containing its own leaf and all layout edges on which v is marked for some block. Indeed, if v is marked on a shore S, the same block marks it on any smaller nested shore containing v. Opposite shores grow, so its class can only shrink there. Every marked edge is therefore connected to its own leaf by marked edges. For any H-edge uv every layout edge on the path between their leaves belongs to T_u or T_v by the preceding cover, so the two subtrees meet at a layout node. Place v in every bag indexed by a node of T_v. This covers vertices and edges and has connected occurrences. At each internal layout node the bag size is at most 3 times the per-cut marking bound because the layout is subcubic. At a leaf, its own vertex is marked on its singleton shore for the whole-graph block if n>=2. The single-vertex graph is immediate. This gives a supplied tree decomposition of H of width at most 3*2^(r+1)B-1. Marks and bags can be computed directly by comparing cut neighborhoods in polynomial time.

**Composition and ports.** Apply the bounded-treewidth conservative spanning-minor theorem in bounded-treewidth-additive-catalogue.md, in its empty-template case, to H and the displayed bags. Preserve every fixed catalogue sentence, connectedness, and individual protected vertices using distinct port bits in the conserved group and distinct prescribed surviving ports. The theorem keeps designated port branches distinct; alternatively require an additional CMSO sentence saying that no resulting vertex carries two port bits and every bit occurs. Initially every port bit occurs exactly once, and in any physical spanning minor its occurence identifies its unique branch. These auxiliary bits may be omitted from the queried catalogue if they are only physical protections. Composition with the initial edge deletions gives actual spanning connected branches in G and preserves all named kernels. The uniform bound is the computable bounded-treewidth bound evaluated at the width just displayed and the fixed charge, query, and port parameters. The finitely many CMSO tests, tree calculations, bag construction, and bounded-treewidth reduction give fixed-parameter polynomial time. This argument preserves the specified catalogue; arbitrary extra CMSO properties can change under the initial edge deletions.

The local structural assertion is independent of logic: for any B supplied connected vertex sets on a width-r layout, the union of their priority-weighted induced minimum spanning trees has the explicit width bound above. The whole-graph tree and named-kernel formulas specialize it to this problem.
