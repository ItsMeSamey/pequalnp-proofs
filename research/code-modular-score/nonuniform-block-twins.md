# Variable twin fibers of connected block graphs

Replace every base vertex v by a nonempty clique or independent fiber of size s(v) at most fixed k, with complete joins across base edges. The resulting connected charged graphs are WQO under conservative spanning minors with finite representative labels and protected ports.

Root the base block incidence tree at a physical vertex. Order each fiber arbitrarily. Record on each node its type and cumulative k-vector of clone charges on the root path inclusive, padding unused positions by zero and assigning connectors zero. Record all local clone labels and charges, sizes, clique/independent types, and distinguish the root. Give each edge a priority (s,t), where s is the size of its unique physical endpoint and t encodes the whole source charge in its downward subtree. Rank this finite alphabet lexicographically with s primary. Stabilize total charge. By Robertson–Seymour's gap tree WQO, as stated in Bowler–Nigussie Theorem 5′, §1.1.3 and Remark 2 (https://www.math.uni-hamburg.de/spag/dm/papers/BowlerNigussie_TreeIdeals.pdf), a forward meet-preserving embedding respects all node labels, has the exact priority on the first and last edges of each replacement path, and never uses a lower priority on the path. This theorem assumes a fixed finite maximum priority. The marked root matches exactly.

Cut the first edge below each matched parent connector toward each nonroot target physical vertex. The resulting regions each contain exactly one matched physical vertex, as in block-graph-conservative.md. Their total charges match by subtracting outgoing subtree charges from the incoming charge (whole sum at the root). For each target physical vertex v, the skeleton of its region consists of its matched vertex and the internal vertices on the paths replacing its incident target edges, including the adjacent matched connectors when they lie in the region. On each arm, every intermediate physical vertex has capacity at least s(v), by the gap bound on its two incident path edges.

For 2≤i≤s(v), assign strand i exactly clone i of the matched vertex and of every intermediate physical vertex on these arms. All remaining clones of the region go to strand 1. Each strand has a connected spine because consecutive physical vertices on each arm share a source clique. Physical fibers off the spine attach to strand 1, either at a spine fiber across a neighboring fiber or at a spine connector via its neighboring spine fiber. Unused clones at an intermediate spine fiber have a neighboring spine fiber toward the matched vertex and thus reach strand 1 even for false twins. All clones at the matched vertex are used individually.

For every embedded target edge the strictly intermediate sum of k-vectors is zero: subtract the cumulative source vectors at the matched endpoints, then subtract the lower endpoint's local contribution. This equals the same calculation at the target's two consecutive nodes. Thus each strand i≥2 has exactly its intended charge, and the region total gives the result for strand 1. At each matched connector the immediate physical neighbor along each incident arm carries every strand of its owner; these neighbors are distinct members of one source clique. Every required pair of clones in different incident target fibers is therefore simultaneously adjacent. Within true-twin fibers use the edges between matched clones. Representative labels and one-hot protected ports are retained.

At fixed k this class has an effective full-catalogue library by the emptiness loop of modular-width-conservative.md: a finite tree representation uses clique connectors, fiber nodes and individual clone leaves; physical adjacency and all previous predicates are interpretable there. Even at unbounded fiber sizes, twin-catalogue-pruning.md first bounds every supplied fiber at fixed query dimension and port count without changing the catalogue or emptying any fiber. Composing gives a finite computable catalogue bound at unbounded multiplicity. Only the bounded-multiplicity case has been proved WQO by this argument.
