# Completing a specified bond to an exact cographic plane

Let G be connected, let each ordinary edge have gain in Γ=F₂⁴, and let H≤Γ have dimension two. Write q(v) for the XOR of incident edge gains, counting both ends of loops. For a permitted support X, the image of the cuts supported in X is the span of the q-sums on the components of G−X. Indeed a shore supports its cut in X precisely when it is a union of these components. On a disconnected base graph the same formula holds. Target attainability means equality of this image to H.

Let C be any specified cut with gain h∈H nonzero; a bond is sufficient but not necessary. There is a deterministic polynomial-time test and witness for an exact plane whose support contains all of C. Delete C from the partition graph **keeping the original charges q**. Quotient the charges by h and test for component sums spanning exactly the specified line H/⟨h⟩ in Γ/⟨h⟩. Every choice then gives an original support containing C and thus attaining h. Its score equals H iff its projected score equals this line. Use the deterministic three-element binary-matroid algorithm with the incidence matrix of the remaining graph and the vertex-by-three charge matrix. The kernel desired in the coefficient space is the plane annihilating the prescribed score line; three re-based named elements and a circuit query in the dual find it, followed by deletion self-reduction for a witness. Dependent named columns present no issue for the terminal-minor test, or one can restrict to the effective charge span and test in its own dimension. No requirement that G−C remain connected enters the incidence formula. A single-label augmentation that changes q by recomputing divergence after deleting C would answer a different question.

When C is a bond, its two shores are connected, each with original sum h and projected sum zero. The line test is equivalent to a projected line existing in one of the shores, because the two shore catalogues combine independently and zero is available on each whole shore. The successful side can split into two connected parts by the rank-one adjacent-part merger argument; their original sums are the two elements of H outside ⟨h⟩, as they add to h. Together with the other whole shore they give one compatible three-part partition. Testing all cuts of cardinality at most b takes m^{O(b)} polynomial time for fixed b, and is complete when a solution has a nonzero H-gain bond of that size contained in its permitted support. Every plane witness admits two independent gain bonds: choose a spanning forest inside its parts, extend to a graph spanning tree, and use fundamental cuts at the added edges. Those cuts avoid the forest and their gains span the component-sum image.

For two given independent H-gain bonds C₁,C₂, their joint support extends to the plane exactly when every connected component of G−(C₁∪C₂) has sum in H. The union already supports both cuts. Its image cannot decrease by allowing further edges. Thus the union itself witnesses equality iff all its connected corner components have zero tag modulo H.

For a bridge e the singleton {e} is a cut. If its gain is outside H, e cannot be permitted by any solution, so contract it in the partition graph and add endpoint charges. If its gain belongs to H, allowing it never damages a solution: when e was absent, the enlarged supported cut space is the old space plus the singleton cut vector, by cancelling that coordinate. Hence allow all such bridges, whose gains generate a guaranteed subspace B≤H. Two independent safe bridge gains immediately certify YES. If dim B=1, remove all safe bridges from the partition graph, retain the original charges (contracting forced unsafe bridges as above), and run the same three-name line oracle modulo B. Its answer on the entire remaining graph is exact. A zero-gain bridge may always be allowed as well. These rules generalize to a separately selectable cut-code direct summand whose whole image is contained in H.

Independent-height tests alone fail. For a four-connected planar control take the octahedral graph K₆ minus 03,14,25. Vertex charges, in binary-mask decimal notation, are (0,4,8,1,6,11), and H={0,1,2,3}. Total charge vanishes, and charge rank is four. Actual bonds with gains 1,2,3 respectively have shores {3}, {0,1,4}, {0,2,5}. An abstract three-bin assignment of heights 1,2,3 uses {3}, {1,4}, {0,2,5}. In any H-valued partition vertices 1 and 4 belong to the same part (tag bit 4), as do 2 and 5 (tag bit 8). These two pairs and vertex 3 must then occupy separate parts for the height span to be two. Each pair is a missing edge so both need the only remaining vertex 0 as connector. This is impossible. Every zero-total charge vector on a connected graph comes from edge gains, by solving the incidence equation on a spanning tree, so this is a physical cographic-private example. The triangle-plus-charged-leaves example is also rejected directly: each out-of-plane pendant bridge forces contraction, and after those contractions the proposed three parts cannot have the required sums.
