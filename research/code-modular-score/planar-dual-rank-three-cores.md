# Physical carriers near dual gain rank three

Use the exterior-transfer semantics and physical suppression in planar-physical-patch.md. All statements refer to entire supported binary cycle-image spaces.

**K4.** Suppose a dual block has gain image W of dimension three and a subdivision of K4 on which the cycle-gain map is injective. This carrier realizes all seven lines, since every nonzero cycle of K4 is simple (four triangles and three four-cycles). It realizes exactly six planes. A proper restriction of cycle rank two omits some edge and lies in its coordinate hyperplane; deleting that edge alone attains this hyperplane. The six coordinates are distinct nonzero covectors since K4 has neither bridges nor two-edge bonds. Both zero and W are attained. Thus only the seventh plane needs testing in the original block. If absent the K4 carrier is exact.

**Dipoles.** In two-vertex dipole subdivisions with internally disjoint paths and gain set A, the cycle-image of each selected edge set equals the span of differences of gains of its complete paths. Incomplete paths form only hanging or isolated trees. With W the full difference span, any S≤W is attainable precisely when some coset of S is affinely spanned by its intersection with A (zero is always attainable). If |A|>2^(dim(W)−1), some coset of each S contains more than half its points, hence affinely spans that coset. This gives a physical universal carrier of cycle rank at most 2^dim(W)−1 after discarding duplicates. For dimension three five paths of distinct gain suffice. Search for the bounded labelled subdivision by fixed-group disjoint paths. Every cycle can pass through the same two vertices, so disjoint cycles for all gains are unnecessary.

**Two-terminal line-valued networks.** Let a biconnected dual block meet the rest of the dual graph at just two distinct vertices u,v, with local cycle-gain image a line L, even if the global image is larger. The effect of any internal edge choice is determined by its full cycle-gain image W and, if u,v are connected internally, the gain of one internal u–v path modulo W. Indeed any external simple cycle crossing the interface uses one u–v path on each side. All other cycles are generated within the separate shores. W includes cycle gains in components away from u,v; reducing a path gain modulo this whole W is valid when forming the total span.

Choose a nonzero-gain cycle C omitting u or v whenever one exists, otherwise any nonzero cycle. By 2-connectivity there are disjoint arms from u,v to distinct points of C, truncated on first contact. An arm may have length zero; for a parallel-pair cycle the two endpoints serve as distinct contacts. The carrier formed by C and the two arms realizes W=0 with u,v disconnected (empty), W=0 with u,v connected with either possible gain (choose either arc and both arms), and W=L with u,v connected (all edges). All u–v gains in the full block lie in a fixed coset of L. The last potential state, W=L with u,v disconnected, exists precisely when a nonzero simple cycle omits u or v; C alone realizes it in that case. A balanced block instead reduces to one u–v path of its fixed gain. Keep the two external vertices and the arc contacts separate when suppressing degree-two vertices. Normalize switching on the two fixed external vertices to zero: reduce each chain to one edge with its total gain by internal switches. Thus a physical bounded substitute suffices in an arbitrary external dual skeleton, including one of gain rank at least three. No simultaneous multiport conclusion is required.
