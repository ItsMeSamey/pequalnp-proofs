# An outer-face obstruction without a nontrivial three-edge cut

There are fully charged cubic 3-connected planar hosts of unbounded treewidth, with no nontrivial three-edge cut, in which four linearly independent charge types each appear a positive fraction of the time but an algebraically attainable named plane is infeasible.

Start with the plane triangulation which is the bipyramid over an N-cycle, N≥8. Delete a rim vertex; its four neighbors bound an induced quadrilateral hole. As a second triangulation use the octahedral sphere with each face subdivided as a regular triangular mesh of side t≥4. Remove one original octahedron corner of degree four. Its four neighbors again induce precisely the boundary of a quadrilateral hole. Glue the two triangulated disks along the holes, identifying boundary cycles, retaining the simple graph structure. A triangle is contained in a single side because there are no edges between the two interiors and no diagonals on the common quadrilateral. Every triangle in the bipyramid was a face. Every 3-clique in the refined octahedron is likewise a face: represent its vertices by integer triples of absolute-coordinate sum t, with an edge changing two coordinates by one and staying in a common orthant. Three pairwise adjacent vertices have no positive and negative entries in the same coordinate across the triple, so lie on one original face. Inside its triangular grid, triangles are elementary triangles. Consequently all triangles of the glued sphere triangulation are facial. Its simple plane dual G is cubic and 3-connected. Every three-edge cut is a bond since G is 3-edge-connected; by plane duality it corresponds to a triangle, hence to the three edges incident with one dual vertex. In particular G has no nontrivial three-edge cut.

The dual face for an original bipyramid pole has length N+O(1): after gluing, only the deleted rim vertex and the bounded degrees on the second boundary matter. In a retained octahedron face unaffected by deleting the corner, group pairs of elementary triangular faces into unit rhombi in a parallelogram grid of side floor(t/3). Contract the dual edge across each rhombus diagonal. Adjacent rhombi share an edge, so this gives a square grid minor of that side length. Choose N comparable to t², making the giant face length and the rest of the graph both linear in the size of G.

Label three disjoint consecutive intervals of the giant facial cycle, of even length 2 floor(N/8) each, by e1,e2,e3, respectively. All other vertices receive e4. The first three multiplicities are even and the total number of vertices is even since G is cubic, hence all four multiplicities are even. Each is Θ(|V(G)|). Let u=e1+e2+e3 and v=u+e4. Disjoint vertex subsets achieve these sums by taking distinct representatives in each interval and one additional e4 vertex for v. In a connected partition with sums u,v,e4, however, each of the first two parts must meet all three intervals. Select one point of each part per interval; around the face there are at least three color changes within the three pairs, hence at least four in the cyclic sequence. Four points therefore alternate between the two disjoint connected parts. Paths joining the two same-color pairs cannot be disjoint in the closed disk complementary to the open face. Contradiction.

This counterexample excludes a full correcting switch guarantee even once all nontrivial cuts of size three have been split. It concerns transfer-certificate existence, not difficulty of deciding planar instances, for which a separate dual-routing algorithm applies.
