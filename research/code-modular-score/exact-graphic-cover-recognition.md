# Exact recognition of graphical cycle covers of a graph cutspace

For a supplied connected ordinary graph G define δ to be the minimum of dim Z(H)−dim cut(G) over graphs H on the identified edge set with cut(G)⊆Z(H), all spaces binary. Then δ is the least cost g+j of a closed connected surface of Euler genus g with finite sets of distinct points identified in classes of sizes m_i≥2 (j=Σ(m_i−1)), admitting a topological embedding of G. The edge-free case has cost zero. Loops and parallel edges are permitted.

Forward direction. The algebraic dual construction of Abrams–Slilaty, as reviewed in graphic-algebraic-dual-genus-attributed.md, produces c normalized surface components of total Euler genus g, s excess physical vertices, and u excess dual vertices, with δ=g+(s−c+1)+(u−c+1). Both parenthesized quantities are nonnegative. Realize c−1 intersurface vertex amalgamations using connected sums, with total Euler genus g. The resulting connected surface has a graph G′ recovering G upon the remaining j=s−c+1 vertex identifications. Instead of installing handles, identify precisely these points of the surface. The graph then embeds topologically in the quotient: no edge interiors are identified and exactly the desired graph vertices coincide. This proves cost≤δ.

Reverse direction. Subdivide any G edge meeting an occupied singularity in its interior. Detach the singular classes, keeping one split vertex at each occupied preimage with incident germs; a vertex with all germs on one sheet need not split. This yields an embedded graph G′ in the closed underlying surface. Recover the subdivided G_s from G′ by at most j vertex identifications on the same edge set, so cut(G_s)⊆cut(G′) and the dimension difference is at most j. The latter follows directly by keeping only vertex potentials equal across each identified pair. Take disjoint ribbon neighborhoods of the edge-bearing components of G′ and cap their boundary circles, giving a cellular embedding for each. Their Euler genera sum to at most g: decompose the original closed surface along these circles; total Euler genus is the sum of the capped genera on both sides plus twice the cycle rank of the connected adjacency multigraph of the pieces. This identity follows by additivity of Euler characteristic and applies to nonorientable pieces. The disjoint union of the geometric duals therefore gives a cycle supercode of cut(G′) of defect at most g. This covers cut(G_s) with defect at most g+j. For each original edge path contract all but one segment in G_s. This shortens the cutspace on the contracted coordinates (set to zero then puncture), and the same shortening on the supercode gives the cycle space of its graph after edge deletions. Common shortening does not increase codimension. Retain the prescribed edge identities on the surviving segments. This constructs a graphical cover for G, proving the equality.

For fixed d enumerate surfaces of Euler genus g≤d and all unordered multisets of class sizes with total excess j≤d−g, including the empty multiset. Each valid type has an effective finite triangulation depending only on d: triangulate its surface with marked points in pairwise disjoint closed vertex stars, then perform the indicated identifications. Such a complex has no 3-book and no isolated vertex. Every pair of finite point configurations of the same type on a connected closed surface differs by a homeomorphism. Test embeddability by Colin de Verdière–Magnard, Theorem 1.1. Their Theorem 9.1 also returns a combinatorial map of an augmented subdivided graph on the detached surface and the identification data. Delete the auxiliary edges and vertices, retain subdivisions of the original G, then implement the reverse direction above to output the physical cover. Finally verify graph-cycle inclusion and codimension by elimination. The finite testing cost is f(d) times a polynomial in the input length; in particular extracting the cover need not preserve the embedding tester's quadratic bound after explicit matrix checks. For a disconnected graph δ is the sum of the minima of its components: shortening any supercode to each edge block gives disjoint graphic supercodes of these component cutspaces, supplying the lower bound, and disjoint union supplies the upper. Isolated vertices do not affect δ. This is an algorithm on ordinary graph inputs, not a general recognition theorem for graphical supercodes of arbitrary binary subspaces.

Primary references: Abrams and Slilaty, Construction 3.1 and Propositions 3.2 and 4.2, https://doi.org/10.1142/S0218216506004683 ; Colin de Verdière and Magnard, Theorems 1.1 and 9.1, https://doi.org/10.1137/24M1712400 and https://arxiv.org/html/2107.06236 .
