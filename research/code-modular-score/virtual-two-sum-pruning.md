These are the proper two-terminal network conditions; they permit a physical spanning two-branch model retaining the distinct poles and an edge between them. Indeed the side is connected under these assumptions. Take a spanning tree and sever one edge of its pole-to-pole path, contract each side to its own pole and retain the severed edge. Only physical edges owned by the side may be used. Fix one such model per child when processing its parent. The resulting charge transfers to the poles can be arbitrary.

Freeze everything outside the child interiors and side-owned edges, in particular all vertices of X. For any other child, default to its fixed two-branch model. Assign each branch to its pole's part, transferring its private charges to that part, and in the witness select the new edge exactly when both poles belong to the same part. This keeps the exterior and any kept private pieces of every original part connected: each lost excursion across a defaulted side had both ends in the same part at its poles, so the edge replaces it. Any single-pole excursion may be omitted. Any surviving original part fragment in a kept side attaches to its old part or was wholly private and marked. No distinct parts are joined. A child with no private vertices and just physical pole edges may likewise be defaulted.
