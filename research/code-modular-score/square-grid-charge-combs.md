# A charge dichotomy on arbitrary square grids

On an n by m grid give every vertex an arbitrary h-bit binary charge, h≥1, totaling zero. At most one exterior port is fixed at the upper-left corner. The algorithm produces a connected physical minor on ≤h²+h+1 vertices realizing all subspaces of binary h-space, or displays at most 3h²+2 exceptional rows such that the remaining rows form O(h²) consecutive bands. Each band's vertex labels outside four side columns lie in an explicit proper subspace. Both outcomes may be available on the same input.

Scan rows 2 through n−1 and columns 3 through m−2. Select a cell when its row contains some label outside the current basis span, add that independent label, and skip the next two rows. Reset the basis after every h selections in a strip, always keeping the two-row buffer even after completing a strip. Stop upon obtaining h completed strips. On failure fewer than h² pivots occurred. Remove their rows and their two succeeding rows, and the extreme rows. Each remaining interval was scanned with a fixed proper span and all its considered cells lay in that span. This includes small grids with no candidate cells. Elimination takes O(nmh²) bit operations.

On success the spine of each strip follows column 2 from one row above its first pivot to one above its last pivot. For every pivot (r,c) use a horizontal tooth on row r−1 from column 2 up to c, keeping (r,c) separate. The h connected combs and h² singleton toggles are disjoint. Their complement is connected: column 1 and the right corridor meet via the free bottom row. On a tooth row a surviving cell is already in column 1 or to the right of the tooth; on ordinary rows only the spine can obstruct travel to the right. On a pivot row a surviving cell between the spine and the pivot can step down one row and head right, since that row has no tooth or pivot. A surviving column-2 cell to the left of a pivot occurs only after the spine ends and can use the same downward step. Each pivot is adjacent above to its comb and at the right to the background; each comb is adjacent to column 1.

Contract each comb into one vertex and the background into one vertex, summing charges and retaining the corner port on the background. To realize a subspace of dimension j, give j combs sums forming a basis. Their respective h independent singleton toggles permit arbitrary choices, starting from each actual comb charge. Every unassigned toggle, unused comb, and the background form one connected final part whose sum is the sum of the basis because total charge is zero. Thus the same physical minor realizes all subspaces and preserves the full original table.

Running the scan in the other orientation gives an optional stronger failure record. If both scans fail, remove their exceptional rows and columns plus four side margins from the opposite scans. The remaining bounded rectangular arrangement has charges in each cell belonging to the intersection of its two certified proper spans. The spans may vary by cell, and the interfaces can be arbitrarily long. This is not a universal bounded-minor result.
