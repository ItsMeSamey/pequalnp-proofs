# A fourteen-plane promise for exact four-root traces

Use the hypotheses, 160 polynomial witness-returning tests and plane coordinates P of port-four-combined-filters.md. If the total number of attainable planes is at most fourteen, those same tests decide exact membership deterministically and return a witness. An arbitrary input still gives unconditional positive certificates, and a failed target-necessary test provides a negative certificate.

An apartment is the set of six pair-spans of a basis for the four-dimensional named row space. Every attainable plane P belongs to an apartment entirely contained in the attainable spectrum. Extend a rank-(r−2) independent ordinary witness for P to an ordinary basis. Express the four named columns in its coordinates. The two rows indexed by the added columns form a basis of P, since the corresponding deletion from the ordinary basis gives exactly P. The coordinate matrix has rank four, so extend these two rows to a four-row basis using rows of this matrix. Omitting any pair of its four basis indices from the full ordinary basis attains the indicated pair-span. This proof applies also when the original witness requires completion to rank r−2, by the normalization in port-four-common-basis.md.

To verify the finite implication fix the target U. Assume that all 160 tests returned rivals although U is attainable. The actual spectrum contains an apartment through U, and the stabilizer of U acts transitively on these apartments by isomorphism of ordered bases. Fix one such apartment, with five rivals preinserted. Search all supersets whose rivals hit every test. When a test remains unhit, branch on the first chosen available plane seeing that test, excluding earlier candidates in subsequent branches. When every test is hit but apartment coverage is incomplete, continue branching over arbitrary additions up to the size limit. At a hit accept only if every selected plane, including U, is covered by an apartment in the family. The sum of the largest remaining individual coverage counts gives a safe pruning bound. The accompanying exhaustive checker finds no such family with at most thirteen rivals. It finds an abstract family with fourteen rivals. Realizability of that particular abstract family is undetermined.

Without enforcing apartment coverage, all seventy minimum ten-rival blockers fall in five target-stabilizer orbits of sizes 9,18,16,9,18 in the representative order printed by the checker. A second necessary condition is chart connectivity. Fix any two-dimensional chart complement L. All ordinary bases in the corresponding two-column quotient form a connected graph under basis exchange. Two adjacent bases share r−3 independent vectors. Their annihilators within the full ordinary dual are two-dimensional subspaces of the same three-dimensional space, and intersect nontrivially. Restriction to the named vectors is injective on each annihilator in this chart, so the two projected planes coincide or meet in a point. Consequently every nonempty chart subset of the spectrum is connected under plane adjacency. Four of the five orbits violate chart connectivity when U is added. The remaining size-nine orbit has closure with U consisting of all planes contained in a hyperplane H or passing through a point x of H. It contains no apartment: choose a basis point outside H, then its three pair-spans with the other basis points cannot all contain x.

The fourteen-plane bound uses apartment coverage alone. It is a promise on the input spectrum. It does not assert a general procedure for recognizing spectrum size, or hardness at a larger size.
