# Full score image from one family with robust probes

Let A be a nontrivial finite abelian group of odd order. For p dividing |A| use the space A/pA. There are computable thresholds L,M depending only on A with the following property. In a graph with unsigned A edge scores, take L mutually edge-disjoint simple cycles C_i. For each i and each nonzero scalar χ of any A/pA, assume there are M edge-disjoint probe cycles, each meeting C_i at exactly one edge of nonzero χ-weight. The graph's even supports then attain every value in A. Probes for different (i,χ) need not be disjoint. More generally it suffices that for every even baseline y, the χ weights restricted to C_i and signed by y have M edge-disjoint nonzero simple cycles. The single-edge hypothesis implies this robust form directly. The conclusion and proof persist after any baseline translation under the robust hypothesis, including arbitrary fixed edge sign changes in the single-edge version.

At an even baseline x each candidate cycle has signed increment g_i(x)=score(x XOR C_i)−score(x). A packet is an ordered list of such cycles, and its rank is the sum, over relevant primes, of the dimensions of the spans of its gains reduced in A/pA. Put R=Σ_p dim(A/pA). We show that sufficiently many packets of equal full gain tuple and rank k, at a common baseline y, give either saturation or arbitrarily many packets of higher rank at a new common baseline (all requested sizes depend only on A). Packets at a given stage have mutually disjoint constituent cycles and disjoint underlying edge sets. Their widths remain bounded, at most doubling each step.

To start, fix a nonzero χ. Each g_i can be nonzero: if its original gain vanishes, flip one probe to change χ(g_i) by twice a nonzero value. Every realized one-cycle increment occurs on a uniform positive fraction of even baselines, bounded below solely in terms of A by the graphic code fibre-density theorem in minimal-regular.md. Indeed the vector of signed increments on any bounded list is a fixed affine translate of a finite-group unsigned score on that same graphic code, with components restricted to individual cycle supports. Averaging, then pigeonholing full gain, supplies arbitrarily many identical rank-positive packets, or repeat the preceding argument in the explicit χ projection to guarantee rank-positive modulo p when the original gain is nonzero but χ vanishes. More precisely, if χ(g_i(0))=0 the single probe (or one nonzero robust probe at zero) gives χ(g_i)≠0; otherwise zero itself does.

Call two packets bad partners if their concatenation ever has rank larger than k. If the bad-partner graph has a sufficiently large matching, each matched pair reaches a higher-rank full tuple with probability at least ε(A,width)>0 by the same joint fibre-density bound. At some common baseline sufficiently many disjoint matched pairs reach higher rank. Pigeonholing full tuples yields the next stage. Otherwise the endpoints of a maximum matching cover all bad pairs. Discard them. From a sufficiently large starting family retain four packets with no bad partners. Their full gain tuples at y are identical.

If k<R choose p and nonzero χ vanishing on the common reduced span. Select one constituent C_j of the first packet. Let η equal χ of the edge weights signed by y, restricted to C_j. Any even support z avoiding one of the other three packets leaves its full gains unchanged in passing from y to y XOR z. The gain of C_j changes by −2η(z) in the χ coordinate. If η(z) were nonzero, the rank of the union of the two packets would rise: the avoided packet alone supplies all original dimensions in every component, and C_j supplies a new dimension in the p factor. Thus η vanishes on all even supports avoiding each of these three packets. Apply packet-separators.md with distinguished C_j. The robust probe cycles project to M disjoint nonzero even supports on the contracted graph on C_j, each of which contains a nonzero simple cycle; choose M beyond the three-packet bound for the maximum width. This contradiction excludes the small-matching case when k<R.

If k=R, one packet's list of gains generates all of A: by the finite abelian subgroup criterion, a proper subgroup lies in an index-p maximal subgroup for some p. With at least exp(A) disjoint identical packets at that baseline, choose among the copies of each increment independently to reach every element. The thresholds are effective by backwards induction from this final multiplicity, using at most R successive strict rank increases, width at most 2^R, and the parameter-only joint density. This gives a sufficient saturation certificate. No algorithm to find such a candidate family in every high instance is claimed.
The robust hypothesis does not follow from large packings in every scalar projection. In the simple 3-connected graph K_(3,n), give a selected edge incident with each of many colored degree-three vertices the weight of one chosen generator, and all other edges weight zero. Use the same hub for all selected edges. Allocate arbitrarily many colored vertices of each generator type, pairing each with a distinct uncolored vertex. The four-cycle through a pair, the selected hub and a second hub has the specified generator gain. These four-cycles form disjoint edge packs with arbitrarily many representatives of every generator, so the full image is attained directly. But each simple cycle uses at most six edges; for any fixed candidate C, every nonzero restricted probe must consume one of those edges, whence it has at most six edge-disjoint probes.
