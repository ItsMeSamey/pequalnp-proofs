# Removing the component-count restriction on packet separators

Fix a physical edge set C of an undirected multigraph and an odd abelian group score η supported on C. Let N≥3 packets, pairwise edge-disjoint and disjoint from C, each be the union of at most s connected edge sets. Suppose every even support avoiding any single packet has η-score zero. Contract all components of G−C and retain the C edges as a multigraph. The number of pairwise edge-disjoint simple cycles of nonzero η-score in this multigraph is at most 2(Ns)²N(Ns+1)². Isolated vertices and loop ends are retained. Empty packets give the immediate zero case.

Call a contracted vertex marked if its original component contains any packet edge. There are at most Ns such vertices since each connected member lies in a single component. A nonzero simple cycle must meet some marked vertex: otherwise it is still even after deleting any packet and refining the components, hence lifts to an even support avoiding that packet. The lifting criterion is exact: any subset of C with even degree at each component of G−(C∪packet) can be completed by pairing its physical odd boundary inside each of those components.

Split each given nonzero simple cycle into arcs at its successive marked vertices. For a single marked vertex the arc is the entire cycle based at this vertex, and a loop has its two physical ends there. Choose one arc of nonzero unsigned score per cycle; these arcs are edge-disjoint. They fall into at most (Ns)² types by their ordered pair of marked endpoint components, choosing orientations arbitrarily. Their interiors visit only unmarked components.

Inside each marked component make ordinary nodes for the connected components after removing all packet edges; put a hub for each connected packet member, adjacent to all ordinary nodes it touches. Paths in this incidence graph model connectivity before and after deleting all hubs of any one packet. This remains valid if several members of one packet meet vertices (overlapping edges within a packet may first be merged into connected components). Choose a fixed ordinary anchor in each marked component. For each physical endpoint of each arc, choose an incidence path from its ordinary node to the anchor. Mark the packet of the first hub on that path, if any. At most two packets are marked for the arc. Select any unmarked packet k. After deleting k, an endpoint remains connected either to its anchor (if the path avoids k) or to the first hub of the path (which survives). Thus for fixed ordered endpoint components and a fixed selected packet, each endpoint has at most Ns+1 possible refined component classes.

If more than the claimed bound of arcs existed, three would agree in both endpoint components, selected packet and refined endpoint classes. Any pair of these arcs gives an even C-set after refinement: interior component vertices never split, and the matching refined endpoints cancel in binary incidence. This includes equal original endpoint components and the case of cycles based at a single marked vertex. The lifting criterion gives an even support avoiding k and therefore having score zero. Since η is supported on C and the chosen arcs have disjoint edges, the scores of every two arcs sum to zero. Three such nonzero scores are impossible in a group of odd order. This proves the claim.

No bounded-common-component promise is needed. The result is a separator lemma under exact scalar annihilation on packet avoidance, not itself an algorithm for higher-rank or coprime targets.
