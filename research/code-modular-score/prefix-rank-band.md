# High prefix rank produces a physical diagonal band

For a rectangular grid with vertex labels in F₂^h let P be its two-dimensional prefix array. Fix a character χ into F₂. If the binary matrix χP on an interior rectangle of cut positions has sufficiently large rank as a function of h and k, then there are k row cuts and k column cuts in that rectangle, in their original orders up to reflecting one axis, on which P has constant vector A above the diagonal, B below, and D on the diagonal, with χ(D+B)=1. Reflection only reorders the selected prefix entries in this assertion. Consecutive rectangular blocks between the selected cuts have charges equal to their four-corner XOR, independently of reflection. Thus physically contracting intervals into single rows and columns produces an interior (k−1)-square whose charge is A+B on the diagonal, D+A just above, D+B just below, and zero elsewhere. Edges of the ambient grid are retained outside the selected range or contracted into exterior strips. Apply inside a port-free rectangle if no identifications among ports are allowed; alternatively refine every exterior strip at port coordinates, requiring that extra cuts do not cross the selected range in the relevant axis.

Here is an elementary constructive bound. Choose N large enough that every N indices with two fixed permutations have a subsequence of the desired length monotone in both permutations after the finite color thinning below. Rank r gives at least r distinct scalar row words. Start with at least 2^(N+1) distinct row words. Choose a varying column, record two distinct rows at which it differs, then keep a largest class of rows agreeing on that column, deleting the recorded pair. Iteration produces N fresh columns c_i and row pairs x_i,y_i that agree on all earlier c_j and differ on c_i (if needed start with 2^(N+2) rows). Thin by single-index colors (P[x_i,c_i],P[y_i,c_i]) and pair colors (P[x_i,c_j],P[y_i,c_j],P[x_j,c_i],P[y_j,c_i]) for i<j, using finite Ramsey. On this subsequence, earlier-column scalar entries of later rows are a common constant independent of choice x or y. Select uniformly the row from each pair whose diagonal scalar differs from this constant. Two applications of monotone subsequence thinning order the retained row and column positions (independently reverse either sequence); renumber by increasing row order and reflect the column order if necessary. If the row order reverses the original indexing then A and B interchange, so the guaranteed scalar difference may be χ(D+A)=1. Start with 2k retained indices. When χ(D+B)=0, necessarily χ(A+B)=1. Keep the odd-indexed rows and the even-indexed columns in aligned order. The new diagonal and upper triangle both equal the old A, the new lower triangle equals the old B, and the desired difference is one. Otherwise keep k matched indices directly. The 2D difference identity proves the physical claim. All thresholds are computable; for fixed h,k the greedy thinning and a brute-force homogeneous-subset search are polynomial.

In particular a scalar projection with unbounded rank on port-free cut rectangles proves the existence of arbitrarily long physical bands with a repeated nonzero scalar entry and only three occupied diagonals, no matter how dense the input. This is a local minor certificate. It alone does not preserve the original global catalogue in the forward direction. Exterior strip sums and incidence may affect that catalogue.

There is also a direct positive decision consequence, with no assumption on the exterior. For k≥10 define U=span(A+B,A+D), of dimension at most two. Every subspace of U belongs to the ORIGINAL row catalogue of the connected, zero-total grid. The three band labels are exactly the two generators and their sum. For a line use a single physical interior rectangle from the appropriate diagonal as a connected component. For U of dimension two use one rectangle of each of two independent types, at widely separated indices. These rectangles have disjoint one-cell collars in the contracted grid, lying away from its boundary. The complement of their singleton images is connected: route around each missing vertex around its collar. The complementary part contributes exactly the sum of the selected charges. Lift these parts by the connected rectangular branch sets. This certifies all subspaces of U even if protected ports make contraction of exterior strips inconvenient: directly take the same interior rectangles as the exceptional parts of the original grid and use their connected complement. Port coordinates are then never identified.
