# Connected three-partitions with two heights and variable tag dimension

Let each vertex of a connected graph carry a charge in F₂² ⊕ F₂ᵐ. The target consists of three connected nonempty blocks whose tags are zero and whose heights are respectively 1, 2 and 3 in binary-mask notation. The dimension m is part of the input. This decision problem is NP-complete even for simple 3-connected bipartite graphs with exactly three nonzero height labels, total charge zero, and full charge rank m+2.

**Reduction.** Use the NP-completeness of deciding whether a 3-uniform hypergraph admits a two-coloring in which every triple is mixed. A primary source proves hardness even for linear 4-regular 3-uniform instances: https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03395 . Use inputs with at least three variables and at least one triple. Index the m triples by E and give the tag space one basis vector e_E per triple.

* Make three hub vertices r,b,c of heights 1,2,3. Both r and b have tag equal to the sum of all e_E; c has tag zero.
* Add a neutral vertex for every variable and join it to all three hubs. Four extra neutral vertices adjacent only to the hubs may be included. Use these extras in the uniform-affine statement below.
* For each E add two distinct check vertices with zero height and tag e_E, each adjacent exactly to the three variables in E.

All charges sum to zero, and the check charges span tag space, leaving both height directions available. In the bipartition the hubs and checks lie on one side; all variables and extras lie on the other. Deleting at most two vertices leaves a hub, a variable, and at least one remaining neighbor for every surviving check. Every surviving variable attaches to the hub, and every surviving hub to the variable, so the remainder is connected.

A mixed coloring gives connected red and blue hub blocks: put each variable at its color hub and one check of each triple in each color block. Each check has a same-color variable neighbor. The third hub forms the last block, and extras may go to any hub. Conversely, all three hubs must occupy distinct blocks to obtain height rank two, since their three heights are the only nonzero heights and sum to zero. In the block of r, the tag coordinate for E vanishes only if that block contains exactly one of the two E-checks. The same argument at b accounts for the other check. Each needs a variable neighbor in its own block. Thus every triple has both a red and a blue variable; variables in the third block may be colored arbitrarily. Verifying a partition is polynomial. This argument also covers the equivalent catalogue question allowing more blocks: rank two still separates the hubs, and both hub blocks still require their E-checks.

**One NO instance defeating every rank-six linear tag projection.** For the seven triples of the Fano plane, each pair of points lies in a unique triple. If all triples were mixed, each triple would contain one same-color point pair, giving seven such pairs. A bipartition of seven points has at least nine same-color pairs, a contradiction.

Let L be any linear map of the seven tag coordinates of rank at most six, keeping the two height coordinates and the zero-tag target. Choose a basis of its column space from the seven columns and take a line E whose column was not used. Color E red and its four-point complement blue. Every other line meets E in one point and is mixed. Put the checks for E both at r, and split every other pair between r and b. Relative to zero tag this produces the same defect L(e_E) in each of the two hub blocks. Express L(e_E) as a sum of the selected basis columns. For each used column F, move its blue check to red. This toggles L(e_F) in both blocks. Each moved check has a red neighbor, each retained blue check a blue neighbor, and every variable stays attached to its own hub. This realizes exactly the requested three heights with zero projected tags.

Seven coordinates suffice to keep this NO instance negative by the original reduction, so the rank threshold for a linear projection of its tags is exact. The argument also works with the redundant encoding using a separate equality coordinate for (r,first check) and (b,second check) per edge: select E outside all triples contributing basis columns; either check of any other mixed triple can be moved independently to the opposite color.

**Scope of affine recodings.** With four extra neutral hub-neighbor vertices installed, the same NO instance defeats every *uniform* affine tag rewrite t ↦ L(t)+u of linear rank at most six, including arbitrary translation u. First perform the linear construction without the extras. There are 24 original vertices, hence zero or two odd-sized blocks. Assign one extra to each odd-sized block and distribute the remaining extras in pairs to a hub. Each block is now even and connected, so the common translation contributes zero to every block tag. The argument leaves heights unchanged. Vertex-dependent offsets can change block sums independently of size, and no obstruction for such rewrites or for general gadget reductions follows here. In particular, NP-hardness for two tag coordinates remains unproved.
