# Finitely many surviving components at an arbitrary supplied separator

Let G be a finite connected graph with F₂^p vertex charges and a specified nonempty set S of h vertices (no separation-size threshold is needed). Use the catalogue of score subspaces of connected partitions. A single charge-summing spanning minor keeps all members of S distinct and leaves at most

  Σ over attained W of [h + (p+1) dim W]

original components of G−S uncontracted. Every other component is physically absorbed into just one of its neighboring S vertices. With at most three independent charge bits this upper bound is at most 16h+96. Polynomial-time construction uses one witness per attained entry; the three-bit binary three-elements method supplies them without an oracle. For larger p the construction is conditional on receiving the witness list. The bound counts remaining private components, with no limit on their individual sizes. It preserves the catalogue of this input; it does not assert agreement under changes to the exterior or protection of the individual numerical charges on S.

Choose for each component B of G−S one adjacent port s(B) in S. Its default physical reduction contracts an internal spanning tree and one edge to s(B), deleting all other incident edges including other port contacts. This adds the full charge of B to s(B) and joins no two ports. Only vertices of B and the designated port share a branch. The reverse lift of a default action puts all of B into the port's part.

Fix an attained space W of dimension r. A witnessing connected partition can be reduced to k≤r+1 parts without changing W: in the graph of touching parts, merge an adjacent pair whenever the part-score matrix keeps its rank. At a stall each edge-pair indicator lies in the row space of that matrix, and the edge indicators of a spanning tree are independent. Therefore k−1≤r. Each merge can be found by direct rank tests.

Mark any private component containing an entire part that avoids S. Such a part cannot intersect two private components; at most k components are marked. For each part touching S, build an auxiliary graph on its same-part ports. Add its ordinary port-port edges. Also link any two of its ports connected by a path whose internal vertices lie in the same private component and in this part. This auxiliary graph is connected by decomposing physical paths at port visits. Choose a spanning tree; mark one supporting component for each tree edge requiring one. Over all parts there are at most h−1 such marks. Together these marked components retain connectivity on all ports and preserve every part missing S. In any retained component, every connected piece of a port-touching part restricted to that component has an edge to a port of its own part: follow a path in the part from the piece to its first port.

Consider initially defaulting all unmarked components. For each, record a vector with one p-bit coordinate per part: the difference of its original charge contributions and the contributions from defaulting its whole private mass to the part of s(B). These differences sum to zero over the k part coordinates for each component, so their span has dimension at most p(k−1). The total of the difference vectors is the sum of some at most p(k−1) members, by expanding it in a basis chosen from the list. Mark these components as well, and keep their original assignments. All part scores now agree with the original ones. Port tree paths lie entirely in kept components and the port set; all pieces of additionally kept port-touching parts attach to such ports. Port-free parts remain intact. Thus there is a physical witness in the reduced graph.

Union marks over all attained W. Any additional component retained only for a different witness can realize this witness's default by attaching its full connected interior to its designated port. Every witnessed score space survives; lifting connected partitions across physical contractions and edge deletions excludes any new entry. The result is connected since at least one witness can be chosen to include a single spanning part, or, independently, one may ensure a global spanning tree on S is retained by adding at most h−1 marks. More precisely the single spanning part witnesses the score generated by the total vertex charge and is unchanged by the initial partition reduction; use that witness for its entry, so no extra marks are necessary. Summing h−1+k+p(k−1) gives the stated bound. For p=3 the 16 subspaces have dimension sum 24.

For independent rank below three, use the charge span as ambient space. Equivalently pad to a three-dimensional coefficient space and use three distinct independent terminal coordinates when the projected names are independent, with the smaller-dimensional case treated in its own span. In rank two a line trace on a basis (a,b) with terminal sum equal to the requested generator is a circuit through both names, since a two-element circuit traces a pair of equal nonloops. The zero/full traces come from empty/full ordinary contractions when feasible. For rank three, apply the primal or dual three-named circuit test described in rank-three-three-minor-list.md. The method requires no high connectivity.
