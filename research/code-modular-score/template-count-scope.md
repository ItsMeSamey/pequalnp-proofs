# Which forbidden-minor template counts can be bounded?

Let K(a,b) be the class of represented binary codes with a regular reference within the two given directional budgets. This is minor closed: apply the same physical contraction/puncturing and deletion/shortening to input and reference; neither quotient distance increases, and regularity survives.

## A computable bound for negative certificates

Fix w. There is a computable m(a,b,w) such that every NO code possessing a NO physical minor of branch-width at most w has a negative certificate of at most 3^m ordered-survivor templates (canonical order and all Fano relabelings as in two-sided-templates.md). This is a bound on the existence of a certificate; the carrier width w can be much smaller than the host width.

In that minor take a minor-minimal nonmember M. Its width stays at most w. All minor-minimal nonmembers are pairwise incomparable up to isomorphism. Bounded-width GF₂-representable matroids are well-quasi-ordered by minors by the established bounded-width theorem of [Geelen, Gerards and Whittle](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~jfgeelen/Publications/bw_wqo.pdf), with their [correction](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~jfgeelen/Publications/bn-corr.pdf). Thus there are finitely many such M of the prescribed width. This does not assume unrestricted binary matroid well-quasi-ordering.

Compute a bound on their sizes as follows. Feasibility for (a,b) is described by the counting-MSO formula in near-regular-recognition.md. Single-element deletion and contraction can be interpreted in matroid logic (contraction independence is tested by adjoining a basis of the contracted set). Consequently “nonfeasible and every proper one-element deletion/contraction feasible” is another effective formula. For increasing s test if some width-w represented matroid satisfies this formula and has more than s elements; cardinality greater than a specified s is expressible with s+1 distinct element variables. The fixed-field bounded-width parse-tree theory is decidable [Hliněný and Seese](https://www.fi.muni.cz/~hlineny/papers/matr-mso-decid3.pdf); the needed modulo-two leaf counts are finite automaton states under the same translation, as in [Strozecki](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2011.02.005). Emptiness is a finite tree automaton test. Finiteness makes the loop halt; it need not produce a useful numerical bound.

For a NO minor on at most m elements, prepend its physical minor recipe to every local choice of seven survivors, contractions and deletions. There are at most 3^m such recipes. Every proposed reference candidate induces on this minor a reference within the same budgets. That induced code is necessarily nonregular, so at least one of these templates rejects the candidate. On a supplied width-w NO minor, repeatedly use bounded-width recognition to delete or contract elements as long as infeasibility is preserved. This obtains the small carrier constructively, hence the certificate, in f(a,b,w)poly(input) time. Adaptive extraction can then be restricted to that carrier. The argument does not locate such a carrier in an arbitrary unbounded-width host from an existence promise alone.

## No universal bound on arbitrary adaptive runs

Take a host F7 cycle code in direct sum with m regular seven-element graphic components. Each seven-element component B has reduced K5 columns e1,e2,e3,e4 and e1+e2,e1+e3,e1+e4. At budgets (0,2), quotient the first F7 representation by a nonzero direction u (regular rank two) and, in component i only, quotient B by 1111. Its seven distinct columns modulo 1111 are all nonzero points of F₂³, making the trial reference R_i simultaneously regular in every other component and Fano in i. The u-only alternative is a valid regular reference. All R_i satisfy any initial forbidden-minor template witnessing nonregularity of the original host: such a template's seven survivors must lie in the original F7 component. For R_i, since F7 and F7* are connected, every rejecting template must have all seven survivors in component i. Any such template passes every R_j with j different from i, regardless of other deleted/contracted elements. Following R_1,...,R_m therefore forces m failures independently of how the separation oracle extracts its obstruction. Host width and budgets stay bounded.

This also gives a lower bound of m on the number of templates needed to classify regularity everywhere in this host's candidate space, even though its decision answer is YES. It does not prove that a cleverly chosen decision search takes m rounds, nor that minimum negative certificate lengths are unbounded at these budgets. A simpler regular-host example works at (0,1) if an algorithm is not required to precheck the host before proposing an unnecessary perturbation.
