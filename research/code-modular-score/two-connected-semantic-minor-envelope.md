# A bounded 2-connected intermediate spanning minor

Let G be a finite simple 2-connected graph. A supplied spanning minor model of a connected simple graph H partitions V(G) into h nonempty connected bags. Every required H edge has a physical edge between its bags. Quotient edges in excess of H may be deleted. Assume h≥3. There is a polynomial-time construction of a simple 2-connected graph K intermediate in this same spanning minor relation, with

    |V(K)| ≤ h + (h−2)(2h−5).

For h=3 the bound is four. The construction keeps all bag names and treats every retained vertex as a connected branch of the original graph. Any abelian vertex charges are summed on contractions, so the original H model and its bag sums remain available in K. No rank or charge assumption is needed for the order bound.

## Reduction inside bags

While the graph has more than three vertices, call an internal edge uv good when removal of both ends leaves a connected graph. Contract any good internal edge, simplifying loops and parallel edges. The quotient is 2-connected: removing the merged vertex leaves the specified connected complement, and removing any other vertex gives a quotient of a connected graph. All bag connections and required interbag contacts survive. A bad internal edge may be deleted whenever it is not a bridge of its bag. Each component after deleting u,v in the current graph attaches to both u and v, by 2-connectivity. At least two such components exist. They give internally disjoint alternative u–v paths, so deleting the bad edge preserves 2-connectivity even after subsequently deleting any one vertex. Its deletion also preserves bag connectivity and all interbag contacts. Stop when neither reduction applies. At three vertices all bags are singletons. Each operation reduces the sum of vertex and edge counts; connectivity tests and their repeated application take polynomial time.

Otherwise every surviving bag B is an induced tree: each internal edge is bad, and each is a bridge of its bag. Let c be the number of components of the exterior K−B. Here K denotes the reduced graph. For any internal edge uv every component of K−{u,v} contains an exterior vertex. Indeed an entirely internal component would yield an alternative u–v path inside B, contradicting that uv is an internal bridge. Each original exterior component lies intact in a component of K−{u,v}. Thus every internal edge separates at least two exterior components upon removal of its endpoints; when c=1 the bag is a singleton.

For c≥2 fix one exterior component S. Every internal edge separates S from at least one of the other c−1 components T. Fix T and contract the exterior components to routers for this counting argument only. Two S–T paths exist that share no vertex inside B. To see this, give each B vertex capacity one and each router and each adjacency capacity two. No size-one cut is possible because K stays connected after deleting any one B vertex. Integral flow yields the two paths, each simple after removing repeated-router detours. Shared routers are permitted. Distinct routers are nonadjacent, and each path visits at most c routers. Its intersections with B are therefore at most c−1 disjoint nonempty connected subpaths. The two collections of subpaths are mutually disjoint inside B.

If removing u,v separates S,T in K then it does so in the router graph as well: any router traversal avoiding u,v lifts through an exterior component unaffected by their deletion. Hence each path hits {u,v}; their distinct B capacities force different endpoints, so uv joins two of the selected subpaths. In the tree B contract all these disjoint subpaths. Edges joining their contracted vertices form a forest with at most 2(c−1)−1 edges. Thus at most 2c−3 internal edges separate this fixed pair. Over all T there are at most (c−1)(2c−3) internal edges in B.

Choose a spanning tree of H. For each B its exterior has c≤deg_tree(B) components since the other connected bags are joined along every remaining tree edge. Consequently the sum of c−1 over bags is at most h−2, and always c≤h−1. Summing the internal-edge bounds and adding one vertex per bag proves the claimed order. Only the earlier reductions are actual graph operations; the routers and paths serve to count the remaining vertices.

## Consequence for rank-three catalogue distortion

Assume now G has binary vertex charges of total zero and span three. Its catalogue consists of all subspaces of F₂³ generated by the part sums of connected vertex partitions. If H is a semantic spanning minor (its catalogue equals that of G), every intermediate K constructed above also has this catalogue, by lifting connected partitions. The full three-dimensional entry and total zero give h≥4. No named individual pins are imposed here.

Use the four-core cover result for 2-connected graphs in this setting: at most three four-vertex 2-connected spanning minor models on K cover all its catalogue entries. For clarity, each four-core is a square, diamond or complete graph with simplex charges. If one point is missing, all cores miss that same point and cover all entries except possibly planes in its three-plane pencil; use one specified-plane core per attained pencil plane, or any core when none is attained. If every point occurs, take a core and, when needed, a second covering its missing point. Their missing plane sets intersect in at most one plane (the distinct missing-point pencils meet in just one plane; a complete core itself omits just one plane). One more specified-plane core suffices. This invokes the separate specified-point and specified-plane four-core theorems.

For such a covering list in any host, fix one physical contact for each required interbag edge. Write A for their union. For a host spanning tree T define D(T) as the sum of cc(T[B])−1 over every bag B of every model, plus |A outside T|. The global parameter D_min minimizes this quantity over all covering lists of at most three models, their contact choices and spanning trees. Lift the chosen K models to G along the physical K branches. Lift a spanning tree of K to a tree of G using one fixed edge per tree adjacency and a spanning tree inside every branch; choose consistent lifts for all contacts. Internal deficits are unchanged because each enlarged branch is a connected subtree inside every bag containing it. Each four-bag model on K contributes at most |V(K)|−4 in internal deficits, and needs at most six contacts. Hence

    D_min(G) ≤ 3(|V(K)|+2) ≤ 3[h+(h−2)(2h−5)+2].

Conversely, from a covering list, its contacts and a tree of distortion D, add enough actual edges to connect the tree components inside each model bag and keep all its contacts. This adds at most D edges to the tree. The resulting physical spanning subgraph preserves the entire catalogue by witness inclusion and monotonicity. Its treewidth is at most D+1 (add one chosen endpoint of each extra edge to all bags of a width-one decomposition of the tree). The previously established bounded-treewidth conservative catalogue theorem thus gives a representative of computably bounded order in D. This is an equivalence of bounded parameters with freedom to choose new covering models. It supplies neither an algorithm for finding an optimum covering list nor an unconditional bound on D_min for all inputs.
