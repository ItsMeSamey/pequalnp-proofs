# Recognizing full-rank charge switches between three connected cores

Use three ordered colors and four-bit target sums totaling the sum of all vertices. Choose a subspace S of dimension d, and let R be all vertices with label outside S. Assume |R|≤p for the chosen constant p. For a fixed assignment of R to colors whose sums agree with the targets modulo S, the following physical certificate gives deterministic polynomial decision of certificate existence and construction of a partition. It generalizes the private bases of highly-connected-cographic.md to arbitrary pairs of recipient colors. No graph connectivity or expansion hypothesis is needed for certification.

Choose at most 2d distinct actual vertices T outside R. For each t choose a pair of different colors i,j. Its transfer vector is its label in the ordered coordinates for colors i and j, omitting the third color coordinate. Require the transfer vectors to span S². In G−T seek three vertex-disjoint nonempty connected sets H1,H2,H3 containing their assigned R vertices. Every t must neighbor each of its two designated sets. Every connected component of G−T must contain some H. This test allows any distribution of transfer edges over the three color pairs, not only transfers to and from a single last color.

Enumerate the physical toggles, endpoint pairs and actual neighbor contacts, and an anchor per color. Reject incompatible terminal requirements or residual components with no anchor. For G−T test three disjoint connected sets containing their respective bounded lists of terminals (assigned R vertices, neighbor contacts, anchors). This is a fixed-size rooted-minor query for an edgeless three-vertex pattern with specified terminal lists. Repeated terminal sites of one color may coincide. Return physical sets by the constructive theorem or by deleting edges while feasibility persists: the trees of a minimal surviving model then use all surviving edges, so nontrivial components and required isolated vertices give the sets. Use the original G−T for component tests and subsequent growth. For fixed d,p all vertex guesses and rooted-minor queries take polynomial time.

Grow the three sets in G−T by frontier expansion to cover all its vertices. Each toggle may now be assigned independently to either designated color and remains attached to its core in both choices. Start with any baseline such choice. All per-color deficits belong to S because all outside-S labels have fixed owners. Solve over the binary field for changes giving the first two deficits; by the rank hypothesis this always works. Conservation supplies the third. All non-toggle charges, including unknown charges on internal routing vertices, have already been counted before solving the switch equations. Nonemptiness and connectivity are preserved.

A single compatible rare assignment with this certificate proves a positive. A target-independent sufficient input condition is that, for some S with |R|≤p, all assignments of R have certificates. This is recognizable by the same fixed-parameter enumeration (polynomial for fixed p,d). On that class the projected score compatibility test is exact and yields witnesses. Outside the class failure of a certificate cannot refute a partition. For d=0 one can instead directly decide sparse instances by testing each target-compatible assignment; that case precedes this note.

For an explicit transfer-rank example use three hub vertices with zero charge and eight singleton toggles, two of each basis type. For e1 and e4 use pairs 12,23; for e2 use 12,13; for e3 use 13,23. Each toggle is adjacent to both designated hubs. Include hub edges if desired. The two distinct transfer vectors per charge coordinate span both output-coordinate directions; the full rank is eight and the label total is zero. Yet none of the three color pairs carries a full basis on these designated switches. This is a strict generalization of the designated star-of-private-bases certificate, without asserting alternative cores could not yield other certificates.
