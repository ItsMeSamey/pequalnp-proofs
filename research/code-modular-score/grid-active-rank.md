# An odd-score graphic grid cannot hide all directions outside its named switches

Let N named row cycles be pairwise edge-disjoint, and likewise N named column cycles in an undirected graph. Arbitrary overlap between a row and a column is allowed. Suppose the total weight on their common edges is nonzero in a finite abelian group of odd order on every one of the N² cells.

Use the column vectors a_e of a generator matrix for the entire binary cycle space C as its physical evaluation forms. Aggregate unsigned physical weights at identical forms, ignoring inactive zero-weight types. Forms evaluate simultaneously on the named cycles, giving a linear restriction map to F₂^(2N), regardless of whether the named switches are independent. Every form occurring on a cell evaluates as e_i+f_j, since each named family is edge-disjoint. Conversely if it has this evaluation, every physical edge of that type belongs to that cell. Nonzero cell total yields at least one active form of each cell type. Let L be the span of the active forms, and t the dimension of the kernel of the named evaluation map restricted to L.

Choose a linear section of the map on its image. For each cell select an active representative and color the cell by the difference between it and the section of e_i+f_j. This is one of at most 2^t kernel vectors. A single color class cannot contain the complete bipartite graph K_(3,3). Indeed on its nine cells every dependence of the graphical column vectors e_i+f_j has even cardinality, as each edge covers one left vertex. Adding the common kernel discrepancy to all nine sections preserves every such dependence. Conversely any dependence among the actual columns projects to a relation among e_i+f_j. The resulting restriction of the cographic evaluation matroid would therefore be M(K_(3,3)), which is not cographic since K_(3,3) is nonplanar.

For completeness a K_(3,3)-free color has O(N^(5/3)) edges: sum over right vertices the number of triples of their left neighbors, which is at most twice binom(N,3), then use convexity of binom(d,3) or the cubic mean bound on the degrees d. Covering all N² cells gives 2^t≥c N^(1/3), hence t≥(log₂N)/3−O(1). The same proof applies to the cells with nonzero score in a given quotient, yielding the corresponding bound with that cell count in the numerator.

There is a score interpretation of the rank. For odd-order labels, the directions of universal zero translation of the score function on C are exactly the annihilator of L. Indeed a translation which flips some active parity changes its coefficient by minus twice its nonzero aggregate, and nonzero parity forms together with the constant are independent for additive coefficients in an odd-order group by Walsh orthogonality. Thus the bound certifies genuinely active freedom beyond the visible named switch coordinates. It does not prove joint surjectivity or find a target witness on an unrestricted graph.

## A necessary uniform-collinearity condition for a missing target

For N edge-disjoint even supports C_j let g_j(x) be their signed increments at a cycle baseline x, with values in F_p². If the full graph image misses any target, at any one baseline all but a number K(p) of the g_j(x) lie on one line. Indeed if two independent nonzero gains each occur at least p−1 times, disjoint switches would fill the entire plane. There are only p²−1 possible nonzero gains, so if no nonzero gain repeats that much, all but a bounded number are zero. Otherwise every repeated gain lies on the line of one repeated gain and the remaining nonzero gains account for at most (p²−1)(p−2) indices. Consequently at most K(p)N unordered index pairs are noncollinear at any baseline.

The proper-binary-class fiber density theorem of minimal-regular.md shows that whenever a pair j,k is noncollinear at some baseline, a constant ε(p)>0 fraction of all baselines realize the same two increments. Indeed the pair is an affine score in F_p⁴ of the physical bits, weighting edges of the two named supports and multiplying those weights by minus two. Double count incidences of such pairs with uniform baselines. At most K(p)N/ε(p) pairs can ever be noncollinear. A graph with this many edges has an independent set of size Ω_p(N), for example by the degree bound or Turán's theorem. On the corresponding subfamily every pair's increments are collinear simultaneously at every baseline, not merely on switches from the two named families.

If two members j,k have nonzero common initial gain b, write D_j,D_k for the spans of their own active evaluation forms, and T=D_j∩D_k. At baselines annihilating T, any assignment on D_j compatible with zero on T extends with value zero on all of D_k. Thus if the two increments are universally collinear, g_j(x) lies in span(b) whenever x annihilates T, and similarly for k. A single row flip moving g_j outside that line implies a nonzero restriction to T. Enlarging to the spans of all edge forms of the two disjoint supports gives the cographic rank identity
dim(intersection)=c(G−C_j−C_k)−c(G−C_j)−c(G−C_k)+c(G),
where c counts components including isolated vertices. This gives a jointly separating cut obstruction whenever the dimension is positive. It does not alone force all reachable targets.
