# Conservative physical catalogue representatives on bounded-treewidth distance-hereditary graphs

Fix treewidth w≥1, finite charge group Γ including protected ports, and binary named dimension. Connected DH graphs under this promise have computably bounded physical spanning minors with the identical full named-kernel catalogue, with no diameter or degree condition.

Use the ternary split tree and fixed catalogue CMSO automaton from accessible-split-run-pumping.md. Orient a leaf-to-leaf spine. Cut immediately after each blocked star. The only prefix vertices accessible at that cut are S_i, the nonempty active leaves of its hanging center subtree. These sets are pairwise disjoint; consecutive S_i,S_{i+1} are completely joined via the transmitting intervening spine. Order each set arbitrarily.

Take two cuts i<j of exact capacity |S_i|=|S_j|=k with every intermediate capacity ≥k. At every site keep the first k contacts as ordered anchors. For consecutive cuts h,h+1 let U_h be the physical leaves in the interval (including the center subtree of the second blocker). In the graph induced by U_h and the k incoming anchors, initialize k disjoint branches each with its incoming anchor and the matching outgoing anchor in S_{h+1}. These pairs are physical edges. Each component of G[U_h] touches the exterior by connectedness. If it touches the prefix, it touches every incoming anchor, by the complete join across the first cut. If it touches the suffix, it contains a member of S_{h+1}, adjacent to every incoming anchor. Thus grow the initial branches by repeatedly assigning an unassigned neighbor, exhausting U_h. Record charges newly assigned to each lane, excluding its incoming anchor. Choose the assignments deterministically and consistently for this spine and k, before selection of i,j.

Glue the assignments along the k named anchors per intermediate site. The result partitions the entire deleted interval and S_i into k connected branches. Anchors at intermediate cuts were charged once only. If the summed k-vector is zero, each surviving receiver keeps its charge. The spliced target graph consists of the surviving induced side graphs plus all edges between S_i and the active leaves of the final suffix. All those edges are witnessed by the ending anchors, which exhaust S_j. Extra quotient edges can be deleted. Equal CMSO prefix transformations at i and j give equal full catalogue (same retained incidence marks and continuation convention as in the transmitting-run lemma). One-hot charge coordinates preserve each port.

For each k≤w, work separately in each maximal consecutive run of capacities ≥k, computing k-vector prefix sums of these tile charges from the start. Tag each exact-k site with its sum and the finite prefix transformation. There are at most M_k=|Γ|^k times the number of possible transformations distinct tags. Repeated tags in a run give a certified rewrite. Two consecutive capacities >w are impossible: their complete join contains K_{w+1,w+1}, which has a clique minor of order w+2 (contract w disjoint matching edges and retain the two unused opposite vertices). In the absence of a rewrite, any interval wholly above w has length ≤1. Put L_{w+1}=1 and recursively L_k=M_k+(M_k+1)L_{k+1}. An interval with capacities ≥k has at most M_k exact-k sites; splitting there proves its length ≤L_k. Thus an irreducible spine has at most L_1 blocked turns.

Repeat either a transmitting-run rewrite or a k-lane rewrite until none exists. Each step strictly reduces physical vertices, preserves the catalogue and gives a minor, so treewidth never increases. Each spliced tree is again a valid ternary DH presentation. At termination, absence of transmitting runs reaching the fixed threshold C bounds the tree diameter by (L_1+1)C. Maximum degree three then gives a computable finite bound on graph vertices. All searches, active sets, assignments, records and composed physical witnesses can be recovered on the given or computed split presentation. No decomposition of width w is needed. The assertion is about full catalogues and makes no WQO claim.
