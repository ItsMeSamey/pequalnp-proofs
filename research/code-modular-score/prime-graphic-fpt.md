# Deterministic FPT for an unsigned odd-prime score on graphic cycles

Given edge labels in F_p for odd prime p, one can decide and recover an even edge set of prescribed unsigned score in f(p)poly(input). We use the integral Erdős–Pósa theorem for unsigned nonzero cycles in groups without order-two elements (Thomas and Yoo, recovering Wollan) and the rooted unsigned disjoint paths algorithm of Liu and Yoo. No computed integral transversal is needed.

## Constant-factor minimum-cost nonzero cycle

Give vertices nonnegative rational costs y, and edges lengths (y_u+y_v)/2 so simple-cycle lengths are their vertex costs (also for loops and parallel pairs). For each root choose a shortest-path tree of its component. Enumerate XORs of at most three distinct fundamental cycles, decompose each even result into simple cycles, and retain the cheapest nonzero simple cycle found. This works for scores in any abelian group.

Take an optimal nonzero simple cycle C and a root on it. Write C as the XOR of fundamental cycles of its non-tree edges. For each such edge uv, the two distances from the root to u,v are bounded by the lengths of the two pieces of C−uv partitioned at the root. Thus each of these fundamental cycles costs at most cost(C). If all XORs of at most three chosen cycles had zero score, induction in the parity-indicator inclusion-exclusion formula shows that for each subfamily J of size one, two or three, its intersection score times (−2)^(|J|−1) is zero. Intersections of at least two distinct fundamental cycles equal intersections of their tree paths (private chords are distinct). Every nonempty intersection of arbitrarily many paths is determined by at most three: fix one path, then choose two extremal intervals on it. Retain a second path whenever only the first was needed. Thus each higher-order intersection coefficient vanishes by multiplication from one of these smaller ones. The entire XOR would score zero, contradiction. At least one enumerated XOR is nonzero and decomposes into edge-disjoint simple cycles, including a nonzero one of cost ≤3 cost(C). The oracle also detects absence.

## Fractional cover or full spectrum

Let k=p−1. Use an effective threshold F≥1 such that absence of k vertex-disjoint nonzero cycles implies a transversal of size ≤F. Thomas–Yoo give the relevant unsigned integral Erdős–Pósa theorem via wall, Ramsey and linkage bounds. Subdividing to a simple graph preserves cycle labels; a hitting subdivision vertex can be replaced by an endpoint of the original edge, and vertex-disjoint cycles project to vertex-disjoint cycles.

Run volume-stopping rational ellipsoid feasibility on 0≤y_v≤3, sum y≤2F+1, and nonzero-cycle costs ≥1. Separate the explicit bounds first, then run the approximate oracle. If it returns a cycle of cost <1, use its actual violated linear inequality. Otherwise accept the point, certifying every nonzero cycle has cost ≥1/3. If a transversal of size ≤F exists, multiplying its indicator by 3/2 and adding 1/(4n) to every coordinate yields the center of an inverse-polynomial-radius ball satisfying all true inequalities. All cuts are valid, so volume stopping before any acceptance implies no such transversal exists. Then k vertex-disjoint nonzero cycles exist, and all of F_p occurs: adding any nonzero increment to the subset-sum choices grows any nonempty proper subset of the prime group. Return YES. For witness extraction use greedy edge deletion with the exact decision algorithm.

## Pairwise-independent short lists

If a y is accepted, each connected component of an inclusion-minimal attaining support has nonzero score, contains a nonzero simple cycle, and hence has y-mass ≥1/3. At most p−1 such components occur by the prefix-sum argument. Give vertex v ceil(n y_v) copies, total ≤n(2F+2). Pad to the next power of two q by copies of any vertex. If total is zero only the empty target is feasible. In the uniform field-element-to-copy distribution every required component has probability at least 1/O(F+1). Take L a sufficiently large constant times p(F+1). If q<L use all vertices having copies, a bounded list. Otherwise enumerate all lists of L field values αt+β on fixed distinct t in F_q as α,β range over the field. These samples are pairwise independent. For any fixed required component the number of hits has variance ≤its mean, which is ≥L/O(F+1); Chebyshev and a union bound show some list hits them all. There are O(n²(F+1)²) lists. This does not require any list to hit all graph cycles.

For each list introduce one private zero-weight loop at every listed vertex. A feasible even support hit in all components can append one loop per component as a fixed anchor. Expand each vertex into its zero-weight clique of half-edge ports; replace each edge by its unique wire of the same weight, subdivided into a length-three path for simplicity, its weight on one segment. An Euler tour on a component becomes a simple port cycle using each port once, and disconnected components lift disjointly. Conversely port cycles project to an even edge set of the same unsigned score after discarding auxiliary loops. Enumerate a subset of the bounded root list and one score per chosen root summing to the target. Delete the chosen anchor wires completely, retaining their pairs of endpoint ports as distinct path terminals. Invoke Liu–Yoo for vertex-disjoint paths with those terminal pairs and unsigned scores (forbid every other group element). Closing the paths along their removed private wires returns port cycles. All calls have parameter-bounded terminal lists and a constant polynomial exponent. The zero target is immediate.
