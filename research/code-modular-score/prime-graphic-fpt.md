# Deterministic FPT for an unsigned odd-prime score on graphic cycles

Given edge labels in F_p for odd prime p, one can decide and recover an even edge set of prescribed unsigned score in f(p)poly(input). We use the integral Erdős–Pósa theorem for unsigned nonzero cycles in groups without order-two elements (Thomas and Yoo, recovering Wollan) and the rooted unsigned disjoint paths algorithm of Liu and Yoo. No computed integral transversal is needed.

## Constant-factor minimum-cost nonzero cycle

Give vertices nonnegative rational costs y, and edges lengths (y_u+y_v)/2 so simple-cycle lengths are their vertex costs (also for loops and parallel pairs). For each root choose a shortest-path tree of its component. Enumerate XORs of at most two distinct fundamental cycles, decompose each even result into simple cycles, and retain the cheapest nonzero simple cycle found. This works for scores in any abelian group.

Take an optimal nonzero simple cycle C and a root on it. Write C as the XOR of fundamental cycles of its non-tree edges. For each such edge uv, the two distances from the root to u,v are bounded by the lengths of the two pieces of C−uv partitioned at the root. Thus each of these fundamental cycles costs at most cost(C). If all XORs of at most two chosen cycles had zero score, induction in the parity-indicator inclusion-exclusion formula shows that for each subfamily J of size one or two, its intersection score times (−2)^(|J|−1) is zero. Intersections of at least two distinct fundamental cycles equal intersections of their tree paths (private chords are distinct). For a common nonempty edge-intersection path H of at least two paths, record for each path its next edge beyond each endpoint of H, using a private dummy for each termination. Neither coordinate is constant by maximality of H. Some pair of records differs in both positions: if two differing in the first agree in the second, any record differing from their second must differ in its first from at least one of them. The two paths for the resulting pair have edge intersection exactly H. Thus each higher-order intersection coefficient vanishes by multiplication from one of these smaller ones. The entire XOR would score zero, contradiction. At least one enumerated XOR is nonzero and decomposes into edge-disjoint simple cycles, including a nonzero one of cost ≤2 cost(C). The oracle also detects absence.

## Fractional cover or full spectrum

Let k=p−1. Use an effective threshold F≥1 such that absence of k vertex-disjoint nonzero cycles implies a transversal of size ≤F. Thomas–Yoo give the relevant unsigned integral Erdős–Pósa theorem via wall, Ramsey and linkage bounds. Subdividing to a simple graph preserves cycle labels; a hitting subdivision vertex can be replaced by an endpoint of the original edge, and vertex-disjoint cycles project to vertex-disjoint cycles.

Run volume-stopping rational ellipsoid feasibility on 0≤y_v≤3, sum y≤2F+1, and nonzero-cycle costs ≥1. Separate the explicit bounds first, then run the approximate oracle. If it returns a cycle of cost <1, use its actual violated linear inequality. Otherwise accept the point, certifying every nonzero cycle has cost ≥1/2. If a transversal of size ≤F exists, multiplying its indicator by 3/2 and adding 1/(4n) to every coordinate yields the center of an inverse-polynomial-radius ball satisfying all true inequalities. All cuts are valid, so volume stopping before any acceptance implies no such transversal exists. Then k vertex-disjoint nonzero cycles exist, and all of F_p occurs: adding any nonzero increment to the subset-sum choices grows any nonempty proper subset of the prime group. Return YES. For witness extraction use greedy edge deletion with the exact decision algorithm.

## Pairwise-independent short lists

If a y is accepted, each connected component of an inclusion-minimal attaining support has nonzero score, contains a nonzero simple cycle, and hence has y-mass ≥1/2. At most p−1 such components occur by the prefix-sum argument. Give vertex v ceil(n y_v) copies, total ≤n(2F+2). Pad to the next power of two q by copies of any vertex. If total is zero only the empty target is feasible. In the uniform field-element-to-copy distribution every required component has probability at least 1/O(F+1). Take L a sufficiently large constant times p(F+1). If q<L use all vertices having copies, a bounded list. Otherwise enumerate all lists of L field values αt+β on fixed distinct t in F_q as α,β range over the field. These samples are pairwise independent. For any fixed required component the number of hits has variance ≤its mean, which is ≥L/O(F+1); Chebyshev and a union bound show some list hits them all. There are O(n²(F+1)²) lists. This does not require any list to hit all graph cycles.

For each list introduce one private zero-weight loop at every listed vertex. A feasible even support hit in all components can append one loop per component as a fixed anchor. Expand each vertex into its zero-weight clique of half-edge ports; replace each edge by its unique wire of the same weight, subdivided into a length-three path for simplicity, its weight on one segment. An Euler tour on a component becomes a simple port cycle using each port once, and disconnected components lift disjointly. Conversely port cycles project to an even edge set of the same unsigned score after discarding auxiliary loops. Enumerate a subset of the bounded root list and one score per chosen root summing to the target. Delete the chosen anchor wires completely, retaining their pairs of endpoint ports as distinct path terminals. Invoke Liu–Yoo for vertex-disjoint paths with those terminal pairs and unsigned scores (forbid every other group element). Closing the paths along their removed private wires returns port cycles. All calls have parameter-bounded terminal lists and a constant polynomial exponent. The zero target is immediate.

Primary sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.11266 (Thomas–Yoo); https://people.tamu.edu/~yyoo/stoc25.pdf (Liu–Yoo Theorem 1.3).

## All cyclic odd prime powers

Let G be cyclic of order g=p^a for an odd prime p, and let H=p^(a−1)G be its unique subgroup of order p. First, the same LP/port procedure decides every target in H for arbitrary input G-labels, without a quotient oracle. In the low-fractional-cover case the proof already works for G: a minimal solution has fewer than g nonzero-score components. In the large-packing case use an Erdős–Pósa threshold for (g−1)²+1 vertex-disjoint nonzero cycles. The threshold applies since G has no element of order two. Among that many cycles some gain δ≠0 occurs at least g−1 times. Independent choices give the entire cyclic subgroup generated by δ. Every nontrivial subgroup of a cyclic p-group contains H. Thus every target in H is attained, even if δ does not generate G. This restricted-target decision is stable under deleting any input edges and therefore gives a witness by deletion self-reduction.

To solve an arbitrary target b first recursively solve and recover a witness x₀ modulo H, rejecting if none exists. The quotient is the cyclic group of order p^(a−1), with trivial group as the base. Replace each weight w_e by (−1)^{x₀(e)} w_e. For an arbitrary even set z the original score of x₀ XOR z is the original score of x₀ plus the translated score of z. The residual target b−score(x₀) belongs to H. Apply the standalone restricted-target algorithm and return x₀ XOR z. A feasible original solution always yields such a correction by XOR. Witness self-reduction at this stage only deletes edges in the translated correction problem, whose target stays in H, so no quotient recursion occurs inside this self-reduction. There are at most a stages and O(a m) standalone decision calls, giving deterministic f(p^a)poly(input) time.

## Additional coprime labels with controlled support

Let the group be cyclic G of order p^a times a finite abelian B of order coprime to p. Suppose every nonzero B-edge label is incident with one of h supplied vertices U (or find such a cover by the ordinary bounded vertex-cover search on those edges, treating loops as forced vertices). This allows unbounded graph degree and arbitrarily many nonzero B labels. The mixed problem takes deterministic f(|G|,|B|,h)poly(input).

For the recursive base G=0, every nonzero-score component of a minimal B-solution meets U, so the rooted port algorithm with the entire U as candidate list solves B alone. For an odd lifting step target in H×{0}, use the fractional packing/cover test only on the odd coordinate. In the large-packing branch enough disjoint odd-nonzero cycles force at least |G||B| copies of one identical joint increment (δ,y), δ≠0. By coprimality its cyclic subgroup contains H×{0} (multiply by ord(y), a unit modulo p). In the fractional-cover branch take U together with the short root list. Components of nonzero odd score are hit by the sampled list, and any remaining nonzero joint-score component meets U. A minimal witness has fewer than |G||B| nonzero components. The rooted solver uses the full joint group scores. Weight sign changes at a translated baseline do not enlarge the B support. Quotient recursion and deletion traceback are thus valid as before.

For elementary binary B there is a more general intrinsic parameter λ, the minimum fractional vertex-hitting mass for B-nonzero simple cycles in the original graph. The same factor-two oracle and volume method produce a cover of such cycles of cost O(1+λ) with every required cycle mass ≥1/2, by doubling the trial total budget until acceptance (fractional feasibility permits the same uniform-slack ball using a scaled feasible optimum). Binary edge labels are invariant under signs, so this same fractional cover stays valid after all translations and edge deletions. Replace U by a second pairwise-independent list using this cover and enumerate the product of the two list families. For a fixed witness at least one pair hits all components, with ≤|G||B| total nonzero components. B alone also has the elementary binary linear-system solver. This gives deterministic f(|G|,|B|,λ)poly(input) with no supplied hitting set or assumption on where nonzero binary labels occur.

## Unrestricted simultaneous binary markers

One can remove λ for elementary binary B. This gives deterministic FPT for G × B, with G cyclic of odd prime-power order and B arbitrary elementary binary. The period lemma itself holds for any finite abelian odd-order G.

For each nonzero χ∈B* consider simple cycles whose binary χ-gain is one. Apply the approximate fractional-cover feasibility procedure to that scalar binary labeling with the half-integral Erdős–Pósa threshold for N such cycles, valid even for order-two labels by Thomas–Yoo. A low outcome supplies a bounded-mass fractional cover; a high outcome implies N χ-odd cycles with each vertex in at most two of them. All thresholds below depend only on |G| and |B|.

For a high χ, pigeonhole a large fixed subfamily with identical full B-gain δ, where χ(δ)=1. For any even base x, flip a subset of its cycles by XOR. Each edge belongs to at most two cycles, so the odd score increment is a G-valued Boolean quadratic in the switches: for an edge in cycles i and j its contribution is (1−2x_e)w_e(s_i+s_j−2s_i s_j). With enough cycles, pigeonhole the unary coefficients and use finite edge-color Ramsey on pair coefficients to find m=exp(G) switches with all unary coefficients equal and all pair coefficients equal. This subset can depend on x, whereas δ does not. Switching all m adds zero to the odd score since m and binom(m,2) are multiples of the odd exponent. The binary increment is mδ=δ even with overlaps. Thus δ belongs to the subgroup P of global binary periods: translation by (0,δ) preserves the attained joint score set. The packing is needed only for this existence argument.

Let L be the span of all low characters. Every character annihilating P must be low, because each high χ is nonzero on its period δ. Thus L-perpendicular is contained in P. Retain a basis of low characters as the new binary constraints, projecting labels and target. Summing their individual fractional covers gives bounded mass and covers each nonzero projected binary cycle with mass at least 1/2. The fixed-cover odd-prime-power algorithm above therefore solves the projected problem. Any solution to it differs from the full requested binary target by an element of L-perpendicular, which can be corrected at fixed odd score by the period argument. This proves exact decision without constructing periods. For witness recovery greedily delete edges using this exact full decision, recomputing classification on each restricted graph. Internal projected witness extraction uses its fixed low covers, so the exponent remains independent of the group parameters.
