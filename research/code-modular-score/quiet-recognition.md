# FPT recognition of an odd-group quiet edge modulator

Given a graph with arbitrary unsigned finite odd-abelian-group weights, find at most k edges whose deletion makes every even support have score zero, or report that none exist. The algorithm runs in f(k)poly(input), with polynomially many group operations per fixed-parameter branch. The quiet-ports algorithm can thus find its own modulator.

Choose a binary cycle-space basis. For each physical edge e let a_e be its linear evaluation on this basis, i.e. the corresponding column of a generator matrix. These columns represent the cographic dual. For deleted edges F, residual free parameters are annihilated by U=span{a_e:e∈F}. On this parameter subspace two forms agree exactly when congruent modulo U. For each nonzero coset sum the weights of all its edges. The residual score is identically zero iff every such aggregate is zero. Indeed distinct nonzero parities are independent as functions for additive coefficients in an odd-order group: sum a supposed zero identity against the ± Walsh character of any designated nonzero form. Only that form contributes, with multiplier minus a power of two, invertible on the group. Zero-coordinate edges contribute nothing. This also proves that replacing U by any larger space preserves quietness.

Maintain a guessed subspace W of dimension at most k intended to lie in U. Project the original forms modulo W, sum weights by type, discard zero aggregate types and the zero form; call the resulting set of distinct forms A, and m=|A|. Recompute from the physical input at every recursion node, including all physical columns (also zero-weight ones) in eventual realization. If A is nonempty and dim W=k the branch fails. If 0<m≤2^(k+1), try all nonzero shifts h=a or h=a+b for distinct a,b in A. Any feasible U extending W must contain at least one such shift modulo W: each nonzero weight either projects to zero modulo U, or needs another nonzero weight type in the same U coset.

If m>2^(k+1), enumerate all shifts h≠0 such that |A∩(A+h)|≥m/2^(k+1), by tabulating pairwise differences. Some such h lies in any feasible U/W: at most 2^k forms of A lie in U/W itself, while every other form must have a partner with nonzero aggregate in its coset. Sum the partner incidence counts over the fewer than 2^k nonzero directions of U/W.

There are only f(k) heavy shifts, independently of m. Let d be their span dimension, and choose an independent heavy basis. In each coset of this span their edges form a d-dimensional cube. Its intersection with A has size at most C(d+k+1)^2: choose an original representative column per point. They all lie in an affine subspace of dimension ≤d+dim W, hence a linear subspace of dimension ≤d+k+1. These distinct columns restrict the simple regular cographic representation, so Heller's quadratic upper bound applies. A subset of s hypercube points spans at most (s log₂s)/2 edges. For completeness split on a coordinate: the smaller half contributes at most its size in crossing edges, and binary entropy is at least twice the smaller side's relative mass, proving the inequality inductively. Sum over the cosets and count both orientations. The d heavy incidences give d/2^(k+1) ≤ log₂(C(d+k+1)^2). This bounds d by a computable function of k, hence bounds the total number of heavy shifts by 2^d. For every candidate adjoin an arbitrary lift of h to W; this strictly increases its dimension.

At A empty, W would ensure quietness if it can be enforced by edge deletions. For every basis vector of W extend the corresponding linear form on the original cycle parameter space to a binary edge label via ordinary elimination. Give each edge its tuple of these labels in Γ=F₂^(dim W). Then W⊆span{a_e:e∈F} precisely when no surviving even support has nonzero Γ score, equivalently when no surviving simple cycle has such score. Apply ordinary Group Feedback Edge Set (GFES) with budget k, also retrieving the deleted edges. This standard directed-label convention agrees exactly here since every binary label is its own inverse. A failed realization leaf can be discarded: any solution at a larger subspace containing W would also realize W at this leaf. Nonzero binary loops must be deleted, zero loops do not obstruct. If a simple host is needed distinct private subdivisions of each edge preserve labels and the deletion optimum, as one segment can represent removal of its whole edge. The branching argument proves completeness and the leaf test proves soundness.

Crucially the leaf uses binary labels, not the original odd unsigned labels.

If simultaneous elementary binary score coordinates must vanish too, their global linear forms must be included in U. Add these forms at the realization step (reject if the resulting dimension exceeds k), or initialize W with their span and charge that dimension towards the recursion depth. This preserves the fixed-parameter analysis.
