# Connected contraction certificates on low-degree graphs

Let G be connected with four-bit vertex labels of total zero and target plane with basis u,v. Partition its vertices into connected clusters; Q is the simple quotient with each vertex labeled by its cluster XOR. For either graph compute A, the catalogue of all pairs of sums of disjoint vertex subsets ignoring connectivity, by three choices per vertex in 16² states.

If Q has vertex connectivity at least 205, the deterministic ordered-sum algorithm highly-connected-cographic.md gives a connected partition with sums u,v,u+v whenever (u,v) belongs to A(Q). Lift this witness by coloring whole clusters. A(G) must contain the requested pair for any feasible original named kernel by the three-component rank lemma. Thus agreement at the requested pair suffices for exact decision; agreement of whole catalogues gives a full-spectrum certificate. If the original algebraic test passes but the quotient test fails, this certificate is inconclusive. Every condition is efficiently testable. In particular at most one nonzero charge per cluster guarantees the entire catalogues agree even without requiring any connectivity.

Generate candidates by multi-source BFS from all vertices whose charges lie in a selected subset of the 16 label types, using parent pointers and consistent ties to keep clusters connected. The empty seed set is skipped. One can test every such subset, or merely some, in polynomial time. No guarantee is claimed for arbitrary inputs.

Example: replace each vertex of an m-by-m grid by a 205-clique, with complete joins for neighboring sites. Deleting fewer than 205 vertices leaves an occupant at every site and a connected graph. Maximum degree is at most 1024. Expand each macrovertex into a full binary tree of common depth D=10, one distinct leaf per incident edge, and join the corresponding leaves for each macroedge. This physical graph has maximum degree three, and unbounded treewidth by the grid minor. A half-grid cut has O(m) boundary vertices and Θ(m²) volume, ruling out constant vertex expansion. If all roots have nonzero charge and all other vertices zero, the BFS recovers the expansion clusters: every inside distance to its root is at most D while crossing from another root already costs at least D+1. The charged vertex count is linear in physical order since D is fixed. A fully nonzero example gives each nonroot the same nonzero charge w and all roots nonzero charges different from w. There are evenly many nonroots in every tree, so its XOR is the root charge. BFS again recognizes all roots by their types. If two disjoint bases of four-bit root labels occur, all algebraic target pairs are already feasible on Q, hence on G. Even multiplicities of roots may be chosen to ensure total XOR zero. This family demonstrates a verifiable certificate, not all labelings of these graphs.
