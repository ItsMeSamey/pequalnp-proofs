# Turning a small ordinary-edge augmented minor into a physical catalogue kernel

Take a connected G with ordinary binary incidence matrix A (one row per vertex) and fixed p dense named columns B (rows are vertex charges). For each ordinary subset X the named kernel is {y : By belongs to span(A_X)}. Its orthogonal is the span of the component charge sums in the chosen spanning subgraph. Suppose a specified ordinary contraction/deletion sequence on the augmented binary representation [A | B], retaining all named elements, leaves m ordinary elements and exactly the original catalogue. Then one can construct a connected conservative spanning minor with this catalogue on at most p+2m+d+1 vertices, where d is the number of explicitly protected vertices. For protection either the sequence never merges two of them, or the catalogue hypothesis uses independent one-hot port columns as well (count them in p in the latter variant).

Perform all ordinary contractions first. In the physical graph these give connected vertex branches, with every coordinate of B summing on each branch: quotient by an edge's incidence identifies its endpoints' basis vectors. Loops contract as deletions. Denote the resulting connected graph by G0. After deleting the nonretained ordinary edges, get the possibly disconnected spanning graph J on the same vertices. It may have arbitrarily many isolated vertices, including nonzero charges. Zero or parallel ordinary edges cause no problem. If using the augmented port-catalogue hypothesis, a pair of pins cannot already have been merged: the original singleton-partition span projects onto the full port-bit space, whereas any partition after merging two unit port rows has equal bits on that pair. Row-space isomorphisms or zero-row removal in the matrix minor do not change named kernels. Work on the unreduced vertex representation when constructing the physical branches.

In G0 reserve a set S of all endpoints of J's retained ordinary edges and all protected vertex branches; let s=|S|≤2m+d. Let W span the charges of vertices outside S; these are isolated in J. Temporarily attach an extra independent unit bit to each member of S. Start with G0's singleton partition and merge adjacent parts whenever its generated subgroup in F₂^{p+s} stays fixed. The elementary merging lemma (all-bounded-rankwidth-catalogue.md) gives at most p+s+1 final connected parts; the procedure uses ordinary binary rank tests. The initial span is generated by (a_i,e_i) for i in S and (W,0). Its projection has rank s, so the final partition has a separate part per terminal. Indeed the bit supports of parts are disjoint; two terminals in one part reduce the rank. Charges on the separate terminal parts are a_i+w_i for some w_i in W. The terminal-free parts span exactly W: projecting to the temporary flags annihilates every relation involving any independent terminal row. The temporary flags may now be forgotten.

Contract these connected parts in G0. Keep the m distinguished edges (or their simple equivalents where parallel/loops occur); all other edges may initially be omitted. For any subset of J's distinguished edges, the component partition of the s terminals is identical in this quotient. Its sums modulo W agree with the old terminal component sums, and terminal-free parts independently generate all of W. Thus the actual full component-sum span agrees for every choice of distinguished edges. Restore enough quotient edges from G0 to make the bounded graph connected. They cannot remove the demonstrated entries because edge selection remains optional, and cannot introduce a catalogue entry absent from G because every physical quotient partition lifts to a connected partition of G. This proves exact equality as well as charge preservation and port separation. All constructive steps after provision of the minor model are polynomial, and no generic algorithm to find the abstract small minor or a general WQO is assumed.

Conversely a physical kernel on n vertices gives an ordinary augmented minor with at most n(n−1)/2 ordinary elements after removing loops/duplicates. Thus, for this full-catalogue objective with pins encoded among the named columns when needed, computable bounded size for a catalogue-preserving ordinary minor and for a physical minor are equivalent up to the explicit bounds, conditional on constructive access to the respective models. This equivalence does not turn a small single-query minor into a simultaneous catalogue representative.
