# Parallel strands with finite abelian vertex charges

Use the definitions and universal exterior semantics of subdivision-prefix-minor.md. Let g=|A| and e be its exponent. Consider arbitrarily many internally disjoint strands with the same ordered distinct endpoints u,v and no protected interior vertices. Their compressed total size has an explicit bound independent of their multiplicity. Edges in a fixed exterior retain their identities.

First shorten each strand to at most g edges as in the preceding theorem. Treat any change to the charges on u,v produced by these contractions as part of the current exterior. Classify a current strand by its internal total S and the set P₀ of prefix values. There are at most g·2^(g−1) classes since P₀ contains zero. A state is either through (all edges used), or a nonempty subset P of P₀ of cut values. The number of states is 2^{|P₀|} ≤ 2^g.

Within a class the same state can be realized on any strand. A through state joins the endpoints and contributes S to the joined component. A cut state contributes the same detached subgroup D=⟨P−P⟩ on every strand and endpoint residues p and S−p modulo D, for any p in P. This follows from the preceding theorem, regardless of the order of the prefix values. The whole collection of states specifies whether the endpoints join. Its detached subgroups generate their sum D*. Endpoint charge contributions modulo D* depend only on the multiset of states; through contributions go to the joined component. Consequently permuting states among strands of one class preserves the entire score subgroup for each fixed exterior.

If a class has more than e·2^g members, designate any e of them for removal. For every configuration some state occurs at least e+1 times in the class. Permute states so that the designated strands carry that state, then omit the designated strands. Another strand with that state remains, so endpoint connectivity and D* are unchanged. The lost contributions in the quotient A/D* sum to zero, since e copies of any element vanish. Conversely, extend any surviving configuration by setting the designated strands to the same single-cut state, cutting the end edge at v. Their total internal contribution at u is eS=0 and they do not join the endpoints. In this reverse construction the end-edge prefixes equal S, so no detached intervals occur.

Physical realization: on each designated strand delete the end edge at v and contract the rest toward u; the absorbed labels total eS=0. No vertex is discarded, u and v remain distinct, and at least one class member survives. Repeat until at most e·2^g copies per class remain. Thus at most g·2^(g−1)·e·2^g strands per unordered endpoint pair suffice, each of length ≤g. For a loopless subdivision on h specified core vertices with any multiplicities, the order is at most h+binom(h,2)(g−1)g e·2^(2g−1). These coarse constants give a deterministic polynomial construction for fixed A. This theorem allows arbitrary fixed exterior edge selections, but not fixed or distinguishable states on individual removable strands.
