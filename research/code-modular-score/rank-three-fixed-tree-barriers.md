# Three-bit spanning-support controls

If the total charge t≠0, each attainable span U contains t and equals the preimage of its image modulo t. Project binary three-space onto its quotient by t, use the unrestricted two-bit unicycle algorithm, and retain those physical edges with their original charges. This gives an unrestricted three-bit full-catalogue unicycle for nonzero total. It does not apply to full rank at total zero.

On K4 charge the four vertices by the three units and their sum. The shore-sum map identifies a subset uniquely up to complement. Each 2+2 split is therefore the unique two-part witness of its nonzero line. A line always has a two-part witness by the merger lemma. Keeping all lines requires all six edges, since both pairs in each required split must stay connected. Thus cycle rank three can be necessary even without fixing a spanning tree. On K_{p+1}, p≥2, the analogous p units and their sum force every two-vertex shore edge by the same uniqueness argument, giving cycle rank ≥p(p−1)/2.

For a stronger lower bound when the starting tree is prescribed, take the square of the path 0,...,n, n≥6, plus a new vertex z adjacent to 2 and 4. This is one two-connected block. The initial T is the base path plus 2z. In binary three-space with basis a,b,d, assign a to 0 and 1, b to n−1 and n, d to 2 and z, and zero elsewhere. The plane W=<a+b,d> is attained by even base vertices, odd base vertices and {z}: their respective sums are a+b+d, a+b and d.

For any tree, the spans realized by a partition are spanned by the cut colours of its omitted edges and by the total. Indeed a cut colour is the sum of the blocks on its shore, and the sum of any block is obtained by summing the incident tree-edge cut colours at total zero. Hence any T' realizing W has a tree edge whose fundamental cut projects to a+b modulo d. Both end pairs {0,1},{n−1,n} must split across this cut. Any excursion via z between base vertices in a shore can be replaced by the edge 2--4, so each colour of the cut forms a connected set in the base path square, reaching both end pairs. Any consecutive monochromatic interior pair would cut off the opposite shore between its two ends, since base edges span at most two positions. Thus all n base-path edges cross the cut. A fundamental cut in T' crosses only one of its edges. T' retains at most one old base edge and possibly 2z, requiring at least n−1 edges outside T.

In particular any W-preserving spanning subgraph containing this T has cycle rank ≥n−1, since a spanning forest of a witnessing partition extends to a witnessing tree within it. This rules out bounded exchange distance from an arbitrarily fixed basis, even for a single prescribed plane. It does not rule out choosing a bounded-cyclomatic support from scratch. The example itself has bounded treewidth.
