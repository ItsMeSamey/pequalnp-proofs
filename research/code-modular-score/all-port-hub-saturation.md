# Context saturation by charged components adjacent to all ports

Take a connected graph with b≥1 distinct named ports, charges in V=F₂ᵖ and total t. A connected partition determines the partition π of its ports, the span D of the sums of parts disjoint from the ports, and a residue in V/D for each port block. Necessarily these residues sum to t+D. Call such a state compatible. A spanning charge-summing minor with the ports in distinct branches lifts each of its states. A minor realizing all compatible states therefore has exactly the host's states. This equality also survives gluing any fixed exterior at the named ports with a consistent convention on shared port charges: joining port partitions merges their open blocks, their charges add modulo the sum of the two detached spaces, and the previously detached components persist.

Call a component of the graph after removing the ports an all-port component if it has an actual neighbor at every port. Selected distinct all-port components can be contracted to distinct hub vertices of their respective total charges. Absorb each other component in full into any one of its adjacent ports, which exists by connectedness. The port branches and hub branches are disjoint, connected, and cover all vertices. Preserve all hub-port incidences and delete extra edges. This gives a physical complete bipartite graph on the ports and hubs, with inherited charges.

For b=3 and p=1, two distinct odd all-port components suffice. Keep these two hubs. With D=0 and all ports separate, both hubs must be assigned to port blocks. Assigning both to one port changes no parity from the port labels; assigning them to two distinct ports toggles that pair. These are all even differences from the port labels. For a pair and a singleton, assign the first hub to connect the pair and the second to either block, realizing both compatible parity patterns. For one block assign both hubs there. For D=V leave one odd hub detached and use the other to join the unique nonsingleton port block if there is one, otherwise assign it to any port. This proves universality for the five-vertex K₃,₂ for either total parity. A sole odd hub can fail even if the ports are joined by extra edges or by paths with exclusively zero-charged interiors: take all three ports and all other vertices even. With three separate ports and D=0, exactly one port block can be odd, so the compatible triple (1,1,1) is absent. This last obstruction applies to a single odd vertex in total, not to arbitrary connected components of odd sum.

For arbitrary b,p choose distinct all-port components as follows, with no reuse across roles: one for each nonzero vector v with component sum v, b−1 more of sum e for each vector e in a fixed basis of V, and floor(b/2) more of arbitrary sums. The graph obtained above has at most b+(2ᵖ−1)+(b−1)p+floor(b/2) vertices. Fix any compatible state. From the first family leave the representatives of a basis of D detached as singleton parts. Choose a background port block. For every nonsingleton port block assign it a separate hub of the last family to join all its ports; the bound floor(b/2) suffices. Initially assign every other hub to the background block, where it has a port contact. For each nonbackground block the difference from its requested residue is a vector of V/D. Choose a lift to V, expand in the fixed basis, and move one reserved correction hub per used coordinate from the background into that block. At most b−1 correction hubs per coordinate are needed. Moving them preserves connectivity because every moved hub is adjacent to ports both before and after, and the separate joining hubs stay put. By the total-sum condition the background residue is now correct. The detached singleton sums span precisely D. This also covers b=1, p=0 and D=0. At fixed parameters, the components, their charges and their port neighborhoods are computed by graph traversal; reserving specified charges before arbitrary extra hubs yields the certificate whenever the stated inventory is present.
