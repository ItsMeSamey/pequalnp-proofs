# Physical pumping of bounded separator chains, including dense additive backgrounds

Let a connected charged graph G be presented as the union of a physical spanning graph H and all edges specified by a fixed symmetric adjacency template on finitely many vertex types (only distinct vertices can be adjacent). No template edge may be cancelled. The empty template allows H=G. Fix the conserved group including protected bits, a fixed family of CMSO queries including the complete named-kernel catalogue, and an adhesion bound p. A sufficiently long chain of physically separated progressing cuts of order ≤p in H yields a strictly smaller conservative spanning minor with the identical catalogue. Zero-transverse tiles of bounded size give such chains without a background; the additive case allows arbitrarily many crossing background edges.

Place permanently in X one vertex of each realized background type (if the template is empty no representatives are needed). One may add any bounded set of extra permanent vertices. Work in H−X. A chain consists of nested vertex separations with disjoint successive separators S_i of size ≤p, left sides increasing and right sides decreasing, such that later separators are wholly on the right of earlier ones and earlier separators wholly on the left of later ones. Boundaries belong to both incident slabs. Allow empty separators. Require at least one ordinary vertex strictly between each two successive selected separators. These conditions hold for mutually disjoint ordered bounded separating tiles after selecting separated tiles and retaining an intervening nonempty tile. Stable permanent vertices may first be discarded from the tiles; only surviving useful gaps count.

At order k>0 consider a chunk of consecutive separators. Test for k vertex-disjoint paths in its closed slab from its first to its last separator (in H−X). If found and all sizes are ≤k, each intervening separator has size k and is met exactly once by each path, in the same separator order. Number paths arbitrarily, ordering each cut by these physical lanes. Within each slab between consecutive cuts use the path segments as k disjoint connected seeds. Keep the permanent vertices X as separate additional seeds. Assign every other vertex of a component of the slab touching a seeded H−X path or separator by connected growth. Each remaining component in H−X has no H−X edge outside the slab, since its only exits are the seeded separators. It has an H edge to X or a template edge leading out, by connectedness of G unless it is the whole graph (excluded by the progressing chain). In the latter case its endpoint can meet a physical permanent representative of the required neighboring type. If the endpoint itself is permanent, it is already a seed; otherwise the representative is a distinct vertex. Attach the whole component to such a permanent seed along one physical edge. This includes components connected to another component by template edges: one such edge also provides a direct representative contact. If X is empty and G=H, no unattached component exists. For k=0 the same argument works without paths: connected components between empty separators attach to permanent seeds. Assign all vertices after the incoming separator through the outgoing separator exactly once. Sum the charges added to each of the k+|X| receivers in the slab, counting the k incoming anchors only in their earlier slab.

Tag each separator in a linked chunk by its cumulative charge vector and the finite CMSO type of the H-prefix including the ordered named separators and X, retaining charges, protected labels and template types. A partial graph contains its boundary vertices. Equal prefix types can be substituted under gluing along named vertices by the CMSO disjoint-union composition theorem (equivalently, quantify set membership on the finitely many shared roots separately). Apply the template interpreted by a quantifier-free formula after gluing. If the tags at two cuts agree, identify the corresponding left and right separators by the k actual connected spanning lanes and absorb all other deleted vertices through the specified receivers. Their total charge increments vanish. The desired output is the union of left and right induced H-pieces glued at these ordered separators and X, with all template edges restored. Every desired H-edge is witnessed on one retained side after the physical paths identify its roots. Every desired template edge between distinct surviving vertices was already physically present between their retained representatives, with matching types at identified roots as assured by equal prefix types. Delete all surplus quotient edges. Both the catalogue and all protected ports are preserved. The output's H-layer is an ordinary minor of H: keeping only the k disjoint path segments for the identifications, one may delete the other middle vertices for this H-only certificate. The progressing gap guarantees strict physical shrinkage (one may require two intervening cuts before choosing a repeated tag).

If there are no k disjoint paths, Menger gives a separator T of size <k in this closed slab, allowing endpoint vertices. Split the slab at T by assigning to the left all vertices reachable from its incoming separator outside T in the slab minus T, putting the remainder on the right; T belongs to both sides. Extend the two sides by the original exterior pieces. Since the old separators were the only exits, this gives a valid nested replacement separation in H−X. Use disjoint chunks separated by at least one unused progressing gap; their replacement separators stay pairwise disjoint and progressing.

For an explicit finite recursion let M_k bound the tag count at order k. At order zero, M_0+1 progressing cuts suffice. At order k choose N_{k−1} disjoint chunks each of M_k+1 cuts, leaving two unused cuts between chunks, where N_{k−1} suffices for the lower order. If any chunk carries k paths, repeated tags suffice. Otherwise the N_{k−1} smaller separators give a reduction by induction. This is constructive by ordinary vertex-capacitated flow, finite-state evaluation on supplied bounded-width pieces or exhaustive evaluation in general, and the recorded spanning assignments. The lemma is local and does not on its own preserve a prescribed rank-width-two layout. A physical rank-zero transverse tile U separates the prefix and suffix in H by definition, giving direct applications; a scalar background handled by the template gives a rank-one deficient application when the supplied layout also has rank-width two. Arbitrary signed Schur complements are not positive templates.
