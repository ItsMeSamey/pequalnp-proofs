# Orientation bounds for nearby regular references

The alternating lower bound and compatible upper bound of local-port-orientations.md have the correct quantifiers. A regular physical reference has a regular minor for EVERY orientation. The compact reference and the orientation variables of an upper witness may instead be chosen together. Testing the former by ∀O ∃S and the latter by ∃O ∃S with the compatibility clauses uses fixed-budget CMSO on the central represented matroid. Orientations carry a constant alphabet at each triple, so the formula size depends on distance budgets and not the number of pendants. Noncanonical orientation sets may be excluded by a validity guard. The existing finite-state port relation and colored-set traceback recover the orientation and regular reference in the existential upper search; no single witness for all orientations is assumed in the lower. Testing each successive budget through OPT takes parameter-dependent time and a graph-size exponent independent of the number of branches.

The common physical restriction/projection reconstructing the compact code is a left inverse on each compatible restored code. For K4 this is the image of the full preimage under the pairwise-XOR trace map on even triples; for its dual the only redundant trace difference is the known triangle 111. The chosen unary restrictions likewise have a physical inverse minor. Disjoint ports preserve each other's compatible pure cycles/parity equations on construction. Nonexpansion in both directions then implies isometry of BOTH relative codimensions. In particular the upper is the best distance achieved by this restoration family, and an equality with a valid lower yields a full physical optimum and witness.

In the connected stress test, deleting at v all original incident edges except the virtual e=vw forces e=0 since v lies outside the support of the six-terminal syndrome. This leaves the two constituents uncoupled and produces the advertised direct-sum minor after dual simplification on the other side. Minimum one-sided optima add on a direct sum by projection (subcodes) and shortening (supercodes). The total-distance-two witness and the lower bounds of three for each monotone route are compatible. These values do not prove worst-case approximation sharpness. Alignment and zero padding of parity-check row bases give the different matrix perturbation objective max(a,b); d_total=a+b.

## Two stronger lower-bound options

First, fix directional budgets prior to quantifying orientations. Let L_box be the minimum a+b such that for every O its compact minor has some regular reference with both directional distances at most (a,b). Then L≤L_box≤OPT, by carrying the directions of a single physical witness through all common minors. This is the feasible-upset intersection already present in local-port-orientations.md, made explicit as a total-distance lower bound. Comparing it with the repaired upper is valid. No strict separation is asserted for the orientation family.

Second, suppose the fixed local physical deletion/contraction recipes for the two orientation options at each pendant are available. In that pendant execute only deletions prescribed on BOTH recipes and only contractions prescribed on BOTH. Retain all other elements, even those scheduled for opposing operations. Let p be the maximum cardinality of such a residual local ground set over the pendants. The resulting single represented physical minor D has every orientation core as a minor, by simply completing the appropriate outstanding prescriptions. All ground-set identifications can be made after the fixed operations. Thus its optimum regular distance lower-bounds the full physical optimum and dominates the maximum-of-orientations lower bound. Its feasible directional upset also refines the intersection across orientations. D is not assumed to be a left-invertible compact replacement, so an optimal reference on it is only a lower-bound certificate in general.

Compute this joint lower by bounded-width recognition with parameters central width w and p. Bundle the central triangle ports in a width-O(w) tree. Across a cut separating no bundle, join the side-local branch relations to the original central code. Its left/right linear relation is represented by at most 2^λ Cartesian rectangles (one per boundary class), where λ is its binary connectivity on that cut. Applying independently a local relation to each side maps each rectangle to a rectangle, possibly empty. This bounds the output connectivity by λ: any rectangle contained in an output linear relation lies in just one class modulo the shortened left/right subcodes (the two crossed pairs in a rectangle certify these differences are shortened). Hence a cover by 2^λ rectangles has at most that many nonempty output classes. The new tree can directly replace each bundle by at most p local physical leaves; any new inner cut has connectivity at most p by its small side. Its width is O(w+p). A central port with no remaining physical positions can be omitted, using the same local-relation argument to account for a constraint on the central code. All local operations and the represented D are constructive by elimination. This gives an independently testable certificate when p is moderate, including cases where individual original pendants are large. If p is unbounded the argument provides no FPT guarantee in central width alone.
