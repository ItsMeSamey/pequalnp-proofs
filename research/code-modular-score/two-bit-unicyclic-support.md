# An unrestricted two-bit catalogue has a unicyclic support

For every connected simple graph G with at most two queried binary coordinates there is a polynomial-time construction of a connected spanning subgraph of cycle rank at most one with exactly the same named-kernel catalogue. All vertices and physical ports survive. Additional port indicators count toward two only if they are also queried. The bound of one cycle is sometimes necessary.

Pad to two bits. Every component-sum span contains the total t and lies in the full charge span V. The one-block and singleton partitions attain the two extreme spans. If dim V≤1 or t≠0 they exhaust all possibilities and any spanning tree works. Otherwise V is the binary plane and t=0. In a spanning tree T give edge f the colour c_f equal to the sum on either shore of T−f. Each vertex charge equals the sum of the colours on its incident edges, so tree colours span V. Each nonzero edge colour gives a catalogue line. A line generated by v≠0 is attainable in G iff some bond (cut with two connected shores) has shore sum v: a line has a partition with at most two parts by the bounded-witness merger lemma and both charges then equal v. A tree displaying all three colours suffices.

If T misses v, it displays the other two colours, red and blue. Form the fundamental bipartite graph with tree-edge nodes f, nontree-edge nodes e, and adjacency when f is on the tree path joining the ends of e. If no component contains both red and blue, no bond has shore sum v. Indeed every binary cut is the XOR of the fundamental cuts indexed by its crossing tree edges. Each fundamental cut is contained in its own bipartite component. The componentwise sums have disjoint supports and are each cuts, so a minimal nonempty cut lies entirely in one component. Summing vertex charges over its shore cancels all interior tree-edge colours in pairs. All resulting colours lie in the component's monochromatic span and omit v.

Otherwise choose a shortest red-to-blue path. It is chordless, and its interior tree-edge nodes have colour zero. If its initial segment is f_0,e_1,f_1,e_2 with interior tree node f_1, exchange f_1 for e_1. Binary basis pivoting adds the incidence f_0,e_2 by the old three incidences f_0,e_1; f_1,e_1; f_1,e_2 and old nonincidence f_0,e_2. Later nontree path nodes have no incidence with f_1, so the suffix persists. The replacement edge has f_1's old cut colour; other tree-edge colours change only by possibly adding that colour. These are the same row changes on the named columns in tree coordinates. Consequently all colours persist in this zero exchange. Recompute a shortest path, now strictly shorter. Eventually a chord e meets both a red and a blue tree edge. Exchanging that red edge for e adds red to the colour of the blue edge, producing v. Keep the union of the trees just before and after this final exchange. It has one cycle and all three lines are witnessed in its spanning trees. The zero and full-plane entries persist and edge deletion introduces no entries. The number of shortest path and exchange operations is polynomial.

A triangle with the three distinct nonzero vertex charges has total zero and realizes three different catalogue lines. Any spanning tree has only two edges, so it realizes at most two such lines (a connected bipartition of a tree is the cut of a single edge). The established bounded-treewidth physical reduction applied to the selected subgraph now gives a computably bounded representative with any fixed number of separated physical ports, without a host width promise.
