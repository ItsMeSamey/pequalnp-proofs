# Restriction nullity of inclusion-minimal unsigned group scores

Let the cycle space of a binary matroid be equipped with physical element weights in a fixed finite abelian group G. Suppose C is an inclusion-minimal cycle achieving its score, the sum of its weights. On the restriction to C the all-ones word is the unique word of that score. Write d for its cycle dimension. For regular matroids d is bounded by a function of G alone.

## Bound for an arbitrary finite abelian G

Take any binary column representation of the dual of the restriction, in basis coordinates [I|A]. Its row space is precisely the restricted cycle code. For every ordered triple of basis indices, contracting all the other basis columns represents the remaining columns by their traces on those three indices. If all seven nonzero traces appear, choosing one representative for each gives a Fano minor. A regular matroid has no such minor. Color every triple by its lexicographically first missing nonzero trace.

For any predetermined h, finite Ramsey gives, at sufficient dimension d, a sequence of 2h basis indices on which all triples have one color. Each physical column on this ordered sequence avoids that fixed binary subsequence of length three. A sequence with six or more runs would contain an alternating subsequence of length six, which contains every binary length-three word (choose one bit in each consecutive pair). Thus the trace has at most five runs and four transitions. For each of the h disjoint consecutive pairs use the sum of the corresponding basis rows as a code direction. These directions are linearly independent by the identity columns. At each physical coordinate at most four directions are nonzero.

Starting at the all-ones word, vary these directions independently by h Boolean bits. Every physical bit is a function of at most four bits. Its group-weighted contribution has a multiaffine expansion of degree at most four by subset Möbius inversion over G. Write e for the exponent of G and L=24e. A second finite Ramsey application, successively homogenizing the coefficient colors (in G) of all j-subsets for j=1,2,3,4, gives an L-subset on which each degree has a single coefficient, provided h is sufficiently large in G. Switching precisely those L bits changes the score by the sum of those four coefficients times the respective binomial coefficients (L choose j). Each multiplier is a multiple of e because j divides 24 and (L choose j)=(L/j)(L-1 choose j-1). This produces another word attaining the original score, contrary to uniqueness. The resulting computable iterated Ramsey bound depends only on G. It applies to any binary matroid whose restricted dual excludes Fano as a minor; regularity suffices. The proof concerns inclusion-minimal score witnesses, without a general bound on packing versus nullity.

## Sharper prime-power group bound

For G a product of cyclic groups of prime-power orders q_i for one prime p, put D=sum(q_i-1). Consolidate identical physical coordinate forms by summing their weights. There are at most m=d(d+1)/2 distinct nonzero columns by Heller's simple regular matroid bound. For odd p the Lucas/Walsh construction in README.md expresses the target indicator over F_p as a linear combination of products of at most D coordinate bits. Each coordinate bit is a linear combination of the constant character and its Walsh character. Consequently the Fourier support lies among the products of at most D distinct physical characters, at most (m+1)^D possibilities. The unique word indicator on d variables has every one of its 2^d Walsh coefficients nonzero (they equal plus or minus 2^{-d}). Thus 2^d ≤ (1+d(d+1)/2)^D, which gives d=O(D log(D+1)). For p=2 the same Lucas construction over the Boolean polynomial ring yields degree at most D after substituting the linear code bits. A singleton indicator has degree d, giving d≤D, even without regularity.

Primary support for Heller's bound, recalled in the proof of Theorem 1.5: https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02080v2 . All statements concern the cycle code restricted to C, so physical loops of the dual may be ignored and duplicate columns aggregated for score purposes.

## All proper minor-closed binary classes

For any proper minor-closed class of binary matroids, its class of duals is also proper and minor-closed. Fix an excluded simple binary projective geometry PG(k−1,2) for those duals: every fixed excluded binary matroid is a minor of a sufficiently large binary projective geometry (lift each column by the sum of two new private unit vectors, take the simple restriction consisting of the lifted columns and all private units, then contract the units; this also realizes loops). Each projection onto k basis coordinates therefore omits one nonzero k-bit pattern, or the projection minor contains the geometry. Color k-subsets by a missing trace. In a homogeneous Ramsey subsequence each physical column avoids this fixed subsequence, whence at most 2k−1 runs and at most 2k−2 transitions. The previous Boolean-arity argument now uses degree a=2k−2 and L=(a!) exp(G), increasing k to at least two when needed. This bounds minimal support nullity solely by the class and G.

For the sharper prime-power bound, the distinct column traces on a d-element basis never shatter a k-set. Sauer–Shelah gives at most sum_{j<k} binom(d,j) distinct column types. Thus the Walsh proof gives d=O(k D log(kD+1)). The 2-group bound d≤D is unconditional.

## Deterministic XP and randomized FPT for one unsigned score

Fix R large enough for the Ramsey argument, depending only on the group and excluded geometry. In any R-dimensional coordinate subcube of the basis coefficient space every nonempty target fiber contains at least two points. Indeed the homogeneous directions may be chosen inside these R free coordinates, and translating any target point to the origin only complements some physical affine bits, preserving arity. A minimum-Hamming-weight target point consequently has weight less than R: otherwise a subcube freeing R of its ones contains a second target point of lower weight. Enumerating basis vectors of weight below R gives deterministic XP with witness for every fixed proper binary minor-closed class.

A nonempty fiber with this no-singleton R-subcube property has density at least 2^(1−R). Induct on cube dimension, simultaneously for all positive R. Below dimension R the claim follows from nonemptiness; for R=1 a nonempty fiber is the entire cube. Otherwise split along any coordinate. Two nonempty halves each inherit the same property and density bound. With exactly one nonempty half, no (R−1)-subcube of that half has singleton intersection: together with its counterpart in the empty half that would violate the original property. Induction in the nonempty half now gives twice the needed density there. Thus independent uniform sampling of basis bits finds a witness with failure at most epsilon in O(2^R log(1/epsilon)) trials. Verification is exact, so decision has one-sided error. These bounds allow arbitrary offsets in physical bit functions whenever their linear parts are in the same represented class.
