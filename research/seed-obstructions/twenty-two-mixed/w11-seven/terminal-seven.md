# At least seven terminal products

Project to fourteen raw variables, nine point variables and five line variables. The normalized size is c+2r−1 for c prefix gates and r matched terminal ANDs. Every raw variable is essential. On the six edge-only instances, the fifteen singleton positives form a connected graph with parts of sizes nine and five and no isolated edge. On instance 1, the fourteen singleton positives form a connected component with parts of sizes eight and five, plus an isolated point variable. The latter variable is essential via an additional positive. Every one-coordinate assignment is forced negative.

For r≥9 there are at most one prefix gate and five raw–raw products, insufficient for the matching. If r=8 there are at most three prefix wires; at least five terminal products must be raw–raw, and these five consume every raw line terminal. Every other terminal has one prefix and one raw point operand. The 5+three largest raw point degrees are at most 13 for the six edge-only instances and at most 12 for instance 1. This cannot cover all singleton-edge positives.

Let S be the coordinates occurring as direct operands of prefix gates. Its complement U appears only at terminals, once each by matching. Every edge at u∈U must be covered by the unique terminal involving u: another term is independent of u and would accept the opposite singleton, a forbidden row. Two members of U cannot share a product, because this would demand an isolated-edge component. If one of them is the isolated point of bank 1, its line partner would have a singleton neighbor that this term cannot accept. In particular U is independent: on an edge at u,v∈U, their unique terminals would both need to be the same terminal. Therefore |U| distinct terminals are anchored on its vertices. If |U|=r, no edge can lie in S, since each terminal is zero when U=0. Thus U,S bipartition the connected singleton component.

For r=7, |U|=7 would force the larger connected part (size eight or nine) to fit inside a seven-coordinate side. Consequently |S|≥8. With at most five prefix gates there can be at most two wires between them, counted with multiplicity. For the six edge-only instances, two dirty-to-dirty wires are already necessary. It follows that c=5, there are precisely two such wires and no other internal prefix operand wires, and eight different raw variables occupy the eight remaining source positions.

The enumerator seven.cpp selects each pure and then mixed topological layer, using nondecreasing (maximum operand, minimum operand, operation) to avoid equivalent orders. Such an order exists: iteratively assign the smallest available key; a gate newly made available contains the just-created larger operand. The two internal edges and the disjoint raw slots are enforced explicitly. A useful prefix gate has a positive row and a dirty mixed prefix gate has a negative row. Equality with any existing wire on the positive and negative antichains would save a gate in a minimum separator. The final matching subset search requires all positives covered and every terminal negative-safe. Branching on an uncovered positive is exhaustive for minimum circuits, whose terminals each have a private positive row.
