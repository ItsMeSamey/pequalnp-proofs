# Eleven point leaves, eight line leaves, one surplus use

Consider a connected residual cone with eleven point boundary wires, eight line boundary wires and 19 mixed gates. There is one surplus use relative to a tree. The eight line singleton signatures include all seven raw singletons and an extra signature S. Lower each line signal to the OR of its singleton signature. This preserves positives and only lowers negatives. A signature of size s needs at least s−1 original line gates, since at least s raw line inputs feed its binary cone.

**Every S with at least three members is excluded at total 33.** The point-only lower bound used here is the 21-gate singleton-signature theorem (seven_certificate/README.md): computing all seven four-point masks by union and intersection needs 21 gates even with all masks meeting every target in at most one point available free. No pair-safety requirement on the final point outputs is needed.

## Restricted samples

Set the extra line leaf to constant one, retaining every positive. For negatives use only rows with an active member of S; their extra leaf is already one. Write N for these rows, recorded as pairs (Q,L) of point and line masks. In some cases remove one point c from every raw point input and from all primitive point features. On a positive at another point this changes nothing. At a negative in N it only lowers the formula. The projected primitive singleton masks still meet every star in at most one point, so the point-only lower bound applies with no cost for this projection. In recovering star signatures we need to check only retained singleton points. Every nonconstant monotone point expression on the projected leaves is false on the removed singleton; adding it by a raw point OR to the stars to which it belongs restores the desired full signatures.

Suppress constants and remove dead gates after clamping the extra leaf. Equivalently one can leave constants formal until their local trees are evaluated. The remaining graph has at most one surplus use. Point subtrees created by clamping may be retained as single side expressions: after fixing all nonpoint leaves of a tree to constants, a nonconstant expression on m original point occurrences costs at most m−1 gates.

All seven raw lines remain essential, because each has a retained incident point and the zero-line singleton is rejected there. A line-containing subtree of a tree will be called good if it rejects the zero-line values of its incident retained singleton points and accepts them with their own line alone active. At a split of two independent raw-line groups choose two retained incident endpoints whose zero-line pair is dominated by an N negative. An AND would force each opposite child true with its own lines zero on one of these endpoints and hence both true on their union. This is impossible. Such zero-line pair rejections inherit through splitting ORs, and through point AND guardians containing a resident endpoint, because the point guardian accepts that endpoint. Thus all splits are OR. Point AND guards accept every retained singleton of every resident line. At any given line, private guards are AND guards kept on its path; dropping the others is justified by an N negative at which all dropped guards are true. Point OR attachments may be ignored for a particular ordinary line term; raw-line activation and the point AND guards suffice to make that term true. A circuit with a repeated raw line Y uses the ordinary skeleton on the other six. For Y, take the OR of the Y=1 cofactors of all OR-attachment sides. These point operands are disjoint from the ordinary private AND sides. An accepted Y singleton reaches at least one true OR attachment because all ordinary leaves are zero. For rejecting a singleton exterior to Y, a negative with Y active and a point on an ordinary line below the chosen attachment activates all its ancestor guards. These are the usual repeated-one-label arguments; the checker verifies their required point/line witnesses after projection.

## Full restricted witnesses

For all s≥4 and all concurrent triples the verifier finds a removal set of size zero or one such that:

* Every retained singleton with zero lines lies below a row of N. For each distinct pair of target lines there is an N point set containing a retained point of both, as needed at a split.
* For each retained point p and any line U there is an N negative containing p and meeting U at a retained point.
* For each line T, each retained singleton p outside T and every other U, an N negative has T active, contains p and meets U at a retained point.

These conditions reproduce singleton-only versions of the one-surplus reconstruction for all seven lines. Here are the counts explicitly. If the cone is a tree, form the conjunction of private guards for each line; the nested shared groups contain a common other U, so a negative of the last type forces rejection at p. The seven nonconstant expressions have disjoint point occurrences and use at most P−7 extra point gates for P=11. A repeated point leaf can count twice, increasing this by at most one. A repeated raw line uses the ordinary skeleton described above without any additional cost. If a shared gate with no raw-line residents becomes a pure point expression, compute it once on its own point leaves. Replacing its two uses by two point occurrences costs at most one net additional point occurrence after its construction.

For a shared gate D with raw-line residents, the upstream tree sees each resident once and all effects of a resident line go through D. At every retained singleton on a resident line, D is false with zero lines and true on its incident positive. Pinning D to one makes its exterior accept that singleton with all lines zero. D is false at every retained zero-line singleton p: choose an N negative containing p and meeting a resident line at q. If D accepted p with zero lines then on that negative D and the pinned exterior would be true by monotonicity. For at least two residents, apply the ordinary private-guard proof inside D. Choose a second resident U in every shared group on the T path, or any other resident if none are dropped. The extension meeting U also forces D false since its pinned exterior accepts the point on U. The conjunction therefore rejects p. In the exterior set both D occurrences to zero; this preserves outside positives, lowers negatives and allows ordinary private recovery there. The two regions use disjoint point leaves.

If D has just one resident Y, its upstream region can instead be lowered to Y AND G, with G the conjunction of its point AND sides. Its AND sides accept every incident retained Y singleton; other singleton positives have D=0 already. In the exterior unfold the two Y AND G uses, treating G as a repeated point occurrence, and apply the repeated-line argument. If pD of the P point leaves occur under D, preparing a nonconstant G costs at most pD−1, and two G occurrences replace those pD leaves. Net additional cost is at most one. For a constant G remove it. Consequently the seven signatures on retained points require at most P+1−7=5 new point gates for all one-surplus topologies. Restoring a removed point costs at most three ORs since no point is on more than three residual lines. Thus the original point subcircuit had at least 21−8=13 gates. Together with 19 mixed and at least two line gates this excludes total 33.

## Nonconcurrent triples

For the remaining 29 triples, the row with T active, p outside T, and a hit on U is always available when T belongs to S or U is outside S. We use this directional condition. Unless specified otherwise no point is removed.

In an ordinary tree or ordinary skeleton, for T in S use its usual private guards whose only resident ordinary line is T. For T outside S keep every guard whose only resident **outside-S ordinary line** is T. These groups remain disjoint over the two kinds of targets. For T outside S all dropped guards contain a common other outside-S ordinary U. If no guard is dropped, no such U needs to be active; the existence of any N negative with T active containing p suffices. The verifier provides this using a different outside-S line, whether or not it is resident in the tree. For T in S any common other resident line in the dropped groups is usable. This recovers exact singleton signatures; the same argument applies if a point boundary is repeated or a pure point subtree is shared.

For an upstream region with at least two resident lines, D vanishes at every zero-line point singleton, because for any p an N negative contains p and a point from at least one of any two specified residents. This suffices for the pinned-exterior argument. Inside D, when T belongs to S, use any permitted resident witness U. For T outside S the directional argument works if there is another outside-S resident that can activate the pinned exterior. Thus at most one target can fail inside D, when D has exactly one outside-S resident. Omit it and compute that single star directly from four raw point bits for three gates. Even in this case there are six disjoint private expressions across D and its clamped exterior, costing at most P−6 before the direct construction. The total augmentation is at most P−6+3=8.

For a repeated raw label, or a shared mixed D with sole resident Y outside S, omit Y and compute it directly. In the mixed case D has empty zero-line singleton signature, as verified for all outside-S Y. Set D (or the raw Y slots) to zero, retaining the six outside positives. The directional ordinary extraction costs at most P−6, giving the same total bound. If the repeated label Y belongs to S, use the repeated-label skeleton. Its special OR-side test needs an N negative with Y active and a hit on a chosen ordinary resident; the directional condition imposes no restriction on that ordinary witness because Y belongs to S. For a shared mixed D with sole resident Y in S, its zero-line singleton proof may fail at just one point c. Project out this point and normalize D to Y AND G as above. The verifier checks the directional singleton extensions with the witness required to meet the punctured line, all split pairs, and the empty zero-line singleton signature condition for this Y after projection. The seven expressions then cost at most five additional gates before up to three restoration ORs. Thus every nonconcurrent triple requires at least 13 original point gates; with at least two line gates and 19 mixed gates this is at least 34.

Run `python verify_clamp.py` in this directory to verify the restricted-negative conditions on the pinned original theorem. The seven-target singleton point-gate lower bound is a separate prerequisite.
