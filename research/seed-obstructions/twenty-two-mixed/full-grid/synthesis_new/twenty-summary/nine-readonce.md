# Nine-line read-once exclusion independent of point width

Let P be the number of point boundary leaf occurrences in a read-once mixed tree with nine line inputs. Its mixed cost is P+8. Under total size at most 33 the earlier point-width exclusions imply P≥11. The 7,140 ordinary and 24 missing-singleton distinct banks are classified in syn_bounds/twenty/read-once-nine.md, including its cost-six replay. Every ordinary arrangement has either seven reconstructed target signatures with extra point cost at most P−7+c+2, where c is the line cost, or four disjoint pair-safe literal point groups. The first branch yields total at least 36+c−(c+2)=34. Missing-singleton arrangements also yield four groups and cost at least three line gates. In every distinct case c≥2.

For four disjoint groups, the pure point minimum at widths 11 through 17 is bounded below respectively by 14,12,11,10,9,8,7. These are exhaustive cutoffs, not matching upper bound claims. The checks are in syn_quotients/fourgroups14, syn_bounds/twenty/four-private-twelve, syn_quotients/fourgroups11_w13.zip and syn_quotients/diagonal_fourgroups.zip. Thus pure point plus P is at least 24, sufficient after adding eight mixed-offset gates and two pure line gates.

For P≥18 each of the four group-bearing wires picked from distinct groups accepts all four points of its target. Each such wire must be a distinct paid gate since all free point masks are transversal to that target. The earliest of these gates has at least two paid ancestors, as four singleton-positive points of one target require at least four raw leaves, each raw leaf meeting the target at most once; a circuit of one or two binary gates depends on at most three distinct raw leaves. This supplies at least six paid gates and P+6≥24.

A null line wire can be pinned to zero after replacing the genuine line functions by singleton disjunctions. Read-once simplification leaves ≤8 occurrences and at least seven distinct signatures by the raw line coverage bound. Similarly duplicate signatures among nine original inputs can be identified, giving seven distinct singleton signatures with at most two surplus reads or eight signatures with at most one. At width eight the genuine extra disjunction over s raw labels needs s−1 gates, no more than the original line part which computes that singleton trace. The P-independent width-seven and width-eight reconstruction theorems then apply. These reductions also cover mixed trees whose point width shrinks during pruning.

For the large-width argument one may also choose four distinct physical representatives if groups were specified as sets of formal occurrences. Regard each private conjunction as a set of underlying point-wire IDs. These four sets are pairwise incomparable by inclusion since their four-point singleton targets are distinct and incomparable. Every subfamily of r≤4 sets has union size at least r: an antichain on at most r−1 elements has size below r for these r. Hall's theorem then selects different representatives.
