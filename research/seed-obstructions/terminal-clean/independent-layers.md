# An independent layer of dirty mixed gates

Let a terminal-clean separator have k terminal ANDs, g pure gates and d dirty mixed gates with no ancestor relation between distinct dirty mixed gates. Its size is s=g+d+2k−1. Write a mixed AND as A∧B and a mixed OR as C∨D, with the point operand first. Regard each pure operand as its support on singleton-side coordinates. All replacements below preserve covered positive incidences and only shrink outputs on negatives. A singleton-side clamp to raw p is valid since its original operand accepts p alone and is monotone.

Each terminal can be replaced by ordinary clean rectangles. Pure–pure yields one rectangle directly. A mixed OR with a pure filter leaves only the opposite-side arm: a same-side product accepting any singleton would accept a forbidden negative extending it. Two mixed ORs with positive-bearing operands on both point sides cannot yield a clean product because a negative extends any two singleton points. An operand with empty singleton support can be discarded from an OR when preserving positive rows, or makes an AND's positive support empty. The same observations allow direct pure replacements for degenerate ORs.

An AND with a pure filter or two ANDs combined gives a single rectangle whose point and line factors are each intersections of at most two pure signals. If nonempty, the rectangle has a singleton side by absence of a four-cycle. Clamp this side to its raw singleton and construct the other intersection with at most one pure gate.

A mixed AND times a mixed OR gives two rectangles (A∩C)×B and A×(B∩D). If nested or one empty, retain the containing product, costing at most one extra pure gate. Otherwise A and B have size at least two: if A has size one then a nonempty first rectangle contains the second, and symmetrically for B. The two intersections must now be singletons by absence of a four-cycle. Clamp to those two raw centers, obtaining two rectangles with zero additional pure gates.

Let a count terminals requiring one added pure gate and b count terminals yielding two rectangles. Put r=k−a−b, including discarded empty terminals. The equivalent pure-prefix construction uses at most g+a pure gates and at most k+b rectangles. Applying the sharp pure charge g'≥28−k' from ../mixed/rectangle-dag.md gives g≥28−k−a−b. Consequently s≥27+d+r. In particular s≤33 implies d≤6 and at most 6−d terminals can be replaced at no additional pure gate or rectangle cost.

## Globally minimum separators

The terminal ANDs in a globally minimum separator form a matching on their input wires. If two terminals share H, factor their disjunction as H∧(a∨b). A final OR tree can be reassociated freely, and this factorization saves a gate even if a,b,H have other uses. When the whole dirty mixed layer is independent, each terminal requiring one extra pure gate uses at least one dirty mixed input. Each terminal splitting into two rectangles uses two dirty mixed inputs. Matching implies a+2b≤d. The converted rectangle circuit therefore costs at most g+a+2(k+b)−1≤s, and the known rectangle lower bound gives s≥34. Consequently any globally minimum separator of size at most 33 in terminal-clean form has two dirty mixed gates joined by a directed path. The earlier d+r≤6 bound holds even without assuming global minimality.

## Isolated dirty mixed gates

Among minimum-total terminal-clean circuits choose one minimizing the count of dirty mixed gates. Call a dirty mixed gate isolated if it has neither a dirty mixed ancestor nor a dirty mixed descendant. It has pure parents and no dirty consumers, so by terminal matching it has exactly one terminal consumer. If this terminal has a pure partner or a second isolated dirty mixed partner, apply the per-terminal conversion above. Each extra pure gate is paid for by at least one removed dirty mixed input, and a split is paid for by two removed inputs. A useful new pure intermediate accepts a singleton on its side and therefore some negative. The replacement remains terminal-clean, does not increase total size, and strictly reduces dirty mixed count, a contradiction.

Every isolated gate is therefore paired at its terminal with a nonisolated dirty mixed gate. The matching makes this pairing injective. If d dirty mixed gates have e internal wires between them, there are at most 2e nonisolated gates, since a syntactically mixed gate cannot feed a syntactically pure gate. Consequently d≤4e. A single wire between dirty mixed gates forces d≤4. This uses the minimum-dirty-mixed convention after minimum total.
