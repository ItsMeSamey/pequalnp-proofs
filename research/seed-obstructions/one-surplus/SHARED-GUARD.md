# A sole repeated line-dominated signal cannot yield size 33

Assume the mixed output cone has seven distinct line-side boundary signals and one surplus read. Replace the seven line signals by their forced raw singleton bits. This preserves positives and rejection of negatives. Suppose the repeated signal S is a raw line or a mixed signal dominated by one raw line T. For the latter assumption the following weaker condition suffices: S is false at every residual positive whose line coordinate differs from T.

If S is a raw line, the known repeated-line formula bound is 35 gates. Otherwise the inputs upstream of S form a tree with no alternate outgoing connections, since S is the only surplus read. If the upstream tree contained any raw U other than T, that U could affect the output only through S. At an incident positive (p,U), S is zero by assumption. Monotonicity makes it zero also at (p,0), and all remaining boundary inputs coincide there. The output would give the same answer at (p,U) and (p,0), contradicting acceptance of the former and domination of the latter by a negative. Thus T is the only raw line in this subtree. The same distinguishability argument at (p,T) shows S is true there for each of the four incident p.

Let Q be S with T set to one. The replacement T AND Q is pointwise at most S by monotonicity (indeed equality holds when S globally implies T). It agrees with S on the required positives, so it preserves separation. If the subtree has t point-leaf occurrences, it contains t mixed gates. Setting T to one and simplifying constants computes Q with at most t−1 point-side gates, since it is a formula on t point leaves. Build Q once, replace the two consumers of S with separate gates T AND Q, and delete S's upstream mixed subtree. The transformed circuit has at most one extra gate. If Q is constant one, use T without the two gates; Q cannot be zero.

The transformed mixed part is a tree with one repeated raw line. Its point circuit can be shared arbitrarily, including the two occurrences of Q, since the repeated-line theorem counts point leaf occurrences. That theorem gives transformed size at least 35, so the original circuit has size at least 34. This applies to eleven point and seven line boundaries in particular.
