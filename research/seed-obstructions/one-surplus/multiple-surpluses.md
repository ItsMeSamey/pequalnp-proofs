# A private-guard bound with several repeated wires

Consider a connected mixed cone with seven singleton line boundary signals. Count k operand uses beyond a read-once tree. Let b be the number of distinct non-output wires, including boundary inputs, with fanout at least two. The sum of their fanouts is k+b, and b≤k.

Cut each repeated wire immediately after its gate (or after the input itself for a repeated boundary wire). This gives b+1 tree regions with one endpoint each, the repeated wire endpoints and the circuit output. An original input leaf belongs to the region at its first downstream fanout, or the root. Every path from that input to the original output goes through this endpoint. A repeated input may itself be the only node in a region. A region treats each incoming occurrence from a preceding region as its own formal leaf.

Fix a region H with e resident original line leaves. At each incoming formal leaf substitute the pure point OR of the singleton signature of the supplying wire at all original lines zero. This decreases the supplying function on all inputs. It agrees on point singletons when all external lines are zero and a resident line alone is active, since no resident line has any direct route to a preceding repeated endpoint. In particular it agrees when all lines are zero. For a point p on any resident T, the original root rejects all lines zero at p and accepts just T. All effects of T go through H, so H has values zero and one in these two situations. The substituted region is good on all its resident lines.

The root region after substitution separates its resident positives and rejects all original negatives. The ordinary read-once guard argument extracts a pair-safe private guard conjunction for every resident line. For nonroot H with e≥2, choose a second resident U under every shared guard on the T path, or any resident U distinct from T in the absence of such guards. At a positive for any point on U, the exterior context with H forced to one accepts with all lines visible outside H zero. On a raw negative extending T, an outside pair and a point on U, the exterior context with H forced to one still accepts by monotonicity. Thus the actual H on that negative is zero. Substitution of point functions and removal of ignorable OR leaves only decrease H. The resident private guards therefore form a pair-safe conjunction. A nonroot region with e=1 is counted as one possible lost target; a region with e=0 loses none.

Across all regions there are k+b incoming occurrences of repeated wires. Every maximal pure point guard other than an original point boundary leaf must contain at least one of these positions, since original internal gates in the mixed cone were all mixed. Private guard sets of distinct line labels and different regions are disjoint. Each contaminated conjunction can be charged to a distinct occurrence. If s of the b nonroot regions have just one resident line, at least 7−(k+b)−s target masks are intersections of original point masks, with private groups rejecting all exterior pairs. In particular the count is at least 7−3k.
