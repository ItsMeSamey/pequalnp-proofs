# Two shared endpoints with one and at least two resident lines

Assume two repeated endpoints of fanout two, one D with sole resident line T and another H with e≥2 resident lines. Their regions partition the original P point leaves. The circuit needs at least 34 total gates.

If H has no incoming repeated supplier, extract its e targets inside H by the pinned-exterior argument in shared-subtree.md, using at most pH−e point gates. Clamp H to zero. Its all-lines-zero value vanishes at point singletons, and its own raw lines are inactive on outside positives. Outside, D has a single resident T with point signals and constants. Normalize D=T AND G, with pD point leaves computing G in at most pD−1 gates when nonconstant. The exterior tree has 6−e ordinary raw labels and up to two guarded T copies. Clamp T to zero and extract ordinary targets from private AND sides. Factor G once outside the T cofactor of the disjunction of OR sides. repeated-one-label.md gives disjoint point accounting, and guard preparation cancels its single introduced occurrence. With one ordinary label there are no shared guards and its entire path is private. With none, use G AND exterior(T=1). Raw D or constant guards cause no extra cost. The seven outputs use at most P−7 additional gates.

Otherwise D supplies H. D is earliest, so normalize it to T AND G as above. The e resident lines of H and the 6−e exterior ordinary lines are disjoint. At T=0, D=0. Since each line resident at H is blocked by H, H vanishes on zero-line point singletons by the zero-line lemma. On H's own positive singletons H must be true. Recover its e ordinary targets with private AND sides by the pinned-exterior argument. Recover the exterior ordinary targets with D,H clamped zero, also using private AND sides. If the exterior has one ordinary target it has no shared guard to remove. If it has none omit this step.

Let u count all point leaves below D and those in OR sides in either ordinary skeleton (all exterior point leaves when there is no exterior ordinary line). The six ordinary targets use disjoint point occurrences outside these u, so they cost at most P−u−6. If u≥2 the raw disjunction of the four points on T needs only three more, giving at most P−5 new point gates.

If u≤1 let Y be the disjunction of H's ordinary-skeleton OR sides evaluated at D=G. Let F be the disjunction of the exterior ordinary-skeleton OR sides at D=G and H=Y, or the whole exterior expression evaluated there if it has no ordinary lines. Every incident T singleton passes F: its ordinary leaves are false, so an accepting trace reaches an OR side in the exterior; any true H likewise reaches an OR side of its own.

If F accepts an exterior pair X, fix a passing exterior OR side, or the exterior expression itself when there are no exterior ordinary leaves. In the former case choose an ordinary V resident beneath the attachment. If Y(X)=1 choose a passing H-internal OR side and an ordinary U resident beneath it. A sampled T-active negative extends X and meets both prescribed neighborhoods when present. On this negative the real D dominates G(X), and each AND ancestor of the chosen inner side passes via U using its clamped guard. Thus H is at least Y(X). The selected exterior side is true on the negative and its ancestor AND guards pass via V. With no exterior ordinary labels the entire exterior already passes. This contradiction proves F is pair-safe. Its only point dependencies are the u counted leaves; for u≤1 a nonconstant monotone function is an existing point wire. This again gives the P−5 augmentation bound.

The original P+8 mixed gates and 21-gate point lower bound yield at least 34. The two-neighborhood extensions are checked in the original repeated-line raw witness computation.
