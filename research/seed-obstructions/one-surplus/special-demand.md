# A shared line-free region must feed both reconstructions

Consider the one-special-resident arbitrary-surplus proof, allowing arbitrary line-free nonroots. First construct the six ordinary private outputs as in aggregate.md with line-bearing region inputs pinned to zero and with the recursive zero-line cofactors of line-free regions. Independently construct the T envelope by retaining the special region, the root if free of ordinary lines, the OR sides at ordinary regions, and the intact tree at each demanded line-free region. Unused regional outputs can be discarded. At a line-free region independent of T its zero-line cofactor suffices for the T tests, as in special-independent-empty.md. An empty-baseline line-free region needs no ordinary computation.

Assume that every remaining line-free region with nonempty singleton baseline reachable from T is demanded by at most one of the ordinary and special reconstructions, after these substitutions and recursive demand propagation. The total lower bound of 34 follows: use a single output per line-free region, either its zero-line point cofactor or its T envelope, or their common cofactor in the T-independent case. Each original leaf occurrence is used at most once across the retained regional definitions, and each intermediate signal has at most its original number of reads. The seven-output count in single-special-any-surplus.md applies. Thus an unresolved architecture must contain a T-dependent line-free nonroot with nonzero baseline demanded on both sides.
