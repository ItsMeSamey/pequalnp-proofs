# Two ordinary private uses of a line-free wire and five special features

Let T,U be the singleton-resident nonroot lines and H the line-free nonroot in the P11 seven-line case at 20 mixed gates. All have fanout two. Suppose H's occurrences appear in two different ordinary private expressions. Neither H nor its original point leaves enters the T,U envelopes. The ordinary expressions use H's zero-line cofactor Q, obtained by pinning incoming line-resident suppliers zero. An empty Q is covered by the two-arm proof without sharing.

Let b count envelope point leaves, disjoint from the five private subtrees and H's leaves. Computing Q once and using it twice costs at most one extra read in the ordinary-output accounting, so these cost at most 11−b−4. The tightened two-arm certificate supplies both special outputs in at most b gates for b≤4; for b≥6 two raw unions suffice. Thus only b=5 requires consideration.

Let h≥1 be the count of original point leaves of H used to build nonempty Q. The five ordinary expressions have at most 6−h original point occurrences outside H and two Q occurrences. If either Q occurrence is discarded by simplification, at most one private expression demands it and the one-promotion or zero-demand result applies. Otherwise both are counted. Five nonconstant expressions need at least one leaf each.

If h≥3 at most five ordinary occurrences remain; both distinct Q-using targets would be Q alone. If h=2 there are at most six occurrences, so one Q group is singleton Q with target X, the other has at most two inputs Q,z. Distinct four-point Y is incomparable with X, so its two-input function can only be z, not AND, OR, or Q. The three remaining groups are original singleton wires, giving four distinct exact original masks.

If h=1, Q equals its sole point wire v. If one Q group is singleton, v is an exact star X. If the other group uses three leaves, the other three ordinary groups are singleton and complete a quartet with v. If it uses two leaves (v,z), its target is z by incomparability. At least two other groups are singleton, again giving four exact original masks. Two singleton Q groups are impossible. Otherwise both Q groups use exactly two inputs and the three remaining groups are singleton exact masks. Write the two input pairs (v,z₁),(v,z₂). A projection gives a fourth exact mask. A pair-safe AND requires some operand to have the exact target mask: if both were strict supersets, choose an exterior point from each, extending by a distinct exterior point if necessary. Both operands would accept this exterior pair. Thus both groups must be ORs. The four-exact cases are impossible by the width-eleven fourteen-point-gate bound.

Appending v OR z₁ and v OR z₂ to the original point circuit computes five pair-safe stars with at most fifteen gates. This meets the five-star equality case of the pair-safety gate-charge theorem (../twenty-mixed/five-star-equality.md). Every paid mask is contained in one of the five ordinary stars. Free point signals are transversal to every star, so every special-feature mask meets each of T,U in at most one point.

For either T or U its four positive singleton valuations on the five features have nonempty disjoint supports. At least three have unit support. If such a unit feature held at an exterior singleton, the monotone exact envelope would accept it. Thus it is contained in its star, and transversality makes it the original singleton point. Across both stars this gives at least five distinct singleton features since their intersection has one point. Five singleton features cannot supply nonzero valuations on every point of the seven-point union.

A separate b=5 proof uses ../twenty-mixed/five-features. For each special pair the negative tests with all five ordinary neighborhoods force at least one target to be reconstructible on singleton signatures in at most two gates from its five features and the original raw singleton points. The other target costs three raw unions. This proof does not use the ordinary-private arity cases or the equality characterization.
