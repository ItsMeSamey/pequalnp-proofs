# One special line, an ordinary repeated region and a line-free region

At P=11, L=7 and k=3 suppose the three repeated endpoints carry one, e≥2 and zero resident original lines. Each has fanout two. Call the singleton-resident line T and the line-free endpoint H. The root and the e-resident region contribute six disjoint pair-safe ordinary private expressions under the substitutions in aggregate.md. They pin line-bearing suppliers to zero and use H's zero-line point cofactor Q. Computing Q requires only original point leaves in H, since all its possible upstream suppliers have resident lines and hence empty zero-line singleton baseline.

Count ordinary private expressions using Q. If none, the ordinary reconstruction can discard Q. If two distinct expressions use Q, both H occurrences are in ordinary private AND sides and neither enters the T envelope. In either case special-demand.md gives the 34-gate lower bound.

If exactly one ordinary expression uses Q, call its target X. Allocate to the five other ordinary private expressions all original point leaves in their disjoint retained subtrees; leaves elsewhere, including those inside H, form a common bank of b point features. The five expressions cost at most 11−b−5 gates. Both X's extracted expression and the T envelope are monotone functions of only these b features. X uses its own point leaves and Q, with other formal inputs pinned zero. The T envelope keeps the line-free H tree but drops every ordinary private AND side, including all five allocated subtrees. The X expression accepts its four positive points and rejects every exterior pair. The T envelope accepts its four positive points, and any T-active negative meeting all six ordinary neighborhoods forbids acceptance at every feature valuation it dominates.

Apply the two-arm certificate in ../twenty-mixed/two-arms to these T and X signals. At least two distinct X-exclusive q for each T-exterior p admit T-active negatives containing p and q and meeting the five lines other than T,X. Such a q lies in X, so these negatives meet all six ordinary lines. Conversely for an X-exterior p and any T-exclusive q, both p and q are exterior to X. Pair-safety forbids X's acceptance at the featurewise union of their valuations. If p=q, extend by another exterior point. These are exactly the marker soundness conditions for the two-output replacement, supplying both exact singleton signatures in at most b+1 gates. For b≥5 two direct raw unions suffice. The five other outputs cost at most 11−b−5, for at most seven newly appended gates. The seven signatures need 21 point gates, so the original needs fourteen paid point gates and twenty mixed gates.
