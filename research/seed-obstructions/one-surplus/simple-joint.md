# Parallel singleton regions with simple joint attachments

Suppose exactly two repeated endpoints, both of fanout two, have disjoint upstream regions and one resident line T and U respectively. Each normalizes to T AND G_T and U AND G_U as in README.md. Let pT, pU, pC count original point leaves in these two regions and the exterior tree, with P=pT+pU+pC. The two guards use at most pT+pU−2 new point gates and accept all four singletons incident to their own lines.

In the normalized exterior skeleton on the five ordinary lines, assume every OR attachment containing both exceptional labels has exactly one occurrence of each. All other attachments are unrestricted. Then the original circuit has at least 34 gates.

Reconstruct ordinary targets as in separated-repeats.md. At special T factor G_T outside its entire OR: in the exterior side formulas replace all guarded T occurrences by the raw bit T, set U to zero, and take the T-cofactor. At all points where G_T=1 this cofactor is the original one. Where G_T=0 the final AND is zero. Thus exterior-pair rejection remains valid, and all incident positives have G_T=1. Similarly factor G_U for U. Both guards are each used once.

Any OR-side formula B clamped at both raw bits zero rejects every point pair X. Choose an ordinary V resident at its attachment. A sampled negative extends X and meets V (check verify-joint.py). If B₀₀(X) were true, every ancestor AND guard would be true on that negative and all other ancestors would pass B, a contradiction.

For a joint B with one leaf of each special, assign to it the special incident singletons it needs to accept. If it contributes only to one target retain its one cofactor. Otherwise choose assigned T-positive p and U-positive q. Within B the ancestors of the least common ancestor of T and U have only point sides. Their AND guards are true on all assigned points, because any true bypass through a point OR without the exceptional leaf would make B₀₀ true. If the least common ancestor were AND, its U branch clamped to zero would be true at p and its T branch clamped to zero would be true at q. Both branches would then be true on the zero-line pair {p,q} and so would B₀₀, a contradiction. The least common ancestor is OR.

For each assigned T-positive all point AND guards on the T-to-root path within B must be true: any accepting derivation bypassing the T leaf would accept with both bits zero. The conjunction of these guards suffices for B acceptance when T is active, because the branching gate is OR. Use it as a replacement cofactor. The same holds for U. The two conjunctions share only the AND guards above the branching gate. Build the common conjunction once and use it in both expressions. This adds at most one gate compared to charging disjoint point occurrences: c≥1 common leaves produce a shared wire at cost c−1 and create two occurrences in the final expressions. Empty products simplify.

There are at most two joint attachments as each needs a T and a U leaf. The seven nonconstant outputs therefore require at most pC+2−7+2 new point gates in addition to the pT+pU−2 guard-production cost. This totals at most P−5. The original mixed circuit has P+8 gates, while the seven point targets need 21 point gates. Total ≥21−(P−5)+P+8=34.

## A joint attachment with one T and two U occurrences

Here there is just one joint B. All occurrences of U are in B. Other side formulas can be assigned to their unique exceptional label. Let A be the T-positive point singletons assigned to B. If A contains at most one point of T outside U then |A|≤2, since the neighborhoods meet in at most one point. Give B entirely to the U cofactor and supply T with the disjunction of those at most two raw singleton point inputs as an additional term. This accepts exactly the desired singleton fragment and is safe for every T-exterior pair. The two additional leaf occurrences fit the pC+2−7+2 bound.

Otherwise fix two points q1,q2 in A outside U. Inside B follow the unique T-leaf path: at each gate its side formula contains only points and possibly copies of U. The zero-U cofactors of all AND sides accept A, because B₀₀ vanishes on point singletons and bypassing the T input at an OR would otherwise accept the same singleton at zero lines. The conjunction of these AND cofactors passes the assigned positives, and implies B acceptance at raw T with U=0. It is a valid smaller T cofactor.

For U take the disjunction of the U=1 cofactors of the OR sides on this inner path. All U positives pass B with T=0, so must pass one of these sides. If an inner OR cofactor accepts X outside U with G_U(X)=1, choose a sampled U-active negative extending X, meeting {q1,q2} and meeting an ordinary V resident at the outer B attachment. At least one q is present, so every inner ancestor AND side is active via its zero-U cofactor. The exceptional U leaf gates activate via G_U(X). All outer ancestor ANDs activate via V, giving forbidden root acceptance. The disjunction hence is safe after its factored guard. The OR and AND sides used by the two outputs are disjoint. This excludes 1+2 and, by symmetry, 2+1. The outstanding parallel shape has all four exceptional occurrences in one OR attachment.
