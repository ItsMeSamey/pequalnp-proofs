# One raw line of arbitrary multiplicity

Consider a formula on P point signal occurrences, six once-occurring ordinary raw line bits and r≥1 occurrences of the seventh raw bit T. For a separator of the residual sample, the original straight-line circuit that computes the point signals, together with the formula gates, has at least 33+r gates.

Keep the skeleton formed by the paths from the six ordinary leaves to the root. A skeleton split has two ordinary-containing children. At an attachment the other child is a side formula containing only point leaves and copies of T. Write G for side formulas at AND attachments and B for those at OR attachments. Throughout, a subscript 0 or 1 fixes every T occurrence in the side formula.

With T=0, each skeleton region accepts all resident ordinary incident singleton inputs and rejects all zero-line point sets of size at most two that contain a point on a resident line. These properties hold at the root. At a split, choose a point on a resident line of each child. If the split were AND, acceptance of each positive would force the opposite child true at its point with all lines zero. Both would then be true on their union, contrary to rejection. Every split is therefore OR. Each child inherits rejection and inherits its own positives since the opposite child is false there with its own lines zero. At an OR attachment the same reasoning applies. At an AND attachment G₀ accepts every singleton on every resident ordinary line, so rejection of the relevant zero-line pairs and acceptance of positives propagate into the skeleton child. This establishes the properties inductively.

For an ordinary L, form H_L as the conjunction of G₀ from the AND attachments with just L resident. It is true on every singleton on L. Shared AND guards on the L path have nested resident sets and therefore all share an ordinary U different from L. If there are none, choose any other ordinary U. For any exterior pair X outside L the sample has a negative with L active, containing X and meeting U. On that negative every shared G is true by monotonicity and the U singleton property. If H_L were true on X the L leaf would pass through every attachment and split, a contradiction. This proves exterior-pair safety.

For T form H_T as the disjunction of B₁ across all OR attachments. On an incident T singleton, all ordinary leaves are false. Trace the true root downward, choosing a true child at a split and continuing into the skeleton child at an AND attachment. At some OR attachment a true B₁ must be available, proving acceptance. For each B choose an ordinary U resident at that attachment. If B₁ accepts an exterior pair X outside T, a sampled negative with T active containing X and meeting U activates B and every ancestor AND guard, as U is resident at each of them. This forces root acceptance. Hence H_T rejects all exterior pairs as well.

The seven nonconstant target expressions above use disjoint point-leaf occurrences. Their side formulas are disjoint, each is used at most once, and constant leaves can be simplified after T is fixed. Thus they take at most P−7 additional binary point gates in total. The seven pair-safe neighborhoods need 21 point gates, so the original point subcircuit has at least 28−P. The mixed formula has P+5+r gates. This proves the claim.

With only one repeated wire D of fanout r the case of a nonroot mixed D with exactly one resident raw line follows by the normalization in README.md. Normalize D to T∧G and give each use its own copy of the final AND at a cost increase at most r−1. The formula lower bound then gives original cost ≥34. Pure point repeated leaves are counted as occurrences in the ordinary tree. A raw line repeated r times is covered directly. All other resident-line counts are covered by aggregate.md.
