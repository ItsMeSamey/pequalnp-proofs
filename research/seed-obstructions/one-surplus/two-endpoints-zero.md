# One special resident and one dependent line-free region

At P=11, seven raw line labels and k=3, suppose exactly two repeated endpoints: D with sole resident T, and H with no resident lines, reachable from D. Six ordinary lines reside at the root. Repeated point inputs may be unfolded into the excluded case with at most two surplus uses.

The fanouts sum to five. If u≥1 D occurrences feed H, at most four D or H occurrences enter the root. Pin D to zero and H to its zero-line cofactor for ordinary extraction. Six ordinary lines have disjoint pair-safe private expressions. A private conjunction whose side trees contain no D or H occurrence is an AND of original point boundary wires: a nontrivial tree made entirely of pure point inputs could not be an original mixed gate. Four such private expressions suffice by the width-eleven fourteen-gate bound.

At least one D or H occurrence lies inside a root ordinary-skeleton OR side. Every true T-positive with all ordinary leaves zero needs a true OR side, by propagating upward from the false resident leaves. An OR side true on a singleton p with all lines zero is impossible: select ordinary V below the attachment and a forbidden zero-line extension containing p and a point q on V. Every ancestor split is OR, and each ancestor AND guard passes q on the V-positive assignment because V is pivotal on q. Each such guard is independent of the unique V leaf, so passes on the extension. Thus OR sides without D,H cannot pass on T-positives.

Unless four ordinary groups are untouched, three distinct ordinary private expressions each contain at least one formal occurrence and an OR side contains a fourth. Thus each contains exactly one, with no further occurrence available, and u=1. The sole OR side depending on T contains just one formal leaf (D or H) and original point wires. D contains T and point wires. If H lies on the path, H contains one D leaf and point wires. All three trees are combs along their distinguished leaf: every off-path subtree contains pure point inputs and must be a single boundary wire. On each T-positive the distinguished OR side is false with all lines zero and true at T active. Every AND sibling on the distinguished path from T through to this OR side therefore passes on this point. Conversely if T is active and all these literal siblings pass, the OR side passes irrespective of the OR siblings.

Their conjunction accepts all T points and rejects every pair X of T-exterior points. Choose a T-active negative containing X and meeting an ordinary resident V below the OR attachment. Its point q on V activates all ancestor AND guards independently of V as above. If the conjunction accepted X then the side and hence root would accept the negative. The single-neighborhood extension is included in the earlier repeated-line checks. This fourth literal group uses point leaves from D, possibly H, and the OR side, disjoint from all three untouched ordinary groups. Therefore the point side needs fourteen paid gates.

The proof holds with any number B−1 of line-free nonroots, provided D is the only nonroot with a resident line and k=3. A line-free mixed region must have at least one formal input (a repeated point boundary can instead be unfolded). Thus it traces back to D, the unique possible initial mixed region, and D is earliest. The total nonroot fanouts sum to B+k, so at most B+3−(B−1)=4 formal occurrences can enter the root. Equality in the critical case forces every line-free nonroot to have just one incoming formal occurrence and the root OR side to have just one. The path to its formal leaf consequently traces uniquely back through a sequence of single-input combs to T. The same literal qualifier completes the proof.
