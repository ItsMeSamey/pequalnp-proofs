# Seven line boundary wires and exactly one surplus operand use require at least 34 residual gates

Use the connected output cone of mixed gates. Suppose it has P pure point boundary wires, seven pure line boundary wires, and one use beyond a tree, hence P+7 mixed gates. The seven line signatures are raw singletons by the packing theorem. Lower each to its raw input bit without increasing any negative or losing a positive.

The cone has one repeated wire D with two downstream occurrences. A repeated point boundary wire gives a read-once cone on P+1 point leaf occurrences and seven line leaves with the same point subcircuit. The seven-line guard theorem gives at least 34. A repeated line boundary wire gives the repeated-singleton-line tree bound of 35. Otherwise D is mixed and its upstream tree contains at least one resident raw line T. Every path from T to the output passes through D.

**Zero-line lemma.** For every point singleton p, D(p,0)=0. Pick any q incident to T. At the positive (q,T) the root accepts. Set both D occurrences to one in the exterior context; it still accepts by monotonicity. Now change T to zero. Exterior input values are unchanged, since T has no path to the exterior avoiding D. Thus the pinned exterior accepts q with all lines zero. If D(p,0)=1, D is one on the union of p and q with zero lines, and the exterior accepts that union. This contradicts an original negative. Every one- or two-point set with all seven lines zero is dominated by a sampled negative. verify.py checks all 91 possibilities against the original 676 rows and the accompanying row indices.

If D has at least two resident lines, shared-subtree.md applies with S empty. Inside D, extract each resident line's four-point target using only its original private point guards; the pinned exterior accepts every singleton on a second resident line, which supplies the outside-pair witness. Set both D positions to zero for the outside lines. Their positives are preserved by the lemma and all negatives decrease. Outside guards use disjoint original point leaf occurrences. Collapsing point-only subtrees after constant simplification costs at most one less gate than their leaf count. The seven targets are recovered using at most P−7 additional point gates. They require at least 21. Including the P+7 original mixed gates gives at least 35 total.

If D has only the resident line T, let H be its point cofactor at T=1. With t point leaves below D, this cofactor needs at most t−1 gates by constant simplification. Replacing D by T AND H decreases values and preserves every positive: on T-positives the cofactor agrees, and on other positives D was already zero. Compute H once and duplicate the T AND H at D's two occurrences. Compared to the t mixed gates in the old D tree, this uses at most t+1 gates. The new mixed tree has precisely one repeated raw line T and all six other raw lines, with pure point signals allowed to repeat as occurrences. It requires at least 35 gates by the repeated-line theorem. The original therefore requires at least 34. A constant one cofactor removes the two new ANDs and makes the same comparison immediate.

The numerical dependencies are the preceding packing theorem, seven-target point-gate theorem and repeated-line tree bound. Together with the independent mixed-frontier exhaustive checks, this gives two proofs excluding eleven point wires and seven line wires at eighteen mixed gates.

An independent proof audit checked the shared-endpoint substitution, the point-gate count, the 91 zero-line witnesses and all 1,512 exterior-pair extensions used by the seven-line guard argument. Run `python3 verify.py` in this directory for the zero-line witnesses. The [multiple-surplus corollary](aggregate.md) requires a separate structural hypothesis on repeated endpoints.
