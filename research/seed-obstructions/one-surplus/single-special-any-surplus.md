# One singleton resident region, arbitrary surplus

Assume the seven original raw lines each feed a single source of the mixed DAG. Split the DAG at each repeated endpoint as in aggregate.md. Suppose exactly one nonroot region has one resident line, called T. Suppose every other nonroot region has at least two resident lines. The root can have any resident count. Under these hypotheses, the total circuit needs at least 34 gates.

For each of the other six lines extract a private point expression in its own region exactly as in aggregate.md, pinning incoming endpoint occurrences to zero. These expressions take their point leaves from the AND-side subtrees of the resident line's skeleton, on edges dedicated to this resident line. Distinct expressions use disjoint leaves. Removing constant-pinned subtrees cannot introduce new point leaves. A region with ordinary lines will be called ordinary.

Build a new point output for T across the regions in topological order. In T's own region retain the tree and set its raw line to one. In a root without resident lines retain its tree. In an ordinary region use the skeleton spanned by its resident lines and the region output, including the unary path above their common ancestor. For each unary OR node retain the subtree on its nonresident side. Output the OR of all retained subtrees, or zero if there are none. In all three cases substitute the new outputs of prior regions for their formal occurrences. No original point leaf used in the six private expressions lies in these OR sides.

With only T active the new outputs dominate the actual outputs. At an ordinary region every true trace must enter an OR side, since all resident leaves are false. The two intact region types preserve domination by monotonicity. Therefore the new T output covers every incident singleton.

For any exterior singleton p choose an original negative row n containing p with T active and with a point on every other line's neighborhood. The 63 choices exist by verify-all-neighbors.py. Inductively each region whose new output is true on p is actually true on n. In an ordinary region, the true OR side is actually true on n by monotonicity and induction. Every ancestor split on the path upwards is OR, by the zero-line pair argument on the ordinary resident skeleton (pin earlier endpoints zero). For an ancestor unary AND, choose any ordinary resident line V below its resident child. Choose a point q of n on V. At the V-positive singleton q the region must be true and the branch from V must propagate through this AND: earlier endpoints are independent of V, and pinning them zero preserves singleton behavior. Equivalently the region depends on V there, since its all-lines-zero baseline is false (for a root this follows by the forbidden zero-line singleton). Thus its side guard is already true with all previous endpoints pinned zero and point q, hence true on n. This proves propagation. In the two intact region types the conclusion follows directly by induction and monotonicity. A new T output accepting p would therefore accept n in the original circuit, a contradiction.

Count the private expressions and new T output as one multioutput point circuit. Each original point boundary leaf is read at most once before endpoint substitution, because the selected subtrees are disjoint within every region. A nonroot new endpoint that is retained can be read at most its original fanout d. If it is constant, simplify and discard it. Each nonconstant subtree with h remaining leaves costs at most h−1 gates. Sum over the six private expressions, the terminal T expression and every retained intermediate endpoint, counting a unary alias as one leaf and zero gates. Extra cost is at most P−7 plus the sum of d−1 over retained intermediates, hence at most P+k−7. Since all seven exact singleton neighborhood signatures need 21 point gates, the original paid point gates number at least 28−P−k. Adding P+6+k mixed gates yields 34.
