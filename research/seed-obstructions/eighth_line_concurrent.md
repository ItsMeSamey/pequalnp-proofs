# Two or three concurrent lines in the extra signature

Use the notation of eighth_line_good.md. Let the extra signature S consist of two or three distinct lines through c. Every singleton p other than c is omitted by some U in S, so F1 at p with zero raw lines rejects. All seven raw lines remain essential in F1. Each has some p other than c on which its activation changes the output from zero to one.

Regard the cofactor J1 of the extra-input region as a point leaf. Define goodness using only the incident points other than c. In addition, keep rejection of a zero-raw-lines point pair whenever both points belong to the complement of some U in S and at least one endpoint lies on a resident line away from c. This rejection holds at the root because the pair is covered by a sampled negative with U active. It propagates through a line-splitting OR. It also propagates through an AND with a point guard: at the resident endpoint the latter is true by goodness.

Suppose an AND separates two nonempty groups of raw lines. If there are L and K from its two respective groups and a U in S different from both, choose distinct points p in L outside U and q in K outside U. Neither is c. Acceptance at the two required incident singletons forces the K child to accept p with all raw lines zero and the L child to accept q with all raw lines zero. Both therefore accept the pair with zero raw lines, contrary to the propagated rejection. The good-tree induction now goes through all line-splitting ORs and all nodes joining a point leaf to a line-containing child, using p different from c. If S has three lines, this proves there is no line-splitting AND. If S has two lines, the first possible exceptional AND must have precisely the two lines of S as its residents, one in each child: if either group contained an additional line, some choice of L,K would admit U.

In the absence of such an exceptional AND, perform the group and private-guard analysis from eighth_line_good.md. It preserves every positive other than those at c by the same argument. For a raw line T outside S even its incident positive at c, if present, makes T pivotal in F0: the value with no raw lines is zero and the value with just T is one while the extra input remains zero. As the raw leaf T is used once, every AND sibling on its path is true at that positive. Thus every ordinary guard for a group containing T accepts its entire neighborhood, and if the group carrying J contains T then J0 accepts its entire neighborhood. For T in S these conclusions are needed only away from c.

To prove pair rejection for a private conjunction when dropping ordinary shared guards, use their common other line U punctured at c. When dropping the extra guard J, its other line V is also allowed to be punctured if T contains c. When T does not contain c it is outside S; then the only case requiring V is that a second V outside S shares the J group, and J0 covers V fully by pivotality. The numerical verifier covers both situations. Importantly, this rejection is a consequence for the original formula. The proof may first delete the point-only OR leaves as in eighth_line_good.md, since doing so lowers the original formula on all negatives and leaves its positives at all p different from c untouched. Ignore ordinary shared guards on the sampled witness because each is active there. The appropriate retained cofactor of J remains a lower bound for the actual J. This gives rejection of every outside pair independently of whether a positive at c survives the OR deletions.

The private conjunctions accept all singleton points of their respective neighborhoods except possibly c for T in S. Restore c for each such T by one final OR with the raw point c. Its outside-pair rejections survive since these pairs exclude c. We have built seven canonical pair-safe outputs using at most P−7+|S| further point gates. Hence the original point cost is at least 28−P−|S|. The tree uses P+7 mixed gates. A line signal containing |S| raw singleton lines depends on at least |S| distinct primitive line inputs and requires at least |S|−1 line gates. The total is at least 34.

Thus the three-line concurrent case is excluded, as is the two-line case unless the clamped tree has the AND join specified above.

## The two-line exceptional AND

Let the exceptional node have children B1 and B2 in F1, containing raw lines L1 and L2 respectively. Its ancestors satisfy the good-tree rules away from c. No further AND can split raw lines: above this node the two exceptional lines are together; in other branches they are absent; and below either child there is only one raw line. Above the exceptional node, every AND has a point guard covering both neighborhoods away from c, and ORs forward truth.

On each singleton p of L1 outside c the L1 positive forces the exceptional AND true. Hence B2 with L2 fixed to zero accepts p. For an outside pair X of L1 take a sampled negative with L1 active, containing X and meeting L2 away from c. The extra signal is true. B1 with L1 active is true: at each singleton of L2 away from c the L2-positive test had forced B1 true with L1 zero. All point guards above the exceptional AND are true for the same reason. If B2 with L2 fixed to zero accepted X, monotonicity would force the root true at this negative. Thus B2 with L2 zero rejects every outside pair of L1. Swapping the indices gives the other point output. These arguments use F1 before any OR deletion.

Each opposite-child zero cofactor is nonconstant and costs at most one fewer gate than its child's number of original point-leaf occurrences. Where the child contains the extra-input region, fix the extra bit to one. The two sets of point occurrences are disjoint. For each of the five other lines, the route from its unique raw leaf to the root avoids the exceptional AND. The private-guard argument applies on that route: at each branching OR, setting its other branch false gives a lower bound. If J is in the exceptional block it need not be computed again elsewhere; otherwise at most one other line needs a nonconstant J cofactor as above. All point-leaf occurrences used to compute the seven outputs are disjoint, including the inputs to any retained cofactor. The same bound P−7 additional gates therefore holds before restoring c at cost two. The total is at least 34.
