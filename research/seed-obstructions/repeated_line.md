# A read-once tree with one repeated raw line needs at least 35 gates

The mixed tree has seven distinct raw lines, one of them T used twice, and P point-leaf occurrences. A line signal with singleton signature {T} may be replaced by its raw bit; this preserves the positives and lowers negatives. Keep the point signals themselves. There are P+7 mixed gates. We show that the original point subcircuit produces seven pair-safe neighborhood outputs with at most P−7 further gates.

Two facts from the samples will be used. Every one- or two-point input with zero lines lies below a negative. For any target L and outside pair X a negative with L active can be chosen containing X and meeting each of two specified other target neighborhoods. The second fact includes the case of a single other neighborhood.

Use goodness as in read_once_guards.md, additionally propagating rejection of zero-line point pairs. At a line-splitting node whose two resident raw-line sets are disjoint, choose any positive point in one group and any positive point in the other group, say p and q. If the node were AND, the positive in each group forces the opposite child to be true on that point with its resident lines zero. The AND would accept the zero-line pair {p,q}. Such rejection propagates through OR and through an attached point AND guard because that guard is true on each assigned resident positive. Thus independent line groups always join by OR. A point-only OR leaf on the path of a pivotal positive is false there. A point-only AND guard is true on all assigned resident positives. A line subtree that evaluates true can be passed up through all OR ancestors and any AND ancestors whose point guards are true.

Let D be the least common ancestor of the two occurrences of T. Above D the two occurrences stay together, so distinct resident raw-line groups are independent. The preceding rules apply there and at all nodes in other branches. In particular D receives all the positive obligations of its resident lines. For any point pair with a point on one of those lines, and with all lines zero, D is false: root rejection propagates along the OR ancestors and through the point AND guards active on that point.

## D is OR

For each of the four T positives, assign its point to a child of D accepting it. If a child receives none, set its T occurrence to zero: all T positives are still accepted by the other child, all other positives are unchanged, and negatives stay rejected. This produces a seven-line formula with at least one fewer gate, so the 34-gate seven-line bound suffices. The same argument applies whenever one T occurrence can be deleted. After constant simplification any new pure point subtree can be cut as a boundary signal. No line-only subtree can combine two uniquely occurring raw lines: an AND would make their singleton activations indistinguishable from zero, and an OR would make the two raw singletons indistinguishable from one another despite their distinct incidence neighborhoods.

Otherwise each child receives a nonempty fragment of T. Within that child other raw line labels occur only once, and the T occurrence now has the positive obligations assigned to it. Zero-line pair rejection is inherited from D. The independent-group proof works even if the assigned fragments have no common points. Thus the entire tree has only line-splitting ORs and attached point leaves. Lower-bound its output by selecting a line term at any true raw occurrence and all point AND guards on the route to the root. All AND guards on this route accept its assigned positive singletons.

Call the six lines other than T ordinary. For an ordinary line L keep the guards on its path whose set of resident ordinary lines is precisely {L}. The other guards on that path all contain a common other ordinary line U by nesting. On a negative with L active and Q meeting U, all dropped guards are true. Thus the retained conjunction rejects each outside pair of L; it also accepts all L singletons.

For a T occurrence keep the guards on its path that have no resident ordinary line. All dropped guards on that path contain a common ordinary line U, so the analogous test with T active shows this term rejects every outside pair of T. It accepts the positive points assigned to that occurrence. OR the two reconstructed T terms. Guards used by both terms can be taken outside the OR, so each retained point occurrence is used once. None is used by an ordinary output. Constants representing empty conjunctions can be simplified. The seven resulting point expressions are nonconstant by their positive and negative obligations and have disjoint input occurrences.

## D is AND

The two children cannot both contain an ordinary line: choose positives from two such lines and apply the zero-line pair contradiction at D. Write A for a child containing only T and point leaves, and B for the other child. Write G for the ordinary lines in B. Use a subscript 0 or 1 on A for its cofactor at its own occurrence of T.

If G is empty, both children contain T as their sole line input. Form the T output from their 1-cofactors and all point AND guards higher on the T path that have no ordinary residents. Any dropped guards contain a common ordinary line and can be removed by the outside-pair extension. Ordinary outputs are constructed through their respective paths as in the OR case. All retained point occurrences are disjoint.

Suppose G is nonempty. Every positive of G forces A0 to accept its point, and A1 accepts all four singleton points of T. Put N equal to the points of T rejected by B when all its raw lines are zero. If N is empty the T occurrence of B can be deleted. Otherwise B is good on the full neighborhoods of G and on the nonempty assigned set N of T. In particular its zero-line rejection on the ordinary positives follows because A0 is true there, and D is false. On a point pair with at least one endpoint on a line of G, A0 is true, so B inherits zero-line rejection of that pair from D. A line-splitting AND inside B is impossible: the two positive endpoints witnessing the contradiction can be chosen with at least one on a line of G, since only one label is T. This rejection propagates down through the ORs and attached point guards by the same argument. Thus in B all nodes are either line-splitting ORs or attachments of point leaves.

Construct the six ordinary outputs from their path AND guards with exactly one ordinary resident line, as before, excluding the exceptional AND D itself. For L in G, there is in addition the sibling A. If G has one line, include A0 as a factor for that ordinary line. Otherwise A0 accepts every singleton of another V in G, so it is already true on a negative chosen to meet V. At such a negative A is at least A0. Together with the common other ordinary line U for all dropped point guards, this uses at most two other-line requirements. Every constructed ordinary output accepts its neighborhood and rejects all outside pairs.

Construct the T output using B alone. Set its ordinary raw-line bits to zero and its T bit to one. Replace every attached point AND guard whose group contains an ordinary line by constant one. Keep all point leaves attached via OR, including those unnecessary to the assigned obligations N; they can supply the other T positives. The result accepts the whole T neighborhood, since the original B was true on every T positive. It uses none of the point-leaf occurrences retained for the ordinary outputs.

If this expression accepted an outside pair X, trace a true term back to its T leaf or to a point OR leaf. This tracing selects one true child at each OR and keeps every AND guard on the selected route. Among point guards removed on that route, choose an ordinary line U from the smallest resident ordinary group. They all contain U. If none were removed, take any U in G. On a negative with T active, Q containing X and meeting U, each removed guard is true. All retained guards of the selected term are true by monotonicity. Its leaf is true as well, whence B is true. A1 accepts all singleton points of U since A0 already does. Every AND point guard above D also accepts U. Thus the root accepts this negative, a contradiction. This proves the T output rejects all its outside pairs.

In either LCA case there are seven nonconstant point expressions with pairwise disjoint occurrences of the original point leaves. Cofactors are computed by constant simplification of their own subtrees, including A0 when retained. Each expression costs at most one fewer gate than its number of point-leaf occurrences, so their total added cost is at most P−7. The point charge theorem yields at least 28−P original point gates. With P+7 mixed gates, the total is at least 35.
