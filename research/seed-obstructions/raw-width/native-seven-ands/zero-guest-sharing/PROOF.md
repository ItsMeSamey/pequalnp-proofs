# Exact shared OR debit with two exceptional AND outputs

On a fixed set of singleton probes, write S(v) for the set of atoms making wire v high. Point probes alone suffice for the private-host application. OR unions these sets and AND intersects them. Cut all but at most two AND sites to zero on these probes, as permitted when the other sites are clean on them. The two remaining sites may occur in either order, may depend on one another and may have arbitrary fanout.

Let U range over neighborhoods with pairwise intersection of size at most one. Every raw primitive has at most one atom in each U. For each U choose k_U target wires whose original singleton supports lie wholly in U. Let P_U be the union of the selected supports. The original circuit contains at least the sum over U of |P_U| minus k_U paid OR gates in the joint ancestry of the targets. Empty groups contribute zero. The targets, internals and donors may be shared. The conclusion requires no restriction on the other-cube values of the two exceptional ANDs.

**Expansion and one-group debit.** Choose a fair left/right bit for each exceptional site. Compute only on singleton atoms of U. A cut zero and an ordinary primitive contribute at most one atom. When the ORIGINAL singleton support of an exceptional site restricted to U has size at most one, supply that set for free, even if its parents' projected calculations have enlarged. Otherwise replace that site with its chosen operand. These replacements can only enlarge each projected wire value, by induction. The resulting OR-only network for the targets has only zero and singleton sources. Join the k_U target wires by at most k_U minus one artificial ORs. Bypass gates with an empty operand and replace any signal with zero or singleton support by that source. The remaining output collects at least |P_U| distinct atoms. Its connected binary DAG needs at least |P_U| minus one ORs. Hence at least |P_U| minus k_U charged original ORs remain for this group, for every choice of the two bits. Each charged OR had at least two ORIGINAL U atoms: every enlargement originates at an expanded AND with at least two original U atoms and then propagates through ORs, or through other expanded ANDs already having two such atoms. The input to an enlarged OR therefore already had these original marks.

**Why the same OR is paid at most once in expectation.** Consider a physical OR G that can be charged to two distinct groups U,V across the possible choices. Its actual support contains two atoms of each, so is not contained in U or in V. Every retained path from G to a U target must pass an expanded site. The first exceptional site encountered when tracing backward from that target has its full original support contained in U, since the intervening path consists solely of ORs. It must have at least two U atoms or tracing stops there. An analogous site with support in V is different. Call these two sites D_U and D_V. They exhaust the available exceptional sites. There cannot be a third group to which G can be charged. For U the calculation always stops at D_V, whose support intersects U in at most one atom. Thus charging G in U requires an actual OR-only path from G to the chosen operand of D_U. It cannot reach both operands by ORs: then the original D_U would inherit every atom of G, including one outside U. The U probability of charging G is therefore at most one half. The same holds for V. An OR chargeable by a single group has expected charge at most one as well. Sum these inequalities and average the preceding deterministic one-group debit. The physical joint-ancestry bound follows. No independence between different groups' elections is required.

**Native zero-guest consequences.** Each private point-facing host operand has its bare point support contained in its own tag's neighborhood, by the unique private-point cut. The seven tag neighborhoods meet pairwise in one point. Each available compound 129 or 4160 meets any one neighborhood in at most one atom. The six clean terminal outputs vanish on the singleton probes. Thus the four private point-facing operands alone cost at least the sum of their four singleton sizes minus four actual upstream ORs, for every placement of the two dirty sites. Include any further point-facing ports having their actual point support within their own tag stars and apply the same joint criterion. Since six exposed clean terminals need five collector ORs disjoint from upstream gates, a size-33, eight-AND candidate can afford at most 20 such upstream ORs. In particular it cannot have one full four-petal point port for each of the seven native tags: those cost 21, independently of the two dirty sites' positions.
