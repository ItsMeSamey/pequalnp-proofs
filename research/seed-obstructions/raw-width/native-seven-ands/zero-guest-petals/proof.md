# Three physical ORs per unbacked private tag

Let s be the number of private tags with only their own private site as a singleton address in the zero-guest native representative with exactly two dirty ANDs and six isolated clean outputs. For each such tag u, its point-facing arm takes value one at each of the four bare points in N(u). Every u path first reaches its private AND there, so the incident toggle at p must raise that AND and the point arm sees the same value as at bare p. Its bare point support contains no other point, by the private point cut and signed darkness. The unbacked product is clean since cutting there removes both private q and u, and a negative with the product high would dominate the accepted q,u input after the cut.

Cut every point-facing arm at earlier AND outputs, giving a physical OR-only cone. Each of its signals has bare point support contained in its four-point neighborhood. Native free point pairs meet a neighborhood at most once. Call a petal affected if one of its boundary AND signals has at least two bare points there. Only dirty ANDs qualify, and a signal cannot affect distinct petals since their neighborhoods meet once. In every unaffected cone select all physical OR gates whose bare support contains at least two petal points. Truncation at the remaining signals yields at least four boundary sources and hence at least three such ORs. These marked sets of gates for distinct unaffected petals are disjoint.

For one affected petal, truncate its full physical cone at signals containing at most one of its four marked points. At any reached AND select one child; its actual support contains the parent's. At reached ORs keep both children. The retained connected graph covers the four marks by boundary signals carrying at most one each, so it has at least three binary ORs. Each has at least two of these marks and cannot have been charged in an unaffected cone.

If two distinct petals are affected, let their private points be qA,qB. In each original arm cone there is a direct OR reader of its q. Indeed q belongs to no supplied compound and to no OR-only cone of any earlier AND: by private uniqueness only its own AND can see q. The arm is high also at other bare points, hence cannot itself just be q. The two direct readers are distinct since the other private point is absent from the respective arm and OR support only grows. Neither direct reader belongs to a charged unaffected cone, since its private q is absent there. Setting both q inputs zero therefore refunds at least these two ORs, replacing each reader by its other operand at all fanouts.

The remaining ordinary triples A,B meet in one point w. On their five bare points every gate acts by union or intersection of singleton traces. Grant all previously charged unaffected OR gates as free inputs: their traces meet each triple at most once. All genuine inputs satisfy the same bound. Grant every five-bit mask satisfying it, every intersection freely, and arbitrary reuse. The two desired outputs have masks 7 and 25, indexed w,a1,a2,b1,b2. The attached two_triples.py exhausts 0 through 3 unions followed by intersection closure and finds no state containing both target masks. Every missing union generates one catalogue state; a redundant union need not be counted. Consequently the pinned two-target circuit requires four further ORs, giving six including the two refunded direct private readers. This proves at least 3s distinct physical ORs in total. Five collector ORs are additional by clean-terminal isolation. No positivity condition on the dirty ANDs' bare-point traces was assumed.
