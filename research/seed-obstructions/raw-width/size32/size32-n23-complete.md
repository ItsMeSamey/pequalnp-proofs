# Width 23, size 32, seven mandatory line owners

Run python verify831.py with Python 3.10 or later from the extracted directory. The verifier first checks the census and the 800 exclusions described in REPORT.md. It then reconstructs the retained point pools, baseline-omission domains and incompatibility graph independently from the sample, checking each stored cache against the reconstruction.

For a high subset H of the physical composite masks, the all-zero local line cofactor of the physical region holding the singleton p is monotone with H. Within a tested pool A, Z comprises exactly those points at which that cofactor passes. If H is contained in H', a common point in the first Z must be in the second. Each such point names a nonmandatory physical supplier. In one tested pool, these holders are distinct by the compulsory masking tests. Across pools a holder may be shared. Grant three interchangeable colors for the physical suppliers. If p passes in a holder, an original masking negative for another resident q cannot dominate that input assignment. Otherwise the holder passes on a negative on which the change at raw q requires it to fail. These exclusions yield the undirected coloring graph. Twenty former orbit representatives reduce to seven, of total physical mass ten.

For a fixed retained set K of distinguished rows over label set U and billed labels V within U, each point in K outside Z needs its own bin with demand N(p) intersect V. The tests for distinct neighborhoods, nonincidence, pair compatibility, point-bearing darkness and mandatory-owner singleton line cofactors are the same as in REPORT.md. For every already named physical holder, its cofactor on charged singleton lines is zero if it holds a point of K. It is also zero at any u where a retained u-active negative is an original masking negative for any resident q. This last test is based on the physical location of q on the original sample, and applies even if q was not omitted in this projection. All remaining labels on V are granted as that supplier's mask.

To bound deliveries, expand the physical helper graph backwards from the disjoint terminal bins. At a named helper of fanout f, keep one use to be expanded into its predecessors, grant one other use its whole permitted mask for free in its recipient bin, and account for the other f−2 uses by unrestricted mergers before expansion. A named helper uses one of the three surplus units even if all of its reads are irrelevant. An unnamed helper of fanout f takes at most f−1 mergers of recipient demands, including disjoint demands. The mandatory owners initially pay two copies of each singleton label. Additional owner occurrences use one unused surplus unit apiece. Unused reads can simply be discarded. This reverse expansion is acyclic, allows reconvergence, and yields a successful branch of fits0 whenever the physical routing exists. Operations for different free masks can be ordered by the recurrence: all available mergers may precede the delivery of a free mask, since membership in the union grants at least the coverage obtained by delivering it before merging. After that, free masks may be applied in any order, even to the same group, and may be discarded. The singleton-column bound is the special case where a known dark physical supplier cannot reduce the column's demand.

The verifier first allows every coloring and every surviving choice of omissions. Each surviving model is tested against further (H,A,U,V,K) demands. One violated constraint suffices to remove it. Each added demand is reconstructed directly from actual positive and negative rows and applied to every subsequent model. Four representatives need respectively 1,3,1,4 additional constraints. The other three already fail the singleton-column test. The physical circuit exclusions are conditional on the original seven mandatory owner cuts and at most three additional read-surplus units, as in REPORT.md.
