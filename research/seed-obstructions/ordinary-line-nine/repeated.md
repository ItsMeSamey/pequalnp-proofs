# Eight line wires and a repeated boundary input

At 19 mixed gates, eleven point wires and eight line wires leave one surplus read. The eight line signatures comprise seven singletons and an extra S. When the repeated wire is a point input, unfolding it counts the point wire twice as a leaf occurrence and gives the previously excluded eight-line read-once tree with the original point circuitry.

If the repeated wire is S itself, the resulting tree has seven ordinary raw lines and two loose leaves with identical bit patterns. The arguments in exceptional.md and README.md apply. As the patterns are always 00 or 11, at most one resident requires its cofactor in a joint attachment, so there is no duplication cost. For a pair S its own raw labels give the only possible exceptional split; both opposite-child tests pass. There are at most |S| defect repairs and the original line precomputation costs at least |S|−1. Thus this excludes all 120 choices with |S|≥2. If S is a redundant raw singleton, the repeated-one/two-label ordinary-line results apply.

Suppose raw line L instead is repeated, and lies in S. Keep the other six raw leaves as the ordinary skeleton. With L zero and S pinned high, there is no possible raw-splitting AND: the only possible missing pair when |S|=2 contains L, and larger masks have no missing edges. For ordinary j retain the Q_j certified by pivoting from all ordinary raw lines zero at the pattern with L=0 and S indicating j in S. At every private AND attachment take its appropriate point cofactor, fixing the two occurrences of L to zero. There is only one attachment with a loose S leaf; choose a companion with smaller or equal S bit unless the cofactor must be retained. At most one ordinary target retains that side, since no other resident with smaller bit is an exception only at a unique minimum. Shared ordinary guards use the certified points of a resident companion. As in exceptional.md use a common companion for nested groups when the bits are identical. The checked negatives certify all exterior singleton rejections.

Recover L by ORing every ordinary-skeleton OR-side formula, with L=1 and S=1. At an L-positive all ordinary raw leaves are zero, so any true trace reaches at least one such OR side. If its cofactor accepts an exterior singleton x, choose any ordinary resident v at its attachment. A sampled L-active negative extends x and a point of Q_v. This passes every ancestor AND guard by monotonicity from the positive at v, since both exceptional bits are true at the negative. Hence it would pass the OR side to the output, contrary to rejection. This L output is fully recovered.

The ordinary targets use point occurrences only from AND sides, and the additional L target uses only OR sides. Computing seven nonconstant expressions costs at most P−7 before repairing the missed ordinary incidences. At most |S|−1 repairs are necessary, already covered by the pure line cost. All 441 raw-L-in-S choices with |S|≥2 pass the finite calculation.

When L is outside a nonconcurrent S, erase both L occurrences and retain just the other six target labels. Pinning S high gives a good six-ordinary skeleton; the private guards with one special attachment recover the six intact singleton targets from at most P−6 additional point gates. Six targets require 18 gates, hence the original point side requires at least 13. Nonconcurrence implies at least three primitive bits in S and hence at least two line gates. Together with 19 mixed gates this gives 34.
