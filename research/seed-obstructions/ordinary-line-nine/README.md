# Two additional line signatures in a nineteen-gate mixed tree

Suppose a 33-gate residual separator has eleven point boundary signals, nineteen mixed gates and nine line boundary signals comprising the seven singleton line signatures and two further non-singleton signatures S and W. The mixed cone is a tree. Lower every line leaf to the OR of the raw lines in its signature. On positives this preserves every value and on negatives it can only decrease the output.

Pin the two additional leaves to one. Assume the resulting formula rejects each singleton point at seven zero raw lines. This is certified whenever, for every singleton point p, a sampled negative containing p has raw lines meeting both S and W. The test covers 4,371 unordered pairs including identical masks. In the distinct non-singleton bank enumeration with exact minimum line cost at most six, it covers 4,278 families in 280 raw geometry orbits.

The pinned formula is good on all seven raw lines. The seven-line read-once argument shows that all raw-line splits are ORs. When the only raw-line-containing child has a sibling at an OR attachment, the sibling can be removed. On every incident positive it was false in the pinned formula, hence also in the original, and the removal can only decrease negative values. Every remaining term consists of its raw line T and its AND attachments. An attachment containing no extra leaf is an ordinary point guard. For a T term the shared ordinary guards on its path all accept all singletons on another line U, by choosing U in their smallest nontrivial resident group. The ordinary private guards of distinct targets have disjoint original leaf occurrences.

A special attachment J has at least one loose extra leaf. For each resident T its point cofactor at the true extra pattern of singleton T accepts every point on T. This follows from acceptance of its term on the corresponding original positives. On any negative with T active the actual guard dominates this cofactor.

If both loose leaves occur in one attachment J, give each resident T its two-bit pattern. The cofactor for another resident V is no greater than the cofactor for T when its bits form a subset of T's bits. In that event J may be omitted from T's private term. A negative extending a T-exterior pair and meeting V enables J, and meeting U also enables the ordinary shared guards. The existing raw two-companion witness check provides such negatives for every exterior pair. The private product must therefore reject that pair. Retain the T cofactor when no such V exists. At most two patterns are unique minimal elements on residents, by the width of the two-bit subset order.

If the two loose leaves lie in different attachments, their resident groups are disjoint or nested. In disjoint groups the one-bit order leaves at most one resident needing its cofactor per attachment. In nested groups choose at most one target per attachment to retain that attachment's cofactor. For each target, every other special attachment on its path has a single common companion V resident there whose active bit is no greater than T's at that attachment. The small checker proves this statement on all 1,680 multiplicity profiles up to seven residents. It permits either exception to be absent and includes equal groups. Thus just two companion lines U,V are sufficient here as well. Any extra loose leaf lost in a deleted OR attachment needs no cofactor. Constants arising in a retained cofactor can be simplified.

With different attachments all retained factors use disjoint original point occurrences. The seven pair-safe expressions therefore take at most P−7 extra binary point gates at P=11. The same holds for one attachment with at most one retained target. For one attachment with e point leaves and two retained targets, constructing its two cofactors separately uses at most twice e−1 gates when both are nonconstant. Counting them as two factors gives at most e extra gates over P−7. Constants only reduce the count. Alternatively construct both special targets directly from their four raw singleton points at six gates total, and build the other five using at most P−e−5 gates. The excess over P−7 is consequently at most min(e,8−e)≤4. Each reconstructed target accepts all four incident singletons and rejects all exterior pairs, so it is nonconstant.

The seven pair-safe singletons require 21 point gates. The original point subcircuit consequently requires at least 21−(11−7+4)=13 gates. The two non-singleton boundary wires cost at least two line gates because each is computed. Nineteen mixed gates give at least 34 total.
