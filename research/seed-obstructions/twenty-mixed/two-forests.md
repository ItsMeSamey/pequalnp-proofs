# Two compulsory three-gate patterns

Let the eleven selected point wires have four raw singleton masks and seven paid masks, with at least one selected nonraw signature outside all seven residual stars. Let distinct selected paid wires have the exact four-point singleton signatures T,U and reject every exterior point pair for their respective stars. Suppose the point program has eleven binary gates.

Every paid singleton signature is nonempty, differs from every original primitive signature, and differs from every earlier paid signature. Otherwise replace a zero by constant propagation, an equal raw signature by its raw representative, or a repeated signature by its earliest occurrence. The resulting point program costs at most ten on the singleton domain. Its boundary, after removing repeats, retains at most eleven signatures and at most seven distinct nonraw signatures, including the original nonraw nonlocal signature. It still distinguishes the original 1,323 point obligations. The six-output theorem rules out a nonlocal signature with at most six nonraw selections and the released seven-output ten-gate cutoff rules out exactly seven. This argument need only preserve singleton signatures.

Consider the actual function at T. An AND producing it would have two parent masks containing T. If both contain an exterior point, the union of such exterior singleton witnesses is an exterior set of size at most two accepted by the AND, contrary to pair safety. Hence at least one parent mask equals T, already impossible. Its OR parents therefore have strict-subset signatures. If neither is a triple they are complementary pairs. Otherwise its triple parent A also rejects every exterior pair of T. If A were an AND, one of its parents would have to be contained in T; this parent contains A, so it must be A or T, violating distinctness since it precedes both. Thus A is an OR and has a two-point parent contained within A. Every such inner pair or triple is paid because no nonsingleton original primitive is contained in a residual star.

The same argument applies independently to U. No paid mask found by one argument occurs in the other: their stars meet in just one point. Each host therefore has its full star and either a complementary pair partition or a nested pair and triple among the eleven paid gates. These six singleton masks are independently realizable as two initial three-OR trees. Replace the old computations of their signatures with those trees, then evaluate the remaining five gates in original order. Every boundary signature is preserved. Thus the pair-safe roots force simultaneous three-gate patterns in all remaining nonlocal cases.

Each normalized forest gate computes the OR of its mask's raw singleton variables. For any monotone original gate with that same singleton mask, this OR is pointwise below the original gate, since every active singleton extends monotonically to all point inputs containing it. Computing the five unchanged gate operations on these replacements can only decrease their values while preserving all singleton outputs. The old mixed cone therefore still accepts every required singleton positive and rejects every original negative. The normalization supports necessary conditions evaluated on the full negative rows, including raw-operand occurrence charges.
