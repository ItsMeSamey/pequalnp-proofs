# Two singleton-resident regions and shared point features

Let T and U be the two distinct ordinary raw lines resident in singleton nonroot regions of the seven-line mixed network. The other five lines are ordinary. Assume that each of their private expressions is reconstructed with its incoming region endpoints pinned zero, preserving that line's singleton positives. This holds, in particular, when every nonroot repeated endpoint has a resident original line. An additional zero-resident endpoint with nonzero all-lines-zero singleton cofactor needs separate leaf accounting.

Keep the two singleton regions' full trees. In each region with ordinary resident lines take the disjunction of the nonresident sides at the unary OR attachments of its ordinary-line skeleton. The root without resident lines, if present, keeps its full tree. Link the region expressions using their original edges. Set T=1,U=0 to obtain an envelope F, and reverse the flags for an envelope H. Each is a monotone function of the b point leaves present in these expressions. These leaves are disjoint from the five private ordinary expressions, which use only their regions' private AND-side subtrees.

On a T-positive, every true trace in an ordinary region with all its raw residents zero reaches some OR side, so F dominates the true root. Conversely, if a sampled T-active negative meets all five ordinary neighborhoods and its feature valuation dominates x, then F(x)=1 would force actual root acceptance. Propagate induction through the regions: intact regions are monotone; in ordinary regions the active OR side passes every ancestor split, which is OR by resident-line pivotality, and every ancestor AND guard accepts the incident singleton of a resident line present in the negative with earlier endpoints pinned zero. The same statements hold for H and U.

The arms have three exclusive positives each and one common point. For any T-exterior singleton p, at least two of the three U-exclusive points q individually admit a T-active negative containing p and q and meeting every ordinary neighborhood. The verifier derives this from the raw theorem, with 96 instances having exactly two admissible choices and 282 having three. This also shows that an envelope is false at every singleton outside its target.

Let c be the feature valuation of the common point, and let x₁,x₂,x₃ and y₁,y₂,y₃ be those on the respective exclusive triples. Repeated valuations are allowed. Define A as the union of the principal upsets of c and the three x values, and B from c and the three y values. Monotonicity gives A≤F and B≤H. Each x must be outside B and each y outside A. The enumerator checks all such assignments, sorting each triple and swapping the two arms when useful.

A replacement h for T is safe provided each accepted feature valuation z has A(z OR yᵢ)=1 for at least two distinct marker positions i. If h accepted an outside singleton p of valuation z, at least one of those marker positions q would admit the sampled negative above. The feature valuation at that negative dominates z OR yᵢ by point-function monotonicity. Thus F(z OR yᵢ)=1 would force acceptance, a contradiction. This is symmetric for the U replacement. The two marker positions must be different physical points but may have equal feature valuations.

Enumerate all sets of signals simultaneously achievable by AND/OR over b independent feature bits and free constants in at most b+1 gates. For each simultaneously achievable pair h,g satisfying those safety bounds, count one more OR for each of the four positive singleton points missed on either arm. Such repairs use the original raw point bit and cannot add an outside singleton. For a constant-zero output, the direct four-point union costs three gates. Constant signals used by a candidate program can be simplified. The checker succeeds if its chosen pair's joint cost plus repairs is ≤b+1. It checks all 156 feasible profiles for b=3 and all 29,595 for b=4. For b=2 there is one profile modulo arm order; fewer features cannot realize both exclusive arms. For b≥5 both raw stars cost only six gates.

Hence, if the five ordinary outputs cost at most P−b−5 and use disjoint point occurrences from the b features, the original point circuit gives all seven full singleton signatures with at most P−4 further gates. For P=11 this is seven new gates. The seven-signature lower bound of 21 yields at least 14 original point gates, giving at least 34 total when there are 20 mixed gates.

## Reproduction

Run `c++ -O3 -std=c++17 verify.cpp -o verify && ./verify`. The columns report feature count, distinct lower-function pairs, positive-valuation profiles and uncovered profiles. Run `python3 geometry.py PATH_TO_ONE_SURPLUS` with the theorem JSON and sample decoder from that directory.
