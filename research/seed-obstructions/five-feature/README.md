# A repeated raw line supplying one of two uses of a shared mixed gate

Consider a seven-line residual with two repeated wires, both of fanout two: a raw line T and a downstream mixed endpoint H with sole resident raw line U. One T use enters H, the other enters the exterior directly. Both H occurrences enter that exterior. The original mixed cone has P+8 gates on P distinct point boundaries. It needs at least 34 total gates.

Clamp T,H zero and reconstruct the other five ordinary stars by private AND guards in their ordinary skeleton. H vanishes on zero-line singletons by its blocked resident U. Let f be the OR of the exterior OR-side attachment formulas. Its point leaves are disjoint from the private guards. It uses at most one direct T occurrence and at most two H occurrences. Let b count its point leaf occurrences and d count those below H. The latter has one T leaf and one U leaf, and d≥1 since H is mixed. Write q=b+d. The five ordinary outputs cost at most P−q−5 point gates. To recover all outputs at cost ≤P−5 we may spend q for T and U. If q≥6, build both stars directly from raw point bits. It suffices to consider q≤5.

Use truth tables on these q point occurrences as independent variables. With one target raw line active and the other zero, evaluate f using the actual unit cofactor of H. Denote the resulting two functions a,b'. Each target cofactor accepts all its incident singletons, since the ordinary leaves are false and a true OR side must be reached. If a cofactor accepts an exterior pair X, choose a passing OR side and an ordinary V resident at its attachment. The sampled negative extending X with the target active and meeting V makes the actual H at least its unit cofactor, and passes the side and every ancestral guard. This proves pair safety. The baseline z=f(0,0) rejects every point singleton: if a clamped OR side passed p, adding any point on its resident ordinary V would activate every AND ancestor on a forbidden zero-line pair.

## Replay

Run ./replay.sh using C++17 and Python 3. The raw JSON and decoder come from ../one-surplus. No SAT solver is needed. See ../parallel-algebra/README.md for the shared lower-replacement and majority-vector arguments.

seq.cpp enumerates all 15,052 symbolic triples (with arity) by separate tree recurrences for exterior f and inner H. Each recurrence returns its four cofactors on the two distinguished leaf labels. Contiguous point leaf intervals in child trees only relabel the inputs. With f indexed by (0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1), the resulting a is f10 OR (H10 AND f11), and b' is f00 OR (H01 AND f01). The arity-q variables occupy the low q coordinates of a five-coordinate truth table. reachn.cpp enumerates jointly constructible point output pairs in at most q gates, allowing constants that simplify away.

seqcheck.py first tries exact outputs and then monotone lower replacements agreeing on all positive valuations with z=0. Next it enumerates minimal valuations of three exclusive points and the common point. The OR of any two same-label exclusive valuations fails the opposite cofactor, since the corresponding point pair is exterior to it. The checker looks for replacements meeting just those obligations. It also uses the certified one-versus-one or one-versus-two majority constructions in ../parallel-algebra/README.md where their Hamming-distance cost fits q. No case at q≤4 survives. At q=5 eight minimal configurations remain. expand.py enumerates their 28 assignments of multiplicities among the three exclusive points on each side.

## Switching the witness OR attachment

Fix target T, opposite U and any T-exterior pair X. At least two of the three U-exclusive points have the following property: for EVERY ordinary V different from T and U, a T-active sampled negative extends X, contains that marker point and meets V. marker.py checks 504 cases with two such markers and 1,008 with three.

Let x be a five-bit valuation. If at least two opposite marker valuations w have x OR w dominating a chosen positive T valuation, it is safe to accept x as a sufficient condition for the T output. Indeed, if X activates x, one of those two marker points is universally usable. Fix it, choose a dominating relation as above and fix an outer OR attachment passing the selected T valuation. There is a negative containing X and the marker, with T active and meeting the attachment's resident ordinary V. All required point variables are active, so the real H dominates its target cofactor at x OR w. The attachment and all ancestor guards pass, a contradiction. This argument works when the two markers require different OR attachments.

strong.cpp enumerates formulas of up to two gates on the five point-feature inputs plus four raw singleton bits of one target. It checks acceptance of the four points at their chosen minimal feature vectors. With raw target bits set to zero, every accepted feature valuation must satisfy the preceding safety test. Actual incident points dominate their minimized feature vectors, and exterior pairs turn off all target-specific raw bits. For each of the final 28 assignments one of the two stars has such a two-gate formula. The other uses three raw OR gates. Thus all q cases admit the required reconstruction and total original cost is at least 21−(P−5)+(P+8)=34.
