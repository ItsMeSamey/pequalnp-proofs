# An optimal 13-mixed-gate circuit needs exactly 34 residual gates

The preceding verifiers prove that a residual separator using 13 mixed gates has seven point-side signals with the prescribed four-point neighborhood signatures and seven line-side signals with singleton line signatures. Since the output cone must connect 14 distinct boundary wires with 13 gates, it is a tree consuming each boundary wire once. Replace every side signal by the OR of its singleton signature. This keeps each residual positive unchanged and can only decrease signal values at negatives. Every compatible tree therefore also separates with these canonical leaves. The exhaustive cherry-contraction checker finds exactly the OR of the seven matched ANDs, modulo reassociation and reordering of the same operation.

Consider target line T and its corresponding point-side signal. On every negative row with T active the line-side signal is true, since its singleton signature contains T. Therefore the point-side signal rejects that negative's point input. Each of the 36 pairs of points outside T is dominated by such a negative. Monotonicity forces the point signal to reject all those pairs and their singleton subsets.

For any point-side wire A write S(A) for the set of points where A accepts a singleton. Binary AND and OR act on signatures as intersection and union. Charge a point gate to T if its signature is a subset of T of size at least two. Charges for distinct lines are disjoint. An exterior gate has signature not contained in any of the 13 projective lines. None of the free primitive point wires is exterior or charged, since each is a singleton or a subset of a prefix line. Write h for the number of exterior gates. Other gates may remain uncounted.

A signal is dirty for T when its signature contains an outside point. An AND of two dirty signals accepts some outside pair or singleton: take one accepted outside singleton from each operand and use monotonicity. Thus a pair-rejecting AND has a clean operand. A clean operand with at least two singleton points is itself a gate charged to T.

The target output has signature T, so it is charged. It cannot be the first charged gate. An OR output could then combine only clean inputs each with at most one point. An AND output would need a clean operand already containing all of T. Thus every target needs at least two charges.

If a target has exactly two charges, let G be its earlier charged gate. An OR output would have to receive G with at least three singleton points, and G would reject outside pairs as the output does. But a first charged, pair-rejecting AND can only intersect with a clean input of size at most one, and a first charged OR can combine at most two points. Repetition of G as both operands of the output would also require G to be pair-rejecting. Therefore the output is AND with a clean predecessor of signature T, necessarily G. Since G has all four singleton points, it is the AND of two dirty operands A and B: a first charged OR can cover at most two points. Both A and B strictly contain T in their signatures. The other operand C of the output must also contain T. It cannot be G again, since G accepts an outside pair or singleton, and it cannot be a different clean input of size four since that would be a third charge. Thus C also strictly contains T. All three operands are exterior gates.

The outside portions of S(A), S(B) and S(C) are nonempty and pairwise disjoint. If two contained the same point, that point together with an outside point accepted by the third operand would make the output true. The argument holds even if C depends on G or some operands are reused. The three exterior wires are distinct and each of their three pairwise signature intersections equals T.

Let q denote the number of targets with just two charges. Each requires three exterior wire pairs, and no pair can give two different intersections. Thus 3q ≤ h(h−1)/2. If h≤7, this implies q≤h. If h≥7, the same follows because there are only seven targets. Accounting for all seven targets and exterior wires therefore requires at least 2q+3(7−q)+h ≥21 point-side gates. Seven independent four-neighbor ORs attain 21. Together with the 13 mixed gates, the exact total is 34.

This excludes every residual circuit of size 33 with 13 mixed gates, with arbitrary fanout, any number of line-side gates, and arbitrary interleaving. Any successful 33-gate residual circuit needs at least 14 mixed gates. A raw 34-gate circuit, if it exists, yields a residual circuit of at most 33 gates on setting its seven omitted inputs to zero and propagating constants. This loses at least one gate because an erased positive and a negative are otherwise ordered on the projected inputs. Hence the raw target can only be solved in a way surviving this mixed-gate restriction.
