# Eight point signatures and seven line signatures

Run `python3 verify.py` with a C++20 compiler available as `c++`. The runner decodes the 676 sample columns from the hash-checked theorem statement, derives the 1,323 point-side separation obligations, regenerates the families, compares every family to the stored list, and checks the six classes. Dependencies are Python's standard library and the C++ standard library.

A mask records the singleton points on which a monotone point-side signal is true. For a positive singleton point a and a negative point set b whose line coordinates dominate the positive line coordinates, some point signal must contain a and avoid b. Maximal b for each a suffice. The seven verified pairwise exclusive point obligations need seven different slots, so these slots may be numbered by the obligations they witness. The eighth slot is free. Several slots may witness the same obligation. Every feasible unordered family admits at least one such numbering.

To enumerate families missing a canonical mask M, the solver forbids equality to M in **each** slot. It branches over all seven choices of M and unions the results. A partial slot has required and forbidden bits. An unmet row branches over all compatible witnessing slots, setting the required point and forbidding the negative points. An unmet inequality to M branches over a bit on which the slot can differ. Once all constraints are met, any remaining free bits are enumerated. Sorting the eight completed masks then deduplicating removes slot permutations. Sorting preserves multiplicity. Equal masks, including an eighth copy of a canonical mask, are allowed during search. Every accepted noncanonical family has eight distinct masks as checked in the runner. All families containing all seven canonical masks, including repetitions, belong to the separate canonical-superset case.

For 175 families one canonical four-point mask is replaced by two proper subsets whose union is that mask. Their sizes are (1,3), (2,2), (2,3), or (3,3), with counts 28, 21, 84, and 42. In the remaining cases a singleton common to two or three canonical masks is split from each. The family adds the singleton and all the remainders. The respective counts are 21 and six. This package exhausts the singleton-signature possibilities subject to the separation obligations. A mixed-formula exclusion also requires checking those possibilities in the formula model.
