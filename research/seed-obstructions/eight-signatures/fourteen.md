# A 33-gate residual separator requires at least 15 mixed gates

The seven-point-wire lower bound applies irrespective of the number of line signals. For 14 mixed gates an output cone uses at most 15 distinct boundary signals, seven on the line side at minimum. A remaining 33-gate circuit must use exactly eight point and seven line boundary signals. Its mixed part is read-once and at most 19 point gates are available.

When the eight point signatures include all seven four-point neighborhoods, at least six of their outputs are forced to reject every exterior pair by raw dominance checks. Count h exterior gates and q two-charge targets among six pair-safe lines. These six plus the seventh target of full singleton signature need at least 18−q+1+h gates. At total 19 or less, h≤q≤6. Since 3q≤h(h−1)/2, this forces h=q=0. The seventh target cannot be made with a single charge and no exterior gates, because an OR of two clean inputs of size at most one cannot cover four, and an AND of two strict supersets would require exterior gates. Thus at least 20 point gates are necessary in this case. This includes repeated signatures.

When any canonical signature is absent, the complete eight-mask enumeration has 202 possible families. In 175 of them, two proper subsets X and Y replace one neighborhood T with X∪Y=T. The verifier checks that both corresponding outputs reject all pairs outside T and that the other six canonical outputs reject their respective exterior pairs. Computing one additional OR of the two signals gives seven pair-safe full targets. This would use at most 20 point gates, contradicting their 21-gate lower bound. The remaining 27 families each extract a common singleton from two or three intersecting neighborhoods. The independent exhaustive read-once verifier rejects all 27 families when the seven line signatures are canonical. This establishes the 15-mixed lower bound.

For the latter 27 families, verify_split.py also confirms pair safety of every three-point component outside its original four-point line. A three-point pair-safe component requires at least two gates charged to that target, by the same first-charge argument as a full pair-safe target. For r shortened neighborhoods the point program needs at least 3(7−r)+2r gates since the exterior-gate pair bound covers the savings from any full targets costing only two gates. This gives 19 point gates for r=2 and 18 for r=3.
