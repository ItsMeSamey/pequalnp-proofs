# A shared mixed tree with no ordinary resident label

For the two-line signature {T,U}, let the other five labels be ordinary. Suppose the shared mixed subtree D contains no ordinary label. Write d for its point occurrences, b for the exterior point occurrences in ordinary OR sides, and p for all exterior point occurrences, so d+p=11. Let h count D slots in private ordinary guards. It is enough to augment the original point bank by seven gates. At all ordinary positives D takes its point-only value D0, obtained by pinning all special labels zero. The five private outputs together use at most p−b+h−5 gates given D0. Their negative proof uses ordinary-active extensions as in pair-special.md. If needed D0 costs at most d−1 whenever nonconstant. Constant slots can be dropped.

For h=0, use both exact special cofactors on d+b points, substituting D in the exterior OR sides. Apply the b+1 two-target result of pair-special.md with d+b in place of b, giving at most seven total. For h=2, D never occurs in an OR side. The two special functions are paired constant prunings on b leaves, with each raw special label occurring at most once and the extra input fixed one. For b≤4 pruning costs ≤b; for b≥6 six raw gates suffice. For b=5 use the argument below. Ordinary costs including D0 are at most 7−b.

For h=1, pin the sole private occurrence to zero and let M consist of the affected ordinary target's lost singleton positives. Every member activates D0. If |M|≥3, retain the original ordinary reconstruction, costing at most 6−b including D0, and pin every OR-side occurrence of D to one. For either special label W at least two members of M are outside W. A purported exterior false positive on a pair X selects a true OR attachment. There is a W-active negative containing X, meeting the attachment's ordinary resident and one of those two markers. This makes the actual D true and satisfies the attachment's ancestor guards, a contradiction. Thus the pinned cofactors work. Pruning costs ≤b for b≤4, and six raw gates suffice for b≥5. If |M|≤2, leave the private occurrence pinned zero, restoring its lost members by OR with their singleton inputs for at most |M| gates. The special attachments now involve D at most once. Substituting its actual tree gives one read-once expression on q=b+d point occurrences with at most one raw flag each for T and U and the extra label pinned one. The following bound gives augmentation (p−b−5)+|M|+(q−1)≤7.

## Single-tree extraction

Let f be the disjunction of special OR sides, with any single D slot expanded. Its two special cofactors accept their own four singleton points and reject each exterior pair. Positivity follows by tracing the ordinary-zero expression; rejection follows by extending an accepted exterior pair to a special-active negative meeting the ordinary line at the selected attachment. For the stronger witness needed below, the same extension can hit one of any two selected opposite-line exclusive points as checked in ../sequential/verify_geometry.py.

Both raw flags must occur since their respective cofactors are pivotal at the three exclusive points each. Take their lowest common ancestor and let the children be A (T) and B (U). On each exclusive point all AND ancestors pass and no ancestor OR sibling is true, by pivotality. At an OR split take A(1) for T and B(1) for U. At an AND split take B(0) for T and A(0) for U. These outputs cover the exclusive points. If the provisional T expression accepts an exterior singleton or pair, choose a T-active negative containing it and meeting one of two U-exclusive points. Choose also an ordinary resident of the true exterior attachment when needed. Every AND ancestor passes at that negative because it passes at the chosen U-exclusive point. For the AND split A(1) also passes because A(0) passes at all U-exclusive points. This makes f true and contradicts the attachment rejection argument. Equivalently, since the constructed negative includes a U-exclusive point, that point also passes every AND ancestor above the attachment on the ordinary skeleton when the chosen ordinary resident is met. The U proof exchanges the labels.

At the intersection point c, if all OR-ancestor siblings are false, both split cofactors pass, hence at an OR split at least one provisional output contains c, and at an AND split both do. Restore with ≤1 raw OR. Otherwise a true OR sibling above the split uses at least one point occurrence outside A,B, since no constant-true OR sibling can preserve pivotality. Restore with ≤2 raw ORs. Each provisional output is nonconstant by its acceptance and rejection, and its cost is at most the number of its own point occurrences minus one. The totals are therefore ≤q−1 in both cases.

For precision, choose the ordinary witness before extending the exterior pair. If the split is inside one actual attachment, use that attachment's resident. If the split belongs to the synthetic disjunction of attachments, the split is an OR and the provisional output already selects a true actual summand on the exterior pair. Use that summand's resident. No AND split can belong to the synthetic disjunction.
