# Two members in the extra line signature

In the P=11, 19-mixed-gate case, let the extra line input be the OR of lines T and U. At least one paid line gate is required. It suffices to construct seven exact singleton neighborhoods with at most seven additional point gates; then the original circuit costs at least (21−7)+19+1=34. The numeric part below establishes a restricted two-special-target result. It does not claim that every architecture with this two-line signature is excluded.

## The two-special-target finite calculation

Suppose five ordinary outputs are reconstructed in at most 6−b gates and T,U are represented by two pair-safe cofactors on b other original point occurrences. Suppose moreover their expressions have the following common form. One point-leaf tree with up to one T and one U flag may supply a single intermediate Y to a second point-leaf tree, where it has at most two uses. The two trees together use at most one of each T and U flag. Constants are allowed. For the T output substitute (T,U)=(1,0); for U substitute (0,1). An occurrence of the original extra line input always has value one under either of these settings and can be simplified. Empty inner or outer expressions may be treated as constants.

For b≥5 the two raw target neighborhoods cost six gates, within the allowed b+1. For b≤3 all possible ordered cofactor pairs can be computed jointly with at most b+1 gates. For b=4, 48 of 5,336 enumerated pairs lack an exact joint realization in five gates. Denote one pair by a,c. On each point of the T neighborhood the valuation of the four feature wires satisfies a. At the three T-exclusive points it also falsifies c, since a pair-safe U output rejects every outside singleton. Replace each exclusive valuation by a minimal subvaluation in the a=true,c=false region. At most three distinct vectors result. The union of any two such vectors still falsifies c: the union is dominated by the actual feature valuation on the corresponding two exterior points of U, where c rejects. The analogous condition holds for the U-exclusive valuations, and the intersection point dominates a minimal valuation satisfying both. For every one of the 120 resulting necessary configurations the verifier finds two jointly computable five-gate functions. They are pointwise bounded above by a,c and accept their respective chosen minimal valuations. They therefore remain pair-safe on the actual inputs and accept all required singleton points. This proves the b+1 bound also for b=4. The enumeration uses no properties of the point features beyond monotonicity and the accepted singleton/rejected exterior pair constraints. Run `python verify_two_targets.py`; joint.py contains its truth-table recurrence.

## A shared mixed gate with at least two ordinary resident lines

Take all lines except T and U as ordinary. Let D be the sole shared mixed gate; assume at least two ordinary raw lines have their only path to the output through D. Set all three special line leaves to zero for ordinary-singleton analysis. For ordinary lines resident at D, D is pivotal and the exterior pinned at D=1 accepts every singleton on each resident neighborhood. At zero raw lines D rejects all singletons, by the global two-point negative argument. The ordinary skeleton inside D reconstructs each resident ordinary target using its private AND sides at zero special values. A (possibly shared) other ordinary line in this region supplies a witness both for every dropped inner shared guard and for the pinned exterior. Outside D, replace its two occurrences with zero. Outside ordinary singleton positives are preserved. The remaining ordinary targets are reconstructed on their ordinary skeleton by private AND sides. If there are no ordinary lines outside D, omit this extraction.

Let b count the point occurrences at ordinary OR sides in both regions (all exterior point occurrences when no exterior ordinary line exists). The five ordinary outputs use disjoint point occurrences away from these OR sides, so cost at most 11−b−5=6−b. Let Y be the disjunction of the inner OR sides with the special flags left free. In the exterior replace both D occurrences by Y and take the disjunction of the exterior OR sides, or the entire exterior expression if it has no ordinary line. The resulting T- and U-only cofactors meet the hypotheses of the finite calculation. On a positive of either special line the ordinary leaves are zero, so a true trace goes through an OR side and likewise reaches an inner OR side if the actual D is true; replacing D by Y cannot lose the positive. If the reconstructed T cofactor accepts an exterior pair X, select a true exterior OR side, if such a side is needed, and select a true inner OR side whenever Y is true on X. Extend X to a T-active negative meeting ordinary lines resident at both selected attachments, or just the one required line if fewer attachments exist. Each selected attachment is true and its AND ancestors are true; hence actual D dominates Y(X) and the exterior accepts. This contradicts rejection. The proof for U is identical. The marked negative checks in ../sequential/verify_geometry.py imply all these two-other-line extensions. Together with the five private outputs, these two cofactors establish total cost at least 34.

The same five-ordinary argument directly covers a repeated boundary line carrying T, U or their OR. In that case there is no shared intermediate Y, and the outer two cofactors are simply constant prunings of one point tree. For b≤4 the pruning bound in ../sequential/verify_prunings.py suffices even if the T or U flag occurs twice; for b≥5 use two raw stars.
