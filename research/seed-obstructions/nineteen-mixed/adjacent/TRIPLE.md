# The adjacent triple block

Use the line19 exceptional-split notation. S={t,c}, R={v,c}. An AND D joins child A, containing only raw c, to child B, containing raw t,v. The other four labels are idle. Q_j are the certified pivotal singleton points on raw j; restoring the seven full signatures from their certified points needs at most four raw-point ORs. The idle labels have no defects.

Any raw split whose cross labels have a jointly witnessed point pair must be OR, even below an earlier AND. On the two singleton witnesses, the root with loose bits pinned to one rejects at zero raw and accepts at the named active raw. If their LCA were AND, the two children would accept their union at zero raw by the opposite positive. All higher AND siblings pass by one of the two positives, contradicting the witness. The arm split inside B is therefore OR.

When both loose leaves lie inside D, all four outside lines have disjoint ordinary private groups rejecting every exterior pair. The mixed tree outside D has only ordinary point attachment leaves. On each positive of any block label all changing line leaves lie inside D, so every ordinary AND guard above D passes that singleton positive. A shared ordinary guard group containing any block label contains the whole block. The ordinary one-companion construction for each outside target is therefore valid, with either another outside line or a line in the block as companion. The verified four-group lower bound on an eleven-wire point bank excludes this placement.

Assume at most one loose leaf is inside D. For c take the union of the OR-side attachments in B, evaluating any loose leaf there at one. On every Q_c positive B must be true, with both raw arms zero; the raw-arm split is OR, so some OR-side attachment is true. If a selected attachment accepted exterior x, choose an arm y from the smallest resident arm scope on its path. At a c-active negative containing x and a point q of Q_y, the selected side passes, since its loose cofactor is enabled. All AND siblings above the side, as well as A and every AND sibling above D, pass by the y positive on q: those siblings contain no active raw y and c enables both loose bits. Thus the union rejects all exterior singletons. Its point leaves are used only for c.

For each arm keep all ordinary AND guards on its path in B whose resident set contains just that arm. The other ordinary AND guards contain both arms and pass on a negative meeting a point of the opposite arm's Q. To reject x outside t choose a t-active negative meeting x and Q_v. A is true on the v positive despite its raw c being zero. Every AND sibling on t's path that also contains v among its resident labels likewise passes by that positive, except possibly when it depends on R: v activates R but a t-negative need not. Use the symmetric rule for v and S, as follows.

* If R is in A, retain A's cofactor at raw c=0 and the t pattern for t. For v drop A by comparison from Q_t, since v activates R. There is at most one loose leaf in A.
* A special AND side in B with one resident arm is retained for that arm at its own pattern. With both arms resident, retain its cofactor for the arm not activating its loose bit, and compare from the opposite arm for the other. A loose OR side in B is used only for c.
* A separate one-bit AND guard above D is retained for the arm not activating its bit. Above D every arm shares every ordinary AND guard with the other arm.

All retained factors are true at the certified positives by raw pivotality. If they accepted exterior x, the chosen negative would pass each retained cofactor by monotonicity, each dropped guard by its opposite-arm point, the arm OR, and D. The two arm expressions and the center's OR sides have disjoint original point occurrences.

For each idle label keep its ordinary private guards. A separate special guard already assigned to an arm above D drops for the idle label via that arm, whose bit is zero. A separate guard with no block resident contains only idle labels, all with zero bit. Retain its cofactor for a unique resident if its scope is a singleton, otherwise drop it using another idle label. Among all dropped nonprivate guards on an idle path, scopes are nested. If their smallest required scope contains another idle label, that label suffices for all larger guards. Otherwise the smallest required scope contains the idle label and the full three-label block, hence both arms. The two arms can supply the respective zero bits for R and S, and either can supply an ordinary guard. Thus at most two companions are needed. triple_exterior.py checks every exterior singleton in every active idle line against every pair of other idle/arm companions, as well as the arm and center witnesses.

If both loose bits occur in one exterior attachment J and J is not an AND guard above D, no arm needs it; its idle residents all have pattern 00 and at most one needs a cofactor. If J is an AND guard above D and has any idle resident i, both arms can drop it. For t use a t-active negative meeting x, Q_v, and Q_i. The idle positive enables J at 00 and the opposite arm enables A and the ordinary guards. Symmetrically for v. Among J's idle residents retain J00 only for a unique idle, otherwise use a second idle label. For the center, c enables both loose bits and the arm comparison already enables J. If J contains no idle resident, the four idle lines have disjoint original pair-safe groups: an outside shared ordinary group containing a block member contains the full block; use a negative meeting its union of certified points. The pair-witness check in verify.py covers this requirement. Apply the four-group bank lower bound.

In the disjoint-expression cases each nonconstant cofactor on e original point occurrences needs at most e−1 gates, and disjointness gives at most P−7 additions over all seven expressions. With P=11 and at most four repairs the additional cost is at most eight. The seven singleton target masks cost 21, exceeding the twelve original point gates plus eight. This excludes all adjacent three-line exceptional blocks.
