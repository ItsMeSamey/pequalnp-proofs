# Sixteen mixed gates do not fit the 33-gate residual budget

A putative solution already needs nine point-side and seven line-side boundary wires. Sixteen mixed gates permit at most 17 boundary wires. The ten-point/seven-line case is a tree on seven line leaves, excluded by the seven-line guard theorem. For nine point wires, the preliminary nine-family verifier excludes every signature family containing a feasible smaller basis at this budget and enumerates 16,924 remaining unordered families. This verifier tests all of them from the original theorem.

Take a raw positive and negative for which the negative line vector dominates the positive one. If precisely one of the nine candidate singleton signatures contains the positive point and avoids the negative point set, that point function must reject the negative. All other point signals and all monotone line signals active at the positive are forced active at the negative. The rejecting function therefore rejects every pair of points inside the negative point set.

For each target line T, retain signals whose signature lies inside T and which the preceding implications force to reject all 36 exterior pairs. Add the four raw singleton point signals of T. A 16-subset dynamic program finds the fewest available signals whose union covers T. ORing them produces the signature of T and rejects its exterior pairs. If c_T signals suffice, at most c_T−1 extra gates are needed. The seven-target point-gate lower bound 21 therefore gives 21−sum(c_T−1) as a lower bound on the original point gates, allowing unrestricted point-only AND, OR and sharing.

The bounds are 15 for 15 families, 16 for 108, 17 for 918, 18 for 2,877, and 19 for 13,006. The family list hash is pinned. At nine point and seven line boundary signals, at most 17 point gates are available. Seven line singleton signatures are the individual lines by the equality theorem, and all boundary functions may be lowered to ORs of their singleton signatures for the mixed search. A connected circuit with 16 binary gates and 16 distinct frontier signals has one extra read. The enumeration considers every pair, both mixed operations, and keeping one operand for its second consumption. It rejects a state if a positive is dominated by a negative on all live signals. Semantic merging preserves row vectors and the point/line/mixed type. A symmetry check on the raw 28 positive and 245 maximal negative vectors gives 65 representatives for all 1,041 families. Every frontier becomes empty by gate eleven.

With nine point and eight line signals, the mixed cone is read-once. At least one line gate is needed, leaving at most 16 point gates. In each of the 123 cases the script enumerates every line program of at most one or two gates, according to the remaining budget. Repeated operands allow padding. It takes each eight-wire selection and checks all raw line-side pair constraints. Each survivor is the seven raw singleton signatures plus one further signature. All 4,092 combinations of point and line families are rejected by exhaustive mixed-only binary-tree contraction.

Run python3 verify.py with a C++20 compiler. By default the script reads nine.txt. An independently regenerated copy may be passed as the sole argument. It uses temporary executables and rejects budget-limited outcomes. The external mathematical inputs are the seven-point charge theorem, the seven-line guard theorem and the preceding family enumeration and superset exclusion.

To regenerate the charged family table and replay the [ten-point boundary proof](extra-proof.md), run `python3 verify.py nine.txt /tmp/l-meta-charges.txt` followed by `python3 verify_extra.py /tmp/l-meta-charges.txt`.
