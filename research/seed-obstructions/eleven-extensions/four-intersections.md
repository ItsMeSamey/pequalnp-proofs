# Four target masks must be intersections of point boundary signatures

Consider a residual separator with seven line boundary wires, one extra operand use in its connected mixed cone, and at most 33 total gates. The seven line wires may be lowered to their raw singleton line bits. If a point wire is shared, repeat its point leaf as two occurrences and apply the seven-line read-once guard bound of 34. A shared raw line also gives at least 35 by the repeated-line tree bound. Thus the sole shared wire D is mixed, with a tree of ancestors and e≥1 distinct resident raw lines. No other raw line in the whole circuit is duplicated.

Let Q be the OR of the raw singleton point bits of the singleton signature of D when all resident lines are zero, using constant zero when this signature is empty. At both occurrences of D in its context, replace D by Q. This never increases a value: on every input Q≤D(point,0)≤D(point,lines). Every positive involving an outside line has singleton point and all resident line bits zero, so every outside positive is preserved. We use this substitution only for its structural consequences and do not charge its construction here.

The outside context is a read-once tree on its outside raw lines and on point occurrences, including its two formal Q leaves. Collapse maximal subtrees containing only formal point leaves into point guards, even when one of those leaves has the constant-zero function. The ordinary line-guard argument applied to this context recovers each outside target as the conjunction of its private point guards. Their singleton intersection is precisely the target. These private guard sets are disjoint across outside lines. Every maximal pure point subtree that is not an original point boundary leaf contains at least one formal Q occurrence: before substitution every internal context gate was mixed, and the only changed inputs are those two positions. This also covers Q leaves themselves. Hence at most two outside targets need a private guard other than an original point boundary leaf. At least 7−e−2 of the outside target signatures are literal intersections of original point boundary signatures; the count is taken as zero if it is negative.

If e≥2, the audited resident-subtree argument in shared-subtree.md gives the private-guard conjunction for every resident target before any replacement. Its guards are original boundary leaves because the upstream tree of D is mixed. Thus all e resident targets are also exact intersections of original point masks. In all cases at least four distinct targets are recoverable from the original bank by intersection alone. For e≥2 the lower bound is five. A target T is recoverable exactly when intersecting all point masks that contain T yields T. Take the intersection of an empty list to be the full thirteen-point set.
