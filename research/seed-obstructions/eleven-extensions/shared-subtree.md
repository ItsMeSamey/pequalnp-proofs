# One shared mixed subtree with at least two resident lines

Suppose the connected mixed cone has seven line boundary wires and one extra use over a tree. Replace the seven line signals by their corresponding singleton raw bits. Suppose the sole repeated wire is a mixed gate D with at least two distinct line leaves E below it. Its upstream circuit is a tree. The context outside D is a tree with two formal occurrences of the signal from D. No resident line occurs elsewhere. Let P count the distinct point boundary wires, so the cone has P+7 mixed gates.

For every point p incident to any T in E, the root rejects p with all lines zero and accepts p with just T active. The only changing input to the context is D, so D(p,0)=0 and D(p,T)=1. Thus D satisfies the goodness property of the seven-line guard argument for its resident set. The context with both D occurrences set to one and all external lines zero accepts p. This holds for every point on every resident line.

Extract private point guards for a resident T inside D, as in the read-once theorem. The ANDs attaching guards to a group of ≥2 resident lines accept every point on every line in that group. Pick U different from T in the smallest such group on the T path, or any other resident U in the absence of grouped guards. On the raw negative extending T, the chosen pair exterior to T, and a point on U, the context with D set to one accepts, by monotonicity from that point on U. Since the root rejects, D is false. Deleting the ignorable point OR leaves can only lower D, so the surviving T term is false. Every group guard accepts by U, hence a private guard of T is false. We obtain the full pair-safe target by ANDing the private guards. Let u be the number of point leaves below D unused by all these private-guard conjunctions.

Let S be the singleton-point signature of D with all resident lines zero. It is disjoint from the union of E. Replace both appearances of D in the context by one pure point signal Q whose value is at most D(point,0) on all point sets and agrees with it on singletons. The OR of the raw singleton point bits in S always works and costs at most |S|−1 extra gates for nonempty S. This substitution preserves the outside-line positives and decreases every negative. The resulting context is a read-once tree in the outside line leaves and its point leaf occurrences. Pure point subtrees formed by normalization can be retained as guards. A pure subtree with k original leaf occurrences costs at most k−1 extra point gates. Counting those gates along with all private-guard conjunctions gives at most P−7−u+2+c added point gates across all seven outputs, where c is the cost of Q. If S is empty, replace D by zero, drop the two constant occurrences, and use at most P−7−u instead.

If S is nonempty then u≥1. In the good subtree D, after the ignorable point-OR leaves are removed, all terms require a resident line. Therefore a nonempty S requires at least one such removed point leaf. For S nonempty and c≤u−1 the total extra point cost is at most P−6. Since seven recovered signatures require 21 point gates and the original cone uses P+7 mixed gates, the original circuit has at least 34 gates.

In particular the conclusion holds whenever S is empty or consists of one point. The latter Q is a raw point with cost zero. For any choice of six or all seven resident lines, at most one point is outside their union in this instance, so all these one-surplus architectures require at least 34 gates. Other subtree sizes are covered whenever their actual all-lines-zero signature meets the stated cost condition.

The same conclusion holds whenever S is the signature of an actual raw point primitive: raw primitives are ORs of their singleton signatures on all point sets, so that primitive gives a dominated choice at cost zero. shared-subtree-geometry.py checks every resident-line subset. For four resident lines, 8 of 35 choices have every subset of the missing points available as a raw primitive (or zero). For five resident lines, 17 of 21 choices have this property. For six and seven, every choice does.

A one-gate shared mixed subtree is also excluded regardless of its operation or singleton spill. Duplicating that one gate increases total size by one. The resulting mixed tree has eight line leaf occurrences with exactly one repeated singleton, so the repeated-line lower bound of 35 gives 34 for the original DAG. Duplicating a shared pure point wire is free and the ordinary seven-line guard theorem directly gives 34 by counting point occurrences.
