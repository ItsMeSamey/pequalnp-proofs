# A redundant eighth line input in the read-once mixed tree

Suppose the boundary of the mixed tree consists of seven singleton line signatures, one further line signature S, and P point-leaf occurrences. Replace the line signals by their singleton ORs, preserving positives and lowering negative values. Write F1 for the formula with the extra line input fixed to 1. Assume F1 rejects every singleton point with every raw line bit zero. This holds whenever the target lines indexed by S have no common point. For any singleton p choose a nonincident line in S. Its pair with p lies under a sampled negative. At that negative the extra signal is 1, so monotonicity forces the stipulated rejection.

F1 accepts all required positive incidence pairs because clamping upwards preserves their acceptance. Thus it is good at the root in the sense of read_once_guards.md. In particular, every raw line is essential in F1. Let J be the maximal subtree containing the extra input and no raw lines. Its parent has a sibling subtree containing raw lines. Apart from its one extra line input J has e point leaves. Write J0 and J1 for its two point-only cofactors. They satisfy J0 ≤ J1. Each nonconstant cofactor takes at most e−1 point gates on these point leaves, since setting the extra leaf constant and simplifying gives a tree with at most e leaves.

Treat J1 as a single point leaf of F1. If it is constant, an absorption that discards its sibling with raw lines is impossible by essentiality. An identity that would simply remove the J1 gate may instead be left in place. The ordinary good-tree proof therefore applies, apart from a possible constant guard. Every OR that joins a point leaf to a line-containing subtree can discard the point leaf. At a positive the OR's point leaf is false even in F1, so deleting it also preserves acceptance in the original formula, whose values are bounded by F1. At a line-splitting OR the branch not containing the active raw line likewise evaluates false in F1 on that positive. Consequently the same OR deletions applied in the original formula preserve its positives and lower it on negatives.

If J is so discarded, the seven-line theorem applies directly to the remaining ordinary guards. Otherwise J decorates a resident group A at an AND node; A is nonempty. For a singleton line T in A the corresponding positive term requires J1 on all four points of T if T belongs to S, and J0 on all four otherwise. In particular J1 accepts all points in the union of A, and J0 accepts all points in the union of A outside S. At a negative with T active, the actual guard equals J1 whenever T belongs to S and is always at least J0.

Apply the private-guard construction to the other point leaves. For each T the ordinary shared guards on its path all contain a common other target line U when any are present. In treating J use the following rules.

- If T is outside A, J is irrelevant.
- If T is in A and S and A has a second line V, the T term has J1 true on every negative whose points meet V.
- If T is in A outside S and another line V belongs to A outside S, the T term has J true on every negative whose points meet V because J0 covers V.
- In the cases not yet covered, either A is a singleton or A outside S is a singleton. Only one target can remain in either event. For it retain the appropriate cofactor J1 or J0 as an extra private guard. On negatives the actual J is at least this cofactor.

For any T and outside pair X the checker supplies a negative with T active whose points contain X and meet both U and V whenever required. If fewer than two other lines are needed, repeat an arbitrary available one. Thus even after dropping all ordinary shared guards and every non-retained J, the private conjunction at T rejects every outside pair and accepts every singleton of T. A retained constant 1 can be omitted. Constant 0 cannot serve a positive target. Every target has at least one substantive private factor, by the acceptance/rejection conditions.

There are at most P−e ordinary private factors across the seven outputs. If a nonconstant J cofactor is retained, computing it costs at most e−1 gates and adds one factor. In all cases the point subcircuit therefore produces the seven pair-safe outputs using at most P−7 additional gates. The point-side charge bound yields at least 28−P original point gates. The tree itself has P+7 mixed gates, hence total cost at least 35 before counting any line gates.

The result also applies to concurrent S whenever the stated F1 zero-line condition happens to hold. With at least two distinct lines in S this condition can only fail at their common point, if one exists. The verifier verify_eighth_extensions.py checks the 5,292 numerical obligations involving two other lines.
