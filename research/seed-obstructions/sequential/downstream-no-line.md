# A downstream shared region with no new raw line

Assume a seven-line mixed cone has exactly two fanout-two endpoints. The upstream mixed endpoint D has the single raw line T and pD≥1 original point leaves. The downstream endpoint H has no resident line, pH point leaves, and one or both occurrences of D. Let pO count the remaining original point leaves and P=pD+pH+pO. The cone costs P+8 mixed gates. As in README.md normalize D to T AND G, with G computed from at most pD point leaves. Let H0,H1 be the point cofactors that clamp every D occurrence inside H to zero or one. They satisfy H0≤H1. A nonconstant cofactor costs at most pH−1 point gates.

Use the skeleton of the six ordinary lines outside T. With T clamped zero, D is zero and H=H0. The usual private AND sides yield six pair-safe ordinary outputs. Let b be the number of original exterior point-leaf occurrences at the skeleton's OR sides, and h the number of formal H0 occurrences in the six private expressions, counted before any simplifications. There are h≤2. Once H0 is supplied, the six outputs cost at most pO−b+h−6, since their point expressions are disjoint trees. If H0 is constant or has empty singleton signature it may be clamped to zero in the latter case (and kept as its value for a constant); this preserves ordinary positives and lowers negatives when setting it to zero. The resulting cost bound is pO−b−6. Raw line signals and constant-free point inputs all vanish at the all-zero raw input.

## Both D occurrences feed H

Every path from T reaches H before the exterior. The zero-line singleton argument therefore forces the singleton signature of H0 to be empty. All ordinary outputs can clamp it to zero. On T positives G and H1 are both true and the exterior with H set to one accepts. Take f to be the OR of its ordinary-skeleton OR-side formulas with H=1. The T function G AND H1 AND f accepts all its incident singletons. If an exterior pair X passed it, choose the accepting OR side and a T-active negative extending X and meeting an ordinary neighborhood resident at that attachment. On that negative D=1, H=H1 is true, and the selected side and the ancestor guards are true. This contradicts rejection. The T expression uses at most pD+pH+b original point leaves, disjoint from the six outputs, so the augmentation is at most P−7 and the total is at least 36. This case also permits a raw upstream T by omitting G.

## One D occurrence feeds H, one feeds the exterior

Here pH≥1 since the genuine downstream gate receives only one incoming D occurrence and has no raw line of its own. Define f(x,y) to be the OR of the exterior ordinary-skeleton OR sides, with the direct D set to x and H set to y. An empty OR is zero. The point function G AND f(1,H1) is pair-safe for T: its positives were accepted by some OR side with this setting. Conversely, any accepting exterior pair chooses a side; a T-active negative extending the pair and meeting an ordinary neighborhood below that side makes D=1 and H=H1, forces all ancestor AND guards true, and gives a contradiction.

We need an augmentation of at most P−5. If h=0, compute T by three raw OR gates. This fits since the ordinary cost is at most pO−b−6 and pD+pH≥2. If H0 can be clamped to zero this argument works for any h. Otherwise pay pH−1 to compute the required H0. The direct three-gate T construction then fits precisely whenever pD+b≥h+1. For h=2 no OR side contains H. Now the T expression G AND f(1,H1) has at most pD+b point leaves, and it is nonconstant by pair safety. Its cost is at most pD+b−1. With the ordinary and H0 costs the total is at most P−6.

The only case left is h=1, pD=1 and b=0. Here G is an existing point wire or constant one. With no point leaves at the OR attachments, f uses at most one x and one y leaf; hence f(1,y) is either one, y, or zero. Zero is impossible on the T positives. If it is one, use G itself. If f(1,y)=y then f equals y or x AND y. In either case all of these effective leaves are in a single OR side. For f=y, a nonempty H0 singleton would make that OR side true with all lines zero at that point. Extending its singleton to a sampled negative meeting a resident ordinary line at the attachment activates all ancestor AND guards, contradicting rejection. Hence the empty-signature case already covers f=y.

In the case f=x AND y, if pH≤2 the two cofactors H0,H1 together cost at most pH gates. They are both constant prunings of one point-leaf tree (as in verify_prunings.py). Computing G AND H1 costs at most one additional gate. Together with the six ordinary expressions, cost at most pO+1−6, the total is at most P−5.

Assume pH≥3. Let L be the unique ordinary line whose private expression uses the one H0 slot. Delete that occurrence by replacing it with zero and let M be the singleton points of L no longer accepted. Each point of M necessarily activates H0 and hence H1. If at least two such points lie outside T, the point wire G is already a pair-safe output for T. Indeed, a T-exterior pair X accepted by G extends to a T-active negative meeting one of the two points of L and also the ordinary line V at the attachment of x AND y. If V=L, meeting a marker suffices; the verifier with any third ordinary line still provides such an extension. On the negative D is true by G and H is true by H1 at the marker. The OR side and all its ancestor guards are therefore true, a contradiction. Use the original private expressions including H0 for the ordinary outputs in this case.

If at most one member of M is outside T, then |M|≤2 since the lines meet at one point. Use the private expressions with H0 zero, adjoining the raw singleton point bits of M by OR to the output for L. These OR additions preserve exterior-pair rejection since their points lie in L. The six nonconstant expressions use at most pO+|M| leaves and no H0 computation, so they cost at most pO+|M|−6. Compute T directly by three gates. As pH≥3≥|M|+1 and pD=1, the total is at most P−5.

Thus the split-use case costs at least 34, and the both-nested case at least 36. The marked-point checks used here are included in verify_geometry.py.

## Raw upstream T in the split-use case

Now pD=0 and G is constant one. The same skeleton and private expressions apply, and h=2 is covered by the same cofactor bound (a nonconstant point output uses at most b−1 gates after fixing the direct T slot). If h=0, the direct three-gate T construction fits whenever pH+b≥2. The remaining possibility pH=1, b=0 makes the T function f(1,H1) a function of H1 alone, where H1 is constant or one point leaf. Since a pair-safe T must be nonconstant, its output is just that point leaf.

For h=1, computing H0 and T directly fits whenever b≥2. For b≤1 and pH≤4, compute both H cofactors jointly with at most pH gates by verify_prunings.py. As one H occurrence already belongs to an ordinary private expression, f(1,H1) contains at most one H1 occurrence and b other point occurrences, so it costs at most b gates. Even charging one H0 leaf to the six private expressions, the total is at most P−5.

Suppose pH≥5 and b=1. Remove H0 from the affected ordinary expression, restore its at most four missing singletons by raw OR leaves, and compute T directly. The cost at most pO−1+4−6+3=pO fits P−5. In the remaining b=0 case f(1,H1) cannot be constant because it is pair-safe. If f=y the H0 singleton signature is empty as above, so ignore it for the ordinary outputs and compute H1 with at most pH−1 gates. If f=x AND y, use the same affected set M as above. Two members of M outside T would make the constant G=1 pair-safe, which is impossible. Therefore |M|≤2; deleting H0, restoring M and computing T raw costs at most pO+2−6+3=pO−1≤P−5. This completes the raw-upstream case.
