# Two sequential shared mixed gates need at least 34 residual gates

Assume the connected mixed cone has seven singleton line boundary signals and exactly two wires of fanout two, both genuinely mixed. Their endpoints D then H each have one resident line, T then U. At least one use of D feeds the tree region ending at H. All other wires in the cone have one use. The two possible distributions of D usages are covered below. No bound for a raw upstream duplicated line in the split-use case is asserted here.

Replace the seven line signals by their singleton raw bits, preserving positives and lowering negatives. Write pD,pH,pO for the distinct point boundary leaf occurrences in the tree regions ending at D, H, and the output. Their sum is P. The connected cone has P+8 mixed gates by the binary operand count. To force total size at least 34, it suffices to reconstruct seven pair-safe point outputs from the original point circuit in at most P−5 additional gates. Each such output must accept its four incident singletons and reject all pairs of distinct points outside its line. The 21-point-gate lower bound for those seven outputs is an input theorem (equality.md, “An optimal 13-mixed-gate circuit needs exactly 34 residual gates”).

Every point pair with no lines is dominated by a sampled negative; it can in fact be extended to meet any specified residual line. Every pair X outside a line T can also be extended to a sampled negative with T active and points meeting any two specified other lines. More precisely, for distinct T,U,V and any two of the three points of U outside T there is such a negative containing X, meeting the two-point set and meeting V. The bundled verifier checks these facts.

An endpoint with a resident line L has empty singleton signature with all lines zero. To see this, the exterior with its endpoint pinned to one accepts each singleton on L even with all lines zero, since the L-positive forces the endpoint to one and all influence of L runs through it. Acceptance of an arbitrary p by the endpoint at zero lines would therefore make the original root accept the zero-line union of p with an incident point of L.

The region of D has the sole raw line T. At each T-positive, toggling T must toggle D, so every point AND sibling on the path from T to D accepts its point. Dropping point OR siblings can only decrease values. Replace D by T AND G where G is the conjunction of these AND siblings. This preserves T positives and every other positive (D was zero there). Computing G costs at most pD−1 additional point gates when nonconstant; if there are no guards omit it as constant one. A genuinely mixed first region has pD≥1.

We use the following elementary argument several times. The skeleton of a formula on selected once-occurring ordinary raw lines consists of paths from those leaves to the root. A node with two skeleton children is a split. A node with one skeleton child is an attachment, whose other child is its side formula. Set the special inputs to zero for ordinary singleton positives. Each ordinary positive forces acceptance while each zero-line pair containing its point forces rejection. A split AND is impossible: at the two positives, one from each child, the opposite child has all lines zero and is true. At the union of the two points both children would be true with zero lines. The rejection propagates from the root through splitting ORs; it also propagates through attached ANDs because their side is true on every resident ordinary singleton. At OR attachments the side is false on every resident positive with its ordinary line zero. Thus splits are OR, and every AND side evaluated at special zero covers its resident ordinary neighborhoods. For an ordinary L, retain only AND sides with sole ordinary resident L; all other AND sides on its path cover a common other ordinary line V by nesting. On a chosen L-active negative extending an outside pair and meeting V, those dropped sides are true. Acceptance by the retained conjunction would force root acceptance. These private conjunctions give pair-safe ordinary outputs. After constants have been fixed, a nonconstant side expression with k point-leaf occurrences uses at most k−1 point gates. The private outputs use disjoint occurrences.

## Both uses of D feed H

Both T and U influence the exterior only through H. The exterior pinned at H=1 accepts every singleton on T or U even with its own raw lines zero. H has empty singleton signature with all lines zero, so replacing both its exterior occurrences by zero preserves the five outside-line positives and lowers negatives. The ordinary skeleton argument gives five pair-safe outputs using at most pO−5 gates, counting any point subtrees formed by constant simplification inside the ordinary private expressions.

Inside H, unfold the two normalized T AND G occurrences. There is one ordinary line U and two T occurrences. The ordinary U-positive toggles H. Its path sides at AND cover the U singleton neighborhood with T=0. Construct the U output as their conjunction. If it accepted an exterior pair X, take a U-active negative extending X and meeting T. Its U path would make H true, and the exterior pinned to one accepts because its points meet T. This is impossible. For T take the OR of the 1-cofactors of all OR attachments to the U path, fixing the T leaves to one while keeping the point guards G in their two occurrences. At a T-positive, H is true with U zero; tracing down the path eventually finds a true OR side. If a T 1-cofactor accepted an outside pair X of T, a T-active negative extending X and meeting U activates this side, every higher AND side by U, and the pinned exterior by U. This too is impossible. The two recovered outputs use disjoint point occurrences, at most pH+2 including both copies of G. Hence at most pH added gates suffice once G is given. With the outside and G costs the augmentation is at most P−6 and the total is at least 35. If G is constant, removing its slots only helps. In this both-nested branch alone, an upstream raw T is also allowed: no G slots are needed, the two nonconstant internal outputs use at most pH−2 gates and the total is at least 36.

## One use of D feeds H and the other feeds the exterior

Here H sees U and the input D once each. On all U positives H must be pivotal because U has no other route. Let W be the T-incident singletons on which H is true with only T active. If W is empty, set the incoming D slot of H to zero; this keeps all positives and lowers negatives. The circuit has at most one surplus use and is excluded by the one-surplus seven-line theorem (repeated-one-label.md and aggregate.md in the companion one-surplus material). If W is nonempty, the H region is good on all four U singletons and the assigned T fragment W with D viewed as the second raw label (G is true on all T positives). With U=D=0 this region rejects any point pair X. Otherwise extend X to meet U in a sampled negative: the exterior pinned at H=1 accepts since it did on the U-positive even with its direct D slot zero. The true H would force root acceptance.

The two independent labels in H cannot join by a split AND, by the ordinary zero-pair argument at one point from U and one from W. Dropping point-only OR attachments preserves all assigned positives and lowers negatives. Thus H can be replaced by

`C AND ((U AND A) OR (D AND R))`.

Here C,A,R are conjunctions of disjoint sets of H-region point leaves, with an empty conjunction interpreted as one. Both C and A cover U, while C and R are true at all points of W. The replacement also keeps the H value on all outside singleton positives: with no assigned line active H was already zero. A T-positive outside W already had H false.

In the exterior apply the skeleton argument to the five ordinary lines, with the direct D and shared H formal slots zero for the ordinary singleton positives. Let f(x,y) be the OR of all its OR-attachment side formulas, where the direct D slot is x and either occurrence of H is y. Let b be the total number of point-leaf occurrences in these OR sides. The five ordinary outputs can be reconstructed in at most pO−b−5 gates, since these OR sides are disjoint from their private AND sides. Whenever a particular OR side is true at a negative meeting an ordinary line V resident at that attachment, every AND side above it is true and it forces root acceptance.

If b+pD+pH≥6, constructing the two remaining neighborhoods directly with three raw OR gates each already gives total augmentation ≤P−5. Below assume b+pD+pH≤5. Use the two point expressions

`K_T = G AND f(1,R)` and `K_U = C AND A AND f(0,1)`.

Both accept their four positive singletons by tracing the true root down the ordinary skeleton with all ordinary lines zero. For T the substitution allows y=R instead of C AND R, so it only enlarges the OR sides. For U, its positive forces H=1 and D=0. For pair safety of K_T, let its alleged outside pair X select an accepting OR side. Choose a T-active negative extending X and meeting both U and a resident ordinary V of this side. On this negative G is true, D is true, and C is true by U. Hence H is at least R, making the selected side true. For K_U choose a U-active negative extending X and meeting the selected side's ordinary V; both C,A are true there by the hypothesized acceptance of X, so H=1 and the real D is at least zero. Both cases contradict rejection.

### Finite cofactor count

If R is the empty conjunction, the two cofactors f(1,1) and f(0,1) are prunings by constants of the same point-leaf tree. To see this, suppress chains whose other child has no point leaves; fixing x,y can either pass the remaining child or replace the subtree by a constant. A whole point subtree can be made constant by setting all its leaves to the same constant. The resulting common point tree keeps the operator of every original node with two point-containing children. For b=0 both outputs are constants. For 1≤b≤4, verify_prunings.py checks that any distinct pair of such prunings is jointly computable in at most b gates, with constants simplified freely. Equal cofactors require only one read-once construction of at most b−1 gates. Construct G and combine it with its cofactor for at most pD gates; combine C,A and the other cofactor for at most pH gates. Thus the required special outputs cost at most pD+pH+b.

Otherwise let r≥1 be the number of point leaves of R. Then b≤3. The universal verifier treats R as an independent point variable. The two symbolic cofactors are obtained from a single formula with b independently labeled point occurrences, at most one x occurrence giving the pair (1,0), and at most two y occurrences giving the pair (R,1). Exhaustive formula recursion by partitioning the leaf multiset enumerates 4, 12, 67 and 627 distinct cofactor pairs for b=0,1,2,3 respectively. Reachability by at most b+1 AND/OR gates on the b+1 point variables is checked by enumerating sets of signals. This covers every pair except twelve, all at b=3. When the pair is reachable, preparing R takes at most r−1 gates, adjoining G costs at most pD gates including its computation, and adjoining the C,A factors to the U output costs at most pH−r. Altogether the two special outputs require at most pD+pH+b gates.

For any of the twelve remaining pairs b=3 forces pD=pH=r=1. Thus C=A=1, R is an original point leaf, and G is either an original point leaf or constant one. Write F(R)=f(1,R) and J=f(0,1). The verifier checks the truth-table implications F(0) implies J and J implies F(1), and that J takes at most two point gates. Since K_U=J is pair-safe, its singleton signature is exactly U. Consequently any T-positive accepted with R=0 must be the intersection point of T and U.

There are at most two nonzero OR-attachment summands of the cofactor pair in any of these twelve cases. Otherwise group three or more summands by associativity as a nontrivial OR of two parts, one itself an OR of two nonzero parts, contradicting the checked partition test. A zero pair contributes nothing. Since F(1) holds at all four U singletons, at least two of the three U points outside T satisfy the same OR side with x=y=1. Fix this side, these two points, and its resident ordinary line V. If an outside pair X of T activated both G and R, the marked-point extension provides a T-active negative containing X and meeting both V and the chosen two-point set. The real D and H are both true there. The selected OR side accepts by its marked point, and every ancestor AND passes by V, contradicting rejection. Thus G AND R is already safe on every outside pair. It accepts all T singletons except possibly the intersection with U, since a missing singleton would have to be accepted through F(0). Restore the intersection if needed by OR with its raw point. This takes at most two gates, in addition to the at most two for J; the available special-output budget is pD+pH+b=5.

Adding the five ordinary outputs gives at most P−5 extra gates in every split-use case. The lower bound follows. The finite parts use only Python's standard library. Run

```sh
python verify_geometry.py
python verify_prunings.py
python verify_sequential_small.py
```
