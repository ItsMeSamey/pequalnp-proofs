# Missing-singleton nine-line banks at nineteen mixed gates

A residual circuit of at most 33 gates cannot have eleven point boundary signals, nine line boundary signals of the form below, and a read-once mixed cone. The form consists of the six raw line singleton labels other than T, together with three distinct pair labels b_i = T ∨ U_i. The decoded sample constraints allow precisely 24 choices. The three nonraw line signatures cost at least three line gates. A binary tree on twenty leaves has nineteen mixed gates, leaving at most eleven pure point gates.

The entire checker runs with Python 3 and a C++20 compiler supporting unsigned 128-bit arithmetic. Run `python verify.py`. The line classification uses neither a SAT solver nor a graph library. The [four-group certificate](../eleven-irreducible/README.md) documents the underlying point projection argument and its separate seven-neighborhood theorem.

## Nine labeled line leaves

Lower each boundary signal on its own side to the OR of the coordinate singletons it accepts. All positives have one selected point and one selected raw line, so this agrees on positives. Lowering on negatives preserves rejection. For the argument about original point guard leaves one can keep the actual monotone point signals throughout. Treat the nine entering line signals as independently variable at the leaves of the mixed tree. This gives a monotone Boolean function on synthetic line activations. At raw line L its leaf activation is S_L. Thus S_T consists of the three pair leaves, S_Ui consists of its ordinary raw leaf and b_i, and each of the three remaining labels V has just its ordinary raw leaf.

Remove the eleven point leaves to obtain the nine-leaf binary line skeleton. A removed point leaf occurred on a unary segment of this skeleton and was attached by a mixed AND or OR. Every original gate remaining as a binary branch has two line-bearing children. The least common ancestor of two line leaves therefore has an unambiguous AND or OR color.

Pick a point p of T exterior to all U_i. Such a point exists because distinct projective lines meet once and T has four points. At p with raw line T all three pair leaves are pivotal. Turning any one off leaves at most the two other pair leaves active. That synthetic input is dominated by loading the two corresponding exterior raw lines, and a sampled negative extends that point and those two raw lines. All three pairwise least common ancestors of the b_i are consequently AND. At an OR splitting two pivotal inputs, an accepting child would keep the gate true when the other input was removed.

At any fixed point assignment, with all ordinary raw leaves zero, the function of the three b leaves is either constant or the conjunction of a subset. After fixing point leaves and other line leaves, each node is constant, passes a surviving child, or joins subexpressions. Every node capable of joining two surviving b-containing children is AND.

## The 118 possible line graphs

Use an edge for AND and a nonedge for OR. For a given raw negative with point set Q and line assignment Z, fix the point input to Q. Take a coordinatewise maximal false assignment of the nine synthetic line leaves above the actual nine-bit signature of Z. Its zero leaves, called a maxterm, are pairwise OR-separated in the skeleton. Indeed flipping any one zero leaf makes the root true. An AND node with two such leaves in opposite children could not have been made true separately by each flip. The maxterm includes at most one b leaf.

For every raw line L meeting Q the maxterm must hit S_L. A singleton positive on L at a point of Q would otherwise be below this false input. When the zero leaves include b_i, they must also include the ordinary marker of every touched ordinary line except possibly U_i. When they include no b, T must miss Q and all touched ordinary markers must be present. These at most four candidate subcliques must avoid the actual active coordinates at Z. A surviving graph must contain an OR clique of one of those forms at each negative.

For each raw negative, any edge required to be OR by every candidate there is pinned to OR. Across the negatives these pins together with the three known AND edges leave only nine free bits of the 36 pair colors. Exhaust their 512 choices. Binary least-common-ancestor color graphs have no induced four-vertex path in their AND graph: recursive joining by OR is disjoint union and joining by AND takes the complete join. An induced path on four vertices and its complement are both connected, so neither operation can create the first induced path spanning both children. The checker drops the forbidden patterns and rechecks each negative's candidate cliques. The two raw-symmetry orbits produce the same 118 surviving labeled graphs.

## Six pivotal markers

A marker for a line is a physical leaf pivotal at every incident singleton positive of that line. Use six distinct markers exclusive to six distinct selected lines. If none of the single pair leaves ever accepts a singleton in isolation, take all six ordinary raw leaves. Turning off the raw marker of U_i in an incident positive leaves b_i in isolation, so that marker is pivotal. The three ordinary V markers are also pivotal since setting them to zero gives the zero-line input.

Otherwise take an isolated accepting b_i on a singleton p. At most one index has such a singleton: if b_i and b_j separately accept at p and q, both also accept at their point union when activated separately. The b-only cofactor there is a conjunction or constant, so the zero activation would accept. Every zero-line input on a point pair is dominated by a negative. The same argument shows that b_i is pivotal at every T-positive q. Otherwise at p ∪ q both b_i alone and the other pair leaves without b_i would accept, with the same contradiction.

Use b_i as the T marker, the other two U raw markers, and the three V raw markers. These six leaves are exclusive within the selected six labels. Their pairwise branch colors are OR. The raw computation finds sixteen zero-line negative point masks Q touching all seven lines; every one of the thirteen points occurs in at least one such Q. Choose one containing p. At Q the maxterm must contain b_i because isolated b_i already accepts. It cannot contain either of the other pair leaves. It must therefore include each of the other five ordinary markers in order to hit all their S_L. This proves the OR-clique condition checked in the three exceptional modes.

## Four disjoint original point-guard groups

Consider a selected target L and the path from its pivotal marker to the root. At every L-positive the value depends on that marker, hence every AND sibling is true there. Erase OR siblings, which only lowers the computed term. Retain as private guards the original point leaves attached by AND at unary path segments for which L is the only selected marker below the attachment. These sets of retained point leaves are disjoint for different targets. They accept every singleton on L.

The finite test asks whether two other selected lines K and V witness all remaining AND siblings for L. For a discarded unary point guard above a group of markers, at least one witness must have its own marker in that group. The guard accepts every singleton on that witness since its marker is pivotal. For a binary AND with the L-marker in child A and line-containing sibling B, at least one witness K must have its marker in A and satisfy S_K ∩ B ⊆ S_L ∩ B. The sibling then accepts every singleton on K when the synthetic lines are set to S_L. It was true at every K-positive, and its relevant line input only increases. Witnesses can differ across siblings. A binary AND separating two nonempty marker groups is impossible if one target L's activation misses the opposite side B: at the intersection p with a target K from B the child A is false at zero lines because L's marker is pivotal there, while B needs to be false at zero lines because K's marker is pivotal. At p,L the branch B would stay false. The checker also verifies that this optional impossibility test eliminates no enumerated tree.

For every exterior point pair of L, a sampled negative exists with L active, that pair selected and selected points on both other witness lines. The verifier checks all 3,780 instances. If all retained private guards accepted the exterior pair, all path AND siblings would be true on that negative's point assignment at S_L, by monotonicity and the witness tests. Together with the active marker they would make the root true. Thus the intersection of the retained original guards rejects every exterior pair. At least one guard is retained because otherwise that conjunction would always be true.

For each surviving line graph recursively enumerate all binary refinements: partition each leaf subset into two children in every unordered way whose cross-pair colors are constant, and recurse on both. The 118 graphs have 127,440 refinements in total. With no isolated pair leaf the counts for four, five and six successfully witnessed targets are 630, 94,005 and 32,805. For each isolated index the six-marker OR clique leaves 74,400 compatible refinements: 5,760 have four successes and 68,640 have five. Every possible tree therefore has four disjoint groups of original point boundary leaves whose conjunctions recover the four corresponding target masks and reject every exterior pair.

## Eleven point gates cannot supply the four groups

The source point/ adapts the projection-only verifier in four-group-certificate.zip to threshold twelve. It covers eleven boundary slots even when some signatures repeat or some slots are redundant for raw separation. It discards every use of a unique private raw row to justify a slot's existence. Only raw rows with a unique possible covering slot propagate forced coordinate choices. Four distinct exact full targets already cost twelve gates by the 3k point theorem. To count certainly paid distinct masks in a partial interval, greedily select a subset of the nonraw intervals whose mask ranges are mutually disjoint. Only those charge gates; repeated mask slots cannot double-charge. For projected duplicates among the selected distinct masks the usual excess gates can be deleted.

A disjoint guard group either contains the exact target singleton signature (retain one such mask), or consists of at least three strict supersets of the target. Every exterior point pair misses some member's signature, by monotonicity and pair rejection. With three strict supersets the three exterior portions must be pairwise disjoint. For a group of m masks, at most ceil(m/2) already intersect exactly in the target: split into near-equal halves, and if neither half has that intersection choose a common exterior point from each half, contradicting exterior-pair rejection. This is a reconstruction statement on singleton signatures and does not require the normalized singleton member itself to reject exterior pairs.

The point computation grants the union of all 24 symmetry images of the 31 actual masks, namely 55 masks, and checks all five symmetry orbits of four targets. The verified seven-row clique for each orbit anchors the four group guards and three raw separation witnesses in distinct slots. It exhausts the assignments of all remaining slots, respecting symmetry within each class. Projecting to any retained line subfamily J lets the verifier delete paid projected transversals or duplicates and reconstruct targets using known group intersections, clean partial masks, or raw singletons. If h gates can be deleted and d more recover the retained targets, the unprojected circuit needs at least 3|J|−d+h. The embedded certificate provides the exact derivation and replay of 3|J|, allowing every transversal of the retained lines for free.

The five quartet searches visit 4,309, 1,902, 1,498, 4,002 and 5,014 nodes. No eleven-slot bank survives. This point calculation includes redundant slots and therefore needs no separate point-width-ten lower bound.
