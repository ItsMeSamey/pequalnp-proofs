# No 33-gate residual circuit with 17 mixed gates

A mixed gate has a point input and a line input among its transitive ancestors. Count the distinct purely point and purely line signals entering the connected mixed output cone as its boundary wires. Earlier obstructions force at least ten point boundary wires, seven line boundary wires, and 17 mixed gates in a 33-gate residual circuit. A cone of 17 binary gates can use at most 18 boundary wires. At eleven point wires and seven line wires it is a tree and the seven-line guard theorem gives total size at least 34. This certificate excludes the two remaining possibilities, ten point wires with seven or eight line wires.

Each real point or line signal can be replaced in the mixed cone by the OR of its accepted singleton coordinates. This preserves every residual positive, since both inputs there are singletons, and can only lower negatives. Equal singleton signatures can denote different functions on the original negatives. The tests retain them as separate signals. line_basis.py independently verifies that with eight line boundary signals all seven line singleton signatures occur. It anchors seven slots at the pairwise-incompatible line packing pairs and checks that no eight signatures can omit a specified singleton and meet every mandatory separation.

## Split on ten point signatures

Every feasible family either contains a feasible smaller family or is inclusion-minimal. The seven- and eight-boundary certificates characterize all bases through eight. The nine-boundary archive enumerates the 16,924 irreducible nine-signature families. Its ordered list nine.txt is hash-pinned and was regenerated by the preceding certificate.

* If the ten signatures contain the seven canonical neighborhoods, retain five targets, requiring at least 15 paid set gates even when all transverse masks are free. The other two full targets are transverse to the retained five and each requires a paid gate. Thus at least 17 point gates are needed.
* For all 202 other minimal eight-signature bases, extend-eight verifies the two-extra profile and charge proof requiring at least 17 point gates.
* For every minimal nine-signature basis, extend-nine enumerates all 8192 extra masks, including zero and repeats. Raw dominance, point reconstruction and near-equality filters leave two lists for the mixed searches.
* irreducible-ten enumerates all 1,218,270 inclusion-minimal ten-signature families, reconstructs lower bounds with raw data, and applies the five-target and equality tests. Only 23,274 can use at most 16 point gates, and only 6,543 can use at most 15.

With seven line wires the mixed cone has one extra read. The one-copy checker tries both operations and every pair of operands, including retaining an operand for a second consumption. With eight line wires it is read-once and spends at least one paid line gate. The read-once checker tests affordable extensions of the seven singleton line signatures. For the nine-basis extensions it tests all 128 possibilities for the eighth signature. Both searches preserve mandatory positive/negative separations and reject all candidates.

Every 33-gate residual solution would therefore require **at least 18 mixed gates**. Unrestricted feasibility remains open.

## Reproduce

Use Python 3.10+, a C++20 compiler, GNU coreutils, gzip, Bash and the Python SAT dependency in irreducible-ten/five-target/requirements.txt. From this directory run:

```sh
bash verify.sh /tmp/seventeen-check
```

The driver rebuilds and byte-compares the mixed-search inputs, then reruns both searches, including the complete indexed augmentation files required for the one-copy proof. The pinned raw statement and checked nine-family list are in irreducible-ten/source. Stage-specific README files explain the counting arguments and search domains. The prior structural dependencies are raw-to-residual optimum transfer, the point-gate lower bound and equality for seven pair-safe targets, the seven-line guard bound, and the at-least-ten point-boundary result.

## Verification scope

Two independent runs regenerated the 1,218,270 irreducible ten-mask families. The mixed-tree checker rejected all 479,589 cases and accepted its positive control. The one-copy checker exhausted 1,123 representatives from irreducible families, 1,713 from distinct-mask extensions and 65 from repeated or zero masks. These are finite computations on the pinned rows. The reduction to these cases also uses the structural arguments listed above.

The first combined replay exposed a representation mismatch between the nine-mask extension stage and the one-copy checker. Each extension now exports its one-based base index, extra mask and lower bound. A full set comparison against the inputs used in the separate one-copy replay returned equality for all 26,754 distinct-extension rows and 1,122 repeated-or-zero extension rows.

After the conversion, both downstream mixed searches passed on the regenerated outputs. The [final-stage log](final-stages.log) records the extinction depths and tree control. The earlier enumeration, histogram and byte-comparison stages had already passed in the combined run.

A subsequent [complete driver replay](full-verified.log) regenerated the inputs and ran both downstream searches in sequence.
