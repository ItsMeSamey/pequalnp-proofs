# Seven pair-safe target outputs require 21 point gates

Suppose the point circuit produces all seven four-point neighborhoods as singleton signatures and each such output rejects every two-element set outside its neighborhood. Three ORs for each neighborhood achieve 21 gates. The following counting proof gives the matching lower bound.

Call a gate a charge for T when its singleton signature is contained in T and has at least two elements. Such a charge is unique to one T, since two neighborhoods intersect in exactly one point. Every free primitive whose signature is contained in T has signature of size at most one, because the supplied point preprocessing only makes subsets of the first six neighborhoods. A first charge made by OR thus has size at most two. If made by AND, its two parents must have points outside T, because their signatures contain the charge. Choosing an outside point from each parent gives an accepted off-line input of size at most two. Such an input extends to an off-line pair. Thus a first charge cannot itself be a valid output.

Suppose there are only two charges for T. Its output must be the second. If it were an OR, the first charge would need at least three singleton points and be off-line-pair safe, which is impossible by the preceding argument. If it is an AND, both parents contain T, and one parent must be clean (signature contained in T), else the AND accepts an off-line pair. The clean parent is the first charge and has signature T. It is therefore an AND of two strict supersets A,B of T. The other output parent C must be a strict superset of T as reusing the first charge cannot repair its errors. The three outside portions must be pairwise disjoint: if two share a point, selecting it and an outside point of the third makes the output accept an off-line pair. Therefore the three pairwise intersections are exactly T. Each of these signals is a paid gate (free primitives have size at most four), and none is a charge for any target.

Let h count paid gates outside all charge classes and let q count the targets with two charges. The count is at least h+21−q. Each two-charge target uses three different unordered pairs of h gates. Their intersections identify the target, so pairs cannot serve two targets. Thus 3q ≤ h(h−1)/2. If at most 20 gates suffice, q≥h+1 and h≤6 since q≤7. For h≤6 the pair inequality gives q≤h, a contradiction. This proves at least 21 paid point gates given the boundary signatures.
