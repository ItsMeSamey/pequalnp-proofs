# Eleven irreducible point signatures and seven line wires

This replay addresses a connected residual cone with eleven distinct point boundary signals, seven line boundary signals, and one extra operand occurrence. A binary tree on these eighteen inputs has seventeen gates. The extra operand use brings the mixed count to eighteen, leaving at most fifteen pure point gates in a circuit of size at most 33. A bank is irreducible here if deleting any of its eleven singleton signatures destroys some required separation on raw point–line samples with comparable line inputs. Reducible banks are handled by separate nine- and ten-signature arguments.

The replay uses two prior lemmas. The [seven-neighborhood package](../../seven-neighborhoods/README.md) contains the independent finite proof and replay of the first. The notes read_once_guards.md, repeated_line.md, shared-subtree.md and four-intersections.md cover the guard arguments for this instance. The driver checks all 3,780 raw negative extensions for a target, exterior point pair and two other lines, as well as the zero-line pair domination. The point-mask theorem gives a lower bound of 3k binary unions or intersections for any k of the seven target lines, even if every mask meeting each retained line at most once is free. The one-extra-use guard argument gives four distinct target lines with pairwise disjoint sets of point boundary guards. For each of those four lines T, all its guards accept all four individual points of T, and their conjunction rejects every pair of exterior points. The search below only needs this consequence. It allows all 35 choices of four targets.

The four-group reduction uses the two appearances of the sole shared mixed wire D. If D has e resident lines, its replacement by the OR of its line-zero singleton spill produces a read-once tree on the other lines. After extracting the private guard conjunctions, at most two outside groups contain a changed point-only block, since each changed block contains at least one of the two replacement occurrences. The remaining outside guards are original boundary leaves. When e is at least two, each resident line also has its original private guards by the resident-subtree result. Point leaves are disjoint across these private groups and between the resident subtree and outside context. Thus e=1 gives at least four groups, and e≥2 gives at least five whenever four outside groups alone do not suffice. The notes four-intersections.md and shared-subtree.md give the reduction in the original terminology. Shared pure wires and repeated raw line leaves are excluded by the previous read-once bounds.

## Guard groups

Write the singleton signature of a guard as a thirteen-bit set. For every pair of exterior points of T, some guard in its group must have a signature disjoint from that pair. Otherwise every guard would accept the pair by monotonicity. In particular, intersecting the guards' singleton sets gives exactly T. If a group contains a signature equal to T, retain just that one mask for this combinatorial test. This retains the required mask property. Otherwise each guard has some exterior point. A group with one or two strict supersets fails the exterior-pair test, since one exterior point from each member hits all the members. Thus a normalized group either has size one and equals T, or has at least three strict supersets. For three members their exterior parts are nonempty and pairwise disjoint. Sharing of an exterior point between two parts would let that point and one point from the third part hit all three.

To compute T from m given group masks, at most ceil(m/2) operands suffice. Partition the group into two parts whose sizes differ by at most one. If both intersections contain an exterior point, one such point from each part hits every member of the group. For m=1 use the existing exact mask. In all cases at most floor((m−1)/2) additional binary intersections suffice. This claim uses the singleton signatures alone after normalization.

## Input and symmetry checks

The Python driver decodes the 676-row statement, checks its fingerprint, derives the 28 required positives and 245 maximal negatives, and computes all 24 joint point and line coordinate permutations preserving those samples. It derives five orbits of target quartets. The orbit union of the 31 free point-primitive masks has size 55. Allowing all 55 simultaneously makes the cost bound invariant under the 24 permutations. Every one of them is a transversal of the seven targets.

For a positive with singleton point a and a negative with point set b, take the pair (a,b) whenever the negative contains all the positive line coordinates. The possible point boundary signatures X that separate it have a in X and b disjoint from X. For each a retain the maximal b. This gives the independently reconstructed 1,323 rows. An irreducible bank must cover them all and each of its slots must cover some row uniquely. Indeed a private row for one slot extends to a maximal row with the same positive. A slot covering that maximal row covers the original one as well.

For each quartet a seven-row incompatibility clique is supplied and validated. The first four anchors each require a whole target T and exclude an exterior pair. They are satisfied by a slot chosen from the corresponding private group. The last three anchors are required raw rows. Distinct rows in the clique cannot be satisfied by the same signature because some required point of one row is forbidden by the other. The seven anchors therefore label distinct slots. The four remaining slots are unanchored. Enumerate the assignments of all seven slots other than the first four to the four groups or to the unused class. The unanchored slots are ordered by class, and then by numeric value within a class. Within a class an anchored slot uses the least compatible available signature in preference to an unanchored slot. Each signature can satisfy at most one anchor, so this choice eliminates only alternative labellings.

The bitwise search maintains forced presences and absences for each slot. It propagates raw rows that can be covered by only one slot. Every slot in an irreducible bank also needs a private row. Intersecting the requirements common to all its possible private rows forces further bits. The search branches both ways on a remaining bit. It rejects incompatible role assignments, groups of size two, six simultaneously exact canonical lines, and incomplete or redundant raw coverages. Six exact targets would need at least eighteen point gates. It enforces that nonsingleton groups have strict supersets, enforces the disjoint-exterior condition for three members, and enforces every exterior-pair condition in larger groups.

## The projection bound

For each subset J of target lines project every point mask to the union of J. This commutes with both gate operations. All masks meeting every line in J at most once may be taken for free in the 3|J| bound. A known paid boundary output that becomes such a transversal can have its construction gate removed and its projected mask used for free. If several known paid boundary signals become the same projected mask, retain the earliest and delete the others. Distinct slots in an irreducible bank have distinct singleton signatures, hence known paid slots initially count distinct gates. The search uses conditions that hold throughout a partial interval of possible signatures.

If the remaining circuit can recover each target of J using d further gates, and h paid gates have been deleted by projection, its original cost is at least 3|J|−d+h. Three singleton unions per target give a default construction. A known clean pair on T saves one gate. A clean triple, two complementary clean pairs, or two full-bearing masks intersecting exactly in T save two. An existing full clean T saves three. Clean means that all exterior points disappear on the chosen projection. The half-group argument above gives additional recoveries for the four private targets on every projection that retains them. Independent improvements for the same target are combined by taking their maximum. The counter computes the bound for all 128 subsets of the seven targets and excludes a bank if any lower bound reaches sixteen.

## Final frontier

For each surviving bank, replace each true point boundary signal by the OR of the raw singleton points in its signature. This replacement agrees on singleton positives and can only decrease all negatives. The seven line signals likewise use their singleton raw line bits. The exact frontier search projects the checked positive and negative samples, removes dominated negatives, and processes successive binary gates with the one permitted surplus wire use. A frontier is a multiset of live signatures, including each wire's positive/negative sample values and whether it is pure on one side or already mixed. A gate consumes two live occurrences and appends their union or intersection. Exactly one use may retain an operand for a second consumption. Two pure operands on the same side would constitute a pure gate outside this mixed cone. A positive/negative pair must remain separated in the forward direction by some live coordinate, since later monotone operations cannot restore this separation after it is lost. The transition checks the pairs for which a consumed wire was the only distinguisher. All other pairs are preserved by an untouched wire or by both operands. Every surviving bank is passed to that checker. The Python driver requires a terminal status of zero on every bank and treats an unresolved state cap as a failure.

Run `python verify.py` with Python 3 and GCC or Clang available under `c++`. The counter uses C++20 bit operations and native unsigned 128-bit arithmetic. The eleven-bank driver uses only the Python standard library. The separate seven-mask certificate lists its own solver dependencies. It builds temporary binaries and checks all inputs from the statement. It prints the relaxed bank counts and a SHA-256 hash for each sorted deduplicated bank list. To replay a single orbit, append its integer quartet code to the command. After enumeration the driver independently verifies that every emitted bank covers the raw rows irreducibly and admits four disjoint exterior-pair covers.
