# A read-once mixed tree with seven line inputs needs 34 residual gates

Consider the connected cone after cutting at every input to the mixed gates that depends on only one side. Assume this cone is a tree with each boundary wire used once and exactly seven line-side boundary wires. The packing and equality-signature verifier force those line signals to have the seven distinct singleton signatures. Let P be the number of point-side boundary wires. The tree has P+6 mixed gates.

Replace the seven line signals by their corresponding raw bits. On residual positives the line assignment is a singleton, so this preserves every positive. Each original signal is true whenever its corresponding line bit is true, so replacement preserves rejection of all negatives. Keep the original point signals throughout.

Call a subtree good if, for every resident line L and point p on it, it rejects the point singleton with all resident lines zero and accepts the same point with just L active. The root is good. Every incident pair is a residual positive, and the singleton point with no lines is dominated by a sampled negative. At a good node that splits its resident lines between both children, take one line from each child and their intersection point p. If the gate were AND, its acceptance of the two incident pairs would force both children to accept p with no resident lines active. That would violate rejection at p with no lines active. Thus the gate is OR. Both children inherit goodness, because rejection with no lines active forces both children false there, while acceptance with one line active forces that line's child true.

At a good node with just one child containing lines, the other child is a point-side leaf, since every internal gate of the cut tree is mixed. For AND, its point guard accepts every singleton on every resident line, and the child containing lines inherits goodness. For OR, its point leaf rejects every such singleton, and the line-containing child again inherits goodness. Replace every latter OR by its line-containing child. This preserves all required positives by the local goodness property, and only decreases values elsewhere.

The resulting tree uses OR to combine groups of lines and AND to attach point guards to a group. Distributing for the argument yields a term for each line T, consisting of T and all point guards along its path. A guard is private to T when T is its only resident line. For a fixed T the groups belonging to shared guards on its path are nested. When any such guard occurs, their smallest group contains a second line U and every shared guard on the path accepts every point singleton of U.

Form H_T as the conjunction of only the private guards of T. It accepts every singleton on T. For any pair of distinct points outside T, the checker supplies a negative with T active, both points selected, and a selected point on U. If there are no shared guards, choose any U different from T. On this negative all shared guards of T are true. The T term must be false, so some private guard is false. This establishes that at least one private guard exists for T, and by monotonicity H_T rejects each outside pair and every outside singleton.

The seven functions H_T have the target singleton signatures and reject all their outside pairs. From the original point subcircuit they require at most P−7 further ANDs: each private guard belongs to just one line and each line has at least one. The 21-gate point-side charge bound in equality.md gives at least 21−(P−7) original point gates. Adding the P+6 mixed gates gives at least 34 gates in total. Any additional line-side gates only increase this total.

The numerical check in verify_guard_negatives.py covers 42 ordered line pairs and 36 outside pairs per line.

At the 33-gate budget the [point boundary bound](nine-boundary.md) requires at least nine distinct point-side inputs. Together with seven line-side inputs this gives at least 16 boundary wires. A connected cone on 15 mixed gates can have at most 16 boundary wires, with equality only for a read-once tree. It would have exactly seven line-side inputs, so the preceding 34-gate bound excludes it. Thus every 33-gate residual solution needs at least 16 mixed gates.
