# A chromatic lower bound on additional gate uses

Take a frontier of a monotone circuit, with each current position regarded as an independent variable. A private positive/negative pair for i has i=1 at its positive, i=0 at its negative, and every other coordinate of the positive at most that of the negative. Any monotone separator is sensitive to i at both rows, since toggling i in either row then produces a vector ordered with respect to the opposite row. Join distinct positions i,j by an edge if each has a private pair at one common positive and each has a private pair at one common negative. The partner rows used to establish privacy may differ.

If a suffix has k extra operand uses over a tree, its conflict graph is (k+1)-colorable. In a connected output circuit at most k non-output nodes have fanout above one, counting frontier variables. Starting at each variable, follow its unique outgoing edge until the first fanout node or the output; a variable of fanout above one ends immediately. Color it by this endpoint H.

Cut the incoming cone of H whenever a previously encountered fanout node other than H supplies an operand. Treat each such incoming occurrence as a separate formal variable. The resulting local formula is read-once in these ports and in every frontier variable of H's color. Each port is independent of these frontier variables: a path from such a variable to a cut port would have encountered that port's fanout earlier than H. Likewise all paths by which any of these variables affect the final output pass through H. Hence sensitivity to i at a sampled assignment forces sensitivity of this local formula to i at the induced input. If two variables of the same color were adjacent, both would be positively sensitive at one local input and negatively sensitive at another. Every minterm admitted by the first would contain both, and every maxterm admitted by the second would contain both. A minterm and maxterm of a monotone read-once formula intersect in exactly one variable by induction on its root. This rules out the edge.

The bound applies irrespective of point/line ancestry, and it is a necessary test only. In particular any odd cycle requires at least two additional uses.

As a check, odd majority on n≥3 inputs has the complete conflict graph. For any two inputs there is a minimal majority input containing both, and a maximal rejected input with both coordinates zero. Each is sensitive on its corresponding boundary. The graph therefore forces at least n−1 surplus uses in every monotone DAG for majority. For n=3, the four-gate formula (a AND b) OR (c AND (a OR b)) attains two surplus uses.
