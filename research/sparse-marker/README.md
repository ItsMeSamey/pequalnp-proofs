# Sparse interpolation for a singleton parity marker

Given a binary square matrix A and row h, seek x with xᵢ(Ax)ᵢ=0 and hx=1. Specialize the occurrence-weight polynomial of [Codex and Wang, equation (2)](https://airaxiv.com/papers/pdf/2611.0031/) to the two choices in each guard. Over characteristic two it becomes

    P(t) = Σ_x (hx) ∏_i [(1+x_i) + t_i(1+(Ax)_i)].

The coefficient of the squarefree monomial indexed by S is the parity of the kernel vectors of A[S] on which h[S] is one. It is one precisely when this kernel is one-dimensional and h[S] is nonzero there. Every such selection yields a feasible witness. Conversely, let S be the support of a minimum-support witness. Its all-ones restriction is in the kernel and has positive marker. An independent kernel direction would yield a nonzero marker-zero direction there. Adding that direction to the all-ones vector gives a smaller witness. Thus P is nonzero precisely on satisfiable instances.

**Deterministic algorithm under a promise.** Given an upper bound s≥1 on the number of nonzero coefficients, the [solver](sparse.py) returns a witness or correctly rejects in poly(n,s) bit operations. Write e(S)=Σ_{i∈S}2^i. For each of the first n(s−1)+1 primes p, substitute t_i=T^(2^i mod p). A chosen nonzero coefficient survives for some p: the product of its exponent differences from the other support exponents has absolute value below 2^(n(s−1)) when s>1 and there is more than one term. At most n(s−1) distinct primes divide the product. A prime missing it isolates the chosen term even modulo p, hence also among the actual univariate degrees. A single term survives every prime. The tested polynomials have degree at most n(p−1). The first k primes have magnitude O(k log(k+1)) and can be listed deterministically in polynomial time. No finite-field generator is needed.

For evaluation use general polynomial weights l_i,r_i on the two guard choices and keep hx as a separate factor. In expanding the product of n+1 affine factors, a term survives summation over all Boolean assignments exactly when all n variables occur. There are just two possibilities. One row supplies its constant, with the other rows using the variables bijectively, or two rows use one variable twice while the remaining rows use the remaining variables bijectively. Enumerate the exceptional row or the repeated variable and row pair. For each multiply the indicated coefficients by the determinant of the remaining square linear array. The permanent equals the determinant in characteristic two. Fraction-free elimination in GF(2)[T] computes all these minors in polynomial bit time because their degrees are polynomially bounded.

At a nonzero specialization choose successively either (l_i,r_i)=(1,0) or (0,1) preserving a nonzero result. At least one works by linearity in the weight pair. The final value is one and ordinary binary row reduction recovers the unique solution. The promise is needed only for rejection after exhausting the primes. This also gives a deterministic positive-instance search with running time polynomial in the actual support size by doubling the guess until success.

**Exponential support survives local coordinate changes.** Take one isolated zero row and column with marker on this coordinate, and m disjoint off-diagonal symmetric two-by-two exchange blocks. A block's selected principal matrix is invertible exactly when both or neither endpoint is selected. Hence P=t₀∏(1+t_a t_b), with 2^m terms. In the original homogeneous weight pairs, separate one endpoint of every edge on each side of a coefficient-matrix flattening, placing the marker vertex on the left. The nonzero rows contain a 2^m-dimensional identity matrix, so the flattening has rank 2^m. Arbitrary invertible local linear changes in each pair act by invertible tensor-product maps on its two sides. Rank is preserved, and at least 2^m coefficient entries must remain nonzero.

More generally, attach the same isolated marker to the symmetric bipartite block [[0,B],[Bᵀ,0]]. Its support count is precisely the number of bases of the binary matroid represented by [I|B]. A selected row subset R and column subset C gives an invertible principal block exactly when the two have equal size and B[R,C] is invertible. This is equivalent to retaining the identity columns outside R and adjoining the B columns in C to obtain a basis. Thus the reduction preserves the count even though the isolated vertex always gives a witness.

Run `python -B check.py`. The [checker](check.py) independently sums over Boolean assignments, computes every selector coefficient, and compares the solver's witness with exhaustive search on 240 instances of dimensions zero through five. The result leaves unrestricted deterministic polynomial time open.
