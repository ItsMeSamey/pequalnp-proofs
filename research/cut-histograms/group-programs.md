# Deterministic satisfiability for fixed semidirect products

All groups below are fixed. A word with constants may have inverses and repeated variables. Input size L counts occurrences after expansion of terms as words. A group program multiplies instructions, each choosing between two constants by reading a Boolean input. A list word restricts each variable to an arbitrary supplied subset of the group. Targets can be any subset, since its size is constant. All algorithms can return assignments.

## Fiber hitting

Use the explicit input-oblivious hitting lemma in other-alphabets.md and support-differences.md. On a fixed-exponent abelian r-group parameter domain it hits a conjunction of a bounded iterated-difference-degree nonzero predicate in characteristic r and a polynomially character-sparse nonzero predicate in one different characteristic. The set has polynomial size for fixed degree, alphabet and polynomial sparsity bound, independent of the coefficients. Finite splitting fields can be used. Products combine any constant number of same-characteristic tests. For independent parameter blocks with uniform such slice bounds on each block, the Cartesian product of their sets hits: starting with any witness, replace one block at a time, retaining a witness at each step.

For a finite r-group T the augmentation ideal J of its group algebra over characteristic r satisfies J^|T|=0. For nontrivial T quotient by a central element z of order r. The group algebra map has kernel (z−1), a central principal ideal whose r-th power vanishes. Induction on the quotient proves the assertion. Consequently the product of L group elements A_i expands in the algebra as the sum over all increasing subsequences of length less than |T| of ordered products of A_i−1, including the empty product. Taking the basis coordinate at a target element gives its exact zero-one predicate. There are polynomially many terms. Uniformly bounded degrees in a given parameter block therefore stay bounded, and uniformly bounded character sparsities stay polynomial, with separate bounds in different blocks.

## Word theorem

Let G=N⋊H where N is nilpotent and H is abelian. Suppose each Sylow subgroup H_r acts nontrivially on at most one Sylow subgroup N_s with s different from r. Word satisfiability is in deterministic polynomial time. In particular the hypothesis holds whenever |G| has at most two prime factors (counted without multiplicity).

Give each group variable independent primary parameters for its H and N parts. Parameterize H_r by additive cyclic coordinates and N_r by a fixed bijective lookup of r-primary digits. A common fixed prime-power exponent per sort suffices by reducing coordinates. The H component of a prefix is an affine sum of letter parameters. Its action on the next letter's N_s component is consequently a fixed lookup of constantly many affine forms and the letter's local N_s parameters. For an inverse letter, conjugate its inverse N component by the prefix updated by subtracting its H component. Sylow factors of N are characteristic, so they multiply independently.

Fix all sorts other than r. The lookup for N_r has bounded r-difference degree: every function on its fixed finite r-group argument domain has bounded degree by nilpotence of its translation augmentation ideal, and affine pullback preserves the bound. The group-algebra expansion gives bounded degree for equality of the final N_r product to its target. The final H_r equality is itself such a bounded-degree lookup. For the one possible varying N_s with s different from r, a fixed-size lookup of r-primary affine forms has bounded character sparsity over a fixed splitting extension of characteristic s. Pullback preserves this bound. The expansion gives polynomial sparsity for equality to its target. All other primary outputs are constant on this slice. The fiber hitting lemma now applies successively to every sort. Constants and equality of two words are handled by multiplication by the inverse second word. The number of sorts depends only on G.

## Boolean programs and lists

Let G=P⋊H where P is an odd p-group and H a 2-group whose action on P has elementary abelian image. Boolean program satisfiability, even with affine parity queries per instruction, is deterministic polynomial time. In the acting image, any two-choice instruction is affine, so every prefix action is determined by constantly many affine bits. Each conjugated P-factor has constant Walsh sparsity in characteristic p. In the H group algebra each instruction has degree at most one in characteristic two. The respective expansions give polynomial sparsity s and constant degree d for final component indicators. Apply the binary hitting lemma.

This also solves words with arbitrary lists. Empty lists reject. Restrict each variable temporarily to any pair of allowed values; the resulting word is a binary program, interpreting inverse letters by the two inverse choices. By the binary density lemma each nonempty target fiber of this restricted program has density at least 2^(-d) s^(-2^d). This holds uniformly over choices and word restrictions. Choose arbitrary baseline values, and take a satisfying assignment differing in as few positions k as possible. Restrict each of its changed positions to its two values, fixing all other positions. Its unique satisfying assignment has density 2^(-k). Hence k ≤ d+2^d log₂ s = O(log(L+2)).

Let c encode the fixed alphabet in bits, n be the number of occurring variables, and K an integer upper bound just obtained. Construct a cK-universal family on cn bits. In a field of binary order Q > 2^(cK)(cn+1), enumerate u,t and take bit j to be Tr(u t^j), indexing from zero. The bias of any nonempty parity is at most (cn−1)/Q by character orthogonality in u and the polynomial root bound in t. Fourier inversion gives probability at least 2^(-cK)−cn/Q for any prescribed pattern of at most cK bits. Construction is polynomial, including finding the defining field polynomial by enumeration for logarithmic extension degree. Interpret invalid values as baseline. Run the binary solver on the pairs of baseline and allowed guessed value, for each family member. One pair system admits the minimum-support witness. This proves deterministic arbitrary-list word satisfiability for the stated class. In particular the dihedral group of order 2m with m=2^a p^b satisfies both program and list conclusions by splitting off the odd rotation subgroup.

## Comparison and scope

Idziak, Kawałek, Krzaczkowski and Weiß give randomized bounds for the abelian-complement two-prime word class in Theorem 4 and discuss dihedral groups in Corollary 5. These group-word and program results concern explicit products. Shared operation circuits can unfold exponentially. We make no claim to a general deterministic circuit satisfiability algorithm or to settling P versus NP. [ICALP 2022 primary paper](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2022.127).

A separate bounded-depth application of support-differences.md concerns a polynomial-time evaluable Boolean guard given as the conjunction of one GF(2) nonzero test of degree d and one odd-field Walsh-sparse nonzero test of sparsity s. Its explicit hitting set has size n^O(d) s^O(2^d), up to factors exponential in O(d log(d+2)) from construction. If d ≤ (1−ε)log₂ n and s is polynomial, search takes 2^O(n^(1−ε) log n + log² n) time. For a singleton accepting input the density bound forces n ≤ d+2^d log₂ s. In a binary-AND, unbounded XOR/NOT implementation of the GF(2) guard, degree is at most two to the multiplicative depth, so singleton acceptance with polynomial s requires depth at least log₂ log₂ n−O(1). The claim concerns only this hybrid guard representation.
