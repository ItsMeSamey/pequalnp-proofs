# Sparse Walsh predicates on joint Boolean quadratic fibers

Let g be a function on F₂ⁿ, given by a list of s nonzero Walsh coefficients over a field of odd characteristic. Let q₁,…,q_t be explicit affine Boolean quadratics with requested values b_i. For each fixed t a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm decides and witnesses g(x)≠0 and q_i(x)=b_i for every i. In particular any fixed number of mod-four constraints and any fixed finite abelian odd p-group score on affine binary forms admit joint polynomial-time support testing. First impose all parity bits as affine linear constraints; each remaining mod-four high bit is quadratic, and the odd-primary score gives the sparse g of README.md. No positivity or ordered-field assumption on g is required.

Combine repeated Walsh indices, returning no if g=0. For t=0 nonzero g suffices. Write B_i for the alternating polar forms, M=max(2,s²), L=ceil(log base 4/3 of (8M)), and

R = 2tL + 2t + ceil(log₂(32LM)) + 10.

If some nonzero linear combination of the q_i has polar rank r<R, enter a symplectic basis for this combination. Guess the r nondegenerate coordinates, leaving at most 2^R branches, polynomial in s for fixed t. On each branch the chosen quadratic combination is affine, so impose its requested sum of values by Gaussian elimination and replace the original t conditions by t−1 complementary linear combinations. Restrict g by mapping each frequency through this affine substitution, combining equal indices and their signs. Its support size cannot increase. Recursing to depth t handles low rank. It remains to prove that when every nonzero combination has polar rank at least R, an answer always exists.

Let Δ be the set of all nonzero differences of indices in the Walsh support of g, so |Δ|≤M. It suffices to find an affine subspace x₀+W on which all quadratics have the requested values and no ℓ in Δ vanishes identically on W. The Walsh characters of g then restrict to distinct characters up to nonzero signs. Their independence in odd characteristic gives a point with g nonzero. The subspace only certifies the deterministic high-rank decision; the algorithm need not construct it.

We recall the elementary real-valued estimate for counting quadratic fibers. On an affine subspace of codimension c each nonzero combination retains polar rank at least R−2c, since restricting a bilinear form to a codimension-c subspace loses at most 2c rank. The absolute bias of a Boolean quadratic of rank r is at most 2^{−r/2}, by decomposition into symplectic planes and the radical. Expanding the t target equalities into signs shows that the fraction in the joint fiber is 2^{−t}(1+δ) with |δ|≤(2^t−1)2^{−(R−2c)/2}. For c≤tL+1 our choice gives |δ|≤1/8. In particular choose x₀ in the requested fiber.

Inductively sample L directions. After j choices w₁,…,w_j let U be the covector span of all B_i(w_l,·), so dim U≤tj, and K=U^⊥. Draw v uniformly among the points of K satisfying q_i(x₀+v)=q_i(x₀) for all i. The estimate applies on K, with translates absorbed into the affine terms. Orthogonality keeps x₀+span(w₁,…,w_j,v) inside the joint fiber. The directions need not be independent. Maintain the event that U never contains an element of Δ. Initially U=0.

Given a good history, for each ℓ in Δ the hyperplane ℓ(v)=0 inside K has relative size 1/2. The same fiber estimate applies there. Thus the conditional probability ℓ(v)=0 is at most (1+1/8)/(2(1−1/8))<3/4. To bound the risk that the enlarged U contains an ℓ, observe that this requires B_λ(v,·)∈ℓ+U for some nonzero λ in F₂^t, where B_λ=Σ λ_i B_i. This linear map has rank at least R, so at most 2^{n−R+tj} ambient v satisfy that requirement for a fixed ℓ,λ. The sample space has size at least (7/8)2^{n−tj−t}. Union over ℓ and λ gives risk at most 2M 2^{2tj+2t−R}. The chance of ever leaving good histories is therefore ≤2LM 2^{2tL+2t−R}<1/8. Along good histories the chance a given ℓ is missed on all L samples, while staying good, is ≤(3/4)^L. Summing over Δ gives at most 1/8. Some outcome stays good and separates all differences, as required.

The decision procedure is deterministic: test polar ranks for 2^t−1 combinations; accept in the proven high-rank case and branch explicitly in the low-rank case. The bound on s is inherited by all recursive calls. For a witness fix input coordinates sequentially, keeping a successful branch of the same decision procedure. The argument uses real Walsh estimates only to count binary solutions of the quadratic constraints, never to lift the coefficients of g from their field.
