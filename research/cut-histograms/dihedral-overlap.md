# Explicit dihedral word equations and modular score interactions

For each fixed odd m, word satisfiability with constants on the dihedral group of order 2m and attainable residues of weighted sums of affine Boolean forms modulo m are polynomial-time many-one equivalent. The modular-expression-to-group construction has prior art in [Complexity of Modular Circuits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.02947). The historical conditional lower bound for two different odd prime factors is stated in [ICALP 2022, Corollary 5](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2022.127). This equivalence and the algorithms below are unconditional.

Write a,s for a unit rotation and reflection. Each unrestricted variable supplies its reflection bit. For an affine form b, let U be the word of its variables, prefixed by s for a nonzero affine constant. Then U a^(-w/2) U⁻¹ a^(w/2) is the rotation a^(wb). Multiply these factors and the inverse target. Every Boolean tuple lifts, and all rotations of the chosen lifts cancel.

Conversely encode each variable by a reflection bit and a fixed number of binary selectors surjecting onto all rotations. Solve the final affine reflection equation by parametrization, rejecting an inconsistent system. For positive letters add their rotation times the sign of the preceding reflection sum. For inverse letters subtract their rotation times the sign of the updated reflection sum. Each summand is a fixed-size lookup of the local selectors and one affine bit, even after parametrization. Its Walsh transform over Z/m exists since 2 is invertible. Replace each sign by one minus twice its Boolean affine form. The resulting single score has polynomial size. Weights may be expanded in unary for fixed m. The smallest odd m with two distinct prime factors is 15. Complexity counts expanded word length.

## Shared subspace

Require a bounded-degree GF(2) indicator and h fixed explicitly polynomially Walsh-sparse tests for nonzero values in odd characteristic. For each spectrum choose one reference frequency and span the differences to it to obtain U_i. Empty spectra reject. Multiplying by the reference character preserves the nonzero locus, which depends only on evaluations of U_i. Supplied superspaces also suffice.

Set I_i = U_i ∩ (sum of the other U_j) and W=sum_i I_i. At k=dim W, decision and witness recovery take 2^k times polynomial input size for fixed h and degree. The images of U_i modulo W form a direct sum: if sum u_i belongs to W, write the sum as sum w_i with w_i in I_i. Each u_i−w_i belongs to I_i since their sum is zero. Fix evaluations on W, choose independent bases for the separate quotient images lifted within U_i, and complete to a full dual basis. Each odd predicate varies only in its own block. Its Walsh sparsity cannot increase under affine substitutions, and binary degree stays bounded. Apply the single-off-prime oblivious hitting set (support-differences.md) to each block and the degree-only set to the leftover block. Sequential coordinate replacement preserves any satisfying tuple. Bases and overlaps are obtained by Gaussian elimination. For two predicates W is their intersection. For arbitrary fixed odd m use the Lucas indicators for each prime-power residue in README.md. No assertion of universally small overlap is needed.

## Locally separated additive scores

Let a score to a fixed finite abelian group A be given as a sum of local scores, with a supplied rooted binary tree whose leaves are the local scores. Each leaf j depends only on evaluations of a known binary dual subspace U_j. For node t put V_t=sum of its leaf spaces and B_t=V_t ∩ (sum of all exterior leaf spaces). Let w=max dim B_t. Empty sums denote zero. For each leaf require a subroutine deciding which scores it attains conditional on a specified boundary functional in B_j*. It may use linear parameterization and the shared-subspace solver above for restricted leaf scores. Alternatively single affine terms or lookup tables have direct leaf algorithms. The following DP is polynomial times 2^(3w) plus leaf initialization for fixed A, and allows an arbitrary number of leaves.

At each node t, store (b,a) when a functional on V_t restricting to b on B_t realizes score a. At children u,v enumerate b_u,b_v and the candidate parent b. Test linear consistency of the three restrictions on B_u+B_v+B_t by Gaussian elimination. If consistent, combine any present child scores by addition. To verify sufficiency, fix a consistent functional on the sum of the boundary spaces. Independent child witnesses already agree on V_u ∩ V_v, a subspace of both child boundaries. Their glued functional agrees with b on B_t: write z∈B_t as x+y for x∈V_u and y∈V_v; since z is also exterior to t, x belongs to B_u and y to B_v. Consistency then implies the equality. Necessity follows by restriction. The root has empty boundary and every resulting functional extends to the ambient dual. Backtracking and linear extension recover an input. Witness storage can instead recompute leaf assignments after backtracking. This argument is also the state-space gluing principle for conventional linear-code trellises.

This handles large overlap of the prime-specific spectra. For example, build an odd-modulus-15 score consisting of independent blocks on disjoint binary input coordinates, each contributing both modulo 3 and modulo 5. Global prime indicators can share every coordinate. A tree grouping by independent blocks has zero interblock boundary, with single-coordinate blocks giving constant leaf time. More generally, adjacent overlapping blocks of supported linear forms have width set by their span intersections, irrespective of how many coordinates occur in both prime outputs globally. Handle a separate global binary guard by this DP only if it has its own suitable local representation and compatible state aggregation.

## Keeping an arbitrary global binary guard

For constant d one can also allow a global GF(2) guard P of degree at most d with no factorization on the supplied tree. Strengthen the leaf oracle to find a witness for a boundary/score with any extra degree-d GF(2) nonzero guard. This oracle is available, for example, from the shared-space algorithm at a leaf with bounded prime overlap, and from explicit small tables. Add a zero-score leaf on a direct complement of the full leaf span, making the root space the entire binary dual without increasing old widths.

Each table entry now stores actual witnesses forming a basis of the span of their degree-at-most-d monomial feature vectors (including 1), in a fixed basis of the current space. At most D=sum_{i=0}^d binomial(n,i) witnesses per entry are retained. At a leaf take the annihilator of the current feature span. For each basis vector of the annihilator ask the strengthened oracle whether the corresponding degree-d polynomial can be nonzero. Append the first returned witness and repeat; if none succeeds, the span is complete. At most D growth steps and O(D²) calls suffice per entry.

At a compatible triple all pairs of witnesses from the two child entries are compatible and satisfy the parent boundary. Compute their glued feature vectors and retain a basis over all score splits and boundary triples. To see completeness, extend the linear gluing map from the subspace of compatible pairs to the whole direct product of the two child coordinate spaces. Each parent monomial evaluated after this extension is a sum of products of child monomials, each of degree at most d. Thus the parent feature is a linear map of the tensor product of the two child features. Bilinearity proves spanning from the retained pairs. Every retained basis vector is an actual feasible witness. At the root test P on this basis for the desired score. If it attains one anywhere, some basis witness attains one. Cost is 2^(3w) times polynomial in the input and D, plus the indicated leaf calls. This feature enrichment avoids requiring a compatible local decomposition of P.
