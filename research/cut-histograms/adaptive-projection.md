# Adaptive elimination of private parity frequencies

Consider any fixed number of sparse Walsh polynomials over possibly different odd fields. We seek a message where every polynomial is nonzero. For each polynomial multiply by a Walsh character to move an arbitrary support frequency to zero. Nonvanishing is unchanged. Let V_i be its frequency span after this normalization. If V_i is not a subspace of the sum of the other V_j, find a binary message direction v annihilating all other V_j but not V_i. The other predicates are invariant under x→x+v. Split g_i into its two lists g_i^0,g_i^1 according to the bit a·v of each frequency. Both lists are nonempty because the normalized list contains zero.

For any fixed x, the values of g_i at x,x+v are g_i^0(x)±g_i^1(x), so at least one is nonzero exactly when at least one bucket value is nonzero. This uses 2 invertible in its field. Replace the original problem by the disjunction of two children, replacing g_i by the respective bucket. A witness to a successful child lifts by trying both x and x+v. Recompute the affine frequency spans separately in each child, since the various subsets can have much lower dimension. No false positives result from eliminating the private direction. If the original joint support is empty, every leaf rejects.

Let S=Π_i s_i for the initial list sizes. Every split divides the leaf's positive integer potential Π_i s_i into two smaller positive integers with the same sum. Thus the entire tree has at most S leaves and at most 2S−1 nodes irrespective of the private elimination order. At a leaf where no such direction exists, enumerate evaluations on the joint frequency span of dimension r_leaf. Remaining ambient directions are immaterial. This yields a deterministic poly(input)·2^{max r_leaf} algorithm at fixed number of predicates. Small leaf dimension may be certified even when the initial common dimension is unbounded. For two factors the nonzero terminal core has identical normalized frequency spans in both. The private-elimination rule here assumes no additional Boolean condition varying along v. The separate product-block criterion of mixed-positive.md allows a globally coupled bounded-degree Boolean condition under its stated hypothesis.

A different parameter is the number of binary dependencies among the combined distinct frequencies. List the m nonzero frequencies a_j appearing anywhere; let H be a parity-check matrix of the code of attainable bits z_j=a_j·x. If this check rank is k, iterate through z_j∈{0,1}, tracking the partial syndrome H z and the additive evaluations of all original Walsh lists in their respective fields (each chosen sign contributes the vector of coefficients with that sign). There are at most 2^k times the product of the fixed field sizes many states. At the end require syndrome zero and each evaluation nonzero; reconstruct signs by backpointers and solve a_j·x=z_j. For a supplied order one can discard syndromes outside the span of the remaining columns, so only 2^{dim(span prefix ∩ span suffix)} syndromes survive across a cut. This gives deterministic polynomial time for logarithmic maximum trellis width of the frequency code, including cases of large shared span. The treatment of list spectra here is distinct from an algorithm on a low-width input generator matrix.
