# Exact cut-size histograms and their support

For fixed m≥1, H_r(G) counts distinct graph edge cuts whose cardinality equals r modulo m. The counts are ordinary integers and include the empty cut. Every single fixed bin is in FP for m∈{1,2,4} and is #P-complete under polynomial-time Turing reductions for all other m, even on connected simple bipartite graphs. In contrast, every fixed-modulus bin has a polynomial-time nonemptiness test on arbitrary graphs. On planar graphs all fixed-modulus exact bins are in FP.

## Counting

Write F_G(z)=Σ_S z^{|δ(S)|}, summing over all vertex subsets. Divide by 2^{c(G)} to count distinct edge sets. Let n,e,c denote the numbers of vertices, edges and components. For z≠0,1 the random-cluster expansion gives

F_G(z)=z^e 2^c (1/z−1)^{n−c} T_G((1+z)/(1−z),1/z).

Indeed each edge contributes z times (1+(1/z−1)[same spin]), and each subset of selected equality edges leaves two color choices per component. At a primitive mth root ζ, the histogram yields F_G(ζ) by summing ζ^r H_r and restoring its multiplicity.

For m outside {1,2,4}, replace each edge of a loopless multigraph G by a private two-edge path. The resulting graph L is simple and bipartite. Summing over the new spins gives F_L(ζ)=(1+ζ²)^e F_G(z'), with z'=2ζ/(1+ζ²)=1/cos(2π/m). This is finite, real, and |z'|>1. Its Tutte point lies on (x−1)(y−1)=2 and is outside every exceptional point of Jaeger, Vertigan and Welsh. Thus evaluation at z' is #P-hard. Loops have no effect on F and the known prefactor accounts for them when passing back to Tutte evaluation. Connected inputs suffice under Turing reductions by multiplicativity. [Primary dichotomy](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0305004100068936)

Even a single bin suffices. Attach j pendant edges at a common vertex for j=0,…,m−1. This multiplies the distinct-cut generating polynomial by (1+z)^j. A fixed-bin oracle therefore yields the Fourier values by a Vandermonde system with distinct eigenvalues 1+ζ^k; each unknown carries a known nonzero Fourier phase. The operation preserves connectedness, simplicity and bipartiteness. The bin belongs to #P by guessing one shore representative per cut (fix one root to zero in each component). Exact operations use fixed-degree cyclotomic fields, and counts have O(n) bits.

For planar graphs the planar Ising hyperbola is polynomial-time evaluable, so every Fourier evaluation and consequently every bin is computable in polynomial time, with F_G(1)=2^n handled directly. [Vertigan's planar theorem](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539704446797)

For a binary affine code represented injectively as x=x₀+uG with arbitrary additive coordinate weights in a fixed abelian group of exponent dividing four, every bin is in FP. For each character compute its Gaussian-integer sum Σ_u i^{Q(u)}. Expanding each parity bit modulo four yields only constant, linear and even-coefficient quadratic mixed terms. For q=Q−Q(0), B(v,w)=[q(v+w)−q(v)−q(w)]/2 is symmetric bilinear over F₂ and B(v,v)=q(v) mod 2. By elimination split off nonisotropic lines with B(v,v)=1, contributing 1+i^{q(v)}. In the remaining alternating space split off planes with B(v,w)=1, contributing 1+i^{q(v)}+i^{q(w)}−i^{q(v)+q(w)}. Radical lines contribute 2 or 0 according as q(v) is 0 or 2. Multiply the factors and constant phase and invert the group Fourier transform. This proves the easy cases for all binary affine codes, including represented regular matroid cycle and cocycle codes. Hardness for regular cycle codes follows by cographic realizations of graph cut codes, and hardness for regular cocycle codes by graphic realizations.

## Graph decision at every fixed modulus

With arbitrary additive edge weights modulo m, removing R from a successful shore changes the cut score by a quadratic polynomial in the indicators of R with zero constant. This follows by expanding each cut indicator s_u+s_v−2s_us_v. Color each vertex of the successful shore by its linear coefficient and each pair by its quadratic coefficient. By finite Ramsey, any sufficiently large shore contains 2m vertices with a common vertex color and common pair color. Removing these preserves the score because m divides both 2m and binom(2m,2). Thus some witness shore has bounded size depending only on m, and enumerating bounded-size shores recovers it. The argument permits a fixed finite abelian group by using its exponent and coloring by group elements. Loops have zero cut indicator.

## Arbitrary affine binary codes at prime powers

For fixed d=p^a write N for the integer sum of selected coordinate weights reduced to [0,d−1]. Write D=d−1 and choose τ in [0,d−1] with τ≡D−target mod d. Over F_p the coefficient of z^D in (1+z)^{τ+N} is the exact zero/one residue indicator. Indeed (1+z)^p=1+z^p, so the coefficient is the product of binomial coefficients of the corresponding base-p digits. The low a digits of D are all p−1. The coefficient is one precisely when τ+N ends in these digits, and zero otherwise.

Truncate the product (1+z)^τ Π_i[1+x_i((1+z)^{w_i}−1)] at degree D. Every selected nonconstant factor costs at least one degree. For p=2 substitute the binary affine linear forms x_i over F₂ and reduce by u_j²=u_j. The multilinear expansion represents the zero function exactly when every coefficient vanishes. For odd p use x_i=(1−ε_i χ_{v_i})/2, with χ_v(u)=(−1)^{v·u} and ε_i=±1 encoding the affine offset. Products of characters add their indices over F₂. Dynamic truncated multiplication with sparse coefficient dictionaries computes the indicator in n^{O(d)} time, with at most Σ_{j≤D}binom(n,j) distinct frequencies. The characters are independent over F_p by orthogonality, since 2 is invertible. The residue is attainable precisely when the coefficient dictionary is nonempty after cancellations. Conditional tests after fixing a code parameter recover witnesses. Several fixed constraints over the same prime are handled by multiplying indicators. An additional parity constraint, including the factor 2 for m=2p^a with p odd, is an affine restriction over GF₂ imposed first.

## One additional mod-four condition over an odd prime-power test

The parity of a modulus-four score is an affine GF₂ constraint. Restrict to it first. The high bit is then a Boolean quadratic Q on the remaining parameters: it is the degree-two elementary symmetric polynomial of the active bits, counting weighted coordinates with their multiplicities modulo four. Let g be the odd-primary indicator on that same parameter space and s its Walsh support size over F_p. The case g=0 is immediate. We can decide whether g is nonzero on Q=b in polynomial time. In particular this covers modulus 4p^a on arbitrary binary affine codes.

Compute the polar rank r of Q and split into an r-dimensional nondegenerate space and its radical. Fix radical coordinates at values giving a nonzero slice of g; this existence argument does not require finding the slice. The two restrictions then have nondegenerate phase h=(−1)^Q up to affine terms and Walsh support of g of size at most s. If this slice avoids Q=b then hg=±g. In the Fourier domain multiplication by h is, up to diagonal nonzero scalings and permutation, a Walsh transform on 2^r indices. Indeed completing the square gives the Fourier coefficient of h at z as a nonzero constant times a quadratic sign in z, with polar form the inverse of the original nondegenerate pairing. The constant is nonzero in F_p since the unnormalized Gauss sum is ±2^{r/2}, as follows from a symplectic-basis decomposition. Consequently the convolution kernel indexed by z+w factors into a row sign, a column sign, and the nondegenerate bilinear sign in z,w.

For any nonzero vector on a binary group of size N in odd characteristic, support size a and Walsh support size b satisfy ab≥N. Its translates span a space of dimension b by character diagonalization. The supports of any basis of translates together cover the group (all translates are linear combinations of the basis, and all translates together cover the group), giving the bound. Apply this with N=2^r to the Walsh vectors of g and hg on the chosen slice. Avoidance would imply 2^r≤s². Thus if 2^r>s², both quadratic values occur on the nonzero set of g. Otherwise enumerate the ≤s² nondegenerate-coordinate assignments. On each slice Q=b is an affine linear condition on the radical; restrict the sparse dictionary to it and combine equal frequencies to test nonvanishing. Restricting one parameter and recomputing yields a witness by self-reduction. This gives decision; it does not give the corresponding exact multiplicities. No general assertion for arbitrary mixed-prime moduli on binary codes is needed for the all-modulus graph cut decision theorem.

## Consequences for fixed groups and regular codes

For a fixed finite abelian weight group A with arbitrary coordinate weights, exact bins are in FP when the exponent divides four. Otherwise take an element g of order m outside {1,2,4} and label all graph edges by g: this embeds the hard cyclic histogram. For any fixed target b in its cyclic subgroup use the corresponding hard cyclic bin. If b lies outside that subgroup, attach one pendant edge of weight b. It must be selected to obtain b, reducing the hard zero bin to the chosen bin. The regular cycle and cocycle subclasses inherit the same counting dichotomy. For regular affine codes the one-codeword decision algorithm for arbitrary fixed abelian groups in the companion [regular score algorithm](../code-modular-score/regular-score-dp.md) applies after changing the sign of weights on coordinates with affine shift one and translating the target. It uses a regular-matroid decomposition, graph cut scores and graph even-subgraph linkage. Thus counting versus support is classified for arbitrary fixed cyclic moduli on represented regular affine codes as well. The all-modulus code statement here uses regularity; the prime-power and mixed-modulus arguments above permit arbitrary binary codes.
