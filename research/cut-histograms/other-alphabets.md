# Modular weights of affine codes over arbitrary fixed finite fields

Let the alphabet be F_q with q=ℓ^b fixed, ℓ prime. Let A be a fixed abelian score group all of whose prime divisors belong to {ℓ,p} for some prime p≠ℓ. Given any represented affine F_q-code and a target, one can deterministically decide and find a codeword of prescribed A-score in polynomial time. Each code coordinate can have its own arbitrary score map F_q→A. Ordinary nonzero-symbol Hamming weight modulo ℓ^k p^a is a special case. The same proof gives an explicit polynomial-size set of messages hitting every nonempty target, independent of the input code and scores except their size bounds.

Represent all message and affine coordinate symbols by their b components over F_ℓ, giving a prime-field message space F_ℓ^n. A local function on one coordinate symbol has a polynomial expression of degree ≤b(ℓ−1) in its prime-field bits: expand as a sum over the q possible values, using the value-indicator product Π_j[1−(u_j−v_j)^{ℓ−1}]. Substituting affine coordinate forms preserves this degree bound.

For a primary ℓ^k score factor use the coefficient of z^{ℓ^k−1} in (1+z)^τ times the product over coordinates of (1+z)^{w_i(x)}, where τ≡ℓ^k−1−target modulo ℓ^k and 0≤w_i<ℓ^k. As in the binary proof this is the zero/one target indicator over F_ℓ. In the truncation, each positive z-degree factor introduces one local function (the relevant binomial coefficient of w_i), while the constant term of each factor is one. Thus degree is at most b(ℓ−1)(ℓ^k−1) in the prime-field message variables. Multiply across the ℓ-primary factors to obtain a bounded-degree polynomial P, used through the condition P≠0.

For the other primary group choose a fixed finite extension of F_p containing a primitive ℓ-th root ζ. Every local function on one symbol has at most q Fourier characters χ_a(x)=ζ^{a·x} after affine substitution, by Fourier orthogonality since p≠ℓ. Perform the same bounded-degree truncated products in the p-characteristic coefficient field for each p-power score modulus. Characters multiply by adding indices in F_ℓ^n. Thus the joint p-primary condition has a polynomial-size sparse Fourier predicate g, in time polynomial in the input length for the fixed parameters.

It remains to test g≠0 and P≠0 over their respective fields. A direction space W isolating a Walsh frequency now means its additive F_ℓ-character differs from every other support character when restricted to W. Every coset meets the support, and the shifts W and e_i+W connect the support by quotient connectivity (use repetitions of the unit directions to reach all elements of F_ℓ). If an initial support point has P nonzero return it. If P vanishes there, another support point with nonzero P exists exactly when some support edge has ∆_v P=P(x+v)−P(x) nonzero. A nonzero derivative makes at least one endpoint nonzero, even though the derivative itself need not be Boolean or equal to one. The degree decreases, and g(x)g(x+v) tests joint endpoint membership with at most squared Fourier sparsity. This proves the finite-difference recurrence, with explicit witness recovery.

For a universal W pool hash a=(a_0,…,a_{n−1}) by Σ a_j t^j over an extension of F_ℓ of order greater than (n+1)(s+1)², where s bounds the Fourier sparsity. Some hash is injective on the actual frequencies by the polynomial root bound. The row spaces over F_ℓ all have polynomial size. The same recursive sums of universal pools used in support-differences.md provide the fixed polynomial-size hitting set. Handle absent primary factors by taking their indicator constant one.

For mixed fields this proof concerns the additive statistics of a single unknown affine codeword. Different local constraints may be imposed by different fixed primary factors; the distinct coefficient fields never need be identified.

## Fixed abelian prime-power alphabets, including ring-linear codes

The same conclusion holds for a fixed finite abelian ℓ-group alphabet A=Π_j Z_{ℓ^{e_j}} and coordinate maps that are affine homomorphisms from a product of such groups. This includes additive subgroups and affine translates supplied by generators; generate their additive messages by scalars in R=Z_{ℓ^e}, e=max e_j. Redundant parametrizations leave decision and witness recovery unchanged. It includes codes linear over a fixed finite ring when their supplied additive generator parametrization has this form.

Use difference degree of an F_ℓ-valued function: degree ≤D means every (D+1)-fold iterated additive difference vanishes. A local function on A has degree ≤D₀=Σ_j(ℓ^{e_j}−1). Indeed, in the F_ℓ group algebra of translations, the standard generators T_j satisfy (T_j−1)^{ℓ^{e_j}}=T_j^{ℓ^{e_j}}−1=0. Every translation minus identity lies in the ideal generated by T_j−1, whose (D₀+1)st power vanishes by the pigeonhole principle. Affine homomorphism pullbacks preserve the difference bound. Products of functions have degree at most the sum of their degrees, by the finite-difference product rule Δ_v(fg)=(Δ_v f)(T_v g)+f Δ_v g and induction, noting that translations preserve degree. Thus the ℓ-primary Lucas indicator has difference degree at most D₀Σ(ℓ^k−1). The recursive algorithm only needs evaluations of its successive differences, available in fixed-depth bounded time per evaluation of the input score, so it need not construct a polynomial normal form.

For p≠ℓ take a fixed finite extension of F_p with a primitive ℓ^e-th root. Local functions on A have at most |A| Fourier coefficients and pull back by affine homomorphisms to characters of R^n times phases. This gives the same polynomial sparsity. The support graph and nonvanishing derivative argument hold verbatim for the additive group R^n.

For an oblivious direction pool, take the earlier field-evaluation hash matrices over F_ℓ and lift their entries arbitrarily to R, defining R-linear maps R^n→R^k. Given a nonzero difference of two frequency vectors choose its common ℓ-adic valuation j<e. Divide every coordinate by ℓ^j and reduce modulo ℓ, obtaining a nonzero vector. If the field hash maps this vector to a nonzero value, the lifted hash maps the original difference to a nonzero value in R^k. Thus fewer than n residue-field seeds can fail for any one difference, and some seed separates the entire s-element support. Its transpose image has at most |R|^k=(ℓ^k)^e points and separates the corresponding characters. Every chosen row-space point is computable in polynomial time for fixed e, giving the same oblivious recursive hitting set.
