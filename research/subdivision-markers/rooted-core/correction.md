## Minimum linear correction rank

Allow h additional homogeneous score checks G(x+y)=0, in addition to the two pinned vertices. Form a finite multigraph F whose vertices are the attachment groups and whose m edges are wire pairs; a wire gives a loop if its endpoints share a group. In the arbitrary-context model, the minimum correcting rank lies between m−tau(F) and m−nu(F), where tau is the minimum vertex cover size and nu is the maximum number of pairwise vertex-disjoint wires (a loop uses its one vertex). When F is bipartite these bounds agree by the bipartite matching theorem. In particular they agree when groups contain ports from just one shore. This is an exact result for the shared-pin bank. It makes no assumption about the computational power of other private gadgets.

To prove the lower bound, assign every wire one representative endpoint, to be its potentially mismatched selected port. For any binary mask of wires, the parity vector of selected representatives by group is obtained with a matrix whose columns are the corresponding unit vectors. Its rank is the number of occupied groups. Every mask in its kernel is realizable in a permitted context: mark the representatives, select no other data ports, and within each group pair the marked endpoints by disjoint fresh paths (length two on the same shore or three across). Their internal guards hold. The total mismatch weight is even, so parity of the selected left and right data bits is equal, and the common slack bit enforces both pin guards. Thus validity requires G to be injective on the kernel. The least possible number of occupied groups is tau(F), since the occupied groups form a vertex cover and one can assign endpoints to any cover. This proves h≥m−tau(F).

For the upper bound impose independent equality checks individually on all but a maximum matching. Each attachment group contains at most one unchecked wire. Defects are zero on every checked port. Both ports of an unchecked wire cannot carry a defect; each group has even total, so all unchecked defects vanish as well. This gives h=m−nu(F).

For a specified G there is an exact criterion even without bipartiteness. For each nonempty mask in ker G form the subgraph of F on the masked wires, discarding isolated vertices. Some connected component must have an odd number of edges. Indeed a mask can be oriented to yield even representative counts at every vertex if and only if every component has even edge count. In a connected component choose representatives for all nontree edges arbitrarily, then choose the tree-edge orientations from leaves toward the root to make every nonroot vertex even. The root is even exactly when the edge count is even. Loops contribute their one representative to the unique incident group. The external-path construction then realizes precisely the masks giving obstructions.

Allowing h arbitrary affine checks on the vertices of this fixed bank does not lower the rank cost if every equal data assignment is required to remain possible. The pins are fixed; the private slack pair is equal to the parity of the left data, also the parity of the right data. Substituting these values reduces any check to an affine form in x and y. Vanishing on every (x,x) forces its constant to be zero and its two coefficient vectors to agree. Thus all such checks reduce to G(x+y).
