# Width of opposite-unique cores under a graphic rank perturbation

Suppose a presented binary representation is M=D+UH, where D is the vertex-edge incidence matrix of a graph G without isolated vertices and U,H have inner dimension s. Scores are t binary linear functions of the two raw vectors. Let (A,B) be one uniquely specified ordinary witness at a target, where A and B partition E. Let alpha be a vertex functional producing its cocycle, and beta=alpha U. Assume A has at most p odd-degree vertices in G. Suppose additionally that beta H, an edge signature, is an ordinary graph cut on G−Z for some vertex set Z of size k. Then G has bounded tree-width as a function of s,t,p,k. In an opposite-unique core its matroid therefore also has bounded size by the rooted-core bound. The width claim below concerns a unique spanning-partition witness. Merely minimizing the support of a witness in a graph would not give this conclusion.

Let u be the cycle-space dimension of the subgraph with edge set A. A graphic dependence supported on A with zero H output is an M-cycle. Its primal score must be nonzero unless it is zero, since toggling it preserves disjointness against B. Hence u≤t+s. Similarly the space of ordinary cuts supported on B has dimension components(V,A)−components(G). Choosing representative vertex potentials linearly and imposing zero U-output costs at most s dimensions. The remaining variations are also M-cocycles supported on B; ordinary dual-score uniqueness costs at most t more. Hence components(V,A)−components(G)≤s+t. There are at most u+p components of (V,A) with edges, since each is either cyclic or has an odd-degree leaf. A component of G with no A edges has at least two vertices unless it has only loops. Loops contribute nullity on B, which is bounded by s+t using the opposite witness if needed. Thus in the loopless setting components(G)≤u+p+s+t, and the total number of vertices unused by A is bounded. In the looped setting with both opposite witnesses, there are at most 2(s+t) loops, by applying the graphic-cycle bound to A and B; freeze them and their endpoints in the construction below, and discard isolated vertices newly created by their removal.

Mark all unused vertices, vertices of odd A-degree, A-vertices with degree at least three, and Z. The number marked is bounded: summing degree minus two within the edge-bearing components gives at most 2u together with a correction of at most p for degree-one vertices. In each remaining pure cycle component mark a representative. Split A into paths between marked vertices (including closed paths beginning and ending at the same marked vertex). Bounded total A-degree at the marked vertices follows from the same degree excess estimate, so the number of paths is bounded. Freeze the first and last edge in each path and all edges in paths of length at most three; freeze the original potential at the involved anchors and short-path vertices. Remove marked vertices and the interiors of entirely frozen paths, keeping the distinct exposed endpoints of all longer paths as terminals. Delete previously frozen edges. The remaining disjoint path problem has a feasible solution covering every remaining vertex. Parallel edges are permitted by the theorem below.

Write beta H as a cut delta gamma on G−Z and extend gamma arbitrarily elsewhere. In the unfrozen region replace alpha by the switched vertex potential alpha+gamma; it is constant along each selected path, since the required dual evaluations there are zero. Preserve on each rerouted path the sums of primal score labels and H labels (binary). These fix the full new primal score and Hc, and the unchanged path endpoints fix Dc, hence Mc=0. Express the ordinary dual score on a cocycle with frozen beta as the sum of vertex weights times the potential plus one fixed term. Preserve also all s vertex moments alpha U. The fixed gamma switch merely adds known constants to these linear moment functions. Give each edge the two endpoint weights added in each coordinate modulo four, separately for the t+s binary vertex moments. The path total counts its internal vertices twice. Since endpoints are fixed, exact modulo-four equality preserves each internal moment parity. Give each rerouted path its original constant switched color, maintain frozen colors, and give uncovered unfrozen vertices switched color zero. The original linkage covered them all; thus the new dual score and alpha U are unchanged. The cocycle stays zero on every selected edge. Any linkage meeting these fixed abelian label sums yields the original target, so uniqueness forces it to cover all remaining vertices. Liu–Yoo Theorem 2.2 bounds the residual tree-width. Adding the bounded set of removed vertices back bounds the tree-width of G. Graphic branch-width is bounded by this tree-width plus a constant; a rank-s change of the matrix increases every cut connectivity by at most 3s, by the rank-s perturbation bound for each of the three defining ranks. [Liu and Yoo](https://people.tamu.edu/~yyoo/stoc25.pdf)

A second sufficient condition permits arbitrary beta H when A has boundedly many odd-degree vertices: if the support of all t+s vertex-moment coefficient rows has bounded union, mark those vertices as additional anchors. On each remaining path retain the sum of its beta H edge signatures in addition to the primal gains. This ensures that propagating the potential from either fixed endpoint produces exactly the prescribed value at the other. Distinct path interiors do not intersect. The moments are unchanged because all their contributing vertices are fixed. The argument applies to rows appended to D as well: for M=[D;H], the primal boundary is always zero, and the cocycle has form alpha D+beta H with beta freely fixed, so only the t dual moments need preservation. In a single-row lift of a nonbipartite graph, the two opposite cocycles differ by the all-ones edge vector, which is not a graphic cut. They occupy the two distinct cosets of the graphic cut space in the lifted cocycle space; one is graphic, so one may take beta=0. This is an unconditional bounded-width case even with entirely arbitrary score rows.

For the one-witness version with loops, freeze all primal loop bits and remove all loops before routing. The dual loop entries depend only on the fixed beta. The at most u endpoints of selected loops can be marked wherever they lie in the remaining nonisolated graph. Vertices that become isolated after graph-loop removal retain their original potentials and give fixed moment contributions. Restoring them later does not increase graph tree-width. In the residual loopless graph, the number of vertices unused by A is at most 2(s+t): each such vertex contributes a separate A-component within its original G-component, and a component having only unused vertices has at least two. Thus the loop handling requires no second witness for the width assertion.
