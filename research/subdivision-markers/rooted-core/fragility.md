# Unique terminal minors and finite-field structure

Use the two-root-per-score augmentation in README.md. Fix one admissible target q with a unique raw witness (A,B) and assume A and B partition all ordinary elements. Write N_q for the matroid on the labeled roots obtained by contracting A and deleting B. The terminal trace of the unique augmented witness is a disjoint cycle/cocycle pair in N_q. If another assignment C,D of contraction and deletion gives that same literal matroid, its terminal pair lifts to the original augmentation with zero cycle bits on D and zero cocycle bits on C. Its exact terminal values still yield q. Uniqueness forces A disjoint from D and B disjoint from C. As each pair is a partition, C=A and D=B. This is precisely N_q-fragility in the fixed-ground-set definition of Geelen and Hoersch. Their Conjecture 1.2 is exactly the bounded-width statement that would suffice here. It requires only one witness; the second opposite witness gives a second literal fragile minor realized by the reverse split. [Fragility, definition and Conjecture 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.03263)

For arbitrary targets without uniqueness, a witness can contract all primal-used ordinary sites, delete all dual-used sites and assign unused sites arbitrarily. Conversely a terminal pair lifts across these minor operations preserving disjointness and score. Thus fixed t decision reduces to checking a finite list of binary matroids on 2t specified elements for literal minor containment. This does not itself solve rooted minor recognition.

A 2016 workshop abstract announces bounded path-width for binary N-fragile matroids. The cited talk abstract contains no proof, while the later published Geelen–Hoersch article states the general finite-field bound as a conjecture. No complete proof of the exact needed binary theorem was verified here. A weaker established result gives bounded weak block-triangularity, which by itself does not furnish a bounded-width decomposition. [Workshop announcement](https://matroidunion.org/?page_id=1830) · [Theorem 1.4.2, Extending Pappus' Theorem](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/items/19529045-7361-4ae2-9773-0a4d86cd6197)

## Projective geometry and proper minor classes

The ground set is a cocycle, the sum of the two opposite cocycles. Hence the binary representation is affine and all circuits are even: not even a triangle can be a restriction. Dually all cocircuits are even. Also, for every subset R, the nullity of R intersect A and of R intersect B are individually at most t, since a zero-score cycle in one would switch its witness. This gives |R|≤2rank(R)+2t. After contracting a set C, the analogous bound on |R| costs up to 2rank(C). Withdrawal of the restriction hypothesis is substantial.

In fact every binary matroid presented in standard form [I|P] is an ordinary minor of a binary matroid with a partition into two circuit-cocircuits. Index a square matrix C by disjoint copies U,U′ of the row indices of P and V,V′ of its column indices. Put C=I+K with P in the four U/U′ to V/V′ blocks of K and all other entries zero. Its dimension n is even, K²=0, C is invertible, and all row and column sums are one. Define S by bordering C with a column and row of ones and zero bottom right. S is of odd order n+1, with even row and column sums. It has rank n and both kernels consist of the all-ones line. Border S in the same manner, writing the result as T. In [I|T] put A=the first n+1 basis elements and last nonbasis element; let B be the complement. A is a circuit by its single all-ones relation and a cocircuit by the one-dimensional left kernel of S. B is a cocircuit by its single all-ones corelation and a circuit by the one-dimensional right kernel of S (with its border value determined). Retaining just rows U and columns V in the fundamental chart gives [I|P] by basis contraction and nonbasis deletion. Thus unmarked complementary circuit-cocircuit and tight nullity alone exclude no ordinary binary minor. This construction does not establish witness uniqueness.

Geelen–Gerards–Whittle's broad structural announcement is not a substitute for rooted fragility or a proof of projective-geometry minor exclusion. Grace and van Zwam showed the general vertical-connectivity perturbation statement false over the ternary field, and give refined hypotheses. This is not a binary counterexample. A March 2026 binary paper still explicitly uses the binary structure assertion as a hypothesis. Even a valid highly-connected theorem needs a decomposition retaining terminal certificates on its pieces. The established projective-geometry exclusion theorem for excluded minors to field representability concerns a different class.
