## Explicit bound by linked cuts

Put w=b+2t in the boundary-dimension convention. Set D=(2t+1)·(product over j=0 to w of (1+2^(4j))−1). The total number of ground elements, including terminals, is at most 2^(D+1).

Take the binary Lagrangian chain group N of the augmented matroid and a linked branch decomposition of width at most w. Root it at a leaf. For the elements S below an edge write N×S for the chains supported on S, restricted to S. The restrictions of full chains to S modulo N×S form a vector space of dimension 2j, j the cut width. Use the compatible ordered boundary bases in claim (6) of the proof of Oum's labeled Theorem 6.1. If nested cuts S properly contained in T have the same width and no smaller width on their connecting path, there is an actual sequence of deletions and contractions on T minus S taking the T piece to the S piece and its ordered boundary basis to the ordered basis of S.

Tag each cut by its subset of distinguished roots and the two boundary-coordinate strings of the fixed witnesses. Suppose the tags agree at two such linked cuts. In the minor keep each witness on the complement of T and use its original restricted values on S. These two parts glue because the S values have the same boundary coordinates as the T values, under the ordered boundary minor; Oum's Proposition 6.8 preserves the connection to the exterior. Equivalently, take a representative of the old T boundary class respecting the erased-coordinate conditions. Its image and the desired S restriction differ by a chain supported on S; lift this difference from the T piece and add it before gluing. Only the retained coordinates require disjointness in this minor, where they are the original values. There are no distinguished roots in T minus S. Hence both scores survive in a strict rooted minor, contrary to the complementary supports.

On a nested path the set of roots below the edge changes at most 2t times. In a segment with fixed roots and widths at least j, all occurrences of width j are mutually linked. At most 2^(4j) of them occur because each of the two witnesses contributes 2j boundary bits. Removing these occurrences breaks the segment into at most 1+2^(4j) pieces of strictly higher minimum width. Induction gives a segment length at most the product from i=j to w of (1+2^(4i)), minus one. The whole path has length at most D. A rooted binary decomposition of this depth has at most 2^(D+1) leaves, allowing for the root leaf. The resulting bound is double exponential in O((b+t+1)^2).
