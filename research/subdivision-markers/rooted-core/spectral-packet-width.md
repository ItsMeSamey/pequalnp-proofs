# Hereditary spectral obstructions and a bounded-width subclass

In a maximal clean chart let N be the clean cross-incidence block. Let e be the number of exceptional incidences to be canceled, at most the sum of the exceptional row and column counts, and h=dim H. For any submatrix M on independently restricted rows and columns, set r=Mc for c in ker(I+MᵀM). Within these selected rows and columns every selected vertex has odd degree, because the full row and column parity vectors are exactly r and c. Outside this submatrix choose no clean vertices. The exceptional effects are linear in c. On their joint kernel, uniqueness of the q raw support forces injectivity of the score gain modulo H. Since every toggle there realizes its whole coset, the missing score bounds that dimension by 3−h. Thus ν(I+MᵀM)≤e+3−h on all submatrices, with e replaceable by the actual exceptional-effect rank.

In the all-single-exception case each exceptional charge is a mask parity and the two parities agree: r·r=cᵀMᵀMc=c·c. The precise spectral necessary condition is therefore δ(M)≤3−h, with δ the nullity after adjoining the row of ones to I+MᵀM. Transposition preserves δ via c↦Mc.

## Smallest square matrices

Put k=4−h, so δ≥k obstructs. Such a matrix needs at least k+1 rows and columns: the eigenspace injects into the even subspaces on both sides. At the minimum square size n=k+1, its Gram matrix is I or I+J, since I+MᵀM is symmetric and annihilates the even hyperplane. For h=0, n=5. In the second case its columns are even and M+J has Gram I. For an orthogonal 5-by-5 matrix every row has odd weight and none has weight 5. Five weight-three rows would give five pairwise-intersecting distinct 2-element complements; such a family is a star of size at most four or a triangle of size three. Thus there is a singleton row. Removing it and its forced column leaves an orthogonal 4-by-4 matrix. An orthogonal 3-by-3 matrix must be a permutation (weight 3 is incompatible with any other odd row); consequently an orthogonal 4-by-4 matrix either has a singleton and is a permutation or has all weights three and is a complemented permutation. Up to independent row/column permutations and full complementation the two 5-by-5 minimal patterns are I and the direct sum of [1] with J₄+I₄. For h=1, n=4, Gram I+J is impossible: its columns would have even weight yet I+J is invertible. The exact patterns are the permutation and complemented permutation. The direct Gram enumerator found 1,440 labeled 5-by-5 examples and 48 labeled 4-by-4 examples.

## Bichain charts and low-rank perturbations

A bipartite graph is bichain when each shore is a union of at most two chains under inclusion of full neighborhoods. This is stronger than being Ferrers separately on the blocks of a finite coloring. Let H_m denote the graph of the 2m-by-2m matrix with inclusive lower-triangular blocks on the diagonal and strict lower-triangular blocks off it, as in near-square-pivot.md. It is bichain. At each index take the two rows and two columns as one packet; selecting packets according to z∈GF(2)^m gives masks R(z)=C(z)=(z,z). Their degree parity vectors are *exactly* the masks, and each has even parity. Thus δ(H_m)≥m.

In fact δ≥k on any bichain matrix requires at least k vertices in each of its two row and two column chains, hence at least 4k in total. For any nonzero even eigenvector its selected rows cannot all lie in one neighborhood chain. Every selected column has a selected neighbor, so the largest selected row in that chain would see all selected columns, contrary to its required odd degree and the even column count. Dually neither column chain can contain all selected columns. Therefore restriction to each of the four chains is injective on the even eigenspace. The packets of H_k attain this bound exactly. Independent exhaustive checks find first δ≥3 at 6-by-6 and first δ≥4 at 8-by-8. For that search order the two row chains and enumerate columns by the two prefix lengths. Three mutually incomparable columns are prohibited. Zero columns and duplicate pairs can be removed without changing δ: equal columns force equal eigenvector entries, so deleting a pair does not change row parities, and the omitted entries are reconstructed as the common column's dot product with r. The row statement follows by transposition.

More generally suppose N differs from a bichain matrix B by rank at most s over GF(2), with the same rows and columns. In an induced H_m of B take the packet space and impose (N−B)C(z)=0 and (N−B)ᵀR(z)=0 on its restricted submatrix, at total cost at most 2s. Impose the e exceptional effects as well. As above all remaining toggles are attainable, injective modulo H, with a proper affine score image. Consequently m≤2s+e+3−h. Thus B excludes the fixed induced bichain graph H_m for m=2s+e+4−h. For a clean block with singleton universal exceptional row and column, their effects vanish on every packet and e can be set to zero in this formula. If an induced copy interchanges the two shores, the same packets apply by transposition (H_m is connected for m≥2).

By Atminas, Brignall, Korpelainen, Lozin and Stacho, Theorem 4, every bichain graph excluding a fixed induced bichain graph on v vertices has clique-width at most 24v³−12v². Applying it with v=4m gives a bound for B, hence also for its rank-width. The bipartite adjacency perturbation has rank at most 2s, so every cut-rank and hence the rank-width of N increases by at most 2s. Inserting a bounded number of exceptional vertices similarly raises rank-width by at most their number (attach their leaves at a common point of an old decomposition). This proves a bounded-width result for charts within bounded GF(2) rank of the bichain class, in just one orientation. No general bounded-width theorem for arbitrary charts or arbitrarily many globally nested types follows from this argument.

Primary source: [Minimal Classes of Bipartite Graphs of Unbounded Clique-width, Theorem 4](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~stacho/public/bichain-min-j.pdf).
