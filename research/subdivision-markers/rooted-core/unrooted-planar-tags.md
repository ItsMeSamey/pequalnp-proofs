Use the eight-site NO base of adaptive-prefix-obstruction.md. Its exact image is all four-bit scores except 15. Its fundamental rows are 9,2,4,9. The nontrivial rank-two component has a basis and two copies of their sum, a triangle with a doubled edge. The other two components are parallel pairs. Thus its ordinary matroid is planar graphic. Add a sufficiently large zero-scored square-grid cycle matroid by direct sum. This preserves the exact score image and the full rank of the linear score. In the protected augmentation the grid remains a direct summand, since its gain rows are zero. Every member of any fixed finite list of ordinary planar graphic decorated targets occurs unrooted inside a sufficiently large grid. But no rooted template whose literal score semantics imply 15 attainable can occur with the required named terminals: any such minor witness would lift to a prohibited base score.

This tests even a one-hole planar graphic input with unbounded branch-width. The assertion concerns tests of unrooted occurrence alone, including fixed planar gadgets attached locally at named elements. A procedure inspecting the representation and the labels can preprocess the direct sum. This construction does not rule out such a procedure. The high-branch-width grid result supplies no named terminal correspondence. Primary source: Geelen, Gerards, and Whittle, https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~jfgeelen/Publications/grid.pdf .

## Three-connected ordinary hosts

Triangulate every unit square of a large square grid by one diagonal, add a vertex outside the outer square, and join it to every outer boundary vertex. The resulting simple plane triangulation is three-connected. Reserve three vertex-disjoint facial triangles near the boundary and an arbitrarily large disjoint interior grid. Choose one marked edge e_i on each reserved triangle. Use scores (sum_i c(e_i),d(e_1),d(e_2),d(e_3)). If all three dual bits are one, disjointness forces primal parity zero. Every dual triple is attainable by potentials on the six distinct endpoints, with c empty. For any dual triple with a zero at position j, impose a constant potential on the entire j-th triangle while giving the other two marked edges the prescribed differences. Its three-edge cycle can now serve as c and has primal parity one. Thus the unique hole is 1111 and the linear score rank is four. The canonical marked augmentation retains the disjoint grid as a restriction: ordinary columns are the incidence columns with the primal gain row appended, which vanishes on the grid. This rules out bypassing the examples above by requiring three-connectivity of the entire ordinary input. It makes no connectivity claim about the augmented matroid.

For a YES control on the identical ordinary graph choose a fourth disjoint facial triangle outside the reserved grid, and add the primal bit of one of its edges to the primal score. Any prescribed three dual differences are compatible with a constant potential on the fourth triangle, so every score is attained using that triangle or the empty cycle. Both marked augmentations answer YES to every member of the same fixed list of unrooted planar-template tests, and both ordinary inputs are literally identical.
