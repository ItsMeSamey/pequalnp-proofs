# The common-exception rank-three chart is impossible

Assume four score bits, no hidden freedom, complementary unique cycle/cocycle witnesses (A,B) and (B,A) of scores q,r, and a distinct absent score p. Use a maximal-A basis I. All clean columns Y=B outside I have one augmented signature; |Y|>1. The pure dual gain space Q has dimension three. Suppose all clean rows X=A∩I have a common pattern on A outside I. A single exceptional A column suffices since A is a cycle and its entries on the clean basis rows must then be one.

Use the conclusions of one-column-gains.md: for b=L(B), the three distinct nonzero gains f=q+b, g=r+b, h=p+b lie in Q; each column's zero-row gains span a space avoiding all three. Every row has a zero entry. The clean matrix N has even row and column sums; hence G=NNᵀ is a simple Eulerian graph. Two rows with comparable N-neighborhoods have Gram entry zero. Incomparable rows admit two columns exhibiting the opposite patterns 10 and 01. Toggle the two fundamental cycles and these two dual rows at the forward witness. The two dual changes cancel on every exceptional A column, and the two primal changes cancel on every exceptional B basis row. The resulting disjoint pair has score q plus the two row gains; its primal support differs from both A and B (the two B columns toggle and A's exception stays selected). Therefore a Gram edge is impossible between rows whose gains differ by 0, f+g or f+h. Finally D(A)=g is the sum of the clean row gains, because the basis restriction of this cocycle is precisely X.

If f,g,h are independent, put u=f+g, v=f+h, w=g+h and o=f+g+h. The admissible gains are 0,u,v,w,o. Each o row is Gram-adjacent to every nonzero plane row of gain u,v or w: their zero sets are disjoint by the forbidden gains, and their odd sizes in an odd column ground set imply their even row supports intersect oddly. Since g lies outside the plane spanned by u,v, the number of o rows is odd. Inside the plane a u row has possible edges only to v rows, and a v row only to u rows. Eulerianity now forces every u row to have odd degree into v and vice versa. The two class sizes have equal parity. Their combined gain and the contribution from w rows lie in {0,w}; the total gain is o or o+w=f, contradicting g.

If f,g,h are dependent, their three values are all the nonzero gains of a plane F. Distinct admissible nonzero gains outside F cannot occur together in a column zero set, so their two row classes are completely joined in G by the same parity calculation. Their difference cannot be f+g or f+h, so it can only be f. At most two distinct nonzero gains occur. Their total count is even since g belongs to F. The gain sum is then 0 or f, contradicting g.

Thus no chart satisfies these hypotheses, for any number of row signatures and allowing zero row gains. The case of two distinct exceptional row patterns requires a separate argument.
