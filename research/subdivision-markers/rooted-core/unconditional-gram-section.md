# A common linear Gram section for arbitrary column signatures

In the native four-bit opposite-unique setting, put the hidden-score quotient into four bits first. Use the usual partition A,B and targets q,r,p. Fix a maximal-A basis. Denote clean row/column set by X,Y, clean matrix N, exceptional A nonbasis incidence of row i by E_i, and exceptional B basis incidence of column j by F_j. Let g_i,h_j be the corresponding dual and primal gains. Put G=NNᵀ and let π project scores modulo the nonzero q+r. To row i attach the vector
`θ_i=(π(g_i+Σ_j N_ij h_j), E_i, Σ_j N_ij F_j)`.
Write Θ for the linear map summing these vectors on a chosen row set. The clean row/column sums are even, so G has zero diagonal and row sums.

**Two switches on the same section.** For nonempty u in ker Θ take v=Nᵀu. Toggle precisely the clean dual basis selectors u and the clean primal nonbasis selectors v. Exceptional changes in both represented supports vanish by definition of Θ; the score change is either zero or q+r. At the forward model the newly activated clean dual selectors on rows in supp u are disjoint if each such row's primal value is toggled off, i.e. supp u⊆supp Gu. The clean primal additions on v coincide with dual removals Nᵀu=v. At the reverse model feasibility holds when all new clean-row primal additions Gu are among the clean-dual removals u, i.e. supp Gu⊆supp u; the clean column updates again align. In both cases the switched raw pair differs from its starting pair and cannot be the other designated pair because the exceptional A nonbasis primal coordinates stayed fixed. Hence supp u and supp Gu must be incomparable. This derivation allows arbitrary original marker gains.

Equivalently there is no nonempty θ-neutral subset inducing all odd degrees, and X is the unique induced-even subset with its full θ sum. For the latter take T=X outside supp u; since G1=0, the condition G1_T supported outside T is exactly the reverse inclusion.

**Consequences.** The exceptional counts e=|A outside I| and k=|B∩I| satisfy e,k≥1 and e+k≤3. Every E_i has odd weight (A is a cycle) and each F_j has odd weight (B is a cocycle). Thus E_i is a unit coordinate in dimension e, and ΣN_ij F_j has even weight in dimension k. With the three quotient score bits, the θ vectors lie in an affine space of dimension at most d=3+(e−1)+(k−1)=e+k+1≤4. There are at most 16 classes, or eight if e=k=1. Their linear span dimension r≤d+1≤5. The transpose gives the identical statement on G'=NᵀN.

* Each θ class is independent (an edge is a neutral all-odd pair). Every pair of classes induces a bipartite chain graph (an induced 2K2 has zero tag sum and all degrees odd).
* The induced matching number is at most d since the sums of the endpoint tags of an induced matching are linearly independent in the difference space. A neutral collection of edges would induce all odd degrees. No two vertices of one type are twins: if they have identical external neighborhoods, deleting the pair from X preserves induced-evenness and the θ sum.
* No independent set can span θ(X) if |X|>r. Otherwise at most r members of the set would make an induced-even representative with the full sum.
* For every diagonal binary D we have nullity(G+D)≤r: a kernel vector in ker Θ would satisfy Gu=D u supported in u. Also nullity(I+G[S])≤r for every vertex set S: extend a principal-kernel vector by zeros; its restrictions give supp u⊆supp Gu. In particular rank G≥|X|−r.
* A maximum matching of G has size m≥(|X|−r)/2 since an alternating matrix of rank 2s has a nonsingular principal submatrix on 2s coordinates whose Pfaffian supplies a perfect matching. If I is its induced matching number and m>0, the conflict graph on the m edges has at most |E(G)|−m edges and independence number at most I. The elementary inverse-average-degree bound yields 2|E(G)|≥m+m²/I. Thus large actual Gram graphs necessarily have quadratic edge count in both shores.

**External rank needed by any chain block.** Suppose G[W] is a chain graph, e.g. the union of two θ classes, and let b be the binary rank of G[W,X outside W]. Then |W|≤4(r+b). To see this, write t for the binary rank of the Ferrers off-diagonal block of G[W]. This equals the number of distinct nonempty nested neighborhoods. Representative rows and one column from each successive neighborhood difference give an induced half-graph of order 2t. Let E=|W|−2t. The whole chain submatrix has nullity E before diagonal changes. On the half-graph its lower triangular all-ones block H has inverse L lower bidiagonal. Collapse each row/column twin class with nonzero neighborhood, and ignore isolates. The resulting chart is the canonical half-graph. The sum of class multiplicity excess and isolates is E. At least t−E matched positions have both their classes singleton. Choose a nonconsecutive set J of these positions of size at least max(0,ceil((t−E)/2)); let D1,D2 select J. Kernel equations yield y=L D1 x and x=Lᵀ D2 L D1 x. The principal submatrix of LᵀD2 L on the selected coordinates is identity, so each vector on selected coordinates extends to a full kernel vector. In the full chain graph set diagonal entries to one only at those selected singleton classes. At each other class the equations depend only on its aggregate neighbor sums. Therefore the full kernel is the preimage of the quotient kernel under classwise aggregation. Its dimension is at least E+max(0,ceil((t−E)/2)), which is at least (2t+E)/4=|W|/4. At most b dimensions can spill across the boundary of W; the others would give kernel vectors of a full diagonal perturbation of G, whose nullity is at most r. This proves the bound.

These are necessary constraints on the Gram graphs derived from both actual witnesses. No bound on their overall rank-width or on the underlying matroid branch-width follows here.
