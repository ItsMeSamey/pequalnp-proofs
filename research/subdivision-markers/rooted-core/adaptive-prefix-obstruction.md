# Adaptive projected-score oracle obstruction on native binary instances

Start with a standard binary matrix [I|R] of rank four on eight elements, row masks low bit first. The YES matrix has R=(9,2,4,1); the NO matrix has R=(9,2,4,9). The primal selector gains are (8,8,8,2) and the dual selector gains (1,2,4,1). Let c_e be the last primal nonbasis selector and d_f the last dual basis selector. By evaluating the disjointness condition on all 256 selector pairs, the score-image masks for flag settings (c_e,d_f)=(0,0),(1,0),(0,1),(1,1) are

* YES: 7fff, 5555, 3fff, 8aaa (hexadecimal).
* NO: 7fff, 5555, 3fff, 5545.

Score 15 therefore occurs only with both flags one in YES, and never in NO. The 27 raw witnesses in the 00 case agree on all eight original coordinates for the two charts and cover every other score.

Given a secret subset J of m named atoms, extend the nonbasis element e in series by the atoms of J, and extend the basis element f in parallel by the remaining atoms. Keep e,f themselves as unqueried sentinels. In standard form the series atom is an added basis row supported just on the existing e column; the parallel atom is a nonbasis column supported just on the f basis row. All added gains are zero. The cycle bits of the series atoms equal c_e, while their dual bits are free with the combined class dual parity projecting to the base dual bit. The cocycle bits of the parallel atoms equal d_f, with the dual analogous freedom for primal sum. Thus every resulting raw pair projects to a base raw pair with the same score; every base witness lifts.

A prefix may impose primal zero (deletion) or dual zero (contraction) at any subset of named atoms. A YES instance keeps score 15 exactly when each restricted member of J is contracted and every other restricted atom is deleted. Use the lift with zero free selectors on added elements for sufficiency; necessity follows from c_e=d_f=1. For a flag-00 witness the same lift is entirely zero on all added coordinates and is feasible after every prefix, for every secret and either chart. All attainable-score images at these restrictions are therefore either full or all except 15.

Consider an oracle algorithm that may ask arbitrary rank-at-most-three linear projections of the original score map on any such prefix, and exact score queries only on prefixes leaving at most k named atoms unrestricted. Projected queries have identical answers on YES and NO, and may even return identical actual baseline witnesses: a nonempty fiber of a rank-at-most-three map on four bits contains at least two scores. All exact queries other than testing for 15 admit the same baseline answers and witnesses. For an exact query of 15, at most 2^k secrets agree with all its fixed orientations. On the all-NO transcript an adaptive deterministic algorithm needs at least 2^(m−k) exact queries to distinguish the NO input from every secret YES input. Taking a uniform secret on YES, versus NO with equal prior probability, gives the same order for bounded-error randomized query complexity.

This lower bound concerns arbitrary valid witness selection for the stated black-box queries. It does not allow inspection of the represented matrix, modifications of the score map beyond projection, restrictions on the eight original elements, or additional oracle statistics. In particular the series and parallel classes are recognizable from the represented input. It rules out an oracle-only extraction from the finite-core size theorem, not a polynomial algorithm that uses the representation.
