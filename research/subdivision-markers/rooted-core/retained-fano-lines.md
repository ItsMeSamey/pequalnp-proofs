# Fano pieces along retained triangles

Take a simple three-connected triangulated planar grid containing m vertex-disjoint triangles (cap its outer face with a vertex adjacent to all boundary vertices). Its binary cycle-matroid representation has mutually independent triangle spans L_i of dimension two. Add a private coordinate z_i and all four columns z_i+a for a in L_i, retaining the original triangle. Each augmented triangle is a Fano restriction.

The resulting simple matroid is three-connected. In any separation of connectivity at most one, the restriction to each three-connected Fano piece and to the graphic core has at most one element on its minority side. The majority sides agree because the overlap consists of three elements. The majority spans each piece as it omits at most one element and the pieces have no coloops. Thus the minority has rank at most one and by simplicity has size at most one.

There is no PG(3,2) minor. For two pieces with spans V1,V2 meeting in L containing all its nonzero points in both pieces, let K1,K2 be spans of columns contracted from the two sides. In the quotient by K1+K2 the images of the two spaces meet in the image of L: if x1+x2=k1+k2 then x1+k1=x2+k2 belongs to L. Every surviving complete projective restriction spans a space contained entirely in one image side, since two proper subspaces cannot cover a vector space. Every needed point from the other side can be represented by an actual point of L surviving with the same image. The contraction kernel on the chosen side i is K_i+(K_j intersect L), achievable by contracting a basis of the latter subspace among the retained line points. Each projective minor thus occurs on one side alone. Induction reduces to the regular graphic core or a Fano piece of rank three.

The m Fano spans form a direct sum. Suppose a binary representation is changed by an additive matrix of rank s, with error space P. Discard at most s Fano blocks so the remaining joint span misses P: choose at most s block coordinates detecting the whole intersection of P with the joint original span. In each retained block i the errors on its original dependency space form K_i inside P. Choose at most s donor blocks spanning all K_i together and contract their perturbed columns. Each original dependence of a remaining block is restored, as its error is in the donor span. Conversely any newly dependent combination, reduced modulo P, was originally zero because retained block spans are independent modulo P. If m>2s there is a surviving Fano minor. Thus distance from every regular binary representation on the same labelled columns is at least ceil(m/2) in additive rank, even if errors couple blocks. Width is unbounded since the graphic grid is a restriction.

For terminals lying in the central ambient, an unmarked leaf can be dropped while preserving every trace whenever all three overlap points are available as ordinary columns. Its contracted off-line subset contributes to the central span just a subspace of L_i; contract an ordinary triangle basis of that subspace instead. The private z_i are independent, so no other cross-leaf cancellations occur. Conversely a central witness remains feasible after all dropped leaves are restored and ignored. For t physical named elements, mark every leaf containing such an element, including overlap elements. The triangles are disjoint, giving at most t marked leaves. Enumerate the ordinary off-line contraction subset in each marked leaf (at most 16^t branches). A contracted private pivot identifies z_i with a vector of L_i; the remaining contractions quotient central vectors in L_i and can be implemented by contracting corresponding triangle edges, using virtual parallel copies when an edge is named. With no private pivot the z_i row remains a fixed extra equation. Projected physical roots are supported on a bounded number of vertices (each central edge or triangle point has at most two endpoints). Apply graphic-supported-trace.md to the residual graph, excluding original named edges from ordinary choices. This computes the exact full named kernel spectrum on the supplied layout. Unmarked leaf substitutions lift by ignoring those leaves.

Virtual copies implementing a forced contraction are never free choices. Even when its projected vector becomes zero, the named column remains in the terminal matrix. Lifting replaces the forced quotient with the enumerated off-line subset. Keep full vertex incidence rows if deleting physical named edges disconnects the ordinary graph; equivalently retain all component-total equations when reducing incidence rows.

More generally, if primary and secondary spans meet in L and every terminal lies in the primary span, then span(X∪Y) intersected with the primary ambient equals span(X)+(span(Y) intersect L) for primary ordinary X and secondary ordinary Y. Dropping the entire secondary side preserves the spectrum whenever each attainable secondary intersection with L is spanned by available ordinary overlap points in that intersection. A full ordinary projective interface suffices independently of the secondary side's size or structure. This criterion is necessary for a uniform replacement based solely on the overlap and secondary data: use the overlap itself for primary ordinary columns, add a separately named basis of L, and test the exact missing subspace. On particular primary configurations additional columns can also permit the replacement.
