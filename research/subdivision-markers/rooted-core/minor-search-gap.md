# What bounded cores do and do not provide for search

For any prescribed pair of attained scores q,r, branch at an ordinary atom by imposing primal zero (deletion) or dual zero (contraction), with exact scored lifts. If every atom loses one of q,r in both possible minors, disjointness forces opposite usage there in every realization of q and of r. The two targets each then have a unique ordinary raw support pair and they partition the remaining atoms oppositely. Thus finite-core.md bounds every such minor when some score p is absent. The argument also works without an absent score, with weaker cap obtained from e+k≤t in place of t−1. A common free offset space is quotiented at each step. When two actual target witnesses are supplied, compatible restrictions can be constructed by linear switches; that is distinct from discovering a witness for an unknown target.

For a score-deficient instance with s attainable scores, group any doubly indispensable atoms by a chosen unordered pair q,r lost respectively in the deletion and contraction branches. Each q witness uses that atom primally and every r witness dually (or vice versa). A restriction chain that keeps q,r cannot remove such an atom, and cannot introduce new raw witnesses contradicting its forced usage since all scored minors lift. A jointly minimal minor for that pair therefore contains its entire group and has at most B(t) atoms. In particular a full-spectrum-minimal deficient minor has at most binom(s,2)B(t) atoms. This is an existence statement for exact-preserving branches.

**A native obstruction to guessing reduction choices at random.** Three independent copies of the binary two-element parallel matroid realize all four-bit scores except p=(1,1,1,1). In copy i the nonempty cycle and nonempty cocycle each use both elements, so at most one can be selected. Assign its primal gain to coordinate one and its dual gain to coordinate i+1. The three latter bits determine which dual choices are forced; the first bit is free if some dual bit is zero and forced to zero when all three are one.

Take the direct sum of this gadget and a length-m binary circuit K. Assign K primal gain w≠0 (place it at any one atom) and give every K cocycle zero gain. The total image is full: K alone contributes the subspace {0,w}, and adding this to the one-hole image fills p. Every p witness uses the entire circuit primally. Any minor that imposes primal zero on even one K atom consequently loses p and has exact image all scores except p, as long as the three baseline copies are retained. A restriction imposing only dual zero on K atoms retains p, again with full image. This holds under the exact scored-lift semantics rather than dropping the gain when an atom vanishes.

All projections onto at most three independent bits have full image throughout these branches, whether p is reachable or not. If all but k of the K atoms are independently assigned deletion/contraction branches with probability one half, the probability of preserving p is 2^{−(m−k)}. Even enumerating every possible retained k-subset cannot repair a large collection of wrongly guessed primal-zero choices. This refutes a uniform random-branch argument or a detector using only the YES/NO answers to projected score queries on these prefixes. The displayed circuit is itself recognizable by linear algebra; the example does not preclude a structure-aware algorithm or an oracle that returns full witnesses. Its purpose is to separate small-minor existence from locating a compatible contraction/deletion set.

## Acquiring all alternative scores

For each q distinct from the requested four-bit target p, use the constructive three-bit solver on the quotient by the line spanned by p+q. The fiber over the projected target consists exactly of p and q. A negative answer rules out p. A returned witness either proves p feasible or supplies an original-coordinate q witness. Thus fifteen calls suffice to solve the instance or to seed every other score. In the latter case both a complete image and an image missing only p remain possible. Compression preserving those fifteen seeds need not preserve p.
