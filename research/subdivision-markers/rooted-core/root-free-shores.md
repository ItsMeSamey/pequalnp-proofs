# Finding and pruning arbitrary terminal-free shores of connectivity at most two

For a binary vector representation with named columns T, the trace of a choice F of ordinary columns is the coefficient kernel {x:T x belongs to span(F)}. Unselected ordinary columns are deleted and selected ones contracted. The procedure preserves every exact kernel at once and produces original ordinary choices on traceback.

For each four-element subset X of the current ordinary ground, minimize lambda(A)=rank(A)+rank(E−A)−rank(E) subject to X⊆A⊆E−T. This is unconstrained submodular minimization on the other ordinary elements with X adjoined. Matrix rank gives its evaluation oracle. Thus any terminal-free shore of size at least four with lambda≤2 is found if it exists. Let B=E−A and W=span(A) intersect span(B). Its dimension is lambda(A).

For a two-dimensional W and a candidate nonzero line point h, let u,v be its other two nonzero points, so u+v=h. Append synthetic named elements u,v to A only for this local test. A subset of A has span meeting W in precisely <h> iff u,v lie on a common circuit of that local matroid. Indeed the ordinary part of such a circuit sums to h and cannot span either u or v by minimality. Conversely a minimal subset expressing h inside an exact-line witness closes to a circuit with both points. A common circuit exists exactly when the two elements lie in the same matroid component. One can delete ordinary elements while this remains true to recover a circuit.

Replace A with one optional ordinary column for each attainable line in W. The empty choice gives zero, and the complete plane is attainable on both sides of the replacement. To see at least two attainable lines, take an independent basis of A and project it modulo W. Choose a basis among the projected elements. For each remaining basis element, its projected fundamental relation lifts to a vector of W; these lifts form a basis of W. Each fundamental support alone realizes its line. For dimension one use the one nonzero point, and for dimension zero use no columns. Store one ordinary witness for every attainable subspace, including all of A for the plane.

For exterior ordinary choices Q⊆B, the intersection of span(Q∪F) with span(B) is span(Q)+(span(F) intersect W), for F⊆A. This identity proves both directions for all named coefficient kernels, including dependencies among named elements. Backtrack by replacing the chosen subset of the at most three virtual columns with the original chosen witness for their whole span. Reverse each replacement once even if later cuts included some of its synthetic columns. Synthetic elements need not occur simultaneously in a matroid minor. A named column is never removed.

Repeat until no tested shore qualifies. Strict size decrease gives at most n passes and O(n^5) rank-oracle submodular minimizations in total, plus polynomial linear algebra and witness reconstruction. The number of distinct local states never exceeds five. No fixed terminal count is needed for this reduction. The remainder has every set of four ordinary elements separated from the whole named set only at connectivity at least three. This implies no bound on its size. When a supported exact final solver applies, composing it with this reduction preserves all of its accepted kernels. Primary submodular algorithm: Iwata, Fleischer and Fujishige, https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0004089 .
