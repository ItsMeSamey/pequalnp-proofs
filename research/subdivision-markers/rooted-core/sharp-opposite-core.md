# Sharp bound for two opposite unique scores

Let M be a binary represented matroid on n ordinary elements. Give each disjoint cycle/cocycle pair the sum of two linear scores on its supports, in a t-dimensional binary space. Suppose distinct targets q,r each have a unique ordinary raw support pair, respectively (A,B),(B,A), where A,B partition the ordinary ground. Then n≤2^(t−1). No missing target is needed. A fixed affine score translation is harmless. A common freely selectable score subspace may first be quotiented out, using the projected dimension and targets. Uniqueness counts ordinary raw supports irrespective of hidden-lift multiplicities.

In any basis write [I|R], and let the full selector s=(x,y) specify dual basis bits x and primal nonbasis bits y. The represented cycle is (Ry,y); the cocycle is (x,Rᵀx). This is a bijection between selectors and ordinary pairs before imposing disjointness. Put J=[[0,R],[Rᵀ,0]]. The conflict indicator is the coordinatewise product s*(Js); its weight is always even since the cycle and cocycle are orthogonal. Write L for the linear score on selectors after removing any fixed offset.

Let a select (A,B), and let 1 be the all-one selector. The selector of (B,A) is a+1. Since the two supports partition the ground, Ja=1+a and J(a+1)=a. In particular J1=1. Let K be the kernel of L projected modulo span(q+r); its codimension h is at most t−1. Also 1 lies in K since L1=q+r.

For z in K let w=(I+J)z. The conflict vectors of a+z and a+1+z are respectively (a+z)*w and (a+1+z)*w: at each selected site the other represented bit equals w_i. These two vectors partition supp(w). When z differs from both zero and 1, both trial selectors differ from both designated selectors. Each has score q or r. Uniqueness forces both conflict counts to be positive, and each is even. Thus w has even weight at least four.

On K the kernel of I+J is exactly span(1). Its image is an even binary linear code of length n and dimension n−h−1, with no word of weight two. Choose a rank h+1 parity-check matrix with an all-one first row. Its columns must be distinct: a repeated pair would give a weight-two codeword. The remaining h bits distinguish n positions. Hence n≤2^h≤2^(t−1). The zero-dimensional code is covered by the same argument; if n=0 the bound is immediate. For t=4 the bound eight is attained by the native eight-element chart in two-signature-core.md.

## Constructive consequence for supplied witnesses

Suppose the two opposite selectors a,a+1 are known feasible at q,r without a uniqueness promise. If n>2^h, compute K and the kernel of I+J on K. If it has dimension above one, find z outside span(1) with w=0. Both trials have zero conflict. Otherwise the even image code has redundancy h+1. Repeated parity-check columns i,j give a solution in K of (I+J)z=e_i+e_j. Since the two conflict counts are even and sum to two, at least one trial has no conflicts. In either case this supplies a new ordinary witness to q or r using Gaussian elimination. With free offsets use the quotient, then choose the original offset for the realized target.

For any two supplied witnesses which are not opposite on every ordinary element, a compatible restriction preserves both: delete where both primal bits are zero, or contract where both dual bits are zero. If the starting witnesses are opposite, the newly obtained witness differs from its old target witness on some formerly covered site, making it compatible there with the other target witness. Repeating exactly score-lifted restrictions therefore reduces a pair of seeded targets to at most 2^(t−1) ordinary sites, without requiring a missing score. If a quotient by free offsets identifies both targets, a single raw witness realizes them both and ordinary reductions can follow that one witness instead. This does not find a witness for an unseeded target.
