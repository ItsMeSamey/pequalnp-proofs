# Finite opposite-unique cores via an even binary code

Assume the t-bit scores q,r are attained uniquely by the disjoint cycle/cocycle pairs (A,B),(B,A) on a partition of a binary matroid, and p is missing. Assume both parts are nonempty. The statement concerns raw supports; a common freely selectable score space is first quotiented out and the quotient can be padded to t bits.

Use the maximal-A chart and the common Gram section of unconditional-gram-section.md. With e=|A outside I| and k=|B∩I| we have e,k≥1 and e+k≤t−1 because supported A cycles and B cocycles give an injective subspace of scores through q, avoiding p. Let n=|X| and let R be the rank of the map Θ from subsets of X defined in that note. Its gain component is modulo the nonzero q+r. Each exceptional A trace has odd weight (A is a cycle); each clean row has even weight (B is a cycle); and the exceptional B trace of each Y column is odd (B is a cocycle). Consequently θ_i lies in an affine space of dimension at most (t−1)+(e−1)+(k−1), and R≤t+e+k−2.

Put K=ker Θ. If u∈K is nonzero, its support and that of Gu are incomparable by the forward and reverse switches. Since every exceptional A trace has odd parity, u has even weight. Gu also has even weight, since the clean Gram graph is Eulerian. The intersection of the supports has even size, since G is alternating over GF(2). The two differences of supports are therefore nonempty even sets. It follows that (I+G)u has even weight at least four. In particular I+G is injective on K, and its image there is an even binary linear code of length n, dimension n−R and minimum nonzero weight at least four.

For n>0 a full-rank parity-check matrix for this code can have the all-one vector as first row. Its columns must be distinct because a repeated pair would put a weight-two word in the code. Hence n≤2^(R−1)≤2^(t+e+k−3). This argument includes the zero code. If n=0 there is nothing to bound. Apply exactly the same proof in the dual matroid with complement basis and interchanged shores to bound |Y| by the same formula; exception counts e,k swap. The full ground set then has size at most 2^(t+e+k−2)+e+k, hence at most 2^(2t−3)+t−1.

For t=4 the unconditional bound sharpens to 34. If e=k=1, both clean shores have odd size because A,B are each self-orthogonal, and each clean size is at most eight, hence at most seven. The full ground then has at most 16 elements. If e+k=3 exactly one clean shore is even, so their sizes are at most 16 and 15, giving 34. These upper bounds are not asserted sharp. The raw four-bit examples at size eight and exclusions at sizes 10, 12 and 14 are in two-signature-core.md, unrestricted-ten.md and projected-collisions.md.

If either A or B is empty, the other equals the entire ground and is both a cycle and cocycle. Pure cycles (at the all-primal witness) and pure cocycles (at the all-dual witness) each have injective score restrictions and cannot span all t bits because p is absent. Their dimensions sum to |E|, so |E|≤2(t−1). Thus the stated bound for t=4 also covers these degenerate partitions. The quotient argument requires common offsets available independently for every raw pair.

The result is a size theorem for two opposite unique witnesses in one score-deficient instance. No algorithm for arbitrary score reachability or for finding a suitable score-preserving minor is claimed here.
