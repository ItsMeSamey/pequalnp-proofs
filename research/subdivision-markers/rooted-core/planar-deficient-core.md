# Planarity of deficient opposite-unique four-score ordinary cores

Assume the hypotheses of sharp-opposite-core.md with four score bits, and assume at least one score is absent. Then the underlying ordinary binary matroid is planar graphic.

The sharp bound gives at most eight elements. Both the all-ones cycle and all-ones cocycle exist, since each is the sum of the two complementary designated supports in its corresponding space. Thus every circuit and cocircuit has even cardinality. A seven-element Fano restriction is impossible, as a triangle survives as a circuit of the original matroid. Suppose instead that contracting one nonloop e gives the Fano matroid. The original rank is four. The all-ones cocycle gives a linear functional taking value one on every column, including e. Each quotient vector modulo e has exactly one representative of value one. Consequently the eight columns form the full affine hyperplane in binary four-space. Its row space is self-orthogonal, since the intersection size of two affine hyperplanes on binary three-space is even (constant functions included). Rank four implies that the cycle and cocycle spaces coincide. For each of their sixteen vectors h the translated supports (A+h,B+h) form another disjoint full pair. Uniqueness of the original score forces the linear score increments to have trivial kernel on this four-space. They therefore exhaust four-bit score space, contradicting deficiency. Contracting a loop is a deletion. Duality rules out the dual Fano minor.

The binary hypothesis excludes the four-point line. The cycle and bond matroids of the two Kuratowski graphs each have at least nine elements. Tutte's excluded-minor characterization therefore gives the claim. See https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1959-0101527-3 . Planarity applies to the ordinary core only. Locating a compatible minor with its protected scores in an arbitrary input remains open here.
