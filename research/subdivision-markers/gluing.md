# Marker-preserving gluing over at most two shared vertices

Work with induced even-degree vertex sets in finite simple graphs whose vertices carry labels in the binary t-space. The following algorithm assumes a given tree of edge-owning pieces. Child interiors are disjoint from the parent and other child interiors. Each child meets its parent on at most two vertices; several children may have common ports. Each original edge has exactly one owner. Intersections obey the running intersection property for each vertex. Child subgraphs are their full subtrees of owned edges and vertices; only their ports can meet the outside. Charge each vertex label in the topmost piece containing it. This convention gives every child table the labels of its interior, excluding all exterior port labels.

For a child with ports u,v store the set of attainable interior labels for each port selection and their common boundary-degree parity q, with all selected interior vertices even. Parities count only owned edges in its subtree. The boundary parities agree by the handshake identity; unselected ports have parity zero. With one or zero ports all boundary parities are zero. Store parent pointers for recovered witnesses.

## A two-port representative

Let d equal one if the child owns edge uv anywhere in its subtree. With the boundary selection b alone, the boundary parity is d b_u b_v and the interior label zero. A parity flip can occur only for b_u=b_v=1. In that case a flip is possible precisely when the child contains an alternate u-v path after deleting the direct edge. Necessity follows since removing the possible direct edge from the induced edge set leaves exactly u and v odd and therefore connects them. For sufficiency choose a shortest alternate path. It is induced in the child graph with that direct edge removed. Taking its whole vertex set realizes the flip with interior label p, the sum over its strict interior. Other edges outside the child's scope are excluded from its boundary parities. The direct-edge indicator and a canonical alternate path can be obtained directly from the full original child subgraph.

To make the parent skeleton, insert the child-owned direct edge when present and an additional virtual path u-z-v with fresh middle vertex when an alternate exists. The activation e=s_u s_v s_z equals one exactly for the fully selected path. The generated skeleton is even at z, so no one-ended selected path occurs, and an isolated z has e=0. If there is no path set e=0 identically; its label contribution is also zero. In all cases this gadget models parity q=d b_u b_v+e and supplies the canonical interior label e p. The gadget has no separate real vertex label. Different virtual paths receive different middle vertices, including parallel paths with the same pair of ports. Unique ownership of a physical direct edge avoids duplicates.

A child attached on one vertex contributes no edges to the skeleton. Its baseline contribution is zero for both possible port states, attained by taking only the selected port if any. A child with no port has the empty baseline.

To compute a nonroot table at ports u,v augment the skeleton by an additional parent-port virtual path whose activation is prescribed as the requested outgoing parity. It adds this bit to both port degrees and otherwise changes no real degree. Thus the completed skeleton is even exactly when the represented real child has the requested two boundary parities. Prescribe the two port bits as well. With one port just prescribe that bit; a zero-port root needs no augmentation.

## Preserving arbitrary marker labels

For any state of the skeleton, the true interior label set of child j is read from its previously computed table. Subtract its canonical contribution to obtain a correction set containing zero. Given an actual global witness, the sum of all child corrections is the sum of at most t of those same corrections: use a basis of their span. Keep those children and reset every other child to its canonical witness for exactly the same boundary selection and parity. Conversely choosing one attainable correction per child and agreeing port selections gives a global witness with the prescribed labels and all interior degrees even. The argument also applies to one-port and zero-port children.

One can locate the exceptional children without an n^t factor. Assign binary IDs to the at most n children. Any q≤t distinct IDs have distinct signatures on at most q−1 bit positions: start with no positions, and add a distinguishing position from any unresolved pair until all signatures differ. Enumerate the q, those positions, the q distinct ordered signatures, and q nonzero correction labels. For each bucket i require at least one member j whose prescribed correction is in its state-dependent set. All other children can use baseline. Since buckets are disjoint there is no conflict of child indices. The position count is bounded by (1+log n)^t, which is at most f(t)n by maximizing x^t e^(−x). Signature and label guesses cost 2^{O(t²)}. Empty lists handle q=0 and t=0.

Suppose the augmented skeleton is parametrized by selection bits s_v of degree ≤a and r further degree-≤a output conditions for its exact validity. Each child eligibility is a Boolean function of at most three individual skeleton vertex bits, hence degree ≤3a on the underlying parameters. With a fresh freely selectable bit w_j per bucket member, require the sum of w_j times eligibility_j to be one over the binary field. This is possible exactly when the bucket is nonempty among eligible indices and has degree ≤3a+1. The marker equations contain real local labels and the canonical e p terms, plus the already guessed child corrections; their degree is at most 3a. Prescribed parent activation is one further degree-3a condition. There are O(t+r+1) output equations. Viola's sum-of-small-bias hitting algorithm for bounded-degree maps in subdivision.md gives f(a,t,r) n^{O(a)} time, times polynomial-time map evaluation. For fixed a and bounded maximum local rank r this is fixed-parameter in the marker count. A bounded cover can alternatively be handled by local branching.

Enumerate all parent states and labels and process the tree bottom-up. Eligibility lookups are in complete finite tables, so degree and output count do not grow with the depth. A successful bucket equation exhibits an eligible child; select one in each bucket and follow its stored witness, using the canonical witness for all others. Tables and recovery pointers require only a polynomial factor times a function of the parameters. Two-vertex parallel pieces of arbitrarily high multiplicity can instead use direct XOR convolution over the t labels and the common boundary parity with boundary selections fixed.

The one-port audit four/blockglue.py compares 489 full subtree tables from 161 generated trees with direct enumeration. The two-port audit four/twoport.py compares 1,431 two-port subtree tables from 414 decompositions, including all combinations of direct and alternate paths, multiple attachments to the same port pair and reversed port order. Both scripts test finite recurrences. The fast generator and its own tests are in prg/. No claim is made that every graph has an eligible piece decomposition.
