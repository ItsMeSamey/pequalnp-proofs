# Projection obstructions for positive pins in arbitrary simple graphs

A graph gadget has disjoint observed vertices T, private vertices, and positive pins P among the private vertices. A standalone witness is a vertex set containing P whose induced subgraph has only even degrees. Let R be the family of observed supports of all witnesses. Its upward closure accepts an allowed mask U⊆T if some witness uses only observed vertices in U. The results here apply to exact projected relations with no external promise excluding masks.

## Two private pins give a vertex-path family

If the two pins a,b are nonadjacent, {a,b} itself realizes the empty support. The same conclusion holds for fewer than two pins. Suppose ab is an edge. For each allowed mask U, delete T∖U and the edge ab. A witness implies a surviving a–b path: in its even induced subgraph the edge ab belongs to a cycle. Conversely a shortest a–b path in the surviving graph, with ab restored, is an induced cycle and gives a witness.

Delete ab, keep the visible vertices T∪{a,b}, and for every component of the other vertices make its visible neighborhood a clique before removing that component. This produces one fixed undirected graph K on exactly |T|+2 vertices. Connectivity between visible vertices after deletion of any observed set is preserved, by contracting excursions through hidden components to edges and expanding torso edges back to walks. Hence the upward closure of R is exactly the predicate that a and b are connected in K[U∪{a,b}]. Conversely every monotone predicate of this path form occurs as an upward closure: when K lacks ab, use the gadget K+ab with both endpoints pinned. If K contains ab the predicate is constant true and a private triangle through the forced edge realizes it.

Suppose the empty support is infeasible. Then K has no edge ab. The inclusion-minimal attainable supports are precisely the inclusion-minimal sets of internal vertices of a–b paths in K. The maximum number of pairwise disjoint such supports equals the minimum number of observed vertices meeting every support. Indeed the two quantities are the maximum number of internally disjoint paths and minimum internal vertex cut, respectively. Both are zero when there is no path. The same equality persists after arbitrary observed vertices are forbidden. The graph reduction is needed here because paths in the original gadget may share hidden vertices freely. [Menger, Zur allgemeinen Kurventheorie](https://doi.org/10.4064/fm-10-1-96-115)

In particular no graph with at most two private positive pins has a projected relation whose upward closure is the k-of-n predicate for 1<k<n. All k-subsets would be minimal. Their maximum disjoint packing has size floor(n/k), whereas a transversal requires n−k+1. These disagree since k(n−k+1)−n=(k−1)(n−k)>0. This rules out even the 2-of-3 predicate, where no individual observed vertex is mandatory.

## Arbitrary attachments change the forcing question

Allow a host to add vertices and edges meeting a gadget only at its ports T, with distinct edge ownership. A partial witness has even degree at every selected private vertex while selected ports may have odd degree. Every partial witness can be completed in some host: pair its odd ports and add an external two-edge path for each pair, selecting the fresh middle vertices.

If a gadget with at most two private positive pins has any partial witness, it has one using at most two ports. Nonadjacent pins can stand alone. Otherwise extend a partial witness to a host as above, take the induced graph of that full solution, and find a shortest cycle through the pin edge ab by removing ab and taking a shortest a–b path. The cycle is induced. If it contains no ports, it already witnesses the claim. Otherwise start at each pinned endpoint and follow the cycle away from ab, stopping at the first port encountered. Keep the two initial segments and ab. When both segments reach the same port they exhaust the cycle. Every internal private vertex still has degree two, since the cycle was chordless, and private edges on those segments necessarily belong to the gadget. Any edge between two retained ports is harmless for partial validity. This establishes the upper bound. It is tight: the gadget path u–a–b–v with private pins a,b and ports u,v requires both ports. Thus a universally composable replacement using two positive pins cannot force selection of three ports. In an isolated gadget, adjacent pins on a chordless cycle force the entire cycle, including arbitrarily many observed vertices.

## Through-witness affine restriction for any number of pins

For a full witness W let A be the binary adjacency matrix on W and K its kernel. The all-ones vector belongs to K. For each v∈K that vanishes on P, the coordinates 1+v give another witness contained in W: A(1+v)=0 guarantees an even number of selected neighbors at each selected vertex, and every pin is retained. Consequently the projection R contains an affine flat through W∩T of dimension at least rank(π_(W∩T) K)−|P|. Coordinates of T outside W stay zero. More precisely the contained flat has direction π_(W∩T)(K∩ker π_P). For a unique traced output this direction must vanish for every witness; equivalently, the evaluations on all selected observed coordinates restricted to K belong to the span of the pin coordinate evaluations. This is a necessary condition expressed on a candidate witness and places no bound on an unspecified gadget's size.

The independent checker enumerates all induced supports and compares their upward closure against torso reachability, compares direct packing and transversal enumeration, and checks the contextual two-port bound by ignoring parity at ports. It passed 9,966 graph/observation-mask instances: every graph through five vertices (with two distinguished pins and every observation choice) plus 1,500 seeded larger instances through eleven vertices.
