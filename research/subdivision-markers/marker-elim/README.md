# Eliminating arbitrary markers into hidden 2-SAT coordinates

Input over the binary field consists of affine markers and product guards (a·x+c)(b·x+d)=0. Native single-alternative guarded bits are the case with first factor x_i. Neither acyclicity nor symmetry is needed. The method returns a witness in original coordinates. It also applies to disjunctive guards whenever every nonempty violation flat has codimension at most two after imposing the markers.

## Recognition on the marker slice

Compute a point and kernel basis of the marker system, thereby writing every assignment satisfying the markers as x=x0+Uy. Reject an inconsistent slice. A guard violation asserts that both chart-affine factors equal one. Eliminate dependent equations in each resulting flat; discard empty violations and reject a full-space violation. For a remaining forbidden flat let W_i be the span of its one or two independent normal vectors. Translation of this flat affects its right hand sides only.

Seek an independent dual basis D making each W_i a coordinate subspace, so each is spanned by elements of D. Give a nonzero normal v weight equal to the number of W_i containing it. Each W_i has at most three nonzero members, all enumerable. For any basis D the weight sum equals the sum of |D intersect W_i|. Each summand is at most dim(W_i), with equality exactly when the space is coordinate in D. Find a maximum-weight independent set greedily, considering normals by decreasing weight, and complete it with standard unit vectors of weight zero. The linear matroid exchange argument proves optimality: in weight order an independent prefix can be exchanged into an optimum basis, replacing one element of no larger weight at each new insertion. The maximum equals the sum of dimensions exactly if a simultaneous basis exists. Every optimum basis then works. This recognizes the condition independently of how the input presents equivalent guards; checking just their given pairs of normal vectors is insufficient.

Write z_j=D_j·y. In a simultaneous basis each violation fixes at most two of the z bits to one tuple. Exclude that tuple by one width-at-most-two clause. Ordinary implication-graph 2-SAT decides this formula, and invert the full-rank chart to return x. This gives exact polynomial decision and witness recovery on the recognizably compatible class, with no decomposition and no restriction on graph density, marker number or polar rank. These arguments use standard linear matroid greedy and 2-SAT. The specific elimination step is their application to the intrinsic normal planes of the marker-restricted violation flats.

## Computable coordinate-deletion parameter

Use the weight-optimal basis just constructed, breaking ties by numeric order. For each flat form its row matrix in those coordinates with at most two rows. After fixing coordinates S, the restricted normal space is the row space of the matrix with those columns removed, unless the projected system is inconsistent, in which case the violated flat disappears. We seek the least |S| ensuring all these projected row spaces are coordinate independently of right hand sides. A row space with support T equals the coordinate span on T exactly if its rank is |T|. Equivalently, all its nonzero columns are independent. The minimal obstructions are two equal nonzero columns and three distinct nonzero columns in a rank-two row matrix. These criteria include dimensions zero and one.

If some obstruction remains, branch on deleting each of its at most three members. Any successful deletion set must hit it. Increasing the depth limit until all obstructions vanish finds a minimum set of size s in time O*(3^s). With all 2^s settings of the deleted coordinates, discard inconsistent forbidden flats and convert each remaining flat to its unique forbidden tuple on its surviving support. Run 2-SAT for each branch, undo the coordinate chart on success, and reject if all branches fail. Total deterministic time is O*(3^s+2^s) and working space is polynomial. The minimization here is for the specified basis and the uniform normal-space condition. A different basis or branch-dependent redundancy could give a smaller semantic parameter.

## Separation and verification

On n=2m vertices, take the acyclic matching guards x_(2i+1) x_(2i)=0, and pin x0=1. Before pinning, the polar form of the sum of guards is block-diagonal with m alternating nonsingular 2-by-2 blocks. After the pin, the rank remains 2m−2. The forbidden normal spaces remain coordinate, hence s=0. More broadly the compatible class includes any affine invertible re-encoding of an arbitrary 2-CNF; the linear changes may make every displayed guard dense, while the 2-SAT interaction graph itself is unrestricted. Such examples use general two-affine-factor guards. To realize them as single-head native guards, give each displayed first factor a separate head bit, pin it equal to that affine factor with a marker, and let its guard require the second factor to be zero when the head is one. Make chart variables unrestricted; all dependencies then have depth one. This is a polynomial slice representation with unrestricted marker count.
