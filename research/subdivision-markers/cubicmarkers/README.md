# Two pins and one parity on subdivisions

Let G be simple and subcubic. Assume every edge has an endpoint of degree at most two (as happens if every edge of a subcubic base graph is subdivided). Every union of vertex-disjoint cycles is induced on its vertex set. Indeed, any incident edge at a degree-two vertex of a cycle is used by the cycle. This excludes all chords and all edges between distinct cycles.

For nonadjacent positive pins a,b one further affine marker w of arbitrary support can be decided in deterministic polynomial time. Start with {a,b}. If its parity is correct return it. Any marked vertex outside T=N[a] union N[b] could be added as an isolated vertex to flip the parity. Thus in the remaining case w is supported on T, of size at most eight. Enumerate membership on T with the pins selected and correct parity, and for each selected vertex there enumerate whether it is isolated. Reject adjacent proposed isolates or an isolate adjacent to a selected cycle vertex. Remove unselected vertices of T and closed neighborhoods of the isolates. Seek a spanning subgraph in which the selected cycle vertices of T have degree two and other vertices have allowed degrees zero or two. This is the parity (g,f)-factor problem, with g=f=2 at prescribed vertices and g=0, f=2 elsewhere. Any successful factor gives a union of vertex-disjoint cycles, which by the hypothesis is induced. Reinsert the isolates. Conversely, a witness restricts to one of the enumerated traces; its cycles form the required factor since irrelevant isolates outside T may be discarded.

For adjacent positive pins a,b and arbitrary weights supported on degree-two vertices, there is also a deterministic polynomial algorithm on this graph class. A witness consists of a cycle through ab and anticomplete components. If the latter flip the parity, just one marked vertex from them suffices in isolation. Enumerate no additional vertex and each possible marked vertex v outside N[a] union N[b], forbidding N[v] in the latter case. Seek a cycle through ab with the residual parity. Convert vertex weights to edge weights by assigning each marked degree-two vertex's contribution to one of its two incident edges, XORing contributions at collisions. Do this before any deletions. Every cycle now has the same parity under either encoding. Delete ab and seek a simple a-b path of the required parity corrected by the edge weight of ab. Along the unique chain of blocks between a and b in the block-cut tree, balanced blocks (all cycles even weight) contribute fixed path parity computable by vertex potentials. Bridges do likewise. Any unbalanced two-connected block supplies both parities between its entry and exit: find an odd-weight cycle from a potential conflict on a fundamental cycle, then two vertex-disjoint paths joining the entry and exit to distinct points on this cycle, by two-connectivity and Menger's theorem, truncating at the first cycle vertices. The two arcs differ by the cycle parity. Concatenate the block paths and restore ab. Disconnection after deleting ab means no solution.

The adjacent-pin algorithm does not cover arbitrary weights on branch vertices. Neither result settles three general markers on subcubic graphs.

## General parity-marker solver

Let t be the number of marker bits and Δ the maximum degree. Enumerate at most t pairwise nonadjacent isolates and delete their closed neighborhoods. This suffices because the XOR contributed by the isolates of any solution can be represented by a linearly independent subset of their labels. All discarded isolates can be omitted. On the residual graph use binary coordinates z along the fundamental cycles of any spanning forest, so each edge bit E_e is a linear form. Each even edge set induces exactly the graph on its active vertices: a missing edge between active vertices would have an active endpoint of degree at most two, which must use both incident edges. Conversely, after discarding isolates every even induced witness is represented.

The activity A_v is the OR of the incident edge bits. Local even parity lets us omit any one of them without changing their OR. At degree zero the activity is constant zero. Thus A_v has Boolean polynomial degree at most max(0,Δ−1). For residual target h and vertex labels L_v, form the indicator product over marker rows i of (1+h_i+sum_v L_vi A_v), with all operations in GF(2). Its multilinear reduction has degree at most D=t max(0,Δ−1). A nonzero multilinear polynomial of degree at most D is nonzero on an assignment of weight at most D: a nonzero coefficient indexed by S is the sum of evaluations over all subsets of S. Enumerate these sparse basis assignments and verify labels directly. This proves deterministic polynomial time for each fixed t,Δ. The runtime is bounded by the product of the numbers of candidate isolates and sparse cycle-basis assignments, times a polynomial in the input size.

solver.py implements the algorithm. test_solver.py matches brute-force enumeration on all 2,196 target instances of simple graphs through order three with all two-bit vertex labelings, plus 1,701 target instances over 300 seeded subdivisions through order fourteen with zero to four markers. check.py separately checks the two-pin cases on 120,372 pin and label choices across 590 graphs.
