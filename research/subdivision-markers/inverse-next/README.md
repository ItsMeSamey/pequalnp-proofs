# Exact dimension of binary affine matrix spaces with affine inversion

For every r>=1, the largest dimension of an affine space of invertible r by r binary matrices on which inversion is affine equals floor(r²/4). For the lower bound choose all I+X supported off the identity in a rectangular p by (r-p) upper-right block. Their inverses equal themselves.

We use the Schur–Jacobson dimension bound: linearly independent pairwise commuting r by r matrices over any field number at most floor(r²/4)+1. See [Maryam Mirzakhani, A Simple Proof of a Theorem of Schur](https://old.maa.org/sites/default/files/pdf/pubs/mirzakhani.pdf). The proof below does not require classification of cases of equality in that bound.

## Central matrices and reduction

Left-multiply the original affine space by the inverse of one of its elements, putting it in the form I+U. Define the linear map L:U→M_r by (I+X)^(-1)=I+L(X). Set D_X=X+L(X). Expanding both inverse identities gives XL(X)=L(X)X=D_X. Polarizing gives X L(Y)=Y L(X) and L(X)Y=L(Y)X. Thus all doubled matrices with off-diagonal blocks X and L(X) and zero diagonal commute. Their squares are diag(D_X,D_X). In particular every D_X commutes with all Y,L(Y), and all D_X commute with one another. Other useful identities are

    X²=(I+X)D_X,
    XY+YX=X D_Y+Y D_X.

If some D_X is nonnilpotent but singular, split the underlying vector space as the kernel and image of a sufficiently high power of D_X. Both summands are nonzero and are preserved by all Y,L(Y). Each restricted space is again a direction space for affine inversion: if Y restricts to zero then so does L(Y), since the restricted inverse is unique. The original dimension is at most the sum of the two projected dimensions. Since floor(a²/4)+floor(b²/4)<=floor((a+b)²/4), it suffices by induction to treat two cases: all D_X nilpotent, or some D_X invertible.

## All central defects nilpotent

Let R be the unital algebra generated by U. Each L(X) and D_X lies in R since the inverse of I+X is a polynomial in X. The ideal J generated by the finitely many central nilpotent D_X is nilpotent, as any sufficiently long product repeats a central factor often enough. Modulo J the generators X commute and have square zero. The nonunital subalgebra they generate there is nilpotent: a word longer than the number of generators repeats a factor. Consequently the nonunital algebra generated by U is nilpotent in End(V). The filtration by its powers acting on V supplies a simultaneous strictly upper triangular basis over the binary field.

Let F_i denote upper triangular matrices with possible entries at least i diagonals above the main diagonal. For X in U∩F_i, D_X=(I+X)^(-1)X² belongs to F_(2i). If Y belongs to U∩F_j, the commutator identity gives [X,Y] in F_(i+j+1), since i,j>=1. The subspace of all degree-i leading parts, for i=1,...,r-1, therefore gives pairwise commuting matrices even across distinct degrees: multiplication at the i+j diagonal is precisely multiplication of leading parts. The direct sum of these leading spaces has dimension dim U by the filtration dimension identity. All have zero diagonal, so adjoining I gives a commuting matrix space of dimension 1+dim U. The Schur–Jacobson bound proves the claim.

## An invertible central defect

Fix X with D=D_X invertible. Then both X and L(X) are invertible. Set C={X^(-1)Y:Y in U}. This space contains I and has dimension dim U. Its elements commute: the relation X L(Y)=Y L(X) gives L(Y)=X^(-1)Y L(X); substituting in Y L(Z)=Z L(Y) and canceling the invertible endpoint factors gives the assertion.

All D_Y commute with X and the elements of C. For a=X^(-1)Y the commutator identity gives [X,a]=D_Y+aD. Also Y²=(I+Y)D_Y, whereas

    Y²=XaXa=X²a²+X[X,a]a
       =X²a²+Y D_Y+XDa²
       =Da²+Y D_Y.

The last equality uses X²+XD=D. Hence D_Y=Da² and [X,a]=D(a+a²). The commutator with D^(-1)X restricts on C to a↦a+a².

Suppose dim C>floor(r²/4). Schur–Jacobson then forces dim C=floor(r²/4)+1. The commuting unital algebra generated by C can have no additional dimension, so equals C; denote it A. Adjoining D also preserves commutativity, hence D belongs to A. The commutator just constructed is a derivation on A and sends every a to a+a², including products. Comparing its value at ab with the Leibniz rule gives

    (a+a²)(b+b²)=0  for all a,b in A.

The finite commutative algebra A splits into its local Artinian factors. Each residue field is binary: setting a=b in the identity shows that every residue value equals its square. The radical N in each factor has N²=0, as 1+a and 1+b are units for a,b in N, and the identity factors as ab(1+a)(1+b)=0. Thus every factor is F₂I+N. Its defining idempotent e commutes with X because [X,e]=D(e+e²)=0. The resulting splitting of V into faithful local representations is therefore also X-invariant.

In one such component of ambient binary dimension m, the restriction of the unit D is I+n for n in N. For a in N the commutator is [X,a]=Da=a, since N²=0. The subspace P=sum_(a in N) im(a) is preserved by X, annihilated by N, and N has zero action on V/P. The identity X²=(I+X)D shows that X²+X+I=0 on both P and V/P. Multiplication by X therefore defines a vector space structure over F4 on each (the zero space is allowed). In particular m is even and at least two. Each a in N is a map V/P→P satisfying aX=(X+I)a=X²a on the target. This is exactly Frobenius semilinearity over F4. The dimension over F₂ of all such maps is half the product of the binary dimensions of source and target. Thus

    dim_F₂ N <= dim_F₂(P) dim_F₂(V/P)/2 <= floor(m²/8).

The dimension of this local factor is at most 1+floor(m²/8)<=floor(m²/4) for all even m>=2. Summing across the factors gives dim A<=floor(r²/4), contradicting the assumption. This finishes the invertible case and the induction.
