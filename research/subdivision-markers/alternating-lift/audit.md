# Audit of the alternating lift and an optimal universal repair

Write a one-alternative native guard as x_i (Mx)_i=0, absorbing any affine constant into its diagonal coefficient since x_i²=x_i. Assume the marker system defines a nonempty affine space H=p+K. Its global defect polynomial is q(x)=x^T Mx. Let c be the fewest products of restrictions of homogeneous ambient linear forms summing to q on H.

## Computing c including homogeneous markers

If the polar rank on K is 2r and J is the signed sum of (-1)^q on H, then c=r for J>0 and c=r+1 for J<=0. Polar rank gives the lower bound r. If equality holds, the differentials of all 2r factors must be independent because they span the image of the polar map. Their combined affine map is onto, so the sum of the r independent products strictly favors zero. This also covers r=0. Conversely symplectic reduction gives r products plus an affine residual, which equals its own square. For homogeneous H=K use homogeneous symplectic coordinates; the residual is then homogeneous as q(0)=0. If J>0, q vanishes on the homogeneous radical, and its nonsingular quotient splits into r hyperbolic planes: splitting off a plane at a nonzero singular vector preserves the sign, while a positive sign guarantees a nonzero singular vector in every positive even dimension. This gives homogeneous factors when H=K. For a nonhomogeneous slice use affine coordinates on H: complete the square on the symplectic variables, and a positive sign implies the radical has no linear part and the constant left over is zero. Every affine form on H extends to a homogeneous one on span(H) by extending its differential on K and assigning its value at p. Thus the formula includes constant q=1 on a nonhomogeneous H. The minimality claim is about product decomposition of q.

## Restoring the original active rows

Subtract the ambient matrices u_j v_j^T of the chosen products from M, giving M' whose defect vanishes on H. If H=K set E=K, M''=M'. Otherwise choose homogeneous f equal to one on H and zero on K, and E=span(H). On K the remaining q'(y)=B'(p,y) follows from q'(p+y)=q'(p)=0. Extend this linear restriction to t; set M''=M'+diag(t)+t f^T. On H its row modification is t_i(x_i+1) and thus vanishes for each selected row. The adjusted defect vanishes both on K and H, hence on E. There is a symmetric zero-diagonal matrix A agreeing with M'' on E: on a basis starting with E, prescribe the columns of its bilinear form having second argument in E from M'', reflect them across the diagonal, and extend the complementary principal block by zero. Consistency follows by polarizing the zero defect on E.

In the simple graph of A, add z_j with neighbors exactly where u_j is one and impose the marker z_j=v_j^T x. Original selected vertices now have exactly their required row parities. Thus every feasible extension projects correctly. For every feasible native x the original selected degrees are even. The parity deficits at selected auxiliary vertices z_j form an even-cardinality set by the handshake lemma in the graph with no repair internals. The construction below cancels exactly those deficits and never touches an original vertex. It follows that this is exact projection on all solutions, even for homogeneous zero targets. Pure marker-free feasibility on a homogeneous instance is of course witnessed by zero.

## A sharp sparse universal repair

For c>=3 add one vertex H' and c-1 vertices w_i. Connect w_i to the three vertices z_i,z_c,H' for each i<c. For any selected port set S and any required even deficit set D contained in S, select H' exactly when z_c is unselected and select w_i exactly when i belongs to D. Every selected w_i has degree two. If z_c is selected, the parity of its new neighbors gives its required bit since |D| is even. If it is unselected then H' has even degree for the same reason. This uses c fresh vertices. For c=2 a single common neighbor suffices; for c<=1 no repair is necessary. The alternative with a fresh hub and c distinct length-two paths has c+1 internal vertices and 2c edges.

To prove minimality for universal graph-only repair, assume c>=3 and take any graph on the ports plus a private set of k new vertices. Every selected new vertex must be even; port selections are prescribed. With all ports selected all 2^(c-1) even boundary outputs must be realizable, so k>=c-1. If k=c-1, the column matrix of incidences from new vertices to ports must have full column rank, since port parities equal this matrix times the new selection plus a fixed offset from direct port edges. Hence every one of the 2^k selections must be legal. By singleton and pair selections every new vertex has even port degree and there are no edges between new vertices. Each column is nonzero by independence, with weight at least two. Now leave out port j and select all other ports. A new vertex incident to j has odd selected degree and cannot be used. To achieve all 2^(c-2) even patterns at least c-2 new vertices must remain eligible. Thus j occurs in at most one column. Counting incidences gives 2(c-1)<=c, impossible. Hence c fresh vertices are necessary. This optimality only concerns graphs required to repair every even deficit for every port selection, regardless of the underlying native instance.
