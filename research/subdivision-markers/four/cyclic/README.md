# Long indispensable guard cycles with one terminal

For integers r≥1 and L≥2, use r independent native rings with free observed inputs x_i. Each ring has bits z_0,…,z_(L−1). Impose z_j ⇒ z_(j−1) for j=1,…,L−1 and z_0 ⇒ (z_(L−1) XOR x_i). These are native one-alternative affine guards. Define its local syndrome y_i=z_0 XOR z_(L−1).

When x_i=0 the ring is a directed cycle of implications. Its only feasible strings are the two constants and both have syndrome zero. When x_i=1 the all-zero string gives syndrome zero and the string with just z_0 set gives syndrome one. Hence across independent rings the possible y are precisely the submasks of x.

Use the single global affine equation XOR_i y_i=1 as a terminal. It recognizes the disjunction of the observed input bits. Its minimum terminal count is one even among arbitrary alternative native encodings with free observations, since a native system without terminals accepts all observations by choosing all hidden bits zero. Each individual ring predicate x_i=1 also costs one terminal.

Every guard is necessary for this particular one-terminal implementation. At the all-zero observation delete an arbitrary guard in a ring. The remaining implications form a directed path. The two formerly adjacent marked vertices z_0 and z_(L−1) are distinct positions along that path. Setting a suffix to one separates these two positions and satisfies every surviving implication. This ring then contributes one, and zero in the other rings produces a false acceptance. This proves syntactic indispensability relative to this implementation. The same disjunction also has a simpler direct implementation.

The hidden dependency graph has r strongly connected components of size L, feedback vertex number r, and residual directed height L−1 after **any minimum-cardinality** feedback set is removed. The sum of individual costs is r. All these measures can grow while the global disjunction costs just one. The one-bit syndrome has a translation representation in the two-dimensional space of affine polynomials, regardless of the number and length of cycles. The actual communication matrix of OR across any split with at least one observed bit on each side also has rank exactly two over any field: its rows have just two distinct values, and the empty and one-bit rows/columns give the nonsingular submatrix [[0,1],[1,1]].

On the identical native rings, using r terminals y_i=1 recognizes the conjunction of all r observations. This relation accepts a single point of the r-dimensional Boolean cube. The existing cyclic affine-chart theorem implies at least r terminals for any encoding with free observed bits, since an accepted observation lies on an accepted flat of codimension at most the number of terminals. Thus the same native dependency graph supports both cost 1 and cost r under different terminal predicates. This does not contradict the two-sided local one-hot equality bound: its translation-rank lower bound uses a diagonal target, while OR across a split has rank two.
