# Minimum physical contractions in the aggregate flow selector

Take a binary matrix D with m columns, two-bit edge colors, linear maps T_e into k checksum bits and target t. Each ordinary physical column for a nonzero color v is (u_e,v₁D_e,v₂D_e,T_ev). The named columns are r=(Σu_e,0,0,t) and k independent pure-tag axes. A physical set X is feasible if the kernel of the named columns modulo span X is exactly the line generated by the first named coordinate. Write opt for the minimum cardinality of such an X, or infinity when infeasible. The unconstrained decision predicate is opt finite. [Physical selector and witness characterization](../aggregate-flow-selector/README.md)

## Exact minimum

For f:E→F₂² with Df₁=Df₂=0 and ΣT_ef_e=t let Z(f) consist of its zero coordinates. Let δ be the minimum |Z(f)| among such flows for which D restricted to Z(f) has independent columns, with an empty minimum equal to infinity. Then

    opt = m + 2δ.

Indeed, any feasible X contains a representation Y of r. Removing X outside Y preserves feasibility. Private rows force one or three chosen colors per block. Their sum defines f, with f_e=0 exactly on the triples, so |Y|=m+2|Z(f)|. Every circuit C in Z has Σ_{e∈C}T_e=0 as a matrix. Otherwise the even selections in the triple blocks could produce a nonzero pure-tag dependency by carrying a suitable constant color along C. Recolor any such zero circuit with one nonzero color. This preserves conservation and checksum, and reduces the zero count. Iterate to independence. Conversely independent zeros permit only zero circulations supported there, so the canonical one-or-three realization has exactly the required kernel. This proves the formula and implies every minimizing X is canonical with independent zeros. In particular opt=m means that a target flow is nowhere zero.

When k≤1 an unrestricted zero circuit can always be recolored without changing the checksum by picking a nonzero color in the kernel of its k-by-two gain matrix. The same argument works at any k when all cycle gain matrices are singular as maps from the two-dimensional domain. For general k, omitting independence from the minimum can change the result. A single loop D=[0], T the two-bit identity, and t=0 admits just the all-zero flow and has opt infinite. The same example embeds in every k≥2.

## A gap on connected simple bridgeless cubic graphs

For every fixed k≥0, deciding opt≤m is NP-complete even for D the binary vertex-edge incidence matrix of a connected simple bridgeless cubic graph. The reduction can use any prescribed t, attain every target in the ordinary decision problem on every output, and give rank k to the checksum map on the conserved flow space. More generally, take any polynomially bounded integer p≥2. The two output cases are opt=m and opt≥m+2p with finite opt. Here m always denotes the output graph's edge count.

1. Strict two-bit flow on a cubic graph is equivalent to proper three-edge-coloring, since a zero sum of three nonzero two-bit values requires all three to be distinct. This is NP-complete by Holyer. To arrange connected bridgeless source instances, a bridge forces its color zero by summing conservation across the cut. A source with a bridge can therefore be replaced by Petersen, a fixed connected bridgeless NO instance (also checked below). Otherwise join different components by deleting a chosen edge from each and cross-connecting the exposed endpoints with two new edges. Summing over either side forces equal interface colors, and proper colorings on the two sides can be permuted to agree on that color. The resulting graph is simple, cubic and bridgeless. Every original cycle broken by a removed edge can be closed through the other side. Iterate. [Holyer, The NP-Completeness of Edge-Coloring, 1981](https://doi.org/10.1137/0210055)
2. Choose an edge e=ab. In each of p copies delete e and join b of each copy to a of the next in a ring. This still gives a connected simple bridgeless cubic graph, by the same replacement-path argument. Every conserved flow has the same color c on all p ring edges, by the two-edge cut around each copy. If the source admits a strict flow, identical copies realize it around the ring. Otherwise, c=0 already gives p distinct zeros. If c≠0, restoring e with color c in each copy proves that each copy contains at least one distinct internal zero. This applies to all flows, even with dependent zeros.
3. Choose any edge e' of the ring. For k>0 replace it by k serial copies of K₄ with edge ab removed, joined by single connector edges at their exposed endpoints, including the two external connectors. For k=0 leave e' unchanged. As above, the resulting graph is connected simple bridgeless cubic and all connector colors equal one common value. A collapsed flow on the ring has at most as many zeros as the expanded flow, since a zero substituted edge accounts for at least one zero connector.
4. Fix c₀=01 and a linear functional ℓ(c₀)=1. In gadget j tag just the edge from a to one of the two other vertices (neither b nor a). Let this edge contribute ℓ(color) to bit j. Other edges have zero tags. A proper flow with boundary c₀ has two proper completions, differing by c₀ on the private edge. Indeed, the opposite edge has color c₀ and the four remaining internal edges alternate the other two colors. The private four-cycle also gives an unconstrained conserved perturbation of constant color c₀ that flips only bit j. Hence the checksum function on the conserved flow space has full rank k. Every strict ring flow can be relabeled to give e' color c₀ and then extended properly for every prescribed t.
5. Every ring admits a spanning tree avoiding e'. Give all non-tree edges, including e', color c₀, and extend uniquely over the tree by conservation. The zeros are a subset of that tree. Attach the proper gadgets realizing any target. No new zero is introduced and the surviving original zero set remains acyclic. Therefore the ordinary instance is always YES on the entire reduction, even when the strict threshold fails. For k=0, any spanning tree works directly.

Membership in NP follows by checking a proposed physical set by Gaussian elimination, or verifying a strict target flow at threshold m. The ring and gadgets have m=p|E(G)|+6k edges. Taking p polynomially large gives additive gaps growing as m^(1−ε), for any fixed 0<ε<1 by choosing a sufficiently large fixed power of |E(G)|. This argument gives no constant relative gap.

## Scope and checks

A size-constrained physical witness makes a different decision problem from arbitrary contraction with a fixed named kernel. The very same ordinary instances in the reduction accept in both cases. A polynomial compiler into the ordinary fixed-k aggregate selector on graphic hosts would imply P=NP by composition with its fixed-k algorithm. This is a conditional implication about such a compiler. Cardinality hardness alone provides neither a compiler nor ordinary fixed-root hardness on general binary representations.

Run python check.py. Its direct physical-set enumeration compared Gaussian span tests and independent-zero optimization on 60 target instances (including k=3). An independent enumeration of cycle-space word pairs gave (minimum common zeros, nowhere-zero count) equal to (0,6) on K₄ and (1,0) on Petersen. All three nonzero boundary colors of the tagged K₄ block had the two required completions and the required toggle.
