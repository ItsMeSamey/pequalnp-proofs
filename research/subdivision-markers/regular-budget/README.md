# Regular augmented normal matroids at deficiency one

We consider affine forbidden flats of codimension one or two over a binary vector space. Discard empty forbidden sets; a forbidden universe rejects. Write N_i for the full normal subspace of flat i and take as ground vectors all distinct nonzero members of their union. Let δ=max_I(|I|−dim Σ_{i∈I}N_i). The empty set contributes zero. The promised case is δ≤1, as for native guards restricted to one marker hyperplane: the j distinct pivot unit normals of any j surviving guards lose at most one dimension on the slice. The decision algorithm below additionally assumes that the explicit augmented normal matroid is regular. It uses a known polynomial regular-matroid decomposition and the two basic solvers already documented. No bound on guard dependence depth is assumed.

## Interface budgets and exact small tables

Work with a basic leaf child C meeting the rest D through an interface of normals K of dimension k≤2. Augment piece ambient spaces with unused virtual normals as needed so their intersection is K. For L⊆K let δ_C(L)=max_{I⊆C}(|I|−dim((Σ_{i∈I}N_i+L)/L)); define δ_D(L) likewise. These numbers are computed by linear/partition matroid intersection (Rado's theorem). Work in the total assembled space for global δ. If arbitrary interface values on L are observed, and all other directions are hidden, the guarded affine chart theorem puts a through-point accepting flat of codimension ≤δ_C(L) through every point of the projected feasible language. In particular δ_C(L)=0 means all L states extend, since independent hidden representatives realize any prescribed true literal from each distinct clause; no accepted point need be assumed. For δ=1 the projection, if not empty, is a union of whole spaces and affine hyperplanes. [Guarded affine theorem](/workspace/pnp-research/research/guarded-affine/README.md)

Let F be the extendible child states on K. Empty F rejects. There is an exact representation R on K with δ_R(L)≤δ_C(L) simultaneously for all subspaces L≤K. Full F uses no clauses. Three points of a plane require one forbidden point with normal plane K; its deficiency is positive only at L=K, where non-full F already forces child deficiency at least one. Two points of a plane fix a single nonzero normal ℓ; the unary's deficiency is positive exactly when ℓ∈L, where projection of F is non-full. A singleton fixes a basis of K and has δ_R(L)=dim L by projected ranks; the original projection is a singleton and hence forces this much deficiency by the chart theorem. These cases cover dimension zero and one too. For any outside subfamily O with span V_O, L=V_O∩K is all it sees on the child side. Maximum union deficiency for this fixed O is |O|−dim V_O+δ_C(L). Replacing C by R therefore never increases global or outside-extended maximum deficiency. Deleting C also cannot increase it.

The budgets obey δ_C(K)+δ_D(K)≤δ_global+k: take maximizing subfamilies on the two sides and bound their combined normal rank by the two quotient ranks plus k. Also δ_C(0)+δ_D(0)≤δ_global. A constraint in one side may use a copied virtual representative for a ground normal belonging originally to the other; the represented spans must be used in these formulas.

## Computing a replacement using only deficiency-one calls

All unconditional calls on a leaf have deficiency ≤1. If δ_C(K)≤1, decide its extensions at every one of the up to four states. Conditioning on a fixed subspace contracts its normals; rank-reduced forbidden clauses are simplified. Every nonzero direction of K is a virtual element, so these are matroid minors of the leaf, and clausal deficiency after simplification is at most the conditional budget. Return the exact R above.

If δ_C(K)=k+1, δ_D(K)=0. Every K state extends through D. Therefore solving the unconstrained child decides the ENTIRE current instance. This includes the case k=1 with child budget two.

The only remaining case is k=2 with δ_C(K)=2. Then δ_D(K)≤1 and its extendible set E on K, if nonempty, has at least two points. Check F nonempty by an unconditional call. A nonzero line ℓ⊆K is safe if δ_C(ℓ)≤1. There can be at most one unsafe direction. Indeed δ_C(ℓ)=2 requires a subfamily I with unprojected excess |I|−dim span(I)=1 containing ℓ in its span. The sets of maximum excess 1 are closed under union because excess is supermodular and the global maximum is one. If two lines were unsafe, their witnessing union would already have excess one and contain K, implying δ_C(K)≥3.

Query feasibility under both possible values of each safe line. Two independent fixed directions specify a singleton. One safe fixed direction with no second independent fixed direction specifies an entire two-point line: any singleton would fix the other safe direction as well. These are exact replacements. If no safe direction is fixed, F either contains at least three states, or it equals a line with its normal unsafe. With at least three it meets every nonempty E. For an unsafe normal ℓ, δ_C(0)=1 implies δ_D(0)=0. If δ_D(ℓ)>0 then a zero-excess outside subfamily must span ℓ. Its union with the excess-one child subfamily spanning ℓ has excess at least two, impossible. Thus δ_D(ℓ)=0 and every ℓ value extends through the outside, so any F equal to a line on that normal meets E too. In both cases deleting C preserves the actual decision against its admitted outside, though it need not preserve the boundary relation. This distinction is essential.

## Basic pieces and decomposition

A graphic augmented normal matroid of deficiency at most one is solved by matching clauses to distinct vertices then testing for a balanced signed directed circuit in each relevant sink component. A cographic augmented normal matroid is solved by matching across a laminar family of triads at arbitrary deficiency. A minor of R10 with parallel extensions has bounded rank and can be evaluated by enumeration. [Graphic proof](../cographic-pairs/README.md) · [Cographic proof](../cographic-pairs/cographic-normals.md)

Use the polynomial-time conflict-tree form of regular matroid decomposition. Nodes are basic matroids of the three types, and adjacent bags meet on 0, 1, or 3 virtual labels. A triple is a binary triangle in each partner. Adjacent sums can be performed in any order. [Fomin et al., Theorem 2](https://sites.cs.ucsb.edu/~daniello/papers/spanMAT17.pdf) · [Dinitz and Kortsarz](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.5146)

To see how to use this statement for constraints, represent each bag over F₂ and start from the direct sum of the bag vector spaces. Impose equality for copies of each shared virtual column, then restrict the displayed ground set to real columns. This realizes exactly the matroid sum: a dependence on real columns holds precisely when within each adjacent summand its selected columns sum to a common linear combination of the shared columns, as in the binary symmetric-difference definition of extended sums. Equivalently, the coefficient of a shared column can be chosen in both summands while no virtual labels remain in the output dependence.

Extended sums may introduce unequal dependence relations on a virtual boundary. Normalize them before processing. When a combination of the shared columns vanishes on one side but is nonzero on the other, contract that latter vector in its bag. It was already zero in the global quotient, so this does not alter the assembled column configuration. Every nonzero combination of at most one point or of a binary triangle is itself a virtual column, also after earlier contractions. Contraction thus leaves each basic bag a minor of its original class. Repeat until the two boundary kernels agree on every edge. Polynomially many contractions suffice since each strictly decreases the sum of bag ranks. The injections of matching boundary spans into both sides are now honest embeddings. By inducting over the tree, each bag embeds in the glued space and the spans of adjacent subtrees meet precisely on their common virtual boundary. Real ground vectors may not span all these ambient coordinates; this is harmless since a linear functional on a subspace always extends. Original and assembled real columns have identical binary dependencies; compute the coordinate identification on their spans by Gaussian elimination.

Assign each unary constraint to its true column's bag. For a rank-two flat the three real columns form a binary triangle. Assign it to the median node of the three owner bags in the tree, allowing coincident owners. Each column whose owner is not the median is isolated in its own branch as seen from the median; the other two already sum to that column, so it belongs to the interface of this branch and is represented by a single virtual element at the median. Thus all three normals and their constants are available in one basic bag. Replacement clauses on an exposed edge use only its existing virtual directions at the neighbor. Repeatedly choose a leaf, apply the decision replacement above, and delete the leaf. The invariant is global deficiency at most one on the new clausal system and decision equivalence to the original system. Unused bag columns are retained or deleted when invoking the basic solvers, as appropriate. Each of polynomially many leaf steps makes a constant number of polynomial-time basic decisions and matroid-intersection calls. The last bag is basic. This gives a deterministic polynomial DECISION algorithm for the stated regular class.

For original cyclic singleton guards with one affine marker, a witness can be obtained without reversing a relaxed boundary deletion. Greedily try setting an active pivot variable y_i to zero, thereby discarding its guard. After each trial the residual remains a native one-marker instance. Whenever the guard is nontrivial its restricted pivot form is a member of the current augmented normal matroid or zero, so restricting y_i contracts a represented element or creates no linear change. All remaining augmented normals are restrictions of old ones; regularity is minor-closed. Skip tautological guards, which stay taut. Keep zero assignments on successful trials; after all tests every remaining nontrivial pivot is necessarily one, and its guard becomes a linear equation. Linear algebra completes a witness. This last argument applies specifically to native pivot guards.

## Removing nonessential clauses simplifies the decision proof

If the current global deficiency is zero, choose independent true representatives by linear algebra and accept. Otherwise prune clauses while the deficiency remains one, obtaining an inclusion-minimal deficient subfamily I. Its full-set excess is one: a proper subfamily attaining that excess would contradict minimality. Every outside clause extends above any fixed assignment on the span of I. Indeed for any outside J the global excess bound applied to I∪J gives dim(span(J) modulo span(I))≥|J|. Rado then supplies independent hidden representatives for all outside clauses and their true values can be simultaneously imposed. Thus solving I suffices, with constructive extension. This pruning uses polynomially many matroid intersection calls.

Repeat the pruning whenever convenient, including before every leaf step. Every proper subfamily of the pruned core has unconditioned deficiency zero. For a proper leaf child C, every one-direction conditional query has deficiency at most one, and δ_C(K)≤k. If δ_C(K)≤1 compute F exactly as before. If δ_C(K)=2 and k=2, all three line queries are safe: when they identify a singleton or line, replace exactly; otherwise |F|≥3. In that case the exterior extendible set is empty or has at least two points by the budget inequality and the chart bound, so the child can be dropped for decision. If all current clauses are in one basic bag, solve there; if none are in a selected leaf, remove it without affecting the outside. This proof does not require unsafe-direction bookkeeping.

Witness search also extends beyond natural native inputs. For a general deficiency-one flat system obtain independent normal representatives for all but one clause. Complete their signed falsity indicators to an affine coordinate system; each represented clause now says its corresponding pivot may be one only when its one remaining affine literal is true (or never, for a unit clause). Any normal in the full space of a flat can be chosen for its literal in such a basis; affine constants enforce the original forbidden pattern. Give free coordinates tautological guards. Branch on a true literal of the one unmatched clause, which gives at most two ordinary native one-marker searches. Its marker normal is a represented element, so the subsequent augmented normal matroid on that slice is a minor of the original regular matroid. This yields a polynomial search algorithm by the native deletion argument above.

## Additional nonregular basic pieces

The leaf argument works on any supplied conflict tree satisfying the same binary vector gluing and full virtual-interface hypotheses, provided that every leaf minor requested by the procedure has a suitable solver. In particular, nonregular pieces of rank at most w can be searched in 2^w assignments. Pieces of corank at most w admit a 4^w search at any deficiency: all distinct constrained triangle-incidence vectors are dependencies in a space of dimension w. Choose s≤w independent such vectors. Their supports cover every constrained triangle, on at most 3s ground elements. Those normals have rank at most 3s−s=2s because the s independent dependencies remain supported there. Enumerate their possible evaluations, check all rank-two clauses, and solve the remaining unary requirements by linear algebra. Contraction/deletion and simplification preserve the rank or corank upper bound as appropriate. This yields polynomial times 4^w on such trees, without asserting a universal bounded-w decomposition.

## Transfer to higher deficiency

As a conditional oracle reduction, the pruned-core argument works at any positive maximum deficiency d. Select an inclusion-minimal family whose maximum deficiency is d. Its own full-set excess equals d and its outside extends universally by the same rank proof. In a proper leaf, δ_C(0)≤d−1. Consequently every one-line probe costs at most d. If δ_C(K)≤d, conditioning on each state uses basic-piece queries at most d. The only remaining case has dim K=2, δ_C(K)=d+1, which by the sum bound forces δ_D(K)≤1. The three line probes on C either recover a singleton or line exactly, or certify at least three states, which intersects every nonempty outside relation. Thus higher-deficiency regular decision reduces with polynomial overhead to the same deficiency on the graphic basic pieces. No polynomial-time algorithm for graphic deficiency two or higher is asserted.

## Nongeneric nature of regularity

On the five-vertex tree with edges 01,03,04,12, require an odd set inducing odd undirected degree at every selected vertex. Handshake proves rejection. Its odd-marker slice has pivot masks (1,2,4,8,15) on a four-dimensional binary normal space and neighbor masks (5,5,2,1,1). All five forbidden flats have codimension two, and every four pivot normals are independent. The augmented normal ground set is {1,2,4,5,6,7,8,9,14,15}. Contract normal 2 and remove repeats; the seven distinct remaining nonzero vectors in a three-dimensional space form the Fano matroid. Thus regularity need not hold even on a connected symmetric tree and a minimal Rado-deficient core.

A nonsymmetric five-vertex regression instance has incoming-neighbor bitmasks (14,12,25,5,9) with all marks present and all targets equal to odd degree. Direct enumeration rejects. It is strongly connected and remains without proper nontrivial directed modules under all 32 row switches. Its augmented normals in the same slice coordinates are {1,2,4,5,6,8,9,12,13,14,15}; contracting 2 again gives a Fano restriction after simplification.

One obstruction to a direct graphic higher-deficiency algorithm is the signed NAE Hall shortfall. On the nine points of AG(2,3), take all horizontal, vertical, and slope-one lines, plus one slope-two line, as ten NAE triples. The first nine have a 3-regular bipartite incidence graph with the points and hence a perfect matching. Shortfall is exactly one. No distinct lines share two points. Deleting any one clause and choosing a satisfying signed inequality between two variables of that clause identifies these variables but leaves every other clause a genuine triple on three distinct variables. All nine remaining clauses now use eight variables, so the branch still lacks a full pivot matching. Branching on the unmatched clause alone does not restore the hypothesis of the directed even-circuit solver.
