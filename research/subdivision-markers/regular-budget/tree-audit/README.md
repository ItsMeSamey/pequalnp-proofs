# Auditing tree pushouts and median localization for binary normal matroids

Fomin et al., Theorem 2 in the linked conference version, give a polynomially constructible tree of graphic, cographic, and deletion/parallel-extension R₁₀ bags. The intersections have size 0,1,3; triples remain circuits in both incident basic bags. Extended sums allow loops/coloops at singleton interfaces and cocircuits in triple interfaces. This is the version of the decomposition used here. [Fomin et al., Spanning Circuits in Regular Matroids](https://sites.cs.ucsb.edu/~daniello/papers/spanMAT17.pdf) · [Dinitz and Kortsarz](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.5146)

For each node t take the binary vector space V_t spanned by its local columns. For each edge e=tu and shared label j let a_tj,a_uj be its adjacent copies. Set Q equal to the direct sum of all V_t modulo the span of (a_tj,a_uj), supported on their two bags. Original columns map naturally to Q. Their labeled dependence space is precisely the cycle space of the assembled matroid: a real subset sums to zero in Q exactly when there is a binary coefficient for every shared label so that adding the chosen shared elements to the real elements in every bag gives a local zero sum. This is the cycle-matching definition of each binary sum, iterated along the tree. Disjointness of differently labeled interfaces and unique ownership of real elements follow from the conflict tree and the binary sum construction. Deletion of the virtual labels from the final ground set retains exactly the real-column dependencies. This argument accommodates a loop at only one side of an interface.

**Ambient space caveat.** Q need not be generated by surviving real columns. For example, two single-element coloop bags glued on their sole virtual element give empty ground set and a one-dimensional pushout. One can add private coloops to avoid empty real ground set and retain the same extra dimension. If an input representation of the real columns is given, identify its real span with their span in Q via a basis and the labeled binary dependence space. A covector on the real span extends to Q; restriction is the converse. Algorithms involving dual assignments can work in Q. Arbitrarily projecting all bag vectors back into the real span can add dependencies within a bag and requires its own justification.

**Image of a bag is a minor.** Fix t. Every local relation x∈V_t killed in Q is a sum of edge generators. Split those edge terms by the branches of T−t, assigning each incident edge to its own branch. In each branch all coordinates other than t sum to zero. Hence its contribution at t is itself killed in Q. This contribution belongs to the span of the local columns on its incident edge. Every nonzero element of that span is a named virtual column: the claim is direct for a singleton and holds for a binary triangle since its three elements exhaust the nonzero vectors in its plane. It follows that the whole local kernel is spanned by those local virtual columns killed in Q. The converse inclusion is immediate. Contract a basis of killed virtual columns and suppress unwanted zero columns to represent the surviving bag image. Contractions preserve graphic/cographic types; the third type stays of rank at most five even with parallel extensions and contractions. This argument permits cascading kills from multiple levels and does not assume initial image injectivity. A cocircuit in a triangle interface alone need not collapse any direction.

**Median of a labeled three-circuit.** Choose three original columns with distinct nonzero images summing to zero, and let t be the median of their owner bags, allowing coincidences. Any branch at t containing an off-bag member of the circuit contains only that member, by the median property. Represent the three-column relation by edge generators and sum the corresponding equations on this branch. Internal generators disappear in Q, and the isolated real column equals a combination of the labels on its incident edge toward t. Its nonzero image is therefore the image of a single virtual label in t, by the triangle-plane observation. On-bag members keep their original representatives. As the contracted bag embeds in Q, the resulting three columns constitute a true three-circuit there. Parallel pairs and loops require separate handling if the normal specification accepts them as degenerate tuples. Simultaneous co-location of arbitrarily many columns does not follow from this result.

The finite checker compares the quotient with direct virtual-coefficient matching on every subset of real elements, tests the claimed kernel description on every bag vector, and locates every genuine real triangle. Across 600 seeded binary trees it verified 61 median triangles and 244 killed local dimensions. The randomly generated two/three-dimensional local spaces exercise the linear lemma independently of regularity, while the cited structural theorem is what supplies the basic-type tree for actual regular input.
