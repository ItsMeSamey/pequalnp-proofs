# Pinning a physical line witness

Let U be the named space, H the requested plane, and F a physical column set with span(F) intersect U equal to a line h in H. Contracting F gives an exact three-dimensional named query for extensions of F. A negative answer can lose the original target.

Take U to be the low four bits and H={0,1,6,7}. Start with the nine physical points R={1,2,4,8,3,12,5,10,15}, a representation of the bond matroid of K3,3. Indeed binary flows of the 3 by 3 bipartite graph are parametrized by the upper-left 2 by 2 entries, and the remaining entries are their row sums, column sums and grand sum. The ordinary ground consists of R and the four additional columns 16,17,20,18. Use F={16,17} for the line 1 and singleton columns for the other eight obstruction lines. F is independent and inclusion-minimal. The original target is attained by {1,20,18}. In any solution extending F, no point of R outside H may be used; also 20 and 18 are impossible because 16 exposes 4 or 2. The remaining columns cannot span 6. Thus a nine-line minimal obstruction, even with an actual physical restriction of the specified isomorphism type, does not justify this pinning choice.

A safe test uses the actual exterior. Put V=span(U,E minus F) and L=span(F). If L intersect V is contained in U, then L intersect V=h. For a witness Y put Y0=Y minus F. Adding F produces named trace (span(Y0) intersect U)+h: write a named vector as y0+f, which forces f into L intersect V. This preserves H since h lies in H and Y0 was part of an H-witness. Hence one exact rank-three query now decides the original plane. Before testing, one may delete all columns whose singleton named trace falls outside H, and any other sites already certified absent in every solution. The proof uses the exterior after those deletions. If a solution is found, lift it by adding F and recording the original name-coordinate quotient.
