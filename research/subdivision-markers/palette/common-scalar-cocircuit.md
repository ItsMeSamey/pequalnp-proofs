# Two scalar residual conditions on the same private basis

Fix candidate extras e,f. Let the other columns be (q_j,l_j) in Q plus U, and require the q_j to span Q. A private basis P determines the linear map s_P:Q→U interpolating its column labels. For arbitrary λ in the dual of U, private vectors x,y and prescribed bits α,β, the following polynomial test constructs P with λs_P(x)=α and λs_P(y)=β, or proves none exists.

In Q plus the binary field form baseline columns (q_j,λl_j) and append the three indexed probes (0,1), (x,1+α), (y,1+β). The represented augmented matroid has ambient rank dim(Q)+1. A cocircuit containing all three probes is equivalent to a hyperplane spanned by physical baseline columns and avoiding each probe. The normal functional equals (φ,1) because it is nonzero on (0,1); its other two evaluations impose φ(x)=α and φ(y)=β. The baseline zeros span the hyperplane by cocircuit minimality. Projection to Q restricts to an isomorphism of the hyperplane, so extract a private basis of zero columns. Conversely the graph of λs_P is spanned by the lifted P and realizes such a cocircuit. Distinct parallel probe indices are permitted; a loop probe correctly rejects. Apply the represented binary three-element circuit algorithm to the dual, recovering a circuit by deletion self-reduction in that dual. Recover physical P from the primal hyperplane complement of the resulting cocircuit. Computing a dual for self-reduction does not authorize deleting arbitrary baseline columns in the primal.

For a fixed prescribed full tableau, a common scalar can identify its two entries whenever it distinguishes that tableau from every other remaining possibility; this need not hold in unrestricted inputs. In particular, suppose each of s_P(q_e), s_P(q_f) lies in a known affine subspace of dimension at most one. Reject target entries outside those subspaces. Choose a linear λ taking value 1 on each nonzero direction (consistent whether they agree or differ). This λ distinguishes both individual entries, so the single test decides the two exact evaluations together. The affine hulls can be computed: for each λ and each bit test that one prescribed evaluation by duplicating the condition; these scalar ranges give all constant linear forms and their values. This covers the common-direction case where translated determinant nonemptiness alone loses information.

In the fixed-extra paired-mask-identity controls, choose λ(u)=1, λ(v)=0, λ(h1)=λ(h2)=0. Requiring λs_P(A)=λs_P(B)=1 hits exactly the positive input; for these particular baselines all extra tableaux involving v have first entry v. This last distinction does not require the full two column hulls to be lines. The test decides simultaneous scalar equations on a basis, not arbitrary two-vector equations.
