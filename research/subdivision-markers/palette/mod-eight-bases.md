# Modulo-eight binary bases and three-dimensional synchronized minors

Let A be an r by n full-row-rank binary matrix with its zero-one integer lift. For r=0 or 1 the basis polynomial is immediate, so take r≥2. For physical weights in the integers modulo eight, the Gram determinant sums the squares of the integer r by r minors with physical subset weights. It gives the correct weighted basis sum mod four. Mod eight a squared minor is one if odd, zero if divisible by four, and four if twice an odd integer.

For an r-set S write D for its binary determinant. For two distinct indices k,j of S set R=S minus those indices, and for a row i let B=det(R,A_k,e_i) and C=det(R,A_j,e_i) over the binary field, where e_i is a coordinate unit. The mod-eight Gram error coefficient divided by four, as a single binary bit, is

    (1+D) sum_{i,k<j in S} A_ik A_ij B C.

For full binary rank the leading factor kills the sum. At corank at least two every cofactor vanishes and the integer determinant is divisible by four (use Smith form, or reduce by unimodular column operations to two even columns). At corank one choose the unique nonzero right and left null vectors c and a. The binary adjugate is c aᵀ. Choose any index t with c_t=1 and add the other indicated columns into column t, making that column equal to the even integer vector A_S c. Dividing this column by two and expanding gives det(A_S)/2 equal modulo two to a·(A_S c/2). In each row the half of its even zero-one summation has parity equal to the sum of products over pairs of selected ones. Since a_i²=a_i, each resulting term a_i c_k c_j is exactly the product of the cofactors B,C. This proves the identity without summing signed terms over different physical bases.

Group the expression by i,k,j. On material excluding k,j use weights reduced modulo two, multiply the answer by the two selected weights, and query the coefficient sum of BC plus DBC over (r−2)-subsets R. The probe vectors A_k,A_j,e_i of all three factors lie in a common space of dimension at most three. BC can use ordinary Cauchy–Binet or duplicate one factor. Correct the original Gram by four times this characteristic-two answer. There are O(r n²) calls and polynomially many other arithmetic operations. The entire construction also works for weights in a fixed explicitly represented binary extension: lift weights to the degree-m monic quotient of (Z/8)[t] lifting the field's defining irreducible polynomial, reduce the error calculation mod 2 and then multiply its lift by 4. No integer basis counts are needed.

Conversely the product of three binary bits is one quarter of the alternating sum of their seven nonempty subset XORs (positive singles, negative pairs, positive triple). For three appended-pair forms in a common three-space, every such XOR is another decomposable bivector or zero. Each nonzero form is a binary basis polynomial in the quotient by its two spanning vectors, evaluated with the same weights; return zero if that ordinary quotient does not span the required rank. Compute the alternating sum over the lifted mod-eight ring, and divide its even coefficient representatives by four to obtain the desired field value. The division is valid because the identity is coefficientwise over the integers before specializing. Accordingly the two *evaluation problems* reduce to one another in polynomial time, including extension weights when the modulo-eight oracle supports that coefficient ring. For scalar binary weights the modulus is simply eight. This equivalence alone supplies no general polynomial evaluator for either side.
