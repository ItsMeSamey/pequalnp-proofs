## A fully tagged basis suffices without triangle generation

Assume directions with every two-bit tag available span all observed K directions. Detect this by grouping columns and Gaussian elimination. Choose a fully tagged direction basis u₁,…,u_d and an actual representative (u_i,b,h_i(b)) at each tag. Each bit of h_i is a function on two bits, hence has multilinear Boolean degree at most two. The 2d bits g(u_i) specify a chart.

Write each physical e=(k_e,b_e,h_e) with k_e=Σ c_ei u_i. Its activation is the linear equation g(k_e)=b_e. Its two-bit residue is R_e(g)=h_e+Σ_i c_ei h_i(g(u_i)). For k=0 this correctly activates precisely pure H columns and preserves their residues. At a fixed chart subtracting the relevant activated basis representatives from an active e produces R_e(g) in H. These residues span the entire intersection with H of the active physical span: after these elementary subtractions its remaining canonical basis projects independently to K. Every exact witness lies in some chart, and every active span has named intersection contained in H. Thus success is equivalent to two indexed records e,f being jointly active with det(R_e(g),R_f(g))=1, including repeated buckets.

For each of O(n²) physical pairs solve its linear tag equations in the 2d chart bits. On an affine parametrization of the consistent solutions the residual determinant has Boolean degree at most four. It is nonzero somewhere iff it is nonzero on an assignment of Hamming weight at most four in the free coordinates: each nonzero ANF coefficient is the XOR of the evaluations on subsets of its monomial support. Enumerate those O((2d+1)^4) assignments and evaluate directly at reconstructed charts. Runtime O(n²(d+1)^4 poly(input)) includes construction, recognition, and physical witness recovery. Return the found chart's needed basis representatives plus e,f, at most d+2 sites. Extend g arbitrarily beyond the observed K span if necessary.

This covers arbitrary sparse supports under a fully tagged basis. It subsumes D+D for decision; the preceding triangle rule still guarantees a shorter witness. A fully tagged coordinate basis plus an extra direction e₁+e₂+e₃ with just one tag and no other directions passes this new test but fails D+D. For a partial-tag basis, the physical representative of u_i may be unavailable in the witnessing chart, so its h_i lookup is not justified on that branch.

### Bounded deficit and a fixed-basis failure

Let t=rank(observed K)−rank(fully tagged directions). Choose a fully tagged basis for their span D and enumerate t indexed physical complement columns independent modulo D. On each such choice impose their individual tag constraints; their gains are constants while the D-anchor gains remain quadratic lookups. Apply the degree-four pair test on that common affine slice. To see completeness, take an exact witness, extend its chart to its projected span plus D, and add compatible canonical D anchors. Choose a basis of its original directions modulo D. Augment that list with additional physical columns independent modulo D plus the directions already chosen, specifying each new actual tag by extending the chart. This reaches a whole observed K basis since the physical projections span it. The resulting active columns still realize H. Some enumerated t-tuple therefore succeeds. This gives deterministic O(n^(t+2)(d+1)^4 poly(input)), polynomial at each fixed t; the exponent depends on t. No fixed-parameter bound follows.

A fixed partially tagged basis can fail to extend an exact witness. On K=F₂⁴ use coordinate anchors 1,2,4,8 with only tag zero and H gain zero. Add points on two disjoint lines (3,5,6) and (7,9,14), giving each successive direction tags 1,2,3 and gains 0,0,h_i on line i. The tags on the two lines extend to a common chart because the lines span complementary spaces. Their two physical triple residues span H. The chart activating all four anchored basis records is identically zero, and cannot activate either positive triple. Distinct directions make the example bucket-simple. This example was supplied by binary_modular_bases. The weaker solver for a chart compatible with a supplied partial basis alone does not decide this instance. Finite regression pnp-chart-basis-check.py compares the fully tagged-basis algorithm to direct physical-span truth, including repeated buckets, and checks each returned witness.
