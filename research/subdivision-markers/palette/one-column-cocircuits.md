# One aligned column replacement and prescribed binary cocircuits

Suppose two rank-r represented matroids agree on a common indexed binary matrix E in verified coordinates, including any supplied compatible row and nonzero column scalings. They differ only at one distinguished column. Its value in M is binary a, and in N is g over GF(16). Assume E spans the binary ambient r-space. A basis of M failing in N necessarily uses this column. Its other r−1 columns from E span a physical binary hyperplane P with a outside P. They extend to a dependent N-set exactly if g lies in its scalar extension.

Write g in any binary field basis of GF(16), producing four binary column vectors, and let w1,...,wd be a basis of their binary span. Membership of g in the extended P is equivalent to membership of every wi in P. If a is in their span no separating basis exists. Otherwise append probes a,a+w1,...,a+wd to the clean binary representation. A common cocircuit through these d+1 indexed columns exists exactly when its complementary physically spanned hyperplane separates a from all the wi in the required manner. This follows by applying its binary functional to each probe. All spanning elements of the complementary flat are clean because every added probe is in the cocircuit. A basis can be read off immediately. For d≤2 this is deterministic polynomial using the binary three-circuit algorithm and duality, including smaller sets of probes by parallel copies.

Conversely take t≤5 prescribed binary cocircuit elements p1,...,pt with all other physical columns E. If E has rank below r−1 no complementary physical hyperplane exists; if it has rank r−1 test its unique span directly. For full-rank E set a=p1 and g equal to the sum of the t−1 differences p_i+p1 (i>1), each multiplied by a distinct member of a binary basis of GF(16). The same hyperplane calculation identifies a common cocircuit through all original probes with a basis difference for these two enlarged matrices. This also covers loops and repeated column types through the unmodified hyperplane condition. Hence single arbitrarily changed GF(16) column comparisons contain the binary fixed-four and fixed-five cocircuit problems, while the slice-rank-two subcase is tractable. This equivalence does not require, and does not prove, realizability of arbitrary replacements by the special equality-spanning perturbation.
