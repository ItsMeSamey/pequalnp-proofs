# Fixed-extra determinant probes and an exact coefficient identity

Take four independent names U with basis h1,h2,u,v, the requested H=span(h1,h2), and private points A,B. A negative input has baseline ordinary points A,B,A+B+u,B+u,A+v. A positive input adds just A+u. Both have marked ordinary extras e=A+h1+u and f=B+h2+u. The actual rank spans all U. The private quotient is connected: the directions A,B,A+B form a triangle and every other projected column is parallel to one of its members.

A solution must use both extras since other columns cannot generate either of the respective h1 and h2 bits. The baseline subset in a solution must span both private directions to cancel their projections independently, and has zero named trace since all its named coordinates lie in span(u,v), disjoint from H. Choose a private basis from it. In a private basis P the map s from private coordinates to the baseline's named parts is linear. Success is exactly s(A)=s(B)=u. The negative input realizes (0,0),(u,0),(0,u) and lacks (u,u). The positive input has just one new evaluation pair, (u,u), since a new basis uses A+u with either B, B+u or A+B+u. Every predicate det(K+L[s(A),s(B)]) over the binary field is affine on the rectangle of evaluations [u i,u j] for binary i,j. The product coefficient cancels because the perturbation matrix has rank at most one. An affine bit positive at (1,1) is positive on at least one of the other three points. Thus all positive-determinant existence queries agree for these fixed extras, including arbitrary affine shifts and either determinant value. The negative claim here concerns this defined query family. Other extras or physical deletions may distinguish the inputs.

To make the original ordinary restriction nonregular, add all seven nonzero pure private points in span(A,C,D), where C,D are fresh private directions and the existing A is shared. The enlarged private quotient remains connected. For any full baseline basis split its original and attached columns. Projected span from the attachment meets the old private space only at zero or at the line A. If it contains A, it contributes s(A)=0 which can be supplied by the old physical A. Thus the old constraints together with that optional A span the original private space and admit an old baseline basis giving the same evaluation. Conversely any old basis extends by C,D. The exact H traces retain their characterization, because a solution's baseline span has zero named trace and must contain physical lifts of A,B (it can be completed by independent private columns). The physically present Fano restriction proves nonregularity.

For the general exact symbolic test, fix extras e,f and a prescribed ordered basis of H with vectors t1,t2. Let Q be a full-row-rank private coordinate matrix of rank r, and l_i the four named-coordinate rows in an arbitrary chart. For an r-element ordinary subset P disjoint from the extras, put a(P)=det Q_P. For each of the eight coordinates form the determinant D_ij(P) of Q_P bordered above-right by the private column of extra j, below-left by the row l_i(P) and below-right by l_i(j)+(t_j)_i. If a(P)=1 this is precisely the discrepancy bit of residual j by Schur complementation over the binary field. Hence the formal polynomial

    sum_P x^P a(P) product_{i,j} (1+D_ij(P))

has coefficient one exactly on the witnessing bases for this ordered pair. Distinct indexed bases have different monomials; there is no orientation sign or parity cancellation among bases. A solution can be normalized to this form for some extras and ordered target basis by extending its private basis to full rank with independent projections, then choosing two independent residuals. Test the polynomials separately or give the pair and target order distinct tags to keep their coefficients separate. This exact identity specifies coefficients and does not give a polynomial-time evaluation circuit. Random evaluation over an extension field requires an efficient evaluator before it can be used for a randomized algorithm. The determinant mask above excludes a particular common-basis probing method; it establishes no lower bound for general identity testing.
