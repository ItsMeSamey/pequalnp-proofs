# Completing the coloop case for binary representability after one deletion

Use the special GF(16) comparison for binary equality-spanning with two independent pin vectors and two output bits. The clean deletion case of full physical rank was treated in one-deletion-disconnected.md. If the deleted physical point e is a coloop of M, choose a basis containing e and shift all labels and both pin targets by its interpolant. The basis itself returns a YES if it matches the pins. Otherwise it is a common basis, the normalized N update is A_j+w g_j/p, and e is the basis unit in the last row. All other physical columns have zero in that last row. Since e must match (its shifted label zero), drop this coordinate from the pins and seek a full equality graph on the clean physical domain, with the same shifted target bits. Test consistency if the remaining pin vectors are dependent. Adding e back recovers a physical full basis. This step uses no change of the two protected output coordinates.

If the last coordinate w_e is zero then it vanishes in every clean N column as well. The comparison after contracting e on both sides is the clean binary comparison since e is also a coloop of N. Otherwise, in N without e its last row is a nonzero multiple of the GF(4) gain row g. By subtracting multiples of that row from the others, N without e is represented by [A;g], where A denotes the original binary clean columns in their clean basis. Its binary matroid assumption implies diagonal scalability of any full-rank normalized chart. If g vanishes everywhere the equality problem has only the zero shifted map. If its entries lie in one binary output line use the scalar three-probe algorithm.

Pick some clean column j0 of nonzero gain. Divide gains and target bits (regarded as GF(4)) by that gain. Now j0 has gain 1; let t=A_j0. Normalize on the clean zero-gain basis units and j0. The quaternary representation of N without e has columns

    (A_j+t h_j, h_j),   h_j in GF(4).

Here j0 becomes the bottom unit. Retain j0 as physical material in all subsequent binary-lift calculations. Choose a binary scaling with row factors in GF(4)* and bottom factor 1, possible by propagation along the support graph since all entries lie in GF(4). Choose arbitrary GF(4) roots in disconnected components. Each h≠0 column consequently has column factor 1/h. If t_i=0, any nonzero physical A_ij on such a column requires row factor s_i=h. If t_i=1 and h=ω (ω²=ω+1), the only choices are A_ij=0 with s_i=1 or A_ij=1 with s_i=ω². For h=ω² interchange ω and ω². For h=1, any t_i=1 row with s_i≠1 must have A_ij=1. These assertions follow directly by requiring s_i(A_ij+t_i h)/h to be zero or one. Zero-gain physical columns are local to one row-factor group.

If all three nonzero gains occur, the two nonunit gains force all rows of t into group 1. Every gain-h physical column is then confined to its own row group V_h; this includes j0. In the binary output lift the material in that sector has output confined to the line through h. Let K be the space generated by the two projected pin portions in each sector and the common two output axes. The sector meets K in dimension at most three, and sector spans are independent modulo K. Sum their exact rank-three local trace tables and test for the specified pin graph.

If just two gains occur rename the nonunit one ω (or interchange the field conjugates). Rows of t lie only in V1 and V_{ω²}. Let t* be the portion of t in V_{ω²}. Each physical gain-1 or gain-ω column has, outside its own group, exactly t*. Group the local zero-gain columns with the gain-h columns for h=1,ω; leave the V_{ω²} zero-gain columns in their own block. Use a central space K generated by the two local pin portions in each group, t* and the two output axes. Each gain block's intersection with K lies within its two local pin directions plus the single lifted vector (t*,h); the other block lies in its two local pins plus t*. Private directions are independent modulo K. Rank-three local palettes therefore recover the exact global traces by subspace sum. Testing the exact graph, extending with independent clean projections and restoring e yields the original physical matching basis. Membership checks and search for a suitable deleted index are polynomial.

Together with the spanning-deletion case this handles any index whose deletion makes the special N binary. It is a result about this equality-spanning representation and does not extend by this proof to an arbitrary pair of binary and quaternary matroids.
