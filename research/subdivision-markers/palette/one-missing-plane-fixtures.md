## A round 15-column exact-plane NO with one missing plane

In V=F₂⁵ use bitmasks, U=span(1,2,4,8), H=span(1,2), B=span(4,8). Ordinary columns are the nine h+b for h and b nonzero in H and B, followed by 16,17,20,22,24,27. The outside columns form three pairs in distinct cosets modulo H with pair differences 1,2,3. Consequently each target line occurs. Any exact H witness must lie in H plus at most one outside coset of H, since two distinct such cosets give a difference in U outside H. It cannot include an ordinary column in U, as none is in H. Each outside coset has only two points, so its span meets U in at most a line. This proves NO by direct linear algebra.

The nine points form a round restriction in U. The complement H∪B contains no affine plane avoiding zero: such a plane would consist of four distinct nonzero vectors with sum zero, which cannot occur in the two disjoint direct summands of just three nonzero vectors each. The union of two distinct hyperplanes of U omits an affine plane avoiding zero, and a repeated hyperplane covers still fewer points. Thus if two hyperplanes of V covered the configuration at least one must contain U, hence equal U. But the six exterior points span V: their projections give three independent directions in V/H and their differences span H. This proves roundness (no partition into two nonspanning restrictions), a precise irreducibility condition. It rules out a nontrivial rank-one seam on two or more distinct simple points on both sides. It permits larger-boundary and non-separation reductions.

Deterministic certificate recipe: for each of the 455 three-element subsets expand its ≤8 XOR sums, keep those with eight distinct sums and exactly four sums in U, and record the sorted four sums. Their union as labelled subspaces has size 34, missing just H out of 35. Full-sized rank-three witnesses suffice by projection-basis extension. Roundness alternatively follows by checking every one of 465 unordered pairs of distinct nonzero functionals has an ordinary point where both evaluate to one. Repeating this check after deleting each of the nine points in U loses roundness. Deleting either point of any exterior pair loses its corresponding target line by the same coset argument. Thus the displayed configuration is deletion-minimal for the combination of roundness and individual target-line attainability. This is not an absolute size lower bound.

The rank-four 12-column round control V minus H in proofsys2026/palette/ggw-plane.md and graphic-star example in port-four-graphic-spectra.md already realize 34 of 35 planes. The rank-five construction adds simultaneous success in *every proper named quotient*. For z∈H nonzero, choose a separately attained target line different from span(z); after quotient by z it projects to H/span(z). For z∈U outside H, the three-space H+span(z) contains additional planes T complementary to span(z), all attained because H alone is missing. Any such T has the same image as H modulo z. For every physical S and Z≤U, the quotient trace is (span(S)∩U+Z)/Z, by the modular law. Any nonzero Z contains some z, so further quotienting proves success for every nonzero Z≤U, while the original is NO. The witness can depend on Z. The older rank-four example fails the contractions along directions inside its omitted H. This result concerns quotient probes retaining all original physical columns; it does not preclude tests requiring consistency of witnesses.

Ambient rank five is minimal for that quotient-obstruction property with four independent names. At rank four the entire ambient space is U. A projected witness modulo a nonzero z∈H with target H/span(z) must use a physical point in H outside span(z), since every contributing column must project into H/span(z) and at least one must project nontrivially. Success for all three z∈H forces two distinct physical H points. They span H already. This rank lower bound does not assert a minimum number of columns at rank five.

The prior same-bucket pin-and-delete solver *does decide this fixture*: each of the three outside pairs yields a line pin whose three-name extension is impossible, so each bucket can be deleted. Hence this example separates independent proper named-quotient feasibility from compatible physical witnesses, without resisting every positive or negative solver. In the 155 rank-three ambient flats the ordinary occupancy histogram for 0 through 6 points is (1,6,30,45,45,24,4); no seven-point flat occurs. In conjunction with roundness and protected rank four, this also blocks the specific ≥7-column protected rank-three shore shrink.

## A bucket-simple rank-six NO with exactly one absent trace

Keep U and H as above and take the rank-six instance E=(5,9,14,18,20,24,29,32,39,42,44,48,54,56,63). The fifteen quotients modulo H are exactly the fifteen nonzero vectors in V/H. If H were realized, extend a witnessing subset to a physical span of dimension four by adding columns independent modulo U and its current span. Its image in V/H would be a two-space and therefore contain only three nonzero vectors. Each has at most one ordinary representative and no representative of zero is present. Three physical columns cannot span that four-space. Thus NO holds without relying on any positive-certificate search.

For this small fixture the following complete check has 32768 subset states. Start with S(0)={0}. If i is any set bit of m, put S(m)=S(m without i) ∪ (E_i+S(m without i)). Encode each span by a 64-bit mask. The 67 subspaces of U are obtained by the same span-expansion applied to the fifteen nonzero four-bit vectors. The family of S(m)∩U has 66 members: exactly H is missing. Consequently every proper named quotient has a witness, as in the rank-five proof. (The rank-five fixture also yields 66 traces.) Every singleton target line, plane other than H, and named hyperplane is attainable. Yet the equal-bucket pair pin is never applicable, since no two physical columns coincide modulo H.

For any m with at least seven elements let j be dim S(m)+dim(S(complement m)+U)−6. The numbers of such m at j=4,5,6 are respectively 67,3792,18960, exhausting all of them; all other j have count zero. Minimum ordinary connectivity among cuts with both sides of size at least i for i=1,…,7 is (1,2,2,2,3,3,3). Thus this small input defeats the specific large protected rank-three shore reduction, has no nontrivial physical rank-one seam, and passes all proper named-quotient probes without a common witness. The direct bucket-capacity argument above decides it. No complexity lower bound follows for solvers combining physical columns and named coordinates.
