# Target saturation and short circuits

Let U have dimension four, let H be the target plane and let q project by H. If Y realizes H, every ordinary column whose q-image is in span(qY) already belongs to span(Y). Thus a solution can be saturated to a flat of q(E), preserving its entire original named trace.

Discard ordinary columns in U outside H and redundant literal parallel copies and loops. A nonzero ordinary member of H can be pinned immediately. For columns outside U, partition by their q-images. If a bucket contains different actual points e,f, then e+f is a nonzero element of H, and span(e,f) intersects U exactly in that line. Ask the three-name exact extension query after contracting e and f, with the names and H mapped through that contraction. A positive answer lifts by adding the pinned columns. After a negative answer delete the entire bucket, because using any one member would allow saturation to include the pair. At most n queries decide the question or produce an equivalent instance simple modulo H. On the eight-point 65/67 control every occupied bucket is deleted; adding the ninth column permits a successful pin.

For a circuit C of q(E) with nonzero actual sum, compute J=span(C) intersect U. If J is not contained in H, no solution flat contains C. Otherwise contraction is an exact extension query with at most three named dimensions, since J contains that nonzero sum. A negative answer then forbids even all but one member: the omitted quotient image is spanned by the others.

The sets avoiding these failed-circuit-minus-one constraints do not in general form a matroid. Take U=span(1,2,4,8), H=span(1,2), and ordinary a=16,b=32,c=49,d=64,e=81. The only circuits of q(E) are abc, ade and bcde. The first two have gain 1, the last zero. Neither pin extends. The minimal forbidden pairs ab and ad have no forbidden subset in bd, violating circuit elimination. If U must be contained in the ordinary span, adjoin ordinary points 4,8,6; they span a subspace meeting H only in line 2, potentially allowing the old circuit to extend, but a physical solution cannot use any of those three points since each belongs to U outside H. They are deleted by the first preprocessing step. No target solution is gained, and no additional circuit-minus-one constraints contained in bd arise: physical-private projections of b,d are independent and none of the added columns supplies their sum mod H. This critique concerns exactly the extracted constraints.

Suppose every flat of q(E) has binary cycle space generated by its circuits of length at most b. In a positive saturated flat the linear map from its cycle space which sums the original columns has image H. Some generating circuit has nonzero sum and its actual trace lies inside H. Enumerate all O(n^b) candidates and use the pin test. This is an unconditional witness finder and a complete decision algorithm under the stated promise. It applies if every circuit longer than b has a chord splitting it into shorter circuits: a chord belongs to the span of the entire original circuit, and stays in the saturated flat at every recursive step. For a supplied graphic realization, a chordal graph satisfies this with b=3, allowing arbitrary physical lifts. Chordality of a supplied graph can be verified using a perfect elimination order: remaining neighbors must be pairwise adjacent at every deletion; checking all pairs is polynomial. Our triangle enumeration by itself does not recognize chordality of an arbitrary binary representation.
