## A one-site hybrid of regular quotients and logarithmic tag deficit

Two distinct full private directions give two affine translates of the tag plane B in the target quotient V/H. Their eight points form AG(3,2): their private projections are independent and the affine hull is three-dimensional. Contract one of these eight to obtain F₇. Thus an instance with all 35 quotients regular has at most one distinct fully tagged private direction. A deletion-only repair making this particular quotient regular must spoil all but one of any originally fully tagged directions.

Try each physical site as a deletion/contraction split, normalizing names and required inclusions by the existing exact-plane branch rules. Accept this structural certificate if each child is explicitly settled, all its 35 quotients are regular, or its tag deficit is at most the chosen bound τ. A found split permits decision with traceback in 2^{O(τ)}poly(input), including n candidate tests. A supplied binary tree of depth k made of such splits can be verified and evaluated in 2^{k+O(τ)}poly(input); searching for a depth-k tree by testing all split sites recursively costs (2n)^k times the terminal cost. This is separate from the earlier 11-site regular-backdoor algorithm: its necessity lemma does not automatically persist for mixed terminals.

For a strict structural example fix m≥2, independent b₁,b₂,e,x₁,...,x_m,h₁,h₂, U=span(b₁,b₂,h₁,h₂), H=span(h₁,h₂). Use columns x_i+t for each t in {0,b₁,b₂,b₁+b₂+e}, plus one physical e and the two independent H columns. The private rank is m+1. No private direction is fully tagged: x_i has three tags, x_i+e one, and e one. Contracting e makes all m remaining private directions fully tagged, hence deficit zero. The quotient by H before this split contains an AG(3,2) minor by the same contraction and has a Fano minor.

Deleting e instead produces graphic quotients for every Q≤U of dimension two. Before adjoining the h-columns the ordinary core is K_{m,4}, whose four right vertex potentials are t=0,b₁,b₂,b₁+b₂+e (their three nonzero differences from 0 are independent). Its quotient by Q∩B is graphic: every point of B is the difference of two of the first three right vertex potentials; contract the necessary edges temporarily adjoined on these vertices. In the resulting core all surviving nonzero elements of B'=B/(Q∩B) still correspond to edges. Now adjoin the two images of the h-columns. Any of them inside the core is in B'; a zero image is a loop. Images independent modulo the core give coloops; a single independent outside image also gives a coloop. If both are in the same nonzero coset modulo the core, their difference is in B', so realize them as two edges meeting a new vertex at one end, with the two other endpoints realizing their difference (use parallel edges if zero). This exhausts the cases, proving the all-regular child. The root of this family has unbounded deficit and a nonregular quotient although one split suffices. The physical H columns make it an elementary YES independently; this example separates the structural recognition conditions only.

A variant gives both YES and NO with all ordinary columns outside U. Replace the two physical h sites by z,z+h₁,z+h₂ using a new independent private z. Leave the named space unchanged. For target H₊=span(h₁,h₂), the three-site star realizes it. For H₋=span(h₁+b₁,h₂+b₂), any witness projects to a span containing both independent h directions at zero z in the coordinates (z,h₁,h₂). This requires all three star columns. Their span then contains the plain h axes, which are in U outside H₋, a contradiction. Both targets are fully physically spanned. There are still no fully tagged original private directions; after contracting e the m core directions are full and the sole additional direction z is incomplete, giving deficit one. Before branching the respective target quotients have the same cube minor in the core, since B meets either target trivially.

For deletion of e, the preceding graphic core proof works unchanged. In each quotient by Q partition the three star points into classes modulo the image of the core. Each interpoint difference within a class lies in B'. The distinct projected classes are independent: they have a common z-coordinate one, and at most three classes exist, so neither a pair nor a triple of distinct classes has zero sum. Each class can therefore use a separate new vertex. Its offsets within B' (after fixing a representative) take at most three values. In the rank-two case any set of at most three points of F₂² is a translate of a subset of {0,b₁,b₂}; choose the new vertex potential with that translation. If the quotient rank is below two, images of the first three right potentials cover B' outright. Repeated offsets use parallel edges. Thus all 35 deletion quotients remain graphic. This paired family witnesses a structural gain without using physical named columns, and the direct projection proof explains the negative member.
