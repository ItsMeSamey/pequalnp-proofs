# A full binary plane target as a seven-element quaternary cocircuit

Write the binary ordinary columns (q_e,h_e,o_e) in Q plus H plus D, with U=H plus D, both summands binary planes, and Q spanned by ordinary private projections. Put v_e=(q_e,h_e) and V=Q plus H. Reject if the v_e fail to span V. Any positive witness may be extended to full private rank by adding independently projected physical columns, preserving the entire named trace. The resulting span projects isomorphically onto V and gives a binary-linear map s:V→D with s(H)=0. Conversely any such map whose equality-active projections (those with s(v_e)=o_e) span V gives an original solution by its active columns.

Identify D with GF(4) as a binary vector space. Form quaternary columns (v_e,o_e) retaining binary coordinates for v_e. Append seven distinct probes z=(0,1) and (h_i,c) for each of two independent binary basis vectors of H and each nonzero c in GF(4). Rank is dim(V)+1 by projection and z. A cocircuit containing all seven exists exactly when the original target is attained.

Normalize the functional of its complementary hyperplane to take value one on z. It is (φ,1). Avoidance of the three translates for each i forces φ(h_i)=0. The hyperplane is spanned by ordinary columns since all probes belong to the cocircuit. Thus its zero ordinary columns project onto the full scalar-extended V. Binary-coordinate projections have equal rank over both fields, so an actual binary basis can be extracted and gives s by restricting φ. Conversely any binary s with two-bit output extends quaternary-linearly by giving its values on the coordinate basis. Its active ordinary columns span the graph over GF(4), and all seven probes avoid it.

For direct universal probe patterns restricted to the quaternary three-space spanned by h1,h2,z, seven is optimal to distinguish this desired hyperplane from every other, independent of ordinary input. Points outside the desired hyperplane normalize to (a,b,1). To exclude every other hyperplane, their affine coordinates must meet all twenty lines in the affine plane of order four. Direct exhaustive checking of its 8008 six-point subsets shows none do. Any smaller set extends to six. The two affine coordinate axes give seven points and meet all lines. This is a statement about this direct pattern only. No algorithm for the resulting quaternary fixed-seven circuit or cocircuit problem is assumed.
