# A minimum universal restriction of a quadratic four-dimensional palette

Let K=F₂⁴. Write k as x₀+2x₁+4x₂+8x₃ for display and take q(k)=x₀x₁+x₂x₃. For every nonzero k the available two-bit tags b are all values other than (q(k),0). A chart is a linear map g:K→F₂². A palette is universal when the directions of its records (k,g(k)) span K in every chart.

**Result.** The 45-record palette is universal, and 24 is the minimum number of its indexed records that retain universality. An explicit optimum discards k in {3,8,11}, retaining both tags (1+q(k),s) for s∈F₂ at the other twelve nonzero k. Neither palette has a fully tagged direction. The minimum is specific to restrictions of these 45 records.

For universality one must meet, for every nonzero linear form l and chart g, the test l(k)=1, b=g(k). In a full three-tag palette with arbitrary missing value f(k), failure is equivalent to f agreeing with an affine map everywhere on a slice l=1, since any affine map on this slice extends to a linear map on K. The polar form B of q is nondegenerate, so its restriction to the three-dimensional tangent l=0 cannot vanish. Consequently q is nonaffine on each slice. Four is the least positive private dimension for any universal palette with no fully tagged nonzero direction. In dimensions one and two every slice has at most two points and is affinely independent. In dimension three enlarge each bucket to three tags and define f as its missing value. The sum of f over the four points of l=1 is a linear function of l with values in F₂². Some nonzero l gives zero, exactly the condition for f to agree with an affine map on this plane.

## A 24-record restriction

The deleted directions together with 0 form the totally isotropic plane L=span(3,8). To fail universality on some slice l=1 the nonaffinity of q there would have to disappear after deleting L. If l vanishes on L, the slice is intact. Otherwise two points are removed. Their difference lies in L. The polar form on the tangent of the slice has radical generated by r=B⁻¹l, which lies outside L when l is nonzero on L. A nonzero discrepancy between q and an affine form on this slice cannot be supported on these two points. Indeed, its derivative in direction r is constant. A nonzero derivative requires one nonzero value in each of four r-pairs, while zero derivative requires its support to be a union of r-pairs. Neither can hold on the removed points. Universality follows. The same argument works for any Lagrangian plane. Direct enumeration finds 96, 64, and 96 charts with respectively 4, 6, and 8 active directions in the 24-record restriction.

## Lower bound within the 45 records

There is one record with second tag bit zero for each nonzero direction. Let D be the set of directions where this record was discarded. Every pair x,y with B(x,y)=1 requires retention of at least one of their two second-bit-zero records. To see this set r=x+y and l=B(r,·). The slice l=1 contains x,y and has tangent radical r. On this slice the indicator of the two-point set {x,y} factors through the quotient by r as a point indicator on an affine plane. Its polar form is the unique nonzero alternating form on that plane, hence its pullback is the polar form of q on the slice. The indicator and q thus differ by an affine function, which extends to a linear form a on K. For chart (a,0) the only eligible directions on the slice are exactly x and y. Therefore D is pairwise orthogonal. Its span is isotropic and has dimension at most two.

There are four symmetry types of D. Each is represented by one of ∅, {3}, {3,8}, {3,8,11}. Every independent orthogonal pair extends to a symplectic basis, so the symplectic group is transitive on ordered isotropic pairs and on nonzero vectors. If S preserves B then q(Sk)+q(k) is linear in k. The permutation of records sending (k,b₁,b₂) to (Sk,b₁+q(Sk)+q(k),b₂) preserves the palette and sends linear charts and nonzero slice tests bijectively to such tests. In particular it preserves the second-bit-zero layer and legitimates the four representatives.

For each of these four D, fix all 15−|D| remaining second-bit-zero records. There are just 30 candidate records with second bit one. To reach total size ≤23 at most 8+|D| may be selected. The independent exhaustive part of the proof is in optimum.py. For every slice and chart missed by the fixed part, form its clause of eligible second-layer records. Discard supersets of other clauses. In a state choose an unhit clause; branch on the first selected member among its remaining candidates, deleting earlier candidates in later branches. An empty unhit clause rejects. Pairwise disjoint unhit clauses give an admissible lower bound on the additional choices. This covers all possible hitting sets up to the budget. The four searches return infeasible using under 1,000 recursive states in total. No SAT solver or external library is required.

Run python check.py and python optimum.py. The first also checks that deleting any one chosen record spoils a chart. The second proves the stated cardinality lower bound. This is a private-direction spanning statement and imposes no condition on any separate two-bit gains.
