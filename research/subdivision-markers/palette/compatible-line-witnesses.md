# Exact overlap for compatible physical line witnesses

Let U be the named four-space and H a requested plane therein. Write π for the quotient by U. Given ordinary subsets F,G whose spans A,B meet U in distinct lines of H, their union is a solution precisely when
           
    dim(πA intersect πB) = dim(A intersect B).

Indeed the trace dimension of A+B is its dimension less its projected dimension, giving two plus the left side minus the right. The intersection A intersect B injects under π since the two named lines are distinct. Thus the condition means that every common private vector has a common lift in both actual spans. It is a rank test on the physical representatives.

Every positive solution Y has witnesses of a stronger form. Choose P within Y projecting to a basis of π(span Y). For each f in Y outside P, take f and the unique supporting subset of P needed for its projection. This is a circuit in the projected matroid, including singleton loops. Its residual is a named vector and the residuals span the trace H. Two with independent residuals give minimal physical exact-line witnesses F,G. Their projected spans are coordinate subspaces on the supporting subsets of P. Their projected intersection is generated by the actual shared elements of P. Conversely two such compatible line witnesses suffice.

Even if F is supplied without a compatible second witness, one exact three-name test decides whether a mate exists. Contract F and ask for target H/(span F intersect U) in the image of U. In a positive solution lift all contracted columns and pick P as above. At least one residual lies outside the line of F. Its fundamental circuit lies in the lifted solution, hence is a compatible mate under the displayed rank test. The reverse implication follows by using the union. In particular one can enumerate all projected circuits of at most k+1 elements by enumerating indexed sets up to that size, then check possible mates. This decides the promise that any positive instance admits a solution containing such a nonzero target-line circuit in deterministic n^{k+1} poly(n) for fixed k. Longer circuits may matter in unrestricted instances.

The following exact input pair tests the limits of directional relaxations. With U the low four bits, let E={16,17,20,21,24,26,28,31} and H=span(1,2). The projection of E is a single parallel class. Every attainable nonzero trace is an affine difference span of a subset of E with the fifth bit removed. Hence any selected subset producing H must lie in one affine H-coset and contain at least three points. Each occupied H-coset contains two points; adding 18 permits the target. Both versions give all 15 exact lines and the full attainable spectrum in each of the 15 three-space quotients. The original gives 65 exact subspaces and the extended one 67.

Six ordinary columns already suffice for a full line catalogue with a missing plane. Take 16+x for x in {0,1,2,4,8,15} and H=span(1,6). All 15 pair differences are distinct. The three H-pairs are {0,1}, {2,4}, and {8,15}, so no physical selection gives H. Six is minimal for this pair of properties: with at most five columns the span must contain U to realize all 15 lines. If its rank is four all physical columns lie in U, and if rank five the private quotient has dimension one. In the latter case an exact line requires a singleton in U or a pair of external columns. With s ordinary columns in U the bound on distinct lines is s plus binomial(5−s,2), at most ten.
