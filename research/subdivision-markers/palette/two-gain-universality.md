# Two gain outliers suffice for the full fixed plane

An equality-spanning instance asks for a binary-linear map φ to two bits with prescribed evaluations at two vectors, such that the matching actual columns span the domain. Subtracting a reference linear map from labels and pins preserves feasibility. Gains here are relative to zero.

Work in the span of original ordinary binary columns E. If the target plane H is not contained there, reject. When the named intersection has dimension below four the known three-name solver suffices. Otherwise choose U=span(h1,h2,d1,d2) and H=span(h1,h2). Give all original columns label 00, add exactly two indexed columns d1+h1 and d2+h2 with labels 10 and 01, and pin φ(d1)=10, φ(d2)=01.

The pins are independent, so matching columns can span the ambient space only if both exception columns match. This forces φ(h1)=φ(h2)=0. Every element of ker φ must be spanned by the matching original columns since in a combination producing zero output the coefficients of the two exceptions vanish individually. The resulting actual original trace is H. Conversely extend a positive original span by actual columns with independent private projections modulo U to get a codimension-two span K still meeting U in H. Then K is complementary to span(d1,d2). The unique φ with kernel K and the stated values on d1,d2 matches the two exceptions and the ordinary generators of K. The correspondence recovers original columns only.

In the GF(16) comparison on any basis of original columns the reference interpolant is zero before homogenizing the pins. After homogenization discrepancies on every original column remain zero. Pinned errors take the two independent prescribed values, giving a nonzero GF(4)+λ GF(4) denominator. Thus the binary and GF(16) normalized comparison matrices agree on all but the two added columns. Solving two gain outliers generally would solve the unrestricted binary plane problem. This equivalence does not establish computational hardness.

For comparison, a single arbitrary outlier is polynomial, as are arbitrarily many gains in one output line. A map interpolated by a full matching span has image contained in the span of all gains. Reject pins outside that line, otherwise identify the gains and pins with scalar bits. Test a binary cocircuit through the three probes z=(0,1), (p_i,1+b_i) for the two pins in the scalar matrix of ordinary columns (t,o(t)), first checking physical t span. A qualifying hyperplane avoiding the probes is spanned entirely by matching ordinary columns and its functional yields the desired scalar map. All-zero gains only admit the zero map. This algorithm assumes collinear actual gain discrepancies, not just a single potentially nonbinary column after arbitrary matrix rescaling.
