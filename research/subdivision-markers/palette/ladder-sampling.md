# Every compatible ordinary quotient can give exponentially rare target witnesses

Take the simple graphic binary representation of a two-by-m ladder (m≥3) with one additional leaf e attached to the top-left corner. For each of the four corners, name the incidence difference between it and e, using four independent named columns. A named column may be a copy of a physical column. Target the plane generated by the sum of the two top names and the sum of the two bottom names.

A physical witness of size r−2 is a spanning forest with three components. In the quotient by its columns, the two top corners must have the same nonzero image relative to e and likewise the bottom corners, with the two images independent. Thus there is a top connection and a separate bottom connection, and e belongs to neither. Both paths cross all m columns. As only two vertices lie in each column, the paths each use exactly one per column, so cannot switch rows. They are the top and bottom rails. These two rails already have the required number of edges and form the unique witness.

For a basis of a named plane Q write its two binary coordinate rows as q and t. On any three-component forest with e isolated and remaining terminal bipartition specified by nonconstant x in four bits, the determinant testing transversality of Q is
     
    (q·x)(t·1)+(t·x)(q·1).

Indeed the two image coordinates of q are q·x and q·(1+x), and the quadratic terms cancel. This is linear in x. Whenever Q accepts the two-element top part, Q therefore accepts a singleton corner i in that part. Isolate i and e. The remaining ladder after deleting i is connected, and contains floor((m−1)/2) vertex-disjoint intact squares by grouping consecutive columns away from the column of i. Contract each square, fix a spanning tree of the connected quotient, then independently choose one of four spanning trees within each square. Their lifts give at least 4^floor((m−1)/2) different forests, all accepted by Q and all missing the target. Thus even the best choice of compatible Q gives success probability at most 1/(1+4^floor((m−1)/2)) when sampling its bases uniformly. Adaptive mixtures of such unconditional samples have the same per-draw bound. Conditional restriction, nonuniform weighting, analytic use of counts, and algorithms inspecting the structure of this graphic input are outside the claim.
