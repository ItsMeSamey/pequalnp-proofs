# Fixed-terminal interior planar groves via surface laminations (candidate)

Input is a connected plane indexed multigraph and seven distinct marked vertices anywhere. Start with positive integer weights. In each triple's target tree, the tree median m is unique. Enumerate m and two ordered distinct indexed edges mx,my leaving it toward the two smallest terminals if m is not a terminal, or toward the other two if it is. Cut these edges and prescribe three components (x,first terminal), (y,second terminal), (m,remaining terminal). Repeated names in one part give a singleton; shared names across distinct parts reject. Do this twice; reject duplicate cut edges. Any compatible cut forest reassembles uniquely and really has median m: paths to the two detached parts leave on their distinct specified edges while the remaining terminal is on the third side or equals m. Weight by all four original cut edges. Thus ≤n²(2M)^4 guesses reduce exactly to ≤13 roots, seven singleton or paired components, with no multiplicity. Retain the original plane embedding even if the cut graph is disconnected.

Give each root a separate tiny boundary hole tangent in a fixed incident corner. Add one orientable band with one cap edge for each pair, or one band with a cap loop at its singleton (two arcs at distinct points of its boundary, routed to separate darts at that root). The resulting surface has bounded topological type and nonempty boundary. Each band's oriented relative co-core intersects just its own cap edge once. On an oriented embedded cycle its signed crossing number is homological, hence invariant under isotopy. A CRSF contributes precisely if its cycles have periods ±e_i, one for each cap, no others. Indeed each cap is then present on its own unique cycle. Removing all caps yields precisely the desired ordinary seven-component forest; conversely adding them gives those essential cycles, singleton loops included. Set cap weights one.

Kenyon arXiv:1001.4028 (rank-two vector-bundle matrix-CRSF theorem) and Kenyon–Wilson arXiv:1107.3377 Theorem 4.5 and proof give the quaternion determinant/Pfaffian of the flat SL2 bundle Laplacian as sum over essential CRSF laminations L of their ordinary weights times f_L=product of (2−trace of monodromy) over components. Contractible cycles vanish. The f_L for disjoint unoriented curves including multiplicities are independent regular functions over C by the cited Fock–Goncharov trace-lamination basis and triangular replacement of trace by 2−trace. Work over Q/C before reducing to a finite field.

Take an ideal triangulation with each boundary hole collapsed to a puncture, for closed-curve coordinates. Let B be the total number of intersections of its finitely many sides with all graph edges after transverse perturbation. Every CRSF lamination has each normal coordinate ≤B, since isotoping out bigons can only lower them. Enumerate all vectors in [0,B]^O(1). Triangle inequalities and even triangle sums determine uniquely all local arc counts; join the ordered endpoints across edges and trace each component. Discard trivial components, deduplicate classes if needed, include peripheral and parallel loops. This recovers all relevant types and possibly unused types. Word lengths per entire normal multicurve are O(B) for fixed topology using the dual spine, and cap co-core pairings follow by abelianization in an adapted marking. Only the curves satisfying the seven homological conditions contribute to the requested sum.

Constructive surface marking: subdivide parallel edges and loops for drawing only, triangulate the initial plane embedding and give it a planar straight-line drawing on a polynomial grid (Fáry/grid algorithm). Choose small rational triangular corner holes at the roots or slightly offset them with tiny access arcs. For a direct alternative, puncture each root's original adjacent face AT a nearby interior point, run distinct cap-access arcs in that face to each root, and attach bands at the punctures; the roots need not physically lie on the boundary after the caps have been placed. The punctures, now distinct points in the plane after boundary collapse, can be joined by a straight-line triangulation incl the point at infinity. With a constant number of holes this has constant complexity; each straight ideal side crosses polynomially many segments of the drawn original graph. Truncate around the holes before adding bands. This gives a cellular mesh for the punctured sphere with a constant number of vertices/edges/faces; the truncated triangles and holes have bounded combinatorial complexity. Subdivide it a bounded number of times to include the band attaching arcs and add the rectangular bands. On this constant-complexity surface mesh choose any fixed ideal triangulation/fundamental cut system, taking arcs polygonal in its cells. Enumeration of topological templates until a verified triangulation is found suffices, since there are only boundedly many combinatorial types at fixed root count. Each arc inside old straight cells can be drawn with boundedly many straight pieces, using a bounded triangulation of any nonconvex truncated polygon. Thus physical graph crossings and edge holonomy word lengths are polynomial in the original embedding. For constructing actual cap-access arcs even for several holes in the same nonconvex original face, choose tiny disjoint discs near each rooted vertex so that each arc lies in its own chosen corner. Multiple disconnected graph components after deleting guessed edges retain the ORIGINAL embedding. Any geometric refinement is for routing/crossing computation only and never subdivides physical forest weights.

The punctured orientable surface has free fundamental group of constant rank k. In a basis choose X_i=[[a_i,b_i],[c_i,(1+b_i c_i)/a_i]] on the dense open chart of SL2^k. Any edge parallel transport or curve word has polynomial Laurent degree and polynomial-bit evaluation. Choose a uniform bound on degrees from the actual total word lengths (multiply max edge word length by matrix dimension for the Pfaffian). After clearing Laurent denominators, an integer grid in the 3k parameters (avoid a_i=0), at degree+1 points per coordinate, is unisolvent. It has polynomial size with the topology fixed. Compute the genuine rank-two bundle Pfaffian from the primary theorem and f_L at each rational grid point. Independence on the dense chart implies full column rank over Q; solve for each type coefficient by rational Gaussian elimination and sum exactly the selected ones. Integer input bit length plus polynomial factors controls all evaluation sizes by matrix-product bit bounds, Pfaffian minors and Hadamard. Fraction-free linear algebra is enough; no characteristic-two trace basis or analytic integration is claimed.

For input weights in GF(2^s) represented by binary polynomials w_e(X) of degree <s, lift to integer polynomials w_e(X)+2 to preserve the residue and assure positivity. The target D=n−3 homogeneous polynomial gives integer lift of univariate degree ≤D(s−1); evaluate at D(s−1)+1 positive integers, interpolate over Q, reduce coefficients mod2 and mod the defining polynomial. Evaluation and coefficient bit sizes remain polynomial. Use previously proved forced-original-index tracing after any nonzero evaluation. This candidate is a theoretical polynomial bound with a very large fixed exponent, not an implementation.
