# Detecting a two-anchor W solver with unboundedly many charged faces

Let T be the set of nonzero-charge faces of a supplied connected plane dual. A T-path joins two distinct terminals and has no internal terminal. Every connected zero-charge anchor carrying any nonzero terminals contains a T-path (take a shortest path within the anchor between two of its terminals, minimizing over pairs). Thus no feasible pair has more independent charged anchors than the maximum vertex-disjoint T-path packing number p. If p≤2 the proof and exact W algorithm in five-terminal-plane.md apply, even with unbounded |T|.

Compute p by ordinary maximum matching. In H replace every nonterminal by two adjacent twins, leave each terminal single, and replace each ordinary simple adjacency by the complete adjacency on its one or two copies. Ignore loops for routing. The matching M0 of all twin pairs has size |V∖T|. Starting there, augment to maximum cardinality. Previously matched vertices stay matched so only unmatched terminals are possible endpoints. The union of this maximum matching and M0 (keep coincident edges twice) consists of alternating cycles, isolated unused terminals and paths with charged endpoints and consecutive twin pairs internally. Contract each twin pair: these are vertex-disjoint T-paths. Conversely k vertex-disjoint T-paths yield a matching of size |V∖T|+k by alternating along the paths while keeping all other twin edges. Thus p=ν(H)−|V∖T|. Computing the cardinality alone suffices even if the matching solver's selected optimum does not itself saturate all twin copies, since augmentation proves some optimum does. With unboundedly many charged leaves, a single-star dual has p=1 and a two-hub bipartite dual with terminals on the large side has p≤2.

This condition certifies an input class, not the necessity of using a larger oracle when p>2. It ignores charge types and may overestimate the attainable number of zero-charge anchors.
