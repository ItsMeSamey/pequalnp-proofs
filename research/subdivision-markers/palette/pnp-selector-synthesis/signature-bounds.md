# All exterior linear constraints from individual incompatible circuits

Write λ_i for site i's coordinate evaluation on the exact first relation space L. For an unbalanced private circuit C every pair realized on one chart simultaneously omits at least one i in C. Consequently W is contained in the sum over i in C of exterior-square ker λ_i. Its annihilator is the intersection of the spaces λ_i wedge L*. This identity follows in a basis adapted to a nonzero λ_i, and a zero signature contributes the zero annihilator.

If the signatures include three independent forms the intersection is zero. Independent λ and μ give intersection exactly span(λ wedge μ); any third nonzero form in their plane preserves that line. A rank-one unbalanced circuit without a zero signature cannot exist in the exact L: all its signatures would be the same nonzero form, so some actual first-relation generator has every site of C in its support. Such a chart would match the unbalanced circuit.

Thus generate the strongest intersection of all these single-conflict linear upper bounds in polynomial time: enumerate every two-plane D spanned by a pair of nonzero site signatures. Test tag consistency on all sites whose signature is a nonzero element of D. If inconsistent, a minimal inconsistent circuit has signatures spanning D, and any basis λ,μ of D yields the annihilator equation λ wedge μ. Every productive single circuit is found this way. The resulting upper bound can be intersected with independent bounds and compared to a lower span of actual compatible witness pairs; equality proves exactness. It need not equal W. On the 17-site absent-H example it reduces the ambient dimension 66 to 47, while the exact W has dimension 33. On the conflicting-tag double copy it gives zero; on distinct axes the full one-dimensional W survives.
