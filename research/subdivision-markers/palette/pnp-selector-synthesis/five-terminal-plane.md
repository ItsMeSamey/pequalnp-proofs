# Recovering arbitrary indexed W on every plane dual with at most five charged faces

Take any connected supplied plane dual D with two-bit vertex charges σ. No connectivity, treewidth or routing guarantee is required beyond connectedness. From any jointly realizable x,y choose one connected anchor spanning all charged faces in each of their monochrome components that contains charges. Distinct anchors are disjoint, each carries zero total charge and contains at least two charged faces. Every pair of vectors constant on each anchor (with independent constants between anchors) is jointly feasible: each monochrome component contains entire anchors or none. The complement of the anchors consists solely of neutrals. If at most five faces are charged, there are at most two anchors.

Normalize both vectors to zero on one anchor by the global face gauge. Then each is a linear combination of the indicator of the one other anchor, when present, and neutral singleton indicators away from all anchors. In their wedge the square of the one anchor vanishes. Each remaining term is a neutral singleton wedged with a jointly feasible relation. With no anchors choose an arbitrary root gauge; all pairs are feasible. Thus for at most five charged faces W is exactly the sum over neutral z of e_z wedge L[z], where L[z] is the span of the relations jointly realizable with this fixed singleton; omit z if e_z itself is infeasible. For zero charges the neutral terms still span full exterior space.

**Compute L[z].** Use any face representative of x. Its value at z may be normalized to zero without affecting the original cut by subtracting its global constant. The paired feasibility of e_z,x requires precisely independent one-bit face-label feasibility of x on each connected component C of D−z under its inherited two-bit charge σ. If C has nonzero total charge then e_z is impossible. Otherwise retain any original face roots for each C. Recover the actual aggregate first space of admissible dual cut words on C as follows. Choose a separate binary edge chain for each charge bit with the required boundary σ on C (tree parity suffices). Give each indexed edge these two tags. Use a private binary matrix K whose kernel is the cut space of C, e.g. rows indexing fundamental cycle incidences. Then for any available edge support S the tag interpolation condition is precisely that each private kernel word supported on S has zero total tag. As in the original planar dual criterion this is exactly the monochrome-component zero-charge rule, now on C. The general scalar three-name first-space algorithm returns a basis generated by actual admissible indexed cut words, in polynomial time with the published certifying backend.

Integrate those words to face bits via a tree, fixing the component root to zero, and extend by zero on the other components and z. Also include the indicator of every component C: adding an arbitrary constant on C leaves its restricted cut word unchanged and preserves joint feasibility. These supply all admissible x modulo global constants and modulo the irrelevant wedge with e_z itself. Each individual returned basis lift can be chosen actually feasible, rather than a Gaussian combination of realizable vectors; component indicators and zero elsewhere are also directly feasible. Convert each lift and e_z back to cut masks of the ORIGINAL dual indexed edges. Keep the resulting actual wedge pair, or stream its evaluation against any queried alternating form. The separate case of isolated C vertices is handled by the constants. At most polynomially many first-space calls, rows and output tests are needed, with no bounded-charged-face graph minor theorem required. A standalone fallback that enumerates finite charts remains exponential when used instead of the polynomial scalar oracle.

This proof does not extend unchanged when there are three anchors: after normalizing one, their two remaining indicator vectors can have a nonzero wedge with no neutral singleton. The new difficult contributions are explicitly confined to such two-anchor products. The promised class refers to the number of nonzero charges in the supplied embedding, not to the number of tags on ordinary physical edges.
