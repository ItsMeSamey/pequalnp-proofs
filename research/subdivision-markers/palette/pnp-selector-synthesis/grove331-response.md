# Exact seven-terminal grove is not determined by all boundary Laplacian minors

Over GF(2), use the same eight-vertex simple host with marks 0,…,6, interior v=7 and desired parts {0,1,5}, {2,3,6}, {4}. The host is the union of the following supports, with all unlisted edges assigned zero. Both assignments include edges 05 and 26 of weight 1. In the first, v is joined by unit edges to 0,…,4 and all clique edges vanish. In the second, 0,…,4 form a unit complete graph and v has only the unit pendant edge to 4. Every square Laplacian minor deleting equally many boundary rows and columns is identical on the two assignments. Indeed both interior blocks are [1]. Schur elimination of the center in the first graph adds precisely a unit clique on those five neighbors in characteristic two (including the diagonal cancellation); eliminating the pendant center in the second just removes it. The common terminal Laplacian and the identical interior determinant imply simultaneous equality by the block determinant identity for every such minor, including nonprincipal minors. This is the ordinary electrical star–mesh reduction.

The first graph cannot realize the two specified nontrivial parts without using v twice. The second has exactly one forest of the requested terminal type: edges 01,23,05,26 and 4v. Its original indexed mask in the checker's mesh ordering is (0,7,10,11,12). Thus the two polynomial evaluations are 0 and 1 even modulo two. This excludes arbitrary functions of the entire boundary minor signature at the supplied weights, even with the fixed host known. It also excludes any boundary-only bordered gadget or formal terminal-edge perturbation whose interior Schur complement and determinant are its only interface, including coefficient extraction after such perturbation. It does not exclude a determinant construction using multiple fresh copies of interior weights, responses after individually reweighting original edges, or other interior information.

An independent linear-span check uses the complete graph on exactly seven marked vertices. Each of its 301 three-block partitions occurs as a spanning forest. For 35 root triples let T_I be the vector indicating they hit all blocks. The 1,225 mixed-minor row vectors are T_I T_J pointwise; by the all-minors theorem orientation signs disappear in characteristic two. Gaussian rank is 105, and the unit vector of the (3,3,1) partition increases that rank. Since the indexed forest weights are distinct monomials, no scalar linear combination of ordinary three-row/three-column-deleted Laplacian minors returns the exact target polynomial even allowing all vertices as roots. The rank computation is reproducible and independent of the stronger response collision.

grove331_response.py checks all 3,432 minor pairs across sizes and enumerates all five-edge forests on both eight-vertex supports; the seven-vertex calculation uses literal partition incidences. This obstruction is separate from the rank-one two-transversal identity and the rank-two projected-YES counterexample. It does not decide complexity of unrestricted (3,3,1) evaluation or unrestricted P versus NP. Kenyon and Wilson’s response formulas assume circular planarity, and their tripartite Pfaffian targets pairings (arXiv:math/0608422 and 0811.1766); neither assumption is imposed on this host.
