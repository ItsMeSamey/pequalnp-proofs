# No fixed physical single-site conflict depth suffices

Start with U_0(S)=exterior-square L(S) on the surviving sites. Intersect over every unbalanced private circuit C the sum of U_d(S minus i) over i in C to obtain U_(d+1). An entirely consistent state gives its exact exterior square. The omission of some common site is necessary for every compatible pair, proving soundness. With M supplied conflicts, depth d takes at most sum_{a≤d} binomial(N,a) times poly(N,M) work for memoized states; generating an unrestricted list of circuits is separately charged.

For any depth q begin with (k,0,0),(k,0,h1),(k,b,0),(k,b,h2) for one private key k, nonzero tag b and independent heights. Each height is separately attainable; both are impossible. Replace each site i by a two-member bundle with fresh private direction a_i; keys a_i,a_i+k have tags 0,b_i and heights 0,h_i. Make q+2 identical copies of every member. In a private relation the parity of the two copy pools in each bundle agrees, by the a_i coordinate. An odd bundle forces g(k)=b_i. The odd bundles therefore define an original compatible relation of the same height. Conversely all original compatible relations lift by assigning g(a_i)=0. Thus the target determinant vanishes on W but is nonzero on the exterior square of L.

After at most q deletions at least two copies remain in every pool. Parallel two-site circuits are balanced. Any minimal unbalanced circuit uses distinct pools and both members of at least two bundles. Its signatures on the exact surviving first space are independent: pairing each of its sites with a different retained copy in the same pool gives a compatible relation whose restriction to the circuit is the respective unit vector. Deleting one site when a twin survives changes L exactly by intersecting its zero-coordinate hyperplane. To prove the reverse inclusion, replace occurrences of the deleted site linearly by its retained twin. Each compatible generator stays compatible, and all vectors already zero at the deleted site are fixed.

Induct upward from depth q. At every visited nonterminal state the circuit has at least four independent signatures. The exterior squares of the child hyperplanes therefore span the entire parent exterior square, by the exterior-duality calculation in signature-bounds.md. The bounded-depth answer is always the entire root exterior square even when exact semantic closure is used at every child. This obstruction is specific to individual physical omissions. Collapsing duplicate sites or series bundles can defeat it; no impossibility for other preprocessors or polynomial algorithms follows.
