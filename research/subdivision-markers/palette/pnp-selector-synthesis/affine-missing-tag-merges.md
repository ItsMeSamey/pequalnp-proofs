# Universal routes across affine-inconsistent missing tags

Maintain disjoint vertex clusters with a chart-dependent indexed physical chain between any two chosen vertices of a cluster. At every chart the chain must consist of active sites, with the required boundary. Assume polynomial evaluation and coordinate degree at most s. Fully tagged link components initialize this invariant with s=2.

Consider two clusters A,B and every observed cross-link f=xy between them. Let T_f be its set of physical tags and M_f the complementary missing set. To disable all cross-links simultaneously the potential differences must lie in all M_f. Relax each membership to the affine hull of M_f and solve the resulting scalar affine equations over the global vertex-color bits. If a link is full it already supplies a route. Otherwise Gaussian inconsistency produces a subset of the scalar violated-check functions whose XOR is the constant one. Group by link, obtaining affine scalar polynomials h_f(g) with sum identically one. Each h_f vanishes on M_f because every selected violation vanishes there. A value one thus guarantees the existence of the active indexed crossing representative. If every cross-link has at least two physical tags then every nonempty missing set has size at most two and is itself affine; in that case inconsistency exactly decides universal existence of an active cross-link. For arbitrary sets this affine criterion is sufficient.

Fix poles a in A and b in B. At a chart, for each f with h_f=1 take the internal chains a-to-x and y-to-b together with the canonical active physical crossing at f, and XOR the resulting indexed chains. The total boundary is a+b because an odd number of terms is selected. All sites retained are active. Each internal term has degree at most s+1. For each crossing representative of tag t its indicator equals the two-bit equality indicator times h_f evaluated at t: all scalar checks grouped at that link depend only on its endpoint difference. Hence its degree is at most two. The complete chain has degree at most max(2,s+1). This constructs a route on every chart, with no endpoint constraint.

During one round use the old clusters to certify all pairwise joins separately. Connect certified pairs into components and fix a tree of these joins per merged component. Any two new vertices connect by concatenating old internal chains and the chosen pole chains along a tree path. XOR addition preserves the same degree bound regardless of tree length. Store a shared acyclic routing circuit, evaluate each relevant chain once per chart, and charge Gaussian systems and explicit path/chain sizes polynomially per round. After h rounds the degree is at most h+2. Add arbitrary tagged virtual links within the new clusters. On a bounded-hole completed lift the symbolic indexed wedge theorem applies with sampling weight ≤2(h+2). Fixed h,b give polynomial recognition and witnesses with no initially supplied decomposition. Try each intermediate round independently. This structural certificate is also an explicit witness of the previously defined bounded-degree indexed section, not a general efficient recognition theorem for arbitrary semantic reachability.

On four physical vertices take fully tagged internal edges a0-a1, b0-b1. Give each of the four crossing links every tag except one; choose the omitted tags to have nonzero XOR around the K2,2. Disabling all four would impose inconsistent gains around the physical 4-cycle. The first closure therefore joins the two full-link components without any physically full cross-link. Disjoint copies yield unbounded rank beyond the original full-link span. This separates certified route discovery, not the output W complexity of a previously intractable instance.
