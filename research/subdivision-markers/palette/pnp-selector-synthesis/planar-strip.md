# Linear-size bounded-width physical family

Use even m≥8. Start with the rectangular integer lattice from x=0 to 4m+2 and y=−4,…,4, with a consistent diagonal in every square. Triangulate the outside on the sphere by adding a single apex coned to all perimeter links. The marked vertices are (4i+3,0), 0≤i<m. Subdivide this triangulated sphere barycentrically once; take the result as D and its plane dual as the private physical graph G. Every original grid path embeds via its old vertices and its traversed edge barycenters.

For any two disjoint terminal pairs take an upper rectangular arch through y=2 joining the first and a lower arch through y=−2 joining the second, using vertical endpoint legs. Their old-vertex sets are disjoint. In the subdivided graph the union of their paths induces precisely the two paths: included barycentric vertices are original vertices and barycenters of chosen edges, and their only possible adjacencies are incidence. Removing an induced forest from a sphere triangulation leaves a connected graph. To see this, order each tree starting at a root and list parents before children. When deleting a vertex at most one neighbor has been deleted; its cyclic link minus at most one vertex remains connected, so deletion cannot disconnect the residual graph. All remaining terminals thus have one background component.

For an individual pair at columns a<b make the first cycle from the upper arch at y=2 and an outer arch starting at (a,0), moving left to (a−1,0), up to y=3, across to b+1, down to y=0 and left to (b,0). The only common vertices of the two arch interiors are none. A disjoint second cycle goes left to right at y=−1, with a bump from (x−1,−1) up to (x−1,0), across to (x,0), down to (x,−1) for every remaining terminal x, and closes around the bottom on y=−3. Use outer left and right columns 0 and 4m+2. Spacing four avoids both marked pair vertices and their horizontal upper-arch neighbors. Both cycles survive subdivision and deletion of one neutral vertex leaves the remaining part of each connected. This gives exactly the robust path and pair certificates of planar-unbounded-annihilator.md.

The original sphere has O(m) faces and edges. Assign each small barycentric triangle to the minimum x-coordinate of the nonapex vertices of its original triangle. Each column owns O(1) such triangles. Adjacent small triangles' original faces coincide or meet in an original edge, and those faces have columns separated by at most one, including faces along an apex spoke (the two neighboring perimeter links share its boundary vertex). Hence the private dual G has a path decomposition by consecutive column groups of constant size. The rank m−2 lower bound on the unique annihilator is linear in physical site count in this family. This does not conflict with polynomial computation of W via bounded private width. Only small projected quantities and indexed supports need to be computed for replay.
