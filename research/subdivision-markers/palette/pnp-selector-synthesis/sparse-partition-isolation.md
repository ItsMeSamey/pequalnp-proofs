# Deterministic two-charge search with few accepted projected partitions

Fix a scalar charge projection λ, an original alternating query Ω and a bound s≥1 on the number of λ-neutral unordered connected three-partitions passing Ω. For each such partition its accepted three-forest polynomial is precisely the product of the three spanning-tree polynomials of its induced labeled subgraphs. These supports are disjoint. Their sum is the explicit one-bit mixed-minor evaluator. No bound on the number of forests within a partition is needed.

In each partition the union of its three Kruskal trees is the unique minimum forest for EVERY strictly increasing sequence of edge costs in original physical index order. Different partitions have different canonical forests. For each of the first M binom(s,2)+1 primes p try costs c_i=(M p+1)(i+1)+(2^i mod p), where 0≤i<M indexes original edges. The mask integer Σ_{i in F}2^i for each canonical forest has at most M bits. The product of the nonzero pairwise differences of ≤s masks has fewer than M binom(s,2)+1 distinct prime divisors, so some tried p separates all of them. Costs strictly increase. Unequal primary index sums cannot tie since each forest residual sum is <Mp; equal primary sums have different residual sums modulo the separating prime. Hence the global minimum monomial is unique. Substituting X_i=z^{c_i} yields a nonzero polynomial of degree O(M³p). For every tried prime evaluate all z in an explicit binary extension of order exceeding this degree. Prime sizes and field construction are polynomial in M,s. Thus nonzero is deterministically detected on the promise.

At a nonzero evaluation use shared deletion/contraction to recover an actual original forest. Check the second charge bit on its three original components. If it fails, subtract the ENTIRE found partition block, via three induced Kirchhoff cofactors at the same weights. All later edge restrictions act identically on each term: an included edge across a known partition kills that block, and an included inner edge contracts inside the corresponding tree cofactor; cycles among included edges contribute zero. Cancel the common nonzero product of included weights in all surviving terms. Restricting the original one-bit evaluator uses charge XOR and pushed-forward alternating rows, as before. After subtracting the known blocks the remaining support is exactly the union of the as-yet-unlisted accepted partitions. Repeat for at most s partitions. Every returned YES is physically verifiable. Under the promise, a completed search also decides NO in deterministic polynomial time in the numerical bound s and input length. Without that promise an all-zero search may remain inconclusive. A uniform W algorithm by this route would require controlling sparsity for the entire adaptive set of queries. This approach permits a single accepted partition with arbitrarily many spanning trees inside an attached dense neutral block.
