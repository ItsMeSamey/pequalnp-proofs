# Intrinsic-width construction for the wedge portfolio

For N>1 indexed physical columns the private connectivity function f(X)=r(X)+r(E−X)−r(E) is integer, symmetric, submodular and zero on the empty set. Gaussian elimination evaluates its oracle in polynomial time γ. It equals the dimension of K_X intersect K_(E−X), including repeated and zero physical columns. Small ground sets are immediate.

Korhonen–Oum v2, Section 1, explicitly gives the deterministic exact threshold search: construct a tree of f-width at most k or confirm the optimum exceeds k in 2^O(k) γN^6 log N + 2^O(k²) γN time. Their specialized rank-oracle matroid statement uses precisely f, with leading N^2.5 log²N term instead. Sequential thresholds construct an optimal tree if its width is desired. Their general connectivity-function oracle applies directly. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.04756v2).

A sharper bound for computing W uses an approximate tree. In Oum–Seymour's matroid branch-width algorithm, Corollary 7.2 in the authors' preprint, the proof gives O(8^k N^4) rank-oracle operations. It uses deterministic basis selection, matroid intersection and finite enumerations. Their matroid connectivity convention is f+1. At k=w*+1, with w* the minimum private intersection width, the algorithm must return a tree of their width at most 3k−1, hence private width at most 3w*+1. Trying k=1,2,... until any tree is returned can only improve this guaranteed bound. Charge rank-oracle costs and Gaussian validation of the returned tree. The 2^O(width) exact wedge DP is correct on this approximate tree. Consequently its intrinsic-width branch runs in 2^O(w*+1) poly(input), including construction of the tree. This strictly improves the weaker squared-width exponent of the exact-width construction in wedge-portfolio.md. [Original Corollary 7.2 proof](https://mathsci.kaist.ac.kr/~sangil/pdf/rwdfpt.pdf).

A fixed-branch dovetail with the first-relation pinned solver and semantic conflict DAG thus returns actual compatible wedge generators in the minimum of their own operation bounds, including preprocessing and traceback. The finiteness guarantee does not require a tree supplied by the user. The Python path-DP tests implement neither of the cited tree-finding algorithms.
