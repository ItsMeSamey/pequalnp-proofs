# Circumference of irreducible zero-charge regions

In an arbitrary connected graph with vertex charges in F₂^d and total charge zero, orient nothing and give each edge of a spanning tree the sum of charges on either shore of the cut it defines. The two shore sums agree. This unique vector-valued tree flow has boundary the prescribed vertex charges, by leaf elimination. A zero edge partitions the region into two connected zero-charge vertex sets.

If the induced region contains a simple cycle of length >2^d, choose a tree containing all but one edge of that cycle. Among the at least 2^d tree edges of its path, either there is a zero flow or two edges have the same nonzero flow a. In the latter case extend the flow by zero on the missing edge, then add a on the full cycle. The boundary does not change. Removing one of the two now-zero edges and adding the previously missing edge gives a new tree, in which the second equal edge now has zero flow. Split there. Thus every connected zero-charge region admits a partition into connected zero-charge induced pieces of circumference ≤2^d. For fixed d find a cycle of length ≥k=2^d+1 by enumerating simple paths on k vertices and looking for a closing path between endpoints avoiding the interior and the used path edges. Every large cycle supplies a successful candidate. At each split recompute on induced shores; there are at most n−1 splits.

In one bit the simple shadows of indecomposable regions must be trees. A zero-charge tree is indecomposable exactly when its charge is the parity of vertex degree at every vertex: otherwise some tree edge flow is zero and splits the tree; when all flows are one, every connected bipartition has its unique crossing edge of flow one. Include neutral singletons. In two bits each indecomposable region has circumference at most four. Such regions may be arbitrarily large: take K_{2,t}, all t leaves of charge a, pole charges b and b+(t mod 2)a for independent a,b. A connected zero-charge side cannot consist only of leaves and must contain both poles. No split exists.

In any plane private host split all induced monochromatic components of a compatible face-pair by this procedure. Expand the wedge into disjoint piece pairs, merging leftover pieces into background. Every resulting pair is genuinely jointly feasible, because all pieces are connected and carry zero charge. Therefore disjoint connected zero-charge pairs of circumference ≤4 suffice to generate W for all two-bit plane hosts; the complement must still pass the component charge check. This is an unconditional structural bound and polynomial decomposition of given witnesses. Searching through all such pieces need not be polynomial. Claw-free graphs with one tag bit have only paths as induced trees, yielding the neutral-component algorithm by this argument as well.
