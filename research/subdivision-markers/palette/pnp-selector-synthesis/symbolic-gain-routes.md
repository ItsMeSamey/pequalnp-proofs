# Polynomial physical expansion without a path-length bound

Let an augmented gain-tagged graph on the same vertices contain all physical edges, with activation defined by endpoint potentials g. Assume its active cycle spaces are generated by the chart-active members of a polynomially constructed dictionary D of augmented indexed private relations. A concrete sufficient certificate is bounded induced-hole length b in the four-sheet lift, which supplies all cycles of length at most b.

Give each virtual edge a single chart-dependent binary physical chain R_e(g), with globally fixed polynomially evaluable site selectors. On every chart activating e its selected physical sites must be active and its incidence boundary must be the boundary of e. Use the physical unit for each original edge. Suppose every indexed selector has Boolean degree at most s in the common global vertex-potential bits (or degree at most s on the affine activation domain of its edge). Nonlinear domain restrictions require a separate argument. Do not infer low degree just from a succinct program.

For each ordered or unordered pair C,D of distinct dictionary words, impose simultaneously the tag equalities for all augmented sites in both supports. Discard inconsistent systems; an affine parameterization of the solution set follows by Gaussian elimination. For each solution g route C,D by XOR of the selected chains; call the resulting physical indexed words u(g),v(g). Each lies in L_g. Their coordinates have degree ≤s after affine substitution, and each coordinate of z(g)=u(g) wedge v(g) has degree ≤2s. For n_free Boolean free coordinates, evaluate only at weights ≤min(2s,n_free). These evaluation vectors span all evaluations: every coefficient of the vector-valued squarefree ANF is the XOR of evaluations on subsets of its support, and every value is a sum of the coefficients. Keep linearly independent actual evaluation vectors with their actual indexed pairs. This proves constructive soundness.

Fix any chart g. The simultaneous routing map on its active edges is linear on indexed chains, takes cycles to active physical cycles, and fixes all original physical cycles. Therefore the routed active dictionary spans L_g. Wedges of pairs of these words span its exterior square, and the pairwise affine sampling includes their values in its output span. Taking all charts proves equality with W. The number of samples is O(|D|² (1+2|V|)^(2s)), with additional explicit polynomial costs for generating the certificate, parameterization, routing and Gaussian reductions. This enumerates the paired span without listing a chartwise spanning physical dictionary over all charts.

For an unseeded application, take every connected component of the graph of fully tagged physical links. Choose any path within it for each pair of its vertices; there is no bound on its length. Add each missing endpoint tag as a virtual edge, retaining the path for it. Select one indexed physical representative for each observed link and tag. At a chart, route the virtual edge along its chosen path using the representative for the actual tag on each link. The activation indicator of a fixed link/tag representative is a product of two affine Boolean bits, so the indexed routing coordinates have degree at most two. All physical tracebacks are explicit in polynomial time. If the completed four-sheet lift has fixed hole bound b, the total work is O(M^(2b) (1+2|V|)^4 poly(input)), where M counts augmented sites. Also test the uncompleted graph. Completion and routing use only physical fully tagged links and their vertex labels. A failure of either sufficient test gives no exact-plane verdict.

The bound applies even to a fully tagged long cycle, whose chartwise physical dictionary can have exponentially many selected cycles. A physical loop gives a nontrivial paired-space test on the same family. This degree argument uses only elementary Boolean subset inversion; its application is to actual indexed expansions rather than scalar target tests.
