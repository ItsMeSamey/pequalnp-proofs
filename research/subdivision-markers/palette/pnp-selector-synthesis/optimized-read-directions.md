# Optimizing singleton first bits

Any nonzero linear functional of a singleton link's two-bit endpoint difference can be its first agreement bit. A complementary independent functional checks the full tag. Work in the dual of the direction space of the dense-disabled affine domain A. For link i the options are a_i,b_i,a_i+b_i, dropping constant offsets. If these have rank less than two, at least one choice is zero. Otherwise the options form the nonzero points of a binary plane P_i.

The smallest possible first-read rank q* is the minimum dimension of a subspace meeting every P_i nontrivially. For budget k start at U=0. Choose a plane disjoint from U and branch by adding each of its three possible points. Each increases rank by one, and every feasible extension contains at least one of them. When all planes meet U, extract a member of each intersection and its corresponding original link functional, choosing a second independent tag bit for each. Iterating k gives deterministic O(3^q* poly(input)) recognition and selection, including q*=0. The subsequent 2^q* Gaussian fiber algorithm constructs the actual join or its countercolor, with the earlier degree bound using q*. The optimization concerns this parameter only.

For a strict parameter separation use disjoint bipartite four-cycles. On three links of one cycle set the missing tag set to the kernel of a chosen nonzero λ (two physical tags offered). The remaining crossing link is sparse. The dense disabling constraints force λ of its endpoint difference to zero, while the difference can range freely on ker λ. Use m disjoint gadgets for each of the three λ. Per-link choice gives q*=0; any globally fixed first bit sees 2m independent directions on A. All left and right vertices can respectively lie on two fully tagged internal paths.
