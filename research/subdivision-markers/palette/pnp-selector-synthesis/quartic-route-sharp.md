# The degree-four indexed routing bound can be tight

Take two disjoint copies of a four-vertex gadget with endpoints u,v, an intermediate routing vertex w and an intermediate return vertex y. The physical links uw,wv have one indexed edge at each of the four tags. The physical links uy,yv have only tag zero. Fill uv with a tag-zero virtual edge routed through w, and select the augmented triangle uv,vy,yu in each copy. These augmented words are simultaneously active exactly when c_u=c_y=c_v in each copy. The two relative colors x=c_w+c_u and z in the second copy are independent two-bit free parameters.

In the routed exterior pair, the coordinate indexed by the two designated tag-3 physical edges on the uw links is the product x1*x2*z1*z2. Indeed its transposed term vanishes by disjointness, and the route in either copy selects that physical edge exactly at relative color 3. Thus it vanishes at every assignment of weight at most three about the specified all-zero origin yet is nonzero at weight four. Those lower-weight evaluation vectors cannot span all values of this one routed pair. The observation is coordinate- and pair-specific: another affine origin, a different sampling design, or independent small physical cycles may find a witness. It provides no running-time lower bound for general paired-space decision.
