# Deterministic deficit-parameter bound for bounded-degree chart encoders

Use nonlinear-chart-images.md, writing k=c+2(t+2) for the slice codimension cap and a for encoder degree. A hidden success certificate on input bits b is the indicator that at most k Boolean polynomials A_i(b) of degree ≤a vanish and one polynomial R(b) of degree ≤aj equals one. All A_i come from affine chart constraints and R from the affine retraction/evaluated determinant. Nonempty success gives density at least 2^(−L) with L=min(n,a(k+j)). The constant encoder is handled by testing one chart.

Over the reals, expand this indicator as the product of factors (1+(-1)^A_i)/2 and (1−(-1)^R)/2. Before combining repeated terms the sum of the absolute coefficients is one. Every resulting phase is the sign of a Boolean polynomial of degree at most h=max(1,a max(1,j)); one sums the constraints in the exponent instead of multiplying them. Any explicit distribution fooling every degree-h phase to additive error smaller than 2^(−L) thus hits a successful assignment. The algorithm only enumerates the generator seeds, evaluates F, and checks the actual physical rank witness. It never expands the exponential number of phases or identifies the hidden constraints.

Viola's Theorem 2 gives an explicit degree-h polynomial generator with seed length O(h log(n+1)+h 2^h log(1/epsilon)). Use output-bit fooling error epsilon=2^(−L−3), so the sign error is at most 2epsilon. Deterministic search takes (n+1)^O(h) · 2^O(h 2^h (L+1)) chart checks plus the explicit generator evaluation costs, and separately charges the given section/profile and T per encoder evaluation. At fixed a and j this is 2^O(t+c) times a polynomial with fixed exponent, in contrast to brute Hamming-ball search with exponent proportional to t+c. For the original full-tag section, j≤4,c=0, so arbitrary explicit constant-degree chart encoders preserve a deterministic fixed-parameter bound in the private deficit t. This is a transfer of an existing pseudorandom generator to the scoped input class; an arbitrary-degree circuit encoder remains outside the algorithm.

Primary source: Emanuele Viola, The sum of d small-bias generators fools polynomials of degree d, Theorem 2. https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~viola/papers/d.pdf
