# Eliminating a connected neutral limb in the paired dual space

Let D be a connected supplied plane dual with connected neutral interior I whose outside neighbors are exactly a,b (or only a). Delete I and, in the two-pole case, add an edge ab, retaining the charges on all surviving vertices. This is a plane minor up to redundant parallel edges: contract the connected interior to a and omit loops. Reconstruct any edge tags with the prescribed boundary independently in the resulting dual. The following identity concerns face label spaces modulo constants, identified with actual indexed cuts in the respective graphs. Let J duplicate the bit of a on every removed vertex. Then

W(D) = (exterior² J) W(D') + sum over z in I of e_z wedge L_D[z].

Here L_D[z] is the span of all original relations jointly realizable with e_z, or zero if e_z is impossible. It is polynomially computable for any number of charged vertices by the three-name deletion/component procedure of five-terminal-plane.md; that part of its proof never used the five-terminal bound.

A canonically lifted coloring has every interior vertex equal to a. It connects the two exterior poles through the limb precisely when their colors coincide, exactly the operation of the new edge in D'. All isolated additional interior regions would have zero charge. This proves equivalence of canonical feasibility and shows that actual reduced pairs lift to actual original pairs. Every summand involving e_z is an actual-pair span by definition.

For the reverse inclusion start from any jointly feasible pair of face labels x,y and recolor its interior vertices one by one to the two-bit color at a. If poles have different colors, any old color component carrying a terminal has a connected terminal-spanning anchor outside I: an excursion into I can attach to the outside only at the unique pole of that color. Recoloring cannot split its chosen anchor. If the poles agree, any vertex z still to be changed has a different color and its old component lies wholly inside I, with no terminals. Thus every old charged component admits a connected charge-zero anchor avoiding z. This also covers a single pole. The new labels stay constant on each such anchor, so remain feasible. If the bits change by α,β at z, the difference of wedges is e_z wedge (α y+β x) over F₂. The two vectors in this term are constant separately on the old anchors (e_z is zero there), so they are jointly feasible. Summing all steps proves the identity. This reasoning avoids assuming that arbitrary simultaneous deletion of many neutral faces is safe.

Finding one such limb requires only inspecting components after every choice of one or two poles and verifying that its interior contains no charged vertices. Sequential recomputation charges polynomially many reductions and scalar calls; preserve or compose explicit lift maps for original indexed witnesses. A polynomial-size basis of actual W generators remains sufficient through the recursion. This is a witness-preserving reduction; families with nonneutral interiors or three boundary poles need a different proof.
