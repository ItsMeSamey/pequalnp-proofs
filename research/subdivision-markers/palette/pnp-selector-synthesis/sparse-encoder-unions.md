# Exact chart-image witnesses from unions of encoder monomials

For an encoder represented by explicit squarefree ANFs on n assignment bits, let E be the distinct nonempty monomial supports across its output coordinates, after parity cancellation. Take a physical section and determinant filtration level as in nonlinear-chart-images.md. The hidden success certificate in chart coordinates has degree at most K=j+c+2(t+2), is one at some image point, and is supported on successful physical charts.

Substitute the encoder into that certificate in the Boolean ring. Each surviving assignment monomial is supported on a union of at most K sets in E: each term in a product of at most K coordinate polynomials chooses at most one monomial from each factor. Repeated assignment variables collapse, and parity cancellation removes terms only. Choose an inclusion-minimal supported monomial M. At the assignment with exactly M set, the nonzero polynomial evaluates to one. This also holds for an empty M. Thus searching the distinct unions of at most K sets and checking the original indexed physical trace at each suffices. No computation of the hidden certificate or its monomials is needed.

Breadth-first union generation visits each distinct union once, at its least number of generators. If U_K unions are found, at most |E| times U_K binary ORs are needed, in addition to the physical and encoder checks and the chosen section construction. Memory is polynomial in the input plus U_K. Consequently, for fixed K the solver is polynomial even for unbounded encoder degree. If the subset poset on E has width w, every union is already the union of at most w inclusion-maximal generators; there are at most sum_{i≤w} binomial(|E|,i) unions for every K. An unconditional cap K=|E| also works without any section: the union of all encoder monomial supports contained in a successful assignment reproduces all activated monomials and therefore its exact chart.

This exploits explicit monomial representation. For example the n-way AND encoder has one monomial; the zero and all-ones assignments exhaust its relevant inputs. The fixed-host NP-completeness result for arbitrary circuit encoders does not give hardness for such monomial lists.
