# Planar one-bit tags admit a unique annihilator of arbitrarily high rank

For every even m≥8 there is a finite planar graphic private host with one-bit tags (embedded in the two-bit alphabet), whose W-perp on its first space L is one-dimensional and whose unique nonzero annihilator has rank m−2.

**Dual construction.** Mark m distinct terminal points on the sphere. For each two disjoint terminal pairs draw three disjoint finite embedded trees spanning, respectively, the two pairs and the remaining terminals. For each individual terminal pair draw two disjoint simple closed curves, one through both pair members and one through all remaining terminals. These drawings exist for each partition separately: start with grouped points in disjoint disks and carry them to the desired points by a sphere homeomorphism matching the finite labeled point set. Within the disks draw the required trees or closed curves, using a lens for a two-point curve. Equivalently use piecewise-linear homeomorphisms and curves. Superpose all these finite drawings in general position relative to other drawings, preserving terminal incidences. Insert vertices at every crossing and subdivide if necessary; extend the embedded finite graph to a simplicial sphere triangulation without identifying any original vertices. Its one-skeleton D is 3-vertex-connected. The trees and cycles of each individual drawing retain their within-drawing disjointness. Only the m marked vertices are charged. No bound on construction size besides finiteness is needed.

**Private input.** Let G be the plane dual of D. Take the indexed binary incidence columns of G. Choose a binary dual edge chain b in D whose boundary is the set of charged vertices, by pairing terminals along paths. Give an edge of G the first bit of its dual chain coefficient b and second bit zero. Dual cut vectors identify Q=F₂^{V(D)}/<1> with the complete private cycle space of G. For x,y in Q let S be the union of their primal supports. The tags interpolate on S iff b sums to zero on every primal cycle supported in S; the second chart coordinate may be zero. Each such cycle is a dual cut of a vertex set constant along D edges outside S. These sets are precisely the unions of components of the graph of equal (x,y) color, because an edge is outside S iff both bits agree across it. The b sum on a dual cut is the total charge on the selected side. Consequently x,y are jointly active precisely when each monochromatic component has even charge. The same statement with only x gives feasibility of a single relation.

**First space.** Write e_v for a face singleton in Q. Each neutral e_z is realizable: deleting z leaves D connected with all m charges. For charges i,j take the vertices P of an arc on their prescribed pair cycle; the disjoint complementary terminal cycle connects all other terminals in D−P. All additional components have no charge. Thus 1_P is individually realizable. Subtracting neutral singletons gives e_i+e_j in L. Conversely every realizable x has even terminal weight. Hence L is exactly the even-terminal-weight subspace of Q. Modulo the span N of the neutral e_z, it is the even terminal space E in F₂^m modulo <1>.

**Neutral directions.** Neutral singletons z,w jointly match because D−{z,w} remains connected. Thus their wedges lie in W. For z neutral and i,j charged choose an arc P of the prescribed pair cycle joining i,j and avoiding z. The complementary terminal cycle minus z stays connected and contains the m−2 remaining charges. Assign bits using the disjoint indicators of z and P. The component criterion holds, so e_z wedge 1_P lies in W. Subtract its neutral-neutral wedges to obtain e_z wedge (e_i+e_j). Therefore N wedge L is contained in W, so every annihilator descends to L/N.

**Charged part.** For distinct terminals i,j,k,l, give the two prescribed disjoint pair trees respectively colors (1,0) and (0,1), and all other vertices color (0,0). The third disjoint tree connects all other terminals. Hence the projected W contains every wedge of disjoint terminal pairs. Put f_i=e_i+e_m for i<m, spanning E. For distinct i,j,k<m the pairs f_i+f_j and f_k are disjoint. Thus any annihilator has the same value on (f_i,f_k) and (f_j,f_k). Symmetry shows every off-diagonal value on the f_i is the same. Alternation gives zero diagonals, so at most one dimension survives.

**Rank.** The terminal dot product on even terminal labels is well defined modulo all ones, and vanishes on N. It is alternating. On any jointly active pair its value is the charge parity of the (1,1) class, zero by componentwise evenness. It therefore annihilates W. On E the radical of dot is exactly <1>; on E/<1> its rank is m−2. This is the unique nonzero annihilator on L and refutes generation in bounded rank, including rank four for m=8. It is not a running-time lower bound for graph-aware paired-space algorithms.
