## Recognizing pure zero-gain limbs beyond projective point sets

Guess a nonzero vector q from the ordinary columns or their pairwise sums. In the quotient by span(q), represent all physical columns and pinned/named vectors, explicitly marking each pin, each exceptional gain, and any retained anchor column. Compute the matroid connected components using a physical/marked column basis and the bipartite graph of its fundamental circuits; loops are singleton components. Take any union of unmarked components of ordinary zero-gain elements, writing J for their pre-quotient span and X for the span of the remaining columns and all pins. Because the quotient component spans form a direct sum, W=J∩X is contained in span(q). Compute W by Gaussian elimination. Use no synthetic point if W=0. Otherwise replace the removed set by one zero-gain q, reusing an optional physical zero-labelled copy if available. Select only strict shrinks. Every local trace on W is realizable: zero via the empty set, W via all locally removed columns. Consequently all old named kernels in X are preserved, with traceback to an original physical set. Matroid rank-one torso semantics are standard; the elementary quotient-component detector and the following equality witness lift supply the algorithmic interface.

For the triangle-free example, take three internally vertex-disjoint paths between s and t, one of length two and the others length at least three. Use the full-vertex graphic incidence columns. No triangle-graph neighborhood strictly shrinks: every edge lies on a circuit and its singleton span lies in the exterior span. Guess q=e_s+e_t as the sum of the two length-two-path columns. After quotient each path becomes a circuit, in separate components. Pin q as a named vector. A union of paths contracts to the virtual q with the same trace, even though the original physical set has no such point. Quotient rank operations are polynomial; there are at most n+binom(n,2) candidates per pass, and at most n strict passes. Recompute markers and candidate intersections after each accepted shrink. On promised inputs names and gain outliers are kept as exterior data.

**Trace-only criterion for equality spanning.** More generally let J span arbitrary zero-labelled physical columns, X span the exterior including pins, and W=J∩X. Fix an exterior map φ_X satisfying its pins and let M be its matched physical span on the exterior. A matching-spanning extension exists precisely when some attained trace K=span(S)∩W of a subset of limb columns satisfies K⊆ker(φ_X) and M+K=X.

For necessity, restrict any successful map, let S consist of all matching local columns, and intersect the full matched span with X. For sufficiency greedily enlarge S with physical columns independent modulo W+span(S) until its span L' maps onto J/W. These additions leave the trace K fixed. Since L'∩X=K is killed by φ_X and L'+X=J+X, extending φ by zero on L' defines a linear map on the whole domain. The old exterior matches and the enlarged S now span the domain. The operations are binary Gaussian elimination, polynomial in the explicit matrix and a supplied witnessing S. This works also when pins are not spanned by the external physical columns alone: the exact condition is M+K=X.

Thus any exact trace-preserving shore representative with physical traceback can substitute for such a zero-gain limb in equality-spanning, independently of its private rank or whether boundary points are physical. In particular the finite rank-three catalogue may be used under its own construction and recognition conditions. No guarantee for limbs with arbitrary nonzero gains is asserted here. For any prescribed candidate q, the rank-one instance above is recovered with just the traces zero and W. A width-boundary library does not itself bound the total cost on every input.

### Arbitrary two-bit gains at a physical rank-one boundary

Lift each local column v with label g to (v,g) in J⊕F₂². Let T be the intersection of the lifted span of a subset S with B=W⊕F₂². For a fixed exterior map φ_X with matched exterior span M, a spanning extension exists exactly when some attained T lies in the graph of φ_X on W and M+pr_V(T)=X. Necessity follows by lifting all matched local columns. Conversely graph containment rules out nonzero purely vertical relations, so S already defines a linear map on its physical span and agrees with φ_X on the overlap with X. Greedily add physical columns independent modulo W plus the physical span of S until their projection covers J/W. Any gains on these columns extend the map freely, and independence prevents new boundary traces. Gluing yields the desired map. These operations take polynomial time given a local traceback subset.

For W=span(q) of dimension one, use the prior polynomial three-name trace algorithm to test each of the four lines span((q,g)) in B on the lifted matrix. Reduce the named space to its intersection with the local lifted span if necessary. Retain one indexed physical q with target g per attainable line, at most four labelled columns. If M=X the empty local subset suffices. Otherwise any success requires M+span(q)=X and a matching residual q, exactly equivalent to the attained lifted line test above. Thus the replacement preserves every external equality-spanning test and reconstructs a full original map using the stored local subset. W=0 needs no columns. Keep domain X and pins explicit. This polynomial local rule works for arbitrary two-bit gains at a physical one-dimensional seam without projective or gain-coherence assumptions. It gives no bound on a remaining high-connectivity core.
