# Rank-deficient binary cleaning with a nonzero core is universal at two deletions

Take the full-span encoding of two-gain-universality.md: all original ordinary E have output zero; the two added points r_i=d_i+h_i have the independent labels e_i; the evaluations on d_i are prescribed to be e_i. Add one new independent private coloop z with label zero and replace the first pin d1 by d1+z with the same prescribed value. Every spanning match must use z, which enforces φ(z)=0, so this preserves both directions of the original decision and witness extraction.

Designate z and r1 for deletion. The clean physical columns E plus r2 then have rank exactly one below the total, and their labels are not linear: any interpolant matching all of E is zero on the old ambient space while r2 has nonzero label. Nevertheless their GF(16) comparison restriction N is binary. Normalize on a basis of old E plus z, all with zero labels before homogenizing pins. The pinned discrepancy denominator is nonzero, and the coefficient w_z for the new outside row is 1 from d1+z. The restriction of normalized N on E plus r2 has that bottom row equal to g/p and top rows equal to original A plus w_top g/p. Killing this update by row operations leaves the original binary A and a gain row supported only at r2. Scale the last row to make that entry one. The clean restriction is therefore binary. This explicit transformation shows that the rank-deficient, nonzero-clean two-deletion case already contains every original full four-name plane input; it gives no complexity lower bound for that problem.
