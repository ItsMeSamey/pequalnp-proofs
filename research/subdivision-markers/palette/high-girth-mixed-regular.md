## Restriction-based mixed discovery and preprocessed cores

At a bad four-name branch with deficit t>τ+k choose a physical restriction R such that U⊆span(R) and R/Q is nonregular for an offending Q. Greedily remove sites from E so long as these two conditions survive; this is polynomial and gives an inclusion-minimal, not necessarily minimum-size, restriction. On ≤k queries outside R, delete physical noncoloops and contract physical coloops. Noncoloop deletion leaves full ambient spanning and cannot create complete tags. A coloop outside R is independent of all the rest and U⊆span R, so its contraction removes one independent private direction without changing the other tags. The deficit drops by at most one per query. Thus R survives on a live rank-four nonregular branch with t still above τ. Every strong ≤k backdoor into the τ-mixed terminals intersects R.

For a partially guessed strong set Y test all 2^|Y| assignments. Stop successfully if every assignment is rank-settled, all-regular or at deficit ≤τ+k−|Y|. Otherwise find R and branch on its sites. If each encountered R has size ≤b, search time is (2b)^k·2^{O(τ+k)}poly(input). It succeeds if an exact-τ strong backdoor exists; the returned certificate may use the stated relaxed deficit. Large R makes that capped branch inconclusive. With k+1 site-disjoint protected U-spanning restrictions, possibly with different offending quotients, the same adversary prevents every adaptive depth-k tree when t>τ+k: at least one restriction remains untouched on its path.

Fixed k,τ,g≥5 admit preprocessed counterexamples to bounded local restriction size. Fix U,H in V=F₂^r. Sample k+1 independent blocks of 12r uniform columns. With probability tending to one projections modulo U have girth ≥g by the union bound Σ_{j=1}^{g−1}binom(12(k+1)r,j)2^{4−r}. The host is round by the union bound 2^{2r}(3/4)^{12(k+1)r} on covering hyperplane pairs. Each block spans V with high probability. Its first 6r columns span V/H with high probability; contract r−5 independent projected columns, leaving rank three. The independent last 6r contain all seven projected nonzero points with failure probability ≤7(7/8)^{6r}. Each block thus has a nonregular H quotient.

There is no physical named site, series or parallel pair, nontrivial rank-one shore or projective triangle; the first follows from projected girth, the others from roundness and girth. Since U has rank four, any augmented boundary-rank ≤3 shore is nonspanning, its exterior spans, and its rank is at most three. Girth ≥5 forces such a shore to have at most three elements. The physical deficit is r−4, and no <g-element restriction even spans U. The disjoint blocks certify no depth-k mixed tree: the all-deletion path leaves an intact spanning nonregular block. This is a residual NO for the tree predicate, not a lower bound for discovering trees on YES inputs or an exact-plane NO. The easier series-Fano plus four physical named axes example has obvious series and named-axis preprocessing: positions with identical coordinates on every dependency of the augmented physical/named matrix can be replaced by their XOR, with all sites of a series class used for inverse traceback.

### High projected girth transfers an adaptive mixed tree to regular backdoor search

Assume the physical matroid projected modulo U has girth strictly above k+2, and write d=dim(V/U). For any ≤k physical assignments, two distinct surviving nonzero private directions cannot coincide after contracting the mandatory sites: such an equality would give a nonempty original binary dependency on at most |C|+2 sites. A surviving direction cannot vanish either. On a still-four-name branch with physical spanning, the ordinary private rank is d−|C|≥d−k and there are no fully tagged directions. Thus for d>τ+k a deficit≤τ terminal cannot occur in any depth-k mixed tree. For d≤τ+k the chart solver is already 2^{O(τ+k)}poly.

Under this large-d promise, any adaptive depth-k mixed tree has only rank/death or all-regular leaves. Its at most 2^k−1 distinct split sites form a strong backdoor into those terminal classes. Each assignment to their union follows some adaptive path. On a further assignment extending the path, an impossible full-size branch stays impossible, a reduced-name branch stays reduced or impossible, and any still-four-name protected quotient of an all-regular leaf is a physical minor of its corresponding regular quotient. Therefore the existing O(22^s poly) strong regular-backdoor solver with s=2^k−1 finds a verifiable answer on every such promised tree instance. It can be run without testing girth first: any certified success remains sound outside the promise. This solves the exact-plane predicate on the high-girth mixed-depth promise, in FPT(k,τ) time. It is not a recognition algorithm for projected girth or minimum adaptive tree depth. A failure of this regular search alone does not refute a general mixed tree.
