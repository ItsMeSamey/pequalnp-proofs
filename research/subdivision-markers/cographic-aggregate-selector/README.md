# Cographic aggregate selectors with fixed checksum dimension

Let G be a connected finite simple graph, n≥1. Let D represent its cographic matroid over F₂ with ker D equal to the graph cut space. Each edge has an arbitrary matrix T_e:F₂²→F₂^k. The physical selector has one aggregate root and k checksum roots, and asks for the exact line spanned by the first root after contracting some ordinary columns. Its columns and the binary-matrix equivalence are in the [aggregate selector](../aggregate-flow-selector/README.md). The vertex colors prescribed during the algorithm add no roots to the representation.

**Theorem.** For each fixed k the cographic selector has a deterministic polynomial-time decision and witness algorithm on G. This includes general vertex checksum matrices H_v with zero sum, precisely the divergences of edge tags on a connected graph.

**Predicate.** The pair of cut words has edge value χ_u+χ_v, with χ:V→F₂² defined up to a constant. Write S(χ)=Σ_v H_vχ_v, where H_v=Σ_{e incident to v}T_e. The zero edges are independent in the cographic matroid exactly when deleting them preserves connectivity. Acceptance therefore asks for a coloring of checksum t that is proper on some spanning tree. For the divergence converse, choose a tree, put the sum of the vertex charges in each child subtree on its parent edge, and set all other edge tags to zero.

**Four-coloring control.** A coloring proper on every edge is an ordinary four-coloring. With T_e=I₂ and t=0 the selector accepts every connected simple Eulerian graph. Alternate colors on a spanning tree. Each vertex has zero charge since its degree is even. In particular the selector accepts K₅. Four-colorability remains NP-complete on these graphs. Add a universal vertex to a simple graph to reduce three-colorability to four-colorability on connected graphs. Pair the odd-degree vertices arbitrarily and add a two-edge path through one fresh vertex for each pair. This preserves simplicity. Each new vertex can avoid both endpoint colors, and each old odd degree becomes even. Membership in NP follows by direct coloring verification. [Garey, Johnson and Stockmeyer, Some simplified NP-complete graph problems](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975%2876%2990059-1)

## Reducing checksum feasibility to a bounded precoloring

Maintain a subspace W of checksum characters and a vertex set U, initially zero and empty. For d=|U| put L=10(k+d+1). While some λ outside W has at most L vertices outside U with H_vᵀλ≠0, include all those vertices in U and add λ to W's span. Every λ in W vanishes on the charges outside U. At most k iterations occur, and each grows d to at most 11d+10(k+1). Hence d≤(k+1)(11^k−1). At termination, each λ outside W has more than L nonzero charges outside U.

Enumerate precolorings p:U→F₂². Keep only those satisfying λ·t=Σ_{v∈U}(H_vᵀλ)·p_v for a basis of W and admitting a spanning tree with a proper coloring extending p. The instance accepts exactly when at least one such p survives. Necessity follows from the predicate. We prove sufficiency on **any** witnessing tree T for this p.

Sample a uniform tree-proper coloring by choosing the root color uniformly and each parent-to-child difference independently and uniformly among the three nonzero colors. Write P for the probability of the prescribed U colors. If d=0 then P=1. If d≥1 the minimal subtree joining U, with unpinned degree-two vertices suppressed, has at most 2d vertices and 2d edges. Fix the colors on the retained sites from one extension. For a segment of length j the transition probability from color a to color b equals 1/4+(1[a=b]−1/4)(−1/3)^j. Every positive value is at least 2/9. Root at a retained site and multiply over disjoint edge segments. Thus P≥(1/4)(2/9)^{2d} whenever p extends on T.

For λ outside W, expand the pin indicator I_p in the characters of the 2d pin bits. The absolute Fourier coefficients sum to one. Each resulting term averages a coloring character with local coefficients a_v=H_vᵀλ off U and arbitrary coefficients on U. If Σ_v a_v≠0 the root average vanishes. If it is zero, write b_e for the sum of a_v in the child subtree of e. The expectation is (−1/3)^s, where s counts edges with b_e≠0. At each vertex the incident b_e sum to a_v, including the root because the total is zero. More than L vertices outside U have a_v≠0, hence s≥L/2. Consequently |E[I_p (−1)^{λ·S}]|≤3^{−L/2}. For λ in W the same expectation is exactly (−1)^{λ·t}P by the pin check.

Fourier inversion bounds the probability of both pins and checksum t below by 2^(−k)(|W|P−2^k 3^(−L/2)), strictly positive with the displayed L, also for d=0. This proves sufficiency without randomizing the algorithm.

## Constructing the tree and the witness

For a fixed number d≥2 of pinned vertices, enumerate up to d−2 additional distinct branch vertices of a minimal Steiner tree, all tree shapes on the resulting sites and all four colors on the additional sites. Leaves belong to U and additional sites have degree at least three. For d=0 or 1 any spanning tree extends the pins. A segment between different colors may have any positive length. A segment between equal colors must have length at least two. Every permitted path has a four-color extension with those endpoints. Delete any physical edge directly joining equal-colored ends of a shape segment, since it cannot serve a different segment.

Test for internally vertex-disjoint paths realizing the shape, using one terminal clone per incidence at a site and a single subdivision vertex per physical edge. Each clone is an endpoint of a demanded path, so it cannot occur internally. Subdivision vertices prevent reuse of edges. Each segment connects distinct sites and cannot traverse both halves at the same site. The paths glue into a tree with the precoloring extension, and a forest extension adds the remaining vertices. Conversely any extending spanning tree supplies an enumerated topology. Fixed-terminal undirected disjoint paths admits a deterministic polynomial decision algorithm. A solution follows by deleting unnecessary edges. [Robertson and Seymour, Graph minors XIII, The disjoint paths problem](https://doi.org/10.1006/jctb.1995.1006)

On the resulting T, use dynamic programming over rooted subtrees, tracking the vertex color and k-bit subtotal and respecting pins. Differently colored parent/child choices combine by XOR. Stored choices give an explicit coloring, which converts to the physical one-or-three-column witness. All tree tests and dynamic programs take polynomial time for each fixed k. The number of guessed branch vertices depends on k, so the stated bound is XP. The reference checker enumerates tiny spanning trees at the precoloring interface in place of a disjoint-paths implementation.
