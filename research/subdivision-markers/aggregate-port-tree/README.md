# Mixed graphic and cographic pieces for aggregate selectors

A fixed-checksum aggregate matrix asks for two words in ker D of total linear checksum t whose common zero coordinates form an independent set of D. Its physical realization uses k+1 named columns for a k-bit checksum. Graphic and cographic algorithms solve this predicate in deterministic polynomial time for every fixed k on a single presented graph. They extend jointly to the following supplied class with unbounded piece width and decomposition depth. [Physical predicate and graphic solver](../aggregate-flow-selector/README.md) · [Cographic solver](../cographic-aggregate-selector/README.md)

## Supplied representation

The input includes a tree of binary vector spaces V_i with a local matrix at each node. Every column is either real or a virtual column assigned to one incident tree edge. Each edge ij has a common space U_ij and explicit injective embeddings into the two adjacent spaces. Each nonzero direction of U_ij is represented by one virtual column at each endpoint. Virtual slots for distinct ports are disjoint, even when represented directions coincide. The global host D consists of the real columns in the quotient of the direct sum of the V_i by the identifications along tree edges. Since the diagram is a tree, each component of a cut injects and the two sides intersect exactly in the indicated U_ij. These claims follow inductively from the pushout of two vector spaces along injective maps. Port subspaces incident to the same node may overlap.

Each full local matrix, including its virtual slots, is either graphic with a supplied graph or cographic with a supplied connected simple graph and the represented column correspondence. Row-coordinate changes may be supplied or found by elimination. Ordinary 2-sums have a single represented nonzero port vector. A supplied binary triangle sum has all three nonzero directions of a two-dimensional interface whenever its representation obeys the stated quotient description.

**Theorem.** For fixed checksum dimension k, maximum port dimension s and maximum number b of ports incident to one node, there is a deterministic polynomial-time decision and witness algorithm on these inputs. Finite pieces of bounded size may also be included using direct enumeration. The polynomial degree may depend on k,s,b.

## Boundary table

Root the tree. A subtree with boundary U has two total vector imbalances f,g in U, a k-bit physical checksum z, and an independent set of common zero columns. For each f,g,z and test subspace K of U, store any one such witness whose zero-column span misses K except at zero. Store its actual trace L, the intersection of that span with U, by Gaussian elimination. At the root use U=0 and zero imbalances. Table size depends only on s and k.

To construct a parent entry, choose one saved witness from every child and let their actual traces be L_i. The torso must supply local imbalances adding to the specified parent imbalances after including those of the children, and local checksum equal to the parent target minus the child scores. In its vector space append, as one **indexed list** of zero-column probes, a basis of each L_i and a basis of the requested parent test K. Require the real torso zero columns and this entire list to be independent together. Repeated or jointly dependent vectors across different ports are retained.

To verify this test, take a basis of each child's zero-column span consisting of a basis of L_i and independent directions modulo its own port. The directions outside the port cannot participate in a relation involving the torso or other children. Eliminating them reduces joint independence, including independence from K, to the displayed local test. All imbalances and tags add linearly.

For completeness fix a feasible choice for one table entry. Replace one child at a time. For child i take as its test space the intersection of U_i with the span of all current outside zero columns and the parent test K. The original child avoids this intersection, so a table witness with the same imbalances and score exists and also avoids it. That substitution preserves joint independence. Repeating over children yields a tuple in the enumeration, for which the original torso colors still work. This proves the table invariant by induction.

## Compiling a torso query

For every represented virtual direction v, replace its column by two parallel copies. For the directions in a chosen port basis prescribe a two-bit color sum equal to the two desired imbalance coefficients at that basis coordinate. Prescribe zero sum on the remaining directions. Both copies can receive a nonzero color in every case, since any two-bit vector is the sum of two nonzero two-bit vectors. Pick one such pair of colors and pin each copy to its chosen color. These copies transmit the imbalance and impose no zero-column condition. For each probe vector add another parallel copy of its represented direction, pinned to color zero. The probe list includes all child traces and the parent test at once.

Enforce each pinned color using two extra linear checksum coordinates unique to that copy. Give real columns their original tags in the first k bits, and put zero original tag on virtual copies. This single aggregate query checks conservation, local checksum, all pins, and exactly the required independence. For b ports of dimension ≤s, at most b(2(2^s−1)+s) copies are pinned. The local checksum dimension is at most k+2b(2(2^s−1)+s). These coordinates belong to the algorithm's local subqueries. The original physical instance has k+1 names.

Parallel extension of graphic columns is graph edge duplication. For cographic columns replace the corresponding graph edge by a path of the required length. The cut colors along the path sum to the original edge color and realize every prescribed summand with that sum. Algebraically the new cographic representation is the required parallel extension. Subdivision preserves simplicity on the stated connected simple inputs. Both single-piece solvers therefore apply. Recover the real torso colors from their returned solutions, join to the saved child witnesses, and compute the actual outgoing trace. All associated spaces and physical witnesses have polynomial size.

## Controls

Run `python check.py` and `python clones.py` beside the sibling `unseeded-root-states` elimination helper. The first checker forms full quotient matrices independently, exhausts their color assignments, compares decision spectra with one-representative-per-test tables, and verifies every recovered coloring on the quotient. It checked 101 tree diagrams and 234 recovered targets, including a three-child star whose port directions have a joint dependence. The second checker compared the explicit checksum and parallel-copy compilation with direct local conditions on 5,120 pinned assignments, of which 56 passed. The local enumerators are finite controls. The theorem uses the cited polynomial-time graphic and cographic routines at those interfaces.

The algorithm permits arbitrarily long alternating sequences of high-width graphic and cographic pieces. A high-degree torso can have too many pins for the local bound. A bounded-degree presentation for every regular matroid and a general algorithm for arbitrary fixed named kernels remain separate questions.
