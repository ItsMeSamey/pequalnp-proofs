# Decision branches from failed contractions

## An exponential conflict-deletion tree on NO inputs

Use k internally disjoint length-two A paths and k such B paths between s,t; all interiors distinct. Put a binary edge score of odd parity on each A path, even on each B path. The requested scores require a primal cycle of odd score and dual functional d(s+t)=1. Add two independent moment tests on bridge columns from extra leaves, both targeted zero, to make combined marker rank four. Neither pure side attains this target. A signed circuit is exactly one A path with one B path. The vector s+t lies in its span and is the sole incompatible dual moment combination that can lie there since the two bridge moments have unique coordinates outside. Therefore the input is NO.

In the following natural backtracking algorithm choose any signed circuit, detect the incompatible moment u in its span, write it as a sum of a minimal subset R of that circuit, and branch on deleting each separate element of R. This branching is complete since a viable circuit cannot contain R. Stop if a feasible contraction is found or no signed circuit remains. For our example either choice of representation R is an entire two-edge path. Both child branches destroy just that one path, and dangling edges never enter later circuits. As long as the two path types survive there is a signed circuit. Hence all root-to-leaf paths have length at least k, and the binary tree has at least 2^k leaves regardless of its circuit choices. The analysis is specific to the stated branching and stopping rules; merging siblings after simplification, exploiting separations, or replacing edge deletion by vertex conflicts defeats this lower bound.

## Adaptive conflict resolution for split graphic scores

On a graph suppose the desired primal score is one bit (required nonzero), and r independent dual moment tests prescribe a linear functional on a subspace of dimension r. A successful primal support contains a signed circuit C. Such C is compatible with a dual if and only if its span avoids every test-vector combination that must evaluate to one. There are at most 2^(r-1) such bad combinations. In the full even vertex incidence representation, the span of C consists of all even vectors supported on C's vertices. If the circuit fails on u, branch by deleting one vertex in supp(u). Any viable circuit misses some such vertex. Once the deletion is made every future circuit avoids this u. The number of unresolved distinct bad vectors therefore strictly decreases on every adaptive branch. Finding a signed circuit or ruling out its existence takes polynomial time by testing fundamental cycles. The tree has depth at most 2^(r-1), size O(n^(2^(r-1))) for n>=2, and every successful leaf supplies C and a dual functional by linear equations in the original graph. For zero prescribed functional the pure case suffices. This elementary solver is for split scores. It proves no complexity bound for arbitrary binary matroids or mixed score spaces.

## Compression of a nonregular part with small terminal boundary

Suppose an arbitrary binary representation consists of an ordinary graphic incidence list plus arbitrarily many other columns in a supplied r-dimensional space U of even vertex vectors. The total marker score has b coordinates. For each of the 2^r restrictions L on U permit only exceptional columns z with L(z)=0. Compute the span of their pairs (z,edge-label) and iterate at most 2^(r+b) possible pairs (w,alpha), recovering one exceptional selection for each by Gaussian elimination. The required graphic edge subset has boundary w and adjusts the primal score by alpha; retain the original dual moments and enforce d restricted to U equals L by r additional dual moments.

Given a pair with t terminals in supp(w), guess colors for its terminals in at most 2^t ways. When both classes are even, add one new dummy edge per pair of same-color terminals, pairing arbitrarily within each guessed class. Require every dummy edge selected by giving it a separate primal marker targeted one. The remaining target now is a homogeneous graphic mixed instance with at most b+r+t/2 marker rows. Any original solution gives a guess by its actual potential colors, which have even terminal counts on each shore, and therefore supplies a solution of the homogeneous instance. Conversely the forced dummy edges change boundary exactly by w and the new constraints leave every old selected edge disjoint from the reconstructed dual. Remove dummy edges and append the stored exceptional selection. The guessed colors need not be explicitly enforced: the converse works whenever the chosen pairs have equal actual potential, which the dummy edges ensure. Parallel edges are permissible.

Thus at most 2^(2r+b+t) bounded-marker homogeneous graphic oracle calls suffice when t uniformly bounds all considered boundary sizes. The algorithm uses the established fixed-marker graphic solver as a black box; it does not assume a bounded-size T-join for large arbitrary boundary support. An alternative promise is that every YES input has a compatible state with t small; one may discard larger states for that promise problem.

### Dimension potential and decision without branch repetition

A stronger potential is the dimension of the test-vector span vanishing on all deleted vertices. Each chosen vertex annihilates the previously surviving bad vector on the next step, in the sense that the vector no longer belongs to this subspace; dimension therefore drops. Depth is at most r. A more direct procedure normalizes the dual potential to zero on a successful circuit. Prescribing its r dual moments on the remaining vertices is a binary linear problem whose right-hand side can be realized by at most r pivot vertices. Enumerate potential-one sets through size r, then test feasible primal scores by linear algebra on their killed edges. This also works for any solution whose entire primal support lies in one potential shore. To apply to four possibly mixed markers with rank-one pure primal image, first test the pure dual target, then prescribe the target minus the unique nonzero primal score. If feasible its dual rank is at most three, since that unique score lies outside the dual image.

### Multiple monochromatic blocks

Let the global primal score have rank p. It suffices that each biconnected block has no two vertex-disjoint cycles of linearly independent primal scores. Two polynomially checkable sufficient conditions per block are that its score rank is at most one, or that deleting some one vertex leaves zero cycle score rank. The latter allows arbitrary score rank on the original block. Include single-edge loops as individual blocks when present.

Decompose any feasible primal support into circuits. Retain a linearly independent subfamily of at most p with unchanged total score, still killed by the same dual. Every represented block uses only one potential color, since cycles in different colors are vertex-disjoint. Enumerate up to p represented blocks and their colors and guess the potential on their shared articulation vertices, at most binom(p,2) such vertices. Define a baseline potential using the guessed values there, assigned block colors elsewhere on the chosen blocks and zero outside. Some guess agrees with the actual potential at every retained circuit vertex, even if a shared articulation belongs to a retained circuit in only one of the two blocks. For each possible primal score fix the prescribed residual dual moments. Any discrepancies from the baseline can be supplied by at most r vertices outside all retained circuits, by a basis choice in the r-dimensional moment space. Enumerate every flip set through size r. For each resulting potential, solve for a cycle with the required score on its killed edges. This verifies disjointness and gives the original witness. With N vertices and m edges the candidate count is O(2^b (1+m)^p 2^(p+binom(p,2)) (1+N)^r) for fixed p,r. In particular rank two allows two different-color circuits in separate blocks of arbitrary width.
