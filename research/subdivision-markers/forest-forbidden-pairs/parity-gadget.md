# Forest joins with forbidden pairs on graphs of treewidth two

It is NP-complete to decide whether a connected simple planar bipartite graph of maximum degree three and treewidth at most two has a forest in which every vertex has odd degree and no specified forbidden pair of edges is selected together. The forbidden pairs are pairwise disjoint. There are no prescribed edges.

## Reduction

Start with the direct matching-guard encoding of a ternary CNF, allowing repeated variables and signs. For a variable of occurrence count t≥1, form a t-edge cycle, allowing a loop for t=1 and parallel edges for t=2. Assign its consecutive edge bits to literal falsity. At the vertex between successive bits demand the XOR of the two occurrence polarities, including the cyclic last-to-first transition. Its total demand is even. Each clause gets a block of three parallel edges between two odd-demand vertices. Its choice bits have odd sum. For each occurrence forbid choosing both its variable edge and clause edge, giving 3m disjoint forbidden pairs. A satisfying assignment can choose one true occurrence per clause. Conversely the selected falsity bits determine consistent truth values, and every odd choice set contains a true literal. [Guard equations](../3sat.md)

1. Keep the blocks disjoint. Replace each nonloop edge by a private two-edge path and each loop by a four-cycle through the original vertex. New vertices have zero demand, so all segments of each original edge have the same bit. Label only one representative segment per occurrence edge for its forbidden pair.
2. Make a path on one new zero-demand vertex per block and attach each to a private subdivision vertex in its block by a single bridge. Since the block's total demand is even, summing its boundary forces its bridge absent. Parity on the remaining path forces every spine edge absent. Attachments have degree at most three. Each block admits a bipartite coloring, whose colors may be flipped to obey its bridge. Variable cycles have treewidth two. A clause block has a tree decomposition with both endpoints and one of the three private middle vertices per bag. Joining via bridges preserves width at most two. The blocks and resulting graph are planar and simple.
3. Replace every vertex of zero demand by a center and three leaves, assigning each old incidence to a distinct leaf. Every vertex now has odd demand. For a leaf with external bit b (zero for unused leaves), its inner edge must equal 1+b. The center's degree is odd exactly when the external sum is even. This proves both projection and unique extension for joins. Forbidden pairs refer to the surviving external edges, never to a star edge. The replacement amounts to subdividing existing incidences and adding pendant leaves. Thus it preserves planarity, simplicity, maximum degree three, and width at most two. Give the leaves the old color and the center the opposite color. The old odd vertices are even in number and every replaced vertex contributes four vertices, so the final vertex count is even.

At any stage a selected cycle can be deleted without changing degree parities or breaking the exclusions. Thus every admissible join contains an admissible forest. Projecting a final forest need only give a parity-feasible old join to recover the assignment. Forest multiplicities need not be preserved. The graph has linear size, and a proposed forest is directly checkable. The host width measures graph edges, excluding the nonlocal forbidden-pair constraints. Even contradictory repeated units (x,x,x) and (¬x,¬x,¬x) have an ordinary join here, while the pairs exclude every solution.

The independent proof review checked the reduction in both directions, including loops, the all-odd gadget, edge IDs, the handshake parity, and all graph restrictions. No novelty claim is made.
