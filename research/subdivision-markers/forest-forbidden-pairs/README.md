# All-odd forest joins with disjoint forbidden edge pairs

It is NP-complete to decide whether a connected simple planar bipartite graph of maximum degree three and treewidth at most two has a forest with odd degree at every vertex and at most one member of every specified forbidden edge pair. The forbidden pairs are disjoint. The reduction uses no prescribed edge. Every unconstrained all-odd subgraph on its outputs is a disjoint union of single edges and three-leaf stars.

## Construction

Take a nonempty ternary CNF with m clauses on active variables, recording each of its signed literal occurrences. Repetitions are allowed. For each variable of occurrence count d, form a simple cycle of length 2 max(d,2). Give each occurrence a private pair of consecutive edges in alternating positions. Odd demand forces one of the two alternating perfect matchings. Name these the true and false states. Designate the edge in the occurrence pair selected exactly when its literal is false.

For each clause use three internally vertex-disjoint paths of length three between the same two endpoints. At the internal degree-two vertices the selected bits on one path must be (w,1+w,w) over GF(2). The endpoints have odd degree exactly when the three choice bits have odd sum. For each occurrence forbid the pair consisting of its designated variable edge and the first segment of its clause path. Every designated edge is used exactly once, even for identical literals.

There are b variable and clause blocks, each of even order. Make an all-odd backbone path on 2b new vertices. Attach one degree-two vertex of block i by one bridge to backbone vertex 2i−1 (one-based). Summing the degrees modulo two over that even-order block forces its unique bridge absent. The path then has a unique odd subgraph, its perfect matching. This justifies using the local degree equations even at the attached vertices. Physical assignments project to truth states and clause choices, and those bits extend uniquely.

The clause subgraph consists of disjoint single edges when exactly one w is set and two disjoint three-leaf stars when all three are set. The pairs ensure that w can be one only for a true occurrence. Every satisfiable source formula has an admissible perfect matching, obtained by choosing one true occurrence per clause. Conversely, odd degree requires a true occurrence in every clause. For a satisfying assignment with rⱼ true occurrences in clause j, the number of admissible forests is ∏ⱼ2^(rⱼ−1); the number of admissible perfect matchings is ∏ⱼrⱼ. Unsatisfying assignments have zero admissible forests.

The host is connected and simple. Attaching bridges to degree-two vertices preserves maximum degree three. Both kinds of block are planar and bipartite. Separate color reversals and embeddings of blocks accommodate the bridges. A clause block subdivides edges of K₂,₃, which has width-two bags consisting of the two degree-three vertices and one other vertex. Cycles, subdivisions, bridges and the new path preserve width at most two. The graph has linear size and even vertex count. Every vertex has odd demand. A candidate forest is directly checkable. ## Counting and stronger host locality

For decision, first replace each occurrence of a variable by a separate clone. For d≥2 occurrences impose (¬xᵢ∨xᵢ₊₁∨xᵢ₊₁) in a directed cycle of the clones. This forces equality with a unique extension of each original assignment. Each clone appears at most four times. Give the vertices of each block and its two path vertices consecutive positions. A cluster has at most ten vertices; graph edges are internal or join consecutive clusters. Thus the host has a supplied order of bandwidth at most 19. Forbidden pairs may cross arbitrarily far in this order.

For exact counting, add one tautology (ℓ,ℓ,¬ℓ) for each unit of polarity imbalance, with deficient polarity ℓ. There are no new variables. The M resulting clauses have balanced literals and M is even. On every assignment T=3M/2 literal occurrences are true. Hence the exact admissible forest count is 2^(M/2) times #SAT of the original active variables. This proves #P-hardness under a single-call polynomial metric reduction, and guessing and verifying the edge set gives membership in #P. If balancing follows cloning, each clone occurs at most 4+3·4=16 times, clusters have size at most 34 and bandwidth at most 67. Inactive declared bits are handled by an extra factor of two each. The constant multiplier concerns forest counts.

The randomized prescribed-edge oracle tests positive pins. These 3m forbidden pairs carry SAT across the blocks even at zero prescribed edges. Removing them leaves a matching on every output, including those of unsatisfiable formulas. An independent reviewer verified both directions of the construction and the graph restrictions. No novelty claim is made.

Run the [graph-level checker](check.py) with Python and NetworkX. It enumerates all all-odd subgraphs of each emitted graph from an independently constructed spanning-tree solution and fundamental cycles. Across 110 graphs it checked all 24,040 physical all-odd subgraphs, including three source formulas that admit no compatible subgraph. Each assignment's forest and perfect-matching counts agreed with direct clause evaluation. The checker also verifies that no selected edge joins two degree-three centers, confirming the star-forest claim, and checks the physical graph restrictions.
