# Parsimonious first-true selection for forbidden-pair forests and perfect matchings

Given a ternary CNF, one can construct in polynomial time a connected simple planar bipartite graph of maximum degree three and treewidth at most two, with disjoint forbidden pairs of edge IDs and odd demand at every vertex. Truth assignments satisfying the CNF are in bijection with admissible all-odd subgraphs. Every such admissible subgraph is a perfect matching and hence a forest. Thus counting either admissible forests or admissible perfect matchings is #P-complete under a parsimonious reduction even on this class. There are no required edges. The graph restrictions apply to its physical edges; pair exclusions are supplied separately.

Use the variable cycles and bridge-connected all-odd path from the [single-choice construction](README.md). In each ordered clause of three literals, the choice bit wᵢ for position i (1≤i≤3) denotes that literal i is the first true occurrence in the clause. Its required tests are “literal i is true” and “every earlier literal is false.” This gives i tests. Use three internally vertex-disjoint paths of lengths 3,3,5 between common endpoints. Along one such path the selected bits necessarily alternate (wᵢ,1+wᵢ,…,wᵢ). Assign a distinct odd-position edge to each of its i tests; one of the first path's two odd-position edges remains unpaired. The endpoint parity requires odd many wᵢ.

On a variable involved in D tests, make a cycle of length 2 max(2,D) and give each test its own adjacent pair of edge slots. Its two alternating perfect matchings encode truth. For each test forbid its assigned clause edge together with the edge of its variable slot selected exactly when the test fails. Slots are distinct even if the same underlying literal appears repeatedly or a candidate position has contradictory tests. Each edge appears in at most one forbidden pair. Unused declared input variables can be represented by independent four-cycles in the same backbone construction, supplying their two choices; if the input has neither variables nor clauses, return a single edge. An empty clause can instead be handled by a fixed zero-witness instance (for example the construction on two opposing repeated unit clauses).

Every block has even order. Attach a private degree-two vertex of each by a single bridge to a separate odd-indexed vertex of an all-odd path on twice as many vertices as blocks. Every bridge is forced absent by the demand parity of its block, and the path's matching is forced. A cycle's local state now recovers a unique assignment bit. In a satisfied clause only the least true index passes all its tests. Odd parity forces its selection, with every path-edge value then fixed. If the clause is unsatisfied every position fails a test. Conversely these local choices give a unique admissible physical solution for every satisfying assignment. With one active path, the endpoints and all interiors have degree one. With all three paths active in an unconstrained subgraph, the endpoints form two three-leaf stars and other selected edges are isolated, since every path has length at least three.

Each clause block has ten vertices and is a subdivision of K₂,₃. Width two, simplicity, planar embedding, bipartiteness and maximum degree three follow as in the cited construction. Clauses of width one or two may first be padded by repeating a literal without changing an assignment. To supply bounded bandwidth as well, introduce one clone per occurrence and, for d≥2 occurrences, force all d clones equal by cyclic clauses (¬xᵢ∨xᵢ₊₁∨xᵢ₊₁). This step is parsimonious. Each clone has at most four occurrences, each occurrence features in at most three tests, hence D≤12. Variable blocks have at most 24 vertices, and a cluster consisting of a block and its two backbone vertices has size at most 26. Order the clusters consecutively. All physical edges stay in one cluster or pass between successive clusters, giving bandwidth at most 51. Exclusions can still join distant clusters. This proof uses no polarity balancing and no division of counts.

The independent audit checked all 2,048 subsets of the theta block's eleven physical edges and all 256 signed ternary-clause/assignment combinations on two variables. The [whole-graph checker](check_first_true.py) uses the separately implemented boundary enumerator in [check.py](check.py). Across 120 graphs, it examined 23,875 all-odd subgraphs, checked that all 577 admissible subgraphs were perfect matchings, and compared the assignment counts separately with Boolean truth, including an unsatisfiable input, repeated literals and unused variables. Run python check_first_true.py with NetworkX in the directory containing the companion checker. No novelty or unrestricted complexity-class claim is made.
