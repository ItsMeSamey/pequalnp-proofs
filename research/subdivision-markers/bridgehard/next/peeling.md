# Direct native compilation of 3CNF via clause pivots

Assume n variables, m clauses, and three distinct variables per clause. Given an ordering, collect clauses by their latest variable and choose one in each nonempty group. Flip each chosen variable's coordinate so that its chosen literal is true at coordinate zero. A chosen clause is z⇒(a∨b), where a and b are truth indicators of earlier literals. Place a fresh bit u immediately before z with single-literal guard u⇒a; place the single affine guard z⇒(b+u). Existential elimination of u recovers exactly the clause: at z=0 use u=0, while z=1 is possible for b=1 via u=0 and for b=0,a=1 via u=1. Other variables have a constant-true guard. Leave the unchosen clauses as terminals. All literal widths in single guards are at most two. This linear-size construction preserves every original satisfying assignment under projection and uses n+r decisions and m−r terminals, where r variables claim a clause. If occurrence is bounded by Δ, r≥ceil(m/Δ).

Let σ be the smallest number of seed variables from which all variables can be reached by repeatedly adjoining the sole unknown of a clause. The maximum r over variable orders equals n−σ. Indeed take the unclaimed variables as seeds and process the claimed variables in order. Conversely take a smallest seed, put it first and follow the order of additions; each adjoined variable claims the triggering clause. This optimum concerns direct native compilations of the stated form. Optimizing the seed is unnecessary to compile any chosen order.

For an unconditional barrier, sample three independent uniform partitions of n variables into triples, furnishing n clauses and degree three. Pick δ=1/10000 and let n range over multiples of 120000 so t=δn and s=3t/4 are integers. For any t-set and specified s clause positions the probability all those triples lie within the set is at most δ^(3s): per partition, draw distinct positions without replacement. The union bound for any offending set is at most

    binom(n,t) binom(n,s) δ^(3s)
    ≤ [e (4e/3)^(3/4) sqrt(δ)]^t.

This tends to zero. Repeated triples across layers also have expected count O(1/n) by pair counting. Thus simple examples exist for which every t-set contains fewer than s clauses. A seed of size at most t/4 that reaches all variables would produce a t-set with at least 3t/4 distinct triggering clauses, a contradiction. Since m=n, every order leaves more than t/4 terminals. This property is independent of clause signs. The argument bounds this direct reduction only, without any hardness assumption.
