# A fixed-checksum aggregate flow selector

For a finite binary matrix D with m columns, consider encoding a pair of cycle words covering every coordinate using one aggregate root and k checksum roots. This construction has an exact decision characterization. When D is a graph incidence matrix the aggregate problem has a deterministic polynomial-time witness algorithm for every fixed k, even on graphs of unbounded width. At most two checksum bits admit a direct Gaussian algorithm for arbitrary D.

## Physical matrix and exact predicate

Choose linear maps T_e from the two-bit color space to the k-bit score space and a target t. Write u_e for the e-th private basis vector. For each nonzero color v place the ordinary column

    (u_e, v_1 D_e, v_2 D_e, T_e v).

Name the aggregate column r=(sum_e u_e,0,0,t) followed by the k independent pure checksum axes. Given an ordinary subset X to contract, delete every other ordinary column. The question asks whether the kernel of the named columns in the quotient by span(X) is exactly the line generated by the first named coordinate. These are k+1 names. The private directions are rows.

**Proposition.** The selector accepts exactly when two words f,g in ker D have checksum t and the set Z of coordinates where both vanish is independent in D.

If r belongs to the contracted span, choose one representation and discard the other contracted columns. This preserves the prescribed line when it was exact. Each private block now has one or three chosen columns. Record its sole color in the first case and zero in the second. Conservation gives the two words, with the required checksum. An even selection in the three-column block can supply any color, and an even selection in the singleton supplies only zero. Consequently the pure tag dependencies are exactly the score vectors of pairs of words supported on Z. They all vanish precisely when every circuit c in Z has matrix gain M(c)=sum_{e in c} T_e equal to zero. This equivalence uses separate test words in each color component. If such a circuit remains, put one fixed nonzero color on it. The change preserves the word equations and checksum and removes its entire support from Z. Iteration leaves Z independent. Conversely, the canonical one- or three-column selections from any such pair span r and admit no nonzero pure checksum dependency. Subtracting r from any further named dependency would give such a pure dependency.

On the Petersen graph with T_e the identity and t=0, put a single fixed nonzero color on both cycles complementary to the spoke perfect matching. Their ten edges contribute zero checksum and the spoke matching is a forest. The selector accepts, although the graph has no nowhere-zero two-bit flow. Exact counts for K4, K3,3 and Petersen are 6, 12 and 0 ordered nowhere-zero flows. Their respective aggregate target sets are all four scores, only 0, and all four scores. Cubic three-edge-colorability is NP-complete. [Holyer, The NP-Completeness of Edge-Coloring](https://doi.org/10.1137/0210055)

## Basis scores and a special Gaussian case

Extend the zero set to a column basis B of D. All nonbasis columns, called chords, have nonzero colors. For e outside B let c_e be its fundamental circuit, including e. These circuits form a coordinate basis of ker D. Each chord can be given any of the three nonzero colors. The allowed scores form the set sum S of A_e=M(c_e)({01,10,11}). Successive set additions take O(3m·2^k) transitions after computing the circuit gains and recover actual word pairs by backtracking.

For k≤2 each A_e is zero, a full line or the punctured two-bit plane. Two punctured planes add to the whole plane. A punctured plane plus a full nonzero line also fills it. Without a punctured plane the sum is a subspace. If a basis misses t but t lies in the full linear score image, the sole exceptional case is t=0 and exactly one nonzero fundamental matrix U, of rank two. The whole matrix gain map on ker D is then c↦ℓ(c)U. The physical coordinate evaluations on ker D represent the dual matroid. A dual basis has at least two nonzero ℓ coefficients precisely when the expansion of ℓ in that basis has weight at least two. This is possible exactly when ℓ belongs to the span of the coordinate evaluations unequal to ℓ. Indeed, a minimal such representation is independent, has at least two members, and extends to a dual basis. Conversely the at least two basis elements used in such an expansion must all differ from ℓ. The complementary primal basis attains zero. This is constructive by ordinary elimination, also for zero, parallel and coloop columns.

## A bounded certificate for every fixed k

Assume D is a graph incidence matrix. Loops, parallel edges, disconnected graphs and isolated vertices are allowed. In a spanning forest process the A_e in any order, starting at S={0}. If |S+A_e|=|S|, all translates S+a for a in A_e coincide. Their differences stabilize S. Those differences span im M(c_e), since the differences among the three nonzero two-bit colors span their domain. Every a lies in that image, so S+A_e=S. A period of a score set remains a period after later set additions.

Keep the chord each time the cardinality strictly increases. There are at most 2^k−1 retained chords J. Their matrix gains already give the full score set S_J, and every omitted fundamental matrix has image in H=Stab(S_J). The cycles in G−J are spanned by those omitted fundamental circuits. It follows that every cycle in G−J has gain with image in H, a basis-independent condition.

Enumerate candidate sets J of at most 2^k−1 edges and one k-by-two matrix for each marked edge. Compute S_J and H. Require t in S_J, ensure that G−J preserves the connected components of G, and use any spanning forest of G−J to test that all its fundamental gain images lie in H. Finally ask for a spanning forest of G−J in which the marked fundamental circuits have their guessed gains. Any such forest attains S_J because every remaining chord contributes a nonempty subset of H. The initial certificate proves completeness. A final tree computation returns both words and the canonical contraction witness. This takes |G|^{O(2^k)} times a computable function of k in the usual edge-list encoding.

## Realizing the marked circuit gains

For each marked nonloop e=ab the tree path from a to b must carry gain M_e+T_e in the finite group of binary k-by-two matrices. A marked loop has exactly its given one-edge gain. In a realizing forest the union of the requested paths is a forest with at most 2|J| terminal vertices. Keep all endpoints, including ones internal to another path. Suppressing its other degree-two vertices leaves fewer than 2|J| extra branch vertices when a path is present. Enumerate placements of these vertices, the forest shape on the placed sites, and the group gain of every shape segment. Require that every marked endpoint pair is connected in the shape and its segment gains have the required sum. Each shape has bounded size.

Subdivide every nonloop edge of G−J through a private vertex. Put its matrix gain on one half and zero on the other. Replace each placed site by one terminal clone per incident segment, copying its incident half-edges and labels to every clone. All clones are endpoints of demanded paths. Mutually vertex-disjoint paths for the shape segments cannot use another placed site internally. A path between distinct clones of the same original site through a private subdivision vertex would have both ends at this site. The shape has no such segment. Distinct segments cannot reuse an original edge because they would share its private vertex. After merging the clones the routes give a subdivision of the enumerated forest. Extend it greedily to a spanning forest of G−J. Each demanded segment label is enforced by forbidding the opposite value in each of the 2k binary coordinates. For k=0 ordinary linkage suffices. The constructive algorithm of Liu and Yoo takes polynomial time for fixed numbers of path pairs, label coordinates and forbidden elements. [Liu and Yoo, Disjoint Paths Problem with Group-Expressable Constraints (STOC 2025), Theorem 1.3](https://people.tamu.edu/~yyoo/stoc25.pdf)

## Executable checks and scope

Run `python check.py` in this directory. The code uses the elementary binary elimination routines in the sibling `unseeded-root-states/solve.py` packet. `selector.py` implements elimination, certificate search and witness lifting. `router.py` implements the shape-to-linkage reduction. Its `brute_linkage` oracle is exhaustive for small tests. The polynomial-time bound uses the published linkage algorithm at this interface.

On 80 multigraphs with zero through four bits, 496 target queries agreed with exhaustive independent-zero-set testing and spanning-forest enumeration. Of these, 295 target queries also agreed with all contraction masks of the physical representation. All 231 returned witnesses passed direct Gaussian tests of the exact named kernel. The checker tested 227 certificate completions and 96 routing comparisons plus an explicit three-segment branching example. The strict flow counts above were computed independently of the selector algorithm.

This classifies one aggregate-root decision construction, including arbitrary input edge tags. A different fixed-root reduction from an NP-complete source, or a general algorithm for fixed named kernels on binary inputs, remains a separate open bridge.
