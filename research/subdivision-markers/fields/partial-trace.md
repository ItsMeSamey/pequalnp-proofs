# Which partial terminal traces need joint information?

Let E be material columns of a represented binary vector space V, with named terminal space U. A material set X has exact trace J(X)=span(X)∩U. Fix an arbitrary predicate P on subspaces of U. The query asks whether P holds for some attainable trace.

**Graded criterion.** The query universally reduces to the disjunction of the accepted exact-trace queries of dimension at most k in the original representation if and only if every basis of every accepted subspace J has a subset of at most k members whose span is accepted. Include the empty span when accepted.

Take a material basis F of span(X) for a given witness X. Choose F0⊆F whose projection modulo U is a basis of the projection of F. For each e∈F∖F0, let b_e be e plus its unique F0 expression after projection. These b_e form a basis of J(X): their preimage coefficient vectors are the fundamental circuit basis of the projected kernel, and evaluation in the original V is injective on coefficients on F. For every S⊆F∖F0, the material set F0∪S has trace exactly span{b_e:e∈S}. This realizes every coordinate subspace of the extracted basis inside X. For an individual line, its actual projected circuit support suffices. All the expressions and a suitable witness for any specified coordinate subset are constructible by Gaussian elimination. Sufficiency now follows from the basis condition. Conversely, use the elements of a violating basis as the whole material ground set. Precisely its coordinate spans are attainable; no smaller accepted trace exists. To keep material elements outside U, replace each such h by x_h,x_h+h with independent private x_h. A trace is the span of just those h for which both columns were selected.

**Exact-line specialization.** Suppose H⊆U and B⊆H∖{0}. A query asking for J⊆H hitting B is equivalent on all binary material configurations to the disjunction of exact-line queries J=span(b), b∈B, precisely when H∖B is a subspace. If it is a subspace, every generating basis of a trace meeting B contains a residue in B. If it is not, there are u,v outside B in H with u+v in B. Use u,v as material columns. Only their two individual lines occur as exact lines. This applies in particular whenever B is an affine hyperplane in H missing zero, or H minus any subspace.

For the single target predicate t∈J⊆H, t nonzero and dim H=d, the sharp universal bound on dimension of the exact traces in this disjunction is k=d. Take a basis of H summing to t, obtainable by an invertible binary change of coordinates. Only the span of all its d members contains t.

**Even the full line palette can lose compatibility.** Let U have basis a,b,c, H=span(a,b), t=a+b. For a YES instance take the six nonzero vectors of U other than t as material columns. The pair a,b spans a permitted trace containing t. For a NO instance introduce independent x outside U and use x,x+a,x+c,x+c+b. Their six pairwise differences are exactly the six nonzero vectors other than t, and every pair realizes its difference as an exact line. These four material columns are independent: their three differences from x form a basis of U. Hence triples intersect U in dimension two. The unique expression of t uses all four and its trace contains c. The two instances have identical complete exact-line palettes and full total terminal trace U. Applying the series-pair construction above in the YES instance gives ordinary projections that are unions of parallel classes in both examples.

**Relation to four-terminal algorithms.** An exact-line query at b always tests the intersection with the full U. In the four-independent-terminal spanning-circuit formulation, their sum can be set to b by replacing the terminal basis within U, leaving all material vectors unchanged. The criterion needs at most fifteen line calls in dimension four. An oracle promised on the ordinary quotient retains the same quotient. For a promise on the full represented matroid, verify preservation under changing terminal columns or use a labelled-residue interface that already accepts different targets. These are query equivalences, not a new general solver. The palette obstruction addresses original-instance answers; it does not rule out queries on changed representations.

For a supplied branch decomposition, an exact trace table answers every predicate. At a node I set V_I=span(E_I), D_I=V_I∩(V_out+U). Store one material witness per distinct span(X)∩D_I for X⊆I. Child traces T1,T2 merge as (T1+T2)∩D_I: any x=x1+x2 in D_I has each summand in its own child boundary. The converse follows from the stored witnesses. At the root the trace is exactly J(X). If the ordinary cut connectivity is at most w, dim D_I≤w+dim U, so subspace enumeration and traceback cost 2^{O((w+dim U)^2)}poly(|E|). This supplies a constructive alternative when one-line answers do not suffice.
