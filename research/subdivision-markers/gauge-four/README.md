# Rank-four gauge support and four designated circuit elements

Represent a binary matroid Q by columns a_e and assign arbitrary binary vector gains lambda_e. Changing each gain to lambda_e+D a_e for the same linear map D preserves the gain of every circuit. Suppose the residual nonzero gains after such a change occur among columns of matroid rank at most four. This promises low rank of the supporting matroid flat, distinct from the dimension of the gain values. The map need not be given: enumerate at most four independent columns, and solve D a_e=lambda_e simultaneously on all columns outside their span. When this succeeds the remaining nonzero gains have the required support rank. This is polynomial for rank four, including parallel columns and loops.

Write E for the support, obtained after gauging. To find a circuit of prescribed gain h, check circuits contained entirely in E by enumerating subsets of at most five elements and using linear algebra. Every circuit using outside elements meets E in an independent set S of at most four elements. Enumerate those S for which the residual gains sum to h, delete E minus S, and ask for a circuit containing S. Any positive answer has exactly the required gain even if its circuit is contained in E. For empty S, ordinary rank comparison detects a circuit. For 1≤|S|<4, a series extension of a designated nonloop element adds a required element while preserving equivalence: replace its column a with (a,1),(0,1), and append zero to the other columns. The two new columns occur together in a dependency; after contracting this pair to the old label, circuits using the pair correspond exactly to old circuits using a. Repeat until there are four labels. A required loop admits only its singleton circuit and is directly checked. Thus general gain target (including zero) and any gain dimension reduce in polynomial time by oracle calls to the four-element problem.

Conversely take any four distinct designated elements T in an arbitrary binary matroid. If T is dependent, a circuit can contain it precisely when T itself is a circuit. Otherwise assign the four unit vector gains to T and zero elsewhere, with target 1111. This is a single rank-four-support instance, with YES exactly when one circuit contains T. A dense presentation can be produced by any gauge without changing the answer. In an independent-target lift of a general nonzero gain query, the terminal basis may always be chosen to sum to the nonzero target; if the realized cycle-gain image is a smaller space, solve for membership and use any basis of that image summing to the target. Equivalently take a row-coordinate change sending the reduced target to all ones. A zero target needs its separate treatment above.

## Smallest all-triples counterexample

Use the eight columns in binary dimension four having odd Hamming weight, four units and the four complementary triple sums. The designated terminals are the units. This is affine geometry AG(3,2). Each terminal triple is in a 4-circuit with its sum. No circuit contains all four terminals. All dependencies have even size, and rank four bounds circuit size by five, while the four units are independent. The matroid is simple and 3-connected: a two-element set has rank two and its six-element complement has rank four; three points have rank three and five have rank four; at a four-four split each side has rank at least three. These inequalities give connectivity function at least two across every split with both sides of size at least two.

This has the least possible number of elements for this counterexample with independent T. The four witnessing circuits project to the four terminal incidence masks with one zero. These masks form a binary basis because (I+J)^2=I in dimension four. Hence nullity is at least four, and rank is at least four. Here the cycle-gain map is bijective and every nonzero circuit has nonzero gain. In particular the full four-dimensional gain space survives removal of redundant score coordinates.

Even the best gauge cannot reduce this example's supporting rank below four. A rank-at-most-three flat contains at most four of the eight odd columns: inside a three-dimensional linear hyperplane not equal to the even-parity hyperplane, odd parity cuts out an affine plane. With fewer than four points removed, the remaining columns are dependent. When all four points of an affine plane are removed, the complementary four points form another affine plane and sum to zero. In every case a circuit remains outside the proposed support. Its gain would have to vanish, contradicting injectivity on the cycle space. Thus the example is genuinely at the rank-four threshold, and all four single-omission tests can pass there.
