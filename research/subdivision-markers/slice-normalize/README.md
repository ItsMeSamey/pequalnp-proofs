# Alternating normalization on a marker slice

Over GF(2) write the native single-literal guard at i as x_i(Mx)_i=0, with offsets absorbed into diagonal entries using x_i²=x_i. Fix a nonempty affine fiber F={x:Hx=h}. Each edit M'=M+KH+diag(Kh) preserves every individual guard value on F because the difference of that value is x_i times row i of K applied to Hx+h. Write q(x)=xᵀMx, using multilinear reduction. A matrix is alternating exactly when this Boolean function is identically zero: its linear coefficients are the diagonal and its cross coefficients are M_ij+M_ji.

**Alternating criterion.** Such edits can make M alternating if and only if q vanishes identically on F. Necessity follows by summing guards. For sufficiency it is enough to show q is a sum of homogeneous linear forms times the affine constraints, as these are exactly the changes to q supplied by the edits. Use an invertible linear coordinate change and independent recombinations of the consistent equations to put them in the form y_j=h_j for j≤r, with at most one nonzero h_j. These transformations preserve the space of permitted correction polynomials. Cancel each quadratic term y_j y_k involving any fixed index j using y_k(y_j+h_j). The residual has only free-coordinate quadratics and arbitrary linear terms, and no constant since q and the corrections vanish at the origin. Its restriction is identically zero, so all free quadratics and free linear coefficients vanish, as does the coefficient of the single fixed-one coordinate if present. Each remaining fixed-zero linear term y_j equals the permitted correction y_j(y_j+0). This proves sufficiency. Finding K uses an ordinary binary linear system for its entries, matching all off-diagonal and diagonal coefficients.

**Symmetric criterion.** The same edits can make M symmetric, allowing arbitrary diagonal, if and only if the polar bilinear form B(u,v)=uᵀ(M+Mᵀ)v vanishes on ker(H)×ker(H). Equivalently q is affine on F. Necessity follows since symmetric matrices have linear Boolean quadratic forms. For sufficiency, the affine restriction admits a homogeneous ambient linear extension. If F passes through zero, extend its linear part; otherwise take a base point of F outside ker H to supply the required constant on F. Subtract the extension from q by a diagonal matrix and apply the alternating criterion, then restore the diagonal. Equivalently solve for symmetric output by imposing only off-diagonal coefficient matching.

## One marker with affine or factorizable restricted quadratic

Solve the one marker equation for a fiber point b and kernel basis, using free coordinates y that are actual ambient coordinate bits with value zero at b. Compute Q(y)=q(b+Uy) by evaluating it on masks of weights zero, one and two to recover its coefficients. An affine Q requires one additional equation Q=0; constant one gives no witness and constant zero imposes no restriction. A product Q=(p+a)(s+c) is zero on the union of the two affine hyperplanes p=a or s=c. Each branch has at most two marker equations in the original variables, and on that branch q vanishes identically. Apply the constructive alternating normalization and the preexisting alternating two-marker solver. It returns the original variable mask directly, since each guard value was preserved individually on its branch.

To recognize the product class exactly, in the nonaffine case compute the polar rank. Independent factors have rank two and their linear normals span the polar row space. If the rank is two take any basis p,s of that space. Its wedge gives exactly the polar form: on the two-dimensional quotient by the polar radical there is just one nonzero alternating form over GF(2). Therefore Q differs from ps by an affine function. Products of independent affine forms equal one on a single point in the two-dimensional quotient, so in this prescribed basis they too are one of the four products (p+a)(s+c). Compare all four, including the diagonal linear coefficients of ps (the coordinatewise intersection of the two normal masks). All higher polar ranks, and unmatched rank-two quadratics, fall outside this algorithm. The procedure uses polynomial time and space even when the global polar rank is four. For example q=x1x2+x2+x3x4+x1+x3+x4 has rank four before restriction and becomes (1+x3)(1+x4) on x1=1.

solver.py returns a witness, returns None when infeasible in the supported class, and raises ValueError for an unsupported restricted quadratic. It locates the existing alternating solver in the adjacent research repository. The normalization functions work for any number of consistent affine equations.

test_solver.py exhausts all 2,198 Boolean multilinear quadratics in zero through four free variables, checking product detection and zero-set recovery against all pairs of affine forms. Across all matrices and one-bit marker equations through three variables, plus 800 seeded systems, 8,452 consistent slices checked both normalization criteria, including guardwise equality at every point. Another 7,029 supported or inconsistent one-marker instances checked the returned witness or infeasibility against exhaustive assignment search.

## Alternating lift for every consistent marker fiber

Let the restriction of the polar form to the kernel of the markers have rank 2r. On the marker fiber write q as a sum of c≤r+1 products (u_j·x)(v_j·x) with homogeneous ambient factors. To compute these, use the same affine chart as above. While its polar matrix B is nonzero, choose B_ij=1 and subtract the product of the two forms given by rows i and j. Its polar is their wedge, which clears rows and columns i,j and has precisely the effect of eliminating the 2-by-2 invertible principal block; the residual rank drops by two. Subtract each product's linear diagonal as well. After r steps the residual is affine. Each free chart coordinate is an original coordinate. A constant one on a nonhomogeneous slice is given by any marker whose right-hand side is one; on a homogeneous slice the residual has no constant since q and the products vanish at zero. Thus the residual is a homogeneous ambient form L on the slice and can be added as L² if necessary. If the residual linear part belongs to the span of extracted factors on a nonhomogeneous slice, shifts by the constant-one marker absorb that part into the r products. This saves the last product whenever the constant also cancels. The one-extra bound is needed in general already for the anisotropic binary quadratic x+y+xy, which has three ones whereas a product with two independent linear factors has only one.

Correct M by adding the sum of u_j v_jᵀ; its quadratic now vanishes on the slice. Alternating normalization as above supplies a graph A. For each pair add a new bit z_j and marker z_j=v_j·x; join z_j to the real vertices in the support of u_j. In the resulting graph, each real vertex's selected-degree parity is exactly its original guard value on the enlarged marker fiber. The correction and the new edges cancel term by term, and gauge normalization preserves guard values individually.

Two repair graphs on auxiliary vertices suffice. In the first add a separate length-two path between every pair of z vertices. In the second add a hub and one length-two path from each z vertex to the hub. All new middle vertices are fresh. For a valid real assignment, start with the prescribed z and no repairs. Every real active degree is even, so handshake implies there are evenly many odd-degree active z vertices. For pair paths, match those vertices arbitrarily and select just the matching middles. For hub paths, select the hub and exactly the middles of the odd active z vertices. Each selected middle has degree two, and the hub has even degree. Either repair yields an induced even-degree support. Conversely every augmented solution restricts to an original witness since no repair vertex neighbors a real vertex. This proves equality of the full projected solution sets; representation multiplicities may vary.

lift.py provides both constructions and by default chooses the smaller repair graph. It adds exactly c marker equations and at most c+min(c(c−1)/2,c+1) vertices. It accepts every consistent affine fiber, even with redundant equations. With no polar rank bound this is a polynomial-size reduction, without a claim that its arbitrarily many resulting marker equations are efficiently decidable.

### Exact minimum product count

The implemented refinement computes the minimum c among products of homogeneous ambient forms on this fiber. The polar rank gives c≥r. With exactly r products all 2r linear parts on chart coordinates must be independent and span the polar row space. This follows because the row space of the polar form is contained in their span and has dimension 2r. Thus the represented function is constant on each coset of the polar radical. With uniform chart input their possibly shifted 2r values are independent unbiased bits, and the sign mean of their sum of products is (1/2)^r>0. Either failure of radical invariance or negative sign bias certifies c>r.

If the residual linear part is outside the polar row space after extracting r pairs, radical invariance fails. If it belongs to that space on a nonhomogeneous slice, the factor shifts described above absorb it. A surviving constant one reverses the sign mean, requiring one extra product. On a homogeneous slice write the residual linear term in pair coordinates, and allocate its coefficients to the corresponding planes. For a plane pq+p use p(p+q), and similarly for pq+q. The remaining problematic planes are pq+p+q, each with negative sign mean. For two such planes in coordinates p,q and u,v, their sum equals

    (q+u)(p+u) + (p+q+u+v)(p+q+v).

This identity follows by expansion with Boolean multilinear reduction and requires no constant offsets. Pair such planes to remove them. If an odd plane remains, keep its product and add the square of its remaining linear term. All plane coordinates are independent so an odd number of negative planes yields negative total sign mean. The extra product is therefore necessary. These cases exhaust all quadratics, including zero polar rank.

test_product_rank.py compares the output number with an independently computed shortest XOR sum of product truth tables and verifies the returned product evaluations, across all 3,297 homogeneous and affine quadratics through four free bits. test_lift.py compares 7,899 entire projected solution sets with brute force and verifies 14,259 repaired small witnesses. It also checks 120 planted random repairs through 64 real variables. test_hub.py independently checks both repair gadgets against brute force on another 240 systems for each gadget and validates 1,306 extensions.
