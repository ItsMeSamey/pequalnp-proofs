# Compressing supplied binary scores

Let ordinary columns of a binary matrix D represent a matroid on n elements. A witness consists of an ordinary cycle c in ker D and an ordinary cocycle d in row D with disjoint supports. Its score is Lc(c)+Ld(d), in a t-dimensional binary space. A supplied common freely selectable subspace H permits adding any vector of H to that score.

Given actual witnesses for named scores, there is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm choosing disjoint deletion and contraction sets. Every named score retains an original-coordinate witness with primal support disjoint from the deleted set and dual support disjoint from the contracted set. Put k=t−dim H and let s count the distinct named cosets modulo H. At most binom(s,2)·2^(k−1) ordinary elements survive for s≥2. For s≤1 every ordinary element can be removed. The input requires neither a missing score nor uniqueness. For two scores at four bits, eight is sharp. The bound for larger portfolios is an upper bound.

## Construction

Maintain original cycle and cocycle spaces with the respective zero restrictions, project onto the surviving positions, and keep sections and kernels by Gaussian elimination. The two projected spaces are the cycle and cocycle spaces of the current minor. The score images of the two kernels give a common free offset subspace. The two lifts have disjoint support off the surviving positions because the permitted removed supports lie on opposite sides of the deletion/contraction partition. The routine reconstructs actual witnesses by solving in the kernel score image.

For two witnesses, delete a site where both primal bits vanish or contract one where both dual bits vanish. If all sites block both choices, the witnesses are opposite full partitions. If their scores agree modulo the current free offsets, reuse one projected pair for both names. Otherwise take the full-selector chart with symmetric zero-diagonal fundamental matrix J and first selector a. On the kernel K of the score modulo the difference of the two target cosets, let h be its codimension. The all-one selector lies in K, J1=1, and h≤k−1. Above 2^h sites the map I+J on K has a nontrivial kernel word outside span(1), or its image contains a weight-two word. To see the latter case, the even image code has redundancy h+1 and admits a parity-check matrix with an all-one first row. Two positions share a column above 2^h sites. For the resulting selector increment z, the conflict sets of a+z and a+1+z partition the support of (I+J)z. Each has even size, so one trial is feasible. Its score lies in one of the two target cosets, and it differs from the former witness for that coset. It unlocks a common restriction. The implementation compares with the safe upper bound 2^(k−1) and finds the weight-two word by trying pairs. [Full selector proof and sharp eight-element chart](../rooted-core/sharp-opposite-core.md)

For s targets, retain a bank of witnesses for each name. Perform a restriction if each bank has a compatible member. Otherwise, assign each blocked site to an unordered pair of names, one forced to have primal bit one throughout its bank and the other forced to have dual bit one. Above the portfolio bound some group exceeds the two-score bound. Temporarily reduce those two names alone to that bound. At least one site in the group was temporarily restricted. Its resulting full witnesses, viewed in the original current problem, newly exhibit a zero in at least one bank at that site. Adding the witnesses increases the number of available zero flags. There are at most 2sn flags between permanent restrictions, at most n permanent restrictions, and at most n reductions per temporary call. Each uses polynomially many binary linear solves, including at most binom(n,2) candidate weight-two solves per switch.

This procedure preserves supplied positive witnesses. An unspecified score can disappear. The bounded-width score-state algorithm handles unseeded inputs under its layout hypothesis. [Unseeded algorithm and coloop example](../unseeded-root-states/README.md)

## Reference implementation and checks

Run `python check.py`. The function `compress(base, primal, dual, seeds, t, stats=None, offsets=())` accepts three lists of nonnegative integer columns or labels and a dictionary from named score integers to pairs of support masks on original positions. The optional offset list generates H. It returns deletion and contraction masks and a dictionary of full original-coordinate witnesses. With an initial H, the difference between the requested score and its returned physical score lies in H. Common initial affine translations can be subtracted from all requested names before calling.

The checker exercises 320 base runs, 60 further runs with input offsets, and independently enumerates the witnesses of the tight eight-element example. The seeded runs forced 162 kernel-word switches, 119 weight-two switches and 47 identifications of target scores in hidden offsets. A separate audit reconstructed every attainable original score of every legal projected pair on 225 small restricted matrices and checked 1,810 individual backlifts.
