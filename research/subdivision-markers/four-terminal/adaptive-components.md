# Adaptive low-dimensional queries for four terminal roots

The four independent terminals span W and sum to t. First restrict W to the span of the ordinary columns, rejecting if t is absent and using the usual terminal compression if the resulting rank drops. The ordinary matroid components span a direct sum of spaces V_i, with projections pi_i. Choose independent local ordinary sets X_i summing to t_i=pi_i(t). In G=W/line(t), of dimension three, let K_i be the preimage on W of span(X_i) under pi_i, modulo line(t). The terminal circuit exists exactly when these local choices have intersection zero. If t_i=0 use the empty set, which minimizes the trace. Recover the circuit from the union and the original roots.

For any boundary P contained in W and containing t, and upper bound D contained in P and containing t, require ker(pi_i restricted to P) contained in D. The local test asks for span(X_i) intersect pi_i(P) contained in pi_i(D). For t_i=0 test the kernel directly. At image dimension at most three, a full bound is linear algebra. A line bound is the exact-line problem with at most three roots summing to t_i. For a plane in dimension three take its defining functional f, append a column t_i with primal bit pinned one and prescribe f on two basis directions complementary to t_i as two dual markers. Disjointness forces zero on t_i. Any acceptable local representation admits such a dual extension; conversely prune the returned ordinary representation of t_i to an independent set. These are upper-bound queries, so a recovered smaller trace suffices. For boundary dimension at most three, dynamic programming over the finitely many possible intersections of upper bounds solves any set of components. A final upper intersection line(t) forces the actual intersection to equal it; every actual local trace is an available upper bound.

Call a component informative if some K_i is proper. Test by excluding each possible vector outside line(t) while representing t_i, using only a two-dimensional boundary and the preceding kernel check. If at least two components are informative, in any YES instance choices can achieve at least two proper K_i: if just one in a solution is proper it equals zero, so freely replace another with a proper choice. Pick one such i and write R for the intersection of the others. Both K_i and R are proper and they meet in zero.

Enumerate i and all nonzero proper H in G. For the true i take H=R if R is nonzero and otherwise choose a line outside K_i. H contains R and misses K_i. Find ANY local witness with K'_i missing H by the line query on its preimage in W, which has dimension at most three. Its concrete K'_i is proper and still misses the unknown R. Solve the rest by the dynamic program on the preimage of K'_i, again at most three-dimensional. The original choices for all remaining components still work on the good branch, independent of the first oracle's tie breaking. A positive branch gives a genuine witness. If no component is informative, answer NO. With one informative component all others always contribute full trace and can be dropped after recording their local representations. A remaining single component can still have full terminal rank.

For a supplied two-separation whose ordinary spans V_A,V_B meet in a line, work upstairs in their direct sum with addition map phi. The preimage of W has dimension five; try both lifts u of t. A true independent expression gives exact upstairs trace line(u) since its original independence makes phi injective on the chosen span. Conversely an exact upstairs trace excludes the nonzero diagonal kernel element, so its independent representation stays independent downstairs and gives exact trace line(t). In the four-dimensional quotient by u, suppose both actual local traces K_A,K_B have dimension at most two and meet in zero. Enumerate a two-plane H containing K_B and disjoint from K_A (extend in a complement). Ask A to miss H, again on preimage dimension three. Its recovered trace K'_A has dimension at most two and still misses K_B. Ask B to miss K'_A using another such query. This finds every balanced positive witness at the supplied separation; the procedure gives no negative classification when all solutions are unbalanced.

Finite checks used 307 binary systems (191 YES) and 4,948 abstract local-family systems (4,785 YES). No exact complexity conclusion follows for a single full-rank component.
