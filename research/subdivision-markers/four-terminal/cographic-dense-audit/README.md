# Rooted circuits over a cographic ordinary restriction and bounded cycle-space extensions

Let O be a supplied binary matrix with edge-indexed ordinary columns, and P a nonempty set of arbitrary additional mandatory columns in the same space. Suppose G is a supplied graph on that edge set with Cut(G) contained in ker O and q=dim(ker O/Cut(G)) bounded. No graphic or cographic representation of the augmented matrix is required. For an ordinary cographic matroid, use a graph representing its dual: ker O is exactly its cut space, hence q=0. Ground-element labels must be preserved. Graphs may be disconnected, with loops and parallel edges.

## Exact span test

If P is dependent, it can lie in a circuit only if P itself is a circuit. Otherwise t=sum P is nonzero. Compute W=span(P) intersect span O and reject if t is absent. Let r=dim W, take a basis w_i with w_1=t and choose edge representatives J_i with OJ_i=w_i. Extend a basis of Cut(G) to one for ker O by q edge vectors K_j. Assign edge e the binary gain (J_1(e),...,J_r(e),K_1(e),...,K_q(e)). For H subset E let L(H) be the image of the cycle space of (V,H) under gain summation. Then

    coords_W(W intersect span O[E minus H]) = proj_first_r (L(H)^perp).

Indeed sum_i a_i J_i represents sum_i a_i w_i. It can be adjusted to vanish on H exactly when it differs there from a sum of a cut and sum_j z_j K_j. Restricted global cuts give *all* cuts of (V,H), by extending vertex potentials. Cut-cycle orthogonality now gives precisely the condition that some (a,z) annihilates L(H). The coefficients z are existential, not fixed. This works on isolated vertices, loops and parallel edges.

There is a circuit containing P iff some X has span(O[X]) intersect W = span(t). In a circuit the ordinary part is independent. Any nonzero trace different from t could be expressed by a proper subset of P, giving a proper dependence. Conversely choose a minimal expression F of t in X. F and P are separately independent, so any dependence must use the unique expressions of t on both sides, namely all of P and F. Return them as a witness.

For q=0 the required H has L(H)=t^perp in coefficient coordinates. For general q enumerate every binary subspace L of dimension at most r+q, retaining just those with proj_first_r(L^perp)=span(e_1). Find any H with L(H)=one such L. The subspace enumeration costs only a function of r+q.

## Routing a prescribed cycle-gain subspace

Let j=dim L. Take a spanning forest in any feasible H. The fundamental cycles of nonforest edges form a basis, including a loop as a one-edge cycle or a parallel-edge digon. Choose j distinct chords whose fundamental cycle gains form a basis of L. The union of those cycles already has image L: containment in H gives one inclusion and the chosen gains the other. It is therefore unnecessary to span G; alternatively a bridge-only extension preserves the image. If j=0 simply take H empty.

Guess j chord identities and their independent gain values spanning L. Partition their at most 2j distinct endpoints into forest components, requiring both ends of each chord to be in the same component. In each component guess a tree with all these terminals retained, including those internally of degree two, and extra vertices only of degree at least three. Every leaf is a terminal, so at most p−2 additional vertices occur for p>=2 terminals. A single-terminal component is a vertex with no edges, important for isolated loop chords. Guess placements of extra vertices and each segment's full gain. Verify that the chord gain plus the gain along the unique abstract forest path realizes its desired cycle gain. A loop chord uses the empty path. Remove guessed chords by edge ID before routing, including mandatory loop IDs when applicable. Parallel mates remain usable.

Route the resulting fixed forest as vertex-disjoint segment interiors avoiding all other named vertices. A fully explicit implementation guesses distinct direct edges and, for longer segments, distinct first and last edges. For each length-two segment reserve its single interior vertex; require longer segments' two tips to be pairwise distinct and disjoint from all reserved interiors and named vertices. Delete the named and reserved vertices. For every remaining long segment find a vertex-disjoint path between the two interior tips having the required residual gain. Every edge ID is used at most once. A returning cycle is represented by its chord and its forest path; there is no same-vertex path demand. In a general equivalent returning-chain implementation use separate first and last edge IDs, process host loops as direct chains and digons as pairs of distinct parallel edges. Bare terminal cloning without a safeguard against immediate return along the same original edge is insufficient for returning chains.

Liu–Yoo Theorem 1.3 returns the paths in O(|V|^8+|E|) for fixed numbers of pairs, groups and forbidden elements per coordinate. Use one F2 coordinate per gain bit and forbid the opposite value on each path. Unoriented sums match the theorem; all group operations have constant cost. Loops need not remain in the middle path oracle, and parallel graph edges preserve their distinct labels. With zero residual paths no oracle is needed. [Liu and Yoo, Disjoint Paths Problem with Group-Expressable Constraints](https://people.tamu.edu/~yyoo/stoc25.pdf)

A successful linkage gives the specified forest plus chords with exactly the verified fundamental gains. Solve t in the ordinary complement by Gaussian elimination on an independent set; return the nonzero coefficients together with P. Complexity is (|V|+|E|)^{O(r+q)} times a function of r+q and polynomial binary-matrix processing. This is deterministic XP and hence polynomial for fixed r+q, not an FPT claim.

## Additional nonordinary columns of rank k

With an optional set of arbitrary extra columns D of rank k, drop optional zero columns and retain a named representative of each distinct nonzero optional vector. There are at most 2^k−1 types. Since P is nonempty, the chosen optional elements of any circuit through P form a proper independent subset, using at most k types and never repeating an optional type. Enumerate these independent subsets Z, including empty, and run the preceding algorithm with compulsory P union Z and the other optional columns excluded. Apply the dependent-compulsory-set check in each branch, thus also allowing all-nonordinary circuits and two-element circuits through a single root and a parallel optional element. Do not remove a type merely because it agrees with a compulsory column. When P is empty, ordinary linear dependence of the full supplied matrix already decides circuit existence, without deduplication.

## Fixed Euler genus and inclusion-minimal D-joins

Let a connected primal graph G0 be cellularly embedded in a surface of Euler genus g, orientable or nonorientable. For O the primal incidence matrix, let G be the geometric dual. Dual cuts are mod-two sums of facial boundary walks, hence are contained in the primal cycle space ker O; repeated edge traversals cancel. Both graphs are connected. Euler's relation |V0|−|E|+|F|=2−g and the ranks of the primal incidence and dual cut matrices give dim ker O − dim Cut(G)=g. Thus the preceding theorem applies with q=g.

To preserve prescribed edge IDs R in the D-join problem, treat the corresponding primal incidence columns as compulsory ports, together with the extra column d=1_D. A circuit consisting of d and primal edges is exactly d plus a forest with boundary d: a forest expression for d is unique on its support and no proper edge subset works. Handle d=0 first: the empty forest is the unique inclusion-minimal zero join. If R is empty one can solve directly; if the compulsory set is dependent use the circuit test as above. Delete R from the ordinary edge list. There is no need to recompute any embedding: ker(O restricted to E minus R) consists of the vectors of ker O vanishing on R, projected onto the remaining edges. In the dual graph contract R, ignoring dual loops upon contraction and deleting them from the edge list. The cuts of G/R are precisely the cuts of G zero on R, restricted to E minus R, since the defining potentials must agree at the ends of every contracted edge. The quotient of the restricted kernel by this cut space injects into the original quotient ker O / Cut(G), by zero extension. Its dimension is at most g, also when contracting parallel edges or loops and when the primal deletion disconnects the graph. This retains every prescribed edge as a compulsory port without duplicating it. For fixed |R| and supplied fixed Euler genus, this decides unrestricted D and recovers the forest. For disconnected input process components separately, with embeddings of bounded Euler genus supplied for the components or the corresponding cut-space presentations supplied.

## Finite verification

Run `node check.js`. The deterministic seed produced 293 independent-root systems, 154 with strict cut-space inclusion and 194 with a root outside the ordinary span. All 30,940 edge restrictions agreed on the direct linear trace, projected gain annihilator and compressed fundamental-cycle image. All rooted decisions agreed with exhaustive matrix circuit enumeration (279 positive), and 293 explicit contractions satisfied the restricted quotient bound. This verifies the finite algebra and pruning; the large-host linkage algorithm is external.
