# Independent audit of adaptive ordinary direct sums

**Reviewer verdict: GO** for a four-dimensional terminal span W, nonzero t, and an ordinary direct-sum partition whose component spans jointly contain W, provided at least two components admit a proper trace in G=W/<t>. Use the span-intersection reduction first if W is not contained in the ordinary span. This theorem covers the stated class; it does not dispose of the one-informative-component case.

Write pi_i for a component projection, t_i=pi_i(t), and K_i(X_i)={w in W:pi_i(w) belongs to span X_i}/<t> for choices spanning t_i. The direct-sum property gives the global trace quotient as the intersection of the K_i. Full G is always locally possible by taking the whole component. Existence of a proper K_i can be recognized by trying a nonzero class w that it excludes and solving for a span containing t_i and avoiding pi_i(w), on an interface of dimension at most two.

In a global YES witness one may require any designated proper-capable first component to use a proper trace: if its current trace is full, replace it by any proper attainable trace. To require a second proper trace, similarly replace a full trace in a second capable component. Replacements of full traces only shrink the intersection and retain the required t_i. For the modified witness the first trace K and the other traces' intersection R are proper, with K intersect R zero. Choose H=R if R is nonzero. Otherwise choose any line outside K. This gives a nonzero proper H containing R and disjoint from K. For this guessed H, any feasible first-component witness whose trace K' avoids H remains compatible with the old other witnesses. Its full K' is proper because H is nonzero. This proves completeness even under arbitrary local witness selection. Soundness follows by checking that the returned choices jointly have zero quotient intersection.

For restricted local queries take the preimage P in W of H or K'. It has dimension at most three. To constrain the preimage trace to lie inside D<=P with t in D, require ker(pi_i restricted to P)<=D, then enforce t_i in span X_i and span X_i intersect pi_i(P) contained in pi_i(D). The kernel test is necessary even when t_i vanishes. If t_i=0, taking the empty choice minimizes local trace and suffices when the kernel test passes. For the first query set D=<t> to enforce avoidance of H. Compute the full K' by linear algebra from the actual returned choice.

The remaining DP may store an **upper bound** on an actual trace. A table indexed by upper bound D is upward-closed as D increases; equivalently, success is preserved when an actual trace shrinks. Intersecting upper bounds to <t> forces the actual traces to intersect there, and any feasible witness is represented by its own upper bounds. Thus no plane entry needs to force exact plane equality. In the dimension-three projected space with allowed plane D' containing nonzero t_i, choose b completing t_i to a basis of D' and c exterior. The plane containment is equivalent to existence of a dual functional zero on span X_i with d(b)=0 and d(c)=1; d(t_i)=0 follows from t_i in span X_i. This uses one primal target and two dual moments. Indeed the prescribed functional on the three-dimensional space extends by zero on span X_i exactly when it vanishes on the intersection. Alternatively, one may enumerate all exact subtraces with the known primal/dual three-terminal trace algorithms.

Retain the actual local X_i and predecessor for each attained DP entry. A completed selection contains t and has span intersecting W in exactly <t>. Choose an independent basis of the selection and recover the unique expression of t in that basis. Its support, together with the four original independent terminals, gives the desired circuit; a proper terminal subset cannot sum into this span. The number of linear-subspace guesses and DP states is an absolute constant per component. This assumes the local decision routines are polynomial and support witness recovery as in the three-terminal algorithm.

An independent abstract catalog audit enumerated all subspaces of the binary three-space and checked 23,612 seeded component-family systems satisfying the two-capable hypothesis. The adaptive choice procedure, deliberately keeping the first eligible local witness, agreed with direct intersection DP on 22,646 YES and 966 NO systems. This checks the adaptive combinatorics, rather than an implementation of the cited matroid oracle.
