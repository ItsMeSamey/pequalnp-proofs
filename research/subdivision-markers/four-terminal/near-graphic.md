# Graphic terminal contraction with a bounded number of exceptional columns

After contracting the roots the problem asks for one circuit with specified binary label sum t. Suppose its represented ordinary matroid consists of graph incidence columns on V and k supplied additional even vectors h_j on V. Both graphic and exceptional columns carry labels. Enumerate each subset J of additional columns. The empty subset is the graphic algorithm. Let H be the XOR of h_j for j in J, and T its vertex support. A linear test can first discard states with no graphic edge set having boundary H and the required residual label. The following is polynomial for fixed k and a bound s on the size of T across states passing that test. The individual exceptional vectors may be dense.

For J nonempty any circuit using precisely J has graphic part F a forest, since an ordinary graphic cycle would be a proper dependency. Its odd-degree vertices are T. If T is empty then F must be empty and one tests J itself for minimal dependence and proper score. Otherwise include all T as named vertices; a nonempty forest with leaf set inside T has at most |T|-2 additional branching vertices. Guess these vertices and the forest after suppressing its unnamed degree-two vertices. Require that odd degrees are exactly T, that unnamed guessed branching vertices have degree at least three, and that its components have edges. There are at most 2s-2 named vertices. The desired forest is a subdivision of this bounded template.

A forest with the required boundary gives a minimal dependence together with J exactly when the XOR h_K for each proper nonempty subset K of J is outside the incidence span of F. Indeed the whole graphic part is the unique forest expression of H, and any competing dependence uses some proper nonempty K since the forest is independent. The span consists exactly of vectors supported on forest vertices that have even parity on every component. For each K guess either one vertex of supp(h_K) absent from F, or a forest component with an odd number of such vertices. Delete all guessed absent vertices, rejecting contradictory guesses. For each path segment of the template guess its F2 edge-label gain and the parity of the number of its internal vertices in each relevant supp(h_K). Check the total score and the component equations, adding named vertices once each. Encode each internal-vertex parity by unoriented Z4 edge weights chi_K(u)+chi_K(v), where chi_K indicates membership in supp(h_K). Along a path their sum is the known two endpoint contributions plus twice the internal parity. Hence every segment has only finitely many fixed-group requirements at fixed parameters.

To use distinct terminals for disjoint paths, replace each named vertex by one copy per incident template segment, distributing its original incident edges to each copy with their original labels, and make these copies the respective prescribed path endpoints. Each linkage must avoid other terminals. A direct edge between two named vertices may connect any corresponding copies; no two different segments of the forest template have the same unordered ends. Identifying copies therefore yields the desired forest subdivision with the declared component data. Conversely every such forest yields linkage paths. Liu and Yoo's Theorem 1.3 finds the paths with the unoriented additive weights, using a finite group for edge gain and one Z4 coordinate per parity certificate, forbidding the complement of the desired value in each coordinate. Their result is needed here since signed and unsigned Z4 differ. Witness recovery appends J and the original four terminals. With at most 2^k omitted-vertex choices and at most s branch guesses, the runtime is bounded by f(k,s) n^{O(2^k+s)} for fixed label dimension, allowing polynomial input-matrix operations. https://people.tamu.edu/~yyoo/stoc25.pdf

This argument uses small net boundary per viable exceptional choice. It provides no unrestricted large-terminal forest compression.
