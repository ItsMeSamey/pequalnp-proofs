# Exact line traces for dense virtual ports on graphic ordinary columns

Let O be a binary vertex-edge incidence matrix, and let W be a fixed-dimensional space given by arbitrary binary columns in the same ambient coordinates. Let u be a nonzero member of W. We seek ordinary columns X for which span(X) intersects W in exactly the line generated by u. The following one-sided randomized algorithm takes n^{O(dim W)} times polynomial matrix work. No sparsity of any virtual vector is required.

## Preprocessing and completion

First find L=W∩span(O) by elimination. Reject if u is absent from L, and henceforth use L in place of W. This also covers additional ambient coordinates that vanish on all ordinary columns: eliminate them in this intersection, before removing or identifying any vertex coordinates. In graph coordinates image(O) consists of the vectors even on every connected component. Identify one chosen vertex from each connected component to a single vertex w, adding their coordinates modulo two, leaving all other coordinates intact. This identifies the componentwise-even vector space isomorphically with the even space of the resulting connected graph, since each old root coordinate is recovered from its component's nonroot coordinates. It respects ordinary edge columns (loops included) and the vectors of L. Thus if the new graph has n vertices its ordinary rank is r=n−1. Input with all ordinary columns zero has already failed the u membership test. Injections and coordinate changes in a supplied graphic representation can equivalently be performed by a basis map on span(O).

Put h=dim(L)−1, and choose W0 with h independent columns spanning a complement to u in L. Let D be the support of u. There is a desired X exactly when some set F of r−h ordinary edges satisfies both conditions

* [F,W0] is a basis of the even space.
* Every component of (V,F), isolated vertices included, meets D evenly.

Indeed take an independent basis of a successful X. Its span misses span(W0) and contains u. Greedily append an ordinary column outside the current span plus span(W0) until their sum is the whole even space, preserving the direct sum. Conversely the second condition puts u in span(F) by the incidence image characterization, and the first excludes every nonzero linear combination of W0. Since u belongs to span(F), this excludes every other direction of L. The first condition implies F is a forest. If the requested line is specified in port coefficient space for a possibly dependent port matrix H, first account for ker H, which every attainable coefficient kernel contains. If ker H equals the requested line, the empty ordinary selection suffices. If it is not contained in the line the answer is no. Otherwise H is injective and the image-space theorem applies.

## Polynomial and Pfaffians

Over characteristic two use one variable x_e per ordinary edge identity. Define P as the sum of monomials x_F over the F above. For each A⊆V of size h+1 containing w let α_A=det W0[A minus {w},:]. Set S=D symmetric-difference A and form

    Y = O[V minus A,:] diag(x) Oᵀ.

Multiply α_A by the sum of det Y[:,V minus B] over B⊆S of size h+1, then sum over A. The result is P. Empty minors have value one.

To check the coefficient, Cauchy–Binet fixes |F|=r−h and gives det O[V minus A,F] times the sum of det O[V minus B,F] over the indicated B. If the first factor is one, F is a forest with h+1 components and exactly one A vertex in each. A contributing B consists of one S vertex per component. Their count modulo two equals the product over components C of (1+|D∩C| mod 2). This factor does not depend on the admissible A. What remains in the outer sum is the Laplace expansion of det [F,W0] with row w omitted, partitioned by the h rows assigned to the virtual columns. No common orientation or sparsity condition is needed over characteristic two. Distinct F have distinct monomials.

Each inner sum is one Pfaffian. Use an alternating block matrix with zero top-left block of size r−h, upper-right Y and lower-right the adjacency matrix of the unit complete graph on S (zero elsewhere). If h+1 is odd append a dummy adjacent precisely to S and to no top vertex. A perfect matching assigns distinct ordinary bottom vertices to the top rows; exactly h+1 ordinary bottom vertices B remain. Their completion is impossible unless B⊆S, and then the number of matchings of the uncovered clique including the dummy when present is odd. This also handles h=0, S empty, and zero-size top blocks. ## Witness and scope

Evaluate at independent uniform values in GF(2^k) with field order at least 2 max(1,r−h). P has degree r−h, so a nonzero P evaluates nonzero with probability at least one half. A nonzero evaluation certifies feasibility, and a failed trial can be repeated independently. Enumerating at most binomial(n−1,h) anchor sets gives the stated fixed-dimension polynomial bound.

From a nonzero evaluation, keep the original incidence coordinates, root, virtual columns and field values fixed. In turn attempt to set each remaining edge variable to zero, retaining the deletion whenever the evaluated polynomial stays nonzero. A failed deletion at an early stage does not by itself prove that edge is indispensable after later cancellations. For guaranteed witness recovery use *coefficient conditioning* instead: for each edge write the current multilinear polynomial as P0+x_e P1. If P0 at the other current values is nonzero, fix that edge absent and continue with P0. Otherwise fix it present and continue with P1, nonzero at those values. In the latter step implement P1 by contracting the edge in the current graph, XORing D and every W0 column's coordinates at its two ends, and retaining its edge ID. A loop has identically zero present coefficient. For a nonloop the quotient of the even space by its incidence column is exactly the even space after that vertex identification. Thus full basis and u-membership conditions for the original F containing the edge are equivalent to the same fixed-h tests on F minus the edge after contraction; the forest component parity condition is preserved. Merged virtual columns may lose rank, or u may become zero, but the **coefficient oracle always uses the fixed h and current stored columns**, never reapplies the initial line-trace preprocessing. Similarly after deleting edges the graph may disconnect; keep the current vertex frame, with no recomputation of its original completion rank, and evaluate the same formula. It remains a coefficient identity on that frame for every edge restriction. In a successful present branch a dependent W0 cannot occur. Root w follows its merged vertex. Once all decisions are fixed the recorded present edges give F; check both conditions in the original coordinates and recover an ordinary line witness directly. No additional randomness is needed after a nonzero initial evaluation.
