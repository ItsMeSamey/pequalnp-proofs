# Two-dimensional interface with one-dimensional local cycle gains

Partition the labelled binary columns (q_i,g_i) into A and B. Let V_S be the span of the q_i on S, and let U=V_A intersect V_B have dimension at most two. For a side S define G_S to be the image of its q-cycle space under the sum of gains. Assume dim G_A, dim G_B <=1. All these spaces are computable by Gaussian elimination.

Pick a q-basis from each side and define a map phi_S on V_S by assigning the original gains of these basis elements. Then g_i+phi_S(q_i) lies in G_S for every element of S: append to i the basis expression for its q-column to form a cycle. Encode this residual gain by at most one binary coordinate. An ordinary circuit entirely in S has its original gain equal to its residual gain. Such circuits can be checked directly via the labelled-circuit reduction to at most one terminal for nonzero target; if zero target is required, use an ordinary independent-basis parity check or handle the zero case as detailed below. The original four-root reduction always asks for a nonzero target.

## Crossing circuits

A circuit crossing the split has two nonempty, independent sides X in A and Y in B. Their sums equal a nonzero u in U. Their spans intersect exactly in the line generated by u: any second common vector would give a different nonempty shorter dependence by the uniqueness of expressions in each independent side. Conversely these conditions suffice for X union Y to be a circuit. Each side's trace in U therefore contains that line. At least one trace equals the line when dim U<=2, because two larger traces would both be U and would intersect beyond the line.

Enumerate u in U other than zero and guess which side has this exact trace. On that side, say A, project its columns modulo U. Require a single circuit in this quotient with lift label u and one guessed residual gain bit. Lift labels are U-components for an arbitrary linear splitting of V_A over U; on a quotient dependence their sum is the true sum of its original q-columns. This test has at most three label bits and a nonzero target (u is nonzero). It is equivalent to independent X in V_A with sum u and trace exactly the line generated by u. In one direction a projected circuit has just one nontrivial dependence, with lift u, so its original columns are independent. In the other direction the trace line and independence imply that the full support is the unique projected dependence.

For B, which need not have exact trace in all of U, project only modulo the line generated by u. Require a quotient circuit with scalar lift coordinate one and its guessed residual gain bit. This is equivalent to independent Y summing to u and places no extra restriction on its trace in U. Every crossing dependence then lies in the exact-side trace line, so the union is a circuit. Check phi_A(u)+phi_B(u) plus the two actual residual vectors against the desired gain. Iterate over the two possible exact sides and the constant number of u values and residual bits. If U is zero no crossing circuit exists. The two or three labelled coordinates of any nonzero target reduce to as many rooted independent columns by placing basis columns in the added label space whose sum is that target. The resulting queries are polynomial-time binary three-terminal tests.

For completeness, with internal zero target and scalar residual gain, pick a basis of the underlying side and check its fundamental circuits. If a fundamental circuit has gain zero it suffices. Otherwise all have gain one. Two distinct fundamental circuits sharing a basis element have nonempty symmetric difference. Pivoting on that shared basis element in the first fundamental circuit turns the second into their symmetric difference, itself a fundamental circuit of gain zero. If no two share a basis element, all fundamental circuits are disjoint, and every other cycle is a union of these disjoint circuits; hence there is no zero-gain circuit. With zero-dimensional residual gain, any circuit suffices.

## Recognizing the (2,1,1) split at global gain rank four

Let G be the vertical subspace (0,g) in the span of all lifted columns (q_i,g_i), so dim G is global cycle-gain rank. Suppose dim G=4. For a split define lambda_Q=r_Q(A)+r_Q(B)-r_Q(A union B), and define lambda_hat by the same expression on lifted ranks. Rank-nullity of projection gives

    lambda_hat = lambda_Q + dim G_A + dim G_B - 4.

For a valid split the right side is at most zero. Nonnegativity forces equality, lambda_Q=2, both local ranks one, and lambda_hat=0. Hence the lifted spans must be complementary, so A and B are unions of connected components of the lifted matroid. Conversely such a component assignment with local ranks one gives lambda_Q=2 by the same formula.

Compute lifted components by connecting elements within the fundamental circuits of any basis, leaving loops or coloops as singleton components. Their column spans V_i form a direct sum. Compute coordinate projections pi_i:G→V_i in this direct sum and their kernels K_i in the four-dimensional space G. For a bipartition of components, G_A equals the intersection of K_i over components placed in B, and G_B equals the intersection over components placed in A, identifying vertical vectors with their gain coordinates. A dynamic program begins with (G,G). Putting component i in A replaces the second subspace by its intersection with K_i; putting it in B replaces the first. Retain a predecessor for each resulting pair. At the end, a pair with both dimensions one is exactly the required split, and its predecessors recover the column partition. The binary four-space has 67 subspaces, so there are at most 4489 states per component. The overall test is polynomial, without a supplied partition. This recognition statement assumes global gain rank four.
