# Four prescribed elements of a binary circuit

Given an explicit binary matrix and four distinguished columns T, seek a circuit of unrestricted length containing T. If T is dependent the answer is YES exactly when T itself is a circuit. Assume T independent, write U=span(T) and t=sum(T). An ordinary set X with span(X) intersect U equal to the line generated by t exists exactly when a circuit through T exists. Combine a minimal expression for t with the terminals for the forward implication. For the reverse, any additional vector in the intersection would force a proper subcircuit via a partial set of terminals. Equivalently a circuit of the ordinary columns projected modulo U has residue t. Indeed any proper projected dependency in a minimal representation of t would be an original dependency or a shorter expression of t.

Compute L=U intersect span(ordinary columns). If t is absent reject. Otherwise replace the four terminals by any basis of L summing to t. Such a basis exists by an invertible change of coordinates carrying its old basis sum to t. The ordinary vectors are unchanged and keep the same trace-line criterion. Thus dimension at most three reduces to Seymour's triple solver or ordinary connectivity. For an inclusionwise minimal YES restriction retaining T, the nonterminals plus T are themselves a circuit; consequently decision suffices for witness recovery by at most n ordinary-element deletions.

Equivalently there is an ordinary B of size rank(M)-3 such that every B union (T minus {i}) is a basis. Starting with the minimal expression above, extend by ordinary columns independent modulo U until they span the ambient quotient. This preserves its trace line. Conversely the four terminal images modulo B then form a circuit and the minimal representation gives a witness. Thus there are FOUR special common-basis tests on B. In ambient F2^4 with standard terminals and every nonzero vector except 1111 as ordinary columns, candidates B have size one and their four complementary minors are the coordinates of the ordinary vector. Every proper subfamily of the tests can pass but there is no simultaneous solution. This concerns conjunctions of individual existence tests and does not exclude a different algorithm.

## Primary sources and scopes

Fomin, Golovach, Lokshtanov and Saurabh, Spanning Circuits in Regular Matroids, require a supplied GF(2) representation. Theorem 5 gives deterministic FPT in the terminal count for regular matroids. Proposition 7.1 concerns the length-bounded Minimum Spanning Circuit even at one terminal. Its hardness does not classify unrestricted length at four terminals on arbitrary GF(2) matrices. https://fedorvf.github.io/articles/2019/2019k.pdf

Geelen, Gerards and Whittle, Triples in matroid circuits, 16 November 2026, discuss Seymour's polynomial triple algorithm for binary matrices in Section 2. Their new result concerns at least three of four independent elements, with a structural statement in the vertically five-connected case. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20960

Unrooted fixed planar minor testing does not preserve specified ground elements. For example the direct sum of a four-circuit and four isolated terminal coloops has an unrooted four-circuit minor but no circuit containing its designated terminals. https://homepages.ecs.vuw.ac.nz/~whittle/pubs/towards_a_structure_theory_for_matrices_and_matroids.pdf

No primary source verifying deterministic or randomized polynomial time, or hardness, for four arbitrary designated elements at unrestricted length in a general represented binary matroid was located by this audit. This conclusion describes the literature search rather than asserting a comprehensive open-problem classification.

## Graphic contraction

For a graphic ordinary contraction Q=M/T, choose a linear section of the projection and label each edge by its residual U-coordinate. A quotient circuit has gain equal to its ordinary-column sum in the terminal span. For a loop test its label directly. For every other edge e=xy call the path solver in G-e with ends x,y and requested gain t+label(e). Restoring e gives a simple cycle and joining its lifted element set to T returns a circuit. Huynh's Theorem 1.1 is deterministic for each fixed finite abelian group and number of demands. Section 1 explicitly permits parallel edges, defines a path to have no repeated vertex, and treats traversal against the orientation by negating the edge label. Over F2^4 this is the same as unsigned XOR. https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34307v1#S1

## Affine-plane reduction on general binary contractions

Check all loops and parallel pairs of Q directly for the desired residue t. Collapse duplicates of the same label within one nonloop parallel class, as t is nonzero and a longer circuit uses the class at most once. If the class has four alternative labels forming an affine plane a+H, with H of dimension two, remove the class temporarily and put back just one parallel representative of gain a. Project every label modulo H and append a bit recording use of the representative. Ask for a quotient circuit of label (t modulo H,1). This uses three label bits, reducible to three independent terminal columns whose sum is the nonzero target via the exact line-trace construction. The representative is required and every other original class member was removed, so any returned circuit can use a genuine plane alternative correcting its gain to exactly t. Conversely every longer solution using a plane option descends. If the test fails, discard precisely those four plane options permanently. A solution using two class members would have passed the initial two-circuit test. Each iteration reduces the number of columns. If no remaining class contains a plane then its unordered pair sums are distinct (in characteristic two any repetition uses four distinct points). Since t cannot occur, at most fourteen nonzero sums are available. Thus every residual class has size at most five. This local bound can be attained by zero and the four unit labels with t=1111. Simple projected binary matroids remain unrestricted.

## A shared binary switch

Fix nonzero delta. A nonloop class is flexible when its option-label set is invariant under translation by delta. For t=delta the presence of any flexible class already gives a two-element solution. Otherwise quotient labels modulo delta and retain one option per class and quotient label. The new target is nonzero. If the rigid-class restriction has no circuit attaining this quotient target, any circuit returned by the full three-bit oracle uses a flexible class. Flip its chosen element to gain the missing delta if necessary. In a two-element circuit the two projected labels differ because the target is nonzero; hence this flip never duplicates the other selected element. Conversely any solution descends after quotient-coalescing, since conflated pairs would have zero quotient score. This proves a further full-rank tractable regime, including systems with only two options in every class and no available affine planes.

## Affine two-determinant obstruction

Let T be four independent units and t their sum. Fix ordinary columns all six pair sums and one triple sum. This main part contains the spokes and rim pairs of M(K5) and is connected, with ordinary span containing T. Add independent new coordinates z,w. Add ordinary columns z,z+r,w,w+z+v, where r is the sum of the first two terminal units and v ranges over the sixteen vectors in span(T). The represented matroid is simple and connected for every v. In fact suppressing the two series columns with w coordinate leaves probe z+v. The columns z,z+r extend the main connected part by a triangle on its existing element r; the nonloop z+v lies in that extended span even if parallel to some fixed column. The series subdivision of the nonloop probe preserves connectedness and removes any parallelism involving it.

In the suppressed rank-five representation a circuit containing all four terminals either uses one main-part column equal to t (none is present), or two columns outside the terminal span whose sum is t. Pairing z with z+r gives only r; the probe must therefore pair with one of these. Exactly the two syntax points v=t and v=t+r are YES.

Suppose a uniform binary-matrix transformation to linear matroid parity over a characteristic-two extension field permits only one pair of columns to vary affinely with v and keeps the remaining scaffold fixed. Each positive witness has a nonzero minor whose determinant has Boolean degree at most two in v. Soundness requires this determinant vanish away from the two YES points. Composing with a binary linear functional nonzero at a successful value gives a nonzero Boolean polynomial supported on at most two points. Its degree must be at least three: if it is a singleton indicator the degree is four; if both points occur it is, up to an invertible affine change of coordinates, the indicator that three coordinates vanish, of degree three. This contradiction also applies to a union of independently sound tests. For two-matroid intersection with just one varying affine column in each matroid, take the product of the two success-minor certificates (or the binary projections of both), again of degree at most two. This is a limitation of a direct affine reduction format, not a complexity lower bound on arbitrary representations, preprocessing, or algorithms.
