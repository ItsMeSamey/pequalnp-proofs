# Rank-one additive graphic perturbations with two prescribed path components

Let A=B+uγ over GF(2), where B is the incidence matrix of an undirected finite multigraph and γ is an edge-gain row. Let R be a nonempty set of prescribed edges. If R is the disjoint union of one or two paths, then for fixed |R| there is a one-sided randomized polynomial-time algorithm returning a verified A-circuit containing R, with no host, sign or demand restriction. The new pruned-host query itself is deterministic polynomial for arbitrary path lengths. The same full algorithm applies to three or more paths whenever the computed root projection has rank at most three, or when the required full projection is inconsistent. For u=0 the ordinary fixed-root graphic cycle problem suffices; below take u nonzero.

Run the existing [gain-one circuit algorithm](../gain-one/README.md) and [two-odd-cycle algorithm](../gain-zero/README.md) on the original host. The former has one-sided error; the latter is deterministic for fixed |R|. Every net-zero A-circuit is either a single balanced simple cycle or a union of two odd simple cycles sharing at most one vertex. It therefore remains sufficient to preserve every candidate balanced simple cycle, and to ensure that any extra witness returned is a genuine original A-circuit of either type or gain.

Delete every nonroot edge incident to a strict interior vertex of any prescribed path, including loops. Keep all prescribed edges. This restriction retains every simple cycle containing R: at each internal vertex that cycle uses both path edges, leaving room for no other incidence. A circuit in the pruned restriction remains a circuit in the original represented matroid.

## Root-coordinate projection

For every binary matrix M and required set R, let Z be the image of ker M under coordinate restriction to R. If the all-ones vector on R is absent from Z, no rooted circuit exists. Otherwise take a basis S among the coordinate functionals on Z. Restriction from Z to S is injective, hence a dependency whose coefficients at all S are one has all of R. Therefore a circuit containing S exists exactly when a circuit containing R exists. Gaussian elimination computes Z, the consistency test, and S. A binary circuit through at most three specified columns can be decided in polynomial time and recovered by deletion self-reduction. Zero, parallel and dependent columns retain their identities throughout. [Binary triple theorem, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.20960v1#S2)

In the pruned host let c be the number of prescribed paths. At a strict interior vertex v with consecutive required edges i,j, any A-dependency x satisfies

    x_i+x_j=u_v γx.

Thus on each component its first edge bit and the single common gain bit determine all remaining required bits. The root projection therefore has dimension at most c+1, and at most c if every strict interior is outside supp(u). For c≤2 the preceding binary triple test always decides the pruned restriction. For c=3 it also works when the actual rank is at most three, including the neutral-interior case. More generally, failure of consistency decides the pruned branch at any rank. The other two algorithms on the unpruned graph protect all witnesses that pruning might remove. When none of the branches returns a witness, the only possible false negative comes from the gain-one branch. The simple-cycle search does not require separating every gain-one or two-cycle witness from a simple witness, since each positive answer already solves the original query.

The bound c+1 need not drop at three components even when the full root pattern is consistent. Take three disjoint required paths ab,bc; de; fg. Add ac and a parallel mate each for de and fg, all of gain zero as are the prescribed edges. Add an odd loop at a and take demands {a,b}. Bridges cd and ef may connect the host. Each full path's root indicator is independently realized by its private balanced cycle. The pair consisting of ab and the odd loop realizes an additional A-dependency with just ab on the roots. These four root projections are independent and span the all-ones vector. This shows a genuine rank-four instance for this compression; it is not an impossibility result for other algorithms.

## A separate exact net-zero query

For one or two path components, the pruned host also admits an exact query for a net-zero rooted circuit, allowing either single or double ordinary cycle. Select a representative r_i per component. Let λ=γ+1_{r_1}, augment the original matrix A with row λ, and add a new port f=(0,1) in the new row. A circuit through f projects to an A-circuit of λ-gain one. Indeed, its support on the original A columns has zero A-sum and λ-sum one. A nonempty proper A-subdependency would lift to a strict subdependency either by itself or together with f, according to its λ bit. Conversely every A-circuit of λ-gain one lifts together with f to a circuit.

Ask for a circuit through f and the at most two chosen ordinary representatives. Since r_1 occurs, λ=1 on the projected ordinary circuit implies γ=0. Its ordinary incidence boundary is therefore zero. At each degree-two internal vertex both required edges occur or neither does, and the representatives force all paths. Conversely every pruned net-zero rooted circuit lifts with f. One can apply the same triple test after first contracting the remaining prescribed columns X in this augmented matrix: if X is dependent no desired circuit could contain it properly. Otherwise any quotient circuit has a unique lift with some subset of X. The propagation argument forces that subset to be all of X. Contraction is optional.

If there is no unpruned feasible two-odd-cycle witness, any successful exact query here returns a balanced simple cycle. If the two-odd-cycle branch is positive, the full problem has already been solved, independently of the separate existence of a feasible single cycle.

root_rank.py implements Gaussian projection and construction of the reduced root set using an exhaustive three-port reference oracle. test_rank.py compares 3,000 random cases against full-support enumeration, verifies 600 further cases on a three-path host whenever the rank criterion settles them, and confirms a consistent rank-four example. contract.py implements the independent optional augmented contraction reduction, with injectable circuit oracle. test_contract.py checks 6,428 matrix/demand/root combinations, including exhaustive gain and demand assignments on two hosts. It verifies the exact pruned net-zero answer and that combining it with the unpruned two-cycle branch preserves all net-zero decisions. All positive supports are directly checked in the original matrix. The polynomial algorithms use the external binary triple solver, not the finite enumeration oracle.
