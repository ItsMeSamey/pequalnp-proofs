# Rooted circuits under graphic lifts and sparse additive perturbations

Write A=B+UV with k perturbing directions. A circuit C has rank B[C]≥rank A[C]−k=|C|−1−k. Thus its restriction in B has nullity at most k+1, including for arbitrary regular references.

This does not bound the number of reference paths. Let B be the binary incidence matrix of a matching of m≥4 edges with columns b₁,…,bₘ. Set u=Σbᵢ and add u to column one only. The resulting columns sum to zero and their last m−1 are independent. The entire ground set is the unique circuit and contains any four designated edges. Its reference restriction has nullity zero, m components and 2m leaves. The same construction on any tree gives a connected counterexample with arbitrarily many branch vertices.

## Pure signed-gain coextensions

For the stacked matrix A=[B;V] on a simple undirected graph, every A-dependence is an even graph edge set of zero binary V-gain. On any nonempty candidate C the exact circuit test is that every degree in C is even, its total V-gain is zero, and the gain map from the cycle space of B[C] has kernel dimension exactly one. The all-edges vector then generates that kernel. This permits graph-disconnected circuits.

For one gain bit, such a circuit is either a single ordinary cycle of gain zero, or two edge-disjoint gain-one cycles sharing at most one vertex. Indeed β≤2, and an even edge set decomposes into edge-disjoint simple cycles. If it has two, a second common vertex gives β≥3. Conversely the two proposed gain-one cycles form a cycle-space basis on their union and their sum generates the kernel. For k gain bits the bounded template algorithm below applies with no defect vertices, adding the V-gain as separate matrix rows.

## Sparse additive columns plus optional coextension

Assume B is the full binary vertex-edge incidence matrix of a supplied simple undirected graph and A consists of B+UV with optionally stacked gain rows W (say h rows). Let D be the union of the vertex supports of U and d=|D|. For fixed k,h,d and t prescribed root edges there is a polynomial-time rooted-circuit search. An additional fixed number of edge-gain bits with a prescribed total can be checked without adding kernel constraints. The bound is XP in these parameters.

For an actual solution C, its odd-degree vertices belong to D by B1_C=UV1_C. Its cyclomatic number β is at most k+h+1 by the rank inequality applied to the stacked system. Count only vertices incident with selected edges. There are at most d leaves. The identity Σ(deg−2)=2β−2c bounds the number of degree-at-least-three vertices by 2β+d, for c nonempty components. Mark all vertices of the solution of degree different from two, all its D vertices, and all endpoints of the prescribed edges. In every component still without a mark add one anchor; such components are cycles and there are at most β. Suppress unmarked degree-two vertices. The resulting multigraph has O(k+h+d+t) vertices and edges by the cyclomatic identity. It may have parallel chains or loops (returning chains). Each prescribed edge is designated as its own chain of length one.

For any A-dependence in C, the B-boundary is supported in D. At every suppressed vertex the two coefficients on its incident edges must agree. Thus each dependence is a union of whole chains. Conversely the boundary of a union of chains is the parity vector of their template endpoints. Construct the small matrix with one column per chain: use the binary endpoint sum (zero for a loop) plus U times its V-gain, and append its W-gain. Keep every original D coordinate, including those absent from the solution. Its kernel expands bijectively to the A-kernel on C. Accept a nonempty template exactly when its kernel is generated by its all-ones vector and the desired root and optional total-gain conditions hold.

Enumerate these bounded templates, images of their distinct vertices, and chain gains. Guess direct edges or, on each longer chain, its first and last incident edges. Require distinctness of selected edges. On a length-two chain the two fixed edges meet at a single interior vertex; reserve it, check its gain, and forbid its use elsewhere. All guessed interior vertices must avoid D and the template vertices. All remaining interior endpoints must be distinct and disjoint from reserved length-two interiors. Delete all template vertices, unused D vertices, and reserved length-two interiors. For the remaining chains request vertex-disjoint simple paths between their guessed distinct interior endpoints, with the prescribed residual binary gain after subtracting the two fixed edges. For a returning chain guess distinct first and last edges at the same anchor; length-two returning chains do not occur in a simple graph. Check that each prescribed root is the required direct edge. A linkage plus the fixed edges realizes the edge subset represented by the template even if there are unused chords in the ambient graph. The number of guesses is n^{O(k+h+d+t)} times a function of the fixed parameters. The polynomial linkage oracle is external. [Huynh, The k-disjoint paths problem for group-labelled graphs, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34307)

For a pure rank-one additive perturbation and a full dependence C with V1_C=1 and u≠0, C is a circuit exactly when C contains no nonempty ordinary B-cycle of gain zero (here a cycle may be any cycle-space vector). Any other A-dependence on C either already has gain zero, or differs from 1_C by a gain-zero dependence. Conversely any nonzero gain-zero B-cycle lies in the A-kernel and differs from 1_C. In particular the B-cycle space on C has dimension at most one. In any rank-k pure additive case with B1_C≠0, V must be injective on the cycle space of a circuit C, yielding β≤k.
