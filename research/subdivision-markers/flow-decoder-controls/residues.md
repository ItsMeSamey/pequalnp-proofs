# What fixed residues can detect about cubic flow existence

Let F(G) count nowhere-zero flows with the three nonzero elements of the labeled group F₂² on a simple cubic graph. At each vertex these labels must be distinct. No orientation is needed. This note concerns summaries of the set of assignments. The original decision is unchanged by all the replacements below.

## A transparent two-terminal piece

Cut an edge of a connected bridgeless cubic graph H, retaining two terminals for reconnection to another graph. In any proper labeling the two boundary colors agree, by summing the flow equations. For each prescribed nonzero boundary color there are exactly F(H)/3 completions, by closing the piece back into H and permuting the three colors. Replacing an edge of another cubic graph with the piece therefore preserves all exterior colorings whenever F(H)>0. Serial pieces multiply the common multiplicity. The replacement keeps a simple connected bridgeless input simple, connected and bridgeless.

K₄ gives two completions per color. The odd n-prism (n≥3) gives (2ⁿ−2)/3. To see the second identity, properly color the top n-cycle. The number of possibilities is the trace of the nth power of the 3-by-3 matrix J−I, namely 2ⁿ−2. The spoke colors are forced and copying the top onto the bottom works. If another bottom agrees at one cycle edge, the common spoke forces agreement at the next. If it agrees nowhere, the incident colors are swapped at every vertex; hence the top color word is 2-periodic, impossible at odd n. Independent edge backtracking at n=3,4,5,6,7 returned 6,24,30,72,126.

For fixed M=2ᵃm with m odd, choose odd n≥3 with 2ⁿ⁻¹=1 modulo 3m. Use one n-prism piece and a K₄ pieces. Every boundary color then has positive completion count divisible by M. All exterior-predicate counts vanish modulo M, including those for nonlocal predicates on the exterior assignment. With k+1 disjoint such pieces in series, any predicate on at most k queried physical edges leaves at least one private interior untouched. Condition on the connector colors and the other interiors to obtain divisibility by M. These conclusions apply to summaries consisting solely of these residues. A solver may use other input information or simplify the pieces.

## Arbitrary linear probes at binary precision

Replace one source edge by k K₄ pieces. For each fixed valid exterior assignment all connector colors are fixed. Each piece has exactly two private labelings. In the standard two binary bits per edge the fiber is an affine k-flat, with independent difference vectors on disjoint private interiors. Expressing flows in any linear cycle basis preserves the affine flat. Every fiber of any collection of b affine binary probes has size zero or a multiple of 2^(k−b) for b≤k. Consequently for k≥a+b every queried count is zero modulo 2ᵃ, even when the probes use every edge. This also covers a decision tree applying ≤b such probes per assignment, by summing the disjoint leaf counts.

A Boolean predicate of positive algebraic degree at most D on those coordinates restricts to degree at most D on the private bits. Its weight on k private bits is divisible by 2^(ceil(k/D)−1) for k≥1. In its multilinear normal form, expand the xor of monomials by ordinary integer inclusion-exclusion. A term involving j monomials has coefficient divisible by 2^(j−1), and their union has at most min(k,jD) variables. The weight of each such term is divisible by 2^(j−1+max(k−jD,0)), hence by the stated power. A constant predicate has weight zero or 2ᵏ. Thus k>aD makes all these counts vanish modulo 2ᵃ for every exterior assignment, and therefore after summing over exterior assignments. This is a direct weight-divisibility argument, with no novelty claim.

There is a genuine distinction at an odd prime p. The Walsh matrix on binary assignments satisfies HHᵀ=2ᴺI, so it is invertible modulo p. Every nonempty 0/1 flow indicator has some nonzero Walsh coefficient, and therefore some dense one-bit parity fiber with nonzero count modulo p. This existence argument gives no procedure for finding the mask in polynomial time. None of the residue obstructions applies to exact support tests.

## A pair of connected graph covers

For a connected bridgeless non-3-edge-colorable cubic graph G, such as Petersen, compare two 2-lifts. The bipartite double cover is connected (G cannot be bipartite) and has a proper 3-edge-coloring by the perfect matching decomposition of a regular bipartite graph. Switching only one nonbridge edge between the two sheets gives another connected 2-lift. Its two switched edges form a 2-cut. Their colors would agree in a flow and closing either sheet would give a flow on G, which is impossible. Every vertex-local message iteration started from labels pulled back from G gives identical per-fiber messages in both covers, by induction. Hence even an unrestricted readout of those multisets alone cannot decide flow existence. Access to the sheet switching data can distinguish the covers.
