# Substitution of native exceptional digraphs

Write YES(D) for existence of an odd vertex set in a loopless directed graph for which every selected vertex has odd selected outdegree. Let D=Q[D_i] be a substitution of nonempty digraphs into a loopless directed quotient, with complete directed joins precisely on quotient arcs. Then YES(D) is the disjunction of YES(Q) and all YES(D_i). A child witness lifts by leaving other children empty. A quotient witness lifts by taking one arbitrary vertex in every selected child. In the reverse direction, record each child's parity y_i in a given odd witness. The external field on that child is h_i=(Qy)_i. If some y_i=1 with h_i=0 then the child restriction is itself an odd witness. Otherwise y witnesses the quotient. In particular a supplied substitution tree with internal quotients Q_t uses at most one local search query per quotient. An internal witness expands by choosing one original representative of each selected child and ignoring the rest of the tree. If only decisions are available, extracting witnesses must instead use a justified local search algorithm. At width k (maximum arity) each quotient can be searched by enumerating at most 2^k subsets.

## Arbitrary offsets and arbitrary dense markers

For every original vertex v impose x_v(p_v+sum_{v->w} x_w)=0. Each vertex carries a label in F2^b, and the XOR of selected labels must equal a supplied target. On a supplied substitution tree use the table T[h,z,a] counting selections with external added bit h, ordinary parity z, and label sum a. At a leaf the empty selection always contributes at z=0,a=0; the singleton contributes at z=1 and its given label iff p_v=h. At an internal node enumerate the child parity vector u and h. Let h_i=h+(Qu)_i. The label-sum distribution of selections for this u is the XOR convolution of T_i[h_i,u_i,*]. Add it to T[h,sum_i u_i,*]. At the root add the two ordinary parity entries at h=0 for the requested label. This gives the ordinary number of solutions. With one bit marking every vertex and all p_v=1 it specializes to the preceding decision law.

The Walsh transform makes each convolution cost O(k(b+1)2^b) integer operations for k children; computing the fields adds O(k^2) per parity vector. Intermediate coefficients need O(n+b+log(k+1)) bits: each forward or product entry has magnitude at most the number of candidate selections, at most 2^n, and an unnormalized inverse may add another b bits. At traceback select a positive summand and use prefix convolutions to find child label vectors recursively, testing all 2^b choices for each child. No calls to a feasibility oracle are needed. The tree is supplied; assigning private representatives or splitting nodes is unnecessary for the dynamic program.

## Polynomial calculus composition

On a NO substitution suppose the children have PC refutation degrees d_i using their own odd markers, and the quotient degree is d_Q. Derive R_i=Y_i(1+h_i), where Y_i is the parity in child i and h_i the external field. Multiply that child's entire local refutation by R_i. Its marker vanishes because Y_i(1+Y_i)=0. Each child guard differs from its global guard by x_v h_i, which R_i annihilates. The required guarded axiom products follow from the global rows by linear combinations and multiplication by individual variables from the two factors of R_i, of degree at most four. Simulating every later step costs degree at most d_i+2 by following the original formal derivation, multiplying its lines by R_i, and multilinearizing with Boolean axioms at the same degree. Finally substitute block parity forms in the quotient proof and use these derived R_i as its guards. This yields degree at most max(4,d_Q,2+max_i d_i). Iteration costs at most two degrees per substitution level beyond the maximum local degree and four. This degree bound applies to standard binary PC with Boolean axioms.

A sharper bound is independent of substitution depth. For each node B let H_B be the common external field and derive R_B=Y_B(1+H_B) bottom-up. Leaf statements are input guards. At an internal B, the previously derived R_C for child C equals its quotient guard plus Y_C H_B. Multiply the local quotient refutation by R_B, which annihilates every difference and the local odd marker. Multiplying an already derived R_C does not multiply its earlier derivation. The resulting bound is max(4,d_root,max_nonroot(d_Q+2)), taking the final root quotient proof without an extra multiplier. Here d_Q ranges over local quotient refutations. For bounded quotient arity this yields bounded degree even on trees of unbounded depth.
