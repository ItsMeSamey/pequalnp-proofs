# Role-splitting a directed Hamiltonian instance at four named columns

The equality question fixes two independent pins p₁,p₂ in the span V of indexed columns t_e, each labeled o_e∈F₂². Seek a linear s:V→F₂² vanishing on both pins whose agreeing columns s(t_e)=o_e span V. Equivalently, some ordinary column basis interpolates both pins to zero. A linearly consistent agreeing set spanning the pins extends to such a basis: keep an independent spanning subset of the set, extend it by any physical columns to a basis, and interpolate their labels.

Directed Hamiltonian cycle is NP-complete on planar digraphs with indegrees and outdegrees at most two. The following attempt to force a Hamilton cycle at bounded pin rank instead transmits a cycle cover. Its precise behavior admits polynomial algorithms in the cases below. [Plesník, 1979](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190%2879%2990023-1)

For each arc u→v use the column b_e=L_u+R_v, giving distinct outgoing and incoming roles to each vertex. To express the full role demand p=Σ_v(L_v+R_v) every role must have odd degree in the chosen arc set. Under the degree bound this requires exactly one occurrence, so the selected arcs form a perfect matching of the bipartite role graph, equivalently a directed cycle cover. A role component lacking a perfect matching immediately rules out a full-cover pin. Distinct directed cycles in a cover need not be connected. For instance take two directed triangles a→b→c→a and d→e→f→d, with further arcs a→d and e→a. The graph is simple, planar, strongly connected and has the required degree bound. Its articulation a precludes any Hamilton cycle. The triangles give a cover. With all offsets zero the role presentation accepts the independent pins p and b_(a→b).

## Arbitrary arc lifts

Allow columns t_e=(b_e,g_e) in an arbitrary binary lift dimension. In particular, appending the unsplit incidence e_u+e_v is redundant: it is the common row map L_i,R_i↦e_i on the role incidence. The same holds for any appended a_u+b_v. The augmented span in that case is the graph of the row map. Every valid pin and every basis transports back to the unlifted role instance.

The bipartite role graph is a union of paths, isolated vertices and even cycles C_i. Let Γ_i=Σ_{e∈C_i}g_e and O_i=Σ_{e∈C_i}o_e. A physical dependence is exactly a union of entire role cycles indexed by a relation of the matrix Γ with columns Γ_i. Every path edge is consequently a coloop. For arbitrary pins in V, one can decide the equality problem in XP time parameterized by min(rank Γ, number of cycles minus rank Γ). In the unlifted case the algorithm is polynomial even for two arbitrary dense pins and offsets.

Indeed, write c for the number of cyclic components and r=rank Γ. A basis keeps every path edge, keeps r cycles J whole with Γ_J an independent basis, and omits exactly one edge of every other cycle. This follows either from the dependence description or by counting rank |E|−c+r. Enumerate feasible J via the smaller of J and its complement. Choose any physical representation xⁱ of each pin pᵢ by Gaussian elimination. A second representation toggles full cycles by ε_i∈ker Γ. For a nonwhole cycle h, omitting its particular edge e forces the toggle ε_ih=xⁱ_e. For j∈J the required toggles follow linearly, since Γ_j (j∈J) is a basis. If a_jh are the basis coefficients of Γ_h, define Q_h=O_h+Σ_{j∈J} a_jh O_j. Then omitting e in cycle h changes the interpolated pin values by the pair (x¹_e Q_h,x²_e Q_h), relative to the scores of the reference representations. Changes from distinct cycles add independently in F₂⁴. A 16-state XOR dynamic program tests the required correction and backtracks the omitted edges. On a purely endpoint-separable lift all Γ_i vanish, so a single such computation suffices.

## Two distinct pins demanding full covers

For any lift rank, take p₁=(p,d₁), p₂=(p,d₂), with d₁≠d₂, and two-bit offsets. The two pins are then independent whenever p≠0. An arbitrary lift can impose many parity bits in d₁,d₂. Even in this setting, equality feasibility reduces to one linear system and a polynomial binary three-terminal query.

Choose a base role matching F₀. Every matching is F(x)=F₀ Δ (union of C_i with x_i=1). Write d₀ and o₀ for the aggregate lift and offset of F₀. First solve
`Γx=d₁+d₀, Ox=o₀` by elimination. Second, find z with
`Γz=d₁+d₂, Oz=0` whose supported Γ columns are independent. These two tests are necessary and sufficient, and there is no coupling between the chosen x and z.

For necessity an agreeing basis interpolates both pins, whose unique representations within it must be matchings F(x) and F(x+z). Their union is a subset of that basis. Its complete role cycles are exactly the cycles with z_i=1. Thus Γ restricted to those positions is independent, and summing lift and offset equations gives the two tests. For sufficiency, any such x and z determine two matchings whose label sums vanish and whose union is independent by the same dependence characterization. Extend their union to a basis and interpolate.

For the second test form ordinary columns (Γ_i,O_i) in the product of the lift space with F₂²; let a=(d₁+d₂,0), and take the two pure offset axes b,c. Seek a subset whose span meets span(a,b,c) precisely in the line generated by a. Independent support z gives that trace. Conversely a set with that trace contains a representation of a. Every Γ-relation among its columns has zero offset, by the trace property. Repeatedly discard a relation from within the representation until the Γ support is independent. This proves equivalence to the established polynomial exact-line algorithm in a three-dimensional terminal space. Decision-to-witness deletion, followed by back-substitution and the displayed matchings, returns a full basis. [Three-terminal line recognition](../../span-separation/three-dimensional-traces.md)

This calculation rules out the stated cover construction as a hardness proof unless P=NP. It covers unlimited lift rank for the two cover pins and limited rank or corank for arbitrary pins. It leaves other combinations of dense pins and unrestricted lifts open.
