# Forest joins, dense markers and opposite-unique signature cores

For a graph, componentwise even demand D, and k prescribed edges, the characteristic-two polynomial decides existence of a minimal D-join containing all k edges in 2^k poly(input,log(1/ε)) randomized time. False negatives have probability ≤ε, with no false positives. After a nonzero evaluation, deletion/contraction at the same field point extracts a verified witness. Its monomials index spanning trees, several of which may extend the same join. [Audited Pfaffian algorithm](../four-terminal/djoin/pfaffian/README.md)

For a predicate on s physical edges, enumerate patterns, delete negatives and prescribe positives, at total oracle cost O*(3^s) in the absence of other pins. An affine score L=UB+VP_S, with B the graph incidence matrix and P_S projection to those edges, satisfies Lx=Ud+Vx_S on joins. This is a genuine composition for a supplied decomposition. One marker row can require arbitrarily many exceptions modulo cuts: label one edge of each of arbitrarily many edge-disjoint triangles. Every cut is even on each triangle. For b forbidden pairs one may branch 2^b ways deleting one endpoint of each pair. The SAT image has 3m forbidden pairs even at zero positive pins, and the decision problem with arbitrary such pairs is NP-complete at host bandwidth 19. A randomized polynomial algorithm for that enlarged problem would give NP=RP and a deterministic one would give P=NP. [Forbidden-pair theorem and graph checks](../forest-forbidden-pairs/README.md)

## Independent-support dense checks

For a binary matrix B, seek x with B-independent support satisfying Bx=d and Lx=h for q arbitrary marker rows. At d=0 only empty support is independent. Otherwise lift each ordinary column to (Bₑ,Lₑ) and take t=(d,h), K=0×GF(2)^q, U=K+span(t). Add prescribed columns P=k₁,…,k_q,t+Σkᵢ where kᵢ are a basis of K. They form a basis of U with sum t.

A feasible support S sums to t in the lift, and B-independence makes the vertical kernel of its span trivial. Thus span(S)∩U=span(t). The only nonzero relation on S∪P uses all columns, giving a circuit. Conversely a circuit containing P yields such an S, since any second vector of its trace on U would produce a proper relation using a proper subset of P. Exclusion of nonzero vertical vectors and lifted independence of S give B-independence. Conversely every independent q+1-terminal binary circuit problem can be expressed this way by taking K complementary to the line through the terminal sum in the terminal span and splitting into quotient and K coordinates. For a dependent terminal set, a circuit can contain it exactly when it already is one.

The binary three-elements algorithm therefore gives deterministic polynomial-time decision and witness recovery when q≤2, including graphic forest D-joins with arbitrary dense gains. Delete ordinary elements preserving feasibility to recover a circuit. For q=3 this equivalence already expresses a general four-terminal problem on binary inputs. If the *lifted augmented* representation is regular, the regular spanning-circuit theorem applies. When deleting a required terminal leaves an aligned graph incidence representation, that terminal is a boundary demand and the other required terminals are physical pins. The Pfaffian route then applies. An independent reviewer compared the reduction on all 10,222 subsets of 160 finite instances. ## Opposite-unique cores and a composition

The cited theorems assume two distinct four-bit target scores have unique raw disjoint cycle/cocycle witnesses (A,B) and (B,A), partitioning the ground, and a third score is absent. Freely available common offsets can first be quotiented. In a maximal-A fundamental chart one clean column signature, including gain and incidence on exceptional rows, implies at most one clean column. Exactly two augmented signatures on each clean shore imply precisely eight ground elements. A large graph does not become an eight-element kernel merely by having four marker bits. Neither uniqueness nor score absence follows from an ordinary forest witness. A graphic example with four pinned coordinates requires two separate primal circuits and two dual cocircuits in every valid witness. [One-signature theorem](../rooted-core/one-signature-exclusion.md) · [Two-signature theorem](../rooted-core/two-signature-core.md) · [Four-primitive example](../four-split/README.md)

The recorded concrete eight-element core attains every four-bit score except the value 14. Let an independent host obey Bx=d with B-independent support, and let Lx be any dense four-bit score. If the core and host are physically disjoint and their sole coupling is XOR of their scores required to equal g, feasibility means some host score differs from g XOR 14. Four one-row deterministic host tests suffice. Recover the core witness at the resulting score by enumeration on eight elements. Two independent copies of this particular core already attain all sixteen summed scores since any two 15-element subsets overlap after any shift. This uses its explicit histogram rather than the one- or two-signature bound alone.

More generally let M be a supplied list of h forbidden host scores on q bits. For a disjoint XOR-coupled core these would be the translated unreachable scores. To recover a global witness, require a core witness finder as well as the valid complement list. If h≤6 the host can be solved using at most q+32 independent-support queries with at most two dense rows apiece. For empty M ask raw feasibility. Otherwise write aff(M)=m₀+W, with dim W≤h−1. Outside this affine hull, inspect the at most q basis checks of its annihilator for a violated check; each is one dense row. Inside the hull enumerate its at most 32 points y. Ignore forbidden y. For the distinct nonzero differences m+y, a random nonzero functional on their span kills each with probability strictly below one half. Thus some functional leaves at most two differences undetected. A second functional maps all remaining differences to one, since up to two distinct nonzero vectors over GF(2) are linearly independent. Extend both functionals to the whole space and test for their values at y. This fiber excludes every forbidden score even if it extends beyond the hull. The necessary searches are on at most five-dimensional spaces and can be deterministic. Seven holes can defeat any union of such fibers: for M the seven nonzero vectors of a three-dimensional subspace, every codimension-two affine fiber covering the allowed zero intersects M. This is a sharp limitation for the covering scheme, with no intractability claim.

For any supplied h one may still test whether this covering method suffices by enumerating each allowed point inside the affine hull and all pairs of test functionals on W. The cost of this test is 2^O(h)poly(input), and outside-hull points still need only one row. The general case beyond the covering condition remains separate.

**Every extremal seven-hole obstruction has this form.** Fix a target y∉M, put D=M+y, and suppose |D|=7. If x is a sum of two distinct elements of D but x∉D, projection modulo the line through x sends D to at most six distinct nonzero vectors. The preceding two-check separator for zero pulls back to a separator of D, contradicting failure. Thus failure forces D∪{0} closed under sums, hence a three-space. Conversely, every codimension-two subspace intersects a three-space nontrivially. Therefore a seven-hole list admits the two-row algorithm except when its holes are an affine three-flat punctured at one point. In that case any point outside the affine hull still takes only one check, and once those possibilities have all been excluded, only the puncture remains. Three functionals separating points of the flat suffice to test it at that stage. This classification also explains all 15 size-seven obstructions in the independent four-dimensional finite census.

In dimension three, choosing all seven nonzero scores as holes requires Lx=0. Thus the exceptional host problem already exactly expresses an arbitrary binary four-terminal circuit query, using the two-way construction above for independent terminals. If that construction gives a nonzero target h, first choose u with u·d=1 and replace L by L+h uᵀ B, so that Lx=h on Bx=d becomes (L+h uᵀ B)x=0. This is a complexity equivalence relative to the explicit circuit question, without a claim of hardness or a general polynomial algorithm for four terminals. In particular, a zero-score component realizes this dimension-three exception.

A [finite checker](check_dense.py) compared all 10,136 subsets in 160 seeded binary matrices with the lifted circuit test. It also checked all 16,384 sets of at most seven nonzero four-bit holes, observing exactly the 15 punctured three-space obstructions. An independent algebraic review verified both directions of the lift, the score-complement algorithm and the equality classification. No novelty claim is made.
