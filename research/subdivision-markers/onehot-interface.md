# A one-terminal classification on two finite choice domains

Observe a one-hot vector e_u from a domain U and independently e_v from V. For arbitrary native affine guards with a single terminal clause, exactly the following relations on U×V can be accepted: the rejected pairs form a bipartite graph whose nontrivial components are complete bipartite graphs.

Indeed the native one-terminal projection theorem, valid also for cyclic guards, puts every accepted observation on an accepting flat of codimension at most one. In binary space the complement of the union of all such flats is an intersection of affine hyperplanes, possibly empty. Write this affine system on the independent observations as Bx+Cy=d. On the one-hot inputs this is equality between a signature B[:,u] assigned to u and a signature C[:,v]+d assigned to v. Equality signatures give the stated component form. Conversely give matching signature keys to the two sides of each rejected biclique. Isolated vertices can be assigned unmatched keys. The accepted relation is the disjunction of coordinate inequalities of the two signatures, each an affine marker literal, with no hidden choices needed. Empty and full relations are covered.

Consequently the relation that accepts only one specified ordered pair cannot be implemented as a one-terminal test when both domains have at least two elements: on any 2×2 submatrix containing that pair, the rejected graph is a three-edge path. More generally, specifying one value on each of k independent domains of size at least two needs at least k native affine terminals. Restrict to an affine Boolean k-cube in the one-hot representation by letting each domain use the chosen value and one alternate. The accepted set meets that cube at exactly one point. Any accepting flat through the point must cut out a singleton there, which requires at least k independent equations. k direct unit-marker equations attain the bound. These results concern projective tests on given upstream observations, not an impossibility proof for the synthesis of an arbitrary transducer when additional terminal resources are allowed.

An explicit upper bound for arbitrary bipartite relations uses ceil(log2(min(|U|,|V|)+1)) terminal equations. Suppose V is the smaller domain. For each u attach an independent native binary choice tree with its root guarded by the observed coordinate x_u, and one leaf for each accepted neighbor v of u (omit empty trees). At most one tree can be active. Its path may stop early, or choose one acceptable neighbor leaf. Assign V distinct nonzero binary codes of the stated length. Equate the XOR of the codes of all selected leaves to the XOR of codes of the one-hot observed v. Since the right side is nonzero and at most one leaf is selected, the input accepts exactly when that v is a neighbor of u. A one-leaf tree needs only its guarded root. This bound gives a concrete native compilation, without asserting optimality beyond the one-terminal characterization.
