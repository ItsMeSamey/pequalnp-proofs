# Cographic ternary choices meeting at a common endpoint

Assume a connected cographic representation on 2n edges in n pairs, of rank n+1, with an independent set containing one edge of each pair. Assume in each pair its two edges join a common vertex to two distinct other vertices. Parallel edges from different pairs are harmless. A target is given either in the represented vector space or as an edge mask f. Convert between them by solving the linear representation. The choices hit the target iff there is a vertex coloring y such that x_e=f_e+y_u+y_v has at most one nonzero in each pair. This is the defining equality of the cographic representation kernel with the binary cut space.

For the pair (uv,uw) the forbidden event is that both edge bits equal one. Equivalently the three signed colors y_u, y_v+1+f_uv, y_w+1+f_uw are all equal. Thus each pair gives one signed not-all-equal clause. The rank condition gives n vertices. The complementary n edges of the supplied independent transversal form a connected spanning unicyclic multigraph. Any j pairs therefore involve at least j vertices, since their j complementary edges form a subgraph of a pseudoforest. Hall's theorem and ordinary bipartite matching assign each clause a distinct variable as a pivot. Here all n variables are assigned.

For each signed clause with pivot i, draw arcs from i to both other variables j, with label 1+sign_i+sign_j. To satisfy that clause, the coloring must realize one arc equation y_i+y_j=label. A satisfying coloring chooses one realized outgoing arc per vertex. Conversely a function choosing one outgoing arc per vertex supports a coloring precisely when the label XOR vanishes on all its directed cycles, by propagation from each cycle through its incoming trees. A choice with this property exists exactly when each sink strongly connected component has a simple directed cycle of label XOR zero. Necessity follows by following chosen arcs in a sink. For sufficiency choose one such cycle per sink and, outside their union, select arcs decreasing distance to the cycles. This also supplies the desired coloring and edge selector.

Replace each label-one arc by a directed path of length three and each label-zero arc by a path of length two, with private internal vertices. A simple directed cycle has even length precisely if the corresponding original simple cycle has label XOR zero. The even-directed-circuit algorithm is deterministic polynomial time; a cycle can be recovered by greedily deleting arcs while the decision remains positive. [Robertson, Seymour and Thomas, Annals of Mathematics (1999)](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/1999/150-3/p05)

The proof applies directly to any signed NAE formula on distinct variables per clause admitting a matching of clauses into variables, even without a cographic origin. Unmatched variables in that general form are free terminal vertices: paths reaching one can stop there and only sinks with no free vertex require a balanced simple cycle. The cographic application uses local pairs and the stated representation. General cographic pairs need not define three-variable NAE clauses.

## Recognizing the graphic condition directly on the marker slice

Restrict the original singleton native guards to a nonempty hyperplane Hx=h. The forbidden event for guard i is the intersection of two affine equations (the active bit is one and its guard is false). Discard inconsistent forbidden events; a codimension-zero forbidden event immediately rejects. In the dual of the slice directions, take the union N of all nonzero normals of every surviving forbidden flat, deduplicated. Each flat contributes either one normal, or three of the form u,v,u+v. If the binary matroid represented by N is graphic, obtain a graphic representation by constructive graphic-matroid recognition. Its representation has the same binary linear dependencies, so its image of vertex colorings as edge differences is precisely the image of all evaluations of the original slice directions on N. Affine shifts are carried in the forbidden values. [Bixby and Wagner, An almost linear-time algorithm for graph realization](https://doi.org/10.1287/moor.13.1.99)

The three different rank-two normals are a triangle in this graph. The forbidden pattern specifies two independent edge differences, equivalently one consistent set of relative colors on the triangle. It forbids exactly the constant assignments on three appropriately signed vertex literals. A rank-one flat gives a signed two-literal NAE clause on its endpoints. For a set of j surviving flats, the union of their normal spaces has rank at least j−1, since it contains the restrictions of the j corresponding distinct pivot coordinates to the marker hyperplane. Their incident graphical vertices number at least rank+1 and hence at least j for j positive. Thus a distinct pivot per NAE clause always exists; if there are free vertices the extension rule above applies. This gives a polynomial algorithm recognizing and solving this native class, with witness recovery by Gaussian elimination on the original slice.

More generally the same algorithm applies to arbitrary collections of forbidden flats of codimension at most two whenever the augmented normal matroid is graphic and a matching of the associated NAE clauses into vertices is found. The one-marker native hypothesis guarantees that matching for every graphic realization.
