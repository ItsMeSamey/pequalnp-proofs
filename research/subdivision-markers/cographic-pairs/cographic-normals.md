# Cographic normal matroids for forbidden affine flats

Given an affine parameter space over F₂ and forbidden flats of codimension at most two, decide whether their union can be avoided. The number of markers and flats is unrestricted. Discard empty forbidden flats; a forbidden whole space rejects immediately. Collect the distinct nonzero linear functionals from the entire normal space of each remaining flat in a set N. A codimension-two flat contributes three distinct vectors u,v,u+v. If the binary matroid represented by N is cographic, avoidance and witness recovery take deterministic polynomial time.

Obtain a graphic representation of its dual. The evaluations of the normals, as the parameter varies, range over the binary cycle space of the resulting graph G, since their dependencies are the cut space. Any computed evaluation lifts to original parameters by Gaussian elimination. Vertex-identify components to make G connected, preserving their direct-sum cycle spaces. Loops (coloops in N) are independent and enter only unary restrictions; handle them directly. N is simple, so G has no bond of order one or two. If it has any rank-two constraint, its minimum cut has size three. [Constructive graph realization](https://doi.org/10.1287/moor.13.1.99)

A unary forbidden event fixes its edge to the opposite bit. A rank-two event corresponds to a three-edge bond; its four possible even patterns are the evaluations of u,v,u+v. Delete the forbidden pattern and intersect all restrictions at the same bond. Obtain the cut shore not containing a designated root by graph search after deleting its three edges.

These rooted shores are laminar. For crossing size-three minimum cuts, the elementary cut inequalities give opposite corner cut sums at most six for both diagonal pairs. Each corner has at least three incident edges, so each has exactly three, and the diagonal intercorner edges disappear by equality in the corresponding cut inequalities. If the four cyclic interface multiplicities are a,b,c,d then all adjacent sums equal three, so a=c and b=d. The original cuts now have even sizes 2a and 2b, a contradiction.

Use the inclusion tree of the shores and the full vertex set. At each region compute all achievable patterns on its boundary subject to descendant constraints and vertex parities; intersect its own restriction. The root boundary is empty. For each state, contract every maximal child to a factor carrying its previously computed three-socket signature, and keep other vertices as even-parity factors. Edges inside a child were already processed. All other edges connect two sockets or give an exposed parent-boundary socket. An edge can cross nested cuts: its same bit persists in consecutive tables, and wires joining different child factors are allowed.

Feasibility of this factor network reduces to perfect matching. A wire occupies both endpoint sockets for bit one. An even factor of degree d is a clique on its d sockets plus a dummy if d is odd; after occupied sockets are removed its remaining vertices match exactly for even occupancy. Child signatures contain only even triples, by handshake. Choose an allowed baseline t (an empty table rejects). For each index i form a socket p_i and private mate q_i. Join q_i q_j precisely when t+e_i+e_j is allowed. Occupied sockets free exactly their mates; this gadget realizes the allowed deviations from t. For each t_i=1, expose a new socket adjacent only to p_i in place of p_i, inverting the external bit. Hence every even three-bit signature is realizable. For fixed physical bits, remove sockets occupied by prescribed one-bits, and prohibit wires for prescribed zero-bits. Apply the same convention to exposed parent boundary sockets. Reject inconsistent prescriptions.

At most four matching calls per shore and polynomial-size matching instances suffice. Record matching solutions, descend the tables to recover an even subgraph, and lift by linear algebra. This is a decision and search reduction; matching multiplicities need not equal parameter multiplicities.
