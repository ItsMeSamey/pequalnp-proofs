# Arbitrarily many one-dimensional interfaces

Consider the supplied tree of binary vector-space pieces in the [aggregate port-tree theorem](../aggregate-port-tree/README.md). A global witness is two words in ker D of prescribed k-bit checksum, whose common zero columns are independent. The physical construction has k+1 names. A child has a port U in the torso and supplies two imbalance vectors in U, a score, and the trace of its zero-column span on U.

**Theorem.** There is a deterministic polynomial-time witness algorithm for fixed k, maximum port dimension s, and maximum number of untreated higher-dimensional child ports incident at one piece. There is no bound on the number of one-dimensional children. Thus arbitrary-degree trees of supplied 1- and 2-sums of graphic and cographic pieces are covered. An arbitrary number of higher-dimensional children can also be treated by the explicit rank certificate below. A root orientation and representations are part of the input. Finite pieces of bounded size with bounded interfaces can use direct enumeration.

## Affine witnesses on a line

Use the full real-column matrix A of the child subtree in its displayed space. Write U=span(p) where p is nonzero. The two-bit value v specifies its boundary contribution vp in the two components. If p is outside span A only v=0 occurs. Any spanning-column basis B of A gives a witness with all nonbasis colors nonzero by assigning those colors first and solving on B. Its zero trace is empty. Keep this single baseline.

Otherwise let P be the unique support in a real basis B summing to p. Prescribe all cotree chord colors except one as fixed nonzero values. For a chord q whose fundamental circuit meets P at a basis site e, the conservation equation makes the color at e equal to b+v+z, with b a fixed two-bit vector and z the color of q. Choose complementary rank-one projections π₁,π₂ of the two-bit space. Set z=d+π₁v. Choose d outside im π₁ and outside b+im π₂. These two transverse affine lines contain only three of the four points. Then q and e have nonzero colors at all four imbalances. All chord colors are nonzero and all other colors follow affinely by basis solving. Zeros lie in B minus e, so their span misses p. This baseline has empty trace for every v and affine score F(v)=c+Lv.

If no chord fundamental circuit meets P, every member of P is a coloop of A. In every witness its color is v, as follows by reading its basis coefficient in each conservation equation. At v=0 the zeros necessarily span p. At v≠0 the same coloop coordinates prevent p from belonging to the zero span. Assign all nonbasis colors fixed nonzero values and solve on B. All four witnesses again have affine score. The baseline trace at every allowed imbalance is contained in the trace of every actual child witness at that imbalance. These cases give direct Gaussian constructions.

## A certificate for larger ports

Use the effective port W=U∩span A. All imbalances lie in W, and every trace lies in W. The zero- and one-dimensional classifications apply when dim W≤1, even for a larger geometric port. Because every nonzero direction of U has a represented virtual column, torso directions outside W can be removed.

For dim W=r≥2 take a real column basis B. Let P be the matrix of basis coordinates of a basis of W and let H consist of the basis coordinates of the nonbasis columns. If a set E of r basis rows has both P_E and a square minor H_{E,Q} invertible, the child has an affine witness of empty trace at every imbalance in W². Set every chord outside Q to a fixed nonzero color. Give all chords of Q second bit one. Solve their first bits using H_{E,Q} so that all r positions E have first bit one under the required first imbalance. Solve both lanes on B. Thus every chord and every site in E is nonzero. Since P_E is invertible, W meets span(B minus E) only at zero. Both the physical colors and their scores depend affinely on the imbalances. For a fixed supplied or computed B, a certificate can be found in polynomial time at fixed r by enumerating E. The test makes no completeness claim over all choices of B. The checker uses this formula also in the r=1 mobile case.

## Replacing all but k baseline children

In any feasible coloring of a torso and its limbs, record the difference between each eligible child's actual score and its baseline score at its actual imbalance. The total difference is the sum of at most k members of this list. Take a basis of the observed differences and express the total in it. Keep those children exceptional and replace the others by their baseline witnesses. Conservation and the global score survive. Joint independence survives because each replacement shrinks or preserves the trace at the child's interface. The new witness also passes the same outside test at the parent. For the exceptional children use the previously proved finite one-witness-per-outside-test portfolios, replacing one witness at a time. Enumerate at most k exceptional eligible children per torso. Treat the boundedly many untreated higher-dimensional children and the parent test explicitly by the pinned compilation in the port-tree theorem.

For each ordinary, nonexceptional line child fold c into the target and put linear tag L on the one real torso copy of the port direction. Its zero-column contribution matches the obligatory line trace exactly at v=0. For a transparent child, replace each chosen basis direction of W by two parallel copies with the same linear score tag for that direction. Delete the virtual slots for the remaining directions. The two copies can both be nonzero with any required sum, including zero. Added zeros in a solution can only strengthen the torso independence test. Produce the baseline child of their actual net imbalance when lifting and include its affine constant once. A child with W=0 uses only its constant and all of its virtual slots are deleted. The explicit exception choices cost at most a polynomial factor n^k. Baselines themselves are computed on the flattened binary subtrees, independently of the graph solver. The unboundedly many unexceptional children add no pin coordinates.

For graphic torsos physical column deletion or duplication is ordinary graph edge deletion or duplication. For cographic torsos use graph edge contraction and series subdivision, respectively. The resulting graph may have parallel edges or loops. For a graph cut assignment a loop has zero edge color and no effect on connectivity of the bichromatic subgraph. Parallel graph edges have the same color, can be merged by summing their tags, and change connectivity in exactly the same way as their representative. Solve components separately by checksum convolution if needed, expand the colors, and recheck the physical witness. This extends the connected-simple cographic solver to the local instances required here.

## A limitation already on a regular triangle

Take three real columns forming the binary triangle in U of rank two, and the three virtual directions represented by parallel copies. All 16 imbalance pairs admit a witness with every real color nonzero. Indeed, their conservation equations leave one free two-bit color with at most three values forbidden. Since each real column is a nonzero element of U, an empty trace requires every one of those colors to be nonzero. An everywhere-nonzero affine two-bit map on the four boundary bits has linear rank at most one. The three affine color maps together therefore have rank at most three, whereas conservation would make their composite equal the identity on four boundary bits. Thus an empty-trace affine witness family need not exist on rank-two ports, even with a regular physical local matrix. This rules out one method of accepting every higher-dimensional child. The particular triangle itself can be routed by retaining its three actual columns.

## Verification

Run `python check.py` and `python hubs.py` beside the sibling `unseeded-root-states` elimination helper. The checks obtained 102 charts with 2,017 feasible boundary records, compared all baseline traces with all attainable traces on those children, verified affinity on every represented input, and checked 200 forty-child score compressions. The triangle check verified all 16 imbalance pairs. Sixty full stars with four to nine limbs gave the same 10,380 attainable imbalance/score/outside-test records before and after limiting exceptions. Independent enumeration additionally tested 750 binary line children and 7,498 feasible boundary records.

The specific aggregate root construction admits these algorithms. Its common-zero independence condition still permits a Petersen witness when strict nowhere-zero flow fails. The theorem supplies neither an unrestricted regular-host algorithm nor a decision-equivalent reduction from cubic three-edge-colorability.
