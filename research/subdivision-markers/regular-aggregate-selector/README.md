# A fixed-checksum aggregate selector on every represented regular host

**Theorem.** Let D be a binary matrix representing a regular matroid. Each column e has an input linear tag T_e from two binary bits to k binary bits. For each fixed k there is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm to find two words f,g in ker D with prescribed checksum and linearly independent common-zero columns, or to decide none exist. The algorithm returns a physical contraction witness for the corresponding k+1-name aggregate-line selector. Regularity is a condition on D, the two-word host. The physical matrix with its private directions and tag roots is constructed from D and can be nonregular. [Aggregate predicate, physical matrix and backlift](../aggregate-flow-selector/README.md)

We use the graphic and cographic single-piece algorithms, the outside-test tables on a tree of represented sums, and the line-port baseline. The new step treats an arbitrary number of rank-two triangle interfaces, including regular triangles with no everywhere-safe affine color family. ## Local algebra

Consider the real matrix A of a whole child subtree and its two-dimensional boundary U in the common space. The effective port is U∩span A. If its dimension is at most one, use the line algorithm on that intersection and delete the unused virtual directions. Otherwise U lies in span A. A two-lane imbalance is a pair a,b in U. For a covector h of U write σ(h)=(h(a),h(b)).

In a real basis write A=[I|H] and let P express a basis of U in these coordinates. Denote row i of P by h_i and row i of H by R_i. For chosen colors z_j on the nonbasis columns, the forced basis color at i is σ(h_i)+Σ_j H_ij z_j. The basis positions with R_i=0 are precisely the coloops of A. The mobile covectors are the h_i with R_i≠0. The following list covers every binary A. Graph structure is used later to realize the replacement in the adjacent torso.

### Two independent mobile covectors

Pick i,j with independent h_i,h_j and nonzero R_i,R_j. If the latter rows are different, they are independent over the binary field. Choose two chord columns making the restricted two-by-two matrix invertible. Give these two chords second bit one and solve their first bits so that basis positions i,j both have first bit one. Other chords have fixed nonzero colors. All zeros lie in the basis minus i,j, so the zero-column span misses U. This is an affine witness in σ.

If the two rows coincide and meet at least two chord columns, give one such chord second bit one and another first bit one. All remaining chords have fixed nonzero colors. The two free bits set both components of the common added color at rows i,j independently. Set them so that i has first bit one and j second bit one. This gives the same empty-trace affine family. Both cases use the transparent, duplicated-basis replacement already proved.

It remains that both rows have just one chord q in common. Retain the three native virtual triangle directions u₁,u₂,u₁+u₂, with u₁,u₂ dual to h_i,h_j. For a free two-bit color z their three colors are

    w₁=σ(h_i)+z,    w₂=σ(h_j)+z,    w₃=z.

Give the actual chord q color z, all other chords fixed nonzero colors, and solve on the real basis. Physical positions i,j receive w₁,w₂. All real colors and scores are affine in w₁,w₂,w₃, so independent input tags on the triangle and a constant reproduce the complete physical score.

Whenever the three proxy zero columns are independent, the actual zero columns are independent. The only possible nonbasis zero is q. A dependence would require its entire fundamental circuit, including i and j, to be zero, making all three proxy colors zero. Furthermore the actual zero span meets U only inside the span of the zero proxy directions. For u represented by real zero columns, let ε be the q coefficient. If w₁≠0 then h_i(u)=ε, and if w₂≠0 then h_j(u)=ε. A nonzero ε requires w₃=0. Thus u is a combination of zero proxy directions. Conversely for every σ one can choose z outside {0,σ(h_i),σ(h_j)}. All three proxy colors are then nonzero and the lift has empty trace. This free triangle coordinate handles the failure of a single affine empty-trace family on a real triangle.

### Mobile covectors of rank zero or one

Remove the coloops of A as an independent direct summand. When the mobile rank is one, call its nonzero covector h₀. The projection of U into the remaining summand is a line, with prescribed color σ(h₀). This summand is coloop-free, so the line theorem gives an affine witness whose zero span avoids the projected line at every color. At mobile rank zero use a fixed basis-supported zero-set witness on the remaining summand. Every real coloop of type h has the forced color σ(h). Coloop multiplicities change the linear checksum, but introduce no extra trace distinction.

These recipes yield a score affine in σ and the smallest possible trace at each imbalance. Its elements are the u in U satisfying both conditions below.

- Every mobile covector annihilates u.
- Every occurring coloop covector h with σ(h)≠0 annihilates u.

Indeed such a u lies entirely in the real coloop summand and is represented by zero coloops in every witness. In the displayed witness a nonzero mobile projection cannot be in the zero span, proving the reverse inclusion.

Use one local leg for each occurring nonzero covector, with the mobile type counted once even when also present on coloops. The evaluated coordinates embed U injectively. If only two types occur, they give coordinates of U and there are two independent legs. With three types the embedding identifies U with the even plane in three independent leg directions. The color sum on the leg for h is σ(h). An ordinary leg represents each coloop-only type. Replace the mobile leg, if present, by two parallel copies whose colors have the required sum. They can both be nonzero. Assign the affine score's linear part to any two independent type coordinates, giving both copies of a doubled leg the same tag. The affine constant is included once. Ordinary zero legs give exactly the claimed minimum trace when the doubled leg has no zero. Other solutions can only add restrictions. With two types the unused third torso direction is deleted when implementing the two basis legs directly. Equivalently one contracts a missing leg of the three-leg representation; making such a leg transparent would impose an incorrect trace constraint.

## Realizing and combining the replacements

The three independent legs with the even plane boundary form the standard binary triangle-to-Y exchange, obtained by attaching the cycle matroid of K₄ on its triangle. In a graphic torso replace an actual triangle by a new center and its three spokes. The leg for h is incident to the triangle vertex common to the two boundary edges on which h equals one. In a nonplanar cographic torso the chosen decomposition makes each triangular port a three-edge star with distinct neighbors. Replace that star in the underlying graph by the triangle on its neighbors. The leg for h is represented by the neighbor edge opposite the old spoke spanning ker h. This correspondence follows by evaluating the boundary colors as differences of vertex potentials. Coindependence of the three old cographic elements ensures that the corresponding graph edges are independent, giving distinct outside neighbors. Distinct virtual stars are edge-disjoint, so their centers are nonadjacent. All required triangle/Y operations can therefore be made before any subsequent ordinary deletions, contractions, subdivisions or parallel extensions. The latter operations leave each resulting local query graphic or cographic. Normalize cographic loops and parallel edges by the preceding line-port theorem.

In a feasible global solution choose for each nonexceptional-candidate child a baseline at its actual imbalance. Choose an all-nonzero proxy whenever the full-mobile triangle case allows it, and both nonzero copies on every transparent leg. The proxy trace is contained in the actual trace. At most k actual-minus-baseline score differences are needed to reproduce their total. Retain those children as exceptions; each other child is represented by its unpinned native proxy, with constants folded into the torso target. Guess this exception set in polynomial time for fixed k. In the reverse direction every returned proxy solution lifts with exactly the computed score, independent actual zeros, and actual trace contained in the span of the zero proxy columns. Internal private directions of each Y are disjoint from all other pieces modulo its U, so equality of an ambient space dimension is not needed. The previously proved joint outside-test portfolio handles exceptions and the parent test, using only boundedly many extra pins. This gives deterministic polynomial time per piece with unlimited incident triangle ports. Disjoint bases of distinct trace subspaces are an indexed list, so cross-port dependencies are detected. At the root the imbalances are zero and the outside test is empty. The final coloring lifts to the original k+1-root physical matrix.

For the decomposition, a represented regular matroid admits a polynomial-size tree of graphic, cographic and R₁₀ pieces glued by 1-, 2- and triangle 3-sums. The 3-connected refinement puts the cographic nonplanar triangle interfaces at degree-three vertices. Use a graphic realization for doubly graphic/cographic planar pieces. The polynomial construction is stated in Bérczi, Mátravölgyi and Schwarcz, Section 2, Theorem 2.5 and its algorithmic discussion. The combined unoriented decomposition including the 1- and 2-sums is stated explicitly in Streit, Proposition 2.2 and Appendix A. In the initial 1-/2-sum decomposition, a preserved singleton anchor occurs in one refined bag, while the new 3-sum slots are disjoint from it. No R₁₀ piece has a triangular interface, so its bounded number of genuine columns can be handled by direct enumeration. The regular binary sum representation is the tree pushout used here: in a triangle, two local three-bit boundary lifts having equal vector sum differ by the all-ones triangle relation, hence can be made equal before deleting the virtual triangle. A one-element boundary has a unique lift. This also identifies the actual real columns and their original tag maps through the decomposition. ## Executable controls and scope

Run `python check.py`, `python check-y.py`, `python check-triangle.py` and `python check-hubs.py` here, with the sibling `aggregate-many-ports` and `unseeded-root-states` helpers. The first control checked all 45,137 feasible physical colorings found on 150 small binary matrices against all four branches of the classifier and validated 3,340 proxy lifts. Independent checks covered 10,080 admissible triangle lifts, 10,240 affine-tag equations and 768 low-mobile imbalance states. Twelve rank-two hub spectra agreed on 7,168 attainable imbalance/score/outside-test records when replacing child choices by local proxies with at most k exceptions. The scripts implement the local classification and exhaustive finite controls. The polynomial-time theorem uses the cited regular decomposition and graph routing algorithms as subroutines.

This gives a decision and witness algorithm for the aggregate relaxation on regular D. The strict nowhere-zero problem can disagree with that relaxation already on the Petersen graph. General binary D, arbitrary fixed-root represented minors, and a decision-equivalent fixed-root reduction from SAT or cubic three-edge-colorability remain separate problems. No standard P versus NP conclusion follows.
