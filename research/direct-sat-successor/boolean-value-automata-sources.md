# Source and scope of the wire compiler

The rank characterization has an immediate reduction to Nisan's theorem. Over GF(2), form the homogeneous free polynomial of degree n whose word coefficient at the binary word x is the Boolean wire value f(x). At cut i its prefix/suffix coefficient matrix is exactly the matrix of f on raw prefixes and suffixes. A layered two-matrix program for f is a homogeneous ABP for that free polynomial, with each edge labelled by the linear combination of the two noncommuting letters given by its two bit-specific coefficients. This identifies the per-cut characterization here with the classical ABP rank theorem. [Nisan, STOC 1991](https://doi.org/10.1145/103418.103462)

Carlyle and Paz proved a foundational real-valued finite-rank realization theorem. Field-valued forward and backward weighted-automaton spaces and Hankel minimization are also treated explicitly by Kiefer. Our fixed-length implementation applies these constructions over GF(2) at each wire and synchronizes both operand matrices on the same original input bit. The artifact supplies an affine-AND netlist frontend, measured construction budget, and original-word SAT witness recovery and replay. [Carlyle and Paz, JCSS 1971](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-0000(71)80005-3) · [Kiefer, field-valued equivalence and minimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.01217)
