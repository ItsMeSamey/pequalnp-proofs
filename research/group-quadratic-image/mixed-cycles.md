# Cycle scores and the cut-image test

Each edge of an undirected multigraph has two weights in a finite abelian group G of order g and exponent m. Select an even edge set C and a cut disjoint from it, with prescribed total score. For a fixed shore, C is an even subgraph of the monochromatic edges.

## Compressing the cycle with arbitrary group weights

Fix a successful shore. Among its even edge sets with the prescribed cycle score choose C inclusion-minimal. It contains fewer than g pairwise edge-disjoint simple cycles: among g+1 partial sums of the scores of any g such cycles two agree. Removing the intervening cycles preserves the score, even degrees and cut disjointness.

The cycle rank β of C is O(g log(g+1)). Repeatedly suppress degree-two chains of the Eulerian graph, representing each pure cycle by a loop. Apart from pure cycles each kernel component has minimum degree at least four and at most 2β vertices by the degree sum. A graph of minimum degree at least three on at most 2β vertices has a cycle of length O(log(2+β)): handle loops and parallel edges first, then grow a breadth-first tree up to half its girth. Delete all expanded chains of a shortest kernel cycle. This reduces β by at most its kernel length, since deleting one compressed edge costs at most one dimension of the cycle space and suppressing chains preserves β. It leaves even degrees. There are fewer than g rounds since the expanded cycles are edge-disjoint. Consequently β≤O(g log(2+β)), giving the asserted bound without requiring additivity under symmetric difference of overlapping cycles.

## Simultaneous low-cut testing

Run the scalar subgroup-growth test in README.md on the cut quadratic. At a bounded threshold R its low characters have at most g(2R+1) pinned vertices U and finitely many joint vertex types outside U. Low cut scores depend on U’s bits and counts of selected vertices of each type modulo 2m. To realize the low equations together with the cycle score, first reduce C as above. Keep colors on touched vertices. Retain at most 2m−1 additional positive vertices of each type outside C and U by dropping others in multiples of 2m. Enumerate the bounded Eulerian topology, placements of its branching vertices and anchors, component colors, extra positives, bits on U, and which vertices of U belong to C. Insert these last vertices as anchors in the topology; forbid other U vertices and extras on linkage paths.

The labelled subdivision method for graphic markers computes the cycle score and counts each positive support vertex of a given type modulo 2m. Give an edge uv the sum of the two endpoint type indicators modulo 4m. Subtract the two endpoint indicators for a fixed path; the resulting residue is twice the desired strict-interior count. Count anchors separately. When stretching the graph put labels of new vertices equal to zero in every type coordinate and place original edge scores on just one subedge. The path predicate uses unsigned sums, as in [Liu and Yoo](https://people.tamu.edu/~yyoo/stoc25.pdf). Loop, parallel-edge and port handling follow the graphic multiple-marker solver. This is deterministic XP in the group and low threshold, since anchor and extra-positive placements cost a parameter-dependent power of n. If all characters stop early this gives exact mixed decision and witness search in that regime.

## When the high cut rank survives a cycle

At stable cut thresholds R,S, choose a high block Iχ,Jχ for each character outside the subgroup generated by the low ones. Their union A has cardinality at most 2gS. Restrict C to avoid A. For any C and provisional shore passing the low tests, fix the colors on C’s support and leave every other bit free. High blocks remain untouched. The low feasible fiber still has density at least d(R) from README.md since it has no more pins or joint types: interactions with fixed vertices of C outside U and with U are constant within each remaining joint type. Fourier inversion now proves full target feasibility for this C. Recover an actual shore by solving the restricted group quadratic. For low-feasibility search remove A only from the linkage host; A vertices remain eligible as extra positives.

A wider sufficient condition allows touched anchors. Deleting s vertices from a high block reduces its log₂ image size by at most s log₂m: deleting one column loses one generator and deleting one row projects out a cyclic group of size at most m. For s≤S/(2log₂m) the remaining block has log-image at least S/2 in dimensions at most S. Choose S with the same bias estimate but exponent S/(2log₂m). More generally enumerate C’s intersection with A, turn touched vertices into fixed pattern anchors, and test the retained bound for every high character. Rejection only excludes this class of cycles.

There need not be any avoiding witness. Take a long ring with a pendant leaf at each rim vertex; a binary cycle marker on one rim edge forces all of the rim. Give each pendant edge cut weight one modulo an odd prime and all rim edges zero cut weight. The quadratic interaction block across the rim and leaves has full rank, yet every nonzero cut coefficient touches the forced cycle. Fixing its color leaves an affine cut problem with all residues attainable when there are enough leaves.
