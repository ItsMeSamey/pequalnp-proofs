# Counting through compatible intersections of long clauses

Split a CNF into a short core K and long clauses indexed in fixed order. Remove tautologies and repeated literals. A false long clause forces one assignment to its support. Join two long clauses if their falsifying assignments agree on shared variables. A set of clauses has a joint falsifying assignment exactly when it is a clique, and then its union is a single partial assignment p. For every clique C its signed contribution is (−1)^|C| #K[p], counting all free completions. The empty clique contributes #K. This is inclusion–exclusion on actual assignments. A graph of degeneracy d lists at most 1+m 2^d cliques: in a degeneracy order select a first vertex and any subset of its ≤d later neighbors, checking pairwise adjacency. Hence if K and every fixing of it admit time T(n) counting, the cost is O*((1+m2^d)T(n)). For an empty or affine core and d=O(log n), polynomially many arbitrary-width clauses are permitted, including m=n^c. Sparse compatibility can be checked directly, independently of clause widths.

An exact cutoff combines large intersections with a residual solver. Expand in increasing index order. Once a selected clique first fixes at least n−u variables, replace ALL its extensions by the single signed count of K AND all clauses with larger index, restricted by p. The equality follows by summing their inclusion–exclusion factors ∏(1−v_i). If the number of prefixes before and at the cutoff is L, including failed candidate generation costs polynomial per prefix by scanning m. Total time is O*(L(T(n)+2^u)); the actual costs add separately over the interior and boundary. The empty interior contributes #K. For example, if every jointly consistent h-tuple fixes ≥n−u variables then L≤Σ_{i≤h}binom(m,i); with m=n^c, h=o(n/log n), u=o(n) and a subexponential short-core solver, this is subexponential even for superlinear m. If h=O(1), u=O(log n) and the short core is hereditarily polynomial, this is polynomial. Simplest h=1: count all short-core models and subtract models with a first violated long clause, enumerating ≤m 2^{n−w} completions for minimum long width w and checking all prior clauses.

One obstruction already occurs at polynomial clause density. Take n=bw, w=(c−1)log₂ n+log₂ log₂ n+O(1), c>1, along a rounded subsequence. On each disjoint block select 2^{w−1} different forbidden w-bit words, each as a full-width clause. Then m=Θ(n^c), and compatibility is the complete b-partite graph on parts of size p=2^{w−1}. Degeneracy is (b−1)p; there are (1+p)^b=2^{n−Θ(n/log n)} distinct consistent intersections. For h prescribed blocks the p^h restrictions of depth h each fix hw bits. Separate exhaustive charging of their residuals costs p^h 2^{n−hw}=2^{n−h}, with h≤b=Θ(n/log n). Further short clauses can connect the primal graph without changing long-clause compatibility or the size of these coordinate restrictions. The displayed family admits direct block factorization without the short core, so the obstruction pertains to charging intersections separately, and is not an algorithmic time lower bound.

For general polynomial density the classical deterministic bound gives 2^{n−Ω(n/log n)} time. See [Chan and Williams, Theorem 1.6](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/derand-apsp-ov-cr-talg.pdf). A November 2026 preprint's Theorem 6.2 gives a long-clause reduction with 2^{sn} exp(2^{−sk}(m+n/k)) overhead using a k-width solver of rate s and explicitly identifies the polynomial-density width obstruction. See [Exact amplitudes of sparse quantum circuits, Section 6](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00090v1). The structural bounds above are elementary derivations; no priority claim is made.
