# Sparse Walsh expansion of nonaffine long-clause blocks

Group the designated long clauses by their variable support. For a group with support S and accepted local indicator f, compute its normalized Walsh transform on its truth table. Its local clause list gives the forbidden words directly. For any choice of one supported character from each group, multiply their Walsh coefficients and take the symmetric difference of their character masks. The resulting term is its coefficient times the difference of the even- and odd-parity model counts of the remaining formula on that mask. This identity holds with overlapping supports and any additional bounded-width core. A running XOR chain uses O(n) auxiliary variables and width three, with a unique extension for each original assignment; the empty mask just gives the core count. If e further exceptional long clauses remain, use signed selectors for them: select a subset of violations using one bit per clause, enforce selection implies every literal is false by binary clauses, and include the parity of the selected bits in the joint parity mask. Summing their signs gives exactly the indicator that none is violated. Two bounded-width counts on O(n+e) variables suffice per Walsh term. Enumerate terms sequentially, caching only within the available space budget. The term bound is the product of local support sizes; constructing truth tables costs a polynomial times the sum of their sizes. In particular the following construction has polynomial preprocessing and subexponentially many core calls.

Take b disjoint w-bit blocks, n=bw, with w at least four. On a block write y for the first w−2 bits and u,v for the last two. The accepted predicate is parity(y) XOR (u AND v) equal to zero. For each forbidden word put in its unique full-support falsified clause. The predicate is balanced because flipping any y bit toggles it. It is nonaffine: its half-size model set would have to be an affine hyperplane, but pinning all y except one to zero gives the nonlinear graph y₁=uv. With the character convention χ_i=(-1)^{x_i}, its expansion is

    f = 1/2 + χ_Y (1 + χ_u + χ_v − χ_{u,v})/4.

Thus the normalized indicator has exactly five nonzero Walsh coefficients for every w. The sign predicate itself has four. Without a core the accepted count is 2^{n−b}; with a core K the count is the above exact signed sum over at most 5^b characters against K, and depends on K. Disjoint block masks give exactly 5^b distinct terms before use of the core. No preprocessing restriction is placed on K.

For any fixed c>1 choose growing integer w and b=ceil((2^w/w^c)^{1/(c−1)}). Then n=bw, w=Θ(log n), and m=b·2^{w−1}=Θ(n^c). The Walsh coefficient support gives 5^b=2^{O(n/log n)} bounded-width calls. On this particular family a still faster representation is available: introduce a running parity and one AND gate per block for an O(n)-variable unique-extension bounded-width encoding, needing just one count. The Walsh argument also applies to arbitrary tables with sparse spectra, with no circuit supplied.

With empty core, or any core allowing the witnesses below, several direct criteria have large parameters.

* Semantic rank. Each long clause has an assignment violating it alone: select its word and any accepted word in every other block. Thus the rational matrix of clause-violation functions has rank m. This rank can fall with an arbitrary additional core.
* Compatibility and unions of pins. Different forbidden words on a block conflict, while distinct blocks impose independent pins. The compatible choices have exactly (1+2^{w−1})^b distinct joint pinning cubes, at least 2^{n−b}. Their compatibility graph is complete multipartite.
* Induced matching and neighborhood unions. Use in each block the w−2 forbidden words with u=v=0 and exactly one y bit one. The choice y_i=0 satisfies the negative literal on coordinate i in the clause forbidding the ith word, and none of the positive literals on that coordinate in the other selected clauses. The resulting induced matching has b(w−2) edges, since other blocks contain no such literal. Subsets of its selected choices yield at least 2^{b(w−2)} distinct satisfied-clause unions.
* Signed literal intervals. In any proposed ordering of all 2n signed literals, restrict to one block's 2w signed literals. Deleting other positions cannot increase the number of runs of a clause. At most sum_{j=0}^p binom(2w+1,2j) subsets of the block's ordered literals are unions of at most p nonempty runs, by choosing their boundary gaps. Consequently at least b(2^{w−1}−sum_{j=0}^p binom(2w+1,2j)) clauses need exceptions in every ordering. For fixed p this is (1−o(1))m.

The last three incidence or compatibility parameters depend only on the listed long clauses and are unaffected by adding an arbitrary bounded-width core. They concern direct parameter tests on the displayed representation.
