# Counting circuits through the structure of XOR wiring

Let n be the number of primary inputs, s the number of circuit gates, and z the combined vector of n+s input and gate values. The circuit is acyclic; its non-XOR gates initially have fan-in at most two. For each arbitrary-fan-in XOR gate form its consistency equation. Write the system Dz=c, including one output pivot per gate, and let h be its number of rows. Signed and repeated input wires are absorbed in c and the binary coefficients. Full polynomial descriptions of the circuit and its witnesses below are part of the input.

## A dense quadratic phase with a low-cutrank layout

Given a quadratic polynomial Q on N bits over the binary field, let G be the graph of its cross terms. Suppose an order of its vertices is supplied such that every prefix-to-suffix binary adjacency matrix has rank at most w. The phase bit Q has a constructible Boolean circuit of O(N(w+1)) binary gates. This concerns the cutrank of the given order and allows the global adjacency rank to be large.

At a prefix P, maintain evaluations of a basis of the linear forms on P induced by the neighbors of future vertices. For the next vertex v, compute its past-neighbor form with at most w XORs, multiply by the new bit and add to the quadratic total. Remove v from the future. The remaining span on P is unchanged or has codimension one in the old span. In the latter case write its defining nonzero linear functional in the old basis, choose a pivot basis vector on which it equals one, and replace every other basis vector with nonzero functional value by its sum with the pivot. Dropping the pivot leaves a basis of the desired span, costing at most w XOR gates. Let W be the new span on P plus v of the remaining future rows. Restriction to P maps W onto that desired span. Lift each chosen basis vector to W, changing its evaluation by at most the addition of the bit v, and add the bit v itself as a basis vector if the restriction kernel has dimension one. All these choices follow from Gaussian elimination on known coefficient arrays, independent of the assignment. This proves the gate bound and a polynomial-time compiler. Linear terms and the constant require O(N) further operations.

In particular, for any fixed-width CNF F on N bits, its signed model count weighted by the phase of Q reduces to two unweighted fixed-width counts. Introduce the circuit's unique gate variables and pin the phase to zero or one. If F has O(N) clauses, bounded density gives exponent O(N(w+1)/√log(N+2)); for arbitrary clause density use the general fixed-width bound with the corresponding log log factor (for polynomial w). A full-rank example is a clique on an even number N of vertices. All proper cutranks equal one, but its polar adjacency matrix I+J is invertible in characteristic two. The clique phase itself can also be evaluated from the Hamming weight modulo four.

For circuits, introduce h Fourier bits y. The signed count with phase

    Q(z,y) = yᵀ(Dz+c)

over all z satisfying the ordinary gate relations and the chosen output pin equals 2^h times the circuit's model count. This is character orthogonality applied independently to the h residual equations. If the bipartite graph of D has a supplied order of cutrank at most w, the compiler and two sparse 3-CNF counts use O((n+s)(w+1)) variables and clauses, with deterministic polynomial space. The exponent is O((n+s)(w+1)/√log(n+s+2)), and all intermediate counts require polynomially many bits. Ordinary gate auxiliaries have unique extension. The original gate values define a unique trace once all XOR equations hold. Reading the dense wire lists and verifying the layout require polynomial time. This argument does not require a low global rank or few dense XOR gates.

For the quantitative Williams transfer, use the published unary hierarchy language, decoder, and canonical W in E with an NP oracle. Given a guessed witness circuit with its cutrank order, check the order by binary elimination. Three copies on the three decoded labels may use fresh COPY aliases for their inputs, adding O(n) ordinary gates. Then the three matrices D appear as disjoint blocks; concatenating their layouts has the same maximum cutrank. The counter runs on O(n+s) bits before the layout factor. For fixed w and s≤c m√log m, with m=dn the label length, the counting exponent is less than n/2 for small enough c. The guessed circuit and layout have polynomial description size. The published nondeterministic hierarchy contradiction therefore yields a constant c(w)>0 such that infinitely often W requires more than c(w)m√log m gates in this restricted class. There is no bound on the number of XOR gates separate from the gate count. For unbounded AND/OR as well, apply Wu and Wang's Section 4 selector constraints, enforce the sign of their selectors with the quadratic phase bit, and use the arbitrary-density counter. This substitutes m√log m/log log m in the conclusion.

## Low-rank correction to a shared linear circuit

Alternatively let A contain only the input incidences of the h exceptional XOR gates, excluding their output identity terms. Supply a certificate A=B+UV over the binary field, with V having r rows. Suppose all output bits of Bz can be computed together with t ordinary binary XOR gates. Constants, projections and reused outputs are allowed. The certificate can be checked in polynomial time by evaluating all linear gates symbolically and comparing the resulting matrices. The auxiliary computation of Bz need not respect the original circuit's topological order relative to z. It is used as a constraint on a *fixed* full assignment z.

Enumerate each possible a=Vz. With a fixed, use the ordinary gate relations and the h equations z_out=(Bz)_i+(Ua)_i+c_i. These are sparse local constraints after introducing t unique-extension variables. To impose Vz=a, sum the 2^r characters (-1)^{u·(Vz+a)} and divide by 2^r. Each character needs only one binary chain of at most n+s XOR gates. Two unweighted sparse width-three counts evaluate its sign. Thus the total time is polynomial in encoding length times

    2^{2r+O((n+s+t)/√log(n+s+t+2))}.

The precision for exact signed accumulation is polynomial in the input size. Use simultaneous constraints on z; uniqueness of original gate values is only used after character orthogonality restores all original equations. The three-copy hierarchy transfer gives, for sufficiently small positive c and δ, a witness language with no such circuit/certificate simultaneously satisfying s+t≤c m√log m and r≤δ m at infinitely many lengths. The guessed certificate must be checked. Three disjoint copies cost 3r and 3t. One can also use the general-density signed-selector interface for unbounded AND/OR with the log log loss as above.

The two views overlap. A low-cutrank bipartite layout can implement a full system of parity checks efficiently via its quadratic phase, and a low-rank perturbation of cheaply computed wiring is handled by expansion. Neither argument yields an unconditional acceleration for arbitrary dense quadratic phases. Directly replacing a dense quadratic by the phase circuit above is useful only with a good verified order. These deductions have not been established as publication firsts.

## Comparison

Wu and Wang already handle arbitrary AND/OR and binary XOR by two unweighted counts. Their second interface treats arbitrary XORs and symmetric gates with an explicit exceptional-gate budget, using affine characters and an extension ring. Here the supplied structure measures the wiring graph or a matrix decomposition. It can hold on descriptions with many arbitrary XOR gates even when the consistency matrix has full row rank. This is a change of structural promise, not a new general unrestricted circuit-size bound. ## Branching rank decompositions

The constant-width conclusion also applies when a subcubic rank-decomposition tree is supplied. Root it at a subdivided edge. For each node with leaf set S, store at most w independent linear forms on S given by the exterior-neighborhood rows, together with the value of its internal quadratic. At a merge of A and B, let R_A,R_B be the children’s full-row-rank basis matrices. The cross matrix M between A and B factors as R_Aᵀ H R_B. Its column space lies in the column space of R_Aᵀ and its row space lies in the row space of R_B, so the small matrix H is obtained by elimination. Computing the cross term from the child vectors and H uses O((w+1)²) Boolean operations. Each parent basis vector restricts on each child to a combination of that child’s basis vectors. Thus O((w+1)²) further gates produce all parent parities. Adding the two child quadratics and the cross term gives the parent value. There are O(N) merges. This proves a classical assignment-preserving compiler using O(N(w+1)²) gates. For fixed w the two-count reduction and quantitative lower bound persist. The disjoint union of the three witness copies admits a decomposition of the same width by attaching their trees.

Related quantum work obtains O(Nw) two-qubit Clifford operations for preparing the graph state of a rank-width-w graph in a model allowing measurements. That model produces a state and uses a different gate cost; the elementary argument here computes the phase as a classical Boolean function on a fixed assignment. [Complexity of graph-state preparation by Clifford circuits](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2026-07-18-2165)

### Amortized gate bound on the same tree

The preceding compiler can be made linear in w. Write a,b for the two child boundary ranks, swapping the children if necessary so a≥b. The projection of the parent span onto A is obtained from A’s exterior span by removing the rows belonging to B. Their span has dimension at most rank(M)≤b. Hence the codimension is at most b. Successive codimension-one basis changes as in the linear-layout compiler cost at most ab XOR gates to retain a basis of the projected parent space. Lift each retained form to any member of the parent space. On B each lift is a combination of at most b stored coordinates, giving O(ab) further gates. The kernel of projection lies entirely on B, and a basis costs O(b²) gates. Computing the quadratic cross term through H uses O(ab+1) gates. This implements a merge with O((a+1)(b+1)) gates; all coefficient computations take polynomial preprocessing time.

Assume w≥1. Each merge costs O(min(w,|A|)min(w,|B|)), since the nonempty child sizes make both minima at least one. Nodes whose two child sizes are at least w are branch points of the subtree of nodes of size at least w; that subtree has at most N/w leaves, each representing a disjoint original leaf set of size at least w. Their total charge is O(Nw). At nodes with exactly one small child, those small child leaf sets are disjoint across the tree. Their total charge is at most Nw. At nodes with both children smaller than w, charge each cross pair of leaves. Every pair is charged only at its least common ancestor, and maximal subtrees of this type have fewer than 2w leaves and are disjoint. Their total charge is also O(Nw). A tree with w=0 has no quadratic cross terms. Consequently the constructive Boolean gate count is O(N(w+1)) for supplied binary rank decompositions of arbitrary shape as well.
