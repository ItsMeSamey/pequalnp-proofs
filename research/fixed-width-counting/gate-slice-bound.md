# A width lower bound for total shared helper circuits

Choose m distinct monotone clauses of width w on n variables. Put N=binom(n,w). On the slice of inputs having precisely w zeros, each clause rejects the unique input whose zero set is its support. Thus all binom(N,m) rejection sets occur.

Allow a separately chosen bounded-fanin finite-basis helper circuit of at most S gates on the input and t additional existential bits. A target CNF can use all input bits, witnesses and gate outputs, with arbitrarily many clauses of width at most k. Require pointwise existential equivalence. There are at most exp(O(S log(n+t+S))) helper circuits by listing topologically ordered gates and their input addresses. For fixed helpers there are at most M=(1+2(n+t+S))^k clauses, by padding ordered literal lists with a blank. At each rejected slice point and each of its 2^t extensions retain one falsified clause. Together the retained clauses reject those m points under existential semantics. Every accepted slice point still has its original satisfying witness. Thus the chosen circuit and an ordered list of m 2^t clauses identify the slice rejection set, even if the original target had arbitrarily many clauses.

Consequently universal compilation requires
log binom(N,m) <= O(S log(n+t+S)) + m 2^t k log(1+2(n+t+S)).
Use consistent logarithm bases. For m=floor(n^(1+epsilon)) and S=n polylog n, at t=0 and w=ceil(log_2 n), log binom(N,m)>=m log(N/m) and N>=(n/w)^w give k>=w-O(log log n). For w=floor(n/2), N>=2^n/(n+1), giving k=Omega(n/log n). For t=O(log n) this last conclusion becomes 2^t k=Omega(n/log n). These statements apply to a joint output CNF without tracking individual clause provenance. Pigeonholing exact solution counts costs at most log(2^n+1) bits, so even a single common-count family can witness the asymptotic counting obstruction. The theorem constrains pointwise compilation with explicit bounded total helpers. It gives no running-time lower bound for count-only algorithms.
