# Splitting wide clauses by common disjunctions

Let S be a CNF of width at most w on the declared input universe. Normalize its remaining wide clauses by removing repeated literals and discarding tautologies. A candidate is a set U of literals obtained by deleting at most w literals from one wide clause. It covers each wide clause C containing U with |C minus U| at most w. Every single wide clause has a candidate, and a jointly coverable nonempty group can be covered by one candidate in this enumeration. For fixed w there are at most m times the sum of binomial(n,j), 0 ≤ j ≤ w, candidates. All covers can be computed directly from the input. Repeatedly select a candidate covering the largest number of uncovered clauses, assigning those clauses to it. This produces a polynomial-time recognizable partition. With g* candidates sufficient, the remaining uncovered count drops by a factor at most 1−1/g* per iteration, so the partition uses at most 1+g* ln(m) candidates for m positive. The bound below uses the actual number g found. A better supplied partition is also directly verifiable.

For a group with common literal set U, let p denote the indicator that all literals in U are false. Let B be the conjunction of that group's clauses after removing U from each. Its satisfaction indicator is exactly 1−p+p[B]. This holds when p=0 because the core disjunction satisfies the group, and when p=1 because only the short residuals can satisfy it. Expand the product over groups. Every resulting term is a sign, a conjunction of pins and a conjunction of bounded-width CNFs appended to S. Inconsistent pin sets contribute zero. Thus at most 3^g calls to a bounded-width counter suffice; free unpinned variables must be retained in the declared universe. Traversing the expansion depth first needs polynomial space beyond the counter since g ≤ m. No independence of groups, disjointness of supports or restriction on S is needed. If g=o(n), this preserves the exponential-rate saving of the chosen bounded-width solver, provided its bound is uniform on the resulting clause counts.

## Dense full-rank example

Take disjoint X and Y of h even variables each, h ≥ 4. Include the h clauses x_i OR (OR Y) and the h clauses y_i OR (OR X). Each has width h+1. In the positive columns, the clause-by-literal matrix has diagonal identity blocks and off-diagonal all-ones blocks J. Squaring over the binary field gives the identity because J squared equals hJ=0. Its rank is 2h, the entire variable count, also after appending the zero negative columns. The first h clauses yield distinct consistent unions for all subsets: a nonempty subset pins Y to zero and precisely its selected x variables to zero. No second-block clause can produce those same pins for any proper first-block subset omitting an x variable. Thus at least 2^h−2 of these final proper nonempty-subset dictionary coefficients survive with coefficient ±1. Individual wide clauses are irredundant: to falsify only x_i OR (OR Y), make Y all zero and set all of X except x_i to one, and exchange roles for the other block.

With w=1 for the wide-clause residuals, enumeration finds OR X and OR Y by deleting one literal from a representative of each block. They cover h clauses each; all candidates covering at least two clauses in the same block must be its full common literal set because they have size at least h. A pair of clauses from different blocks intersects in just two literals and cannot share a candidate. Hence greedy recognition selects exactly these two cores. If S has arbitrary fixed width w_S, use residual width one for candidate discovery and run the counter with width max(1,w_S).

For this example write N for the count of S, and N[A] for its count with the indicated pins. When X and Y both contain a true variable, every wide clause holds. When X is all zero, every variable of Y must be true, and conversely. The exact result is

    N − N[X=0] − N[Y=0] + N[X=Y=0] + N[X=0,Y=1] + N[Y=0,X=1].

Thus six pinned calls suffice regardless of the short CNF or variable interactions it induces. The comparison is with explicit span or union enumeration on the input clauses. It establishes no lower bound on other ways of representing their indicator or on general SAT.
