# Recovering a shared OR library beyond interval clauses

Fix d≥3. Let the n=binom(h,2) variables correspond to edges of a complete graph on h hubs. For each d-set S of hubs, let C_S contain every positive edge literal with at least one endpoint in S. There are binom(h,d) distinct clauses, all of width n−binom(h−d,2). Any fixed-width core on the original variables is allowed.

For each hub precompute by binary OR gates the disjunction of its incident edges. The h stars use at most h(h−2)<2n gates. Each original clause becomes a disjunction of d star outputs. Gate definitions by three width-at-most-three clauses give a unique extension to every original assignment. The resulting fixed-width instance has O(n) variables.

The underlying labeling need not be given. Compute every triple intersection of long literal sets and retain the inclusion-minimal intersections of size greater than binom(3d,2). If the three hub sets have no common hub, an edge in the triple intersection must have both endpoints among their at most 3d hubs; otherwise the sole endpoint within their union would belong to all three. A common hub contributes its entire star. Conversely, each star is exactly the triple intersection of three sets consisting of its hub and disjoint petals of d−1 hubs, since a single edge cannot hit three disjoint petals. For h−1>binom(3d,2) and sufficiently many petals this recovers exactly the stars. As a recognition procedure on arbitrary inputs, verify explicitly that every original clause is the union of at most d recovered sets and check the total gate budget. Enumeration is polynomial for fixed d.

Every ordering of the edge variables yields some clause requiring Ω_d(h) intervals. For each pair of consecutive distinct edges e,f, choose an endpoint of e outside f. A uniform d-set of hubs contains this endpoint and avoids both endpoints of f with probability binom(h−3,d−1)/binom(h,d)=Ω_d(1/h). This counts the expected transitions from present to absent. Summing over n−1 pairs gives the bound. Deleting unrelated signed literals from an ordering cannot increase the minimum number of intervals needed.

The long-clause incidence matrix has binary rank Ω(n). Partition the hubs into comparable parts and choose anchors i₀,j₀ on opposite sides. For each other cross pair i,j, choose any (d−2)-set B outside the four distinct hubs. The rows with hub sets B plus one element from {i,i₀} and one from {j,j₀} sum to the incidence vector of the four cross edges between these pairs. To verify, represent membership of an edge uv over F₂ as x_u+x_v+x_ux_v, where x is the hub set indicator. Taking the two differences deletes every monomial except the four cross terms. The resulting rectangles are independent by the private coordinate ij. Falsifying the last clause in any first-violation order removes only O_d(h) columns and one row. Each other clause retains at least h−1−d literals at a hub absent from the pinned clause. Consequently the residual rank also stays Ω(n) and every residual width grows with h.

These are bounds on the specific interval and rank criteria. They do not rule out other algorithms on the family.
