# Sparse Walsh contractions with signed wide-clause selectors

Write F as a bounded-width core B, arbitrary exceptional clauses E₁,…,E_h, and groups of remaining clauses. Group j has indicator H_j on w_j variables. Groups can overlap: a deterministic default groups equal scopes. Compute each truth table and normalized rational Walsh spectrum by evaluation and fast Walsh–Hadamard transform. Handle zero or identity groups immediately. Each choice of one nonzero frequency per group produces one global frequency (the XOR of their masks) and the product coefficient. Duplicate masks may be merged; alternatively stream all choices for polynomial workspace when the local tables are polynomial-size.

For a global mask u compute the signed sum of its parity character on B-models satisfying all E_i. Introduce y_i and binary clauses (¬y_i OR ¬l) for every literal l in E_i. At fixed x, y_i=1 is possible exactly when E_i fails. The signed sum over y is zero if any exception fails, one otherwise. Encode the single parity u·x XOR y_1 XOR … XOR y_h by a cumulative XOR chain with unique extensions, comparing the final-even and final-odd bounded-width counts. With no parity inputs only the even value occurs. An empty exception gives zero immediately. This accounts simultaneously for the Walsh phase and the wide selectors in two bounded-width counts on O(n+h) variables.

Let T bound these calls. For s_j nonzero Fourier coefficients the total time is poly(input) times (sum_j 2^{w_j} + T product_j s_j), using streaming or sparse convolution. Clearing the denominator 2^{sum w_j} is polynomial-bit since sum w_j is bounded by the explicit input length. The result gives subexponential variable-time for polynomial input when max w_j=o(n), sum_j log₂ s_j=o(n), and h=o(n√log n/loglog n), by the reported fixed-width counting theorem. Space is polynomial when each local table is polynomial-size and the product terms are streamed. The selector gadget is established work; the new hybrid evaluates each product character and the exceptions in one signed count. [Wu and Wang, Section 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.02560v1#S4).

long_walsh_check.py tests 401 formulas including overlapping scopes, crossing short cores, exceptions, free variables, and empty parity masks. All reconstructed indicators, intermediate signed counts and final counts agreed with brute force. The checker directly enumerates the selector and XOR-chain auxiliary bits on its small compiled examples.
