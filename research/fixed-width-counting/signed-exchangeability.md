# Signed exchange classes and exact CNF orbits

Normalize the chosen clause family, discard duplicates, and represent a variable word by its truth bits. Make a labeled multigraph on variables. Insert edge ij of sign e in {0,1} when simultaneously replacing x_i by x_j xor e and x_j by x_i xor e preserves the family, including the corresponding replacements on negative literals. Test both signs, independently, by lookup. Take distinct endpoints; both signs may occur. Each connected component acts on its own coordinates, independently of the other components.

In a connected component choose a spanning tree. Change the polarity of each coordinate by a bit a_i such that a_i xor a_j=e on each tree edge. In these gauged coordinates every tree swap is ordinary. They generate all permutations, for example by conjugating swaps successively along a tree path. In a balanced component all edges are ordinary in this gauge, and the generated group is precisely the symmetric group. Its assignment orbits are the Hamming weights. An isolated vertex is included.

In an unbalanced component at least one remaining edge is negative, including the case of opposite parallel edges. Compose that signed swap with the ordinary swap on the same endpoints to flip exactly those two bits. Conjugation by ordinary permutations generates all pair flips, spanning the even-weight translation space. Every generating signed swap has even flip weight. Hence the group is exactly the permutations together with all even-weight translations. Two assignments are in the same orbit precisely if their parities agree, since their even-weight difference is an available translation. Changing the polarity gauge shifts both a target word parity and an assignment parity by the same fixed bit in a full component.

For an orbit representative clause C, let p_i and q_i count its gauged positive and negative occurrences in component i, with s_i its size. On a balanced component, an orbit member can falsify its literals exactly when q_i <= h_i <= s_i-p_i. On an unbalanced component with p_i+q_i<s_i, either parity has an assignment that falsifies all specified literals: choose an omitted bit to adjust parity. On a full unbalanced component the only falsifying word has ones at the negative literals. Its parity is q_i mod 2. The choices on different components combine independently. Thus C's orbit is satisfied iff some balanced component violates its weight interval, or some full unbalanced component's parity differs from q_i mod 2. This also handles an orbit whose compiled output clause is empty, which rejects all assignments.

Distinct actual clause orbits can be identified without enumerating a group: use the ordered pairs (p_i,q_i) for balanced components, and for each unbalanced component the scope size r_i together with q_i mod 2 only when r_i=s_i. Ordinary permutations align supports. For r_i<s_i arbitrary sign corrections on the support can be completed to an even flip on all coordinates, while for full support parity is necessary and sufficient. Different scope-size profiles can yield identical output clauses after simplification.

With no external core, the exact count is obtained by summing the indicator of all compiled orbit clauses over all balanced weights and unbalanced parity choices. The multiplicity in a cell is product_i binom(s_i,h_i) over balanced components times product_i 2^{s_i-1} over unbalanced components. An arbitrary external core can instead be counted on each cell with the original variables, or conjoined directly with the equivalent compiled conditions. The theorem concerns the group generated by the tested pair swaps; there may be further automorphisms of the family.
