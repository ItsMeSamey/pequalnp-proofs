# Commuting components and a nilpotency-class-two boundary

Each site of a group-selector word chooses g_i(0) or g_i(1) according to a repeated Boolean selector. Write g_i(x)=a_i h_i^x. Move all a_i to the left to obtain C times an ordered product of d_i^x with d_i=A_i^{-1}h_i A_i, where A_i is the suffix product of later baseline a's. Build a graph on distinct selectors by connecting two if some associated d's do not commute. Different components generate mutually centralizing subgroups. Reorder by components while preserving each internal order, compute their output distributions independently, and convolve them by group multiplication. At each component one may use alternation-width contraction, the algebraic routine below, or conditioning and re-normalizing. In particular, for a normal good subgroup H, conditioning k selector labels whose occurrences account for every toggle outside H leaves only toggles in H after absorbing fixed factors, giving at most 2^k good-group computations. This assumes the group and normal subgroup are fixed or their tables and subgroup coordinate data are available.

Let H have a central elementary abelian 2-subgroup Z with quotient H/Z elementary abelian of order 2^b. Fix ordered lifts of a quotient basis and central coordinates in F₂^c. Each quotient coordinate of a normalized Boolean word is affine-linear. Multiplication in collected coordinates has a bilinear carry into the center: moving a right lift past earlier left lifts contributes its central commutator when both bits are one; two copies of a lift contribute its central square when both bits are one. Thus each central coordinate is degree at most two, with Boolean squares reduced to linear terms. To count a fixed target, solve its quotient affine constraints over F₂; substitute an affine parametrization into the c central quadratics. Fourier-expand their joint equality in the 2^c characters of Z. Each term is a binary quadratic Gauss sum. If its phase is uv+Au+Bv+C, summing u,v gives 2 times the phase-sum for AB+C on the remaining variables; A,B are affine. At affine degree the sum is zero for a nonconstant linear part and a signed power of two otherwise. This is polynomial in word length for every fixed such H, independent of arbitrary alternation, including for the dihedral and quaternion groups of order eight.

A fixed nilpotent group of class two can also support #P-complete identity counting. Take the order-27 Heisenberg group of unitriangular 3-by-3 matrices over F₃. Standard generators a,b have central commutator c of order three. Fix the convention [a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}. Four sites using Boolean selectors z,x implement [a^z,b^x]=c^{zx}; negating the first exponent negates the central value. Thus any sum of signed Boolean pair products modulo three is realized in a central group word. We reduce exact vertex-cover counting.

For a graph on n vertices choose t with 4^t>2^n. For every edge uv and each of t copies take fresh independent z_+,z_−. Realize the quadratic Q=Σ(z_+−z_−)(x_u+x_v) by these commutator gadgets. The output is c^Q. With ω a primitive cube root, summing its phase over each pair of fresh selectors at fixed x gives |1+ω^{x_u+x_v}|², which is 4 if x_u=x_v=0 and 1 otherwise. Hence W=Σ_{x,z} ω^Q is the integer P(4^t), P(X)=Σ_x X^{number of edges with two zero endpoints}. Swapping each pair z_+,z_− negates Q, so the counts of residues 1 and 2 agree. With N=n+2t|E| selectors and N₀ the identity count, W=(3N₀−2^N)/2. Its least residue modulo 4^t is P(0), exactly the number of vertex covers, since P(0)≤2^n<4^t. Isolated original vertices may either be declared as unused selectors or removed with their factor of two. Word length and integer bit cost are polynomial; a certificate is one Boolean selector assignment, giving #P membership. This is a single-query counting reduction, not a parsimonious reduction. It neither bounds the number of appearances per selector nor asserts a complete group classification or novelty.

There is also hardness inside 2-groups. Use UT₃(Z/4), a fixed order-64 class-two group whose commutator generator c has order four. Use the same pair-product word Q modulo four, now with t=n+1 and B=2^t. At primitive phase i the fresh-pair sum is |1+i^{x_u+x_v}|², equal to 4 for 00, 2 for 01 or 10, and 0 for 11. Thus W=Σ_{independent x} B^{2e₀₀+e₀₁}, where e₀₁ counts edges with unequal endpoint bits. Each base-B digit is an exact multiplicity because B>2^n; their sum equals the independent set count. Again the swap involution cancels imaginary phases, leaving W=N₀−N₂=2N₀−N_even. N_even is the count of zero assignments of the single binary quadratic Q mod 2, computable by the earlier Gauss sum. Hence one identity query suffices here too. All commutator exponents are central with exact additive order four, verified by ordinary unitriangular multiplication.

For context, Boolean selectors choosing elements of a fixed group are a group-program model; decision satisfiability results alone do not evaluate its number of accepting inputs.
