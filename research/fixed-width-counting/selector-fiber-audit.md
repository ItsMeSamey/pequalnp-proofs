# Closed level curves in the interval-parse construction

Place the occurrences in strictly convex cyclic position, one selector per labeled site. Let P_v be the closed convex hull of the sites of label v. Suppose T is a finite tree and each v has a connected combinatorial subtree T_v, including every edge between its vertices, such that intersecting hulls have intersecting subtrees. Assume at most k subtrees contain any vertex of T. A width-w decomposition of the intersection graph supplies k=w+1.

There is a PL map f from the disk to T with f(P_v) contained in T_v. Triangulate the arrangement with the hulls as subcomplexes. At every vertex the active hull family corresponds to pairwise-intersecting subtrees, whose total intersection is nonempty by tree Helly. Map the vertex to such a tree vertex (unconstrained vertices may go anywhere). Extend edges along tree geodesics. On each triangle, choose one boundary image vertex and cone the already mapped boundary to it by geodesics, or take a simplicial filling of its null boundary walk in the image subtree. This can be done PL after finite subdivisions: traverse consecutive tree edges on the piecewise edge-linear boundary, cancel successive inverse edge traversals and successively fill the associated folded triangles, treating constant steps separately. Every triangle belonging to a hull has all three boundary images inside the corresponding subtree, so this procedure respects the constraint. For an entirely polygonal realization put the sites at the side midpoints of a strictly convex circumscribed polygon, one site per distinct side. Each hull then meets the outer boundary only at its own sites (for fewer than three sites handle the claim directly). Do not use a polygon whose consecutive vertices are the sites without addressing possible hull boundary segments.

On each target edge select one interior point avoiding images of all domain subdivision vertices and all sites. Its PL regular level is a finite disjoint union of embedded polygonal circles and proper arcs with distinct endpoints in boundary gaps. This follows simplex by simplex: inside a triangle that meets the selected value, the level is a segment transversal to its two cut edges and they join across internal edges in pairs. Levels from different target edges are disjoint. The components of T after deleting all selected values are stars, one per tree vertex. Every combinatorial subtree meeting the open star of a vertex a contains a. A point strictly between a and the selected value on an adjacent edge can belong only to a subtree containing that entire edge.

Erase every closed level loop, together with all level curves nested inside an outermost such loop. No proper arc lies within one of these enclosed disks, since each arc reaches the original boundary. Regions cut out by the remaining proper arcs are disks. Before refilling an erased disk, the immediately adjacent exterior region maps into one open star about some a, and the enclosing loop maps to the cut point of an edge incident with a. Any hull entering the erased disk connects within itself to a site on the original boundary, hence meets the enclosing level loop. Its subtree then contains that entire edge including a. Filling this disk into the exterior region therefore preserves the assertion that every hull meeting the region interior has its subtree in the bag at a (that is, its subtree contains a). Removing disjoint outermost disks separately gives the same assertion on the resulting regions. A label whose hull merely meets a retained proper boundary arc is certified by the subtree bag of the arc's target edge.

Insert gap vertices on the outer boundary so every site is isolated on its own boundary segment; insert others to give every region at least three boundary vertices if necessary. All proper arc endpoints are distinct, and each region has an outer-boundary segment available. Triangulate each region by topological diagonals without adding interior vertices. The dual, with outer-boundary leaves, is a trivalent tree. A separating diagonal internal to a region splits a label only when two occurrences lie on opposite sides, in which case connectedness forces its hull to meet the interior of the diagonal (endpoints lie on the outer boundary off all hulls). All such labels therefore belong to that region's bag. A retained level arc has the analogous edge-bag bound. Leaf cuts isolate at most one label. Root at an empty boundary segment in the wraparound gap (add vertices there), delete empty leaves, and suppress unary nodes. Each surviving cut is an existing cyclic bipartition oriented away from that gap and thus an ordinary interval. The max split count is ≤k, allowing the trivial empty-label case separately.

Crucial hypotheses: every hull attaches to the original boundary; subtrees contain full tree edges; all triangulation vertices added after erasing loops lie on the exterior boundary; levels are regular PL levels. An arbitrary continuous f alone need not have tame fibers. It is the existence of the replacement PL map that justifies the proof.
