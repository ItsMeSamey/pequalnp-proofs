# Compiling signed interval-union clauses without a variable per query

## Statement

Let F be an explicit CNF on n declared variables, L its input length. Suppose a supplied ordering of the 2n signed literals makes each nontautological clause the union of at most p intervals. A deterministic parsimonious compilation gives a CNF with O(n) variables, O(n+4^p m) clauses, and width at most max(3,4p), without a new variable per original clause. It takes O(poly(L)+poly(p)4^p m) operations on indices, allowing standard encoding overhead. One validates the promise by sorting each clause's positions, coalescing consecutive positions and counting runs. No recognition algorithm for finding the order is assumed.

For fixed p, the October 25 OpenAI exact-counting theorem then gives deterministic poly_p(L) 2^O_p(n log log n/sqrt(log n)) bit operations and polynomial space, regardless of the clause density and individual widths. Its bounded-density O(n/sqrt(log n)) exponent also applies when m=O(n) and p is fixed. The uniform-width Anthropic theorem gives, in its computational model, a variable-count exponent O(p^2 n log log n/sqrt(log n)), in addition to construction and input overhead; this is sublinear for p=o((log n)^(1/4)/sqrt(log log n)). The explicit compiled formula can always be handed to the fixed-width theorem in the fixed-p result.

## Shared Boolean preprocessing

For a bit array of length N=2n, zero-pad to the next power of two. Place it at the leaves of a balanced dyadic tree. At every node of length 2^j, compute its all-zero bit and its all-zero prefix and suffix lengths in binary with j+1 bits (the maximum length is 2^j). A parent's prefix equals the left child's prefix unless the entire left child is zero; then it is the left child's length plus the right prefix. Suffix is symmetric. Binary multiplexing and adding a fixed power of two take O(j+1) bounded-fanin Boolean gates per node.

Reencode each binary length as two base-B digits H,D with B=2^ceil((j+1)/2), padding if needed. For each digit expose every threshold bit [digit>=v], including constant sentinel values. Decoding all bit patterns by a binary prefix tree takes O(B) gates, and a suffix OR over the equality bits gives all thresholds using O(B) more. Even the looser O((j+1)B) bound yields O(N) total gates, by summing (N/2^j)(j+1)2^ceil((j+1)/2) over tree levels. This is circuit size on individual bits. The data bits stored can be nonlinear functions of the original assignment.

For a fixed threshold t=qB+r, the predicate that a length is at least t has the two witnesses

    [H>=q+1]          or          [H>=q] and [D>=r].

Their three bit addresses are determined from t and the tree node, before reading any data bits. For a non-singleton query [a,b], find the least common ancestor of the two leaves. Its child split divides this interval into a suffix of the left child and a prefix of the right. The interval is zero exactly when both requested lengths fit their precomputed zero lengths. A singleton tests its leaf directly. Thus an interval-zero condition has at most four witnesses, each a conjunction of at most four fixed literals on the shared circuit wires. This is an exact, constant nonadaptive bit-probe scheme in one dimension.

## Query clauses and counting

An original clause fails precisely when every one of its at most p intervals is zero. Taking one zero witness per interval supplies at most 4^p conjunctions of at most 4p literals. Insert the negation of each conjunction as a clause, dispensing with contradictory witnesses and constants as usual. This step creates no variables. Replace each shared circuit gate by the usual width-three equivalence with a fresh output. Every original assignment determines every shared wire uniquely, so the transformation preserves the exact model count. Empty and tautological clauses, overlapping intervals, free variables and complementary signed input literals are handled by the stated convention.

A separated family showing why clause-count and original-width bounds alone do not certify this result is obtained by ordering the positive literals in two disjoint blocks of length Theta(n), then the negative literals. Choose every pair of intervals, one in each block, both of a fixed length Theta(n) smaller than half a block. This makes Theta(n^2) distinct clauses of width Theta(n), with no strict subsumption since all clauses have the same cardinality. The theorem applies with p=2. This comparison addresses specific worst-case guarantees, not lower bounds on the difficulty of the example. Arbitrary subsets of the pair family also obey the promise; signs in the general result are unrestricted.

## Limits of a shared query dictionary

A fixed list of S Boolean features supports at most (S+1)^t 2^(2^t) distinct query functions through at most t fixed bit probes followed by an arbitrary Boolean decoder. Indeed this counts the addresses and truth tables. There are 2^n distinct monotone disjunctions on n Boolean inputs, so for fixed t a single dictionary covering all of them must have exponentially many features. Nor can an O(n)-gate bounded-fanin dictionary usually be tailored to an arbitrary dense family while retaining constant probes: there are at most 2^O(n log n) circuits of that size over a fixed finite gate basis, and each circuit supports at most exp(O_t(log n)) choices per clause. For m=n independent uniform monotone disjunctions the total number of ordered clause lists is 2^(n^2), against 2^O_t(n log n) representable lists. This counts exact query functions for individual clauses. It does not preclude a different computation of their conjunction, signed cancellations, per-clause auxiliaries, or a general SAT algorithm.

## Prior work and checks

* [OpenAI exact model counting, Theorem 1.1](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/main_full_version.tex) and [Anthropic uniform-width bound](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/main/eth/paper.pdf).
* The direct signed wide-clause selector is prior work. [Laakkonen, Meichanetzidis and van de Wetering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08048) reduce general counting to signed binary CNF; [Wu and Wang, Section 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.02560v1#S4) encode signs with an XOR chain and two ordinary counts. For h exceptional wide clauses this uses n+O(h) variables regardless of literal incidence. General arbitrary-width density guarantees from branching appear in [Proposition 6.1 of the learning-theory paper](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2610.00613v2) and [Tulsyan, Section 6](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00090v1). These are distinct from the signed-run promise.
* [The Range 1 Query Problem](https://www.cs.williams.edu/~sam/papers/2014-COCOON-R1Q.pdf) studies exactly static range emptiness and gives constant-query-time results in the word-RAM model. [Alon and Schieber](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06321) give general semigroup product query tradeoffs. Their word accesses or single-semigroup operations are different computational models from bounded-fanin Boolean preprocessing and constant nonadaptive single-bit probes. No priority for solving range emptiness is claimed.
* [On Satisfiability Problems with a Linear Structure](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07876) treats interval-bigraph structure of the unsigned clause-variable incidence graph. It is a different promise from an ordering of signed literals with few runs per clause; even arbitrary 3-CNF has at most three singleton runs in any order.
* The adjacent Python program builds actual width-three gate clauses and the compiled query clauses. It passed 37,886 exhaustive array/interval queries, and all original assignments on 100 signed CNF compilations including empty and tautological clauses. Unique extensions follow from gate equivalences. Its finite tests do not verify the external asymptotic counting algorithms.
