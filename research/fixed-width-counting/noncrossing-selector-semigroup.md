# Noncrossing selector words and their exact counting limit

For site matrices A_i(b), compute the sum over Boolean selector assignments of the ordered matrix product of A_i(s_{v_i}), taking trace if desired. Coefficients can lie in a commutative ring and matrices need not commute. Some site matrices can be constant. Say two selector labels cross if four increasing positions alternate between them. When no labels cross, their occurrence intervals are disjoint or nested; moreover a parent label never occurs strictly inside a child's span. Otherwise one child occurrence on each side of the intervening parent occurrence, together with an exterior occurrence of the parent, would alternate. For each nested span, compute its matrix as the sum over the two values of its own label of the ordered products of its own site matrices, fixed constants and already-summed child matrices. Disjoint free labels have independent choices and distributivity proves correctness. Empty outside spans and selectors appearing once are included. Each position/child enters O(1) matrix operations. Arbitrary common boundary vectors or trace can be applied at the root.

Construct the alternation graph on selector labels. Giving values to a vertex cover leaves a noncrossing word on all remaining labels; the fixed values contribute ordinary constant matrices. The minimum number of selectors whose deletion has that effect is exactly minimum vertex cover. Find a cover of size k by the usual two-endpoint branching search, then run the linear noncrossing procedure for the 2^k assignments. No supplied partition or variable order other than the original factor order is required.

With unrestricted crossing, even two fixed 2x2 zero-one matrices suffice for #P-hardness when EVERY label occurs EXACTLY TWICE. Counting vertex covers in connected cubic bipartite planar graphs is #P-complete (Xia, Zhang and Zhao, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304397507004653). A cubic bipartite graph has a polynomially findable perfect matching. Duplicate its edges to produce a connected 4-regular Eulerian multigraph without changing its vertex covers. Use the cyclic Euler sequence of vertices, recording one tail per directed traversal in order. Each vertex occurs twice. Set M_b[a,c]=1 if and only if c=b and a OR b. At a fixed selector assignment the trace of the product forces each internal state to equal its current selector, uniquely; its remaining conditions require OR on every traversed edge, including wraparound. Thus the trace is the vertex-cover indicator and the sum is exactly the original count. One occurrence per selector is always tractable by replacing its matrix with A_i(0)+A_i(1). The hardness concerns general two-occurrence patterns, not all crossing instances individually.
