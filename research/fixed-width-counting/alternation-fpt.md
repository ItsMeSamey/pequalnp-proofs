# Fixed-parameter contraction at arbitrary selector multiplicity

Let the m ordered sites of a matrix product carry Boolean labels v_i; repeated occurrences use the same Boolean argument. A site's D-by-D matrix A_i(b) may depend on its index as well as the bit. Join two labels in H when their occurrences have a subsequence a,b,a,b or b,a,b,a. Let w be the treewidth of H. We compute the complete matrix sum over shared assignments in 2^{O(w+1)} poly(m) matrix arithmetic, with no restriction on label multiplicity. Once the matrix is computed, arbitrary boundary vectors or a trace can be applied.

### Geometry of the width bound

On a strictly convex circle place the sites in word order and draw the convex hull P_v of each label's occurrences. Singleton and two-occurrence hulls are points and segments. Two hulls meet precisely for alternating labels: disjoint circular arcs containing their respective vertex sets give disjoint hulls when there is no alternation, and crossing chords witness the converse. Every original boundary point other than the sites is outside all hulls.

Take any width-w tree decomposition T of H. Let T_v be the closed subtree carrying v, including whole edges between its nodes. There is a continuous piecewise linear map f from the disk to T with f(P_v) contained in T_v for every v. To see this, draw the finite hull arrangement, triangulating it so all the hulls are subcomplexes. One may work on a polygonal model of the disk with extra boundary scanpoints interspersed strictly convexly among the sites. At each triangulation vertex p the polygons containing p form a clique, so their tree subtrees have a common bag. Assign its node as f(p); points in no hull may use any node. Map each edge along the unique tree path. Fill each triangle in the tripod spanned by its three vertex images: send a new center to the tree median, then extend radially along the three side paths, subdividing so each piece maps linearly into one edge. This extension agrees on shared boundaries and stays in T_v on every simplex belonging to P_v, since subtrees contain all connecting paths. Constant images cause no problem.

Choose one regular interior level point on each tree edge, avoiding images of vertices of a finite triangulation on which f is piecewise linear, as well as constant images of boundary segments. Each level set is a disjoint union of simple arcs ending on the disk boundary and internal simple closed curves. This follows by intersecting each affine triangle with the chosen level on a single target edge: where nonempty it is a segment joining distinct sides, glued two at a time at interior edges. Distinct levels have disjoint inverse images. The arcs avoid the original sites, which map to tree nodes.

Keep the boundary-to-boundary arcs as a dissection. Each resulting region has a single outer boundary formed by these arcs and pieces of the domain boundary; every corner is on the original boundary. All closed level curves in that region are away from its outer boundary. A sufficiently thin connected inner collar of this outer boundary misses every level curve and all retained arcs. Its image lies in one component of T minus the selected levels, the open star portion around one tree node t. Any hull meeting the collar belongs to bag t: a subtree containing a point of this open star must contain t. A retained level arc itself meets only hulls appearing in both endpoint bags of that tree edge.

Add one scanpoint in each gap between successive original sites (including the root gap), if needed using the prechosen strictly convex model. Refine each region by a fan of pairwise noncrossing curves connecting its corners and scanpoints, with open interiors lying in its collar. Such a fan can follow successively nested thin offsets of consecutive outer-boundary arcs near the boundary; interior holes never need to be entered. If a region has fewer than three marked points, no fan is needed. All used endpoints lie on the original boundary away from sites. Each new cut meets at most w+1 label hulls, and the same holds for each original level arc. Any label with sites on both sides of such an arc meets it by connectedness. Hence every resulting cut splits at most w+1 labels. This dissection is combinatorially a triangulation on the boundary scanpoints and additional level endpoints; digons and empty boundary segments may be omitted. Root the triangulation dual at a boundary edge in the chosen root gap, adding an extra empty edge inside that gap if necessary. Leaves in order represent boundary edges, each with at most one site. Removing empty leaves and suppressing unary nodes gives a binary parenthesization of the original word in which each interval I has at most w+1 labels on both sides of its boundary. At singleton leaves this also follows directly. The one-node decomposition tree is covered by the same argument with no levels.

### Algorithm

The geometric map is only an existence proof. Compute B(I), the labels occurring both in interval I and outside I, for every interval. Ordinary interval DP minimizes over binary parenthesizations the maximum |B(I)|: try every split, taking the maximum of the interval boundary size and the two child costs. By the lemma its optimum q is <=w+1. This uses polynomial time regardless of w, and needs no tree decomposition or geometric computation.

For each optimal-tree interval I maintain the matrix obtained by summing the product on I over labels wholly contained in I, conditional on the assignments to B(I). At a split concatenate the two child matrices in word order. Enumerate consistent assignments on the union of their two boundaries, then add the product to the entry indexed by the parent boundary restriction. Each newly internal label is summed exactly once. A leaf whose label is not in its boundary sums its two matrices directly. The union has size <=2q, so at most 2^{2q} scalar-matrix products per internal node are needed. The product order is never changed. With standard multiplication this is O(m 2^{2q}D^3) coefficient operations plus polynomial-time preprocessing. Integer matrix entries of input bit length L yield intermediate entries with O(m(L+log(D+1))+m) bits, by the path expansion. Constant sites can be treated directly without adding labels. This proves the requested FPT bound for arbitrary repeated sites.

### Exact join load

Fill the disks inside outermost closed level curves. Every hull meeting such a disk meets the enclosing level curve because the hull connects to one of its sites on the original outer boundary. Its subtree therefore includes the tree edge containing that level, including the surrounding chamber's bag node. Thus filling the holes does not add any label foreign to that chamber. The boundary proper arcs meet only labels of either neighboring bag. Choose boundary cut endpoints off all hulls, for example with the domain a convex polygon having at most one site at each side midpoint. Every triangle of a refined arc-region consequently meets only hulls from its chamber's bag.

Each internal branch divides the boundary sites into three sectors. A label present in two sectors must meet the corresponding closed triangle, since that triangle separates those sectors. After deleting empty leaves and suppressing unary nodes the same sector membership holds at every surviving branch. Hence its join load |B(left) union B(right)| is at most w+1.

Conversely embed any interval parse tree with site leaves on the boundary in word order. To each label assign the minimal subtree on its occurrence leaves (a singleton uses the leaf). Alternating labels have intersecting subtrees by disjoint-path separation of alternating boundary points. Label sets at the nodes thus constitute a tree decomposition of H. Internal bags are exactly the labels in at least two incident sectors, the join load; leaf bags have size 1. The minimum maximum join load, with leaf size 1 included, therefore equals w+1 for every nonempty word. In interval DP minimize this join load directly, including both child costs, to perform contraction in O(m 2^{w+1}D³) coefficient operations after polynomial planning.

### Direct CNF word and scope

List literal sites clause by clause, with original variables as labels. A two-state matrix acts on the flag for whether the current clause is already true, updating it by OR with this occurrence's literal value. A constant matrix at each clause end allows only true to false, resetting the flag. Starting and ending false gives the model indicator; sum selector choices to count models, multiplying for declared but unused variables. Empty input clauses fail as required. This gives a structural algorithm for any specified presentation order. For this encoding, adding the Steiner subtree of each clause's contiguous occurrence block to the same parse decomposition yields a tree decomposition of the incidence graph: each variable-clause incidence shares its leaf, and at most three clause intervals cross the three sector boundaries at any join. Thus incidence treewidth is at most w+3 (isolated vertices attach directly). Conversely three distinct full-width clauses have incidence K_{3,n} of width three, yet even after reordering all literals and clauses every pair of data labels alternates. In each of three blocks both labels occur, forcing at least three changes and hence four alternating runs in the restricted binary word. The alternation graph is complete. This presentation parameter is not a generic SAT shortcut.
