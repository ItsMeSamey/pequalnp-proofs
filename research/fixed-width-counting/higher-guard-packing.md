# A single uniform half-width counting query for arbitrary CNF

For any normalized n-variable CNF with polynomially many clauses, one exact count of a uniform width-n CNF on 2n variables determines its count with deterministic 2^{O(sqrt(n) log(n+2))} preprocessing and output size. For widths at most (1-delta)n with fixed positive delta, the bound improves to quasipolynomial. Both measures concern a single query; two polynomial-size half-width queries already suffice.

Choose an integer d>=2. Give each clause a set B_i of w_i guards omitted from its positive padding, bringing its total width to n. Let H contain nonempty guard supports lying in at least d omitted sets. Add one full-width guard clause excluding each such support. The zero guard word leaves exactly G. Outside H at a nonzero guard word, at most d-1 source clauses can fail. Inclusion-exclusion therefore computes the total on those guard words: start with (2^n-1-|H|)2^n. For each nonempty compatible J of size at most d-1, add (-1)^|J| times
(2^{|D_J|}-1- number of A in H with A subset D_J) 2^{n-u_J},
where D_J is the intersection of B_i for i in J and u_J is the number of distinct source variables touched by J. Compatibility means the clauses can be simultaneously false. Subtract this known total from the query answer.

Choose B_i uniformly and independently at their required sizes. For each J of size d let w=max w_i and rho=w/n. Expanding over subsets of guards yields E 2^{|D_J|} <= (1+rho^d)^n <= exp(n rho^d), because for each fixed a-set the probability of containment in one B_i is at most (w_i/n)^a. Thus the sum over J has mean at most m^d exp(n rho^d). Derandomize by fixing each guard bit in one set at a time, always retaining a feasible branch with no larger conditional mean. For each J intersect the already completed omitted sets to get eligible positions. Sum over the cardinality a of a candidate subset in these positions. With one partially fixed set in J, split eligible points into forced-in, undecided and forced-out, enumerate the two chosen cardinalities in the first two classes and exclude the last. Multiply binomial counts by the hypergeometric probability of containing the chosen undecided points, and by the single-set containment probability for each still-unstarted member of J. If there are no completed members, all positions start eligible. This costs m^{O(d)} poly(n) per bit with polynomial-size integer arithmetic for polynomial m (common denominator can use the product of all current uniform-set sample space sizes). If m<d there is no blocker family. Otherwise every d-intersection has size O(d log(m+2)+n rho^d). Enumerate its subsets and deduplicate. Time and output are bounded by exp(O(d log(m+2)+n rho^d)) poly(n).

For fixed rho<1 take d=Theta(log(n+2)) with sufficient constant. For arbitrary widths set L=ceil(sqrt(n) log(n+2)), capped at n. First remove every clause of width greater than n-L. Enumerate the union of their falsifying assignments, at most m2^L, and count those satisfying the remaining input. This known number is precisely what must be subtracted from the remaining count. Its maximum width is now <=n-L. Choose d=max(2,ceil(n log(n+2)/L)); then n(1-L/n)^d<=1. For polynomial m the preprocessing and query length meet the claimed bound. Constant-sized inputs can be counted directly. This result gives a reduction and is not an unrestricted exact SAT algorithm.
