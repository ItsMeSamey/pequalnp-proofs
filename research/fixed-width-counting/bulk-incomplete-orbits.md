# Counting across all weight cells with two bounded-width queries

Use the orbit counts in incomplete-orbits.md. In a single assignment cell, one ordinary bounded-width query replaces the list of deletion subsets. Attach a selector to each deleted clause, with binary implications from that selector to falsity of each clause literal. Require exactly T selectors in total, where T is the sum of the numbers of violated clauses in the complete versions of all partially retained orbits. If v omitted clauses are violated, the assignment has binom(v,T) extensions. Since v<=T, the extension count equals the satisfaction indicator. An O(d)-gate full-adder cardinality circuit suffices for d holes.

To avoid cell enumeration altogether, give each incomplete orbit its own comparison. Assume the retained a_o members are at least half that orbit (so its size M_o<=2a_o), its d_o>=1 missing members have been listed, and the source has m clauses. Write d=sum d_o. Share the weight one-hot and threshold predicates and unbalanced parity gates over all components in O(n) bounded gates. The target for orbit o is the product of its locally violated-clause counts evaluated at the current assignment, capped at d_o+1. For every component with local orbit size 1, its local count is a Boolean predicate: a homogeneous full balanced component needs weight zero or full and an empty component contributes one. Combine all these Boolean predicates into one unbounded-fanin AND. The remaining components are at most log_2 M_o in number since their local orbit sizes are integers >=2 and multiply to M_o. For each of these balanced components tabulate its local violation count at each possible weight, capped at d_o+1. Each output bit is an unbounded OR of the one-hot predicates on which that bit is set. An unbalanced component uses the stated parity or constant value. Encode all such capped factors with O(log(d_o+2)) output gates per nontrivial component. Multiply factors with schoolbook binary multiplication, compare and saturate, using O(log²(d_o+2)) gates per factor. Capping is valid for products of nonnegative integers even if a later factor is zero.

For each orbit force its selector count to equal the capped target. Selectors cannot reach d_o+1, so assignments at that cap have no extensions, as needed. The number of choices for selectors on any assignment is a product of zero-one indicators. The target arithmetic, selector sums and comparisons take
O(d + sum_o log(2a_o) log²(d_o+2)) gates. To bound the sum, split log(2a_o) into log(2a_o/d_o)+log d_o. Since d_o<=a_o, log²(d_o+2)=O(d_o) and log³(d_o+2)=O(d_o), the bound is O(d + sum d_o log(2a_o/d_o)) = O(d(1+log(m/d))) by concavity, taking zero cost for d=0. All wire lists have polynomial length. Complete orbit clauses are compiled by the usual threshold rule, and sparse observed exceptions may be imposed directly.

Every unbounded AND/OR-gate Tseitin equivalence has binary clauses plus one wide clause. For each such clause introduce a cancellation switch constrained by binary implications to its falsity, with sign minus when selected. Its local signed model sum is exactly the satisfaction indicator; forward gate clauses remain imposed. Do the same for any other residual wide clause (including wide complete-orbit outputs and wide exceptions). An XOR chain over switches separates even and odd ordinary counts; subtract. Thus for any fixed residual width k>=3 the entire count is two width-k queries on O(n+d(1+log(m/d))+E) variables, where E counts remaining width>k clauses from complete orbits and sparse exceptions. The original bounded-width external core is unrestricted in size. This applies to any polynomial-size CNF with the stated recognized structure; it is not a universal bound with sublinear E or d.

Here k is chosen at least as large as the fixed width of the external core. Otherwise its above-k clauses must also be included in E. A capped nonzero factor is multiplied normally by later factors; in particular a later zero returns zero instead of triggering early rejection.

For a concrete parameter separation take n=2t variables in t pairs and partition those pairs into blocks of L=ceil(2 log_2 n) pairs, along a subsequence or with a shorter leftover block discarded. For each block form the orbit of clauses taking one positive literal per pair inside the block and both positives from every pair outside it. Omit one member per orbit. The supplied independent within-pair swaps give 3^t assignment-weight cells. Input size is polynomial, widths n-L, and d=Theta(n/log n) while m=Theta(d 2^L), so the bulk reduction uses O(n) variables with E=0. Any fixed-width core can cross between the pairs. This separates the stated bulk bound from explicit enumeration of every supplied weight cell; other direct methods for this family are possible.
