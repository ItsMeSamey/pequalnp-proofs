# Exact signed counting at feedback number one

Over the binary field let A(b)=A0+Σ b_i u_i v_iᵀ, with invertible baseline and distinct independent Boolean flags. For dimension d the hidden phase sum over all ordered y,z is 2^{d+nullity(A)}, by summing y first. Let K have entries v_iᵀ A0⁻¹ u_j. If its directed support is acyclic, all principal submatrices are strictly triangular after simultaneous permutation. The determinant identity det A(b)=det A0 det(I+K[S,S]) for selected S then gives constant nullity zero. The resulting total over flags is immediate.

With one supplied feedback vertex, computing this signed integer is already #P-hard under a single exact counting query and polynomial-time postprocessing.

Start with a bipartite graph G=(U,V,E). Make a forward graph with start s, terminal t, and layers U,E,V. Replace each layer vertex by an incoming and outgoing site joined by its own flag edge. Other arcs are constant, from s to each U site, from u to every incident edge e, from e to its V endpoint v, and from every V site to t, with all connectors going from outgoing to incoming sites. Let N be the resulting weighted adjacency (tail indexes rows). Put B=I+N. Add a return arc of independent flag g from t to s, obtaining A=B+g e_t e_sᵀ. The variables on the three layers are x,z,w. Every toggle is a single-entry update. For normalized K computed at the all-zero baseline, an interaction between two forward flags requires a constant path from the head of the first flag edge to the tail of the second. Removing the return flag leaves a DAG.

The matrix B is invertible at every specialization. Its inverse is the finite forward-path sum, with (s,t) entry p=Σ_{e=uv} x_u z_e w_v in the binary field. The return update has rank at most one, so its determinant 1+gp gives nullity exactly gp, interpreting the product as a bit. For k=|U|+|V|+|E| and d=2k+2 the total signed phase divided by 2^d is 2^{k+1}+T, where T counts flag settings with p=1 before choosing g. Summing the character of p over all edge flags makes the contribution zero whenever selected vertices contain an edge, and otherwise gives 2^{|E|}. Thus #IS(G)=(2^k−2T)/2^{|E|}. Dimensions, constructed entries, and the output bit length are linear in k. If a positive count is preferred, the number of assignments giving phase zero is the half-sum of the signed total and the known total number of assignments. A standard parity circuit with uniquely determined gate values represents this event by a polynomial-size CNF.

The reduction concerns exact counts. The bipartite independent-set source is the complement correspondence for bipartite vertex covers. See Vadhan, “The Complexity of Counting in Sparse, Regular, and Planar Graphs,” SIAM J. Comput. 31 (2001), and Xia, Zhang and Zhao, “Computational complexity of counting problems on 3-regular planar graphs,” Theor. Comput. Sci. 384 (2007), doi:10.1016/j.tcs.2007.05.023 (exact bipartite cubic planar vertex cover). Only the source graph has that planarity property.

## Two versus three private selectors per path

In fact the hard family can use one arc s→u per left vertex, one arc u→v per input edge, and one arc v→t per right vertex. Give each arc its own flag. Now the baseline is I and the dimension is just |U|+|V|+2. Use the same private return flag. All path polynomials and scalar formulas hold with the new dimension.

For an arbitrary acyclic forward network with constant and private selector arcs, if every s-to-t path traverses at most two selectors, p has Boolean degree at most two. Check this sufficient condition by longest-path DP with arc weights zero or one. Recover coefficients by evaluating on empty, singleton, and pair supports and using Boolean Möbius inversion. To sum the character of a quadratic, pick a cross term uv and write p=uv+ua+vb+c, where a,b are affine in the remaining bits. Summing over u,v gives twice the character of c+ab, eliminating two variables while preserving quadratic degree. An affine remainder sums to zero if nonconstant and otherwise to its sign times domain size. Recover T from this signed sum. Thus two private selectors per forward path admit polynomial computation, while three suffice for exact counting hardness.
