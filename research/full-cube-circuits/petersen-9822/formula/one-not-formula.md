# Excluding seventeen gates with one inverter and no shared signed binary producer

Assume the size-17 candidate uses one NOT. Contract it to signed edges, and suppose no binary gate then has more than one live outgoing occurrence. The reduced binary tree has 16 binary gates and 17 raw leaves; pushing signs to the leaves yields an AND/OR tree. If the inverter has a nonraw input H it can have only one live consumer, since otherwise H is a shared binary producer after contraction. Thus negative leaves come from the single H subtree. For a raw inverter multiple consumers are permitted; their occurrences become negative leaves of the same raw. Size ≤16 has already been excluded; a physical gate saving below excludes the minimum candidate.

A raw in a negative leaf must have at least two positive occurrences. With no positive occurrence it is decreasing in that raw, contrary to increasing essential dependence. Suppose it has exactly one positive occurrence. Choose the lowest ancestor of this positive leaf whose opposite branch B contains a negative occurrence of the raw. The branch A containing the positive leaf below this ancestor depends increasingly on the raw; B depends decreasingly. Above this ancestor the context D depends increasingly on the displayed ancestor output and decreasingly on the raw through any remaining negative leaves. For fixed other inputs write A0≤A1, B0≥B1 and D0(z)≥D1(z). The target is increasing so its values obey F0≤F1.

If the ancestor is AND, replace all occurrences of the negative literal in B by false (so B becomes B1). Only F0 can decrease. If originally F0=1 then F1=1. If D0 is constant the change has no effect. Otherwise D0 is identity, and D1≤D0 with F1=1 forces B1=1, so F0 stays true. If the ancestor is OR, set the negative occurrences in B to true (so B becomes B0). Only F1 can increase. If originally F1=0 then F0=0. For nonconstant D1, D1 is identity and D0≥D1 with F0=0 forces B0=0; F1 stays false. By monotonicity a single negative leaf in B may also be replaced by that constant. Implement that replacement on its physical input pin (undoing the pushed sign as appropriate) and fold its binary parent. When H is nonraw its tree has no shared binary producer, making this occurrence-level edit physical. For a shared raw inverter, disconnect only the chosen negative output occurrence and fold its consumer. This saves at least one gate.

The labels that occur at least twice form a vertex cover of the original graph. Indeed for an edge ab whose labels occur only once, the two leaf-to-LCA paths, and the LCA-to-output path, must transmit their operands in every section where both variables are essential. Hence only one fixed two-essential signed truth table (and, even with arbitrary exterior output complementation, at most its complement) is possible. But outside zero gives a AND b; giving one different neighbor of each endpoint value one gives a OR b. Those two neighbors are distinct and nonadjacent by girth at least five. The graph has no cover of size five, verified by testing the 252 five-subsets against its thirteen edges. Therefore at least six raw labels repeat. The 17-leaf budget supplies seven repeats beyond first uses, so at most one label can occur three or more times. Each negatively occurring raw needs at least two positive occurrences and hence three total. The negative leaves use just one distinct raw.

A nonconstant monotone inverter input on that single raw equals the raw itself and its producer gates can be bypassed. It remains to exclude a bare raw inverter, including shared inverter output. Its raw has at least two positive leaf occurrences. Their physical reader gates are distinct (a repeated identical operand is removable). At least one inverter reader is distinct from both, since a gate combining the raw with its inverse is constant. Pin the raw false: the inverter, this inverter reader, and both positive reader gates are four distinct removals. The residual monotone circuit would have at most thirteen gates, contrary to the independently established monotone fourteen-gate floor for each vertex-deleted graph. This rules out the stated sector and leaves circuits with a shared binary producer after NOT contraction.
