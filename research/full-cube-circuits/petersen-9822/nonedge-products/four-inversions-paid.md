# Four inversions cannot attain nine OR-or-NOT gates

Count OR and NOT as paid sites; binary ANDs are free in this section. Use the five-cycle paid lower bound four in ../comb/c5-bound.md and the six-cycle lower bound five in ../comb/c6-pin.md. Every input can be peeled first toward an induced five-cycle avoiding it: the graph with that input removed is connected, and contains such a cycle. Peeling essential inputs to zero while the output stays nonzero loses at least one paid site each time, by following a path to its first OR or NOT through zero-valued ANDs. Thus the full graph needs at least nine paid sites and each single-zero cofactor needs at least eight. Equality at nine forces every raw to have a unique first paid site reachable along AND-only paths: if two distinct such sites existed they would both fold at the first zero restriction. An AND-only path all the way to the output from a raw is impossible because every single-zero cofactor is nonzero.

For completeness, at a paid site let S be the fresh raws with unique first site there. Each path from one of these raws to output passes through that site. At fixed outside values the OR's children are each a conjunction of a fixed subset of S, enabled or disabled by outside values (earlier paid wires have no dependence on S). Thus among all nonconstant increasing sections in S the minterms belong to at most two fixed monomials, or to at most one for a NOT. Downstream is unary in the site at fixed exterior values; taking its complement cannot yield a different nonconstant increasing section of an increasing OR. Every internal edge of S occurs as a minterm with empty outside input and every vertex incident to an exterior edge occurs as a singleton minterm on an exterior singleton input. A proper S containing exactly one internal edge has both endpoints on the boundary by minimum degree two, while one with two internal edges has a nonempty boundary by connectivity. The whole graph has thirteen edges. Therefore fresh sets are independent and have size at most two for OR, one for NOT. A paired OR has its two fresh raws on separate arms. An earliest paid site cannot carry a group of size two: its two raw labels would have to appear on separate arms without any earlier signals, giving their disjunction; each label has an exclusive neighbor by minimum degree two and four-cycle freeness, distinguishing those labels on that neighbor's singleton. An earliest paid site carrying only one raw is consequently NOT of that raw in a reduced circuit. The location result paid-first-positive.md restricts every such bare source NOT to a vertex in H={5,6,7,8,9}. Every vertex in the graph has a neighbor outside H, by the thirteen listed edges.

The second- and third-site OR obstructions in three-inversions-paid.md use only these paid bounds and the forms of earlier sites, independently of the count of later inversions. In particular the first three paid sites cannot be ORs. That note also rules out a nine-paid-site circuit with at most three NOTs. Suppose exactly four NOTs attain nine paid sites. The first three are NOTs. In any initial run of NOTs a site that owns a fresh raw y computes the negation of y conjoined with a subset of prior signals. In fact these three NOTs are bare inversions of distinct raw labels. Inductively suppose the earlier ones are bare inversions of x_i. If the argument for a fresh y includes ¬x_i, setting x_i=true makes y invisible. But every distinct y remains essential under x_i=true: if adjacent it is a singleton disjunct; otherwise y has a neighbor not adjacent to x_i since minimum degree two and four-cycle freeness prevent the two from sharing all of y's neighbors. On that neighbor and x_i, toggling y changes the target. A NOT among the first three that owns no raw is the negation of the conjunction of at most two earlier bare negative signals. Its output is a raw or the OR of two raws. Substitute this with at most one paid site and one fewer NOT, violating either the nine-site floor or the three-inversion exclusion. This establishes the three bare inversions. Each is independent of all earlier paid sites and could be placed first, so each of their distinct labels lies in H.

Now examine the fourth paid site. Write z_i=¬x_i for the three previous signals. If the fourth is the last NOT, it owns no fresh raw: all such a raw's paths would then be antitone beyond this last inversion. Its argument is a conjunction of the z_i, making its output the OR of some x_i. Any omitted x_i remains antitone in the rest of the circuit, impossible. If all three occur, pin one to true, so the last NOT now supplies constant one; each other x_j can now influence the rest only through its antitone z_j, yet it remains positively essential under that pin. Thus the fourth cannot be a NOT.

If the fourth is an OR with no fresh raw, it is an OR of two conjunctions of z_i. After absorption only incomparable nonempty terms matter. A singleton term z_i forces the site to one on x_i=0 in addition to the loss of the first paid inverter for x_i, against the eight-site cofactor floor. Otherwise the two terms must be different two-element sets with a common factor z_i. Reassociate as z_i(z_j∨z_k), using one paid OR and free AND. Setting x_j=0 now folds that paid OR and its source inverter, again impossible. Any redundant term would have allowed bypassing a paid site before the restriction.

With one fresh raw v, if both terms contain v, setting v=0 makes this entire paid OR zero. On an outgoing path its zero propagates to a subsequent paid site, which must exist since the v=0 cofactor is nonzero. Both paid sites fold. If a term avoids v, it is enabled when all three earlier raw labels are zero (all z_i are one). This hides v on that slice. A neighbor outside H witnesses that v remains pivotal there.

With two fresh raws u,v, they appear on separate arms with coefficients that are conjunctions of the three earlier signals. At a singleton exterior setting away from the earlier raw labels both coefficients are true. Such a singleton therefore cannot be an exclusive neighbor of either raw: its section would be u alone or v alone, whereas the OR site supplies u∨v. Hence all exclusive neighbors are among the earlier labels. Each raw has an exclusive neighbor by the same degree and four-cycle argument. If a common neighbor c occurred later, take one earlier exclusive neighbor a of u; a and c are nonadjacent since both neighbor u and the graph has no triangles. On outside support {a} the required section is u. On {a,c} it is u∨v. The site sees exactly the same earlier raw values in both cases, so no downstream unary choice can realize these two different nonconstant increasing sections. Thus every neighbor of u and v would be among the three earlier labels, contrary to every vertex having a neighbor outside H.

This exhausts the fourth site. At exactly nine paid sites at least five NOTs would therefore be necessary, regardless of the number of ANDs. For the separate implication about total size use the physical binary-gate lower bound thirteen (the signed surplus argument in ../physical/surplus.md). A circuit of total size at most seventeen has at most four NOTs by that bound, so cannot attain nine paid sites. Every such candidate needs at least ten OR-or-NOT sites and at most seven ANDs. The thirteen-binary bound is used only for this final conversion; these results leave the unrestricted size interval [16,18].
