# A single nonedge-producing AND cannot save a gate

Combine this argument with rank-two-flattening.md and sole-hot.md. In a minimum-size monotone candidate with exactly one AND H accepting a nonedge, terminalize every other nonterminal AND on the domain of inputs of weight at most two. This preserves the output on that domain and does not increase cost. Bypass redundant or dead sites. The singleton-hot case is excluded by sole-hot.md. Thus write H=OR(A)∧OR(B) for disjoint supports A,B. Terminals receiving H have form (H∨P)∧Q, with Q nonempty and P possibly empty. Define D as the union of all such Q supports. The remaining ordinary products, including P∧Q after H is set to zero, form a flat baseline of safe stars. Write E0 for its edge set.

On the pair layer, the extra products can contribute exactly the edges (A∩D)×B and A×(B∩D). Every such edge lies in the graph.

First suppose A={u}. If u∈D, the full uB star is safe and H would reject every nonedge. Otherwise the only extra edges have form uv with v in B∩D. Let M⊆B∩D consist of the endpoints of those u-edges absent from the baseline. If M is empty the baseline suffices. Otherwise set H to zero, prune all dead gates and append the star u∧OR(M). At least |M|−1 original A-opposite bank gates (in the B cone) become dead. Indeed any wire in the B cone containing two distinct elements of M cannot occur in an ordinary star: its opposite raw support would have to be the unique common neighbor u, contradicting that those u-edges are missing. Project all other raw leaves of the original B bank to zero and substitute the unique raw for each wire seeing only one marked raw. The root sees all |M| marks and therefore the remaining network has at least |M|−1 distinct binary joins, each seeing at least two marked raws. They are among the dead gates just counted. For |M|=1 this charge is zero. These refunds pay for OR(M). H and one folded outgoing reader pay for the product and collector OR, and are distinct from its strict B ancestors. This excludes the singleton-shore case, including the symmetric orientation.

Now assume |A|,|B|≥2. The two intersections A∩D and B∩D then have size at most one each, since two elements adjacent to an entire opposite shore of size at least two would form a four-cycle. If one is empty and the other consists of q∈A, replace H by q∧OR(B) at the same cost, or take the symmetric replacement. This replacement implies H and preserves every required pair. Its new AND is safe. If both are empty the whole H branch can be erased.

It remains that A∩D={q}, B∩D={p}. Thus pq is an edge, all of A neighbors p, and all of B neighbors q. After setting H to zero the only missing edges belong to the two stars pA and qB. Assign the missing central edge to only one arm. Denote the assigned missing raw endpoints by M_A⊆A, M_B⊆B. If neither is nonempty, the baseline suffices. If only one is nonempty, retain its original full arm OR and append the full star to the baseline. This adds at most one AND and one collector OR, paid by deleting H and folding one direct consumer of H. It cannot add a nonedge because the full star lies in the target graph.

Suppose both M_A and M_B are nonempty. At least one is a proper subset, since pq is assigned at most once. We compare the surviving baseline with the physical circuit before H was set to zero. For each original OR wire computing a subset U⊆A with |U|≥2 and U∩M_A nonempty, that wire cannot survive in any ordinary star. Indeed its opposite raw operand would be a set of common neighbors of U, hence exactly {p} since the graph is four-cycle free. The baseline would then already include pu for every u∈U, contrary to the definition of M_A. There is no final raw disjunct. Thus all such wires are dead. The same applies on the B side with q.

The original bank for A contains at least |M_A|−1 plus one if M_A is proper, distinct dead ORs of this type. To see this, contract every maximal subexpression disjoint from M_A to a single common placeholder. The remaining sources are the distinct missing raws and, if A contains any other raw, one placeholder. They all reach the root; consequently at least the number of these sources minus one binary joins are required. This last count may ignore any wire of single-source support by substituting its source directly; all remaining joins combine at least two distinct symbols, including a missing raw. Therefore each corresponds to an original OR gate of support at least two meeting M_A. This counting proof allows shared subexpressions. Since A and B are disjoint, their charged gates are distinct. They pay for forming OR(M_A) and OR(M_B), with at least one unused gate of credit.

Append the two missing-arm products and their collector ORs, costing at most four more gates. One is paid by the spare arm gate and one by H itself. If H has at least two distinct direct readers, they both fold when H is set to zero, covering the final two. If H has exactly one reader and that reader is H∨P, then P is a nonempty raw disjunction: its other operand cannot depend on H before H's unique first reader, and every other AND is terminal. That P reaches all receiving terms on the H side. Thus its raws neighbor each element of D, including adjacent q and p, impossible in a triangle-free graph (overlap would already accept a singleton). If its only reader is a product, it is H∧OR(Q) with Q containing both q and p. This terminal disappears under H=0. Its Q-root is an OR, since it contains q and p. It cannot occur in any surviving star: no raw is adjacent to both q and p. The Q-root is distinct from the charged A and B gates because Q contains elements of both disjoint shores. This gives both required refunds.

The constructed flat circuit realizes exactly the target graph edges and has no more gates than the original. Together with sole-hot.md and rank-two-flattening.md, every monotone implementation with at most seventeen gates has at least two distinct ANDs that accept nonedge pairs. The nonedge pairs witnessed at the two sites may differ. The proof leaves the unrestricted size interval unchanged.
