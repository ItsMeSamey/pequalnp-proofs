# A ten-gate monotone minor in the surviving two-owner sector

Assume the cap16 normalization and let both low owners 0 and 3 receive the inverted wire through OR-only paths. Their forced singleton-hot neighbors are 4,5,8. Write d for binary ancestors used only through the inverter and f for its number of distinct direct binary readers. The canonical common-reader exclusion gives f≥2.

The zero-clamped monotone output F0 accepts every edge incident to 4,5,8, because the actual inverted signal is zero there. In particular it accepts every edge of the induced five-cycle (0,4,3,8,5) and all three pendant edges 49,57,68. Pin 1 and 2 to zero, then successively pin 9,7,6 to zero. Each of the latter three is still essential by its displayed pendant edge versus the singleton at the other end. Hence at least one gate folds on each pin. The remaining circuit computes the induced five-cycle on the full cube: it accepts all cycle edges and is bounded above by the target, whose restriction is exactly this cycle. That cycle needs four OR gates by cycle-paid-floor.md, and three AND gates since its five maximal independent sets must be distinguished by the binary choices of a zero operand at each AND. A choice of one operand per AND yields a disjunction of raws by replacing every AND with that operand. It upper-bounds the original monotone function; every negative input is rejected by at least one such choice, by selecting zero operands at all zero ANDs and propagating gate values in order. One disjunction can reject at most one maximal independent set, since its maximum zero input is still independent. Thus 2^a≥5. The clamped circuit needs at least 7+3=10 binary gates, so fifteen original binaries give d+f≤5.

If the entire inverter prefix is private, at least three essential singleton-hot inputs give d≥2. If any additional singleton is hot, it must lie on the residual cycle (neither naked owner can enter H). Two such singletons were excluded in cap16-guard-repair.md. One leaves a path with cost at most four, while d≥3 and f≥2 already pay for it. Thus the singleton-hot set is exactly {4,5,8}. If H differs from their disjunction, it has a positive minterm disjoint from them with at least two further raws. Its private binary prefix has at least five essential raws, hence d≥4. This contradicts d+f≤5. Consequently the only possible private H in this sector is the literal disjunction of 4,5,8. No claim is made here for a nonprivate H.

The remaining private disjunction is also impossible using the positive-4 cofactor floor of eleven (reductions/cap16-singleton-positive.md and its finite label-map receipt). Replace its private cone by (4 OR 5) OR 8. This preserves all other signals and does not increase size; both private ORs fold on 4=true. There is at least one direct binary reader of raw 4 outside both the private cone and the set of direct inverter readers. To see this, compare singleton 9 with the edge {4,9}. The inverter output goes from one to zero. Every binary site directly reading both 4 and the inverter output is unchanged: its pair of inputs swaps 0,1 to 1,0. If these were all the external raw-4 readers, cut their outputs and the inverter output. The surviving network outside the private cone would be monotone in these cut values and the remaining raw inputs. The cut values stay fixed or decrease, contradicting the required output increase. After pinning 4 to one, this distinct external raw-4 reader folds, the two private ORs fold, the inverter vanishes, and every one of its f≥2 direct readers folds. Six removed sites leave at most ten, contradicting the stated eleven-site floor. Therefore every normalized two-naked-owner circuit must have a nonprivate inverter prefix.

The positive-4 budget also constrains shared prefixes. Pinning 4 to one folds the nonraw H root, its NOT and all f inversion users. If f≥3, these already use all five allowable losses. No direct raw-4 binary reader outside H and the inversion readers would then be possible. Compare the independent input {5,9} to the positive input {4,5,9}. Both have H=1, z=0. A direct raw-4 reader must transmit a change, so one of the inversion readers is R=4 OR z. On pinning 4 high this signal is constant one, and its successor also folds. R cannot be the output since it accepts the empty input, nor can it feed H by chronology. An additional inversion reader directly consuming R would combine z with 4 OR z, and either AND or OR is bypassable by absorption. Thus a new site necessarily folds, contradicting the floor. Consequently f=2.

If the H root itself has an ordinary binary reader W besides the inverter, this W is distinct from the direct inversion readers (combining H and its own complement is constant). It folds when 4 is pinned high, spending the sole additional allowed loss beyond H, the NOT and their two inversion readers. On the same independent-to-positive comparison raw 4 must transmit outside H. W cannot read both 4 and H since H≥4, making either binary operation bypassable. As before some inversion reader must then be R=4 OR z. Its successor must be W by the pin budget. But W combining R with H is constant one for OR, or raw 4 for AND since H≥4. Both violate reducedness. Thus the H root is private while some proper ancestor is shared. In the above notation 1≤d≤3.

In fact H directly reads raw 4. Otherwise some pre-H direct reader U of 4 is true at singleton 4, since the nonraw H is true there and all prefix gates are monotone and false on the empty input. There is only one site outside H, its NOT, and the two inversion readers allowed to fold at 4=true. Any direct pre-H raw-4 reader folds, so U is the only one. Its other operand cannot depend on 4 and is false on the singleton; thus U is an OR and pinning 4 high makes U constant one. All its direct consumers fold. They must lie among H and the inversion readers, so its path to H is a direct wire. No alternative raw-4 route reaches H's other operand; therefore this operand is false at singleton 4 and H must OR it with U. On {5,9} versus {4,5,9}, H and z stay fixed. Any change exiting raw 4 or U to the remaining circuit must use an inversion reader, namely R=4 OR z or R=U OR z; an AND with z transmits no change on this comparison. Both R forms are constant one on the positive pin. As before its successor lies outside all inversion readers by absorption, and neither H nor U can be its successor by chronology. This adds a sixth loss and is impossible. The direct reader H must OR raw 4 with a monotone signal P since H is also true on singleton 5. Thus H=4 OR P physically, with P true on singletons 5 and 8.
