# Where the initial inverter can occur at nine OR-or-NOT sites

Use the nine-site equality structure for the full basis, obtained from the four-site lower bound on the five-cycle and the five-site lower bound on the six-cycle. Every raw then has a unique first OR-or-NOT site reachable through AND-only paths, and the first charged site in any topological order is the inverter of a single raw x. Subsequent AND gates cost nothing in this accounting.

Pinning x=true removes that inverter, leaving its output zero. Follow an outgoing path to the target; it cannot be AND-only throughout, since then the target cofactor x=true would vanish identically. At its next charged site the zero propagates through all preceding ANDs and forces that site to simplify. Thus the target's positive cofactor has at most seven paid sites.

For each of x=0,1,2,3 positive pinning gives two raw singleton disjuncts and the edge function of the connected seven-vertex induced residual on V−N[x]. At x=0 or 3 this residual contains the induced cycle 1,2,7,9,6,1. At x=1 or 2 it contains 0,4,3,8,5,0. Set each singleton disjunct to zero, then peel the two external vertices from the connected residual toward the protected five-cycle; every deleted raw is essential at deletion and the protected output is nonzero. Each deletion loses at least one paid site, by following its first AND-only path to OR or NOT. This gives at least 2+2+4=8 paid sites. At x=4 there are three singleton disjuncts and the induced six-cycle 1,2,7,5,8,6,1. The lower bound is 3+5=8. Therefore no initial inverter at any of vertices 0 through 4 is possible in a nine-paid-site circuit, regardless of the number of remaining inversions.

For x=5,6,7,8 the positive-cofactor cost is exactly seven. The induced residual is a six-vertex path, costing exactly four: successively delete leaves down to an edge for the lower bound and collect its stars for the matching OR count. The three extra singleton disjuncts cost exactly three additional sites, by the same deletion lower bound and an OR collector. Thus for these four possible first raws the next charged site reachable from the initial inverter along AND-only paths must be unique. Otherwise pinning x=true would remove at least three paid sites against this exact lower bound.
