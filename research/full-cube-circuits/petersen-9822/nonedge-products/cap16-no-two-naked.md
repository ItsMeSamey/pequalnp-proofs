# Seven fanout charges exclude two naked low owners

Assume the reduced minimum sixteen-site model with fifteen physical AND/OR sites and one NOT. Use cap16-two-owners.md, cap16-clamp-allocation.md, and reductions/third-shared.md. In the candidate with two naked low owners, these owners are 0 and 3, the physical inverter input is H=4 OR P, and P is monotone and true on both singleton 5 and singleton 8. The root H feeds only the inverter, which has exactly two direct readers. All raw entries of owners 1 and 2 have the form x_i OR P_i and their underlying binary partners are distinct shared gates by the signed private-chain result. These two owners are not naked.

Contract the sole inverter by replacing every binary operand using it by a negated use of its underlying source, retaining every operand occurrence. In the trimmed graph the output is binary. The fifteen binary gates have thirty operand slots, used by ten essential raws and fourteen non-output binary sources. Thus the sum of their numbers of uses in excess of one is six. A shared gate or a repeated raw consumes at least one unit. In this graph H is shared by its two negated uses. H cannot be either low partner: as a positive actual partner it would remain true at singleton 4 and on all extensions, impossible by the one-owner domination table. Its direct negation as partner would make the corresponding owner naked.

Whenever all paths from an essential raw i pass through a monotone pre-inversion gate K true on another singleton s, i is inessential on the entire s=true slice. This contradicts the graph predicate here: if i,s are adjacent, compare singleton s to the pair; otherwise i has at least two neighbors and at most one in common with s, yielding a neighbor t of i nonadjacent to s. The independent set {s,t} becomes positive when i is added. This argument applies to any two distinct vertices.

Follow each singly used low raw 1,2 to its first shared binary, or the output. Their first sites are distinct, and neither is either low partner, by reductions/third-shared.md. Hence at least one first site Q is a shared gate. It cannot be H by the preceding observation with s=4. We already have four distinct shared gates P_1,P_2,H,Q. Raw 4 is repeated: if its only use were H, then since the only physical use of H is through the inverter its influence on the monotone physical continuation would be antitone, contrary to essential increasing dependence.

For each of 5 and 8 charge a further unit. If j is repeated, use a raw excess at j. Otherwise follow its unique contracted path to its first shared gate K_j. There is a pre-inversion path from j to P, since P is true on singleton j and false on the empty input. The first fork must strictly precede P on that path: otherwise every influence from j would factor through P, which is already true on the opposite singleton in {5,8}. Thus K_j is a monotone pre-inversion binary gate. It is true on singleton j, since if it retained its empty-input value zero then P would retain its empty-input value as well. It is false on the opposite singleton, by the same pivotal-slice observation. Consequently K_5 and K_8, when both needed, differ. Neither is H. Neither can be either underlying low partner, since as a pre-inversion site K_j has no negated operand use (only H can be used inverted), and a monotone low partner cannot be true at a singleton. Neither can be Q since all influence of its low owner passes through Q and K_j is hot on a different singleton.

The four shared gates, the repeated raw 4 and the two charges at 5 and 8 consume at least seven distinct fanout-excess units. Only six exist. No normalized sixteen-site circuit can therefore have two naked low owners. This does not address zero or one naked owner.

The same contraction verifies an ancillary sign claim independent of this exclusion. If a raw has a unique occurrence and no shared binary on its path to the output, restoring the NOT cannot create a branch: any two outgoing physical uses after a contracted inversion would give at least two signed operand occurrences at its source. The physical path is unique, all side inputs are independent of the raw, and the direction of dependence along it changes once per NOT. An increasing essential output therefore requires even parity. With only one physical inverter, neither its input nor its output depends on this raw. By the exactly-three-shared first-site result, dependency of H on both owners 1 and 2 would require at least four shared binaries, leaving at most two excess units on raws.
