# Eliminating a sole bad AND with a hot singleton

Call an AND bad when it accepts a nonedge pair of graph 9822. Suppose H is the sole bad AND. Terminalize every other nonterminal AND by rank-two-flattening.md. Rerouted safe gates reject all singletons, preserving H's singleton values. If H becomes safe the lower bound applies. Otherwise H now has raw OR-bank operands A,B, and every other AND is terminal. Each receiving terminal has form (H∨P)Q, with P,Q raw disjunctions, possibly P empty. H cannot reach both sides or the output by OR alone because H accepts a nonedge. A,B must be incomparable in any minimum circuit; otherwise H equals the smaller operand.

Let D be the union of the Qs. The ordinary raw products (including PQ) are safe on the rank-two layer. The pairs accepted through H are precisely those meeting all of A,B,D. Assume D nonempty. Put X=A∩B and suppose X nonempty. Singleton rejection gives X∩D empty; every x in X neighbors all of D. Every s in D∩(A−B) neighbors all of B, and symmetrically for D∩(B−A). Both latter classes cannot be nonempty since any x together with one from each would form a triangle.

If |X|≥2, four-cycle freeness gives D={d}. If d∈A replace H by OR(B): every added pair accepted through a receiving product contains d and a vertex of B and was already accepted there. Symmetrically if d∈B. Otherwise only d paired with X needs H. Replace H by OR(X), which implies H and preserves all required pairs. For |X|≤2 its cost is at most one. For |X|=3 the original incomparable A,B each have size at least four. The A-bank root becomes dead: no OR-only path from it can lead into a raw operand facing a nonempty raw side, since such a support has size at most the maximum degree three. Nor can it reach an opposite receiving operand (contained in D), an output OR disjunct, or B by inclusion. All remaining uses went to H. Removing its unused OR pays for the second OR forming OR(X). Larger X exceeds the degree of d.

If X={x} and D avoids both A−B and B−A, only x paired with D requires H, so replace H by x. Otherwise suppose s∈D∩(A−B). B has at least two vertices by incomparability, so four-cycle freeness makes s unique. The required pairs are xD and sB. Replace H by x∨(s∧OR(B)), which implies H and stays true on every required pair. The new AND is safe. The A-bank root contains adjacent x,s. It cannot reach an ordinary operand facing a nonempty raw side: a common neighbor would form a triangle and an overlapping operand raw would accept a singleton. Nor can it reach an opposite receiving operand (x∉D) or B (s∉B). Removing this unused root pays for the extra OR. Reverse the names A and B for the other orientation.

Each construction leaves the output correct on the rank-two layer and all ANDs safe on that layer at no extra cost. Rank-two flattening yields eighteen gates. Thus in a smaller monotone circuit a sole bad AND must reject every singleton.
