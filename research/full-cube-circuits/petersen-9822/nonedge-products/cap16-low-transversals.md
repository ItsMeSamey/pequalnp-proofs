# Six high inputs suffice to activate every pre-inversion gate

Consider the 9822 edge predicate in a reduced circuit with one inverter and fifteen binary gates. Write L={0,1,2,3}. Assume the signed binary zero-cofactor floors of fourteen for 0,3 and total zero-cofactor floors of fifteen for 1,2. A reduced circuit has no constant or duplicate-operand gates.

Each low variable has exactly one physical reader, a binary OR. Indeed a raw inverter on that low has a separate positive reader by essential increasing dependence. Its negative reader is distinct since combining the raw with its inverse gives a constant. Pinning the low zero deletes two binary gates and the NOT, contradicting each applicable floor. With no direct inverter, two binary readers likewise contradict either floor upon pinning zero. A unique AND reader would be forced zero, hence some further binary reader must fold, either directly or through the sole NOT. The pinned output is nonconstant and cannot itself be either forced signal. Essentiality supplies at least one reader.

Two low inputs cannot share that reader. A shared OR would identify the two singletons even with any common exterior input fixed. For an adjacent pair the pair assignment and either singleton would also be identified, although only the former contains an edge. For a nonadjacent pair, each vertex has at least two neighbors and the pair has at most one common neighbor by absence of four-cycles. An exclusive neighbor separates the corresponding singletons.

Set all low inputs to zero and all six remaining inputs to one. By induction in circuit order every binary ancestor of the inverter evaluates to one. If it reads a low, its other OR operand is a high or an earlier nonraw wire, since the four readers are distinct. Otherwise both operands are high or earlier nonraw wires. The same conclusion holds for any binary gate with no inversion ancestor. Thus every such wire has a minimal positive support contained in the high inputs. The inverter input H, provided it is nonraw, satisfies the same conclusion. In particular restricting any subset of lows to zero leaves H nonconstant: the displayed assignment accepts and all zero rejects. This last claim concerns the inverter input and allows the inverter output to become irrelevant after restriction.

The result uses no lower bound for deleting vertex 9. It does not require the high-only positive support to be independent.
