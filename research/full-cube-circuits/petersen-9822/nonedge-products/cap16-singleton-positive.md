# Positive restrictions near the sole inverter

In the reduced size-16 9822 candidate with one NOT and fifteen binary gates, H is its nonraw monotone input. Each low i=0,1,2,3 has a distinct sole gate Gi reading it, a binary OR (cap16-low-transversals.md).

Pinning i=1 (Boolean true) gives the disjunction of the two raw neighbors and the seven-raw induced graph deleting i and both neighbors. Each singleton disjunct is essential even after its companion is set to zero, since the graph rejects zero. Zero-pinning an essential singleton eliminates at least one physical AND, OR or NOT reader, by constant propagation and wire bypass: the resulting function is nonliteral. Perform this on the resulting physical circuit again for the other singleton. The remaining seven-raw physical circuit saves at least two gates. positive_pin.log gives verified permutations to the public unrestricted L-meta-982 lower bounds and checks all 512 assignments for each positive cofactor. The four positive-pin floors are 12,13,13,12.

If H accepts singleton i, pinning i true folds H to one through its monotone prefix: every gate true with other inputs all zero propagates structurally to one. Lose H, NOT, and at least one inverse reader B. NOT cannot be the output of increasing essential F. If H differs from Gi, also lose Gi, a fourth distinct gate. If H=Gi, positive dependence on i requires a direct reader of H besides its inverter, since otherwise all influence enters a monotone downstream circuit only through decreasing NOT H. This reader is distinct from B: a joint H and NOT H gate is constant. Thus pinning eliminates at least four gates either way. The 13-gate floors imply H rejects singleton 1 and singleton 2.

For i=0 or 3, assume H differs from Gi and accepts singleton i. Its 12-gate floor means exactly four gates can be lost: Gi,H,NOT,B. No other pre-inversion gate can be true at singleton i, since it would be structurally constant one after the pin and fold. Raw i cannot feed H directly. Gi is thus the only singleton-hot operand available to H, forcing H=Gi OR Q (a reduced AND requires distinct operands). H has no other reader, since it would fold and B cannot combine H and NOT H. To avoid solely antitone dependence on i, Gi must have a reader other than H. This gate folds when Gi is set true, so must be B. The AND of Gi and NOT(Gi OR Q) is constant; their OR equals Gi OR NOT Q. Both cases bypass H physically, since H and NOT H have no other readers. This contradicts the unrestricted floor of 16. Thus H accepts singleton 0 or 3 only when it equals that particular raw's entry. At most one low singleton is hot.

Any OR-only path from NOT H to an owner's guard requires H to accept both neighbor singletons (cap16-clamp-allocation.md). Since owners 1,2 are adjacent, neither can have such a path.
