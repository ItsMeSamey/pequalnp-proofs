# Nine OR-or-NOT sites require at least four inversions

Use the physical paid-site lower bound nine and its equality conditions. At nine paid sites every raw has a unique first paid site along AND-only paths. For a paid gate its set S of fresh raws consists of precisely those with first site there. At an OR site |S|≤2; if |S|=2 it is an independent pair split one to each arm. At a NOT site |S|≤1. The first site is the inversion of a single raw; its possible positions are 5,6,7,8,9 by paid-first-positive.md. For every raw i the zero cofactor requires at least eight paid sites: the connected graph G−i can be peeled to a protected induced five-cycle avoiding i, giving four losses plus the four-site cycle floor. These prerequisites concern all physical NOT counts.

Before the first OR, a gate input is a conjunction of raw labels and preceding paid outputs. In general the two arms of an OR are each a conjunction of fresh raws and preceding paid outputs: a raw with an AND-only path to the present site is fresh here, by uniqueness. Repeated paid factors or raws are immaterial. With all other raws fixed, every downstream output is unary in a gate that owns all the fresh labels under discussion. A nonconstant increasing section in fresh labels must agree with that OR gate's section rather than its complement.

Write z=¬x for the first site. The second cannot be a paid OR. With no fresh raw it is unary in z. With one fresh raw v the only two-input positive function needing a paid OR is z∨v; on x=0 that site and the first NOT both fold, contradicting the eight-site cofactor floor. With two fresh raws u,v, its form is ua(z)∨vb(z), where each surviving coefficient is either z or 1. Both vertices have an exclusive neighbor: they are nonadjacent, minimum degree is two, and at most one common neighbor can occur without a four-cycle. On an exterior singleton in each exclusive neighborhood, the output section must equal u alone or v alone. Thus the coefficients would need both incomparable patterns (1,0) and (0,1), impossible for two increasing functions of one bit. A redundant OR could be bypassed, contradicting the nine-site lower bound. Consequently the second site is a NOT. A NOT with no fresh raw at this position is redundant or just produces x, which is available raw. Its single fresh raw y gives the only possible forms w=¬y or w=¬(z∧y). If this were the last NOT then all y influence would enter through its negative occurrence, making the target antitone in y. This also proves the at-most-two-inversion obstruction.

Now assume exactly three NOTs. If the third site were the third NOT it could not own a fresh raw (all such input influence would be antitone). Its input is z, w, or z∧w. The negations of z and w are respectively raw x and either raw y or the free conjunction z∧y. In both possible w cases the negation of z∧w is x∨y. Thus a third NOT in this position can be replaced with at most one paid site and no new NOT. This contradicts the nine-site lower bound or the preceding at-most-two-inversion exclusion. The third site must be an OR.

If it owns no fresh raw, the only genuine paid operation is z∨w; x=0 forces z=1 and spends two paid sites. Suppose it owns one fresh v. The OR is the disjunction of two monomials on v,z,w. Ignore absorbed or redundant terms, since a single monomial needs only free conjunctions. A term z alone or w alone forces loss of this OR as well as the corresponding inverter on x=0 or y=0 respectively. If both terms contain v the only remaining nonredundant pair factors as v(z∨w), and the same pin eliminates the refactored paid OR. Otherwise the term without v must be zw. A v-containing term vz or vw factors the whole expression as z(v∨w) or w(v∨z), eliminating two paid sites on y=0 or x=0 respectively. Hence only v∨zw remains. In either model for w, zw=¬x∧¬y. On x=y=0 the site hides v regardless of all future raw values. Consequently every neighbor of v belongs to {x,y}: otherwise a singleton outside this pair would make v pivotal. Minimum degree is two, so v has degree two and its two neighbors are x,y. The degree-two neighborhoods are {4,5},{2,6},{1,7},{4,8}. If w=¬y, both first inverters are eligible to precede all other paid gates and their input labels must both be in {5,6,7,8,9}, contradicting the neighborhood list. If w=¬(z∧y), the list excludes x=9 and first-site restriction leaves x in {5,6,7,8}. But z then has two different next paid sites on AND-only paths, w and this OR (the latter using z in its zw arm). This contradicts paid-first-positive.md. These arguments on the disjunction of monomials also handle the dependent w case: use the specified refactorizations, or ordinary absorption on formal variables, each of which remains valid on their physically reachable states.

Finally suppose the OR owns two fresh raws u,v. Its form is ua(z,w)∨vb(z,w), with coefficients among 1,z,w,zw. Both asymmetric activation patterns must occur by the exclusive-neighbor tests, so the coefficients must be z and w in some order. At every singleton outside {x,y}, z=w=1. Therefore all exclusive neighbors of u and v are among x,y. Each shore has at least one exclusive neighbor, the neighborhoods have at most one common element, and degrees are at least two. Thus u,v have degree two, one common neighbor, and their respective unique exclusive neighbors are x and y. The only such nonadjacent pair is {0,3}, with shared neighbor 4 and exclusive neighbors 5 and 8. Now set raw 5 true, raw 8 false, and all remaining exterior raws other than 4 false. When 4 is zero the output section on 0,3 is raw 0; when 4 is one the section is 0∨3 (here 0 denotes the raw vertex label). The third gate is unchanged as a function of 0 and 3 across these backgrounds since it sees only the earlier 5 and 8 signals besides the two fresh raws. No downstream unary choice can realize these two different nonconstant increasing sections. This contradiction exhausts the third site.

Hence a circuit for this graph at the paid minimum nine must use at least four NOT gates. The statement is conditional only on the established paid floors and unique-frontier equality conditions, not on any estimate of the number of free AND gates; it does not determine the unrestricted minimum total size.
