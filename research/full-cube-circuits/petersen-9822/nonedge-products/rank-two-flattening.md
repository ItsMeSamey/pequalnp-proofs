# A small monotone circuit must accept a nonedge inside an AND gate

In a binary monotone circuit for the 9822 graph predicate on ten inputs, suppose every AND rejects every two-vertex nonedge. Each vertex is on a nonedge, so monotonicity makes every AND reject all singleton inputs. The empty input also gives zero. Thus on inputs of weight at most two each AND implies the target. The argument permits arbitrary overlap of operands and arbitrary fanout.

While an AND h reaches another AND, retain the gate h itself, replace every old outgoing occurrence of h by zero, fold constants and delete dead gates, and take the OR of the resulting output with h. When h was false the old and new outputs agree. When h was true on a weight-at-most-two input, that input was a graph edge; the appended h restores acceptance. All other retained ANDs only decrease pointwise under the rerouting. The appended OR is terminal. Thus every AND remains zero on nonedges and the new output agrees with the target on all weight-at-most-two inputs. One original direct consumer of h necessarily disappears by zero folding. The appended OR costs at most that one gate. Constant propagation can also remove further gates. The set of ordered distinct AND pairs in the ancestry relation strictly decreases: no new such pair arises through OR folding or appending the final OR, and h no longer reaches another AND. This proves termination without assuming a bound on fanout.

At termination all ANDs use raw OR-bank operands and feed only an OR collector. There is no raw-to-output disjunct by singleton rejection. In any live term its two raw supports are disjoint, again by singleton rejection. Their complete cross-product consists only of graph edges by the pair tests. Conversely every graph edge must occur in one cross-product. This flat circuit computes the full-cube graph predicate since every minterm is an edge. It has at most the gate cost of the original.

The supplied graph has thirteen edges, no four-cycle, and minimum vertex-cover size six. In a flat representation each nonempty product therefore describes a star. Combine products with the same center; one extra OR on the arms pays for itself by removing a product and collector input. Two different centers cannot share a nontrivial arm OR, since two common neighbors would give a four-cycle. For t remaining centers and arm sizes s_j the circuit costs at least sum(s_j−1)+t+(t−1), which is at least 13+6−1=18. This proof reuses the distinct-center counting argument of ../geometry/multilinear.md.

Consequently every monotone binary implementation of size at most seventeen has an AND that accepts a two-vertex nonedge. This is a necessary condition; signed circuits and circuits that mask a generated nonedge are not excluded.
