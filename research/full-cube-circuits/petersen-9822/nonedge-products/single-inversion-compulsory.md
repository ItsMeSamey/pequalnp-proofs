# A compulsory input of the inverted signal

Use the one-NOT model of single-inversion.md with b≤16 binary gates and a live, nonconstant output. The zero restrictions at vertices 1,2 have total-gate lower bound 15. Those at 4,5,6,7,8 have lower bound 14. The corresponding immutable nine-input lower bounds are L-meta-10832, L-meta-9386, L-meta-10414 and L-meta-9409 after the relabelings in ../reductions/all_bounds.log (the numbers in that machine log are forbidden budgets, one below the stated size).

Call x compulsory for H if H≤x. Pinning x=0 makes H constant zero; ordinary constant propagation detects this because all gates leading to H are monotone. Indeed their values with all remaining free inputs one detect exactly the wires identically zero. The NOT disappears and each distinct direct binary reader of its output folds. In addition, the root of nonraw H folds. All these binary sites are distinct. If H=x is raw, essential positive dependence of the target on x requires at least one positive binary reader of x in addition to a reader of the NOT output. These form distinct sites in a reduced circuit, since a binary gate reading x and its complement is constant. The output cannot itself be the live NOT of a monotone signal. Thus in both cases at least two binary gates vanish and the residual has no NOT.

It follows that neither vertex 1 nor vertex 2 is compulsory for H. The residual would otherwise have at most fourteen gates against its fifteen-gate lower bound.

Now take x in {4,5,6,7,8}. The fourteen-gate lower bound forces b=16 and exactly two binary-site losses upon x=0. If H is nonraw, those sites are its root and the single direct NOT reader r. All positive raw x readers are among these two, since each would also fold. Some reader in the H cone must encounter x. Hence H itself directly reads x and its other input P is independent of x. Since H≤x its only irredundant form is x∧P. The H output has no positive use: any direct binary reader would also fold under x=0 and the only possible such site r cannot read both H and its negation in a reduced circuit. If r does not also read x, all x-to-output paths go through the inverter, making the target antitone in its essential variable x. Thus r directly reads x. Its OR form is constant one; its AND form equals x∧¬P. Retargeting the NOT to P in the second case deletes the original H binary gate. Consequently every nonraw case at these five vertices admits a strictly smaller implementation; it need not occur in a minimum representative.

Finally suppose H=x at one of these five vertices. There must be exactly one positive binary x reader g and one negative reader r, as all readers fold. If g is an AND, it goes to zero under x=0. A downstream reader distinct from r would fold as a third binary gate. Since the pinned target is nonconstant, g cannot be the output, so g must feed only r. Its other operand cannot depend on x, and the two possible downstream expressions are constant zero and ¬x∨P, respectively. Both leave x inessential or antitone, contrary to the graph predicate. Thus g is an OR. If r is an OR, it goes to one under x=0 and similarly can feed only g. Since g is an OR, this makes g constant one, again impossible. Therefore r is an AND. In particular the only raw inverted forms still requiring attention there have the unique direct entries x∨P and ¬x∧Q. The two other operands need not be globally independent of x when these readers have other downstream paths.
