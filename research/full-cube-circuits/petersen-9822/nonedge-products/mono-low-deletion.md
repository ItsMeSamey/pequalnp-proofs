# Fifteen monotone gates after deleting any degree-two vertex

Assume the unconditional monotone size-seventeen composition floor: a monotone circuit of size at most seventeen requires at least seven AND gates for the ten-vertex target. Let i=0 or 3. By nine-vertex-frontier.md a monotone circuit for G−i needs at least nine OR gates (the paid lower there permits up to four inverters). If its size were at most fourteen it would contain at most five ANDs. Form the disjunction of the two neighbors of i, conjoin it with i, and OR this with the old output. The resulting circuit computes G on the whole cube, has at most seventeen gates and at most six ANDs, a contradiction. The previously checked total-gate deletion bounds already give fifteen at vertices 1 and 2.

In any representation with one NOT and sixteen binary gates, no vertex i in {0,1,2,3} can be compulsory for the inverter input H. Pinning i false would force H false. If H is nonraw, its binary root folds, as does a distinct binary reader of the inversion. If H is the raw i, one binary reader of each polarity is necessary by increasing essential dependence; they cannot be the same site because such a gate is constant. The remaining circuit would be monotone with at most fourteen gates. These arguments impose no restriction on the other raw readers or on sharing.
