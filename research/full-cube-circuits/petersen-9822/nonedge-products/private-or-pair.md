# A private two-input OR containing a low positive-pin vertex

Assume a reduced size-seventeen circuit with sixteen binary gates, one inverter, and a fully private input H=x_s OR x_t, with s=1 or 2. Replace the private portion by this single OR if necessary. The positive cofactor at s has total lower bound thirteen by reductions/cap16-singleton-positive.md. Let z denote the inversion output.

At least one binary reader U of s outside H is not a direct z reader. Choose a neighbor u of s distinct from t (there are two choices of neighbor before this exclusion). At the singleton u, z=1; upon adding s, z=0. A gate directly combining s and z stays fixed because its two input values swap. If all external positive s readers had this form, the rest of the circuit, cut at these fixed signals and z, could only decrease despite the required edge. Pinning s to one deletes H, every direct z reader, and U. The thirteen-gate floor permits exactly three binary losses from the sixteen, so there is just one z reader r and one such external reader U. No other site can fold.

If r were an AND, z=0 would force r to zero, so its successor would have to be U (the pinned target is nonconstant). Then U reads both s and r and would become constant as well, forcing another loss or constant output. Thus r is OR. If U were OR, the positive s pin forces U high. Its successor could only be r; being OR, r would become high and again force an extra loss. Therefore U is AND. Nor can r directly read s in addition to z: in that case it is high on the positive pin and would have to feed U, both inputs of which become high. The same contradiction applies. Consequently outside H the raw s has a unique reader, the AND U; r is a separate OR.

If the pair of hot inputs is {1,2}, the preceding applies to both. Clamp z to zero, deleting its sole OR reader by bypass. Each raw 1,2 still has at most one direct reader, an AND; if its reader is dead the raw cannot affect this clamp at all. The clamp must accept edge 12, because H is true there. With only raws 1 and 2 positive its earliest true binary gate must directly combine those raws (a repeated-operand gate is bypassable). Hence the unique outer reader of either raw is the common product x_1 AND x_2. At singleton 6 and upon adding 1, that product stays zero and z drops from one to zero; no external positive transition is possible in the monotone continuation, contradicting edge 16. Thus H cannot equal x_1 OR x_2. This argument uses no low sole-reader premise from the sixteen-total-site case.

More directly, this rules out any pair containing s=1 or 2. On the opposite pin s=0, the private disjunction H bypasses to the other raw, U becomes identically zero, and some distinct successor of U folds. U cannot be the output because the pinned target is nonconstant. Thus at least three gates disappear and at most fourteen remain, against the signed total floor fifteen for G−s.

Now assume the outer circuit after removing the single private OR and giving it free input z has seven ANDs, hence eight ORs. Suppose the private pair s,t lies in {5,6,7,8} and is adjacent. Both positive cofactors need seven ORs (three neighbor singleton disjuncts plus a P6). The zero clamp F0 has at most seven OR gates, since at least one is lost on following the z=0 path to its first OR. Thus F0 has exactly seven OR gates and pinning either owner high can remove no further OR. Both owners remain essential in F0 on each of their three incident edges. All their direct readers in a reduced F0 are ANDs.

On their edge, the first true nonraw gate in F0 must be the direct product of s and t, since there are no other true raw inputs and neither owner enters a direct OR. Each also needs a different reader besides this product to recognize its edge to some other neighbor with the opposite owner absent. At most one of the two owners can gain any direct readers through aliasing during construction of F0: to create a first new wire alias of a particular raw, the zero clamp must bypass an OR with a zero operand. No constant one arises on this monotone homogeneous zero clamp, and equal-operand collapse to the raw requires a prior alias or an originally redundant repeated-operand gate. The alias first created for s and that for t cannot use the same gate, while at most one OR was lost. Dead gate deletion creates no aliases. For at least one owner its two distinct AND readers were already direct physical AND readers. In the original signed circuit, zeroing that owner bypasses H and forces both these AND gates to zero. A successor of the later of the two AND gates also folds; the nonconstant zero cofactor precludes it from being output. This leaves at most thirteen total gates, contrary to the signed fourteen-gate zero-cofactor floor for each of 5,6,7,8. The outer-seven case therefore excludes the three pairs 57,58,68.

Every outer-seven private pair with a hot vertex in 5,…,8 has a single point at which the inversion enters an OR. Indeed clamping z=0 loses exactly one OR L. Every path from z to output encounters a first OR, since F0 is nonzero. Each first OR loses an operand by zero propagation along an AND-only prefix, so every such first OR must be L. Precisely one operand D of L depends on z: an upstream dependence necessarily follows an AND-only path and forces that operand zero. If both operands depended on z then L itself would be zero and, for nonzero output, an additional downstream OR would fold. Write the other, z-independent operand as P. Every pre-L gate on a path from z is an AND and has no side use avoiding L. On this region D equals z AND W with W=D|z=1; set W=1 when D is z itself. In particular if z has f≥2 direct readers they are all internal to this region and all are ANDs (a direct z operand of L would leave its other operand independent and allow no further reader). Clamp z to one in the region and insert one AND with z at D. All former direct readers fold and the new gate restores D exactly; the other signals depending on z had no external exit. This saves at least f−1 sites. Hence f=1 in a size-minimum representation. If the zero clamp generates a new literal alias of a hot raw, its first such alias must be the sole bypassed OR L, and P must be the raw itself, since it depends on no inverted signal and no earlier alias was available. The physical reader of z may still be AND when W is nonconstant.

A hot-owner alias at L actually excludes the circuit. More generally if P alone is hot at singleton s, it dominates s by monotonicity. Replace the inverted wire z by NOT t and discard the private H. For s=0 nothing changes; for s=1 L remains one and all z influence exits through it. This saves a site. Consequently both hot owners are singleton-cold in P and no projected aliases of either can occur. Each s in 5,…,8 therefore has a physically direct external AND reader U_s, since there are no OR readers in reduced F0. It cannot have any additional positive exterior reader: pinning s=0 bypasses H, both direct readers fold, and a successor of U_s also folds. This successor cannot be another direct s reader, since combining s AND W with s is bypassable by absorption. Four losses violate the signed fourteen-gate floor. Likewise U_s has just one direct successor, an OR: two successors add two losses, while a sole AND successor forces zero and a further loss or constant output.

For the possible adjacent pairs s,9 with s=6 or 7, examine reduced F0. Its positive-9 cofactor needs at least six ORs. Hence from its seven OR gates at most one folds upon pinning 9=true. If raw 9 also had only AND readers, the edge s9 would force a direct s AND 9 product, incompatible with s's sole reader having to transmit the other neighbor edges. So 9 directly feeds an OR R=9 OR Q. This is its unique direct OR and R has no direct OR successor. In a reduced monotone F0 no other nonraw signal can be true at singleton 9: the earliest further true AND would combine 9 and R (absorbs to 9) or identical operands, while a further true OR directly consuming either would cost a second OR on the positive pin. It follows that the other operand of the sole U_s, which must accept each neighbor singleton in F0, is exactly R. Thus R is true on the two other neighbors of s also. These restrictions do not exclude either pair.

Both adjacent pairs with 9 are in fact excluded. Pin the other owner s=6 or 7 to zero in the original circuit. This bypasses H to 9, forces U_s to zero and removes its unique OR reader. Exactly fourteen gates must remain by the signed G−s floor; hence the inverter survives, now directly on 9. The double-deletion target G−s−9 is the five-cycle on 0,4,3,8,5 with a three-edge path attached (at 5 when s=6 and, under reflection, at 8 when s=7). Its eight vertices form a connected unicyclic graph so seven OR gates are necessary by the cycle peeling bound. It has ten maximal independent sets: when the attachment vertex belongs to the set, there are two options on the two nonconsecutive free-side cycle vertices forming an edge, and two options on the last two path vertices forming another edge. When the attachment vertex is absent, the four remaining cycle vertices form a path with three maximal independent sets, and the three path-arm vertices form a path with two; the two neighbors of the attachment vertex on the cycle are the endpoints of that four-vertex path, and every maximal independent set of that path includes at least one endpoint. Thus there are four plus six sets. At least four ANDs are required, so the double deletion needs eleven monotone gates. The fourteen-site single deletion has thirteen binary gates. Setting 9 to zero removes the NOT and must fold a positive reader of 9 and a distinct negative reader, leaving at most eleven. Equality forces unique readers. The positive reader is the physical OR R above: the no-alias property for hot raw 9 makes it a direct physical reader, and it precedes U_s, so is different from all three removed sites. Its other input remains positive on either singleton neighbor of s different from 9 at s=9=0: that property holds in F0 where z=0, and increasing z to one cannot lower the outer signal. The negative reader must be AND. If OR, pinning 9 zero makes it one, so its only possible successor is the positive reader R. As R is also OR it becomes one, causing a third fold or constant output.

In this nine-input circuit write the two entries as 9 OR Q and NOT 9 AND T. With 9=0, Q is monotone in the remaining inputs. On an independent background meeting the surviving neighbors of 9, Q must be false: adding 9 would otherwise leave its positive entry true and lower its negative entry to zero, so no gain would be possible in the remaining monotone continuation. By monotonicity on the 9=0 slice, any independent true input of Q must avoid those neighbors and dominate each (append any undominated neighbor to see this). No singleton can do that: the two surviving neighbors have no other common neighbor, since the original graph contains no four-cycle. This contradicts the singleton positives of Q. Thus 69 and 79 are also impossible in the outer-seven private pair sector.
