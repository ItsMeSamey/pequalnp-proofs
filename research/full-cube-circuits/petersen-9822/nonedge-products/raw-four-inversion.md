# The sole NOT cannot invert raw vertex four

Assume a size-at-most-seventeen circuit with one NOT directly inverting vertex x=4, so at most sixteen sites are binary. Use the zero-pin bound and paired-reader result of single-inversion-compulsory.md. In a reduced representative the only raw entries are one positive OR g=x∨P and one binary reader r=¬x∧Q of the NOT output. Write P0 for the function of the remaining inputs obtained by fixing x=0 in P.

If B is independent in G−x and contains a neighbor of x, then P0(B)=0. Indeed if it were one, the g output would remain one across x changing from zero to one. The r output can only decrease since its final value is zero. With g fixed and r decreasing, no other gate can increase: cut both immediate raw-entry outputs and use monotonicity of the remaining circuit. Yet the target changes from zero to one. Consequently if P0 is true at any independent B, that B avoids the neighbors of x and dominates each such neighbor (has a member adjacent to it). Otherwise append an undominated neighbor to B and apply monotonicity of P0.

At vertex four, the neighbors are 0,3,9. For an independent B avoiding them and x, domination of 0 would force vertex 5, and domination of 3 would force vertex 8. But 5 and 8 are adjacent. Thus P0 implies the edge predicate of G−4 on all assignments. Also P at x=1 is at most P0 pointwise: before g there is no positive entry of x, and the only possible remaining entry is r, whose value falls to zero at x=1. Therefore P implies the original output on both slices.

Replace every occurrence of g by raw x, keep P, and append P to the previous output by a new OR. If P was false this does not change any signal used by the old output. If P was true the old output was true, and the appended P restores it. There is no change to P itself because it precedes g. The single binary OR removed pays for the appended OR. Any reductions yielding fewer than sixteen binary gates are impossible while both polarities of x are effective: pinning x=0 would discard the NOT and two binary readers, leaving ≤13 against the lower bound 14. If the NOT becomes unused during simplification, its old binary reader also disappears, leaving at most fifteen monotone gates against the full-target lower bound sixteen. Thus the transformed circuit again has precisely one positive OR entry and one negative AND entry. Gates consuming both x and ¬x cannot arise in the transformed path: their constant folding would give the same forbidden savings. A direct positive x disjunct at the output is impossible by singleton rejection.

The transformation strictly advances the positive raw entry through the original graph. No previously appended collector OR can become a positive reader of x unless the output has an OR-only path from x: all appended gates lie on the final chain of OR disjuncts. Thus the new positive reader must be an original proper descendant of the removed positive reader. There are finitely many such gates. Repeating with the new P0 gives a contradiction.

For x=5,6,7,8 the domination test alone gives P0≤F(G−x)∨the product of, respectively, {2,4,6}, {2,4,5}, {1,4,8}, {1,4,7}. Each listed triple is the only inclusion-minimal independent set avoiding x and its neighbors while dominating all those neighbors. This last claim is a finite adjacency check; it does not eliminate these four inversion positions.
