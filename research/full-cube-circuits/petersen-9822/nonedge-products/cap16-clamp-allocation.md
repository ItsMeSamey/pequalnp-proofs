# AND allocation under a zero clamp of the sole inversion

Use the four sole entry ORs and the distinct charges in cap16-low-partners.md. Let k count the owners whose zero-clamped partner remains positive on an independent assignment. Let d count all physical AND gates whose output is identically zero under the same clamp. Then the physical number of AND gates is at least k+d+1.

The k charged sites are positive on independent inputs after clamping, so they are distinct from the d sites. The output F0 under the zero clamp satisfies F0≤F by formal monotonicity, and F0 is true on all ones because the monotone nonconstant inverter input is then one and its actual inverse is zero. From the clamped output on all ones trace true operands backwards through ORs until encountering an AND or raw. No raw can reach the output in this fashion since its singleton would be accepted by F0. The clamped zero wire cannot lie on this trace. Thus a physical AND has an OR-only path to the output, is positive on all ones, and accepts no independent input. This distinguishes it from both prior collections. In particular each direct AND reader of the inverted wire is among the d, regardless of its fanout. Under the separate ten-paid-gates floor and overall size sixteen, k+d≤5.

A second necessary condition addresses a common post-inversion partner of two low entries. Let B be an independent witness for the partner, with both owners removed. The partner and inversion input are upstream of both sole entries and hence independent of the owners. The signed guard lemma forces H(B)=0. B avoids both neighborhoods: if an owner had a neighbor in B, adding the owner could not change its already-true sole entry. For every t in either neighborhood having no neighbor in B, the independent input B∪{t} forces H to one. Otherwise the formal inversion pin remains one there as at B, so the partner remains true by monotonicity at that pin. Adding the relevant owner would again be invisible despite closing an edge. At least one such t exists by the disjoint domination table in cap16-low-partners.md. In particular a monotone H with none of these vertices essential cannot support that shared-guard witness.

The exceptional adjacent owners 1 and 2 cannot have the same unsafe partner even when it is downstream of the inverter. Starting at any independent input where their common partner is true, adding both owners preserves both already-true entry values and all other wires, but closes edge 12. More generally the set of all low owners with true partner at an independent input B can be added simultaneously while preserving every nonraw wire (induction in circuit order). Thus its union with B is independent.

One may choose a representative where all four actual partners have independent witnesses. Use physical size minimality at sixteen, and among reduced representatives minimize the number of ORs with a directed path to an AND or NOT. Every low entry OR has such a path because an OR-only path to output would accept its singleton. Replace an entry with a safe partner by its raw owner everywhere and append the partner to the old output using one OR. This is exact by the partner lemma. Any simplification below sixteen contradicts the size floor. No retained OR can gain a path to a non-OR site, and the new output OR has none. The lost entry strictly reduces the chosen integer. Therefore in the selected representative every actual partner is unsafe. Every low variable essential in H must enter through an OR upstream of the inverter; its actual partner is then its zero-clamped partner. If l low variables are essential in H, the selected representative consequently satisfies l≤k and l+d≤5.

There is a complementary count for the remaining r=4−k actual unsafe partners. Call one of these owners guarded if its partner has no OR-only path from the inverter output (allowing a path of length zero). Let T be the guarded owners among the r. Along a true backwards OR trace on an independent witness for such an owner one must reach an actual positive AND. A raw stop would force that raw singleton and all extensions to activate the guard, contrary to the domination table; a NOT stop is excluded by definition. No such AND can be one of the k charged gates: the latter has an independent witness at zero clamp, which the shared OR path would transmit to this owner's supposedly safe zero-clamped guard. Nor can it be the output-trace AND, which has a physical OR-only path to the output and hence is safe on actual inputs as well. A single such AND cannot serve both adjacent owners 1,2: an independent witness for either traced site would activate both partners on the OR-only paths and violate independent simultaneous saturation. Thus if c(T) is zero for empty T, two when {1,2}⊆T, and one otherwise, the physical AND count is at least k+1+max(d,c(T)). The maximum allows these new sites to be among the d zero-clamped dark ones. The actual AND on the trace of any such owner must also have an inversion ancestor: otherwise its independent witness would still activate the zero-clamped guard.

Finally an OR-only path from the inversion to a partner P_i entails H({t})=1 for every t adjacent to i. The inverter then precedes the sole i entry, so H is independent of i. If H({t})=0 its inverse forces the partner true both before and after i is added to the singleton t, hiding the required edge. Thus owners with a neighbor at which H is singleton-cold are automatically guarded.

In fact at most two low partners can have an OR-only path from the inversion. Owners 1,2 cannot both have one, since H is false at the empty input and simultaneous saturation would add edge 12. For the only possible triples {0,1,3} and {0,2,3}, H accepts every singleton of {2,4,5,6,8} and {1,4,5,7,8}, respectively, by the neighbor rule. Therefore every edge except respectively 79 and 69 has H=1 and is already accepted by F0. In the first case replace the physical inverter by a single AND computing edge 79; in the second use edge 69. This gives a monotone circuit of at most sixteen gates. Indeed every target edge either was accepted at z=0 or sets the replacement to one (and F1 accepts every target edge); every independent input sets the replacement to zero and is rejected by F0. Monotonicity extends the edge test to all positives. The independently established monotone size-seventeen floor excludes both triples.
