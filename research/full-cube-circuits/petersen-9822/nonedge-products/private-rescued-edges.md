# Missing edges after clamping a private-pair inverter

Consider a 17-gate circuit for the full graph function in L-meta-9822 with seven outer AND gates, one private OR H=s∨t feeding the unique inverter, and eight outer ORs. Assume the established unconditional ten OR-or-NOT bound, every vertex-deleted graph needs at least fourteen monotone gates, and the zero clamp K0 of the inverter eliminates at least one outer OR (follow a path from the inverter through ANDs to its first OR; the nonzero clamp must dispose of such zero signals). Thus K0 costs at most fourteen gates including at most seven ORs. K0 is monotone and K0≤F pointwise, and equals F wherever H is true.

Let B comprise those original graph edges whose two-element assignment K0 rejects. Adding a product and collector OR for each edge of B restores F on the full cube: all added terms are genuine edges and every positive input contains an edge. Thus |B|≥3, as otherwise the restored circuit has at most nine OR and no NOT, contrary to the ten-paid theorem. No cardinality restriction on the added ANDs is necessary.

In fact B cannot be contained in a star. Otherwise it is exactly the three edges incident to a vertex v, by the degree bound. Pinning v to zero in K0 gives the exact graph with v deleted. All its positive edges already pass K0 and K0 never accepts a graph-negative assignment. The fourteen-gate lower bound forces v to have no live reader in K0, since even one such reader would fold upon its zero pin. Hence K0 is independent of v. If either private hot singleton s equals v, then F agrees with K0 on every incident edge, impossible. For any hot singleton s and any w adjacent to v, first s cannot itself neighbor v since F(s,v)=K0(s,v)=K0(s)=0. Now F(s,v,w)=1 equals K0(s,w), so sw is an edge. Two distinct neighbors of v therefore lie on a four-cycle with s and v, contrary to girth five.

Consequently every candidate under these hypotheses has at least three missing edges and they have no common endpoint. This is a restriction on the private-pair inverter sector, not a full size-seventeen exclusion.
