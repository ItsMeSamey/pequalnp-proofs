# Additional credits in the zero-clamped edge repair

Let J be any nonempty target subgraph containing all edges missed by the clamped output F0. Write c for a binary monotone construction cost of J, d for the number of inverter ancestors used solely through the inverter, and f for the number of its distinct direct binary users. The standard repair outputs F0 OR J, costing at most 15−d−f+c+1 binary sites. The following test gives additional separate credits.

Mark any low entry g_i=x_i OR P_i whose zero-clamped guard is false on every target edge outside J that avoids i. Replace all marked entries by their owner inputs before reducing the clamped graph. For an edge outside J the values of all sites are preserved by induction: at every marked entry either x_i=1 or the original clamped partner is zero. Every edge in J is accepted by the appended repair. On a negative input, deleting positive guard inputs can only decrease F0. The resulting graph is monotone, so the edge calculation proves correctness throughout the cube. In the size count, add a credit for every marked entry distinct from the f direct users and the d private sites. These gates can all be removed as syntactic operations on the initial graph, so no cascade can double-count two charged sites.

For naked owner pair {0,1}, take J with edges 38 and 79, of cost three. For {2,3}, take J with edges 05 and 69, also of cost three. Every missed edge avoids both owners' hot neighborhoods, so these J suffice. Either pair is impossible when d+f+the number of additional marked entries is at least three. For naked pairs {0,2} and {1,3}, the respective residuals are three-edge paths 3-8-6-9 and 0-5-7-9. Each has a four-gate construction b(a∨c)∨cd for path a-b-c-d. A credit total of four excludes these pairs. For pair {0,3}, the residual is cycle 1-2-7-9-6-1 and seven credits suffice by the three-star construction. These criteria use the monotone size-seventeen floor. They leave sectors with fewer credits open.

In the canonical representation minimizing the number of OR gates with some downstream AND or NOT (cap16-clamp-allocation.md), two naked owners require f≥2. Suppose f=1. The unique physical inversion reader R must be an OR z∨Q used on both OR-only paths to the low entries. Its other operand Q is independent of the inverted wire by uniqueness of its reader and acyclicity, hence Q is a monotone raw function. If R is itself one low entry, the path to the other partner makes the first owner's raw an OR-only contributor there, impossible since no singleton can be a monotone guard witness. Otherwise R lies on the paths to both partners, so Q precedes both entries and is independent of both owners. The low-partner lemma gives Q≤F. Replace R by z everywhere and append Q by OR to the output. This is exact: if Q is false the signals coincide, and if Q is true the appended disjunct enforces the correct value. The removed OR was counted in the canonical potential since either low entry has a downstream non-OR gate, whereas no old OR acquires such a path. This contradicts the choice of representative. In particular the two pairs with three-gate repairs require d=0, f=2 and no additional guard credit.

With the independent thirteen-site positive-pin floors at 1 and 2 and their singleton-cold-H corollary from reductions, neither owner 1 nor owner 2 can be naked. Hence any two-naked configuration uses owners 0,3, and the possible missed edges are restricted to the cycle 1-2-7-9-6-1. If H accepted two of the cycle's singletons, removing those two vertices would leave at most two incident edges. Their repair costs at most two gates, already paid by f≥2. Thus H accepts at most one cycle singleton. If it accepts one, the missed edges lie on the remaining three-edge path; they must include the two disjoint end edges, since any two incident edges cost only two. In this case d+f≤3 since the path has a four-gate construction. If the middle edge was already accepted by F0 the two end edges alone cost three, hence d=0 and f=2. These are restrictions on the remaining pair, not its complete exclusion.
