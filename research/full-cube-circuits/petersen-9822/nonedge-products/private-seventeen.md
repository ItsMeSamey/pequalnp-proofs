# Limits on the private inverter prefix at total size seventeen

Assume sixteen binary sites, one inverter and a live nonconstant monotone mask H feeding it. Remove the d binary ancestors used only through the inverter and view the inverted output as a formal input z of the monotone outer circuit. Each of the ten raws must still occur: if its only remaining path were through the original inverter, the actual output would be antitone in that essential increasing variable. The outer circuit has at least five ANDs. Here is the saturation proof. Resolve its ANDs to chosen operands, giving an upper-bounding disjunction of raw literals and possibly z. For each graph maximal independent set M pick zero operands at all ANDs evaluating to zero on that input with the actual z. The resolved disjunction rejects M; adding any raw outside M makes the actual output positive, while the actual z cannot increase. The raw support of this disjunction is therefore exactly the complement of M. There are sixteen distinct such complements. Four outer ANDs would require every direction to yield one of these exact supports; on clamping z to zero the conjunction of all the resolved disjunctions would then compute the target. The direction representation is exact for a monotone DAG by choosing a common zero operand at each zero gate for each negative input. This would give a monotone circuit of size at most sixteen, impossible.

In particular the outer circuit has at most 11−d ORs. Pinning z to zero loses at least one OR: follow a live outgoing path until the first OR, with preceding ANDs forced zero. An AND-only path to the output would make F0 identically zero, impossible on the all-ones raw input where H=1. Thus F0 has at most 10−d ORs. For any singleton s accepted by H the positive cofactor at s=1 is computed by the same zero-clamped circuit. For s=0,1,2,3,4 its OR count is at least eight by the positive-cofactor induced-cycle bounds in paid-first-positive.md. For s=5,6,7,8 the remaining graph after deleting the closed neighborhood is a six-vertex path, so its five edges require four paid sites by cycle-paid-floor.md. Reattaching the three singleton neighbor disjuncts requires three more ORs (each essential singleton raw eliminated at zero costs an OR along its first AND-only path). For s=9 the residual is the disjoint union of a four-vertex path and an edge, with four edges total, giving six ORs including the three singleton disjuncts. Hence d≤2 at a hot singleton among 0,…,4, d≤3 among 5,…,8 and d≤4 at 9.

For a complementary total-size bound the undirected operand multigraph of the outer circuit is connected and has cycle rank (16−d)−11+1=6−d. Fix z=0 and a hot raw s=1. Constant folding and discarding unused nodes cannot increase cycle rank: delete fixed operands and contract bypassed operands, deleting the forced constant subgraphs. Sharing is preserved and parallel operand slots count separately. The positive cofactor on nine essential raws (every other vertex stays essential with s true by minimum degree two and absence of four-cycles) therefore has cycle rank at most 6−d. A binary circuit of size at least L on these inputs has rank at least L−9+1. In particular the positive floor thirteen at 1,2 strengthens d≤1 there.

If all ancestors of H are private and H differs from the OR of its s accepted singleton raws, it has a positive minterm disjoint from these raws of size at least two. Its private binary circuit must connect at least s+2 distinct essential inputs, so d≥s+1. For s≥3 some accepted singleton differs from 9 and the upper bound is at most three, impossible. For s=2 both accepted singletons must be among 5,…,9, and d=3. The private circuit has only four distinct essential inputs, so the only nonlinear term outside the singleton disjunction is the conjunction of the two other raws. In this remaining case the outer circuit has exactly five ANDs and eight ORs: some hot singleton among 5,…,8 requires seven ORs even after the zero clamp, and the outer circuit has thirteen binary gates. No four-low sole-reader assumption enters these bounds.

Private H has at most three accepted singletons even for a pure disjunction. At five or more, d≥s−1≥4 conflicts with the upper bound at some hot singleton different from 9. At four, d≥3 forces all four to lie in {5,6,7,8,9} and d=3. These five high vertices induce the cycle (5,7,9,6,8). With four hot vertices all its edges are accepted by F0. Each hot vertex also has a distinct matched outside vertex, along 5-0, 7-2, 9-4, 6-1, 8-3. Remove the unmatched outside vertex, then pin the four matched outside vertices zero one at a time. Each is still essential by its matched edge versus the high singleton alone. The protected cycle keeps the output nonconstant; therefore each zero pin deletes an OR. At the end F0 is exactly the five-cycle by its accepted edges and F0≤F, requiring four further ORs. Eight ORs contradict the upper bound 10−d=7. Consequently three hot singletons are possible for a private prefix only when H equals their disjunction.
