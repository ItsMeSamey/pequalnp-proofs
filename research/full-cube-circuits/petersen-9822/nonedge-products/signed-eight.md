# Ten paid OR/NOT gates in the graph of L-meta-9822

Count each binary OR and unary NOT as one paid gate and allow any number of shared binary AND gates. Consider H obtained by deleting 1 and 2 from the desk graph. Its ten edges are 34,04,05,38,49,57,79,69,68,58. H is connected, has minimum degree two, and is C4-free. Its vertices in increasing order are 0,3,4,5,6,7,8,9. For every v, H−v is connected and the numbers of induced five-cycles in row order are 2,2,1,1,2,2,1,1. These finite facts are checked by signed-eight-facts.py.

We use the established full-cube lower of four paid gates for C5 (c5final.cpp and c5final.log). If an essential raw x is set false and the cofactor is nonzero, every path from x to the output meets a first paid gate: an AND-only path would force the output zero. Each distinct first site physically folds because at least one of its operands is zero. H−v has paid cost ≥6: delete the two vertices outside a surviving C5 one at a time, with a neighbor still present, in reverse order of growing the connected graph from that cycle. Thus H has paid cost ≥7; under equality every raw has a unique first site.

For each v the positive cofactor has paid cost at least five. The degree-two vertices leave two independent singleton disjuncts and a P5 edge test on the nonneighbors. The degree-three vertices leave three and P4. Pinning each fresh disjunct false loses a paid gate by the path argument. Deleting pendant vertices from P5 or P4 until a single edge survives loses three or two more paid gates respectively. This proves the required positive lower bounds.

Suppose H has a seven-paid circuit. Minimize paid count, then total gates; fold constants and remove dead gates. Call a paid gate a source inverter if all its raw ancestors comprise one vertex x and it computes NOT x. It may be normalized to a bare direct NOT x. Repeated source inverters on x would be redundant. The inverter is x's only first paid site. From its output z=NOT x to the final output, every path also has a first subsequent paid site: an AND-only path would force H identically zero under x=true. Pin x=true; the inverter plus each distinct such next site folds, because z=0 propagates along every AND-only prefix. The lower bound five for the positive cofactor leaves room for at most two paid losses. Thus each inverter has a unique next paid site.

Take the earliest paid gate R that is not a source inverter. All its earlier paid ancestors are bare source inverters. Each raw entering its AND-only predecessor cones directly has R as unique first paid site; each source inverter entering those cones has R as its unique next site and is the unique first site for its original raw. No constituent raw can occur both fresh and inverted: that would give it two distinct first sites. Neither type has an AND-only bypass to the output by the preceding nonzero cofactor arguments. It follows that every raw in R's ancestor set S reaches the output only through R. R itself only depends on S; hence as S varies, there can be at most two functions of the outside inputs.

No proper S of size at least two has this property. If S contains an edge, connectedness gives a cross-boundary vertex s and neighbor t outside S. The assignments on S consisting of the empty set, singleton s, and an internal edge induce three distinct outside functions: all outside zero distinguishes the edge, and t alone distinguishes the others. If S is independent, every s has a nonempty outside neighborhood and no two s have the same neighborhood (otherwise minimum degree two furnishes a C4). The empty assignment and any two distinct singleton assignments give three functions, separated on outside singletons.

If S is the whole graph, all outside circuitry after R can compute at most a unary function of R. The gate R is an OR of two conjunctions or a NOT of one conjunction. Each original raw occurs in just one polarity in these conjunctions. Monotonicity and essentiality force consistent polarity on all variables after accounting for any overall output complement. Thus either H or its Boolean dual would have at most two minimal true sets. H has ten edges; its dual has nine minimal vertex covers, tabulated by the checker. This is impossible. If S consists of one variable, R is a constant, raw alias, or inverter; constants and aliases simplify and a new inverter would be a source. An empty S is constant.

Finally, if all paid gates are source inverters, the output is a single conjunction of signed raws (or a single wire), which cannot equal H. This exhausts the seven-paid case. Therefore H needs at least eight paid gates. Pinning 1 then 2 false removes at least one paid gate each: they remain essential at their respective steps and H survives. Every unrestricted circuit computing the ten-vertex function accordingly has at least ten OR/NOT gates. At total size ≤17 it has at most seven AND gates. This is a component lower bound and does not settle the exact unrestricted size.
