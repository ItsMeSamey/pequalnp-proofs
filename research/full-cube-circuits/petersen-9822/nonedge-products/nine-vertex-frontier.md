# Two nine-vertex cofactors need nine paid sites with up to four NOTs

The permutation (0 3)(1 2)(5 8)(6 7) preserves graph 9822. Consider H=G−0. Its Hamiltonian cycle is 6,1,2,7,5,8,3,4,9,6, with disjoint chords 7–9 and 6–8. Any vertex deletion preserves connectedness and at least one chord, hence leaves a connected eight-vertex graph containing an induced cycle of length at least five. Its OR-or-NOT cost is at least seven by cycle-paid-floor.md.

Assume H has a circuit with at most eight OR-or-NOT sites and at most four NOTs. Choose such a circuit minimizing the NOT count. Each zero pin loses one paid site and leaves a seven-site lower bound. The budget is therefore exactly eight, and every raw has a unique first paid site on AND-only paths. H is connected with minimum degree two and no four-cycles. The fresh-set argument of four-inversions-paid.md applies: each OR owns at most two fresh raws (a pair must be independent and assigned to separate arms), each NOT at most one, and any earliest paid site must be a bare raw inversion.

For a bare source inverter at y, fixing y=true loses both the inverter and at least one next paid site, since the positive cofactor is nonzero. The cofactor consists of its neighbors as singleton disjuncts plus H−N[y]. Directly from the Hamiltonian cycle and chords, for y=1,2,3,4 the degree is two and the residual is a connected unicyclic six-vertex graph; its girth is at least five. For y=5, the degree is two and the residual a six-vertex tree. For y=7,8 the degree is three and the residual a five-vertex tree. By removing the singleton disjuncts and using cycle-paid-floor.md the respective lower bounds are seven, six and six. Hence all bare sources belong to B={5,6,7,8,9}. Those at 7 or 8 have a unique next paid site reached through AND-only paths, since the budget allows only two losses on their positive pins. In H the only vertex all of whose neighbors belong to B is 5, with neighbors 7,8.

In an initial run of up to three NOTs, all are bare and own distinct raw labels. If a fresh y inverter used an earlier bare signal ¬x in its conjunction, y would be invisible upon x=true, but every distinct y remains essential there (for nonneighbors, find a neighbor of y outside N(x) by absence of four-cycles and minimum degree two). A nonfresh NOT among the first three inverts at most two earlier bare signals conjoined; their positive disjunction costs at most one paid site and reduces the NOT count.

Consider an OR with no preceding OR and at most three earlier paid sites. If it owns no fresh raw it combines two conjunctions of the earlier signals. After ordinary absorptions a singleton term causes two paid losses when the corresponding source raw is set to zero. Otherwise both incomparable terms are two-element sets sharing a common factor, and reassociation gives the same contradiction by pinning one signal in the inner OR.

With two fresh raws u,v, both coefficients are true when all previous source labels are zero. Thus every exclusive neighbor of u or v must be among the earlier sources: on any exterior exclusive singleton the output section must be u alone or v alone, not a unary function of u∨v. There is an exclusive neighbor of each since at most one is common. A later common neighbor c is also impossible: for an earlier exclusive neighbor a of u, the settings {a} and {a,c} are independent (triangle-free), the required sections are u and u∨v, and the OR has the same section on both. Hence both fresh vertices would have all neighbors in B, impossible since only one vertex has that property.

With one fresh v, if both arms contain v then pinning v=0 loses this OR and a downstream paid site. Otherwise the raw-free arm is one whenever all previous source labels are zero, so v is invisible then. Thus v=5 and both 7,8 must be earlier sources. The raw-free arm must contain both ¬7 and ¬8: omitting one would hide v on the singleton of that neighbor. Therefore each of those inverter outputs has this OR as its unique next paid site. All influence of the group {5,7,8} goes through this one OR, which depends only on that group after fixing every other earlier source raw to zero. On the remaining exterior, vertices 2 and 3 are free. Three distinct group cofactors occur: empty gives the exterior edge predicate, singleton 7 adds the exterior neighbor 2, and the internal edge 5–7 gives constant one. A single bit depending only on the group cannot provide those three cofactors.

There is consequently no OR in the first four paid sites. A fourth NOT would be the last one, and cannot own a fresh raw: its only influence would be antitone. Its output would be the OR of a subset of the three earlier bare raw labels. Omitting one leaves that source antitone. Including all three lets us set one source true, making the last NOT constant while another source, still essential, remains antitone. This exhausts the alternatives.

Thus deleting 0 or 3 from the original graph still requires nine paid sites in any circuit with at most four NOTs. The thirteen-binary lower bound grants that NOT cap in every circuit of total size at most seventeen and in its restrictions. Consequently any such circuit with exactly ten paid sites has a unique first paid site for each of vertices 0 and 3. Otherwise a zero pin loses two sites, contradicting the cofactor lower bound. This applies to the seven-AND, seventeen-gate sector without assuming uniqueness at any other input.
