# All charged modes need eight OR-or-NOT gates after deleting vertices one and two

Here λ is the minimum number of physical binary ORs and unary NOTs, with binary ANDs free. Consider graph K=G−{1,2}. Its Hamiltonian eight-cycle is 0,4,3,8,6,9,7,5,0, with the two disjoint chords 4–9 and 5–8. This connected graph has minimum degree two and no four-cycle.

For any raw x the zero cofactor K−x is connected on seven vertices and still contains a cycle: deletion leaves a spanning path and at least one surviving chord. Its paid cost is at least six by cycle-paid-floor.md. The positive cofactor is the disjunction of the neighbors of x with the graph on K−N[x]. For the degree-two vertices 0,3,6,7 this residual is a five-vertex tree; for the degree-three vertices 4,5,8,9 it is a four-vertex tree, as is checked from the cycle and the two chords above. Its exact cost including singleton disjuncts is five in both cases: a tree with m≥1 edges costs m−1 OR-or-NOT gates, and sequentially erasing and then reattaching the singleton disjuncts proves their exact additive cost.

Suppose λ(K)≤7. A zero pin of any raw loses at least one paid gate, since a path through only ANDs to output would make the zero cofactor identically zero. Thus λ=7, and every raw reaches exactly one first paid site along paths whose internal nodes are all AND. If there were two, both would fold against the six-site lower bound for the zero cofactor. Similarly the output of any bare source inverter ¬x has exactly one next paid site along AND-only paths. On x=true the inverter and all those next sites would fold; the positive cofactor is nonzero and requires five sites, so exactly one next site is possible.

Reduce redundant unary operations. Inspect the earliest paid gate h that is not just the inversion of a raw. Each earlier paid signal is a bare source inverter. The AND-only children of h therefore see only a set of fresh raws and some earlier bare signals. Every fresh raw influences the rest exclusively through h by its unique first frontier. Every raw whose inverted source enters h also influences the rest exclusively through h: it has the bare inverter as its unique first charged site, and the inverter has h as its unique next charged site. There are no raw-to-output or inverter-to-output routes using only ANDs, by the nonzero corresponding cofactors. Consequently every input in the combined set S reaches the rest only through h, and h depends only on S.

A proper set S of size at least two cannot have this property. If it contains an edge, settings on S giving the empty set, one boundary singleton, or an internal edge give three distinct functions on the exterior. The first rejects each exterior singleton, the second accepts some exterior neighbor while rejecting the empty assignment, and the third is constant one. If S is independent, two of its vertices have nonempty distinct exterior neighborhoods by minimum degree two and absence of four-cycles. Their singleton settings and the empty group also give three functions. One bit depending on S allows at most two. A singleton S at h gives a constant, a raw already available, or a bare inverter, contrary to the choice of h in a reduced circuit.

If S is the entire input set, some raw must be fresh at h since at most six paid sites precede it. If a source-negated raw also enters, the gate is unate in opposite directions on that prior raw and the fresh raw, as its arguments are AND-only conjunctions and h either preserves or reverses both signs. A unary function of it cannot be positively essential in all raw variables. A raw cannot enter both fresh and through its negation because the first charged frontier is unique. Finally, if all eight inputs were fresh then h would be a NOT of one raw conjunction or an OR of at most two raw conjunctions. Neither it nor its complement computes the monotone ten-edge predicate. The case where all seven paid sites are bare inverters is also impossible: one of eight essential inputs would have no paid frontier. This proves λ(K)≥8, allowing any number of NOTs.

Return to G−1 and G−2. Deleting the remaining raw of the pair loses at least one paid site since it is essential and K is nonzero. Thus both nine-input graphs have unrestricted λ≥9. In a full circuit with ten paid sites each of raws 1 and 2 has exactly one first paid site, even without a bound on the number of physical inversions. This conclusion applies to the seventeen-gate, seven-AND composition.
