# The OR and NOT cost of every cycle of length at least five

In a circuit over binary AND and OR and unary NOT, write λ for the minimum number of OR and NOT gates together. AND gates have zero cost. Pinning an essential variable v false loses one paid site whenever the remaining target is nonzero: a path starting from v must meet a paid site, since an AND-only path to the output would force the pinned output to zero. The first paid site on any such path simplifies when its input is forced to zero along the AND prefix.

For a path on k≥2 vertices, successively delete leaves down to a single edge. This gives λ≥k−2. Assign edges to incident star centers, construct each star, and collect the stars. Any such assignment with m edges and t centers needs (m−t)+(t−1)=m−1 ORs. Thus λ(path)=k−2. The same argument for any nonempty forest gives λ=m−1: delete leaves, skipping isolated vertices, until just one edge remains (each deletion loses one edge), and use the same upper construction.

For the cycle on n≥5 vertices, deleting any one input leaves the path on n−1 vertices. Hence λ≥n−2. Suppose equality. Every raw then has one unique first paid site on its paths through only ANDs: two such sites would both fold against the path lower bound. No all-AND path reaches the output. At an earliest paid gate let S be the raws feeding its AND-only cones. All effect of S on the output goes through this one gate, which depends only on S; hence there are at most two distinct cofactor functions on the exterior when S is fixed. For a proper S containing an edge there are at least three: the empty set gives the exterior edge function, an internal edge gives constant one, and a boundary singleton adds its exterior neighbors while still rejecting the empty exterior input. For independent S with at least two members, each singleton has different nonempty exterior neighborhood since two identical degree-two neighborhoods would create a four-cycle. Empty and two different singleton settings again give at least three cofactors. If S is the entire cycle, the output would be unary in a NOT of one raw conjunction or an OR of at most two conjunctions, whereas the cycle has n distinct minimal pair positives. A nonconstant decreasing complement of an increasing function cannot compute this target. It follows that an irredundant earliest paid gate is a NOT of one raw x.

Set x=true. The inverter becomes zero. A path from its output to the target reaches another paid site after only ANDs because the positive cofactor is nonzero, so at least two paid gates disappear. But the positive cofactor is the disjunction of two raw neighbors and the residual path on n−3 vertices. Erasing those two disjuncts leaves that path and incurs two paid losses by the same rule. Its cost is thus at least 2+(n−5)=n−3, contradicting the remaining budget n−4. Therefore λ(cycle)=n−1 by the matching m−1 star construction.

In any connected graph with an induced cycle of length k≥5, delete vertices outside that cycle in decreasing distance along a spanning tree rooted at the cycle, preserving an essential vertex and a nonzero residual at each step. This gives λ≥(n−k)+(k−1)=n−1. For a connected unicyclic graph whose cycle has length at least five, the graph has n edges and the star construction attains n−1. In particular this proves the five- and six-cycle floors used for 9822 without an enumerative assumption.
