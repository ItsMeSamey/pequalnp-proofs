# Nonadjacent public literal guard without a signed single-deletion prior

Assume a reduced minimum 17-site 9822 circuit with seven ANDs, nine ORs and one NOT. The input to the inverter is the literal gate H=s OR t for a distinct nonadjacent pair 56, 67 or 78; H has a positive use. Write z=NOT H. The checked physical contact theorem gives a single direct reader r of z. The inverter cannot itself be output, since its value is one on all-zero input; every effective use is through r. The double-zero monotone restriction needs six ORs and four ANDs. Consequently no more than three OR sites and no more than three AND sites can fold. This counts individual physical sites even if a signal has several consumers.

The hot-OR bound makes every ordinary direct reader of H and each extra raw s or t reader an AND. There exist distinct ANDs U_s,U_t reading their respective owners, because the graph is essential in either owner with the other true, and cannot be symmetric under their interchange. See PairDistinctOwners9822. Choose a positive AND reader W of H. It differs from both raw ANDs: an AND reading H and an owner computes that owner and is redundant. Pinning s=t=0 folds all three sites. If r is an AND, it folds too, and cannot coincide with W or either raw AND (its other operand would be H or a raw forcing H, and the gate would be constantly zero). This contradicts the four-AND residual floor.

Suppose therefore r is an OR, with its other operand D. The reduced binary gate has the form z OR D; a duplicated operand z gives a trivial alias. On the pair00 face, r is constant one. If another OR T directly reads r, T is also forced one, and H,r,T are three distinct folded OR sites. The face has a positive graph transition from 00 to 10 at frozen exterior (choose a neighbor of s with no exterior edge). Take a path of strictly rising wires from raw s to output along this transition. All gates except the inverter are monotone, so such a path exists by backtracking each rising gate to a rising operand. The inverter falls and cannot occur. Neither r nor T can rise from one. If the path traverses H, begin tracing it after H; otherwise trace it from s. Some subsequent OR must occur, since an uninterrupted AND-only path from either source to output would make the entire 00 face constantly zero. Its first OR is distinct from H,r,T and folds too: its incoming operand is forced zero by the traced AND path under the double pin, independently of the exterior. Four distinct OR losses violate the six-OR lower bound. Thus r has no OR consumer.

The only NOT in the circuit reads H, and r is downstream of that NOT. Every consumer of r is therefore an AND. Each of these sites folds because one operand is forced true. The threefold AND budget is already spent on U_s,U_t,W. Each consumer thus belongs to this set and has as its other operand one of s,t,H. On any input on which that other operand is true, z is false and r=D. Otherwise the AND is zero. Replace the r operand at every consumer by D. These rewrites preserve every consumer's truth value on every input; D precedes r. The output is not r, since on all-zero input r is true. Removing the now-dead r saves a gate, contradicting minimality. This proof is scoped to public H; it uses no signed single-vertex deletion floor.
