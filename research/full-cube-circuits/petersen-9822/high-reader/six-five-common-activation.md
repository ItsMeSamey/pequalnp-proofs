# Five-direct closure on the three final common-activation profiles

Use the exact six-switch direction model, six reverse joins, five direct raw labels on distinct axes, and the sixteen maximal independent witness patterns of the ten-vertex graph. These witnesses make all ten raw receiver masks nonempty and pairwise incomparable. A direct receiver is one branch of its axis; a nondirect receiver needs a reverse union. The target has independence number four, minimum degree two, maximum degree three, and no four-cycle. The live outer circuit has at most sixteen binary gates. Thus K0 must miss some target edge, since otherwise K0=F and clamping the live inverter input deletes at least one binary gate, below the known gate floor of sixteen.

In archive one-not-six-cert/six-all.txt.gz the three residual d5 IDs 41208, 41224, 41701 have respectively (k,m,e)=(3,3,0),(3,2,1),(4,2,0). The m conditionally reached switches have the same activation A, the disjunction of one specified ordinary literal on each of k always-active switches. Another e always-active switches do not occur in A. An ordinary literal opposing any term of A is called outward. The k-literal corner falsifying A leaves all m conditional branches silent and leaves the e other ordinary axes free.

There is at least one directly labeled conditional switch. Hence A properly contains a direct raw receiver. A is neither raw nor primitive, so the sole nonraw reverse join must compute it. Each other join produces one of five nondirect raws. No raw can use a different raw, by incomparability, and no raw can use A, because it contains a direct raw. Consequently each nondirect raw is the union of two primitive branches, neither chosen as direct. Before A is available it has to be built from two sources each either a primitive or an earlier nondirect raw (ordinary literal pair). No conditional branch of a switch with activation A can have been available to construct A. Since A is a proper disjunction of k independent ordinary literals, every ordinary operand literal used to construct it is an A literal and all k must occur. For k=4, two distinct preceding raw pair masks partition those four signs; no A literal can be chosen as a direct. For k=3 at least one earlier raw pair X lies inside A, and at most one A literal is direct.

The five direct raws D are jointly omittable by falsifying their chosen signed bits. D contains an edge, hence K0(D)=0 forces H(D)=0, and K1 agrees with F throughout D. The inverter receiver Z cannot be empty (live) or full (H(empty)=0, F(empty)=0). It is primitive, a raw, or A. For Z=A, adding it absorbs every conditional branch. If a missed edge is rescued, its union with Z covers the cube, although none of its three two-label subunions do. An ordinary literal opposite A would already rescue a singleton; without one the two raws must already contain opposite ordinary literals. This excludes Z=A.

For Z primitive or a raw, consider any edge in D. Its two directs and Z must cover the direction cube; no two alone cover (the edge is missed, and each singleton is negative at K1). There is therefore no ordinary complementary pair in their collective rays. If no complete pair of conditional signs is present, choose a direction falsifying all ordinary literals and avoiding the individually present conditional signs, using independently selectable conditional axis bits. Consequently a conditional pair must be present. No two direct labels can supply it; Z cannot supply both signs on an axis since their union would be A, impossible for either a primitive or a raw. Thus some conditional direct t has its opposite branch in Z. Since t and Z together already cover A but cannot cover the full cube, Z contains no outward literal. Every outward ordinary direct O is adjacent to t because t, Z, O cover and H(D)=0.

Suppose there are at least two such distinct outward ordinary directs. Every raw receiver v containing an outward literal must neighbor t. If not, t,v,Z already cover and H(tv)=1; for each outward direct O, positive tvO must therefore be accepted by K0. In the nonedge case v differs from every such O. Its only possible second primitive cannot be opposite t: that would already make K0(tv)=1. Thus t,v,O lack any complete pair of hidden signs. They can cover only via an ordinary conflict between v and O, since v is not full and t has no ordinary literal. It follows v neighbors all such O, contradicting the absence of a four-cycle. Thus at most three raw receivers can carry outward literals.

First consider Z contained in A. At any A=0 direction at least six raw vertices must be present since Z is absent and any omitted positive edge would violate upper correctness. With e=0, only raws carrying outward literals can be present. With e=1, total hits over the two e signs are at least twelve. A raw without any outward literal can hit on at most one sign, since containing both signs of the extra full axis would make it full. If a raws are contained in A, total hits are at most 10−a plus the number of outward raws. Here a≥2 by the conditional direct and earlier nondirect pair. Both e cases contradict the bound of three whenever two outward directs exist.

For fewer than two outward directs, k=4 is impossible by the four-literal construction (at most one unused axis). For 41208 there must be one omitted A axis, one inward direct on another A axis, and all three conditional axes direct. These three, the inward direct and the preceding nondirect raw pair are five distinct raws omitted at A=0. For 41224 the same supposition requires an omitted A axis, an inward A direct, both conditionals direct and the extra ordinary axis E direct. At A=0 also falsify the E direct. The two conditional directs, inward direct, preceding raw pair and E direct give five omissions. This completes the case Z contained in A, including every primitive alternative.

Otherwise Z must equal a nondirect raw receiver j. In addition to its conditional sign opposite t it has a second literal that escapes A. This must be an ordinary sign, and cannot be outward; hence it is a sign of an extra ordinary axis E. It is not on a directly labeled axis: equality to a chosen sign would put that direct receiver inside j; opposition to a chosen sign would make K1 accept the corresponding singleton, contrary to H(D)=0. Only profile 41224 remains, with E the unused axis, both conditional axes direct, and all three A axes direct.

Since Z equals raw receiver j, enabling Z is equivalent to adding raw j to a K0 input. Once j is actually present, its own receiver makes z irrelevant. Therefore K1(X)=K0(X∪{j})=F(X∪{j}) for all X. Each neighbor of j is H-hot as a singleton. No neighbor is in D, by H(D)=0.

On A=0 choose E's sign to suppress j. The two conditional directs, the preceding nondirect pair X inside A, and j are four distinct omitted vertices. A fifth is impossible. Thus no inward A-literal is direct, and the remaining three nondirect raws all hit this corner. Each must contain the chosen E literal: all three outward ordinary primitives are already direct and cannot be used by a nondirect raw. On the opposite E sign at A=0 those three receivers all vanish, since neither can use a direct outward sign nor can any contain both E signs. The omitted set S there includes both conditional directs, X, and these three nondirect raws, so contains an edge. K0(S)=0 implies H(S)=0. But S contains every nondirect raw except j, hence every neighbor of j, contradicting its singleton guard heat. This proves exclusion on all three residual d5 profiles. The earlier reduction to these profiles remains a separate archive premise.
