# Six outer ANDs and one conditional switch are impossible at size 17

Use the arbitrary-guard cut and the role theorem in single-conditional-outer.md. Assume one effective inverter, at most sixteen binary gates, exactly six ANDs in its live outer cone, and exactly one conditionally reached outer AND in the reverse direction cube. Thus the outer has at most ten ORs. A live input z must reach an OR by traversing zero or more ANDs: an AND-only path from z all the way to the output would make K0 identically false, whereas the all-true input has H=1 and actual F=1. Under z=0 the first such OR folds. Hence K0 has a monotone implementation with at most nine ORs. The all-true assertion follows because monotone H is nonconstant; an ineffective or constant inverter can instead be simplified.

There is a missed edge, since otherwise K0=F and deleting any direct z reader would leave at most fifteen gates (full signed lower bound sixteen). All missed edges share a center c by the cited role theorem. Every edge not incident to c is already present in K0, and K0≤F everywhere. Hence on the whole c=0 face K0 computes G−c. The checked nine-OR lower bound on each of the ten single-vertex deletions is RawOwnerClamp9822.deletion_floor. If the nine-OR circuit for K0 depended on c, a live rising path would have to reach an OR before output, since the c=0 cofactor is nonzero. Pinning c=0 folds that OR, violating the deletion bound. Therefore K0 ignores c on every input, and equals the edge predicate of G−c. In particular every edge at c is missed. The target graph has minimum degree two and contains no four-cycle.

At every vertex w the guard is singleton-cold. This holds for c and N(c) since each lies on a missed edge. For w outside their union choose t in N(c) not adjacent to w, possible since distinct c,w cannot have two common neighbors. The positive set {c,t,w} has K0=0, so H({c,t,w})=0; monotonicity extends this to w. Consequently K1 rejects every singleton and the empty set.

Denote the roles of c, its neighbors and z by L,R,T respectively. Every t in N(c) has precisely role R because edge ct is missed. No raw w can have role T. Otherwise K0({c,t,w})=1 by the three-role product for each t in N(c). Since K0 ignores c, this requires w to neighbor every such t, impossible for a raw distinct from c in a four-cycle-free graph. The raw c itself has only role L.

Now K1 equals K0 OR the product of the two raw disjunctions for L and R. No ordinary conflict involving z can add an independent singleton or empty term since K1 rejects them; without raw role T, K0's three-role term is always zero. No raw has both L and R, by singleton rejection. If p has role L and t in N(c) is not adjacent to p, then K1({p,t})=1 and H({p,t})=1. But the superset {c,p,t} has F=1 and K0=0, impossible. Thus p neighbors every t in N(c); only p=c is possible. If w has role R and lies outside N(c), then K1({c,w})=1 despite cw being a nonedge, forcing H({c,w})=1. Adding a neighbor t of c not adjacent to w again gives F=1 and K0=0. Thus R=N(c) and L={c}. The preceding product now adds exactly the star of c; K1 computes F everywhere. Setting z=1 eliminates at least one binary reader of that essential source, giving a monotone circuit of at most fifteen gates, a contradiction.

The same accounting excludes a cone with at most ten outer ORs and at most sixteen outer binary gates, with no change to the single-conditional hypothesis. No restriction on the sharing, syntactic shape, or cost allocation of the monotone pre-inverter guard was used.
