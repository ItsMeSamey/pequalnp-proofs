# Arbitrary-fanout pentagon cut at signed binary size fifteen

Use the private-chain and third-fork premises for owners 1 and 2. The two distinct shared guards P1,P2 precede their raw entries. Each owner's first fork, if any, is distinct from both guards. Orient its fork Qi to be increasing in xi: along the unique path prefix up to that fork, side signals are independent of xi and the signs are fixed. Whenever xi is pivotal, Qi=xi at both settings of xi. Two owners cannot have the same first fork: up to their path merge at or before that fork the segments are single-use, and on any outside assignment where both owners are essential those segments must transmit with fixed signs. At the merge this gives the same two-variable signed truth table up to complement, conflicting with the AND and OR sections witnessed at outside zero and outside {6,7}. The same argument rules out both owners having no fork.

Write A={0,3,4,5,8}, inducing the cycle 0−4−3−8−5−0. Suppose first both owners have forks and let j be the one whose fork Qj is earlier. Both Qj and Pj are independent of xi, since all paths from xi to a shared wire must first cross Qi, and Pj precedes Qj. If only j has a fork, all shared wires are independent of the other owner i, giving the same two independences.

Pin j=true, i=false and 6,7,9 false. This leaves exactly the edge predicate on A. On every independent A-assignment, i is pivotal, so Pi=0 and, when present, Qi=0. At i=true, j is pivotal, because there is no outside neighbor of either owner in A. Thus Pj=0 and Qj=j=1 upon setting j true. Independence from i transports these last two equalities back to i=false. This supplies four distinct shared gates with known defaults on all negative C5 assignments if both forks exist; otherwise there are three.

Cut the outgoing wires of every such controlled shared gate, substituting its default with the original edge sign. Attach one extra disjunct for each controlled producer: its cut-circuit value differs from its default. Along with the cut original output this computes C5. All negatives agree with every default. On positives with some original deviation, the earliest original deviation has unchanged value in its cut computation; if there is no deviation the original output remains true. Each controlled producer now feeds its exceptional disjunct once. The appended OR gates have one downstream use each or are the output. Uncontrolled internal producers have no additional fanout.

For a DAG including the pinned constants, take as potential the number of gate pins reading an A raw plus the sum over internal producers of max(outdegree−1,0). Equivalently count the output as a distinguished single use for uniform accounting. The potential in the cut DAG is at most 5+s_A+u, where s_A is original raw surplus on A and u is original internal surplus at uncontrolled gates. Constant-folding and trimming do not increase this potential: bypassing a binary producer with d live uses to one predecessor may add at most d−1 raw uses or excess uses of that predecessor, paid by the erased producer's excess. Folding to a constant and removing dead gates do not add uses. In the resulting constant-free live signed circuit with b binary gates (nonliteral C5), the potential is exactly b+1 by counting its 2b operand pins. Hence b≤4+s_A+u. The signed C5 floor seven in Face true 668 7 of L_meta_9825 yields s_A+u≥3.

The original circuit has exactly six surplus units among all raw and internal wires. Four distinct controlled shared gates would consume at least four, leaving s_A+u≤2, contradiction. Exactly one owner therefore has a first internal fork, irrespective of total fork count. Its fork Q and both guards P1,P2 each have exactly two uses. Moreover all raw surplus lies on A, since these three controlled units use the other half of the total in the displayed inequality. The sole-reader protected vertices 0 and 3 are in A; thus the only possible multiply read raws are 4,5,8. The raw surplus on them plus all uncontrolled internal surplus equals three. This statement does not assume that the remaining units are all allocated to different raws or to raws at all.
