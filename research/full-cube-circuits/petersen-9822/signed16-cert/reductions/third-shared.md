# A size-fifteen signed solution would need three shared binary gates

Use the normalized signed binary model and the protected private chains for raw 1 and 2. Write Gi=xi OR Pi for the positive raw entries, with Pi denoting an appropriate permanently signed wire. The two underlying guards are distinct shared internal gates.

For each singly-used raw v, follow its unique path downstream until the first gate with multiple uses, or the output if no such gate is encountered. Every influence of v passes through that first site, whose signal, with any fixed output sign, is unate in v with a fixed polarity. Indeed each binary operation along the path is unate in its designated input and all companion inputs are independent of v: if one depended on v its path would have already branched. The signs compose along this unique path.

Raws with the same first site form an independent set in the 9822 graph. Two private paths meet no later than that site, giving a first common binary operation of fixed sign and fixed input signs. At any exterior assignment where both variables matter, the private chains transmit signed raw bits, and the remaining function is unary in the common operation, thus either that operation or its complement. AND and OR are not complements.

In particular owners 1 and 2 have distinct first sites. Neither can first reach its own guard, which precedes its raw reader. Neither can first reach the other guard. To see this suppose raw 1 first reaches P2. Every influence of x1 then factors through P2 and P2 is unate in x1 with one fixed polarity. P2 is also independent of x2 because it precedes x2's unique reader. Set all exterior raws to zero. The target section is AND, and x2 is pivotal at x1=1, forcing P2(1)=0. The section is essential in x1 so P2 must vary and equals NOT x1 on that exterior assignment. Next set precisely exterior raws 6 and 7 true. Consequently the two distinct first sites of 1 and 2 are in the set of all shared gates except P1,P2, together with the output. At least three distinct shared binary gates are necessary. Exactly one owner first reaches Q and the other first reaches the output; the latter raw influences none of the three shared wires. The former owner's own guard precedes Q through its private entry chain. These are necessary conditions only.
