# One shared binary node at fifteen binary gates

Assume the normalized signed fifteen-binary model, a sole shared internal binary wire H, at least two distinct uses of H, and that low raws 0,1,2,3 each have one reader. Cut H to obtain an inner signed formula and an exterior signed formula. Let S be the raws used only inside, U those used only outside, and T those used on both sides. Then F=J(H(S,T),T,U). Each T raw contributes at least one raw fanout excess. Fifteen binary sites, ten essential inputs and one output allow 15−10+1=6 total fanout excess, hence at most five raw excess. H has at least two inputs in S∪T: a single essential input computes a signed literal or constant, which could bypass the shared gate in this signed model. The inequalities concern occurrences, irrespective of later semantic cancellations.

At fixed T, the matrix indexed by S versus U has at most two distinct rows. Here is an equivalent graph test. If S has no cross edge into U, the requirement holds. Otherwise S must be independent; all nonempty neighborhoods from S into U must equal one set K. Let A be the S vertices with this neighborhood. For each t in T having a neighbor in S, require N(t) to contain A or K. Necessity follows at T=0: the empty S-row is the induced U graph, whose empty and singleton inputs reject; each cross singleton adds its U neighbors, and a private S edge supplies a third, constant-one row. At a unit t, any S neighbor supplies a constant-one row while a surviving unblocked cross edge supplies a third row unless all K already trigger the U baseline. Conversely an assignment with an edge in T is constant; on an independent T assignment a hot S singleton either has all of A hot, or all of K hot (apply the unit condition to one chosen t). Thus the only possible rows are the baseline and constant one; with no hot S singleton the only second row is the baseline OR the K flags. No-cross cases also have only baseline and constant one.

An edge ab of this graph has two increasing essential two-variable sections. Fixing all others to zero exhibits AND. Set one other neighbor of a and one other neighbor of b to one, and all remaining other raws to zero; the two chosen vertices are distinct and nonadjacent by girth at least five. This gives OR. If a,b both occur exactly once in the signed formula for H, their gates have a fixed lowest common ancestor after pushing signs to the leaves. On any assignment yielding an essential two-variable section, each branch leading to that ancestor transmits its signed literal and the ancestors transmit the result. The exterior, which accesses these private variables only through H, may select H or its complement; for the result to be increasing in both coordinates, the signs of their leaves uniquely determine which selection is possible. Hence that fixed LCA can give only one of AND and OR in increasing sections. For two once-occurring U vertices the same argument applies straight to the exterior formula since H is independent of both. Consequently physically repeated private or exterior raws must cover every edge of G[S] and G[U]. They are disjoint from T, cost at least one raw excess per chosen vertex, and cannot be low raws.

The ledger enumerates all 11,631 labeled partitions satisfying the raw count assumptions. Among 11,512 with nonempty S,U, 592 pass the one-bit criterion. There are 63 exterior-empty and 56 private-empty partitions. The two empty sectors always pass the bit criterion and none pass the edge-cover condition, since the adjacent single-use lows 1,2 must lie on opposite sides. Exactly 94 partitions retain enough raw excess to cover the within-side edges. The counts at |T|=1,2,3,4,5 are 12,28,32,18,4. The checker additionally tests induced-P4 repeats on exposed two-row slices; these are implied by the edge-cover requirement already enforced. Sixty-one deterministically sampled partitions are checked independently by enumerating the full 1,024 truth assignments and counting cofactor rows. The 94 survivors are necessary possibilities, not circuits.
