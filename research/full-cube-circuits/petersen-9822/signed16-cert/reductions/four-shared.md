# Exactly four shared binary gates are insufficient

Use the protected-chain result and comb/pentagon-arbitrary-cut.md. By reflection (0 3)(1 2)(5 8)(6 7), raw 1 first reaches its shared gate Q, and raw 2 first reaches the output. Name the four shared gates P1,P2,Q,W. P1 precedes Q through the private entry of 1. A single-use 6 cannot first reach P1 and a single-use 7 cannot first reach P2 by the neighboring-guard argument of third-shared.md. Raw repetitions are confined to {4,5,8}; four shared gates absorb at least four of the six excess fanout units, hence the repeated set R has size at most two.

Choose each of seven R and each of twelve orders with P1 before Q. In chronological order choose the raws whose first gate is the current gate from those still unassigned. Include empty bins; the last remaining raws form the output bin. Each bin must be independent by the fixed-LCA lemma of third-shared.md. Additional fanout, arbitrary different signs on a shared gate’s occurrences, and arbitrary accesses to repeated raws are allowed.

Fix all earlier and repeated raw assignments t. Each current bin transmits at most one bit to the later raws, and its gate does not depend on a later raw. Consequently its target table across current-versus-later raws has at most two rows. For guard Pi, at most one distinct row may depend on owner i, because such a row requires Pi=0 for the only reader xi OR Pi. See three-shared.md for the proof and audit_guard.py for the full-table form. This restriction grants previous raw information freely. The optional joint prefix-capacity test never rejects an additional candidate after these tests in the four-gate recursion.

Over 84 chronological/repeated-raw cases, 249, 503 and 811 candidate prefixes survive the first three stages. At the fourth stage there are 53,525 static masks, 8,010 one-bit failures, 953 guard failures, 1,148 nonindependent output bins and zero accepted allocations. All 84 order receipts and individual counts are in four_receipts.log. The result does not require the earliest-OR constraint nor any additional W ordering.
