# Independent second review of the original 9822 model

The unrestricted-fanout physical minimum is 18 gates, counting each AND, OR, and NOT. This verdict covers the original ten-input graph on all 1,024 rows. The publication candidate has SHA-256 `ff40bb6745f18046f74a51ec102e9edbc1e0657dbf95c37bb21b87bc0a7e2493` and its assembly note has SHA-256 `168b5ca7e979091046866373b1cd016377e336cc88b74b4d310d899f4900606a`.

The original list interpreter supplies zero for an unavailable input index. Empty AND and OR have their respective neutral values; a unary AND or OR is an alias. NOT takes the first input, defaulting to zero. Folding those cases, repeated operands and unused outputs gives an ordinary acyclic circuit at no extra cost. A default-zero output cannot be this target. All argument-specific path counts are counts in the resulting DAG, which retains arbitrary repeated readers.

A circuit of size at most 17 with at least two live NOTs gives a signed circuit of at most 15 binary gates by propagating signs on the wires. The signed 16-binary floor already permits complements at the inputs, intermediate wires and output, so this transfer does not require a NOT to be private. Its archived signed-face and finite-search proofs are dependencies.

With exactly one live NOT, replacing its output by an independent input z produces a monotone outer circuit. Every original variable has an ordinary path into that circuit, since exclusive passage through the inverter would be nonincreasing. The outer six-AND exclusion counts the actual live eleven-source outer circuit. Together with the ten OR-or-NOT bound it forces seven ANDs all in the outer cone, nine physical ORs, and total size 17. A shared inner gate may also lie in the outer cone. Nothing requires its positive uses to be removed.

There is a short independent exclusion when the inverter reads a raw x. An increasing essential dependence supplies an ordinary x-to-output path separate from the inverter input. An all-AND path is impossible because G−x is nonzero. Pinning x false therefore folds an OR on the positive path and also fixes the inverter to one. The remaining monotone circuit has at most eight ORs, while every deletion graph needs nine, by the independently reviewed monotone proof.

Its positive-reader and private variants are both covered. The direct OR reader and pentagon bounds dispose of singleton 5 through 8 and the pairs with 6 or 7. For the pairs with 5 or 8 the seven-OR residual has a four-OR positive cofactor at 9. That needs eight ORs. The singleton-9 proof uses the same principle on ten vertices. This covers all possible S.
