# Four protected raw variables at a cap of 15 signed binary gates

All lower bounds here are for binary gate count with free input, output, and internal-wire complements. The source for the seven-vertex core is the original signed theorem h701_11 : Face true 701 11 at line 4476 of L_meta_9825.lean. For the core obtained on deleting vertex 1 and its neighbors (2,6), the isomorphism to the active vertices of face 701 is 3→2, 8→3, 5→4, 7→0, 9→5, 4→7, 0→9. For deleting vertex 2 and its neighbors (1,7) use 0→2, 5→3, 8→4, 6→0, 9→5, 4→7, 3→9.

Pinning 1=true leaves the first core together with two fresh independent OR literals 2,6. Each costs a binary gate: pin it false and eliminate a direct signed gate reader of the essential variable. Hence this positive cofactor needs at least 13 binary gates. The same holds at 2=true. For 1=false next pin 2=true (2 is essential by edge 27), then 7=false (the latter removes the remaining singleton disjunct). This reaches the second seven-vertex core, giving at least 13 from the cofactor at 1=false. For 2=false use 1=true then 6=false. These four estimates require no general lower-bound recurrence. For 0=true and 3=true the corresponding seven-bit cores have signed lower 10 via h695_10 at line 4350 of the same archive, giving positive cofactor ≥12 by two fresh literals.

The two negative cofactors 0=false and 3=false have signed lower 14: the latter is exactly h1015_14 in L_meta_9825; a graph symmetry, exchanging 0↔3, 5↔8, 1↔2, 6↔7 and fixing 4 and 9, transports this result.

At cap 15 each of 0 and 3 must have exactly one signed binary reader, noncontrolling on zero. At either 1 or 2 there are at most two distinct readers (both pins cost ≥13). If there were exactly two, put the raw at the controlling value of either reader g, which folds to a constant. Some consumer of g must then fold too and is distinct from the two direct readers, unless g feeds only the other raw reader h. In that exceptional arrangement h combines x with g(x,P), where P is independent of x (g is upstream of h and these are the only direct readers). The section at the controlling value of x in g is constant and the other is constant or a signed copy of P. Thus h is implementable with at most one signed binary gate on x and P, contradicting minimality. The output cannot be g (it has other live readers). Hence a pin would save at least three binary gates, impossible. This proves a unique raw reader also at 1 and 2.

None of the four unique readers can take another signed raw as its second operand. For either polarity of any different second raw j, a setting with j at its controlling value leaves the protected variable pivotal: when j=0 choose a distinct neighbor of the protected vertex; when j=1 either j itself is a neighbor or a neighbor nonadjacent to j exists by minimum degree two and absence of C4. The unique reader would have concealed that pivot. A repeated operand would be a removable constant or literal. Thus all four sites are distinct, and each must take an earlier binary output as its second operand.
