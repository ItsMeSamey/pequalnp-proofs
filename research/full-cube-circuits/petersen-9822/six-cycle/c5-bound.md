# Exact C5 paid bound and unique raw frontiers

In a six-line recurrence, free ANDs are captured by intersecting signatures. The base bank on all 32 assignments contains true and all nonempty raw conjunctions (allowing false too cannot help: it is absorbed or propagated by AND/OR, and its NOT is true). Every next paid output is an OR of two old signatures or the complement of one. Adjoin it and its intersection with every old signature. At the last step, intersect all old signatures true on all positive rows into g. A new signed output h works exactly when g AND h is the target; try every complement and every pair whose union cannot introduce any forbidden bit in g. The C++ checker c5final.cpp excludes a match over 138811 ordered two-output prefixes, without symmetry or dominance reductions. The formula x0(x1 OR x4) OR x2(x1 OR x3) OR x3x4 costs four ORs and its truth table is checked. Thus the exact cost is four, allowing arbitrary NOT and free AND.

If a graph retains an induced C5 while each of five deleted raw variables is essential at the time of deletion, the four-gate C5 lower plus five first-non-AND path losses forces nine paid operations. An order exists whenever the graph remaining after the prescribed first deletion is connected: grow out from the C5 and remove in reverse order, or use the same argument separately when the extras each have a path to the C5. If every raw can be the first deletion via such an order, a nine-paid hypothetical realization permits exactly one first paid frontier per raw. This is a statement about every direct path through zero or more ANDs to its first OR/NOT, distinct across paths for a given raw only when distinct paid sites are lost.

In the monotone case consider the earliest paid gate, OR of two raw conjunctions A and B. Remove comparable case by absorption. Each remaining monomial contains a private raw. On any minimal edge through such a private raw r, setting r to zero changes the true output to false, so the first OR must change. Since the other monomial omits r, the monomial containing r must be active on every edge incident to r. Minimum degree two forces this monomial to be exactly r. Thus both operands are single distinct raws. Merging these two variables through their sole outlet cannot compute a graph with distinguishable vertices (adjacency of the pair itself or unequal other neighborhoods). Under the stated graph hypotheses the whole monotone graph costs at least 10 ORs.

An earliest signed NOT likewise can act only on one raw: any raw in its monomial needs that monomial true on every one of its incident edges. If that raw is then pinned true, the NOT fires false; at least one additional non-AND before the output goes away, or the restricted output would be identically zero. On the original graph the positive cofactors at vertices 0,1,2,3,4 all cost at least eight combined OR/NOT (two fresh disjuncts plus a seven-vertex core costing at least six for 0–3; three plus C6 costing five for 4). So an earliest NOT at paid cap nine can only be at a raw from 5,6,7,8,9.
