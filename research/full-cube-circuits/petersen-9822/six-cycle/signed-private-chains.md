# Private first two gates at vertices 1 and 2

Assume a minimal signed-binary 15-gate circuit and the four protected readers in signed-protected.md. Let Gi be the unique raw reader of i=1,2. The two cofactor floors for each i are 13, so setting i to the controlling input of Gi removes at most two gates. The gate Gi becomes constant. It is not the output (this cofactor is nonconstant), so it has exactly one downstream reader Hi. Moreover Hi must pass its other input under this pin: if it became constant then either the circuit output would become constant or a third gate would disappear. The nonraw other operand Pi of Gi must have a reader beyond Gi, or its now dead producer would also be a third loss. If its only other occurrence were at Hi, that would give Hi a function of i and Pi whose section at the controlling i is a constant or signed Pi and whose alternate section is a function of Pi. Since Gi and Hi have their other operands equal up to sign, one checks by absorption that the two gates simplify to a literal, constant, or one signed binary gate. In particular Hi's second operand cannot equal Pi up to sign.

The four gates G1,G2,H1,H2 are pairwise distinct. If H1=H2, the paths from inputs 1 and 2 to the output all pass this common gate and the side operands of G1,G2 depend on neither input. On any fixed background yielding an essential two-input section, both private entries must transmit their raw variables with their fixed wire signs, and the downstream circuit is a nonconstant unary function of the common gate. Consequently every two-essential section equals the same signed two-input truth table or its complement. If H1=G2 the same reasoning works: G1 has an input-independent side operand, and G2 combines its output with input 2. The case H2=G1 is symmetric. In each case the all-zero background yields the section x1 AND x2, whereas setting precisely 6 and 7 true yields x1 OR x2. Vertices 6 and 7 are nonadjacent. Both sections are essential in both variables and are not complements. This rules out all three collisions.

Each of inputs 1 and 2 therefore enters through a private chain of two distinct gates, with nonraw side input to the first gate shared elsewhere. These are structural restrictions conditional on size 15, not an exclusion of all such circuits.

## The two controlling bits

Let c1,c2 denote the controlling bits. Setting x1=c1 loses precisely G1 and H1, since the signed cofactor needs at least thirteen. G2 and H2 survive with their mutual edge and the original signed raw x2 pin of G2 intact. Thus x2=c2 folds G2 and H2 in turn, even if an unrelated operand of either remaining gate was redirected during the first simplification. The resulting signed size is at most eleven. At 10 or 01 the cofactor has one fresh OR literal (respectively 6 or 7) and the seven-bit core of h701_11; both sections need twelve. At 00, removing essential vertex 0 from the remaining eight-bit graph saves a gate and leaves a copy of signed h1009_11 (L_meta_9825.lean line 4639; map 3→0,4→4,8→5,9→9,6→7,7→6,5→8). Thus the 00 section also requires twelve. Only the 11 controlling pair survives. All four protected raw entries normalize to positive ORs.
