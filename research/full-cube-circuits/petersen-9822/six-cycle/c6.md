# OR count of the six-cycle edge function

Consider monotone fan-in-two circuits with unrestricted sharing and free AND gates. Six positive probes are the edges; five negative probes are all maximal independent sets. Correctness on these probes is sufficient by monotonicity. Their signatures occupy respectively the low six and high five bits. A signature dominates another if it has all its positive bits and no extra negative bits. Replacing a wire by a dominant wire preserves this order through subsequent gates.

Initially the bank contains all raw conjunctions, including true. Keep its undominated signatures. When an OR is paid, both operands can be chosen from this bank: replacing discarded operands by dominant options only improves downstream behavior in the order above. After making h, close the bank under free AND by retaining the existing bank, h alone, and every h AND b. Indeed any conjunction of old available signals is already dominated by some old bank element; repeated use of h does not change a conjunction. Pruning again is therefore complete for the existence of a correct output. Conversely every represented signature is physically attainable. An OR output already dominated by a bank member cannot help. At the final OR step, if the output includes h, the best further free restriction is to intersect h with all old bank signals covering every positive, retaining all positives and eliminating the largest possible set of negatives. The no-new-OR case is tested separately.

The script c6.py enumerates this recurrence exactly. The 3-OR and 4-OR calls together produced 34,672 distinct memoized states and neither attained the target. Choosing the three even vertices, conjoining each with the OR of its neighbors, and joining the three terms uses five OR gates. The checker verifies that formula on all 64 assignments.

For nonconstant monotone F, introducing an independent singleton disjunct y increases minimum OR count by one, even allowing free AND gates. Start from a constant-folded circuit, minimizing OR gates then total gates. On the assignment with only y true the output accepts. Before the first OR which evaluates true, every true wire is a conjunction of copies of y, hence identically y. Such an OR must exist: otherwise the output would be identically y, incompatible with F. Pin y=false, bypass this OR (at least one operand identically y), and simplify. The residual computes F with one fewer OR. The converse follows by appending an OR. Iterating this argument for vertex 4 of the ten-vertex graph, whose positive cofactor is the C6 edge test or the fresh disjuncts 0,3,9, gives at least eight OR gates.
