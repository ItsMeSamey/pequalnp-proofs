# The adjacent pair contradicts the singleton and edge rows

Use the physical hypotheses and frontier argument of adjacent-owner-cut.md through the separation of the two slots of V. A public use of H is unnecessary in those paragraphs. When H is private there is simply no ordinary path out of H. The singleton-hot OR bound rules out extra raw OR readers in either case.

Fix every exterior raw to zero. On 10, 01 and 11, H is one and z is zero. Let the first operand of V be the unique slot reached by an H-avoiding AND-only path from s, and the second the corresponding slot for t. Compare 10 to 11. Any change in the first operand would yield a changing path from raw t. It cannot pass H or z. If it contained an earlier OR, its first OR could only be V, which cannot precede its own operand. Hence the path would be AND-only, placing the first operand in the t slot set, a contradiction. Its value at 11 equals its value at 10. Likewise the second operand at 11 equals its value at 01. At 01 the first operand vanishes by s domination. At 10 the second vanishes by t domination. Thus V(11) equals V(10) OR V(01).

The output of the circuit on these three rows is a fixed unary function of V. To see this, fix H=1, z=0, V=v. Any other changing raw path to the output must encounter one of the fixed sites or be an impossible uninterrupted AND path. Some singleton has the same V value as the doubleton, so their output values must agree. But G rejects both singleton inputs and accepts the doubleton st. This excludes all adjacent literal high pairs, including private and publicly shared H. It uses the seven-OR double-deletion floor and no signed single-deletion size archive.
