# Adjacent owner cut without single-vertex lower bounds

Assume a minimum full-binary signed circuit for graph 9822 with seven AND, nine OR and one NOT. Its inverter reads the output of the literal gate H = s OR t for adjacent distinct highs 57,58 or 68. Assume H has at least one use outside the inverter. The positive singleton price and exclusive leak give the checked hot-OR conclusion: among all physical ORs only H can read raw s or t, and no OR reads H. A public use of H is consequently an AND. Allow arbitrary additional AND uses of the three wires and arbitrary later use of the inverter output z. All statements are conditional on this literal placement and public use.

Pin s=t=0. The NOT folds on input H=0. The resulting monotone circuit needs seven ORs by the adjacent double-deletion face theorem. Consequently at most one physical OR other than H can fold. Call a positive path from a source s,t,H a path with only ANDs before the first OR, with a direct edge to that OR allowed. For paths out of the raw sources exclude H as the first OR; for paths out of H exclude the edge to NOT. The operand at every such first OR is forced zero by the double pin. Thus every such OR is the same V, if one exists. Positive paths of only ANDs to the designated output would force the entire 00 output face to be identically zero, impossible. An AND-only path from a raw into the inverter would have to enter its unique literal input H, which is an OR, so there is no additional negative escape. When a directed path from a raw travels through H positively, apply this argument after H.

The graph is essential in s on t=1, and in t on s=1. It is also essential in either owner with the other zero. It distinguishes 10 and 01 on some common exterior input. These elementary facts follow by a singleton exclusive neighbor or an edge on the relevant graph slice (PairRawSeparation and PairSymmetricReads contain finite tests). At t=1 fix an exterior assignment at which adding s changes the output. A path of changed signals from raw s cannot traverse H or z, which have fixed values. Thus V exists, and there is an H-avoiding AND-only path from s to at least one of its two operands. Let I_s be the set of V operand slots so reachable; similarly I_t is nonempty. An unmediated raw as operand counts as a path of length zero.

Neither I_s nor I_t has size two. Suppose s reaches both slots. With s=0 both operands and V are constantly zero. When t changes from 0 to 1 on this face, all positive routes from t, including the ordinary routes through H, meet V before reaching the output; the only remaining possible change originating at the inversion is a decrease in z. Viewing z as a free source, the remaining gates are monotone. A positive output change is therefore impossible. The symmetric argument excludes two slots for t.

If I_s and I_t consist of the same slot, its value is zero at both 10 and 01. All direct-owner H-avoiding paths hit that slot before reaching any other OR or output. With H and z fixed across the two assignments, the value of the other V operand cannot differ: any changed path reaching it from a raw without an earlier OR would put that index in I_s or I_t; if the changed path has an earlier OR it already passed V, impossible by chronology. Thus the entire output agrees, contradicting graph asymmetry. Consequently the two different slots are respectively dominated by unmodified AND-only paths from s and t avoiding H.

Retain the original H input to NOT; replace every other use of H by constant one and propagate constants. At H=1 no wire changes. At H=0 the two identified paths still force both operands of V zero because these paths avoid H; this holds even if other operands depend on patched H. Every changed path to output starting from a positive H use must first reach V through ANDs (there is no OR-free output path and the NOT is retained). Thus the output cannot change. Constant propagation removes at least one public reader, which is an AND, giving a strictly smaller admissible circuit. No assumption about the number of distinct raw readers, shared operands, or a signed size lower bound after deleting a single raw was used.
