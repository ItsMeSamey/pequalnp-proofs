# A sole shared helper of at most four binary gates

Use the formula floor and shared-small.md. If H is the only shared output, its h-leaf formula feeds a p-leaf outer formula, p+h≤19. Let S be its singleton-positive variables. Substitute OR(S), or false when empty, then append the graph edges T on which H was true outside S. This replacement initially weakens the function pointwise. The repair restores all missing minimal positives without introducing any false positive. Endpoints of T occur in H. When S is empty at least two outer parent gates fold: H occurs at least twice and equal H siblings simplify. Distinct parents each fold under the substitution, even if nested.

For h=4, |S|=2 leaves at most one missing edge; appending costs at most two and the new shared OR costs one. For |S|=1, three other raws induce at most a two-edge star, costing at most three with the append. For S empty the graph on four vertices is a forest with at most three edges, a star or P4 at three. Its formula costs at most four; appending costs at most five, paid by the original helper and two outer folds. Larger S reduces to raw OR. Only OR4 needs an additional lower bound.

For h=5 and |S|=2 the three other variables contain at most a two-edge star; one new shared OR and up to three append gates suffice. For |S|=3 there is at most one missing edge, costing two to append, plus two for OR(S). For |S|≥4 no correction is needed beyond OR(S). For S empty the helper and two outer folds pay up to six gates. A forest on at most five vertices has at most four edges and vertex cover size at most two by the matching bound for forests. A star decomposition therefore costs at most five gates, six including the append. A nonforest on five vertices in this graph must be a five-cycle by girth five. If H accepts all five cycle edges, its five leaves are distinct, and both root shores are disjoint and cold at zero. An OR root has children cold on every singleton, so no cross edge is accepted, contradicting connectedness. An AND root requires every accepted edge to cross its shores, contradicting oddness. Hence T is a forest.

For h=5 and one singleton s, T is a forest on at most four raws. Except for a three-edge path its formula costs at most three gates and appending it costs at most four. In the exceptional path case all five raws occur once. At s=0 its parent folds, leaving a formula on the other four distinct inputs with no hot singleton. Its root must be AND, since with an OR root no accepted edge crosses the root partition, while the path is connected. Each of the four vertices is incident with an accepted edge, hence its cross-shore factor accepts that vertex alone. Each factor therefore equals OR on its own raws by monotonicity. The shores are the unique bipartition of the path. Since the original formula accepts s alone, it equals s OR this four-raw product. There are 37 induced paths times six remaining singleton choices. The helper is hot on s, each path edge, and the fourth edge of the completed bipartite square.

Run verify-helper-weights.py, the four tight_helper.py invocations from shared-small.md, verify-OR4.py and verify-extended.py. The exact arithmetic excludes all raw disjunctions on two through ten variables at the required outer budgets and the 222 exceptional labels at budget fourteen. The single-shared helper of size at most four binary gates is thereby excluded at seventeen total gates, as is a sole shared pure disjunction of any arity. The proof is confined to monotone binary AND/OR circuits.
