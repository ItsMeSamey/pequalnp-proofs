# A sole shared monotone helper accepting at least one singleton

A per-variable positive cofactor lower bound L_s is 10 or more for s other than 9, and at least 9 for s=9 (official9822/comb/minors.log and its archived graph isomorphisms). A seventeen-gate putative circuit consists of its sole shared internal helper H, using c gates, and an outer formula with at most 18−c leaves.

If H accepts singleton s, pin s to one, replace H by true, and delete its complete private input cone. At least two outer binary parents of H occurrences fold. Equal sibling inputs to one parent would simplify. This proves c≤15−L_s. When H accepts another singleton t, s must also appear in the outer formula: otherwise its influence would pass entirely through H, which is fixed on t=1, yet s remains essential on that slice. On this graph an adjacent pair witnesses that fact, and for nonneighbors select a neighbor of s which is not adjacent to t by minimum degree two and absence of a four-cycle. An outer parent reading both s and H would simplify because s implies H. Thus a separate exterior parent of s also folds, giving c≤14−L_s. Taking a hot s other than 9 bounds c by four as soon as two singletons are hot. The previously certified four-gate exclusion applies.

Now suppose s is the unique hot singleton. Then c≤5 for s≠9 and c≤6 for s=9. The remaining c=5 case has six helper leaves. After replacing H by s, append the edge function of lost positive minimal edges T on the other at most five supporting raws. If T has at most three edges then its endpoints are among at most five vertices so its minimum cover size is at most two; the repair as a final disjunction costs at most five gates. The same holds for a four-edge star. If all five edges of a cycle are lost, they use all five other raws, so the formula after s=0 is read-once. It has no hot singleton. A connected accepted cycle cannot have an OR root since both child supports are disjoint and singleton-cold. An AND root cannot accept an odd cycle across its disjoint shores. These arguments use the girth-five graph, where any nonforest on at most five vertices is precisely a five-cycle.

Thus the only c=5 helpers not already covered by a repair of cost ≤5 have T a four-edge tree on exactly five distinct other raws. Again the function after s=0 is read-once. Connectedness forces an AND root, whose shores form the tree's bipartition. Each factor accepts all its singleton variables (every vertex is incident to an edge), hence equals their disjunction. Since s alone suffices, H is exactly s OR the AND of these two disjunctions. The verifier grants all such masks, including stars, and checks 455 cases.

For c=6, s=9, there are at most six other supporting raws. Enlarge their set arbitrarily to size six among the nine other variables. A positive edge not containing 9 can only be accepted if both endpoints belong to this set, because any remaining helper input on that assignment would otherwise be a single non-hot variable or the zero input. An eligible negative MIS must omit 9. Grant that entire rectangle as the extra label to the outer formula. There are 84 choices; any candidate outer formula would have at most 12 leaves. The standard-library checker reconstructs every label from the original graph and verifies a rational weight strictly exceeding 13 for each of the 455 c=5 domains or strictly exceeding 12 for each of the 84 c=6 domains, after all eligible rectangles are normalized to weight ≤1.

Thus every possible seventeen-gate circuit with exactly one shared helper must have H false on every singleton. This result is conditional only on the earlier formula/four-gate helper exclusions and the positive-cofactor bounds; the cold-helper sector is separate.
