# A sole shared nine-gate monotone helper is impossible at total size seventeen

Take a minimum monotone fan-in-two one-shared circuit for the 9822 graph. Write H for the shared producer, c for the gate count of its inner formula, and J for the outer formula in h and the outside raw labels. The inner and outer formulas have c+1 and at most 18−c leaves. Partition the raws into S (private inner), T (both), U (private exterior). H occurs at least twice in J. Earlier singleton-hot exclusion reduces the case to singleton-cold H.

No edge joins S to U: compare zero and one private raw s at T=0 and a neighboring exterior singleton. Both give h=0 but opposite target values. H must be true on some maximal independent set M, since otherwise H≤F by monotonicity. Were H safe, replacing all h leaves in J by zero and appending H as a disjunct would compute F. Removing at least two formula leaves saves at least two binary gates if the residual is nonconstant; if it is constant false use H alone. This would contradict the established monotone size floor 17. Every H-hot M contains all of S: turning on a missing private raw preserves H=1 and all exterior inputs, yet closes a graph edge by maximality. Hence S is independent. The graph has independence number four by direct enumeration.

For c=9 there are at most nine outside leaves, of which at least two are h and at least 10−|S| are raws. Thus |S|=3 or 4.

If |S|=4 it is maximum, so dominates all remaining raws. Since N(S)⊆T, U is empty and H's ten leaves use every raw exactly once. For every t in T, there is an S neighbor. On boundary assignment {t}, varying S and taking U empty yields both target values; monotonicity of J in h forces H's bit to equal the target on this section. In particular the adjacency between S and T appears exactly among the H-positive two-element assignments. A singleton-cold read-once monotone formula has a pair graph consisting of vertex-disjoint complete bipartite components and isolates: at an OR root the disjoint child graphs are joined by union (both children cold); at an AND root its positive pairs are exactly the cross product of the child singleton supports. Intersecting the pair graph with S×T preserves the biclique-union property. The original graph has no four-cycle. Each of the four private vertices has at least two neighbors, so must be its own component's star center. Eight distinct neighbors do not fit among six other vertices.

For |S|=3 every exterior raw appears once and h twice. U is independent: two adjacent U raws, at their unique leaves of J, would have the same fixed LCA on all essential two-variable sections; the original graph exhibits both AND and OR by girth five and minimum degree two. H is independent of U. Thus S∪U is independent and |U|≤1. The inner formula then has 10−|U| distinct raw labels among ten leaves, leaving at most one repeated label when U is a singleton, none otherwise.

Choose an H-hot MIS M containing S, so H(M∩(S∪T))=1. For any independent assignment a⊆T with a neighbor in S, set U=0. As b runs over subsets of S, F(a∪b) has both values: empty b rejects and an adjacent singleton accepts. J on these assignments is an increasing unary function of H and must transmit H unchanged. Thus H(a∪b)=F(a∪b). Combine these evaluations with singleton coldness and monotonicity to obtain a partial truth table for H.

For a pair of inner variables x,y, exhibit one context where the four prescribed values on them are AND and another where they are OR. Such a pair must include a repeated inner leaf by the fixed-LCA argument. c9minimal.py directly enumerates all 90 labeled (S,T,U) and 141 choices including M. In every case the witnessed pairs cannot all be hit by the allowed zero or one repeated label. The verifier uses only the thirteen input edges and elementary finite truth tables. An independent allocated-leaf protocol solver also excluded the remaining partial-table slices, but that more elaborate computation is not required.
