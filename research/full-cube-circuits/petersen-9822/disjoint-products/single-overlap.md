# One shared raw at one AND

Permit one AND G whose operand cones share a single raw x. All other ANDs are syntactically multilinear. For the given subcubic, four-cycle-free graph the same 18-gate lower bound follows, with arbitrary fanout.

Every ordinary AND is singleton-cold. The baseline singleton support of G is empty or {x}, obtainable using zero or the raw x at no cost. If G has no OR-only path to the output, every terminal product is ordinary, and multilinear.md applies unchanged. If G is terminal, its own singleton support must be empty because the target rejects all singletons.

Write the inputs of G as A,B. On a pair avoiding x, its accepting pairs must use one vertex from each exclusive cone, positive on that side as a singleton. If both A and B are cold at x, a pair containing x cannot fire G: at least one operand sees at most x. G then contributes an ordinary biclique. Otherwise assume A is hot at x while B is cold there. Let U be A's positive singletons other than x, V B's positive singletons, and T={v distinct from x : B(x,v)=1}. Then V⊆T. Precisely the pairs U×V and x×T are accepted by G. Every such pair is an edge. These are the only cases requiring an argument below. Drop G instead if it covers no pairs.

**At most one singleton in V.** When V={y}, use G itself for the y-star on U∪{x}, computed from the baseline singleton projection of A. When V is empty, no such replacement product is needed. For every remaining x–v edge contributed by G, trace B's OR-only prefix. If v is not in V, that edge must reach B through an ordinary terminal AND H of this prefix, with x in one operand singleton support and v in the other. Whenever H already occurs among the original whole-output terminal products, keeping its whole biclique covers its x–v edges. For each additional H used here allocate that AND to one x-star using its opposite operand singleton support. All these x-edges are accepted by B and G. No new spurious positives are introduced.

There are enough distinct OR gates for the new collector even if the global and B prefixes share gates. Let m count distinct retained original whole-output terminal products, including G, and h count additional Hs. In the union of the two prefixes keep OR-only paths from these products to their respective sinks (global output or B), and when V={y} a path from raw y to B. Such a raw path exists because B accepts y and every AND upstream of G is ordinary and rejects that singleton. The subgraph has m+h+|V| distinct sources and at most two connected components, allowing paths of length zero. Binary connectedness gives at least m+h+|V|−2 OR gates. These ORs are free in the simultaneous baseline singleton projection: ORs leading to B have support contained in V and those leading to the output have empty support. The new stars number at most m−1+|V|+h, and thus are affordable with the stated collector.

**At least two singletons in V.** Necessarily U is empty, since otherwise x and any u∈U share at least two neighbors in V. Thus G only contributes the star x×T. Subcubic degree and V⊆T⊆N(x) imply that T contains at most one extra vertex beyond V. Use G to form the product of x and B's baseline singleton sum. If there is an extra vertex v, choose an ordinary terminal AND J of B accepting x–v as before. If J is globally terminal, its ordinary biclique already covers that edge. Otherwise J is free: it is absent from the global output collector and is singleton-cold in the baseline bank, so retaining the global ordinary products or the new G star needs no physical J. Spend this reclaimed gate on an OR adding raw v to the V sum. No other gate is duplicated.

This gives a star circuit of no greater size in either case. The same private-neighborhood and six-center count as in multilinear.md implies size at least eighteen. Two overlapping ANDs, one overlap with more than one common raw, and unrestricted NOTs are outside the claim.
