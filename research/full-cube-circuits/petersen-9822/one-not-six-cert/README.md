# One inversion at size seventeen requires at least seven outer ANDs for graph 9822

Let F be the monotone edge predicate on ten Boolean vertices numbered 0 through 9. Its thirteen positive pair masks are 6, 24, 17, 33, 66, 132, 264, 528, 160, 640, 576, 320 and 288. Consider a reduced binary AND/OR/NOT circuit with exactly one live NOT and at most seventeen total gates. Cut the output of that inverter to a new raw z and retain all gates on paths to the circuit output. This eleven-input monotone circuit, called the outer cone, must contain at least seven AND gates. The bound does not classify larger outer cones, zero-inverter circuits, or multiple inversions.

Write F(x)=K(x,¬H(x)), with monotone inner and outer functions. On a maximal independent assignment I take a rejecting resolution of K, choosing one operand at each AND. For any v outside I, adding v creates an edge and cannot increase ¬H. The fixed resolution must therefore contain v and omits all of I. Enumerating the graph gives sixteen distinct required receiver patterns (mis in the sources). These imply nonempty incomparable raw masks, and every two must intersect somewhere. A circuit of g outer gates has at most g−10≤6 reverse binary joins, since each of eleven essential sources or gates is live and there are 2g+1 total uses including the output observer. Thus at least four of the ten receiver masks are primitive signed branches. The direct labels use distinct axes. No independence assumption on those labels is used.

The accompanying four notes give the proof for at most five outer switches and then six outer switches with four, five and six direct labels. The profiles and generator sources are included locally; legacy paths in the component notes refer to the same source contents. A mask atom denotes one signature of all signed branches, and actual gate chronology is relaxed in the receiver searches. The additional source z is never counted as a twelfth switch or as a free auxiliary union.
