# Five parallel hidden products with both terminal arms hidden-bearing

Fix the six-AND sole-terminal case on the official ten-variable graph with thirteen positive edges and sixteen maximal negative independent sets. Assume all five live hidden ANDs are leaky, as justified in safe-hidden-normalization.md, and suppose each is parallel: both its operands are raw disjunctions. Every hidden product appears on exactly one final operand, because any common label would be safe. Each final operand may also include a raw disjunction. Compute exact raw-bank OR sharing before paying the terminal-label surcharge of h5-exclusive-sink-cost.md.

An arm comprising k hidden products and a raw disjunction distributes into an AND of at most 2^k raw disjunctions. Every such raw conjunct accepts every positive edge (as does the whole arm); its raw variable set is a vertex cover. Its complement is independent, so the conjunct can reject at most one maximal independent set. Both arms together must reject all sixteen. For a split of 2 and 3 hidden products this is impossible since 4+8=12.

For the remaining split let the one-sink operand be U=R OR (A AND B), using letters for raw disjunctions. Its two distributed conjuncts are R OR A and R OR B. If one hits every maximal negative, drop that conjunct from the overall output, preserving exact values on the thirteen positives and sixteen maximal negatives, hence on the whole monotone cube. Identical conjuncts can likewise be reduced to one. The remaining raw disjunction requires at most one extra join of the retained R and A or B from the exact raw bank. If R is nonempty the old terminal's one-sink arm already paid one join; if R is empty no join is needed. The hidden AND disappears with no additional ORs. This is a physical budget-preserving bypass, not an assertion that larger operand masks have monotone cost.

Otherwise the two distinct raw conjuncts reject two distinct maximal independent sets I and J, and are exactly their complements by maximality. The opposite arm must reject each of the other fourteen maximal negatives and cover every positive edge. Every raw label hits at least one of these fourteen, verified for all 120 unordered pairs, so this arm has no raw term. Its four raw products are leaky, necessarily at I or J. For a prospective product, distribute the fourteen negatives between its two raw shores as rejection obligations (at least one shore rejects each). Within each part the largest permissible shore consists of every vertex outside the union of those negatives. Saturating both shores cannot remove a positive edge or erase an existing leak at I or J, and cannot violate the assigned rejections. Therefore the union of up to four resulting positive-edge masks is a valid upper bound on coverage even with unrestricted OR cost. No enlarged physical scope is priced or reconstructed. The 120-pair check finds no cover of all thirteen edges. An independent checker using direct raw-product enumeration instead of partitions also found no cover, with at most eight positive edges covered.

Thus every normalized five-parallel-hidden layout with hidden sources on both operands is impossible or admits a circuit using fewer ANDs and no more ORs. This result retains arbitrary raw overlaps, redundant raw terminal labels, and arbitrary OR sharing. Other hidden dependency DAGs are outside the theorem.
