# Four sinks on one arm, sole nonsink on the other

If four sinks are all on one terminal arm in the normalized both-hidden case, the other arm is U=R OR H, with raw R and the sole nonsink H=A AND B. H must occur there. Distributing U into two edge-covering raw clauses shows its rejection set P on maximal negatives has size at most two. H is false on all of P. Every sink of the opposite arm V is leaky only in P. At such a leak both raw operand parts are true. Clamping away H from that sink thus leaves a leaky raw product whose leaks are still confined to P. Every nonedge raw pair, and every singleton, is contained in at least two maximal independent sets on this graph. The two raw witnesses at a leak force |P|=2. For P={I,J} the two distributed clauses reject distinct maximal independent sets and are their complements. Up to exchange this forces J minus I contained in A disjoint from I, and I minus J contained in B disjoint from J.

Every raw vertex occurs in more than two maximal independent sets, so V has no raw disjunct. Nor can V directly include H or a sink have H on both of its operands, since H leaks outside P. A sink containing H on one operand is its raw-only product OR (H AND D), with D the raw disjunction on its other operand. D rejects every H-leaking maximal negative, since these lie outside P. Therefore the positive gains of all H-mediated terms together lie in the single mask of H-positive edges hit by a raw variable absent from every H leak.

For each of the 120 exempt pairs compute upper grants of four raw-only leaky products as in h5-parallel-exclusion.md. For each pair-compatible original first H add the preceding global mask. On 42761 pair-plus-shores cases no such union covers all 13 edges; the independent direct tester found maximum twelve. Both-marker occurrences, arbitrary raw overlaps and old OR sharing were allowed in establishing the necessity. The semantic overestimate is used only for rejection. A sinkless terminal arm having no hidden input lies outside the both-hidden hypothesis.
