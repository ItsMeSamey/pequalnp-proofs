# The six-clause test after the eight-shore restriction

Use h5-negative-pairing.md for the actual first-product shore list. In the normalized three-sink split 1+2, each of the two terminal arms distributes respectively into two and four clauses, all disjunctions on raw variables plus the actual ordered first two hidden labels H,Q. These six clauses must individually accept all edge inputs and collectively reject all sixteen maximal negatives. Each such clause can reject at most four negatives because replacing Q by its two raw/H operands gives at most two raw/H clauses. A Q-leaking negative must be rejected by one of the six clauses omitting Q, and each such clause rejects at most two. For H matching size m the inequality 16≤12−ceil(|Q_N|/2)+m restricts H to the eight shore pairs with m≥5.

For each H compute all traces D of raw disjunctions with or without H on the official 13+16 rows. Form Q by pairwise AND from D, maintaining the actual permitted raw/H operand order and allowing H on either, both, or neither operand. Distinct traces are merged only in a semantic feasibility test with no OR budget. Keep Q accepting at least one edge, leaking on some negative, with |Q_N|≤2(m−4). The counts in original sorted list order are 324,284,324,275,275,284,231 for m=5 and 1796 for m=6.

For each surviving Q enumerate all raw/H/Q disjunction traces by adding Q optionally to each member of D. Keep the rejection masks of those accepting all thirteen edges. The exact depth-six set-cover recursion picks a remaining negative row and branches on every available mask containing it; removing that mask's covered rows strictly shrinks the set. It may prune when the number of remaining rows exceeds four times the remaining number of clauses. No instance covers all sixteen within six clauses. The finite test is necessary even with unlimited and shared ORs, and never substitutes a new physical mask in a cost calculation. A negative result on the original extremal tests rules out computing the full target over its 1024 Boolean inputs.

This statement concerns the three-sink split one-plus-two. The zero-plus-three split, where the first arm can still have hidden nonsink labels, is separate.
