# Matching capacity of a fixed first hidden product

Let H=A AND B be a productive leaky AND of two raw disjunctions. A clause on raw labels and H that accepts all thirteen positive edges rejects at most two maximal negatives: if H occurs, distribute it into two raw disjunctions, each of which is a vertex cover and rejects at most one maximal independent set.

For a fixed H join two of the sixteen maximal independent sets I,J when H rejects both and accepts all edges in their union. These are exactly the pairs rejectable by one positive-covering raw/H clause, using as its raw support the complement of the union. Each vertex of I minus J has a neighbor in J minus I, and conversely, by maximality. There is an edge inside the union. I,J therefore cannot reject the same raw shore of H. Up to shore exchange, the necessary and sufficient conditions are J minus I contained in A disjoint from I, and I minus J contained in B disjoint from J. The other vertices of A,B can be chosen independently outside I union J.

Visit 120 pairs and all of the latter subsets. For each resulting leaky productive unordered shore pair, compute maximum matching size m on the sixteen row indices. The corrected physical-shore histogram is 29850 of size 1, 4392 of size 2, 678 of size 3, 57 of size 4, 7 of size 5, one of size 6. Remaining products have no pair. The unique size-six product has shores 68 and 130. Independent direct raw-product enumeration agrees. The initially proposed global bound m≤3 was false: shores 2 and 84 have four disjoint pairs (139,201), (523,525), (554,556), (771,773). The initial extension loop erroneously masked with the current subset; the fixed loop uses the original mask and is checked by direct set equality and cardinality, including nonconsecutive masks.

A collection of s such edge-covering clauses rejects at most s+m distinct negatives. Assign each distinct rejected row to just one clause occurrence. Each occurrence gets at most two rows, and the two-row occurrences have mutually disjoint pairs in the graph for that SAME H. This applies equally to repeated clauses and to clauses resulting from expansion of shared wires.

## Four sinks split one and three

The sole earlier H is raw. Expanding the one-sink arm and three-sink arm gives at most two and eight raw/H clauses. All sixteen negatives must be rejected, hence m≥6. For the unique candidate H (positive mask 49 and negative mask 96), all raw/H disjunctions yield 960 observable traces. Among those covering every positive, there are 23 distinct rejection masks. The one-sink arm, including arbitrary residual raw/H signals, has one of 236 two-clause rejection unions P. For each P the opposite arm must reject every row outside P. Give it every permissible raw/H signal as a residual OR, and allow any three leaky products of raw/H disjunctions whose leak masks lie inside P. Their semantic positive grants can only overestimate what a physical circuit can attain. No P reaches all thirteen; independent direct checking gives maximum eleven. This excludes the normalized four-sink one-plus-three case, with original OR costs unrestricted for the impossibility test.

## Three sinks split one and two

Expand the sink layer to at most six clauses on raw,H,Q where Q is the second nonsink, potentially dependent on H. Substitution of its two original operands gives at most twelve raw/H clauses; thus m≥4. More sharply Q leaks, so at least one of the six upper clauses lacks Q. If its leakage contains l maximal negatives, those l rows must be rejected by upper clauses lacking Q, each of which rejects at most two. The post-substitution bound is therefore 12−ceil(l/2). Hence m≥4+ceil(l/2), allowing only eight first raw shore pairs; seven m=5 cases require l≤2, and the m=6 case requires l≤4. These are restrictions, not a complete exclusion.

Actual scopes and all their marker overlaps are retained in the structural reasoning. No semantic saturation result is used to price a different circuit. This theorem does not cover zero-sink arms or cases with fewer than three sinks.
