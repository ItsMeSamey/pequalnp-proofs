# Three negative leaks require fewer than eight linear raw prefix joins

Let H and Q have four distinct nonempty raw operand scopes with pairwise intersections of size at most one. Their exact shared OR cost p is the sum of their widths minus four because no productive OR serves two different targets. No triple leakage set is possible at eleven ORs in the 0+3 layout when p≥8.

After extracting the exclusive sinks there are at most nine residual gates. If p>9 the prefix alone exceeds budget. For p=8 or 9 the four widths sum to twelve or thirteen. Neither hidden alone covers all positive edges: both of that gate's operands would have to be vertex covers, requiring a total of at least 6+6+1+1=14. U needs another OR outside the raw cones, excluding p=9. At p=8 just one join remains and the residual V is empty. Joining one hidden signal with a raw disjunction makes U a conjunction of two monotone clauses. Each contains a vertex cover when U accepts all positive edges. Its complement is independent and thus contains at most one maximal negative. No triple of maximal negatives can falsify U.

If U joins H to Q, each cross union of their raw operands covers the graph. The two smaller operand widths, one from each product, sum to at least six. Since all four widths sum to twelve, they must be equal within each product, giving k,k and 6−k,6−k. Up to interchanging the raw-only products, k=1,2,3. Every cross union is a minimum cover. Enumerate the thirteen six-label masks meeting the thirteen positive edges. For each k-label A list all (6−k)-label C for which A∪C is one of these covers. For distinct k-label masks A,B sharing at most one label, their common list has no distinct C,D sharing at most one label. All three rectangle counts are zero, independently confirmed. The row model is the original thirteen positive edges and sixteen maximal negatives. Monotonicity extends agreement on these extremal rows to the full 1,024 inputs.
