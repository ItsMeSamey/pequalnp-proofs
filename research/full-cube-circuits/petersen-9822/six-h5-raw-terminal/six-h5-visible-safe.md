# Promoting visible safe hidden gates in a sole-terminal circuit

Fix an actual monotone binary AND/OR circuit with six ANDs and at most eleven ORs. Suppose its only exposed AND is its last, so the two terminal operands are OR-only unions of original raw or earlier AND labels (the final output can be identified with that AND; no raw output leaf since all singletons reject). On the official ten-bit function an earlier label is safe if it rejects all sixteen maximal independent inputs. By monotonicity safety is equivalent to implying the target on every input. No raw label is safe. Let J be the set of distinct safe AND labels present in the union of the two operand masks. Every common operand label is safe, since it alone implies both operands. No removal of redundant source labels is used.

If one entire operand consists of safe labels, it is squeezed between F and F and is itself F: discard the final AND. Otherwise each terminal operand contains a non-J primitive. Treat an AND output as a primitive only while tracing OR gates. In the original OR subnetwork of the terminal operands take the J sources and all OR gates whose nonempty formal supports lie in J, with their original input edges. Normalize gratuitous constants and aliases first, or just omit zero inputs. Every j in J reaches a terminal operand using OR edges. On such a path it must leave the pure subnetwork, because that operand has a non-J primitive. Consequently each weak component containing t selected sources has at least one outgoing edge to a mixed OR gate. With n pure OR gates in such a component there are at most 2n internal edges, and connectedness yields 2n≥n+t−1. At least t−1 pure gates vanish when J signals are clamped to zero. One further mixed OR per component loses its pure input and becomes an alias (or zero). Distinct components cannot send to the same mixed gate: both its inputs would then be pure, making the gate pure. Summing supplies at least |J| distinct original OR deletions, even with shared OR DAGs and merged occurrences. Other fanouts of a removed OR use its replacement wire.

Clamp the outgoing uses of every selected AND, but retain the recomputed value of each selected gate for a new collector. Every recomputed wire value is at most its original truth value. Return the OR of the recomputed terminal and all recomputed selected ANDs. If some selected original gate was true, the earliest such gate is unchanged and remains true. If none were true the terminal is unchanged. On any negative every selected value is false originally, and the terminal remains false. Thus the new collector computes the original F. It needs at most |J| new OR gates, all paid by the preceding deletion count. Propagate internal zeros and aliases; if any AND disappears, the new circuit has at most five ANDs and at most eleven ORs. Otherwise the terminal AND and each selected AND have direct OR-only output paths, yielding at least 1+|J| exposed ANDs. These conclusions concern the new circuit, and preserve unrestricted sharing in the original.

In particular, three or more visibly safe labels reduce the h5 case to previously addressed six-AND h≤2 or fewer ANDs. With one or two they reduce h5 to h≤4 or h≤3 respectively. Without visible safe labels the two terminal support masks are disjoint, their unions of negative leakage rows are disjoint, and every hidden or raw label appearing has nonempty leakage. The two rejection sets of the terminal operands need only cover the sixteen maximal negatives; they may overlap. No classification of the remaining disjoint-support cases follows from promotion alone.
