# Exact OR cost after removing terminal-only hidden labels

Fix actual ordered AND operand masks as formal disjunctions of raw and earlier AND labels. Write C(T) for their minimum number of binary ORs with free fanout and singleton aliases, as in exact-shared-or-scope-price.md. Keep every hidden AND slot when computing the residual cost, even if its output no longer occurs in a terminal target. Cost is syntactic in these labels, not up to truth-table substitutions.

For the h5 sole-terminal case normalize the five hidden signals to be leaky on maximal negatives. Draw the hidden dependency DAG with an arc when an earlier hidden label occurs in a later hidden operand. Every live sink must occur on a final operand and cannot occur on both: a common label implies F and would be safe. Select all t such sinks. Denote their sets on the terminal arms by E1,E2 and the residual terminal scopes by R1,R2 after deleting them. Let d count terminal arms with empty residual scope (their full scopes are nonempty). Keep the ten original hidden operands unchanged. Then

 C(original twelve scopes) = C(ten original hidden operands plus R1,R2) + t − d.

More generally this removal applies to any distinct designated targets whose removed labels appear nowhere else, with empty target lists interpreted as free zero.

For the lower bound work in an optimal implementing OR DAG, truncated at primitive AND results. For a target with k>0 removed labels, take its OR cone nodes containing at least one of those labels. None can be used for another target. Stop the cone at the k removed primitives and at its m distinct nonempty incoming frontier signals free of those labels; their union is exactly the residual scope. The cone joins these k+m distinct signals to one target, charging at least k+m−1 ORs by connectedness of the undirected binary DAG (this includes the single primitive case at zero charge). Remove the charged gates. If m>0, join the retained frontier signals to obtain the residual scope in at most m−1 ORs, for savings at least k. If m=0 the residual scope is empty, for savings at least k−1. The two charged cones are disjoint. No reused earlier OR is charged as private frontier. For k=0 keep the target unchanged. Conversely use an optimal residual library, joining each residual to its k extracted primitive labels at exactly the listed surcharge. An empty residual needs only k−1 joins. Joins can be scheduled after the last hidden AND. Thus this is an exact price with unrestricted allowed OR sharing, not a count of deleted gates in the original circuit. The reassembled terminal operands are their original twelve-scope values, so every original truth-table row is preserved.

If t≥3, at most two hidden ANDs are not sinks. Order those first (respect their original relative dependencies), then the sinks in any order. No sink label appears in any residual target. C(residual) therefore concerns at most twelve available primitive labels, ten raw plus at most two earlier hidden outputs, at budget 11−t+d. All actual sink operands still appear as targets and their original products still have to be checked. For five parallel hidden gates the residual uses only the ten raw labels, at budget 8−r where r is the number of nonempty raw parts on the two terminal arms. These reductions do not enumerate or exclude all such prefixes.
