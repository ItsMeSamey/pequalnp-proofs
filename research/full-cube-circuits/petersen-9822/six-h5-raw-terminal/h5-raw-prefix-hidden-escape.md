# A mandatory hidden-label join outside the sinkless arm

Assume the normalized three-exclusive-sink layout 0+3, with H and Q the two hidden nonsinks, both products of pure raw disjunctions. Let p be the exact OR cost of the four original raw scopes. Every sink is leaky on some maximal negative. At any such row V=1, hence U=0. A label appearing as an OR-disjunct of U is false there, as is every wire in U's OR-only ancestor cone. In particular such a wire by itself cannot be an operand of any leaky sink. The OR gates in the four raw-scope cones use only raw labels and number at least p, irrespective of sharing.

At budget 11, stripping the three V sink labels leaves at most nine ORs, including every original sink operand and U. Suppose U needs at least one actual OR involving a hidden primitive. If p≥8, this uses the entire residual bank when feasible. Choose a hidden primitive H appearing in U, renaming H,Q if necessary. As the other nonsink is raw-only, all its hidden descendants must start at a sink operand. The only available wires containing H are H and the one nonraw join U. Each is false at every V-leaking negative, so no sink can use either as an operand. This contradicts the actual nonzero outdegree of H in the hidden dependency DAG. The argument neither prices a saturated OR nor treats semantically redundant hidden occurrences as freely available.

For four distinct pairwise-linear raw scopes at p=8 their cardinalities sum to twelve. No raw-only hidden product can cover every edge: both operands would have size at least six, leaving no room for the other two nonempty operands. Consequently U is not a lone hidden primitive. The exclusion includes every mixed U=H∨Y with Y contained in A or B of H, all other widths, and U=H∨Q. It also excludes cases with positive residual V, whose remaining budget is smaller.

If both H and Q appear in U, the same argument excludes p=7. Each must reach a sink through an OR outside the raw-scope cones and U's OR-only cone. If there are two such joins, U still needs at least one nonraw join and the budget is exceeded. With only one escape join, it must jointly carry both H and Q. The sole nonraw join in U's cone then realizes exactly U=H∨Q. Its rejected maximal negatives P number at most four by expanding into four raw cover clauses. Every raw variable leaks on at least five maximal negatives, and each of H,Q leaks outside P because U includes both. Any nonempty operand on the other side of the alleged sink therefore leaks on an outside-P row where U=1. The escape operand dominates U, so this would produce a false positive. This additional exclusion requires both nonsinks to occur in U.
