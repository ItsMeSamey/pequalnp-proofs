# Same-side sink refactorization

Under h5-exclusive-sink-cost.md, consider two sinks on the same terminal arm having identical formal operand A and other operands B,C. Replace them by one sink A AND (B OR C). Joining B and C uses at most one new OR. In the sink-extracted representation the two products have no other uses; rebuilding their terminal with one merged label saves exactly one join, including when the entire arm consisted of the two sinks. All earlier OR signals can be retained. The distributive identity holds on all 1024 original inputs. Thus one AND can be removed without increasing the number of ORs. This argument modifies a circuit intentionally; it does not change the cost definition for prescribed labeled operands.

In an AND-minimal representation each sink must be the sole true primitive of its terminal arm on some positive edge. Otherwise clamp its occurrence to zero: all positives survive and negatives can only decrease. Leakiness and liveness give exclusive occurrence. Every live hidden gate is true on some positive edge, since clamping a gate false on every minimal positive preserves those inputs.

For five parallel raw-only hidden products, two singleton operands give a raw alias, an unproductive nonedge monomial, or a safe target-edge monomial. After normalization at least one operand of every sink is a nonsingleton raw OR. Within one terminal arm such operands are distinct across sinks by the refactorization. Thus a split of k and 5−k needs at least max(k,5−k) distinct nonsingleton raw OR wires. If A is reused across the two terminal arms in sinks A AND B and A AND C, then A AND B AND C ≤ F: no maximal independent input can hit all three original raw supports. This is a necessary condition, not a census.
