# A replacement rule and the mixed-sector pinned output

Use the mixed case and the notation of two-not-readers.md and two-not-pivots.md. Let i be the negative-reader vertex, H its physical helper, and D the OR of its two neighbors. Neither paid inverter is an ancestor of H: one has sole consumer y=(NOT x_i) AND H, the other is downstream of y. Thus H is monotone before either inversion. Both neighbor singletons are pivotal, so H is positive on each and D implies H.

Replace the one use of H at y by D. On backgrounds where they differ, D=0 and H=1. The new y is zero, its value in the original when x_i=1. All other paths from x_i are cut at y and vertex i has no active neighbor. Hence the new output equals the old target at x_i=1, which is the required value at either value of x_i. Elsewhere y and the output are unchanged. If H has r private binary ancestors which become dead when disconnected, this construction costs at most one binary site to materialize D, implying r≤1 by the global signed binary floor fifteen. If a copy of D is already available outside that private cone and can feed y without a cycle, r=0. In particular a private nonraw H can be normalized to the one OR D at no added cost. Merely having a semantically identical copy downstream of y does not justify using it as an earlier input.

Write L=K(0), U=K(1), and J_ab=J(a,b), with y as first argument and z as second. These cofactors are monotone functions of the nine remaining inputs. Every one of the nine singleton labels j is contained in a pivotal background. For j a neighbor of i, take its singleton. Otherwise choose a neighbor k of i nonadjacent to j; such a k exists because the graph has no four-cycle. The independent pair j,k is pivotal. Since L and J_10 vanish on all pivotal backgrounds, both vanish on every singleton and at zero. With x_i=1, the physical y is zero and F_1=J(0,NOT L). Thus J_01 has singleton support exactly the two neighbors of i; J_01 at zero is false. The functions U and H accept the two neighbor singletons.

More strongly J_01≥F_1 everywhere. A strict difference at B requires F_1(B)=0 and L(B)=1. Thus B is independent, avoids both neighbors, and is not contained in any pivotal independent background. For i=0, enumeration of the nine-bit cube gives exactly six such sets: {3,7}, {1,3,7}, {3,6,7}, {8,9}, {1,8,9}, {2,8,9}. For i=3 use the reflected sets {0,6}, {0,2,6}, {0,6,7}, {5,9}, {1,5,9}, {2,5,9}. Each list is two disjoint three-element partially ordered sets, each consisting of a pair below two triples. By monotonicity, extra positives of J_01 form an upward-closed subset relative to these six exceptions. There are five choices on each component, hence at most 25 candidate nine-input truth tables. No claim of realizability is made.

Every candidate equals D OR T, where T is its restriction with both neighbor inputs zero. For monotone bounded-fan-in circuit size the two fresh singleton disjuncts cost exactly two additional OR gates compared to T. Pin each to zero in turn. Starting at its essential input and following zero-propagating ANDs, the zero would reach the output and annihilate the nonconstant residual unless at least one OR folds first. The second raw remains essential after the first pin, so the two losses are distinct. The reverse bound appends two ORs. The same argument gives exact additive increase of two for ordinary monotone gate count, and a lower bound of two OR losses in every realization. See pivot-family.py for finite verification and the seven-input data.
