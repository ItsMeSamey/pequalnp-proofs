# Two paid inversions at total size seventeen

Assume the signed binary floor fourteen on the zero face of vertex 3 of graph 9822. The graph reflection (0 3)(1 2)(5 8)(6 7) transfers this floor to vertex 0. A circuit at size at most seventeen with two unary NOTs therefore has exactly fifteen binary gates. Trim dead logic, erase constant-dependent and signed-alias binary gates, and cancel serial NOTs. If an inversion disappears, the representative belongs to the lower-inversion sector.

Suppress unary sites temporarily, carrying signs on binary pins and the output. Each essential protected raw i in {0,3} must reach a binary gate. Two distinct first binary readers would both disappear at x_i=0, contradicting the floor. Two pins of the same reader carrying this same underlying raw compute a constant or signed alias. The unique reader must become an alias at zero: a constant binary output also removes a distinct downstream binary consumer, or makes the entire zero face constant if it supplies the final output through unary gates alone.

Consequently the physical reader is either OR(x_i,H_i), denoted P, or AND(NOT(x_i),H_i), denoted N. In N its dedicated unary inverter feeds only this binary reader and the raw has no other use. In P the raw directly feeds its sole binary reader. A joint reader for the two protected raws would, by neutrality at zero, compute their unsigned OR or its complement. Fixing x5=1 distinguishes 05 from 35, ruling out the joint reader.

The signed value transmitted can therefore be interpreted as x_i OR K_i, where K_i=H_i in P and K_i=NOT(H_i) in N. The latter interpretation complements the transmitted bit and all its uses only as a mathematical description. It does not assert free physical inversions. Each H_i is independent of x_i by acyclicity and the sole-reader property. On every row with x_i=0 where toggling i changes the output, the physical opposite pin H_i equals zero in P and one in N, because the two reader outputs must differ.

No K_i is a signed raw literal. For K_i=NOT(x_j), the singleton of a neighbor of i different from j is pivotal. For K_i=x_j, a neighboring j is a pivotal singleton. Otherwise choose a neighbor k of i nonadjacent to j: a distinct j cannot be adjacent to both neighbors of i because the graph has no four-cycle. Then the independent pair j,k is pivotal. Both constants are impossible, since K_i=1 loses essentiality and K_i=0 makes a signed binary alias. Thus H_i must have a binary ancestor.

Type NN spends both NOTs on the two protected raws. The remaining circuit is increasing in their complemented inputs, so it cannot be strictly increasing in raw 0 on the 05 witness. In the mixed case the remaining inverter must be downstream of the negative reader on a path to the output: otherwise that inverter is independent of the protected raw and every remaining path is nonincreasing in the raw. The reflection exchanges PN and NP. These conditions leave PP and one mixed sector for existence searches, not an unrestricted exclusion.
