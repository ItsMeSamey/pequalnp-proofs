# A singleton-cold guard is impossible with seven outer ANDs

Consider a minimum circuit for the ten-vertex graph 9822 with at most seventeen binary AND/OR and unary NOT sites, exactly one live NOT, and seven ANDs in the monotone outer circuit K formed by cutting its output to input z. Write H for the NOT input and assume H rejects every singleton. Let K1 be K at z=1. It accepts all target edges by monotonicity and rejects all singletons by the guard assumption.

Two induced pentagons partition the vertices: A=(0,4,3,8,5), B=(1,2,7,9,6). The three bridging edges are 49,57,68. Deleting any single vertex leaves the graph connected and preserves the opposing pentagon.

The restriction of K1 to either pentagon is exactly the graph predicate of a supergraph of that pentagon, since every three of its vertices contain an original edge and singletons are negative. Every such supergraph needs four ORs even with arbitrary ANDs. For the bare pentagon this is the established cycle lower bound. If there is a chord, take its triangle and remove the other two vertices in decreasing order of distance to it. Each deletion loses an OR: the raw is essential by an incident positive pair and the rejected singleton at its surviving neighbor; pinning it zero folds the first OR on an outgoing path following initial ANDs. An all-AND path to output would force a zero output on the nonconstant residual. The remaining triangle needs two ORs, since any circuit with at most one OR and unbounded conjunctions is a disjunction of at most two monomials and cannot have three distinct minimal positive pairs.

From all ten vertices delete five to either pentagon in descending distance from it, applying the same essentiality argument. Thus K1 requires at least nine ORs. Similarly after deleting any fixed i, select the opposing intact pentagon and delete four more to it. Connectivity of G−i ensures that K1|i=0 requires at least eight ORs. These restrictions do not assume absence of higher-degree extra minterms outside the pentagon.

The budget therefore saturates: all sixteen binary sites belong to K, including a nonraw H with a positive path to output, and all nine physical ORs survive the z=1 clamp. (A raw H cannot reject every singleton.) No OR directly reads z since it would fold under the clamp. In a reduced circuit no gate repeats an operand. On input z=1 with every ordinary raw zero, every nonraw output in K is zero: the first nonraw one would have to combine only the sole true input z, either in an OR reading z or via z AND z. With every raw one, all outputs are one. Hence no nonraw wire becomes a constant under the clamp. Its simplification need only bypass each direct z AND reader to its other operand. Every original directed path from an ordinary raw to the output survives in the resulting circuit with these bypassed nodes contracted. In particular any path avoiding a specified original OR avoids it after projection.

In this physical presentation of K1 each raw reaches exactly one first OR through AND-only paths. Two different such ORs would both fold at its zero pin, leaving at most seven ORs, contradicting the eight-OR deletion floor. A raw-to-output path with no OR is impossible since some original positive edge omits that raw. Let A0 be the earliest OR in the physical topological order. Its operands in K1 are raw monomials, and let U be the union of their supports. Every raw in U has A0 as its unique first OR, so every path from U to output passes through A0, both on the clamp and in the original outer K by the path-preserving contraction.

U is proper: otherwise K1 depends only on A0, making it equal to A0 (zero and all-ones differ), which has at most two minimal positive pairs. If the restriction to U alone has a positive input, then it too equals A0, because with outside raws zero the cut leaves just the Boolean variable A0 and the restriction is monotone, zero at zero. A0 would therefore reject every singleton in U. But the connected graph has an edge ux crossing the nonempty proper U; u and zero would then be indistinguishable at outside input x, contradicting the positive pair and rejected singleton. Hence U is independent. Each u in U has an outside neighbor, so the same distinguishing argument requires A0 to be true on every singleton u in U. Its two monomials must therefore be distinct single literals u,v (if |U|=1 the OR could be bypassed, contrary to the nine-OR lower bound). Thus A0=u∨v on the clamp and both of its operands are individual raw functions there.

The original graph has no nonadjacent vertices with identical neighborhoods (inspect the ten lists, or use minimum degree two and absence of a four-cycle). Choose an outside x that neighbors u but not v, exchanging the labels if necessary. K1 gives identical outputs at {u,x} and {v,x}, and hence accepts both. The second is an original nonedge, necessitating H({v,x})=1. Since H({x})=0, there is a physical ordinary raw-v path to H.

H must lie strictly after A0 on such a path. Indeed choose any positive path from H to the outer output. If the concatenation from v first reaches A0 after H, H feeds an operand of A0 through ANDs only, since A0 was the earliest original OR. On the positive clamp both operands are true on some ordinary singleton, whereas H is cold on all of them and unchanged by the clamp. This excludes that order. H cannot equal A0 for the same reason. Hence A0 feeds H and a path z to A0 would make a cycle in the original circuit. A0 therefore already computes u∨v physically, with z arbitrary. All positive paths from u,v to the output pass A0. All their paths to H likewise pass A0, by extending them along a positive H-to-output path (none of which can return to A0). Every influence of either raw, including through the actual inverter, is mediated by u∨v. This forces identical original outputs on {u,x} and {v,x}, a contradiction.
