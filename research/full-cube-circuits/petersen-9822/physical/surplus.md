# An independent partial exclusion by excess fanout

Allow inversion free on wires and the output, and delete constants, dead sites and binary gates depending on only one of their pins. Any unrestricted AND/OR/NOT circuit then induces a signed AND/OR DAG on the essential variables. For b binary sites and n essential inputs, the sum of fanout minus one over live nonoutput wires is b−n+1, by counting the 2b input slots. Count only live gates and normalize nonessential inputs to zero.

Pinning a raw with fanout f consumes at least f−1 units of this excess. Indeed remove the constant source, then simplify constant, unary or dead nodes; contracting a unary gate into its unique parent merely transfers its outgoing slots, conserving the sum on those two nodes whenever both were live, and all other removals weakly decrease the sum. For simultaneous pins charge the excess of each original pinned raw before contraction. If an essential raw becomes inessential after pinning, fix it too. Its elimination cannot increase the sum.

Write F as the minimal positive monomials of a monotone graph function; pinning or identifying inputs preserves monotonicity. In a trimmed signed circuit inspect its earliest binary site, reading x and y. For each choice of controlling values cx,cy (free input signs), fixing x=cx freezes this site. If y still matters for that restricted target, y must have a separate consumer. Apply the symmetric test to x. For each forced repeated raw, charge at least one excess; then take the strongest result over all ways of fixing the forced inputs. If neither is forced, consider either one repeating, or neither repeating. In the latter case all four joint cofactors of F factor through one bit. There are at most two distinct joint cofactors. Since F is monotone, its (0,0) and (1,1) cofactors realize both when there are exactly two. Identifying x=y=z contracts the gate without raising surplus, so the smaller identified function supplies a lower bound. Both raws essential excludes a one-cofactor constant case. Taking the minimum over these cases, each potential first pair and each controlling orientation gives a valid lower bound.

The small memoized calculation surplus.py returns 4 excess at the full target (3,342 partial problems). Consequently any circuit has at least 10−1+4=13 binary gates in the free-inversion relaxation. This excludes cap 17 with at least five NOT gates. This source claim is separate from the checked conditional Lean bridge. It does not exclude circuits with zero through four NOTs.
