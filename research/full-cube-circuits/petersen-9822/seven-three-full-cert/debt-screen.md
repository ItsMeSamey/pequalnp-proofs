For each subset of nonprimitive activations paid as nonraw joins, every other activation must be a distinct raw receiver. A pair of these masks that is equal or comparable cannot represent two distinct raw vertices. At visits to a partial assignment S of raw vertices, the directional rooms for the 16 maximal independent sets are determined solely by each set's membership pattern on S. Equal patterns yield identical rooms. Distinct patterns have disjoint rooms, including the case that either is empty. Thus each pattern needs as many distinct directions in its room as its fixed multiplicity. This is exactly the Hall capacity check for these rooms. jointclasses.cpp precomputes these multiplicities over all 1024 subsets, replacing the repeated subset-containment scan. The pair-cache in jointcache.cpp stores only the necessary mask comparability and full-union/edge condition; the old canput check still runs after intersection. Cache entries are discarded at every helper-bank family boundary.

For a mandatory raw W with a pure-union cone, let s be the minimum number of inputs whose union is W. Inputs may be any granted auxiliary named activation or primitive branch that can precede the switch consuming W. A connected binary union cone with s distinct input sources needs at least s−1 joins including the raw's paid sink, hence at least s−2 additional helpers. If two mandatory raws intersect in at most one eligible library source even when *all* branches and granted masks are allowed, they cannot share a nontrivial nonraw helper because every such helper is a union of two or more distinct common inputs. Add demands over pairwise independent subsets. Duplicate or single-source helpers can be replaced by that source. jointwidth.cpp implements this necessary width screen ahead of the already-audited common-bank enumeration.
