# Exact sums of mixed circuits with shared multiplexer controls

**Reduction to the archived binary-atom lower bound.** Fix the single language \(W\in E^{NP}\) in the [archived ordinary-circuit SUM theorem](../span-separation/williams-october-sum.md). That theorem gives constants \(\alpha_0,\eta_0>0\) and an infinite set of lengths \(b\) such that at each such length \(W_b\) requires more than \(2^{\alpha_0 b}\) ordinary binary atoms of at most \(\eta_0 b\sqrt{\log(b+2)}\) gates in any exact real sum. Its premise is the October counting manuscript at commit `42907c9e999cc00d6abe7a50bfc1023250edd1b9`. [Original quantitative witness proof](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/42907c9e999cc00d6abe7a50bfc1023250edd1b9/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/quantitative-circuits.tex)

A Boolean atom on \(b\) inputs with \(s\) binary gates, \(d\) dense XOR/AND/OR gates and any finite number of multiplexers using \(g\) distinct control signals is an exact real sum of at most \(2^{g+2d+1}\) ordinary Boolean atoms. Each ordinary atom has at most \(C(b+s+d+g+1)\) binary gates for an absolute constant \(C\). To see this, sum first over all \(2^{g+d}\) guesses for the control signals and dense outputs. Resolve multiplexer aliases, including the controls themselves, and pin each mapped control to its guessed bit. Replace each dense output by its guessed constant. On raw input \(x\), the surviving binary skeleton computes every ordinary wire; it can also evaluate consistency with the original dense gate definitions. Expand the product of the \(d\) consistency indicators into at most \(2^d\) signed terms using the identities below. For each term collect the pins, including the output pin, AND them together, and combine the signs into one parity of the remaining skeleton wires. Inconsistent pins give the zero function. A signed indicator is the difference of the Boolean conjunction of its pins with parity zero and the conjunction with parity one. The scalar denominator for affine gates is absorbed into the outer coefficients. There are at most \(b+s+d+g+O(1)\) distinct relevant wires after alias resolution; sharing them and building one conjunction and one parity gives the stated gate cost. Exactly the actual guesses obey all original relations on a given input. Summing therefore gives the original Boolean function pointwise, without changing its inputs. In particular, the multiplexers alone give an exact \(2^g\)-term expansion into mux-free atoms by intersecting each collapsed output with its control pins, costing \(O(g+1)\) binary gates per term.

Choose positive \(\beta,\gamma,\gamma'\) with \(\beta+\gamma+2\gamma'<\alpha_0\), and \(\eta>0\) with \(C\eta<\eta_0/2\). For sufficiently large \(b\), the additive \(C(b+d+g+1)\) cost fits in the remaining half of the gate budget when \(g\le\gamma b\) and \(d\le\gamma' b\). Likewise the extra factor two fits in the strict exponent margin. Distributing the expansion over a purported \(2^{\beta b}\)-term mixed representation would give an ordinary representation at the forbidden sparsity and gate budgets. This proves the mixed lower bound on the same infinite set of lengths for the same fixed \(W\). These constants work simultaneously for every fixed polynomial multiplexer cap \(b^K\); the pointwise expansion itself is independent of the cap. A uniform scan takes polynomial time in the supplied description length. Duplicate-free normalized signed predecessor lists have polynomial length in \(b\) for each fixed \(K\), with degree allowed to depend on \(K\).

The hierarchy conclusion is thus an algebraic corollary of the archived ordinary-atom SUM lower bound. The direct white-box counter below saves a separate enumeration of all \(2^d\) dense-output guesses by leaving those bits free in the counting instance. Its expansion has \(2^{g+d}\) terms, or at most \(2^{g+d+1}\) ordinary counts after splitting signs. Wu–Wang's Theorem 2 establishes a single-circuit result with a weighted symmetric-gate budget. The comparisons at the end distinguish the resources and claims. [Wu and Wang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02560)


**Counting lemma.** Consider a topologically ordered Boolean DAG with \(n\) primary inputs, \(s\) ordinary fan-in-two gates, and \(d\) affine XOR, AND, or OR gates of arbitrary fan-in in any order. Allow signed literals among the inputs to dense gates and a constant affine shift. An explicit list of wires describes each gate. Given the October manuscript's deterministic fixed-density width-three counting bound, the number of accepting primary assignments is computable in

\[
2^d\,\mathrm{poly}(L)\,2^{O(V/\sqrt{\log(V+2)})},\qquad V=O(n+s+d+1),
\]

where \(L\) includes the arity and wire-list length. The constants are absolute for the fixed gate basis. Each term has sparse clauses even if the number of wires in the given DAG is quadratic. [October manuscript, Theorem 1.1, fixed-density bit time and polynomial bit space](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/42907c9e999cc00d6abe7a50bfc1023250edd1b9/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/main_full_version.tex)

Expose each dense output as a new bit \(z_j\). Compute the ordinary wires from \((x,z)\) and pin the original output. Multiply by an indicator enforcing each exposed gate. For an affine gate whose consistency parity is \(p\), its indicator is \((1+(-1)^p)/2\). For a conjunction with product of input literals \(P\) and exposed bit \(z\), its indicator is \((1-z)+(2z-1)P\). A disjunction can use the conjunction identity with complemented inputs and complemented output. Expand the product over the two choices per gate. Within each term merge all literal pins by wire ID, discarding contradictions. Every remaining sign, including \(2z-1=(-1)^{1+z}\), contributes to a single parity on the skeleton wires. Compute its signed count as the difference of the two parity-pinned ordinary counts. One parity chain costs \(O(n+s+d+1)\) binary gates. A Tseitin variable per ordinary gate and parity-chain gate gives at most four width-three clauses per gate, and at most one consistent pin per wire. Every skeleton assignment has a unique gate extension. Divide the signed sum by \(2^{\#\text{affine gates}}\). For each original \(x\), just one vector \(z\) obeys all gate constraints, by topological induction. Integer arithmetic takes polynomially many bits in \(L\). This also handles zero inputs, empty conjunctions, and reused wires.

In particular, for fixed \(0<\delta<1/4\), products of at most four atoms on the same \(n\) physical inputs can be summed in \(2^{(1-\delta)n}\mathrm{poly}(L)\) when each atom uses at most \((1/4-\delta)n\) dense gates and \(c_\delta n\sqrt{\log(n+2)}\) ordinary gates, for sufficiently small fixed \(c_\delta\). Join the outputs by ordinary conjunction. The Fourier expansion costs at most \(2^{(1-4\delta)n}\), and choosing \(c_\delta\) small makes the residual counting exponent at most \(2\delta n\) for large \(n\).

**Three decoded labels.** There are absolute \(a,b,\beta>0\) and a language \(W\in E^{NP}\) such that, at infinitely many lengths \(t\), \(W_t\) has no exact real SUM of at most \(2^{\beta t}\) such Boolean atoms with at most \(at\) dense gates and \(bt\sqrt{\log(t+2)}\) ordinary gates per atom. Here \(W\) is the October hierarchy witness, based on \(H\in\mathrm{NTIME}(2^n)\setminus\mathrm{NTIME}(2^{n/2})\). The decoder on \(q=n+O(\log n)\) bits produces three labels padded to length \(t=Dn\) and signs with \(O(n)\) ordinary gates for a fixed \(D\). [October local decoder and hierarchy witness](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/42907c9e999cc00d6abe7a50bfc1023250edd1b9/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/quantitative-circuits.tex)

Suppose the representations exist eventually. Guess the atoms and coefficients on each unary length. For \(S\) Boolean atom columns, any exactly representable Boolean vector has rational coefficients with one positive denominator \(Q\) and integer numerators of \(O(S\log(S+1))\) bits each. Take independent columns, restrict to an invertible square row minor, and use Cramer's rule and Hadamard's inequality. The corresponding combination agrees on all rows since the target is in the column span. Denote the integer numerator sum on decoded label \(j\) by \(U_j\). For each of the three label maps separately check that the sum over clause indices of \(U_j^2(U_j-Q)^2\) is zero. Expand into at most polynomially many products of up to four atoms. This forces Boolean values on every occurring label. Next count the sum of the products of the three literal-falsity numerators, using \(U_j\) or \(Q-U_j\) according to the sign. Split into eight sign patterns and attach the decoder sign pins. The nonnegative sum is zero exactly when no indexed clause is violated. All occurrences of the same label get the same value, since one representation serves all three maps. Labels absent from clauses may be assigned arbitrarily. Completeness uses the true witness representation and soundness uses the resulting satisfying assignment. [Williams, quartic Boolean-valuedness check and SUM verification](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.09121)

Each expanded count uses at most four atom copies on at most three decoder label projections, plus \(O(n)\) ordinary decoder gates. Pick \(a,b\) small enough that the dense-gate expansion and October counting together cost less than \(2^{n/4}\) for large \(n\), allowing for fixed machine overhead inside the subexponential-counting exponent. Descriptions, arithmetic, guesses, and number of calls contribute \(\mathrm{poly}(n,S)\), of fixed degree. Then choose \(\beta\) small enough that the total runtime is below \(2^{n/2}\). Hardwire finite exceptional lengths. This contradicts the choice of \(H\).

**Live-arity limit.** The fixed-exponent NP conclusion in the Williams SUM framework uses specialization of a circuit evaluator with its description hardwired. In that simulation the ambient arity can be polynomial in an exponentially large source description. Our white-box counting bound charges the actual gates after specialization. Padding the input of any hard circuit until its ordinary gate count fits the ambient budget shows why specializing a description cannot in general restore the displayed gate bounds in the live arity. The three decoder maps above have ambient arity \(Dn\), which is precisely the bounded projection required for this application. The argument establishes no fixed-exponent NP consequence for this mixed DAG class. [Williams, Lemma 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.09121)

## Polynomially many multiplexers sharing a linear control budget

Fix an integer \(K\ge1\). An atom of arity \(b\) may also contain at most \(b^K\) ordinary 2:1 multiplexers, with at most \(g\) distinct syntactic control-wire IDs across them. Both data and control may come from earlier gates of any permitted type. Shared signals may control arbitrarily many multiplexers. The gate and wire list makes the counts and acyclicity uniformly checkable. Constants and signed controls may be normalized by swapping data ports for a negated control.

**Counting.** Partition assignments according to the values of all distinct controls. In each of the \(2^g\) cases scan the original DAG topologically. Give each multiplexer output the already mapped wire of its selected data predecessor. Map every other gate and its input references as in the previous construction. For each guessed control impose a pin on its actual mapped wire, including controls produced by multiplexers, ordinary gates and exposed dense gates. Use its mapped constant value if applicable. Coalesce all pins after alias resolution and discard conflicting cases. For fixed \(x\), the original evaluation determines a unique control vector, and conversely gate induction shows any valid pinned branch follows precisely that evaluation. This proves exact multiplicity even when a control is selected data for a later multiplexer or multiple controls acquire the same mapped wire. The reduced skeleton has no fresh variables for multiplexers. Its per-term variable cost is still \(O(n+s+d+1)\) and its expansion factor is now \(2^{g+d}\). Polynomial scanning of the original list is included in the clock. Consequently any fixed \(\delta>0\) with \(\delta<1/4\) permits four-atom live Sum-Product when each atom has \(g+d\le(1/4-\delta)n\) and the previous sufficiently small ordinary gate budget.

**Hierarchy consequence.** For each fixed \(K\), the same October language \(W\in E^{NP}\) has, at infinitely many lengths \(b\), no exact real sum of \(2^{\beta b}\) or fewer atoms each containing at most \(b^K\) multiplexers, \(\gamma b\) distinct control IDs, \(\gamma' b\) dense gates and \(\eta b\sqrt{\log(b+2)}\) ordinary gates, for some positive constants \(\gamma,\gamma',\eta,\beta\). Use the actual atom arity \(b=Dn\) in the preceding hierarchy proof. Four copies cost at most \(4D(\gamma+\gamma')n\) exponential branch bits. Choose these two constants first with slack below \(n/8\), then choose \(\eta\) so the October residual costs less than \(2^{n/8}\) for large \(n\). The descriptions and remapping costs are polynomial in \(n,S\) for fixed \(K\), including explicit input lists of dense gates that refer to multiplexers. Choose \(\beta\) after these polynomial degrees. The rational-coefficient reduction, quartic check, and three decoded labels are unchanged.

**Representation size.** A table on \(t=\lfloor2\log_2 b\rfloor\) address bits can be wired as a full multiplexer tree on constants with at most \(b^2-1\) multiplexers and only \(t\) distinct controls. Counting binary-gate descriptions shows some such tables require \(\Omega(2^t/t)=\Omega(b^2/\log b)\) ordinary binary gates, even with the remaining inputs available, since they can be fixed. This establishes strict growth relative to the ordinary binary budget at \(K=2\).

For strict growth over the preceding **mixed single-atom** model, take \(K=3\) and \(t=\lfloor3\log_2 b\rfloor\). The multiplexer tree now has fewer than \(b^3\) gates. On \(b\) inputs the old model has \(N=O(b\sqrt{\log b})\) gate and input IDs. An arbitrary signed dense predecessor list can be normalized using a constant-size code per ID for AND, OR, or parity. Each of the \(O(b)\) dense gates costs \(O(N)\) bits; ordinary gates collectively cost \(O(N\log N)\) bits. Thus there are at most \(2^{O(b^2\sqrt{\log b})}\) old circuits, while the tables give \(2^{\Theta(b^3)}\) different functions. The same comparison works for the single-circuit budgets in Wu–Wang's Theorems 1 and 2: even allowing \(O(N)\) bits per gate for both a signed input set and a symmetric truth table gives \(O(N^2)=o(b^3)\) description bits. This counting argument distinguishes single-atom representation power. It does not lower-bound the sparsity of sums for the table examples.

Wu and Wang give arbitrary-depth \(E^{NP}\) gate bounds for unbounded AND/OR and binary XOR, and for a binary base plus affine and symmetric exceptional gates, charging an arbitrary XOR once and an arity-\(k\) symmetric gate \(\log_2(k+1)\). Their theorem covers symmetric gates beyond the present basis. Here the additional resource is polynomially many 2:1 multiplexers with shared controls, together with an exact real SUM as the output representation.
