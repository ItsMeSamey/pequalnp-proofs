# Hamiltonian cycles as a projection of a positive read-twice CNF

For every field F choose K=F(t) with t distinct from 0,1,−1. One polynomial-time constructible family of positive CNFs P_N, independent of F and t, has clause width at most three and exactly two syntactic occurrences of each variable. Over K its canonical multilinear extension projects to the directed Hamiltonian cycle polynomial HC_N by substituting variables or field constants only. Each arc indeterminate occurs as the image of just one raw input variable. Take N≥2 with one input per loopless directed arc. The construction also works for a polynomial-size Boolean verifier of any selected family of incidence vectors, with the corresponding generating polynomial in place of HC_N.

For a Boolean function P on raw inputs with formal success parameters p_i its extension is the sum over Boolean assignments b of P(b) times the product of p_i for ones and (1−p_i) for zeros. This is multilinear and interpolates P. This representation establishes VNP membership for our uniform family by using the polynomial-size arithmetic evaluation of the CNF on Boolean assignments inside the exponential sum. Its degree in the summand is polynomial by using literal products within clauses and a product across clauses.

## A weighted independent-set calculation

Write R for the symmetric two-state interaction with rows (1,1),(1,0), indexed by 0,1. A graph with this interaction and occupied activities λ_v has partition sum over independent sets of their activity products. First build such a graph whose sum equals HC_N. Use a deterministic Boolean verifier of the arc vector that checks exactly one outgoing and incoming arc per vertex and all-pairs reachability. Pairwise exclusions and disjunctions implement the degree tests, and the Warshall recurrence implements reachability, all with O(N³) fan-in-two gates. Under the degree constraints, reachability is equivalent to having a single spanning directed cycle. Each cycle is represented by one arc vector. Encode the gate equivalences by bounded-width signed CNF, and pin the output to true. An accepted arc vector has exactly one satisfying extension to the gate variables.

Represent each Boolean variable by two activity-one vertices for its positive and negative literal. Join them by R and add a common neighbor of activity −1. Summing this neighbor cancels the assignment with both literals zero, while R forbids both one. For every signed clause add an activity −1 vertex adjacent to its literal vertices. Its sum equals one when the clause is true and zero otherwise. These mediator sums factor conditional on the literal states. For each arc e add a selector z_e adjacent only to the negative literal of e, with activity −(1−X_e). If e is absent the selector is forced empty. If e is present the selector sum is X_e. Hence each accepting vector contributes exactly the product of its selected arc indeterminates, including in characteristic two.

## Equality and degree reduction

Replace each vertex of degree d by an ordered path of d incidence copies with one free terminal at either end, attaching each original edge to its designated incidence copies. For d=0 there are just two terminals. Replace each link along this path by six R edges with five intervening activities −1,−1,1,−1,−1. With D_a=diag(1,a), direct multiplication gives R D_{−1} R D_{−1} R=diag(−1,1). Two such paths joined at an activity-one middle vertex implement the identity interaction in every characteristic. Conditional on the shared state of the copies, their occupied activities multiply. The expanded graph has cubic incidence copies, degree-one free terminals and degree-two equality internals. Its size is linear in the preceding graph's vertex and edge count. The following physical implementation assigns activities directly, without placing any nonconstant expression in a projection.

## Physical positive clauses

Use one positive clause per expanded vertex. Replace each expanded edge by a two-edge path with a new middle clause. For each physical edge put one fresh variable in both endpoint clauses and project it to success 1−t. Optionally put one fresh private input of failure parameter y=1−p in a clause of expanded degree at most two. Middle clauses receive none.

Expand the product of the clause indicators by inclusion-exclusion on failed clauses before summing raw assignments. A selected clause has sign −1. A physical edge pays failure weight t once if either endpoint is selected, and one otherwise. A private input pays y exactly when its clause is selected. Summing the middle selection with its sign gives entries 1−t², t−t², t−t², 0 in endpoint order 00,01,10,11. Thus every expanded edge implements (1−t²) D_q R D_q with q=t/(1+t). After extracting the constant (1−t²)^E for E expanded edges, each expanded vertex of degree a has effective occupied activity −q^a, multiplied by y when it has a private input. All these baseline activities are nonzero.

Give every equality internal a private input and choose its constant y to achieve its prescribed activity. Leave all incidence copies at baseline. Give each free terminal a private input. For an original graph vertex v let B_v be the product of all baseline activities of its copies, including both terminals and excluding equality internals. On the first terminal choose constant failure λ_v/B_v for an ordinary constant-activity vertex, and −1/B_v for a selector. On the second terminal choose failure one for an ordinary vertex. For a selector project its second terminal's raw success parameter directly to X_e, giving failure 1−X_e. In each constant case the raw projection is the field element one minus the stated failure. Equality contraction now gives exactly the original graph, with the one global scalar previously extracted.

Append a fresh positive unit clause twice on the same variable, and project that variable to c=(1−t²)^(−E). Its multilinear extension is the variable itself because the two identical clauses have the same Boolean truth value. For each singly used private input p append the clauses (p OR g) and (g) on a separate fresh g projected to one. At g=1 these clauses impose no condition, including when p maps to X_e, by multilinear interpolation in the remaining variables. Each private input and each guard now occurs twice. The physical edge variables already occur twice. Every clause has at most three literals. All graph orders and incidence orders can be fixed lexicographically. The entire topology, including the duplicated normalization unit, is independent of the field. Its raw input, clause and occurrence counts are O(N³). The substitutions above therefore give an ordinary p-projection.

## Field scope and prior work

If F has more than three elements, choose t in F. Over the two-element field use a root of T²+T+1, and over the three-element field a root of T²+1. The required extension has degree at most two, also after taking its compositum with any larger base field of the same characteristic. All displayed identities are polynomial or use only the specified nonzero denominators.

Using the usual VNP completeness of directed HC under p-projections gives completeness over K. Over either exceptional base field the construction supplies an extension-field projection. Polynomial-size base-field circuits for the CNF extensions would still yield polynomial-size base-field circuits for HC: substitute the extension constants, simulate each wire in a fixed basis of the extension with constant arithmetic overhead, and take the coordinate along 1 at the output. This is a circuit implication and supplies no direct scalar p-projection over the exceptional base field.

Hrubeš previously established all-field conditional arithmetic hardness for multilinear extensions of monotone width-two CNFs. Here the additional restriction is exactly two occurrences per input, with width at most three, and the displayed projection and its small-field qualifications are explicit.
