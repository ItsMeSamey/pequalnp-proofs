# Exact limits of averaging arbitrary private banks

Let D be a public binary affine surjection from raw share words to n secret bits. The projection may first specialize header and gate-address bits. Equivalently one may supply an embedding E(x,r) into the raw inputs with D(E(x,r))=x. All size claims below charge the explicit decoder, constants and interfaces against the bank's gate and footprint budgets.

**A bank fixed by every section.** For any Boolean circuit H of size s on n bits, a bank of size at most s+size(D) can expose the single output H(D(raw)). On every section its value is exactly H(x). This holds for randomized, nonlinear, or atom-specific choices of section, even with x-dependent probabilities, provided they decode to x with probability one. Conditional expectation over any such distribution therefore acts as the identity on the whole pullback of ordinary circuits of the permitted size. For block-parity clones D uses O(M) binary gates on M raw positions. Even the single output can read all positions, as with H equal to parity of the decoded bits. This refutes automatic support removal by averaging; it does not prove ordinary circuit lower bounds or deny other algorithms for H.

**Joint moments invisible in all proper marginals.** Fix p≥2. Suppose the common conditional share distribution provides p−1 independent fair bits r₁,…,rₚ₋₁ for every x, each readable from the shares by a charged circuit. Uniform sampling on fibers of an affine surjection supplies these as linear forms whenever the kernel dimension is at least p−1: take coordinate forms of the kernel in a basis extending a linear section. Use Boolean atoms A_j=r_j for j<p and A_p=H(x) XOR r₁ XOR ⋯ XOR rₚ₋₁. The last bank needs the decoder, H, the required mask circuits and p−1 binary XORs. Given x, the p-bit vector is uniform on the parity coset with parity H(x). Every proper marginal is uniform for every x: fixing k<p bits admits exactly 2^{p−k−1} completions. Yet

    E[Π_j A_j | x] = 2^{1−p} · 1[H(x) = p mod 2].

Independently resampling the atoms produces 2^{−p} for this product on every x. Equivalently the signed product of the signs (−1)^{A_j} is exactly (−1)^{H(x)} before resampling and has expectation zero afterwards. Taking H and its complement gives identical proper marginal data and complementary native product supports. The examples include the pair and triple moments used in gate verification. A chosen fixed transversal need not preserve fairness; the first obstruction holds on every such transversal without any fairness hypothesis.

An exact pointwise signed sum can of course be averaged term by term under one common conditional distribution. If the *new* readings are defined as those averaged atoms, products of their means can then be computed using independent replicas. This is a different quantity from the common-share moment above. One would still need a constructive method, within the verification clock, to obtain or integrate those means and certify their use on every relevant reading. The fixed-bank example retains arbitrary H in a single mean. Integer weights before averaging also allow fractional atom readings after averaging. These arguments do not establish the requisite new verification method.

**Shannon expansion at a deficit of t raw coordinates.** At any fixed raw length M, take any M-input bounded-fanin circuit C with s gates and choose t distinct input positions, 0≤t≤M. For each b in {0,1}^t substitute those constants into C, producing a private bank C_b with at most s gates, one nominated output, and at most M−t named raw coordinates including nominated wires. Use a fixed constant source for each substituted bit; removing unused inputs and renumbering leaves the original gate count unchanged when constants are primitive. If a bank is required to create constants with gates and retains a raw input, the two constants cost at most three additional NAND gates in total; a zero-input constant bank needs a constant convention. Give the atom an AND tree on the bank output and the t signed literals checking that the selected raw bits equal b. It uses exactly t direct raw literal occurrences, t binary AND nodes and 2t+1 tree nodes including leaves under the signed-literal convention. For t=0 the atom is the bank output. For each raw assignment exactly one guard passes. Consequently the unweighted integer sum of these 2^t atoms is exactly C, including on rejected assignments. Every coefficient is +1; a listed realization takes O(2^t(s+t+1) log(M+s+t+2)) description bits in an ordinary indexed binary-gate encoding. Constant simplification or sharing could reduce individual costs but is unnecessary.

In particular, for fixed positive d,k, at each sufficiently large M with integer t=t(M)≤floor(d log₂ M), every M-input circuit with at most M^k bank gates admits such a representation with at most M^d atoms, private footprint M−t, and t direct occurrences per atom; 2t+1≤M^k eventually. This implication is pointwise in M and nonuniform. It holds for each circuit family satisfying the stated size bound at those lengths. A language lacking such representations infinitely often would therefore lack size-M^k ordinary circuits at those same lengths. For a statement allowing *arbitrary* polynomial exponents in the representation, any P/poly language is included when the deficit is O(log M), by choosing the exponents to cover both its circuit family and 2^t. With fixed exponents this gives only the corresponding fixed-size implication. The expansion alone says nothing about larger deficits where 2^t exceeds the available sparsity, and it supplies no hardness claim about NP.

The three arguments apply to different operations: fiber averaging, joint share resampling and disjoint restriction by input literals. None establishes that an arbitrary representation of a padded evaluator has a mask certificate, a smaller exact bank after projection, or an affordable set of Shannon branches beyond the stated budget.
