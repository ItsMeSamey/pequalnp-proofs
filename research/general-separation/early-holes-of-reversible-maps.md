# Selecting an early missing output

Ordinary range avoidance accepts any missing output of a stretching circuit. LexAvoid asks for the first in lexicographic order. Here any of the first K holes is acceptable, with ranks counted from one. [Ordinary Avoid](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2025/210/revision/1/) · [LexAvoid, Definition 2.10](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/313/download/)

Let φ be a CNF on n≥1 variables and let v=(z,x,y) have component lengths n,n,t, totaling m=2n+t. Put E(v)=[z=1^n] AND φ(x). The permutation P(a,v)=(a XOR E(v),v) is its own inverse. The expanding circuit I(v)=P(0,v) is injective, inversion on the image discards the first bit, and one evaluation of E tests membership. A fixed missing string is one followed by m zeros. The construction uses O(|φ|+m) gates and wires including its interface. At most a 2^{-n} fraction of permutation inputs differ from the identity.

There is exactly one hole over each suffix v, namely (1 XOR E(v),v). If φ has least satisfying assignment x*, the first 2^t holes are precisely (0,1^n,x*,y) as y varies. If φ is unsatisfiable the first 2^t all start with one. Thus every answer of rank at most 2^t decides SAT and yields its least witness on YES inputs. Conversely, the least witness or an unsatisfiable flag suffices to return a valid answer. With t=n the permitted rank is 2^{m/3} and the perturbation fraction is at most 2^{-m/3}. With t=n² these become 2^{m−Θ(√m)} and at most 2^{-Θ(√m)}. Both reductions have polynomial output length, and the public hole solves ordinary avoidance throughout.

A polynomial-time reduction from this restricted ranked task to ordinary Avoid would reduce SAT to Avoid. The decision-reduction theorem of Ghentiyala, Li and Stephens-Davidowitz would then put SAT in coAM and collapse the polynomial hierarchy. This is a consequence of a proposed reduction. The construction proves NP-hardness for ranked search. [Primary decision-reduction theorem, revision 1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2025/210/revision/1/)

## Exact query frontier

Let H=2^m and let f be a hidden Boolean predicate on 0 through H−1, with f(0)=0 known and support size at most d≤H−1. For I(v)=(f(v),v), the public hole (1,0) solves ordinary avoidance. Evaluating the map, its reversible extension or inverse, or testing image membership uses at most one query to f.

For 1≤K≤H the exact deterministic worst-case query cost of rank at most K is zero when K>d, and H−K when K≤d. In the first case the public hole has rank at most d+1. Otherwise scan the first H−K nonzero indices in order. The first positive gives the first hole. If none tests positive, at most K−1 positives remain and the public hole works. Conversely, fewer than H−K queries on the zero predicate leave K unqueried nonzero indices. Marking those positive preserves the transcript and changes the required leading bit of every permissible answer.

For 1≤K≤d the bounded-error randomized query bound is Θ((H−1)/K). Sample O((H−1)/K) free indices uniformly. Return the leading-zero hole with least sampled positive suffix if any, or the public hole otherwise. With fewer than K positives either choice works. With at least K positives the sample hits one of the first K with probability at least two thirds for a suitable constant. For the lower bound couple the zero transcript with a uniform hidden K-subset of free indices. They require opposite leading bits, yet a q-query algorithm sees a difference with probability at most qK/(H−1). Its success guarantees force this probability to be at least 1/3.

The last obstruction persists for an easy family. Partition the free indices into floor((H−1)/K) disjoint intervals of length K and allow either none or one hidden active interval. Each probe on the zero transcript hits at most one candidate. With explicit endpoints, the interval predicate and its reversible extension have polynomial-time algorithms, and the first hole is easy to compute. For an affine predicate supplied by its coefficients, find the least input with f(v)=1 by linear feasibility on successive prefixes or detect that none exists. The query lower bound applies to evaluation access. The explicit CNF construction establishes a search reduction without an ordinary time lower bound for SAT.
