# What the fixed-exponent lower bounds leave open

Fix an ordinary circuit basis and a polynomially balanced, polynomial-time verifier for a fixed NP-complete decision language A. If A has no polynomial-time uniform family of polynomial-size circuits, then P differs from NP. A sufficient nonuniform condition is that for **this one A**, for every integer k there are infinitely many input lengths n at which every circuit for A requires more than n^k gates. For an NP-complete A this stronger condition is equivalent to NP being outside P/poly in the sense that NP contains a language without polynomial-size circuit families. Composition with any fixed polynomial-time reduction preserves polynomial-size circuits. Successively fixing witness bits gives polynomial-time witness recovery for Circuit-SAT from a uniform decision procedure.

The [private-bank theorem](private-bank-permuted-gate-headers.md) instead first fixes the support and size parameters and then produces an NP language. The [disjoint Shannon expansion](private-bank-averaging-obstructions.md) identifies an exact comparison class at a missing footprint of O(log n). Every polynomial-size ordinary family admits such representations when the atom-count and gate exponents may be chosen for that family. Conversely, for any family with polynomially many polynomial-size Boolean atoms, exact real coefficients for its Boolean target can be replaced, at each length, by a common denominator and signed numerators of polynomial bit length. Ordinary Boolean circuits evaluate the atoms, add the selected numerators and compare with the denominator. Thus at logarithmic deficit the union over polynomial exponents coincides with P/poly. Extending the private-bank lower bound to this regime while keeping a language dependent on k would still supply only fixed-exponent lower bounds.

Kannan proved that for each fixed k, some language in the intersection of the second existential and universal levels of the polynomial hierarchy lacks O(n^k)-size circuits. Under P=NP, these levels would collapse to P. Thus even the statement that **for every k some language in P lacks O(n^k)-size ordinary circuits** follows from P=NP. Packaging separate hard languages into one NP verifier would require one common polynomial clock. For example, straightforward padding of a verifier with clock n^{a_k} to a common clock N^C requires N at least n^{a_k/C} to cover the stated budget. A size-N^b circuit upper bound can then translate back only with an exponent at least b a_k/C in that estimate, before charging the reduction. Per-exponent lower bounds alone impose no bound on a_k/k that would give the required contradiction.
