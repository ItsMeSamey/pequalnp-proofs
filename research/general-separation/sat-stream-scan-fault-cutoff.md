# A finite SAT fault cutoff for deterministic multipass scans

Fix p≥1 sequential left-to-right passes over one read-only input, with rewinds from the end to the beginning. Between reads, before a rewind and after the final read, the machine may take arbitrarily many deterministic native steps. A public computable deadline D(L) at input length L returns zero if no answer has been produced within the allowed steps. The program cannot inspect elapsed time. Let q≥1 bound the number of possible internal configurations at each transfer between the two input halves or at a rewind. Include all retained control states, storage, register and head values that can affect the next segment, apart from the public transfer position and pass index. Local work and its duration must be determined by the entering configuration, the local input and the public positions. The same proof permits a separate transition rule on each pass.

Take n=1+2p⌈log₂(q+2)⌉. Use a canonical CNF integer encoding with one sign bit per literal and self-delimiting binary positive name and count fields of O(log(v+2)) bits for value v. For x,y in {0,1}ⁿ write the arity n, clause count 2n, then n unit clauses specifying x in increasing variable order and n more specifying y in that order. The header belongs to the first half. Each variable is forced to its indicated value, so the formula is satisfiable exactly when x=y. The word length B(n) and half boundary are independent of both bit strings. In particular B(n) can be computed by encoding the all-zero diagonal and measuring it, and B(n)=O(n log(n+2)).

Suppose every diagonal is accepted within D(B(n)). Divide each such accepting run into at most 2p segments, one for each contiguous half on each pass. Assign the rewind to the end of the second segment of that pass, and assign final native computation to the segment owning its input position. Record at each segment exit its transferred configuration or an accepting halt marker, then fill unused positions with a distinct padding marker. This yields a length-2p transcript over at most q+2 symbols. Since 2ⁿ>(q+2)^(2p), two distinct diagonal words (x,x) and (y,y) share a transcript, including the same accepting segment.

Ignore the external timeout momentarily. On (x,y) the first-half segments reproduce those on (x,x), and the second-half segments reproduce those on (y,y), by induction on the common entry and exit configurations. The reverse selection occurs on (y,x). This also covers an accepting halt before all p passes. Write T for the resulting unclocked native running times. Adding the segment durations gives

    T(x,y) + T(y,x) = T(x,x) + T(y,y) ≤ 2D(B(n)).

At least one crossed input therefore accepts before or at its deadline, even if the other times out. Both are unsatisfiable. If some diagonal had already been rejected or timed out, that diagonal was a wrong answer. This proves a wrong prediction at length B(n) independent of the numerical deadline.

For effective p, q, simulator and deadline, enumerate the finite set of 2^(2n) pairs, compare the simulated output to equality, and retain a wrong root F. To obtain a failed local SAT recurrence test below F, restrict the least occurring name to each bit, dropping satisfied clauses and falsified occurrences and keeping the declared arity. Child encodings never grow with the nondecreasing-length integer encoding. Check terminal truth on leaves and the OR of child predictions at internal nodes. Induction implies a failure somewhere below F, and enumerating its finite restriction tree finds one of length at most B(n). The depth is at most n. This is a computable length cutoff for the specified architecture. The exhaustive discovery time includes D on the tested lengths. [Local SAT refuter](sat-one-local-refuter.md)

The transfer simulation is the standard connection between multipass streaming and communication protocols. The sum-of-durations identity handles a common external timeout without treating elapsed time as a free message. A uniform exact SAT decider in this architecture must have enough passes and crossing configurations on the displayed family to avoid the pigeonhole inequality. A clock readable by the program must be included in the transferred information if it influences later work. The theorem gives no cutoff for unrestricted polynomial-time programs from their clock exponent alone. [Guha and McGregor, *Tight Lower Bounds for Multi-Pass Stream Computation via Pass Elimination*](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~mcgregor/papers/08-lis.pdf)

With the candidate program available for inspection, simulate at most 2^n diagonals and store their crossing transcripts in a dictionary, recording the originating word. Stop at a rejected diagonal or a repeated transcript. In the latter case at most two crossed simulations find a falsely accepted input. A rejected diagonal comes with the model x, so follow its satisfying branch from the zero root until a zero parent has a one child or the true leaf answers zero. On the falsely accepted cross, descend through one-valued children until reaching a one parent with two zero children or a false leaf answering one. Either descent costs O(n) additional candidate calls. In bit time, charge each simulation on a word of length s≤B(n) its actual simulation cost through halt or D(s), including transcript recording where used, plus polynomial work per diagonal for dictionary operations and polynomial work for constructing and checking the path. The tighter discovery bound requires visibility of crossing configurations. With only black-box bit replies the full pair enumeration above remains available.
