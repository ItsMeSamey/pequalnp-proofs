# Numerical evaluation of positive read-two CNFs over the ternary field

Let F be a positive CNF with m explicitly listed clauses, of unrestricted widths. Each variable occurs in at most two distinct clauses. Let P_F be the unique multilinear polynomial over F₃ agreeing with its Boolean value at every Boolean input. There is a uniform deterministic algorithm evaluating P_F at **every point of F₃ⁿ** in O((1+m+L)³) bit operations, where L is the explicit bit length of the incidence lists and the given point (or these data have been read and variables indexed). No division by a possibly zero intermediate field element is needed.

## Multilinear inclusion–exclusion

Let J_i be the set of clauses containing variable i. Duplicates within one clause do not add another incidence. Different clause records, even if identical, are different indices. Write y_i=1−x_i. Then, as a formal polynomial,

    P_F(x) = ∑_{S⊆[m]} (−1)^|S| ∏_{i : S∩J_i ≠ ∅} y_i.

Indeed on the Boolean cube a selected clause is violated if all of its variables are zero. Inclusion–exclusion excludes every violation. The product for simultaneous violations uses each involved variable once; its expansion in the x variables is multilinear. The values on the Boolean cube determine a unique such polynomial by repeated two-point interpolation. Thus this identity can now be evaluated on arbitrary field inputs. It is not the generally nonmultilinear product obtained by first writing each clause separately and multiplying.

Use s_j∈F₂ to indicate clause j lies in S. For y_i=0 the term can survive only if every spin in J_i is zero. Impose those pins. For y_i=1 the factor is always one. For y_i=−1 the exponent of its sign is the Boolean OR of its incident spins. This is zero, s_j, or s_j+s_k+s_js_k over F₂ according as there are zero, one or two incident clauses. A loop convention with the same spin twice gives s_j by idempotence. Add the inclusion–exclusion exponent ∑_j s_j and all these exponents modulo two, then substitute the pinned zero spins. Parallel incidences from distinct variables add their contributions modulo two. What remains is

    ∑_{s∈F₂^r} (−1)^{q(s)} ∈ F₃,

where r counts unpinned clause spins, including isolated ones, and q is a squarefree polynomial of degree at most two over F₂. An empty clause leaves an isolated spin with a nonzero linear coefficient and cancels the entire answer. With no clauses the sum is one. Unused input variables contribute no factor. These rules require no nonsingularity or nonvanishing promise.

## Exact elimination and bit cost

Keep a scalar factor in F₃, initially one. Whenever a cross term uv occurs, write q=uv+uA+vB+C, where A and B are affine forms in the remaining spins. Summing over u first kills the term unless v=A, in which case its multiplicity is two. Summing over v therefore replaces the two spins by scalar factor 2 and exponent C+AB. This identity is an equality of sums in F₃; the constant, affine and quadratic coefficients in the new exponent are computed in F₂ with z²=z. When no cross term remains, write q=c+∑ a_i s_i. The remaining sum is (−1)^c∏_i(1+(−1)^{a_i}). In particular a nonzero linear coefficient gives zero and each unconstrained zero-coefficient spin gives two. Multiply by the accumulated scalar. The same calculation is the quadratic Boolean exponential sum reduced modulo three.

The active quadratic coefficient matrix has at most m² bits. Given A=a₀+∑ a_i z_i and B=b₀+∑ b_i z_i, update each off-diagonal coefficient i<j by a_i b_j+a_j b_i, each linear coefficient by a₀ b_i+b₀ a_i+a_i b_i, and the constant by a₀ b₀. One pivot costs O(m²) bit operations, with at most floor(m/2) pivots. This bound is achievable by sequential bit scans: store a symmetric bit matrix with row delimiters, find a nonzero pair by scanning the upper triangle (or either off-diagonal half), extract the two coefficient rows, and stream the fixed binary vectors along successive rows for the outer-product update. Rewinding a length-m vector once per row costs O(m²). Use unary row/column markers for chosen coordinates; produce their one-hot strings by a scan of the chosen row and replay them with each row. Mark deleted variables inactive instead of physically shifting indexed records. This avoids paying a logarithmic address charge for every bit update. Linear coefficients and constants add only O(m) per step. Initialization can group incidences by variable using sequential sorting by their binary names, remove within-clause repetitions, and group the point values with those records. Even one scan of the dense matrix per actual quadratic contribution suffices within O((1+m+L)³): row/column marker construction and a streamed toggle cost O(m²) per contribution, of which there are O(L). In the standard indexed literal-occurrence model the same algebraic work is O(m³+L m²) bit updates after input parsing. The full encoding length qualification accounts for long variable identifiers and unused point coordinates.

## A polynomial arithmetic representative as a function on F₃ⁿ

For any fixed formula (or a uniformly generated family), specialize the uniform Boolean circuit simulation of the above algorithm to its fixed incidence data, with only the ternary point as input. A polynomial-time bit algorithm has polynomial-size Boolean circuits by the ordinary time-to-circuit simulation; this latter size need not be cubic. To supply two Boolean code bits for x_i∈F₃ use e_a(x_i)=1−(x_i−a)² for a=1,2. Fermat's identity in F₃ gives the codes (0,0),(1,0),(0,1) for 0,1,2. Simulate Boolean AND by multiplication and NOT by 1−z (or NAND by 1−zw); these gates preserve Boolean codes on every field input. If the algorithm returns the two one-hot bits b₁,b₂ for its answer, output b₁+2b₂. This polynomial-size division-free F₃ arithmetic circuit agrees with P_F on all of F₃ⁿ. It is constructed uniformly in the explicit data, and applies to every promised instance.

Precisely, the computed polynomial R satisfies R−P_F∈(x₁³−x₁,…,x_n³−x_n). Successive monic division in each variable gives a remainder of individual degrees at most two; a zero function on F₃ⁿ with these degree bounds is zero by induction and the univariate root bound. No small formal circuit for the remainder follows from the simulation. Agreement on F₃ⁿ does not give evaluation on arbitrary extensions or symbolic indeterminates.

## An explicit formally hard subfamily

The numerical algorithm above is unconditional. For the formal contrast, use the November manuscript's superpolynomial formal arithmetic circuit lower bound for the permanent over F₃, covered by its characteristic-different-from-two hypothesis. [Pinned source and model](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/92b516ef8e2abf66c3db7aa31438d3010bc7956f/preprints/Circuit-lower-bounds-for-Hamiltonian-cycle-and-the-permanent-November-24-2026/build/intro.tex)

Start with K_{r,r}. Replace each vertex by a path on 2r−1 vertices, putting the r alternating port vertices on the original vertex's side of the bipartition. The other r−1 path vertices have no external edges. Reattach each original edge at its own port at each end. This explicit graph has maximum degree three and polynomially many edges and vertices. In a perfect matching of the new graph exactly one port per gadget is matched externally since each of the r−1 nonports must match a distinct port internally. Conversely, deleting the chosen port leaves two even paths, each with a unique perfect matching (including empty paths). Thus original perfect matchings extend uniquely.

Put a Boolean variable on each edge and the positive OR of incident edge variables at each vertex. Width is at most three and every variable occurs in two clauses. Write N for the number of vertices of the new graph. A Boolean assignment is accepted iff the selected edges cover every vertex. Fewer than N/2 edges never suffice; exactly N/2 suffice precisely for perfect matchings. In the unique multilinear extension, the coefficient of a squarefree monomial on S is ∑_{T⊆S}(−1)^{|S|−|T|}F(1_T). Therefore its degree-N/2 part is exactly the perfect-matching enumerator, with coefficient one for each matching. Substituting 1 on internal edges and the corresponding matrix variable on each external edge yields the formal permanent of order r. A polynomial-size ordinary division-free circuit computing the full multilinear polynomial formally would yield a polynomial-size circuit for this homogeneous component by computing all component degrees up through N/2 at each gate, using convolution for products. The projection would contradict the cited formal bound. Consequently this same family has polynomial-size arithmetic circuits for its values at **all** ternary points while its formal multilinear polynomial requires superpolynomial arithmetic circuit size under the cited theorem. The evaluation algorithm and the distinction between these models do not rely on any Boolean complexity-class separation.
