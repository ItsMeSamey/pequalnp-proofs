# Which degree component survives Boolean evaluation?

Let Tour test whether a Boolean N-by-N directed edge matrix is itself one spanning cycle. Write M for its unique multilinear extension over a field F. Subset inversion gives HC_N as its degree-N component, since Tour rejects every support below N and at size N accepts exactly the directed spanning cycles. The November paper gives a quantitative degree-component border lower bound. Lean's corollary12 proves the eventual superpolynomial exact lower bound. In the quotient by the equations x²=x, the coefficient at the squarefree monomial on S is the sum in F of the coefficients at *all* monomials whose support equals S. Every such monomial reduces to the same squarefree monomial. Uniqueness follows from the unitriangular subset-evaluation matrix. The original degrees may be arbitrarily high, and the coefficient sums may cancel. Already over the two-element field the verifier has a polynomial-size arithmetic circuit agreeing at every field point, while the degree-N slice of its canonical representative has the paper's superpolynomial formal lower bound.

The paper computes formal homogeneous components up through the desired degree by convolution, before specialization, and its parameterization includes off-target monomials. Even the complete data through degree N do not determine the canonical degree-N slice on the cube. Let P arithmetize Tour on the cube in polynomial size. It has constant term zero. Let h be the smallest power of two greater than N. Substitute the h-th power of each variable in P, spending at most N² ceil(log₂(N+1)) squarings. The resulting polynomial agrees with both P on the cube and the zero polynomial in every formal degree through N. Yet their canonical multilinear degree-N slices are HC_N and zero, respectively for the powered circuit and zero. For any homogeneous degree-N Q with v gates, add Q(X)−Q(X^h) at cost at most 2v+2 further gates using those powers. The powered circuit now has Q as its formal degree-N component and still agrees with Tour on the cube. All identities hold in every characteristic. ## A sufficient condition for lifting

Take an s-gate arithmetic circuit P for Tour's cube values. Supply a finite exhaustive decision tree with L leaves. Each internal node fixes a raw coordinate to zero or one, and a path never fixes the same coordinate twice. At every leaf require the original circuit after substituting all path constants to have a formally multilinear output in the remaining variables. A checkable sufficient condition is disjoint syntactic variable sets for the operands of every surviving product after substitution and sound constant folding. Propagating sets takes polynomial time per leaf. Semantic multilinearity alone is a promise.

Copy the restricted circuit at each leaf. At a node querying x, join child circuits as P₀+x(P₁−P₀), costing three binary gates. Both children omit x, so the parent remains formally multilinear and agrees with the verifier on its entire subcube. The resulting circuit computes M with at most Ls+3(L−1) gates. Building it takes time polynomial in the displayed tree and circuit descriptions. It uses no homogeneous-component extraction.

Hence, in every fixed characteristic and at every sufficiently large N, the November theorem gives L(s+3) ≥ exp(c(log N)²/(log log N)²). If fixing one common set of k raw coordinates under all 2^k assignments makes the residual circuits syntactically multilinear, take L=2^k. For polynomial s, this forces k = Ω((log N)²/(log log N)²). This elementary expansion bounds a particular way of lifting the cube computation. The original unrestricted Boolean verifier remains polynomial-time. The corresponding implication for the permutation-matrix verifier uses the permanent lower bound outside characteristic two.
