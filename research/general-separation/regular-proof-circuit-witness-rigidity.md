# Sparse witness rigidity against regular two-sided proof circuits

A binary fan-in-two circuit V(a,w) with s gates has two output wires denoting acceptance and value. For each label a of length ℓ, require exactly K≥1 strings w of its fixed proof width to be accepted, all with the same value f(a). K may depend on the circuit and input length and may be exponential. The gate bound counts the jointly shared circuit once. Unused proof coordinates can be deleted: if d are unread this divides every multiplicity by 2^d and preserves f. At most 2s+2 different proof bits are read, counting the two direct outputs even if they are primary wires. Renumber them consecutively. No bound on an originally declared but unread proof space is needed.

Fix the stronger unary hierarchy language H* and its bounded-incidence indexed clause reduction, with q,ℓ=n+O(log n), a uniform O(n)-gate forward decoder and a polynomial-time inverse incidence list on every label. The occurrence-sort construction supplies this reduction from the canonically indexed JMV instance. At infinitely many YES lengths, every satisfying table and every function represented by such a V with at most η n sqrt(log₂ n) gates disagree on more than floor(2^n/n^D) distinct occurring labels, for some fixed η>0 and integer D. Constants are simultaneous across the circuits, their proof widths, multiplicities and tables. **Certification on every branch.** Guess a capped ordinary binary circuit, proof width p≤2s+2 and the capped sorted correction list. Define k(a) as the number of accepted p-bit proofs. Let N=2^ℓ. By exact counting of acceptance on (a,w) obtain M1=Σ_a k(a). Reject unless M1 is a positive multiple of N and set K=M1/N. A second count on (a,w,w') requires both accept; independent proof blocks and a shared label give M2=Σ_a k(a)^2. Reject unless M2=NK². Then Σ_a(k(a)−K)²=0 over the integers, forcing equality at every label, including unused labels. A third count requires both accept and their value wires differ; reject unless zero. These three global tests certify that the guessed relation defines a single total Boolean function f. There is no local promise on a guessed machine. These tests also work at p=0 and K=1.

**Baseline count.** On (i,w1,w2,w3), decode the active flag and three signed labels from i, require activity and acceptance of all three calls, and require that each signed literal formed from its value wire is false. For each bad active ID this has exactly K³ proof triples. For every other ID it has none. Give repeated labels separate proof blocks as well. Divide the resulting integer count by K³ to recover the unweighted violation count N0. The division is exact following the global tests. Gate truth-table Tseitin encodings have unique extension for each declared primary assignment and require at most 4g+1 width-three clauses on V₀=(number of primary bits)+g variables for a g-gate predicate. Retain unused primary variables in the declared count, or explicitly multiply by their powers-of-two factors. The largest query has q+3p+3s+O(n)≤9s+O(n) variables; the other queries have at most ℓ+2p+2s+O(1). Each has density at most five. All exact arithmetic, including products of moments and the division, uses poly(n+s) bit operations since the counts themselves have O(n+s) binary digits. **Patches and clock.** Enumerate the union of active IDs incident to a listed corrected label, sort it and deduplicate globally. For each of its three positions in each such ID, guess exactly p bits, evaluate V, and reject unless accepted. Its output value is the old bit regardless of the selected proof, both on zero and one labels. Merge the records against the sorted correction list on full labels to obtain the new bits and sort back by (ID,position). Count U and U' old and new violated IDs on this union. Accept iff N0−U+U'=0. Unaffected IDs retain their original status. Soundness on arbitrary guesses follows from the global tests and the single table defined by patching f. A close satisfying table supplies the circuit, corrections and all local certificates because K>0 everywhere. The construction imposes no deterministic algorithm for finding those proofs.

For the exact bit-clock poly(v)2^{Av/sqrt(log₂(v+2))}, choose rational η' with 9Aη'<1. Cap s by floor(η'n floor(sqrt(floor(log₂ n)))) and take 0<η<η' to absorb rounding. All four counting queries together cost at most 2^{(9Aη'+o(1))n}. The guessing, inverse enumeration, sorting, proof writing, evaluation and scans cost (1+t)poly(n) on every branch under t=floor(2^n/n^D). The polynomial degree can be chosen independent of fixed D since t≤2^n and p,s≤poly(n); computing the cap is a separate polynomial startup. Enforce all syntax, widths and list sizes with bounded reads and reject nonunary inputs. Choosing fixed D larger than the overhead degree plus two makes the full clock o(2^n/n). Eventual existence of a close pair at every YES length would decide H* within the excluded time after finite hardwiring. This proves the simultaneous statement.

On the uniform ℓ-bit cube, the error is at least 2^{n−ℓ}/n^D, an inverse polynomial. The single lex-first witness-bit language in E^NP inherits this conclusion using the monotone padding of the sparse-patch lemma. Fixing the extra address bits in a regular proof circuit preserves K and per-address agreement; average over fixed paddings to transfer the bound. Ordinary circuits embed with p=0. This enlarges the class excluded by the bound without asserting a strict circuit-class separation or NP membership of the witness language. In comparison, the earlier [SUM verification](np-sum-formula-projection.md) checks guessed linear combinations through products of evaluations. Here the second moment certifies a globally constant, unknown proof multiplicity; three independent proofs permit exact removal of its weight from each bad-clause count.
