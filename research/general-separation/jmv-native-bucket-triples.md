# Quadratically many prefix triples for the native JMV clause addresses

Use the unary hierarchy language H and time allowance T=2^n poly(n) in the [October quantitative circuit proof](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/42907c9e999cc00d6abe7a50bfc1023250edd1b9/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/quantitative-circuits.tex). Its Jahanjou–Miles–Viola decoder maps q=n+O(log n) bits to three variable labels and their signs. We use the field order in the proof of JMV Theorem 1. Concatenate fields in the order below with fixed widths for each n. Keep each region's native fields at the front of its label payload, padding on the right with zeroes to the common width, then on the right to Dn bits. A fixed dummy label and constant gates may be reserved explicitly. Unused region or finite gadget codes may be assigned constant-gate clauses. This specifies the variable-address convention; the hierarchy language is unchanged by this padding. No invariance of a chosen prefix partition under arbitrary permutations of variable addresses is asserted. [JMV, §§2–5, particularly pp. 12–18](https://www.ccs.neu.edu/home/viola/papers/explicit.pdf)

Fix 0<β<1/4, b=floor(βn) and B=2^b. A variable's bucket is the first b bits of its padded label. Let K count the ordered bucket triples actually output by the decoder, ignoring literal signs. **K=O(B²).** This holds independently of satisfiability. Constants below depend only on the fixed machine and the chosen tags.

## Native labels and the gates with three distinct variables

The decoder's input consists of a gate label g=(Region,Label), a two-bit clause selector and a one-bit control. The outer region has constant width. JMV lists four regions, for local verification circuits, input-bit fetching, sorting circuits, and raw configuration bits. Only the ordinary AND-gate clauses can contain three distinct labels. A NOT, Copy or input-wiring clause repeats one of its two labels. Constants, the dummy input clause, and the control-one forced-output clause repeat one label. These clauses yield at most 3B² ordered bucket triples, regardless of how a cross-region edge changes its address. Raw configuration bits are primary inputs. The bit-fetching region has only Copy and constant gates by JMV Theorem 11, including at its shift and reset transitions.

* In a local verification circuit, Label=(i,j,ℓ). The copy number i has log T+O(log n) bits, j selects one of constantly many circuits, and ℓ is the internal label of O(log log T) bits. A local AND has both children within the same copy (i,j); the exceptions in JMV's child rule are x-Oracle and Input gates, which use the repeated-label clauses above. Hence all three prefixes agree for sufficiently large n. This contributes at most B triples.
* In a sorting circuit, Label=(j,ℓ), where j has constant width. For a non-input gate ℓ=(t,w,I,s,d), with t=00, row w of length log T+O(log n), column I, within-configuration position s, and constant-size bit-switch gate code d. The first-input label is (01,w,s), the control-input label is (10,w,I), and a reserved constant uses t=11. The row occurs immediately after the same two-bit tag in all three nonconstant formats. The b-bit bucket ends inside the row for all sufficiently large n. The switch child rule in the proof of Theorem 7 lists four cases: a gate of the same bit switch; an output gate of a preceding switch; a first-input configuration bit or the fixed zero; and a second-input control bit. The first case preserves w. In the second case the child row is a cyclic left shift of w or of w with its leading bit flipped. In the third and fourth cases the row is copied, unless the address is constant. The first-input connection out of the sorting region is attached to an Input gate and already counted as a two-label clause. Thus the parent's prefix and the two children's prefixes are fixed by at most b+O(1) initial row bits, the wrap bit if needed, and a constant choice of header and the two child cases. The precise order of the two children and the placement of the gate in the clause multiply the count by at most a constant. This contributes O(B) triples, even if every child-case combination is included. Any remaining suffix fields read to determine the appropriate child case select from this same constant list. The proof uses neither the number of possible full rows nor the size of a truth table for the switch.

These cases prove the bound. The NC⁰ locality conclusion of JMV by itself would be insufficient: on q≥3b bits a projection decoder can copy three disjoint b-bit chunks into the three label prefixes and pad remaining address bits by zero. All B³ bucket triples occur there even with locality one. This control is not claimed to be a JMV decoder for the hierarchy language.

## Finding the reachable triples

Traverse the binary trie of the 3b output prefix bits in fixed order. Test extension of a proposed trie node by asking whether some q-bit index gives its specified prefix bits. This is one polynomial-description ordinary CircuitSAT query using the explicit decoder. Only extendible nodes have their children visited, so at most O(1+bK) feasibility tests suffice. Equivalently the constant-support property gives a deterministic implementation from the October fixed-density theorem. For each specified output bit forbid every wrong pattern on its at most w=O(1) input coordinates using at most 2^w clauses of width at most w. There are O(n) clauses and q=Θ(n) declared coordinates. Constant functions, inconsistent requirements and short clauses can be handled directly or padded. The October fixed-density exact count tests the resulting instance in 2^o(n) bit time and polynomial workspace. Its primary theorem permits each fixed width, using width at least two. The trie therefore costs K·2^o(n) time without deciding any succinct hierarchy instance.

## Clause verification with sharded witnesses

Suppose ordinary CircuitSAT on explicit gate lists of bit length L is decidable deterministically in O(L^e) time on the multitape model, increasing e to at least one if necessary. At each sufficiently large YES length suppose there is a satisfying variable assignment represented by a separate ≤ℓ-gate binary circuit for each b-bit bucket on the remaining Dn−b bits. Guess all B circuits once, in bucket order, with fixed padded record length O((ℓ+n)log(ℓ+n+2)) per circuit. Verify their encodings. On each reachable ordered triple form one circuit on the original q index bits: evaluate the decoder, require precisely the named three prefixes, evaluate the three selected shard circuits on their respective suffixes, and test that the three signed literals are all false. Accept only if all these circuits are unsatisfiable. A passing guess defines one simultaneous assignment on all variable labels. Every actual clause has its triple in the enumeration. Thus some guess passes at each YES length under the stated representation hypothesis and none passes at a NO length.

Each test uses O(n+ℓ) gates, since the constant-support outputs have an O(n)-gate decoder. There is no scan of all B shard records per test. Write three requests per reachable triple containing the bucket, triple number and literal position. Merge-sort by bucket on a fixed number of sequential tapes, join against the ordered guessed descriptions, and write one copy of a matching record per request. A record may be rewound on its own work tape at linear cost in its length for each copy. Merge-sort the materialized records by triple number and literal position. Ordinary bottom-up tape mergesort costs the data volume times a polynomial in the logarithm of the number of records and the index width; records can be padded to common length and sorted by copying their payload as streams. Circuit construction then reads the three records consecutively. The two sorts, join and initial guess take (B+K)(ℓ+n) poly(n+log(ℓ+2)) bit time, within the stated exponential clock.

For ℓ≤2^(σn) the total clock is bounded by

    2^((2β+eσ)n+o(n)).

Choose positive σ with 2β+eσ<1/2. At all sufficiently large lengths this is within 2^(n/2), contradicting the choice of H if the small-shard hypothesis held at all sufficiently large YES lengths. Therefore, under the specified SAT clock, **at infinitely many YES lengths every satisfying assignment has some bucket whose restricted truth table requires more than floor(2^(σn)) gates**. In particular this rules out witnesses with B subexponential-size shards at those lengths. Combining such shards as a multiplexer would use 2^(βn+o(n)) gates in total, but a large unrestricted circuit need not decompose into small shards, and the conclusion only concerns this fixed native prefix partition. H is used through its hierarchy separation and its original local reduction; no completeness property of H is required.
