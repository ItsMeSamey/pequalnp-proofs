# The clause-width threshold for multilinear extensions of read-twice CNFs

Every signed CNF of width at most two in which each variable occurs at most twice has a linear-size arithmetic circuit for its unique multilinear extension over every field. At width three there is a polynomial-time constructible family of monotone CNFs with exactly two occurrences per variable whose multilinear extensions have a formally hard homogeneous component. For every sufficiently large integer M the family has a formula with 2M clauses and Θ(M) variables. Outside characteristic two, the degree-M component has degree-component border size at least exp(c(log M)²/(log log M)²) for an absolute c>0 and a length threshold depending only on the characteristic. This second conclusion uses the November 24 permanent theorem. Arithmetic circuits have unrestricted depth and fanout, binary addition, subtraction and multiplication, and free variables and field scalars. ## Width at most two

Give assignment bit b of variable i weight w_i(1)=x_i or w_i(0)=1−x_i. Sum the products of these weights over all satisfying assignments. Each variable occurs once in each product, and evaluation at a Boolean point selects exactly its assignment. This gives the unique multilinear extension, since the evaluations of squarefree monomials on Boolean supports form a unitriangular matrix.

Delete tautological clauses and simplify repeated literals. An empty clause yields zero. Regard each remaining binary clause as an edge between its two variables and attach unary clauses as tests at vertices. The resulting multigraph has maximum degree two. On a path, keep two partial sums indexed by the last bit. To append a vertex with bit b, sum the previous states whose bits satisfy the intervening clause with b, multiply by w_i(b), and zero states violating the unary tests. Initialize the first vertex with its two permitted weights. For a cycle, fix its first bit in each of two passes and impose the closing clause at the end. This includes the two parallel edges on a pair of variables. Isolated vertices use their permitted weight sum, and an unused variable contributes one. Multiply the component results. This uses O(1) gates per variable, clause and edge, independent of the number of monomials in the expanded output. Indeed a conjunction of disjoint positive binary clauses has three monomials per factor and exponentially many after expansion.

## Width three

Given M≥1, choose the largest n with 2n²−n≤M. Begin with the complete bipartite graph on n vertices per side. Replace each vertex by a path of 2n−1 vertices alternating n ports of its original color with n−1 spacers of the other color. In each path index the ports by the opposite original vertices. Join the two corresponding ports for each original edge. The resulting simple bipartite graph has 2n²−n vertices of each color and maximum degree three. Add M−(2n²−n) disjoint single-edge components. This deterministic construction takes polynomial time in M. Its new graph B has M vertices of each color, n=Θ(√M), and 5n²−4n+M−(2n²−n)=Θ(M) edges.

Introduce a Boolean variable per edge and one positive clause per vertex requiring some incident edge. Each variable occurs exactly twice. Its satisfying edge sets are the edge covers. A cover requires at least M edges, and a cover of size exactly M is a perfect matching. By subset inversion the multilinear coefficient on a set S is the alternating sum of the Boolean evaluations on subsets of S. Thus coefficients of degree below M vanish, and at degree M the coefficient is one precisely on the perfect matchings. The resulting homogeneous polynomial is the weighted perfect-matching polynomial of B.

On every replacement path, the n−1 spacers must match to distinct ports, leaving exactly one port matched externally. Conversely, deleting any one port leaves two even paths, each with a unique perfect matching, including the empty path. The external edges of any perfect matching therefore specify an original permutation, and every permutation has exactly one internal completion. Giving external edges the independent variables X_ij and specializing all other edge weights to one projects the degree-M polynomial to the dense permanent of order n.

For a size-s degree-component border representation, first extract the complete degree-M layer of each approximating circuit. Homogeneous addition and product convolution through degree M cost O((M+1)²(s+1)) gates, regardless of the other output degrees. Specialization on this isolated finite coefficient space is linear, so it maps the target into the closure of the projected approximants. Taking their degree-n components gives a degree-component border representation of the dense permanent with the same gate bound. The November theorem, n=Θ(√M), and absorption of the polynomial overhead give the stated bound after decreasing the absolute constant. It also bounds exact circuits for the whole multilinear extension. In characteristic two this degree-M component equals the determinant of the weighted bipartite adjacency matrix and has polynomial-size exact arithmetic circuits. The Boolean CNF itself has linear evaluation cost in every case. The edge-cover interpretation of monotone read-twice CNFs is classical. Dagum and Luby prove exact counting hardness for varying 3-regular bipartite graphs. Here the claim concerns the formal coefficient layer for one explicitly specified weighted host at each order M.
