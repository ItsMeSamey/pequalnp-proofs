# Deterministic parity queries for hidden Cartesian products
Let C have n inputs and s bounded-fanin gates. On nonempty support assume its solutions are a Cartesian product on unknown disjoint blocks with nonempty factor supports S_j. Given only C and K0 bounding K=sum_j(|S_j|-1), the following deterministic parity-CircuitSAT reduction recovers an ordinary satisfying word or decides emptiness. Without the promise all positive outputs remain sound.

Try the first nK0+1 primes. Fix one word per factor. Regard each block word as a binary integer in its original positions, with all other bits zero. Each nonzero difference from its marked word has magnitude below 2^n, hence fewer than n distinct prime divisors. A common prime p therefore separates every marked word within its factor modulo p. Let w_i=2^i mod p and D(x)=sum_i w_i x_i as an ordinary integer. In every factor the marked exponent D is unique since equality implies congruence modulo p. Its GF(2)[Y] polynomial sum_x Y^{D(x)} is nonzero. The product is nonzero in this integral domain, and its coefficient at v is exactly the parity of satisfying words with D(x)=v. Thus enumerate p and v=0,...,n(p-1) and query parity of C with D=v. No field construction is needed. On an odd bucket self-restrict its input bits to keep odd parity, costing at most n more calls and no more gates by substitution. Verify the result directly.

The maximum prime is O((nK0+2)log(nK0+2)) by the standard nth-prime estimate; sieve up to successive doublings until enough primes. Time outside oracle calls and query count are polynomial in n,K0+1 and gate description size. Put L=ceil(log2(p_max+1)) and assume L=O(log^2(n+2)). Partition coefficients into b=max(1,floor(log2(n+2)/2)) bit chunks. For one chunk group inputs having the same b-bit coefficient. Compute group popcounts with O(n) gates total via carry-save addition and binary final sums. Multiply each count by its b-bit constant and sum the results using O(2^b polylog n)=O(n) gates. Combine shifted chunk results in O(ceil(L/b)(L+log n)) gates. Total query gates s+O(n ceil(L/log(n+2))), original n inputs, including equality. Polynomial K0 therefore costs O(n) extra gates; K0=2^{o(log^2 n)} costs o(n log n). The support may have 2^{Theta(n)} words at K0=O(n), e.g. independent choices 00 or 11 on n/2 pairs. Sharing variables across factors does not justify this proof. No unrestricted CircuitSAT algorithm or standard complexity separation follows.

During witness extraction a substituted input is removed from the parity oracle's input list. If every query must have exactly n primary inputs, conjoin the fixed literals instead, adding O(n) gates already within the stated overhead. Free unused input bits would multiply the count by two.
