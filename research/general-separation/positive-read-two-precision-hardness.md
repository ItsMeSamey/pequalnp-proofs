# Variable precision in positive read-twice CNF evaluation

Let Q be an explicit positive CNF with at most two occurrences per variable. Let P be its unique multilinear representative over the integers. The input gives Q, its m success coordinates and a precision k≥1 specifying the ring Z/(3^k). Measure bit complexity in the description length plus k, including time to produce the residue. Evaluation takes polynomial time for every fixed k. A deterministic time bound polynomial jointly in description length and k exists if and only if all #P functions are polynomial-time computable. The lower implication already holds for width at most three, two distinct clause occurrences per variable, and the common success coordinate 1/2 interpreted in the ring.

## Fixed precision

Inclusion–exclusion introduces a Boolean spin per clause, with weight (−1)^s. Each failure weight y=1−x contributes y if any incident spin is set and one otherwise. Its two-spin signature in truth-table order is (1,y,y,y). Modulo three the possibilities y=0,1,−1 give respectively an indicator that both spins are zero, a constant one, and the sign (−1)^(u+v+uv) with exponent over F₂. These and the unary factors are affine signatures. For fixed k the set of local signatures is finite. Guo, Huang, Lu and Xia’s Lemma 4.1 gives polynomial time for this weighted Boolean constraint system over an odd prime power. Its proof splits each factor into a tractable lift and a discrepancy divisible by the prime. The companion gives the explicit valuation-sensitive bound, including zero weights and parallel incidences. At literal integer success values in {0,1,2}, its integer sign contraction works at all precisions. [Guo et al., Lemma 4.1](https://homepages.inf.ed.ac.uk/hguo/papers/modcsp.pdf) · [Explicit algorithm](positive-read-two-prime-power.md)

## One half-weight evaluation at linear precision

Given a graph G with m edges, introduce a variable per edge and one positive clause per vertex requiring some incident edge. Its satisfying supports are the edge covers. At the common residue x=2⁻¹ every assignment has weight 2⁻ᵐ. Choose k=m+1 so that 3^k>2^m. Multiply one evaluation by 2^m in the ring. Its least nonnegative residue is the exact integer edge-cover count.

Exact edge-cover counting is #P-complete on simple planar bipartite graphs with degree two on one side and three on the other. Every variable of the resulting formula occurs twice in distinct clauses. One evaluation suffices per input to that counting problem. The primary planar Holant dichotomy also supplies the cubic multigraph version, by placing ternary OR at every vertex and a binary equality at every subdivided edge. The signature pair [1,0,1] and [0,1,1,1], equivalently its simultaneous reversal [1,0,1] and [1,1,1,0], lies in the planar-hard class. Suppressing the equality vertices can create parallel edges or loops. The simple planar degree-two/three theorem supplies the stated width and incidences without either complication. [Edge-cover Theorem D.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.03201#page=34) · [Cai, Lu and Xia, Section 6](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~jyc/papers/dichotomy.pdf)

## Cubic perfect matchings at polynomial precision

Independently, take a simple cubic bipartite graph with M vertices on each side and m=3M edges. In its incident-edge CNF every clause has exactly three literals. Write a_j for the number of edge covers of size j. At least M edges are required, and a cover of size M pairs the vertices. Thus a_M counts perfect matchings. Put r=m+1, B=3^r and k=r(M+1). At the common success residue x=B/(1+B) the denominator is a unit. Multiply one evaluation by (1+B)^m to get Σ_j a_j B^j modulo B^(M+1). Terms below j=M vanish and those above are multiples of the modulus. Since 0≤a_M≤2^m<B, dividing the least nonnegative residue by B^M gives a_M exactly. This reduction uses polynomial precision and exact counting hardness for cubic bipartite perfect matchings. [Dagum and Luby](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0304397592902347)

## Counting upper bound

Represent each x_i and 1−x_i by its least nonnegative residue. A nondeterministic machine guesses a Boolean assignment and rejects unless it satisfies Q. For each bit it then guesses a nonnegative integer below the selected weight, using a fixed number of binary guesses sufficient for any residue and rejecting out-of-range guesses. The number of accepting paths is the sum over satisfying assignments of the products of the selected integer weights. Reducing this count modulo 3^k gives P(x). The machine uses O(mk) extra guesses. This yields the upper bound in FP with a #P oracle and the converse implication, with k charged explicitly.
