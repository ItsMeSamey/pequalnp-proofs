# Additive verification for bounded-incidence clause decoders

Fix a unary H* in NTIME(2^n) outside NTIME(2^{n−ceil(log₂ n)}). Such a language exists because the two powers of two have polynomial-time computable binary representations and unary outputs constructible by repeated doubling in total time proportional to the output length. The lower clock at n+1 divided by the upper at n tends to zero. Altering finitely many lengths to make clocks positive has no effect. [Williams, unary Žák theorem 2.1](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~ryanw/improved-algs-lbs2.pdf)

Assume a uniform reduction for this H*: an input 1^n has a satisfiable indexed CNF exactly on YES lengths. Its q-bit IDs select active signed clauses of three positions (repetitions allowed) or inactive slots. Its labels have ℓ bits, where q,ℓ=n+O(log n). A poly(n)-time constructor gives an O(n)-gate binary decoder of activity, labels and signs. Given n and a label, a deterministic poly(n)-time enumerator lists every distinct active ID having that label in some position, and no other IDs. Polynomially bounded repetition in the enumeration is harmless. The hypotheses refer to the actual ID namespace: repeated encodings of an identical clause all count separately. This theorem assumes those hypotheses and does not supply such a reduction for H*.

There exist fixed η>0 and integer K such that at infinitely many YES lengths, *every* satisfying table differs from *every* Boolean circuit on ℓ inputs of size at most floor(η n sqrt(log₂ n)) on more than floor(2^n/n^K) distinct occurring labels. Under the uniform full label cube this gives error ≥1/poly(n), even for arbitrary extensions to unused labels.

**Additive verification.** Guess one circuit C and a strictly increasing list of at most t full labels with replacement bits. They define a single total table P. Form one q-input circuit for “active and all three signed literals false under C” by decoding once and evaluating three copies of C, using g≤3s+O(n) gates. Its exact count N is obtained by one deterministic fixed-density width-three count on V=q+g variables. The truth-table encoding forbids at most four triples per gate, and asserts the output by one unit clause. Every index extends uniquely to gate outputs. Hence N counts IDs, including duplicate encodings, in [0,2^q]. The bit-clock is poly(V)2^{A V/sqrt(log₂(V+2))} with a fixed multitape constant A. [October fixed-density counting theorem](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/92b516ef8e/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/main_full_version.tex)

Run the incident-ID generator separately for each listed label. Sort all emitted IDs and deduplicate globally. For each remaining ID decode its three signed positions. Emit three records keyed by label with ID and position; merge their sorted stream against the sorted replacement list, repeating the replacement bit for every equal key. Evaluate C on each record (including patched records for the old count) and attach the patched bit, defaulting to the old bit. Sort by (ID,position). In a single scan sum U and U' counting IDs falsified by the old and new triples respectively. The total number of violations of P is exactly N−U+U', since the union of enumerated IDs covers every position that could have changed. Accept precisely when this integer is zero. This handles simultaneous corrections in a clause, repeated literals, and multiple encodings without over-subtraction. A close satisfier supplies an accepting guess. Every guess on a NO instance yields a positive count or a syntax rejection.

Hard-limit all nondeterministic guesses, including lengths and circuit wire references. The polynomial generator time and output cap hold on arbitrary labels, including unused ones, on every branch. There are at most (1+t)poly(n) records of poly(n) bits each. For t≤2^n, multitape merge sorting uses O(n+log n) sequential passes, each with polynomial cost per record. Circuit copying or rewinding for every independently evaluated literal also costs poly(n) per record. Counters fit O(n+log n) bits. Consequently verification costs at most the *sum* of the single counting clock and (1+t)poly(n), on all branches. This proof uses no occurrence-sort construction of a new formula and no assumption about how its bounded-incidence decoder was obtained.

**Parameters.** Take a positive rational η' with 3Aη'<1 and the integer cap s=floor(η'n floor(sqrt(floor(log₂ n)))). It dominates floor(η n sqrt(log₂ n)) eventually for any fixed 0<η<η'. The single count takes at most 2^{(3Aη'+o(1))n} since q=n+O(log n). For explicit t=floor(2^n/n^K), the additive verification overhead is at most (1+t)n^d times a fixed constant, for a fixed d independent of K. Indeed the sorting estimate used only t≤2^n. For fixed K the integer cap can be built by K multiplications and a division on O(n)-bit integers, in poly(n) with a polynomial degree independent of K (or a separate poly(n) additive initialization cost). Choose K>d+2 and also larger than the constant in |ℓ−n|≤c log₂ n for large n. Then all branches cost o(2^n/n). Eventual existence of a close pair on every YES length would, after finite hardwiring, decide H* within the excluded time. At each resulting hard length the error under the uniform label is greater than floor(2^n/n^K)/2^ℓ, and in fact at least 2^{n−ℓ}/n^K by integer rounding. This is ≥n^{−K−c}.

**A single witness language.** If ℓ(n) is strictly increasing and computable in poly(n), recover n from a candidate length by bounded search (ℓ=n+O(log n)). More generally no injectivity assumption on the supplied arities is needed if labels are padded for the witness language: choose a fixed integer c' such that L(n)=n+c'ceil(log₂(n+2))+c' dominates ℓ(n) at every n, adjusting finitely many constants. L is strictly increasing, efficiently invertible, and L=n+O(log n). At length L(n) ignore the trailing L−ℓ bits. At other lengths, or for an unsatisfiable reduction, output zero. On a satisfiable reduction output the addressed bit of its lexicographically first full table. Generate the explicit indexed formula in 2^{O(n)} time and successively query SAT for each table-prefix extension by zero. There are 2^{O(n)} queries of length 2^{O(n)}, including all previously fixed bits, giving a single language in E^NP. The padded uniform distribution projects to the uniform original label. For each fixed padding string any L-input approximator becomes an ℓ-input circuit of at most its gate count by pinning, so averaging preserves the inverse-polynomial lower bound. Constant wires may be freely pinned (or absorbed with constant gate overhead and a smaller η). Since L/n→1, reducing η again if necessary gives the same asymptotic circuit bound in L. Hardness concerns the specified bounded-incidence reduction for H*.
