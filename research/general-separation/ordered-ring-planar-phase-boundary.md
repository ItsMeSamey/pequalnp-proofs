# Ordered-image SAT on a path of even cycles

The fixed [ordered ring image](read-two-ring-image.md) accepts precisely the canonical physical words with satisfiable recovered sources. Give an explicit graph one Boolean variable for each edge. For every vertex include its positive star and all binary negative conflicts between incident edges. Thus the selected edges form a perfect matching. Further positive clauses require the matching to hit specified subsets of edges.

Take any signed 3CNF Φ of positive arity a. For variable i let d_i count its literal occurrences, and make a cycle of length l_i=4 max(1,d_i) with cyclically ordered vertices v(i,0),…,v(i,l_i−1). Write e(i,g) for the edge from position g to g+1 modulo l_i. Between consecutive cycles join v(i,1) to v(i+1,0). This is a connected simple bipartite outerplanar graph of maximum degree three. A width-two path decomposition lists the bags {v_0,v_j,v_(j−1)} of each cycle for decreasing j from l_i−1 to 2, inserting a two-vertex bridge bag between consecutive lists. The first cycle bag contains its entering v_0 and the last contains its exiting v_1. The display verifies both edge coverage and consecutive bags for each vertex.

Every bridge separates an even number of vertices. A perfect matching crosses any such set an even number of times, hence uses no bridge. The two choices per cycle are its even-indexed edges (true) and its odd-indexed edges (false). Designate e(i,4j) or e(i,4j+1) for the j-th occurrence (zero-based) of variable i according as it is positive or negative. Each designated edge is inside its own block of four consecutive vertices, so all designated edges together form a matching. For each original clause require the chosen matching to meet its set of designated edges. Each added clause has width at most three and no designated edge occurs in two added clauses. Repeated or opposite source literals have separate designated edges. An empty source clause gives an empty demand.

Each original assignment gives one perfect matching and each designated edge is present exactly when its literal is true. The extra demands select exactly the satisfying assignments, bijectively, including choices on unused variables. With D=Σ_i max(1,d_i) there are 4D vertices and 4D+a−1 edges. Each vertex produces one star and at most three conflicts. The recovered signed source has O(D+#clauses) rows and literal occurrences, with logarithmic-width names. The physical encoder has polynomial bit cost. The arity-zero case can be evaluated directly, or represented with one fixed-phase C4 and an empty demand when the source is false. Ordinary hardness already holds at positive arity.

Fixed ordered-image nonzero is therefore NP-complete even when the reduction lands entirely in this family. For the restricted language that also requires this shape, a polynomial certificate describes the root, a bijection from edge names to recovered variables, the cycle ordering, bridges, and the star, conflict and demand rows. Comparing the claimed presentation with the source and checking the assignment verifies both requirements, so no recognition oracle is used for membership. No satisfying dictionary or source is attached to the four-field word produced by the reduction.

On the same class of roots, if every extra clause has width at most two, a verified root presentation reduces the problem to 2SAT. Substitute false for each bridge and the appropriate cycle phase literal for each cycle edge. Read a satisfying phase assignment back on the original edge variables. This works for arbitrary signs on the extra clauses as well. The graph and demand lists can be recovered from the fixed ordered encoding of this construction, or a general line-graph frontend can construct and verify the root before applying this rule. A supplied unverified presentation cannot certify a NO answer. The two-versus-three boundary concerns clause size and feasibility; it does not assert that weighted 2SAT optimization is easy.

The root width bound holds for the graph defining the negative conflicts; the positive demand clauses can join distant cycles. On the entire four-field input the construction retains arbitrary original SAT. Previous work already gives hardness for colorful perfect matching on planar graphs of pathwidth two; this proof specifies the cycle-cactus family, disjoint designated edges, source assignment correspondence and fixed-input compiler.
