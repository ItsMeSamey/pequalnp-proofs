# Counting raw CNF models by the span of clause failures

The [kernel](cnf_miss_span.py) accepts an arbitrary finite CNF on signed, one-based variable IDs and a nonnegative declared arity a. Normalize by removing tautologies, repeated literals and duplicate clauses. Retain the empty clause, whose failure indicator is one everywhere. For each distinct normalized clause C let f_C(x) be one exactly when assignment x falsifies C. Let k be the binary rank of these functions, including all assignments as evaluation rows. The kernel selects k original normalized clauses with precisely the same models as the input. It requires no bound on clause width.

**Implicit rank computation.** Begin with the all-ones word on the normalized clause coordinates. For a partial assignment a coordinate stays one while its clause has seen no true literal. Assigning the next variable either value applies a diagonal zero-one mask to this word. Both maps are linear over GF(2), so images of a basis under the two maps together span all next-prefix words. Keep a basis after each variable, with at most the number of normalized clauses many words. At the final level Gaussian pivot coordinates select independent original columns spanning all failure functions. Vanishing on those coordinates implies vanishing on every coordinate by linear dependence. This takes polynomial time and space in the explicit input, including the binary encoding of the arity. Layerwise basis propagation is a diagonal specialization of white-box algebraic branching-program techniques. Common-zero preservation by selecting polynomial constraints from a basis also occurs in polynomial-root CSP sparsification. [Raz–Shpilka](https://www.cs.tau.ac.il/~shpilka/publications/RazShpilka_PIT.pdf) · [Jansen–Pieterse](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2016.71)

**Count and witness.** Let h be the number of names in the basis and t the number of distinct names anywhere in the raw input, including tautologies. Enumeration counts on the h names and retains the first model. Alternatively, inclusion–exclusion ranges over subsets of the k basis clauses. A chosen clause contributes a pin for each of its literals to be false. Inconsistent pins contribute zero, and consistent pins on p names contribute 2^(h−p) with the subset sign. On a positive count, the latter mode obtains a model by trying a zero pin on each successive name, setting it to one exactly when the zero branch has count zero. Both modes return sorted true IDs with every other declared name false and verify the witness against the raw rows.

If the count on the h names is N, the exact output pair is (N·2^(t−h), a−t), representing N·2^(a−h) declared models. This convention also applies at zero. It avoids expanding either a dense witness or an output integer with a bits when most variable names never occur. The caller can read the sparse witness directly.

**Terms charged.** The implementation chooses enumeration when 2^h≤(h+1)2^k and inclusion–exclusion otherwise. Enumeration charges 2^h full assignments. Inclusion–exclusion charges 2^k subsets on a zero count and (h+1)2^k on a positive count, counting inconsistent subsets and every witness query. A basis containing the empty clause or an empty basis short-circuits with zero charged terms. The optional cumulative cap returns unknown, the actual terms spent, and the next required term index on exhaustion. Each term and all preprocessing cost polynomial bit time in the explicit input length. The overall worst-case bound is min(2^h,(h+1)2^k) times a polynomial, in polynomial space.

**Limits of the parameter.** Every variable in a normalized clause is essential to its failure indicator: falsify all its literals and flip just that variable. Each linear combination of the basis functions depends only on their union of supports. Thus the number of variables used by normalized clauses equals h and is at most wk when w bounds their width. The raw t may be larger solely because of tautologies. For a minimally unsatisfiable CNF, deleting each clause permits an assignment satisfying every other clause and failing the deleted one. The resulting evaluation rows are distinct unit vectors, so k equals the number of clauses. The same conclusion holds for any satisfiable formula in which removing each clause strictly enlarges the model set. In particular the parameter can equal the entire normalized clause count.

For m distinct nonempty failure subcubes there is also the intrinsic bound m≤k^(log₂3). Their indicators are tensor products of the three nonzero binary vectors of length two. Slice by one factor and order the ranks of the suffix families as b≥c≥d. Any two different first factors are independent, so k≥b+c. With p=log₂3, induction bounds m by b^p+c^p+d^p≤(b+c)^p: for x=c/(b+c) in [0,1/2], the convex function (1−x)^p+2x^p is one at both endpoints. The empty family is immediate, and a zero-dimensional nonempty cube has rank one. All ternary choices on j variables attain equality. In coding terms the tensors are columns of the generator of the n-fold tensor power of the binary [3,2,2] code. The largest number in a flat of dimension k equals 3^n minus the generalized Hamming weight of index 2^n−k (weight zero at index zero), since the orthogonal subspace vanishes exactly on columns in the flat. This relates the bound to the weight hierarchy of product codes. [Martínez-Pérez–Willems](https://doi.org/10.1023/B:DESI.0000035465.76668.89)
