# Witness rigidity for proof circuits with computable multiplicity

Consider joint bounded-fanin-two Boolean circuits V(a,w),R(a) with s gates in total (shared gates counted once), where a has ℓ bits, w has p bits, V has acceptance and Boolean value outputs, and R has p+1 unsigned binary output bits. Require that each a has exactly R(a)>0 accepted w's, all giving the same value f(a). R can vary with the address. No invertibility or proof-finding algorithm is assumed. Constant R recovers the [regular-fiber model](regular-proof-circuit-witness-rigidity.md); this enlarges the allowed multiplicity profiles without claiming a strict separation between the classes of represented Boolean functions. For example, with a single proof bit, accepting iff a specified address bit is one or the proof bit is zero gives multiplicities one and two on different addresses.

It suffices to consider p≤2s+2 for a size-only bound. After discarding unread proof coordinates in V and renumbering the others, if d coordinates were discarded then every multiplicity is divided by 2^d. On every valid representation the low d outputs of R were zero. Retaining the p+1−d high outputs computes the new multiplicity at no extra gate cost. R reads only a. Representation guesses are required to meet the normalized bound. A circuit model explicitly charging proof width, output ports or integer representation is also covered under a bound on the combined cost.

**Theorem.** Fix the unary H* in NTIME(2^n) outside NTIME(2^{n−ceil(log₂ n)}) with its uniformly invertible, bounded-incidence occurrence-sort formula. For fixed η>0 sufficiently small and fixed integer D sufficiently large, infinitely many YES lengths have this property: every satisfying table differs on more than floor(2^n/n^D) distinct occurring labels from every f represented as above with total size at most η n sqrt(log₂ n). The table and proof circuits use the full ℓ=n+O(log n) named label bits of the occurrence-sort formula. Thus the uniform-cube error is inverse polynomial, including for the lex-first witness-bit language in E^NP using monotone address padding. The dependency is on this recoded formula; its input decoder and inverse-incident algorithm are those of the [occurrence-sort construction](jmv-invertible-occurrence-sort.md). The underlying hierarchy and clock are in the [additive verification lemma](bounded-incidence-additive-witness-rigidity.md) and [Williams, Theorem 2.1](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~ryanw/improved-algs-lbs2.pdf).

**Certify the guessed pair on every label.** Let k(a) be the number of accepted p-bit proofs. Count the following three predicates with the same label a inside each predicate. Use independent blocks when two witnesses or comparison arguments appear.

1. Two accepted proofs give M20=Σ k(a)^2.
2. One accepted proof and one unsigned z<R(a) give M11=Σ k(a)R(a).
3. Two comparison arguments z,z' both below R(a) give M02=Σ R(a)^2.

Use p+1 bits for *each* z so that the equality with R(a) holds even for a forged output above 2^p. This also covers R=2^p and p=0. Require M20−2M11+M02=0, exactly over the integers. This sum of nonnegative squares forces k(a)=R(a) for every a, and thus enforces the natural upper bound. Count labels with R(a)=0 and reject unless the count is zero. Count pairs of accepted proofs at one label whose value outputs differ, and reject unless that count is zero. The five checks establish one total Boolean table f with a positive, known weight at every address. An equality, positivity or agreement failure at even one unused label invalidates the global representation.

**Verify a sparse correction.** One additional count takes an indexed clause i, the active flag, its three signed labels a1,a2,a3, and *three independent* p-bit proof strings. Require activity and that each proof accepts and supplies a value falsifying its signed literal. It returns B=Σ_i b_i P_i, where b_i is the baseline violation bit (zero on inactive indices) and P_i=R(a1)R(a2)R(a3). This count uses V alone; R is used later to recompute explicitly the weight of an affected ID. A repeated label contributes a fresh independent factor for each position. A unit or binary clause repeated to three positions follows the same rule. Alternatively, if the decoder explicitly returns a syntactic constant at a position, test its signed literal directly and require its otherwise allocated proof block to equal zero, yielding weight one for that position. A pinned variable referenced by its ordinary name continues to use its ordinary R weight.

Guess at most t full distinct labels in increasing order with replacement bits. Enumerate, sort and deduplicate their union of active incident IDs. For each affected ID decode all positions, evaluate R at every named position, and guess and verify one accepted V proof for its old bit. Guessing is complete because R>0, and the value is forced by agreement. Join the literal records against the sorted corrections by full address; sort back by ID and position. Compute the old violation b_i and the corrected violation b'_i. The exact corrected sum is

    B' = B + Σ_{affected i} (b'_i − b_i) P_i .

The weights intentionally refer to the same proof counts before and after replacing bits. A patched bit requires no proof of its new value. Positivity makes B'=0 equivalent to satisfaction of all active IDs by the single patched table. Thus NO inputs reject all guesses and a close satisfying table supplies an accepting guess.

**All-path time.** Count acceptance using a unique-extension gate encoding on the declared primary and gate variables with at most 4g+1 width-three clauses for g binary gates. Uniformly implement comparison by a linear-size unsigned comparator, and charge its gates, all formal input blocks and all relevant output wires. Each of the six fixed counts has O(n+s+p) variables and O(n+s+p) clauses, at density at most five using its actual variable count. In particular the sixth count uses q+3p+3s+O(n) variables, where q=n+O(log n). Any shared part of V and R can be copied a constant number of times per predicate. There is a fixed c with the largest variable count ≤cs+O(n) after normalization, and ≥n−O(log n) through the declared label or index inputs. Exact integers, including B and the sparse sums, have O(n+3p+1) bits on certified circuits, as each sum counts at most 2^q IDs of weight at most 2^{3p}; the moment tests on forged pairs also have O(n+p+1) bits. Preserve unused variables or restore their counting factors. The [October fixed-density counting theorem](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/42907c9e999cc00d6abe7a50bfc1023250edd1b9/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/main_full_version.tex) bounds the six counts together by 2^{(Acη'+o(1))n} for gate cap floor(η'n floor(sqrt(floor(log₂ n)))). Fix rational η' with Acη'<1, and use η<η' for rounding.

Sorted tape joins, proof guesses and evaluations, weight multiplications and additions on all sparse records cost (1+t)poly(n), since p,s≤poly(n). Enforce caps, wire references, list order, proof lengths and output syntax on every branch. For t=floor(2^n/n^D), fixed D larger than the sparse overhead degree plus two gives total time o(2^n/n). The degree bound uses only t≤2^n; forming the integer cap costs separate poly(n) startup for fixed D. Hardwire finitely many exceptional lengths to contradict the chosen H* if close pairs existed at every sufficiently large YES length. The [witness-language construction](bounded-incidence-additive-witness-rigidity.md) pads to strictly increasing arities and ignores padding. Pinning those bits in both V and R preserves the defining counts pointwise; averaging over paddings transfers the inverse-polynomial uniform error.
