# A strict term-count improvement for the same explicit CNF

Let n=3ℓ, w=2ℓ≥6. For every w-subset of n base coordinates take the faces specifying every even local pattern of weight 2 through w−2. There are m0=binom(n,w)(2^(w−1)−2)=Θ(3^n/√n) distinct faces by Stirling. A base assignment avoids them all precisely at global weights 0,1,n−1,n. Indeed the attainable local weights form the entire integer interval [max(0,w−n+s),min(w,s)] at global weight s. For 2≤s≤n−2 its intersection with [2,w−2] contains an even integer: if neither even endpoint lies in the intersection, the unclipped attainable interval is internal and has length n−w. Thus 2n+2 base assignments avoid all the faces.

Set T=4n, m=T m0, and use N=m additional coordinates U. Construct a T-by-N binary matrix. Its column vectors all have first entry zero, are nonzero, and are distinct. Begin with the T−1 unit vectors for rows 2 through T and the vector one on every row except row 1. Append other nonzero columns with first bit zero in lexicographic order until there are N. For all sufficiently large n, T≤N≤2^(T−1)−1; discarding finitely many n suffices. The T row words g are pairwise at distance at least two. Indeed two nonfirst rows differ in their unit columns, and the first row differs from any other in that row's unit column and the all-nonfirst column. All columns contain both values, and none are equal or complementary. This list is constructible in time polynomial in the explicit output size by maintaining a binary counter; there is no need to scan the unused portion of the full column space.

For every row g and base face P introduce the ordinary signed clause whose unique failure face is {U=g} × P. The formula has exactly m clauses on a=N+n occurring names and every clause has width N+w. The raw word with binary names has length Θ(m² log m) under the usual explicit literal-list representation: the guard coordinates alone appear in all m clauses and have total identifier length Θ(m log m) per clause. The exact number of declared assignments satisfying the raw formula is

    (2^N−T) 2^n + T(2n+2).

In particular it is positive, so witness work is charged in the implementation's inclusion–exclusion branch.

All rows are distinct and equally wide. Two rows on the same scope cannot differ in exactly one sign: different guards differ in at least two signs, and within one guard distinct even base patterns differ in at least two. Thus subsumption and complementary one-bit pair fusion remove nothing. Every variable occurs with both signs. The guard signed incidence columns are neither identical nor opposite since this holds on the T guard words. Base coordinates have distinct occurrence supports (w<n); every base coordinate is absent from some row, while every guard occurs everywhere. Hence global signed-column twin identification also gives no simplification. These statements specify the named syntactic rules; the guard factorization is available to stronger methods.

Disjoint guard supports make the binary rank k of all clause-failure functions exactly T times the base rank: restricting a linear relation to each guard word leaves only that copy. In particular k≤T2^n. Since every variable of a nonempty face is essential to its indicator, a spanning set of actual clause indicators must collectively use all a names. The existing cnf_miss_span kernel therefore has h=a. For sufficiently large n, (a+1)2^k<2^a, so it chooses inclusion–exclusion, reconstructs a witness, and charges exactly (a+1)2^k terms. Its polynomial preprocessing and per-term bit costs yield total time at most poly(B)2^(T2^n) on the raw word of length B.

On that identical word, full raw-variable truth enumeration charges 2^a assignments and full raw-clause inclusion–exclusion charges 2^m subsets even for the count alone. Since a=m+n and T2^n/m=O(√n (2/3)^n), the optimized bound is 2^o(m), while both raw clocks are at least 2^m. More explicitly, writing γ=log₃2<1, m=Θ(√n·3^n) gives T2^n=O(m^γ(log m)^(1−γ/2)). Even the polynomial bit factors and all positive-count witness queries fit this separation. It is a comparison of specified enumeration clocks on an explicit satisfiable family; the displayed model count itself illustrates an alternative algorithm exploiting the construction.
