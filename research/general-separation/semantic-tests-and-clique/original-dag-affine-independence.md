# Two description-aware actions suggested by the original-DAG corpus

The smallest circuit meeting the frozen resistance screen among 96 seeded samples is index 50. Its live gates compute x₁ OR x₂, NAND(x₀,x₃), and the implication from that NAND to the OR. The output simplifies to (x₀ AND x₃) OR x₁ OR x₂. The three constituent events use independent input sets, giving exactly 13 satisfying assignments among 16. This is a minimum in the specified sampled pool, not a claim about all circuits. [Frozen corpus](original_dag_portfolio.py)

**Independent projections with conditional branching.** On primary inputs uniform on a binary affine slice, maintain for each signal a subspace of linear forms that suffices to determine it. For an affine signal use its single linear part. At a binary gate the sum of the two spaces always suffices. If the spaces have intersection zero, their projections are independent: combining bases gives a list of independent linear forms, jointly uniform on all patterns. Thus, given the two exact marginal probabilities, the gate's truth table gives its output probability by summing the appropriate four products. This requires neither disjoint primary coordinates nor a read-once DAG. If an affine terminal or a constant or unary simplification is available, use it directly.

When independence fails, choose a nonzero parity in the intersection of the operand spaces of a blocking gate, branch on its two values and rerun the computation on the two affine slices. Each slice is parameterized by Gaussian elimination, retaining any constant offsets. If all paths resolve within k splits this computes the exact count in poly(q+m)·2^k time on q inputs and m gates. Marginals are dyadic rationals of polynomial bit length. The code declines if its supplied cap is exhausted. Exact evaluation on the same 96 seeded DAGs succeeded for 60 with no splits, 92 with at most one split, and all 96 with at most two. Index 50 required none. **A scalable hostile example and a quadratic count.** For q divisible by four, split the primary bits into u,v of equal length q/2. Let M be block diagonal over GF(2), using copies of the binary matrix with rows (0,1) and (1,1). Both M and I+M are invertible. Set Q₁=uᵀv and Q₂=uᵀMv, with output Q₁ AND Q₂. All three polynomials Q₁,Q₂,Q₁+Q₂ have polar matrices of rank q. The rank of a bilinear form on restriction to a codimension-d subspace drops by at most 2d, since removing d rows and d columns loses at most 2d in rank. The affine translate can change the linear and constant terms but leaves the polar form unchanged.

Any affine-projection space sufficient to determine a quadratic on an n-dimensional slice has dimension at least its polar rank. Indeed, any direction annihilated by that space is a translation period of the function and lies in the radical. Thus on a codimension-d slice the two operands each need support dimension at least q−2d, inside a space of dimension q−d. Their intersection has dimension at least q−3d. For d<q/3 and sufficiently large q neither is affine, and they cannot equal or complement each other since their XOR also has large polar rank. A rule requiring independent root operand projections on its nonsimplified leaves therefore needs 2^{Ω(q)} leaves, even choosing arbitrary parity tests. This bound concerns the independent-support certificate; it does not apply to correlation-aware evaluation.

For any invertible R on q/2 bits, summing the sign of uᵀRv gives 2^{q/2}: all inner sums over u vanish except when v=0. Expanding the two output-one indicators using this identity gives exactly (2^q−2^{q/2})/4 satisfying inputs. The tests for q=4,8,12 yielded 3,60,1008 respectively. The two-split independence implementation declined each of these three instances.

**A quadratic frontier with unrestricted suffix computation.** For any topological cut, collect the live earlier signals the remaining computation actually reads, including earlier primary wires. Suppose all have certified multilinear GF(2) expressions of degree at most two. Compute the rank r of their polynomials modulo constants. Gaussian elimination yields independent polynomials P₁,…,P_r and expresses every live signal affinely in their r values. The entire suffix is therefore an arbitrary circuit H on these r symbols. They need not be probabilistically or algebraically independent. Evaluate H on 2^r symbolic rows, even the unrealizable ones, and take its ordinary integer Walsh transform W_H. The exact count on the actual q primary inputs is

    2^(−r) Σ_s W_H(s) · Σ_x (−1)^(Σ_j s_j P_j(x)).

Every inner sum is a polynomial-time binary quadratic Gauss sum. This is the Fourier inversion formula evaluated only on realizable rows in the outer primary count. It proves time poly(q+m)·2^r with arbitrary suffix depth and reuse. The implementation propagates degree-at-most-two polynomials through the supplied graph whenever both operands needed for an operation are certified; their product has degree at most four before cancellation. It leaves an uncertified signal unknown. All topological cuts can be checked in polynomial time. Cut zero supplies the usual full primary rank at worst, subject to the implementation cap.

Across the 96 seeded DAGs the selected ranks 0,1,2,3,4 occurred 11,58,16,10,1 times respectively. The single rank-four sample was index 19 and resolved by one affine-independence split. All counts agreed with the independent original-gate truth oracle. Each of the three coupled-quadratic instances had a rank-two cut, four symbolic rows and four quadratic sign sums. The independence and quadratic-frontier codes do not consult the testing truth oracle.
