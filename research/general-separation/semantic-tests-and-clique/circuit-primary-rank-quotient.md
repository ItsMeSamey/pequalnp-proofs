# Exact circuit counting after absorbing affine input directions

Work over GF(2). Write each binary gate's ANF. For a nonlinear gate on current signals a,b introduce a fresh formal z_i for their product and express its actual output as z_i plus an affine combination of a,b and a constant. Propagate affine gates without introducing formal variables. All gate signals are now explicit affine forms in the original q primary bits x and earlier z variables. There are m product definitions. Stack their 2m operands in the vector Lx+Bz+c. Write the designated output as l x+e z+c_o. All matrices and supports are obtainable by polynomial-time bit arithmetic from the original DAG. With actual definitions z_i=product_i, every x has a unique full extension.

## Quotient by the primary inputs

Set r=rank L and ρ=rank[L B]−r. Compute matrices D,R,V by elimination so B=LD+RV, where the ρ columns of R give complementary directions modulo im L and V has row rank ρ. Introduce u=x+Dz and v=Vz. In these coordinates every operand is a component of Lu+Rv+c. Thus each product is now an explicit quadratic Q_i(u,v). Require only the ρ quadratics v=VQ(u,v) and the output equation

    l u + (e+lD) Q(u,v) + c_o = 1.

Projection between old valid assignments (x,z) and new solutions (u,v) is bijective: the inverse computes z=Q(u,v), then x=u+Dz. The defining equations of the original circuit now hold, and the recovered u,v agree by construction. This is a change of variables in the full graph of the computation; no bijection from u alone to x is required.

If l is outside row(L), translating u by a vector in ker L with l-value one toggles the output and preserves all product equations. Since the unfiltered graph has 2^q points, the exact accepting count is 2^{q−1}. Otherwise reduce u to r independent linear coordinates, multiplying full counts by 2^{q−r}. Expanding the indicators of the ρ+1 binary equations by additive characters leaves at most 2^{ρ+1} quadratic exponential sums. Each is computable in polynomial bit time by binary symplectic Gaussian elimination; equivalently eliminate a variable pair with nonzero polar entry, then evaluate the remaining affine character. Intermediate integer lengths are polynomial in q+m. The total deterministic clock is 2^ρ poly(q,m). One can return a witness by self-reducing on the new coordinates while counting with additional affine restrictions, then applying the inverse.

The same displayed equations are explicit XORs of products of at most two affine factors, plus affine terms. There are at most (ρ+1)(m+1) product occurrences after aggregating each affine summand. Their encoding length is polynomial in q+m; the number of variables after reduction is r+ρ=rank[L B]. The [LPTWY deterministic exact counter, Theorem 1.4](https://theory.stanford.edu/~yuhch123/files/polyEq.pdf), supplies the alternative clock poly(q,m) times 2^{n'(1−1/(1100 log₂(max(1,s_occ/n'))+O(1)))}, with n'=r+ρ and trivial handling of an empty variable set. Counts obtained on reduced coordinates require the same free factor. Gaussian elimination of affine combinations of the residual equations is also possible if one charges only the independent quadratic remainders and adjusts affine ranks and free factors.

For example z_0=0 and z_i=(x_i+z_{i−1}) y_i give rank B=m−1 but ρ=0: the coefficient column of each used predecessor equals that of its private x_i. With output z_m, its native ANF contains x_1 y_1⋯y_m, so its degree grows to m+1. Invertibly mixing all primary variables by an affine circuit preserves the quotient parameter. In contrast, for a product chain z_i=z_{i−1}x_i started with z_1 the product of two inputs, private occurrences of earlier z as operands are not absorbable by primary columns. Reusing or altering the primary affine factors permits arbitrarily long chains with large ρ.

The parameter is computable from the given DAG. High quotient rank gives no lower bound on decision complexity. [Related quadratic Gauss-sum implementation](../direct-sat-algebra/quadratic-image.md)
