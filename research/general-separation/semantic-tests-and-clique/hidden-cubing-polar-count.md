# Recovering field coordinates for a quadratic frontier

Let Q be a certified quadratic map between two q-dimensional binary spaces, followed by an arbitrary h-gate Boolean DAG H. Let β be the alternating polar of Q. If β is equivalent under independent unknown linear bases to a direct sum of polars of cubing over binary fields of odd degrees m≥5, the algorithm recovers the blocks deterministically in polynomial time. It tests bijectivity exactly on a single block with any added linear map and on products with acyclic off-diagonal linear dependencies. When yes the count is #H. For arbitrary inputs all claimed forms are explicitly verified. The result concerns recognition and counting after a permutation. It gives no arbitrary root-finding procedure.

Fouque, Macario-Rat and Stern previously gave polynomial-time equivalent-coordinate recovery for a hidden quadratic monomial, including the polar identity and its conjugated field in Section 3.2, and random choices in Section 4. We use that identity. The following derivation gives a deterministic direct-product reconstruction with an elementary exact dimension calculation for the cubic and a sparse trace test for an extra linear term. Canteaut, Couvreur and Perrin analyze the general quadratic EA-equivalence problem. ## Polar computation

Solve over F₂ for all pairs (A,U) satisfying β(Ax,y)+β(x,Ay)=Uβ(x,y). This is linear in their matrix entries and needs only pairs of basis inputs. Equivalently β(Ax,x) and UQ(x) have matching quadratic parts. Under independent coordinate changes both matrices transform by conjugation.

In one block F=F_{2^m} write A(x)=Σ_i a_i x^{2^i} and U(x)=Σ_j u_j x^{2^j}, 0≤i,j<m. These representations are unique by the polynomial root bound and dimension. For β(x,y)=x²y+xy² the quadratic terms of β(Ax,x) arise from the exponents 2^{i+1}+1 and 2^i+2 with coefficients a_i² and a_i. Indices of individual powers of two are cyclic modulo m; coincident powers within a term produce a linear monomial. All quadratic exponents of U(x³) have adjacent cyclic positions {j,j+1}. On the A-side the unordered nonrepeated pairs from distinct terms are distinct except the two i=0 terms indexed by {0,1}. For i≠0 the pair {i+1,0} is adjacent or repeated only at i∈{m−2,m−1}, and {i,1} is adjacent or repeated only at i∈{1,2}. These sets are disjoint for odd m≥5. Each such i contributes an unmatched quadratic monomial and a_i=0. Univariate representatives of degree below 2^m are unique and affine functions have only constant and power-of-two exponents. What remains is exactly A(x)=a x and U(y)=(a²+a)y.

In a direct sum all off-diagonal A_jk vanish. Put x,y in distinct input blocks k,j and examine output j. Its identity requires β_j(A_jk x,y)=0 for all x,y. Every nonzero v admits z outside {0,v} with vz(v+z)≠0, so the common radical is trivial. All off-diagonal U blocks then vanish. The values of each β_j span the output field, since β_j(ax,ay)=a³β_j(x,y), at least one value is nonzero, and cubing permutes the nonzero field elements. Thus the projected A-space is exactly the direct product of field scalar algebras, and projection from the pair space to A is injective.

## Deterministic recovery

Check injectivity, commutativity, closure under multiplication and containment of the identity on a basis of the projected algebra. Its idempotents E satisfy E²=E, a linear test in a commuting binary algebra. Under the promise this space is the direct product of one copy of F₂ per field block. Split the input by the 0- and 1-eigenspaces of each member of an idempotent-space basis, discarding empty components. Intermediate nonempty cells form a direct sum, so there are at most q. The final components are precisely the hidden input blocks: distinct blocks have differing bit patterns because the idempotent space contains a projection onto each block. Define the matching output block as the span of β-values on two basis vectors of that input block, including all basis pairs. Check the resulting spans form a direct sum of the correct dimensions.

For each recovered m-dimensional block require m odd and m≥5. Work in its projected scalar space, extended over the full space as zero on other blocks, and check that this space has dimension m. Select a scalar A corresponding to a field generator a. For every prime p dividing m, the proper maximal subfield scalars are precisely the kernel of the linear map A↦A^{2^{m/p}}+A. Compute the map by repeated matrix squaring, and obtain the prime divisors by trial division on m. Their sizes sum to less than 2^m since p≥3 and m 2^{m/3}<2^m for m≥5. With an arbitrary basis of the scalar space, count the number of completions within each subspace by linear algebra as successive basis bits are fixed. Preserve strict inequality between their summed sizes and the total completions at every step. One of the two extensions always succeeds by additivity. The resulting a lies in no proper subfield. Its companion γ=a²+a also has degree m, because over any subfield containing γ the degree of a is at most two and divides an odd extension degree.

Recover the minimal polynomial of A on the block by linear dependence of its matrix powers. Check irreducibility, for example using the binary Frobenius and polynomial gcd tests, and degree m. This gives an explicit field F with element a. For any nonzero input vector x₀ identify a^i with A^i x₀, 0≤i<m. For any nonzero vector y₀ in the matching output block identify γ^i with U^i y₀ using the companion U from the paired solution. Both are bases under the promise; test them directly. Relative to these two bases Q has cubic field term c z³ for some nonzero c on that block, plus an affine map on all input blocks. Indeed the nonzero scales x₀,y₀ change the hidden cubing scalar, and the field stored in powers of γ on output is the same recovered field. Obtain c as the ratio of the local polar value at (1,a) to a²+a. Verify every local polar coefficient against c(x²y+xy²) and every cross-block polar against zero. Consequently the remaining part of Q is explicitly affine. Test the constant and linear coefficients in these bases. These operations cost polynomially many bit operations in the explicit quadratic input size. On malformed inputs fail closed when any structural check fails. Verification of the polar and bases makes a successful structural recognition sound; completeness holds for all promised polars regardless of their affine perturbation.

## Testing the linear perturbations

On a single block write Q(z)=c z³+L(z)+b. A collision at inputs differing by v≠0 exists exactly when z/v solves t²+t=1+L(v)/(c v³). The image of t↦t²+t is the trace-zero hyperplane, and Tr(1)=1 for odd m. Hence Q is a permutation exactly when Tr(L(v)/(c v³)) vanishes for every v≠0. Compute the linearized coefficients λ_i of L from its matrix over F₂. Expand the trace on the multiplicative group into at most m² terms. For each i,j use exponent (2^i−3)2^j modulo 2^m−1 and coefficient (λ_i/c)^{2^j}. Combine equal exponents and test that all combined coefficients vanish. Exponents use O(m) bits. The reduced representative has degree less than 2^m−1 and cannot vanish at all nonzero field elements unless identically zero. This decides bijectivity with polynomially many field operations even when the maps have nonzero constants or arbitrary linear perturbations.

For multiple blocks add an arc i→j exactly when the off-diagonal linear map from i to j is nonzero. If acyclic, take a topological order. A diagonal permutation lets each successive input be uniquely recovered from the preceding inputs and that output; this is an existence argument requiring no root-finding algorithm. Conversely, the first nonpermutation diagonal following permutation blocks omits an output value for each fixed preceding input, contradicting surjectivity. Thus the individual tests are necessary and sufficient. A cycle returns inconclusive by this rule. As a direct negative example Q(z)=z³+z on a field has Q(0)=Q(1). Pure cubing products stay in the accepted class after arbitrary independent affine changes because translations introduce only block-local affine perturbations in the recovered coordinates.

Native field multiplication followed by polynomial-basis reduction costs O(m²) binary gates by schoolbook convolution and reduction, and squaring costs at most this much. Blocks of width at most O(log q) thus admit raw O(q log q) gates in total for pure cubing. This includes unbounded odd widths as q grows. Arbitrary linear changes and arbitrary added linear terms have their actual implementation costs and can exceed that budget. The retained arbitrary output DAG H may itself consume the full gate budget. No worst-case unrestricted CircuitSAT saving or P versus NP conclusion follows.
