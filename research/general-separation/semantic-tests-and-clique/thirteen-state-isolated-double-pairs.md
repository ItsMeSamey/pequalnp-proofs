# Two readouts on physical thirteen-state tables with isolated doubly constrained pairs

Use the [fourteen-state port model](all-fourteen-two-readouts.md) with explicit physical tables of at least thirteen states. If every edge whose defect fiber at occupancy 11 is a singleton has an isolated two-site component in the overlap graph, allowing parallel tables only on those same two sites, the two-readout problem has a deterministic polynomial-time decision and witness algorithm. Arbitrary symmetric exterior drifts can couple every site to every other site. This includes all overlap matchings. Private physical lifts use supplied row representatives.

If the selected defect is forced at 10 or 01, the [physical affine-fiber theorem](thirteen-state-physical-guard.md) makes the 11 fiber affine. At most one more omission is available, so that fiber is full. At the other single-end occupancy at least three rows remain, hence the selected defect is free. This is a directed rule under the selected-site semantics. At the exceptional single-end occupancy the unoccupied end needs no correction, even if its defect also happens to be fixed. If neither single-end occupancy forces the selected defect, both singletons satisfy arbitrary demands. At 11, two or more defect pairs either have full projections or form an affine line forcing just one endpoint. This gives an ordinary edge or a directed rule. In the directed case the head at singleton occupancy has a free selected defect; its opposite unoccupied tail need not take its baseline if the admissible row requires another value. These choices do not affect an unoccupied vertex. One row at 11 gives the exceptional pair. At 00 no guard is present. Original rows can be reconstructed using the constructive fixed-occupancy algorithm on the original tables.

Treat the entire parallel-edge component at every isolated exceptional pair as a single two-site unit, even if a parallel directed table by itself has a free head. Count each such unit as a two-site component on the S-side of the [fourteen-state algorithm](all-fourteen-two-readouts.md). Every proper occupancy is unconditionally feasible there. The one-readout method, the independent-pair tests and the incompatible-literal branch carry over. The new case occurs when the two independent S-columns are the two sites a,b of one exceptional pair. All other S-columns are then zero. For each fixed y the two independent columns give unique a,b meeting the two original readouts. Test the three proper occupancies by linear algebra on y, completing with all remaining S-sites vacant. If none succeeds, a=b=1 is required for every y.

In this rank-two case there are no other restrictions on y. If the y-columns contributed any nonzero vector, some y would yield a proper pair by invertibility of the two columns. Thus the y contribution vanishes identically. Fix the pair selected and discard the two now tautological readouts. Compute the XOR sum K of all parallel tables’ defect fibers at 11 using the four-state dynamic program. If K contains two or more points, branch on its at most six contained affine lines, obtaining at most one readout on the remaining sites and finishing with the one-readout algorithm. If K is a singleton, its defect pair prescribes two new affine equations on the remaining occupancies. Remove the entire parallel-edge component and its two sites, translating their fixed occupancies into the remaining exterior drifts. Their isolation leaves no dangling table incidences.

The resulting instance again has at most two readouts and no additional y-pins. Each such rank-two continuation has just one child and two fewer sites. All other branches of the preceding fourteen-state algorithm finish with rank at most one. In particular the head-conflict branch never pursues a new rank-two call with a fixed head. The cost is polynomial in the original description. Reinsert every removed pair using the stored rows of all its parallel edges. A singleton-11 edge allows any defect at proper occupancy and thus supplies the selected endpoint’s correction regardless of its parallel neighbors.

This exceptional physical profile occurs. With terminals a,b take three independent private sites with respective terminal neighborhoods {a}, {b}, {a,b}, and prescribed bits 0,0,1. At 11 none is selectable, giving only defect 00. At each other occupancy exactly two are selectable, with independent two-bit defect contributions, so every defect pair occurs. Intersecting exceptional pairs are outside the isolated-pair guarantee.
