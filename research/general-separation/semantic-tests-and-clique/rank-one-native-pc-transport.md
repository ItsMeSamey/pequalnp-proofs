# Formal degree-two transport across a rank-one side with zero or one pin

Use binary polynomial calculus with Boolean field equations, addition, and variable multiplication of a previous line within the formal degree cap. In a simple graph let X be one shore of a rank-one cut, with cross matrix uvᵀ, and let Y be the other. Put s=u·x and t=v·y. The X-guards are x_i((A_Xx)_i+u_i t)=0. The X-side has at most one prescribed vertex, which can be clamped to one. For every D≥2, replacing X by the [exact rank-one interface gadget](rank-two-cut-replacement.md) preserves existence of a refutation in degree D in both directions. Y can contain arbitrary other pins.

Without a pin in X, summing its guards gives exactly st=0, because the internal contributions cancel in pairs. The single-site replacement has precisely this guard under the substitution of s for its input. In reverse retain one site of color one and set all other X bits to zero. The substitutions then transform each input axiom into a sum of input axioms of the same or smaller formal degree. This also transports static certificates within the same bound.

Now suppose p is required in X. Work with x_p=1 and put c=u_p. The side's feasible table F always contains (s,t)=(c,0), witnessed by p alone, and excludes (1,1) by the sum of all guards. We derive polynomials for every other missing point of the binary (s,t)-plane.

* If (1+c,0) is absent, let E be the active free vertices (u_i=1). A free vertex in E nonadjacent to p would supply the pair witness. Thus p is complete to E. No component of the neutral free vertices can touch both p and E, since a shortest path from p to E through neutral vertices, with the direct p–E edges omitted, gives an induced cycle after restoring its end edge. These are the cut and path tests of the [two-readout proof](two-readout-even-p3.md). Take the shore consisting of p and all neutral components touching p. Summing its guards gives the affine equation s+c+ct=0. This includes E empty. Multiplying this derived affine line by 1+t and reducing Boolean squares gives (1+t)(s+c)=0, the indicator of the missing point.
* If (0,1) is absent, c=1, since otherwise p alone works. No other active vertex lies in p's component of the X graph. Otherwise a shortest path from p to a different active vertex, ending at the first such vertex, has odd degree at its two active endpoints and even degree on its neutral interior. With t=1 it is feasible and has s=0. Summing the guards of p's component gives the affine equation t=0. Hence (1+s)t=0 in degree two.

Together with st=0, these yield every missing point indicator in iterative degree two. For each proof, ordinary Boolean reductions cost no excess degree: for example t²+t is a sum of the original Boolean field equations. The argument applies to either side realization, including a gadget whose active color disappears, in which case s is simply constant zero.

For a directional simulation, call the system in which to derive a refutation the working system. Its X side and the other system's X side share F. For every point of F select any witness in the other side. Every assignment of a coordinate on F extends to an affine function of (s,t), because F is a subset of the three affinely independent points (0,0),(0,1),(1,0). Substitute these affine functions for the other system's side variables and retain all Y variables. The other pin on X, if present, is kept identically one.

Any substituted side guard is a polynomial of degree at most two in s,t that vanishes on F. Its squarefree form is the sum of the indicators of precisely the missing points at which it is one. Consequently the working system derives that substituted axiom in degree two. The discrepancy between the other system's substituted outgoing parity and s is affine and vanishes on F; the same indicator argument derives this affine line in degree two. Each substituted outside guard differs from the working outside guard by its selected-site bit times a constant times this affine discrepancy. Multiplication uses the already-derived affine line and has formal degree at most two. This step also works at a clamped outside pin, where the selected bit is one. All substituted Boolean axioms are sums of native Boolean axioms. Affine substitution of the original proof then preserves the degree cap: multiplication by a substituted variable is a linear combination of single-variable multiplications and a scalar multiple. Exchange the two systems for the reverse implication.

This last argument is for iterative polynomial calculus. It uses the derived affine discrepancy before multiplying; a static formal-degree bound requires separate accounting. The conclusion concerns the specified exact interface, with Y retained, rather than complexity of finding unrestricted three-pin solutions.
