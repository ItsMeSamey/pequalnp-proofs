# Private kernel dimension on the three-pin regression graphs

Use raw private vectors w with w_P=0 such that some diagonal completes (A+diag d)w=0. Equivalently (Aw)_i=0 wherever w_i=0. Their binary span dimension r is the parameter in the [fixed-parameter graph algorithm](common-radical-fpt.md).

The seven-site [full-rank three-connected YES and NO fixtures](physical-cap-field-obstructions.md) have respectively r=0 and r=1. All bits are numbered from zero, with the first three pinned. The entire nonzero private list is empty for YES and consists only of mask 104 for NO. One can first solve the three homogeneous pin equations: their one-dimensional kernel on the four exterior bits is generated by 72 for YES or 104 for NO. The adjacency products are masks 104 and 40, respectively. The first product has a bit outside its input support, while the latter is supported on the input. [Exhaustive checker](common_radical_fpt_check.py)

The existing [scalar-only, linearly connected anchor constructions](high-connectivity-anchor-obstruction.md) on 10t+O(1) vertices instead have r≥6t−2 for t≥2, for both YES and NO. In one shore consider the four nonmarked vertices of each marked pentagon, in their path order. Each of the three words H=1111, U=1101 and V=1011 gives even adjacency on every unselected path site and at its unselected marked vertex. Off the shore the complete joins and attached pins see either the word's total parity or zero. On the R shore these supports avoid the distinguished h, and the extra YES vertex s sees the entire L shore and no R pentagon. Thus each H_i by itself and any two odd words on distinct pentagons of the same shore are genuine private vectors.

The span on that shore contains all t vectors H_i, all t vectors U_i+V_i (add two genuine words using the same odd partner in another pentagon), and the t−1 consecutive pairs U_i+U_{i+1}. These 3t−1 vectors are independent: H, U+V and U are independent on a single four-bit block, and the adjacent edges of a path are independent over the binary field. The two shores use disjoint coordinates. This lower bound uses the original graph with its three original pins; no cloned vertices or altered supports are needed. It describes the size of the parameter and does not give a lower bound on actual runtime.
