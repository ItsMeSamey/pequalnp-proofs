# How much unrestricted circuit size can probabilistic gate tests certify?

Let V be the ring of Boolean functions on n input bits. Addition is XOR, multiplication is AND. Sample a pair (X,H), where X is an input and H maps V to one bit. H agrees with evaluation at X on constants and the n input coordinates. For each fixed pair a,b and each operation, suppose the probability that H fails to preserve the operation is at most ε.

For any circuit of s XOR/AND gates with constants, the chance that H(f) differs from f(X) is at most sε. If the output is wrong, at least one gate with its actual semantic operand functions violates its operation. Taking a union over gates counts shared gates once. The basis simulates ordinary AND/OR/NOT gates at constant cost. This lower-bound measure applies to unrestricted circuits for any fixed uniform NP language, including graphs with a clique of the supplied order.

**Theorem.** For every f its error is at most (n+24)ε. For n≥2 some f and distribution attain ratio n/2. Razborov's Theorem 2.4 gives a linear ceiling for probabilistic lower bounds in his approximation framework. Here are explicit constants for the stated Boolean-ring version. The rounding uses the Fourier analysis of the BLR test. [Razborov](https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~razborov/files/approx.pdf) · [Bellare, Coppersmith, Håstad, Kiwi and Sudan](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~mihir/papers/linearity.pdf)

1. For each H let L be its closest linear functional on V, with fixed tie breaking, and let d be its relative Hamming distance under uniform a in V. If p is H's conditional XOR rejection probability for uniform independent a,b, then d≤p. The Fourier expansion of h(a)=(-1)^H(a) gives 1−2p=Σ_l ĥ(l)³≤max_l ĥ(l) by Parseval, and d=(1−max_l ĥ(l))/2. Taking expectations gives E d≤ε.
2. For each fixed f, P[H(f)≠L(f)]≤3ε. With independent uniform a, a discrepancy at f entails an XOR rejection at (f,a) or a discrepancy at a or at a+f. Both last inputs are uniform. This also applies to f drawn independently of (X,H).
3. Write L(a)=Σ_z v_z a(z) in F₂ and set B=diag(v)+vvᵀ. The defect L(ab)+L(a)L(b) equals aᵀBb. For v of weight at least two, any two support indices give an invertible principal submatrix with zero diagonal and both off-diagonals one. With uniform independent a,b the defect probability is (1−2^(−rank B))/2≥3/8. Its unconditional probability is at most 6ε by the AND test for H, rounding discrepancies at uniform a,b (each ≤ε), and step 2 at the independently drawn, possibly nonuniform ab (≤3ε). Consequently the probability that v has weight at least two is ≤16ε. The zero vector has probability ≤3ε since H(1)=1.
4. When L evaluates at y≠X, at least one pinned coordinate disagrees with L. On that sample choose one such i. Testing XOR on that coordinate and a uniform mask a rejects with probability at least 1−2d, using agreement at a and a+x_i. Thus the indicator of a wrong singleton is bounded sample by sample by 2d plus the sum of the n conditional coordinate-test rejection probabilities. Averaging bounds wrong singletons by (n+2)ε. Add the discrepancy at f of probability at most 3ε. The total is (n+24)ε.
5. For the lower example take X=0, choose j uniformly and use evaluation at the j-th unit vector, except that all input coordinates are pinned to zero. For a fixed binary ring gate only j for which coordinate j is an operand or result can cause a defect. Three distinct coordinate functions cannot appear as two operands and their sum or product, so the defect is at most 2/n. Two distinct coordinate inputs to XOR attain 2/n. For OR of all n coordinates the semantic evaluation is always wrong.

## Testing polynomial-size masks against XOR-exact evaluations

Now assume H preserves XOR exactly on V, with the same constant and input pins. Identify assignments with integers i from 0 to M−1, M=2ⁿ. Over F of size 2^(n+3), form A(i)=Tr(u v^i) with independent uniform field elements u,v, using v⁰=1. Use two independently drawn masks A,B for a product test. Each fixed mask has O(n³) Boolean gates. Hardwire the powers v^(2^j), conditional-multiply an accumulator by each successive power according to the j-th input bit, and take the fixed linear output Tr(u·). Each constant multiplication is binary linear on n+3 bits. This is the finite-field powering construction of Alon, Goldreich, Håstad and Peralta. [AGHP, §5, Construction 3](https://johanhastad.se/almostindep.pdf)

**Theorem.** If the AND rejection probability averaged over independent mask seeds and the independent pseudo-evaluation sample is at most ε, every target's error is at most (512/147)ε. There is no restriction on the support size of the linear functional H.

For any nonzero selector w the character bias E[(-1)^(w·A)] is at most δ=1/8 in absolute value. The nonzero polynomial Σ_i w_i v^i has fewer than M roots. When it is nonzero, the trace pairing with u is uniform. Fix a sample and write its XOR-exact H as the linear functional with selector h. Exactness at 1 excludes the zero selector. A singleton necessarily evaluates at X by the input pins. For any other selector D=diag(h)+hhᵀ has rank at least two. Take two independent forms among its rows. Expand the indicator that both vanish to get P[DA=0]≤(1+3δ)/4. Conditional on DA≠0, small bias and independence of B imply P[AᵀDB=1]≥(1−δ)/2. Thus every nonsingleton fails the easy mask product test with conditional probability at least 3(1−δ)²/8=147/512. Averaging proves the claim.

The test requires scalar evaluations preserving semantic XOR on the entire ring. A construction that assigns different states to equivalent circuit presentations or preserves XOR only on a restricted collection requires its own argument.
