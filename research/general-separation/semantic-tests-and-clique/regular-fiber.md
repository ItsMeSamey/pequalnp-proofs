# A parsimonious CLIQUE projection with fixed theta, chromatic number and degrees

**Theorem.** Let V(x,w) be a deterministic bounded-fanin Boolean verifier circuit of size s including all input and witness pins. For each h≥0, one can construct in time polynomial in s+h+1 a graph on N=O((s+h+1)²) vertices and an integer K, with each adjacency a constant or one literal of x. Its K-clique count is exactly the number of accepting w. Every output graph is regular, has diameter at most two, vertex connectivity at least N/2, chromatic number K+8, and complementary Lovász number θ̄=K+10√5−22. Its clique number is K−1 on NO instances and K on YES instances. All instances at a length have the same unlabeled one-dimensional color-refinement signature. The vertex-conditioning theta test below accepts all of them.

A uniform verifier for 3-SAT, requiring all unused witness padding bits to be zero, gives a parsimonious reduction of #3SAT and establishes NP-hard decision on this family. At h=k, where k is the number of classes below, N=Θ(k²), so conditioning Ω(√N) vertices still leaves every graph accepted. The output adjacency matrix uses Θ(N²) bits. These are hardness results on a promise family, not unrestricted circuit lower bounds.

## One clique per witness

Equip V with modes for normal operation, unconditional rejection, and acceptance exactly when w=0. The modified circuit has O(s+1) size. Use one color class per gate, with a vertex for every locally valid assignment to its incident wires respecting repeated wires. Add binary pin classes for every x bit, every witness bit (including unused bits), and the mode bits, as well as a singleton fixing the output to one. Fix constant wires at their prescribed values. Vertices in distinct classes are compatible precisely when they agree on every shared wire. Add one singleton anchor. Its edges to data and mode pins enforce their prescribed values by a literal or constant. All its other edges are present. Pad with universally compatible singleton classes until their number k≥13, counting the anchor. There are O(s+1) classes, vertices and controlled edge occurrences, since only pin tests at the anchor use data or mode literals.

A k-clique chooses one consistent vertex per class. The pins give (x,w,mode) and uniquely determine every gate value. Conversely every accepting w yields exactly one clique. This uses the classical local consistency graph construction; exact counts require coverage of every witness coordinate and deterministic gate extensions. [Feige, Goldwasser, Lovász, Safra and Szegedy](https://www.cs.tau.ac.il/~safra/PapersAndTalks/FGLSS.pdf) · [Kozen, Example 26.8](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/courses/cs682/2006sp/Handouts/138-150.pdf)

## Degree preservation and regularization

For each controlled edge uv introduce private u',v'. For a true literal use edges uv,u'v'; for false use uv',u'v. The private vertices have degree one and each old endpoint receives one edge in both cases. The labeled degree sequence is fixed across data and modes. The k-cliques are unchanged and the static k-coloring extends by giving u',v' the colors of u,v. This uses O(s+1) more vertices.

Choose D≥1 to be the maximum of one and all degrees in this graph A and the fixed B defined below. To regularize, take two halves of D copies with fixed labeled degrees d_i, and connect matching labels i across the halves by D−d_i disjoint cyclic perfect matchings. For left index j use right indices j+t modulo D, t=0,…,D−d_i−1. Every vertex has degree D. Copies may use different data and modes. An outside vertex cannot neighbor two distinct labels in one copy, and the copy-index graph is bipartite. Thus every triangle and every larger clique is internal to one copy. Cyclically shift A's static k-coloring on the entire right half: equal labels at opposite ends now have different colors. Its regularized graph remains k-colorable.

## A blocker with known theta

Let J be the complete join of five pentagons. Take B to be the complete join of a (k−11)-clique and the disjoint union of h+1 copies of J. Its clique number is k−1, its chromatic number k+4, and θ̄(B)=k−11+5√5>k by the join and disjoint-union rules and θ̄(C₅)=√5. [Lovász](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1979.1055985)

Regularize B in identical copies with the same D. The result B* contains B induced and maps homomorphically back to B. Map left copies identically and apply an automorphism π to right copies. Choose π which cycles the core clique (at least two vertices) and rotates each pentagon by one. Every vertex maps to a neighbor, so the intercopy matchings map to edges. Clique number, chromatic number and θ̄ of B* thus equal those of B. For the theta monotonicity, take any feasible Gram vectors for the trace-one program defining θ̄, orthogonal on graph nonedges. Sum them inside each homomorphism fiber. Within a fiber the vectors are mutually orthogonal, preserving the sum of squared norms. Across nonadjacent target vertices the summed vectors are orthogonal. The objective, the squared norm of the total sum, is unchanged.

## The final graph

For F(x) take B* disjoint from the regularized A whose exceptional copy uses the normal mode and whose other copies use reject mode. The k-clique count is the accepting witness count; the blocker gives a (k−1)-clique on every input. Its chromatic number and θ̄ are exactly those of B*, since every component from A is k-colorable. Make a separate fixed F⁺ of identical order by using unique-witness bypass mode on its exceptional copy. This has one k-clique. Take the complete join of F(x) and F⁺ with K=2k. Every K-clique must have k vertices per side, giving the claimed count. Chromatic and theta values add. This connected graph is regular of degree D+N/2. Removing fewer than N/2 vertices leaves at least one on each side and hence a connected join. Starting unlabeled one-dimensional color refinement from one color yields one color class at every round for every input of this order and degree.

For any queried set S with |S|≤h, pick an induced original B from each side. Each has an untouched five-pentagon J. Let T be the queried core vertices of the two chosen B copies. It is a clique. Unqueried core vertices joined to the untouched blocks remain in the common neighborhood of T outside S. Thus |T|+θ̄(residual) ≥2(k−11+5√5)>K. Every set S therefore has a successful branch T⊆S; upper theta estimates give the same result. This also describes one surviving transcript for adaptive include/exclude vertex queries, answering yes only on the chosen cores. The statement addresses this relaxation rule rather than stronger SDP cuts.

The base vertex counts and D are O(s+h+1). At h=k they are Θ(k) in the summed base count and in D, since the verifier classes have bounded sizes and the blocker core sees Ω(k) vertices. The construction gives the stated quadratic order. Related hardness of counting independent sets on regular graphs yields clique-counting hardness on diameter-two complements; the projection here enforces the additional exact invariants and witness counts.
