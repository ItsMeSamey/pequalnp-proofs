# Distance-three universal-pin instances and a three-dimensional trace

Delete the named universal vertex of the original graph and keep the remaining graph K without complementing. An even induced support through all three original pins corresponds precisely to an induced all-odd support in K through the two remaining names p,q. Indeed the universal neighbor flips every selected degree, and the handshaking lemma guarantees that any all-odd support has even size, as required by the universal pin.

Suppose p,q are nonadjacent with disjoint neighborhoods A,B and the join between A,B is complete. Write D for the other unnamed vertices. Selected subsets of A,B must each be odd. At a selected vertex of A, the occupied pin p and the odd number of selected B neighbors together contribute zero parity; the analogous calculation holds on B. Thus removing p,q and all edges of their complete join between A and B gives an exact problem: find an induced all-odd set with odd occupancy on each of A and B. All original selections are recovered by restoring p,q and then the universal vertex.

## A dense class with arbitrary incidence matrices

Assume each of A,B,D independently induces either a clique or an edgeless graph. No restriction is imposed on incidences A–D and B–D. On a solution the cliques on A and B contribute even degree at their selected sites, since each contains an odd number selected. Call their union the left side, D the right side, and write N for the arbitrary left-to-right incidence matrix. Let X,Y be the two occupancy vectors. Since X has even size and the entire residual support has even size, Y must be even. If D is a clique, its internal contribution to each selected right site is one; if D is edgeless it is zero. Set δ to zero in the former case and one in the latter. The selected left sites now require (NY)_i=1, the selected right sites require (NᵀX)_j=δ, and |Y| must be even. For empty or singleton D either graph description gives the same feasibility because |Y| must be even.

Use the [constructive three-dimensional binary trace algorithm](../../three-dimensional-trace/README.md). In the binary vector space on left sites plus one last coordinate take ordinary columns (e_i,0) for left sites and (N_j,1) for right sites, retaining separate column identities. Let a=(1_A,0), b=(1_B,0), c=(0,1). The named space is their span, and the prescribed functional takes values 1,1,δ on a,b,c. The target is a+b. Empty A or B rejects beforehand, so these prescriptions are consistent.

The trace supplies ordinary columns summing to the target and a functional extending the three prescriptions and annihilating every chosen column. Write the functional as (X,δ) and use the chosen right columns for Y. The left part of the sum implies the chosen unit columns are exactly the ones in 1+NY; their annihilation requires NY=1 at every selected left site. The chosen right columns impose the required δ on NᵀX, and the last summation coordinate gives even |Y|. The named functional values give the two odd class occupancies. Conversely, from any legal residual word take its selected right columns and the units in 1+NY. The same identities show they and (X,δ) witness the trace. This gives deterministic polynomial decision and physical witness recovery with arbitrary cross-incidence rank and unbounded degree.

## Why distance three alone remains the general problem

Given any all-odd instance with two nonadjacent compulsory sites a,b, add edge ab and introduce two new leaves p,q adjacent only to a,b respectively. Name p,q instead of a,b. Their odd-degree guards force a,b selected. Each old forced site has acquired two selected neighbors, and every other old selected vertex keeps its degree. Extension by the two leaves and projection are inverse. Thus even A={a}, B={b} and distance exactly three can encode the whole nonadjacent case; adding a named universal pin gives the corresponding native graphs. Adjacent compulsory sites already have the two-site witness. This equivalence is not a hardness result.

More generally if every A vertex has the same neighborhood in D, and likewise for B, odd occupancies on A,B make their influence on D identical to selecting any one representative of each. Summing the all-odd equations over a selected class cancels its internal edges and forces its common external selected-neighbor parity to one. Therefore any solution can be replaced by those two representatives and the same Y, irrespective of internal A,B,D edges. Conversely such a solution is an original solution after restoring the pins. This gives a smaller two-pin all-odd instance on D and the two representatives with the representative pair nonadjacent; its answer is not presumed known.
