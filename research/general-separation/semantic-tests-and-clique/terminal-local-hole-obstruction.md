# Distant switches defeat terminal-local common-hole enforcement

Fix any radius R and desired girth g. There are two biconnected subcubic graphs on the same labels, with identical radius-R neighborhoods of named, nonadjacent degree-two vertices x,y. One contains an induced cycle through both; the other does not. On the selected vertices of the successful cycle, the two adjacency matrices have identical selected-neighbor parity vectors on every original coordinate. This obstructs a fixed terminal-local replacement by a three-pin induced-even graph with faithful extension of the old selected word.

Start with disjoint chordless rims A,B of a common sufficiently large length L. Choose adjacent attachment vertices a0,a1 on A and b0,b1 on B. For i=0,1 connect ai to bi by new paths with disjoint interiors, each of length at least max(g,2). Place x on A and y on B away from the attachment vertices and choose one rim edge cd on A and one ef on B, both outside the radius-R neighborhoods of either terminal and disjoint from the attachments. One explicit choice is L divisible by four and greater than 8(R+g+2), attachments at positions 0,1, the respective named terminal at L/4 and the chosen edge at L/2,L/2+1 on each rim. This graph H0 is biconnected: deletion of a vertex of either rim leaves its other attachment and a connected rim remainder, and deletion inside a connecting path leaves the second path. It is subcubic, with girth at least g. No induced cycle goes through both x and y. Such a cycle would traverse both connecting paths and meet A along its long a0–a1 arc containing x; the edge a0a1 is a chord.

For H1 replace cd,ef by ce,df. This joins the two rims into a single chordless cycle when the interior vertices of the connecting paths are omitted. The two connecting paths are ears on this new cycle, so biconnectivity holds. Every cycle that uses an ear contains its entire length, and the rim has length 2L. The radius-R neighborhoods remain identical: reaching any added edge requires first reaching one of the four switched endpoints using unchanged edges, impossible within radius R.

Let s select all old rim vertices and none of the path interiors. In H1 this is the common induced cycle; in H0 it gives two separate induced cycles. On each switched endpoint the replacement removes one selected neighbor and adds one selected neighbor. All other adjacency rows agree. Consequently the guard values for s agree even before any local preprocessing.

More precisely, consider a proposed translation that acts identically on these two inputs except for carrying through the four distant original edge statuses; it may attach arbitrarily many vertices, select pins, and identically edit original incidences elsewhere. A radius-R replacement driven only by the common labeled terminal neighborhoods has this property when it leaves the distant edges untouched. If the translator is complete by extending the above H1 witness, so the four switched endpoints still have occupancy one in the native extension, carry the entire resulting native assignment to the identically labeled H0 output. All its guards and pin conditions still hold, since the only residual matrix difference annihilates the four selected bits. Soundness on H0 is impossible. The argument does not rule out translations that inspect the global graph to choose different edits, or witnesses encoded without these selected bits.

The [literal LLMT construction checker](llmt_two_terminal_cycles.py) supplies an independent source-specific failure for splitting x and pinning y. The present distant-switch obstruction concerns a class of faithful local replacements.
