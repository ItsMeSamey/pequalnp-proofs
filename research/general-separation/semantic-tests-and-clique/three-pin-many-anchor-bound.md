# A sharp bound on cross-checked anchors with three common pins

Use the fixed [bilateral chart](three-pin-bilateral-anchors.md). For the loopless symmetric binary matrix A of a simple graph, take vectors x¹,...,xᵏ on the same n named sites. Each has the same three pins selected and obeys the three pinned parity equations. Every pair of distinct indices must satisfy xⁱ AND (Axʲ)=0 and xʲ AND (Axⁱ)=0, where AND is coordinatewise. Thus each supplied dependency (xⁱ,Axⁱ) is actually covered by each of the other k−1 charts xʲ, using zero tags in K=[A|I].

If k>floor((n−3)/2), at least one of the supplied vectors is a physical witness, detectable in polynomial time by checking each on the original graph. Indeed the self-defect Dᵢ is the support of xⁱ AND (Axⁱ). This set avoids the three pins and has even cardinality by the handshake lemma on the vertices selected by xⁱ. Two self-defects cannot share a vertex because that would violate their cross-check. If none of the vectors is a witness, their disjoint defects use at least 2k of the n−3 free sites.

This threshold is exact. For k≥1 use the three pins u,c,v, with edges uc,cv. Add disjoint cliques of size k on a₁,...,aₖ and b₁,...,bₖ. Join each aᵢ to u, each bᵢ to v, and add no other edges. The graph is connected. Pin u forces an odd number of selected a vertices. Every selected a then has degree equal to that odd number (the other selected a vertices and u), so an original witness cannot exist. For each i take xⁱ consisting of the pins and aᵢ,bᵢ. It has precisely those two off-pin defects. Its cross-check with any other xʲ succeeds: u and v see two selected neighbors, c sees u and v, and aᵢ sees u and aⱼ while bᵢ sees v and bⱼ. This gives k mutually cross-checked anchors on 2k+3 vertices, in one fixed physical representation. More generally every pinned-parity guess selects odd subsets of both cliques. Two such guesses are mutually compatible exactly when their chosen subsets are disjoint in both cliques. This follows because, in each clique, a site with a neighbor anchor of odd clique weight sees odd parity exactly when it belongs to that anchor.

The bound tests a supplied portfolio. It does not give an algorithm constructing a large family on every YES instance, nor does it assert a lower bound on an algorithm using the original adjacency to choose an anchor. No second-dependency constraint with a prescribed alternating score has been represented in this statement.
