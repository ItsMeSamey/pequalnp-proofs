# Counting a quadratic frontier without expanding a dense outer AND

Let Q(x) be an explicitly certified list of Boolean quadratics on q primary bits, with original outer DAG D(y). Suppose D's accepting rows have a supplied or structurally derived representation as J disjoint affine cells over GF(2). An AND of affine tests has one cell even when its Walsh spectrum has full support. For each cell let kⱼ be the number of independent quadratic equations remaining after the elimination below. Its exact preimage count takes poly(input size) times 2^{kⱼ}; sum the disjoint counts. This replaces enumeration of all rows or all Walsh coefficients of D. The equation solver uses only ordinary elimination and the [quadratic sign-sum rule](original-dag-quadratic.md).

**Recognizing examples from the outer DAG.** Propagate an affine expression through affine gates. Its required output value defines one affine cell. Recursively intersect cells for conjunctions of tests, reducing equations by elimination after each intersection. A multiplexing gate with affine selector has disjoint arms after inserting the appropriate selector equation. When both arms already supply disjoint-cell lists, union these restricted lists; for conjunction, use all pairwise intersections and discard inconsistent cells. The new lists are internally disjoint by construction. Memoize shared gate results, aborting this recognition rule if a supplied intermediate list cap is exceeded. Its attempted work is polynomial in DAG size and cap, counting binary cell intersections. For general independently supplied lists, disjointness is easy to verify by intersections; equivalence to an arbitrary outer DAG requires its own certificate. None is assumed for an arbitrary unstructured list.

**Pullback and repeated elimination.** Substitute the quadratic vector into the cell's linear equations. Work in the quotient ring zᵢ²=zᵢ on an explicit affine parametrization of the current primary slice. Row-reduce the equations by their coefficients on the squarefree monomials of degree two, retaining their lower terms during row operations. The combinations with zero homogeneous quadratic part yield all affine consequences arising from row combinations. Check these affine equalities for inconsistency, then impose the independent ones by Gaussian parametrization. Rewrite all surviving equations on the new slice and repeat. Drop identically zero equations. Each nonterminal iteration drops the dimension by at least one. An inconsistent constant ends the count at zero. This algorithm uses polynomially many arithmetic operations before its final exponential step since all polynomials have degree at most two even after affine substitution.

At termination on dimension t, the remaining k rows have linearly independent homogeneous coefficient vectors. Writing their left sides (including target constants) as P₁,…,Pₖ, the count is

    2^(−k) Σ_{u∈GF(2)^k} Σ_{z∈GF(2)^t} (−1)^{ΣᵢuᵢPᵢ(z)}.

For k=0 return 2^t. Gaussian elimination of quadratic sign sums gives the asserted bound. One can alternatively brute-force a smaller residual slice. The count is exact because all slice parametrizations are bijections and each row operation preserves the common zero set.

**Full-spectrum example with successive reductions.** On independent primaries x₁,…,x_r, y₂,…,y_r and u₁,v₁,…,u_t,v_t, let H=Σⱼ uⱼvⱼ. Define the r+1 frontier wires H, E₁=H+x₁, and Eᵢ=H+xᵢ+xᵢ₋₁yᵢ for i≥2. A binary AND tree accepts when every frontier wire equals one. The gate computing H is shared by every Eᵢ. All r+1 frontier polynomials are independent modulo constants, witnessed by the xᵢ linear terms and the quadratic H. The outer AND has all 2^{r+1} Walsh coefficients nonzero. The homogeneous parts of the pulled-back rows initially have rank r for t≥1. Subtracting H's equation leaves x₁=0 as the sole independent affine consequence. On its slice x₂=0 follows, then x₃=0, and so on. Finally only H=1 remains; two sign sums suffice. The direct answer is 2^{r−1}(2^{2t}−2^t)/2. The original DAG has O(r+t) elementary gates even with high fanout of H. These claims concern the displayed frontier and AND; other DAG simplifications may also succeed.

As a countercontrol, requiring r disjoint products aᵢbᵢ all equal zero gives k=r at the first pass. Its count is 3^r by independence; the affine-elimination rule alone misses that factorization. Consequently the runtime parameter depends on the actual restricted equations, even for a one-cell outer predicate.
