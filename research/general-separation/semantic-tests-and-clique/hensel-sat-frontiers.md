# Exact SAT frontiers for polynomial systems with simple binary Jacobians

Fix b≥1. The input is a square system of explicitly represented, bounded-total-degree integer polynomials, with coefficient bit lengths charged to its size. The decision question is existence of a simultaneous root modulo 2^b. For algorithms producing b-bit residues or doing arithmetic at full precision we charge b, supplied in unary. Write J for the formal Jacobian reduced modulo two. Formal identity means equality in F₂[X], while pointwise identity requires equality after evaluating at every binary point. For the fixed degrees considered here either identity promise can be checked in polynomial time by collecting coefficients of the derivatives, with squarefree reduction in the pointwise case. The stated polynomial algorithms also apply when the promises are supplied.

| Jacobian restriction | Polynomial-time range | Parsimonious NP-hard range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Formal identity | Degree at most 3 | Degree 4 |
| Identity pointwise on binary inputs | Degree at most 2 | Degree 3 |
| Formally block unitriangular with (J−I)²=0 | Degree at most 1 | Degree 2 |

In each hard construction the number of roots at every precision equals the number of satisfying assignments of the original circuit. Decision is in NP when b is charged. At unrestricted b≥1 the constructions already decide the base problem modulo two. General binary quadratic equation solving provides classical context; the reductions below start directly from CircuitSAT. [Bardet, Faugère, Salvy and Spaenlehauer](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.6263)

**Lifting.** At a root u mod 2^j, every candidate lift is u+2^j v for a binary vector v. Expanding modulo 2^(j+1) gives the linear condition J(u)v=−F(u)/2^j modulo two. A square invertible J gives exactly one child per root, so root counts at every precision equal the base count. If the base system is affine as a Boolean function, Gaussian elimination decides existence, produces a base solution, and gives the count as zero or a power of two. Iterating the lift yields a full solution. For an overdetermined system all residual equations must be checked. An invertible selected subsystem only proposes a candidate lift.

**Easy degrees.** A polynomial over F₂ has all formal partial derivatives zero exactly when every exponent in every supported monomial is even. If J is formally the identity and total degree is at most three, subtract the coordinate variables. Only a constant and individual squares can remain in each equation. At binary points these give affine equations. At degree at most two, every derivative is affine, and an affine polynomial vanishing at every binary point is identically zero. This proves the second easy range. Degree-one systems in the last row are directly linear. In one variable X²−X and X²−X+1 illustrate that formal derivative one allows every binary root or none. [Artin–Schreier map](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0A3J)

**A sparse quadratic hardness construction.** Let an acyclic Boolean circuit have primary inputs x and gate outputs y. Use integer polynomials G of degree at most two for binary gates that compute their Boolean values exactly on bits, such as ab for AND and a+b−ab for OR. For every wire w add one variable s_w and the equation s_w−w²=0. For every gate use y−G(s_inputs)=0. For each primary input use x_i²−x_i=0, except add the term 1−out² to the first such equation. A circuit with zero primary inputs can be evaluated directly and represented by X=0 for YES or X²−X+1=0 for NO.

This is square, of size linear in the circuit, with at most three variables in each equation for the usual AND/OR/NOT basis. On the binary cube it exactly enforces circuit consistency and output one, with a unique extension of every satisfying input. Its formal Jacobian is I+L, where the only off-diagonal entries occur in gate-output rows and squared-wire columns. These two variable sets are disjoint. Thus L²=0 identically and a single reordering makes J block unitriangular. The inverse of J is J itself over F₂. Each Boolean satisfying assignment with its consistent wires is already an integer root, so all its unique lifts are those same binary representatives. All solutions at every precision arise this way.

**Formal identity in degree four.** Omit the s variables and substitute the squared operand variables directly into every gate polynomial, retaining the primary equations. The formal derivatives of the composed operands vanish modulo two, so J is formally I. The same consistency and unique-lifting proof applies. The degree is at most four.

**Pointwise identity in degree three.** In the multilinear polynomial for each binary gate replace a linear term a by a² and a product ab by a²b+ab²−ab. The replacement yields the correct integer Boolean values; the two derivatives of the product vanish at all binary parities, since they reduce to b²+b and a²+a. The gate outputs remain their own unsquared variables on the left. Keep the primary equations and output anchor. This gives pointwise identity and degree at most three, with the same count.

**A positive SAT compiler would need a stronger property.** A polynomial-time solvability-preserving compiler into the formal-identity cubic class would decide SAT by the affine base algorithm. Requiring only formal identity in quartic degree, pointwise identity in cubic degree, or explicit inverse Jacobians in quadratic degree already permits every CircuitSAT instance. Hensel lifting solves the higher-precision step; determining which parity vectors are roots is exactly where these reductions place SAT.
