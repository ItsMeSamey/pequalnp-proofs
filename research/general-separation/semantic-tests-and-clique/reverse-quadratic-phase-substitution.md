# Reverse product substitution with affine supports

Normalize the binary DAG to primary bits x, products z_i=A_i B_i of affine operands in x and earlier z, and an affine output. To count, evaluate the unnormalized sign sum by processing products in reverse order. Each recursion node carries an affine support H and a phase χ(P)=(−1)^P, where P is quadratic except for optional cubic terms involving only primary bits. Its value sums that sign on H subject to the remaining product definitions. Start with full support and the designated output phase. Empty supports have value zero.

Canonicalize supports using descending-order Gaussian pivots with all z above x; each pivot depends only on lower free coordinates. Substitute the pivot expressions into the phase and operands. Primary pivots cannot introduce product variables into primary-only cubic terms.

* If the highest coordinate z is free, H is a cylinder along it. Write P=P_0+zD on the free coordinates with D affine. Substituting z=AB gives P_0+ABD. The cubic part of ABD vanishes precisely when the linear parts of A,B,D have rank at most two, since its squarefree cubic coefficients are their three-by-three minors over GF(2). Fold it without branching in that case. More generally fold if the resulting cubic terms involve only primary bits. If neither test succeeds, use χ(P_0+ABD)=χ(P_0)−2·1[A=B=D=1]χ(P_0). One child keeps the projected support; the other imposes three independent affine constraints, and both keep P_0. The rank reduction is in the remaining formal space and need not fix three original input bits.
* If H pins z to λ in terms of earlier coordinates, substitute λ into the phase and project the support. The product check λ=AB is exactly the disjoint union of A=0,λ=0 and A=1,λ=B. Intersect these affine cells with the support and discard empty children. A and B are interchangeable in this rule.

At a leaf parameterize the primary support injectively. A quadratic phase has a polynomial-time binary Gauss sum. For a cubic use the deterministic degree-three exact counter of [Lokshtanov et al., Theorem 1.2](https://theory.stanford.edu/~yuhch123/files/polyEq.pdf). If there are n free primary bits the cost is poly(q+m) times 2^{(1−δ)n+o(n)} for fixed δ>0. Convert the number of ones to a sign sum by subtracting twice that count from 2^n. Combine the signed node weights and recover the root accepting count as (2^q−bias)/2. Polynomial bit work per node plus the sum of actual leaf clocks gives the runtime. With no branches, no support constraints and only deferred primary cubics, the exact output itself is degree at most three, and SAT alone is decidable by evaluating on all inputs of weight at most three. After signed branches that decision shortcut cannot be used independently at leaves due to cancellation.

The [finite checker](reverse_phase_substitution_check.py) verifies all 4,096 affine triples on three bits and 128 full exact-count comparisons on 64 seeded four-product DAGs. Across those comparisons it recorded 1,060 nodes, 229 nonempty leaves, 117 signed splits and 148 pinned splits. Its explicit complete tables and conservative ambient degree tests are for validation, not large-instance execution. For the live three-gate control [(11,3,2),(14,0,2),(11,4,5)] on four primary bits (the [original gate corpus](original_dag_portfolio.py) encoding), truth mask 0xfffa has 14 ones and degree three. Quadratic-only reverse branching took eight nodes/four leaves; primary cubic deferral took seven nodes/three leaves, both giving 14. This single-node improvement makes no asymptotic runtime claim. A uniform sub-brute bound for arbitrary q log q gates, or a hierarchy consequence from these corpus statistics, remains unproved.
