# Fixed pinned induced-even support on line graphs

For each fixed h there is a polynomial-time search algorithm for a vertex set containing h prescribed vertices and inducing all degrees even in the line graph of any simple loopless graph B. A labeled root may be supplied and checked, or recovered by classical line-graph recognition. The proof uses Huynh's fixed-group disjoint-paths theorem. The output consists of the physical names of the selected edges of B, considered as vertices in the original line graph. [Roussopoulos](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(73)90029-X) [Huynh](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34307)

Let F denote the pinned edges of B and W their endpoints. At a selected edge uv its degree in the induced line graph is deg_D(u)+deg_D(v)−2 for the selected edge set D, because B is simple. Thus the two endpoint degrees must have the same parity. In any solution color each base vertex by its D-degree parity. Every edge of D joins like colors. Guess colors at W, requiring equality across F, and partition W into nonempty monochromatic classes. The number of guesses depends only on h.

A certificate for a guess consists of vertex-disjoint trees in B−F, one per class U, containing all of U and no other named vertex. Give that tree its class color α. Require that the sum of deg_F(v) over U equals α times the tree order modulo two. Singleton trees are allowed. In each tree the residual target degree parity at v is α+deg_F(v), taking forced degree zero at unnamed vertices. The sum is even, so leaf elimination gives a tree-edge subset attaining those parities. Add the subsets to F. Each selected tree edge joins two sites with equal degree parity, and this also holds on F by the guess. Chords in B do not have to be selected. Every adjacency between selected edges in the original line graph is included by the endpoint-degree formula.

Conversely take a solution D and the components of B−F on the vertices of each degree color. Partition W by its components. In any component meeting W, handshake on D−F gives the required parity for the whole component. Within it connect the named sites by a minimal subtree T, possibly a singleton. For α=1, if its order has the wrong parity, attach one adjacent vertex of the component outside T by one edge. Such a vertex exists because of the differing orders modulo two and connectivity. The corrected tree has the right parity and at most one leaf beyond its named sites. For α=0 no correction is needed. The original components were disjoint and those without names need no tree.

To search, suppress all unnamed degree-two vertices. All leaves except at most one per tree are named, and the number of branch vertices is at most the number of leaves, with singleton trees handled separately. Thus the number of skeleton sites and edges is O(h). Enumerate abstract skeletons and injectively assign the unnamed retained sites in B−F. For each skeleton edge guess its path length parity. Each tree order is one plus the sum of those lengths. Enumerate three cases per skeleton edge. For length one verify the direct edge. For length two guess one internal site. For length at least three guess its distinct first and last internal sites, checking the two end edges. Require all internal sites guessed in these steps to be distinct across all paths and distinct from all skeleton vertices. Delete the skeleton vertices and the reserved length-two internal sites. For the remaining pairs, find vertex-disjoint paths realizing the guessed parities: both removed end edges together contribute zero modulo two. Give every remaining edge gain one in the group of order two in any orientation. Huynh's constructive fixed-pairs theorem applies directly. When no pairs remain, there is only the short-path verification. Lift the returned paths and use the tree incidences to reconstruct the selected edges. The guessing costs |V(B)|^{O(h)} times a function of h, and the linkage calls are polynomial for each fixed h. No induced-path assumption on B enters the proof.

K₁,₃ with two spokes pinned illustrates why the extra leaf is needed: its line graph is a triangle, and the third spoke must be selected. A three-edge path gives a NO line graph on pinning its three vertices. Both [high-connectivity anchor fixtures](high-connectivity-anchor-obstruction.md) lie outside the class: a left-shore vertex and three pairwise nonadjacent right seven-cycle sites form an induced claw. A simple-root line graph contains none, since the neighbors of an edge lie in its two endpoint cliques. Accordingly recognition declines these fixtures without converting them to an unrelated ordinary linkage problem.
