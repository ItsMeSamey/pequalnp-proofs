# Three specified vertices in an induced even subgraph of a line graph

Given a finite simple graph B and at most three mandatory edges P, one can deterministically find an induced even subgraph of L(B) containing the corresponding vertices, or prove that none exists, in polynomial time. The order, degrees and treewidth of B are unrestricted.

For an edge set D containing P, the degree of uv in the induced line graph on D is deg_D(u)+deg_D(v)−2. Thus every nontrivial connected component of (V(B),D) must have the same degree parity throughout. Components containing no mandatory edge can be discarded. Any component containing a single mandatory edge can be replaced by just that edge, regardless of its former parity. The remaining nontrivial components still have disjoint vertex sets.

Enumerate a subset T of mandatory edges to keep as single-edge components. Require their endpoint pairs to be mutually disjoint and to avoid all endpoints of P∖T. Remove their endpoints from B. Seek a completion of P∖T on the surviving graph in each of two modes.

* In the even mode set the degrees of all vertices to zero modulo two and force the mandatory edge variables to one. Solve the incidence equations by binary Gaussian elimination.
* In the odd mode allow degrees in {0,1,3,5,…}. At a vertex with mandatory degree c remove its c mandatory incidences, and give it the list of residual degrees j between zero and its residual degree bound for which j+c belongs to the allowed set. An empty list rejects this mode. No list has an internal gap longer than one missing integer. The deterministic polynomial general-factor algorithm applies to precisely these residual lists. A decision implementation yields an actual factor by successively deleting an optional edge whenever the current lists remain feasible, truncating lists to current degree bounds. In the final inclusion-minimal feasible available edge set, every available edge occurs in a solution. [Cornuéjols, General factors of graphs](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0095895688900688)

Append T and return the resulting line-graph support. Every output is valid since the single edges are isolated from the homogeneous completion. For completeness take T from all single-mandatory-edge components in a witness. With at most three prescribed edges, at most one remaining component contains two or more of them, so one of the homogeneous modes suffices. The modes do not need to impose connectivity. The empty prescription is immediate.

More generally the same algorithm is complete for any number of mandatory edges if their edge-induced subgraph has at most one nonsingleton component and at most one isolated edge outside it: the prescribed edges of the nonsingleton component cannot be split across witness components. With four arbitrarily placed edges one may have two distinct nonsingleton groups, so the three-edge proof by itself does not apply. In the three-active-site anisotropic construction the algorithm applies when the recovered literal input is given by such a line graph. There is no assumption that an arbitrary exterior is a line graph.
