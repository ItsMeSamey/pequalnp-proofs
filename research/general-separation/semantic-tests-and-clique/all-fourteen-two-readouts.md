# Two affine readouts for arbitrary fourteen-state port tables

Each nonloop edge of a multigraph has an explicit table on two endpoint occupancy bits and two incident defect bits, containing at least 14 of 16 states. Every vertex meets a table. On each occupied vertex the XOR of its incident defects must equal an affine function of all occupancies. The linear coefficient matrix is symmetric off the diagonal. With at most two further affine equations on occupancies, there is a deterministic polynomial-time decision and witness algorithm. It returns original occupancies and one row per original edge. Supplied private realizations on disjoint edge interiors lift these rows to site witnesses.

## Ordinary components and directed edges

If a fixed occupancy forces the defect of a selected endpoint, the two missing states spend the whole budget at this occupancy. The edge is directed with this endpoint as tail. Its defect can be chosen at its baseline (the forced value), and when selected it can be flipped if the head has the opposite occupancy from the trigger. Its head defect can be chosen independently. Call all other edges ordinary. At a single occupied endpoint its ordinary defect can be chosen arbitrarily. At occupancy 11 an ordinary edge has full unary projections, so contains a complementary pair differing by (1,1). This also covers restrictions on unoccupied endpoints.

Take components using ordinary edges, including singleton sites. Let U be the union of components containing any directed head, and S be its complement. U can satisfy all its occupied parities regardless of its occupancies and the directed-tail defects. In each occupied region use the (1,1) toggles along an ordinary spanning tree to correct every even change vector. An ordinary edge to an unoccupied site gives one free bit. If the region is its entire component, an independently adjustable head supplies that free bit. The same proof shows that in an S-component ANY proper occupancy subset satisfies every demand. All directed edges at S point into U. A singleton S-component can be occupied if any of its heads releases it, and otherwise obeys the usual affine parity guard with its incident baseline constants incorporated. Every singleton S-site has at least one release literal.

## One readout involving S

Permit arbitrary affine restrictions on y, the occupancies of U. If there is no readout on S take S empty and solve for y. Otherwise write the one readout as β·x=b(y). If some permitted y has b=0 take x=0. Otherwise b=1 on the nonempty affine slice. If a marked site (β_i=1) belongs to a nonsingleton S-component, select that site alone. Hence all marks can now be assumed to lie on singleton S-components T. Write z for occupancies of the remaining nonsingletons. Taking z=0 or one unit z leaves all these components proper, for every choice on T and U.

For fixed y,z ignore z's own guards for the moment. On T apply the [one-readout component test](canonical-fourteen-two-readouts.md). A marked component D of the symmetric exterior drift graph on T fails to produce a witness exactly when all of D is guarded and its prescribed vector c_D(y,z) equals β_D. The first condition fixes its incident heads to their trigger values. The second is affine, including all baseline constants. Failure on every marked component is thus a single affine solution set in (y,z), possibly empty. Outside it the constructive marked-path argument supplies an occupancy on T. It suffices to try z=0 and each unit z. If for all these choices and every allowed y the test fails, at each fixed y the failure space contains their affine span, covering all z. Success lifts because every tested z is proper. For fixed z, find y by testing scalar violations of the defining failure equations on the permitted affine slice. Newly designated independently released singleton sites can also be included on the y-side.

## Two independent columns on S

Apply Gaussian elimination and the preceding algorithm if the S-columns have rank at most one. At rank two, a pair of independent columns suffices if both sites' occupancies can be freely chosen. Two singleton sites suffice if they have compatible release literals. A singleton and a nonsingleton site suffice by releasing the former and leaving another site of the latter's component zero. Two sites in different nonsingleton components suffice, as do two distinct sites in one component of order at least three, by leaving a spare zero in each component used. Try all such pairs. Fix compatible head values and set all other S-sites to zero. The two independent columns can produce the prescribed readout for the chosen y. There is no additional y-slice in this initial rank-two case.

If none succeeds and an independent pair consists of two singleton sites, every nonzero S-column lies on a singleton (it is independent of at least one member of this pair). The [incompatible literal argument](signed-fourteen-two-readouts.md) gives precisely two nonzero column classes and one head bit h. Release literals in each class prescribe the same value on h, opposite between the classes. Branch on h and move the entire released class to the independently adjustable y-side. The S-rank drops to at most one. The remaining y-slice incorporates the branch and the original readouts after row elimination. Use the preceding algorithm, including its unmarked nonsingleton helpers.

Otherwise an independent pair a,b is itself an entire ordinary component of size two. These are the only nonzero S-columns, since every third nonzero column is independent of at least one and lies outside the pair. Enumerate their four occupancies; each fixes an affine slice of y from the two original readouts. If their occupancy is proper, choose other S-sites zero and solve for y. For occupancy 11 use the following steps.

1. At each of a,b nominate no directed-tail deviation or one incident edge to deviate from its baseline. A nomination imposes its release literal on y. This covers every total directed contribution at the endpoint, since only the XOR of deviations matters and each nominated edge can flip independently.
2. Compute the set K of possible total two-bit ordinary defects at the pair using the internal parallel edges at occupancy 11. A four-state dynamic program stores actual rows. K has at least two points because one edge supplies two choices with all other edges fixed. K is a union of affine lines, since every point pairs with a second point. Enumerate the at most six lines contained in K.
3. The required two-bit ordinary defect is affine in all other occupancies after fixing a,b and the two directed-deviation parities. Each line costs just one affine equation. Remove the pair, add its fixed occupancy contributions to remaining drifts and apply the one-readout algorithm to the other S-components. Retain the previous independently adjustable y-side throughout: the head incidences on deleted directed edges are still available to correct y in the final reconstruction. The original readouts and release literals are y-only affine restrictions.

A successful branch gives the line's actual point, stored internal rows and nominated tail defects. Restoring the directed heads and ordinary spanning-tree choices gives all original rows. Every original witness lands in a branch with its total tail parities and a line through its aggregate ordinary defects. There are at most quadratically many edge nominations. All pair searches, finite-table steps, graph searches and binary linear systems have polynomial bit cost.

With three readouts, even one fixed fourteen-state physical box already represents the native [three-pin induced-even problem](fourteen-port-uniform-boundary.md). Its unrestricted complexity remains unresolved.
