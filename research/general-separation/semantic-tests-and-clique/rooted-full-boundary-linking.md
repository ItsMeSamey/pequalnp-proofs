# Linking three original pins to a verified full boundary

Let P be three distinct pins of a simple graph and X a disjoint site set of binary cut rank three. Write W for its complement and factor A[X,W]=UVᵀ with full-column-rank binary factors. A selection x on X sends s=Uᵀx to W and receives t=Vᵀy from the selection y. Suppose we have actual X-witnesses for each pair s,t with s·t=0, where every selected inner site i has internal degree parity U_i·t. There are 36 such pairs. This is a checkable positive premise. For example, if every nonzero color occurs among rows of U, empty or singleton selections suffice. One may also search witnesses up to any fixed size.

A deterministic polynomial algorithm either constructs an induced-even set containing P or produces a pin-free cut of rank at most two containing X. On a graph with all [rank-two pin-free sides of size at least four eliminated](rank-two-cut-replacement.md), a full boundary of size at least four therefore suffices for YES. No unknown boundary entries serve as an oracle.

Make a simple auxiliary graph on W and three independent new ports Q, with original W adjacency and W-to-Q cross block V. For any P⊆Z⊆W its cut rank here equals its physical cut rank: the Q columns are replaced in the original graph by linear combinations using the full-row-rank matrix Uᵀ. If some such Z has rank at most two, return the complementary physical cut. Otherwise the minimum separating cut rank is three, since P has size three.

By Oum's Theorem 6.1 this minimum is attainable as cut rank in a pivot-minor on precisely the named labels P∪Q. The proof gives an effective procedure: at any unnamed v compare deleting v and pivoting on an edge to any neighbor followed by deleting v. At least one preserves the minimum; for isolated v use deletion. The minimum can be evaluated in polynomial time by submodular minimization or by testing ranks zero, one and two through anchored row and column bases and directed implications. Record all labeled pivots and deletions. [Oum, Theorem 6.1](https://mathsci.kaist.ac.kr/~sangil/pdf/vertexminor.pdf)

Use the principal-pivot convention: an edge pivot swaps the local coordinates x_i and y_i=(Ax)_i at each of its two endpoints in the graph of the linear map. This is the Schur principal-pivot transform on the nonsingular edge pair, retaining the vertex labels. A deletion retains the old pairs on surviving sites when the deleted site's input is zero. Transport the required pin symbols (1,0) forward under the endpoint swaps. On the final six sites choose the prescribed pin inputs and outputs. The cross matrix from ports to pins is invertible, so solve three linear equations for the port inputs and compute their outputs by the graph equation. The pins each have input times output zero. The virtual port products need only sum to zero; this follows from alternation of the whole adjacency.

Lift backward through the recorded operations. For a reversed deletion insert input zero and calculate its missing output. For a reversed pivot swap the endpoint coordinates. These operations preserve the real sites' local zero products, return the pins to (1,0), and preserve the sum of the three virtual input-output products even if a pivot touches a port. In the initial auxiliary graph the port inputs and outputs are exactly s and t for W, as there are no internal port edges. Substitute the stored X witness for this pair. All real W outputs stay unchanged and the X selection has the required internal parity under its actual outside signal. The result is a physical witness on original labels. Requiring an individual zero product on every virtual site would impose an additional condition.

To discover all-seven-color certificates or short-witness certificates of a fixed size h without a supplied cut, enumerate disjoint triples I,J with invertible A[I,J], I inside, J and P outside. Any v inside, w outside must satisfy A_vw=A[v,J] A[I,J]⁻¹ A[I,w]. A failed ordered equation gives a directed implication from v to w for membership of the inside. Exclude all vertices reaching the forced outside; the remaining maximal shore works if it contains I. The colors on every feasible shore with these anchors can be taken as rows A[v,J]. Enlarging to the maximal shore preserves all existing witnesses: their induced graph and colors on their support are unchanged. The change of color coordinates from another full factorization is invertible and relabels the 36 pairs bijectively. Check the maximal shore for all colors or all 36 witnesses of size at most h and retain actual representatives. Enumerating all rank-three basis choices and testing subsets up to fixed h takes polynomial time.
