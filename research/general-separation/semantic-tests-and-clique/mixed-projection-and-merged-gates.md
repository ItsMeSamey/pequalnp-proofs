# A missing quadratic moment and exact composition of its two-port graph

In a simple graph of adjacency A with binary prescribed parity c_i at each selected vertex, the native guards are x_i((Ax)_i+c_i)=0. A universally forbidden pair of selected vertices need not give a quadratic consequence derivable at formal degree three. The following example also realizes a fifteen-state relation on two open sites. When copies of the example share original sites, its network has a constructive component criterion.

## Projection and pinning give different proof questions

Use the eight vertices numbered 0 through 7, adjacency row masks (38,97,129,208,40,147,138,108), and mixed parity mask c=21. There are 23 legal selections. None contains both 0 and 7. The [finite proof calculation](mixed_quadratic_projection.py) reports whether the quadratic x₀x₇ belongs to the full iterative formal-degree closure of the unconditioned guards.

| Degree | Total closure rank | Dimension through degree two | Contains x₀x₇ |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2 | 15 | 15 | no |
| 3 | 69 | 15 | no |
| 4 | 140 | 16 | yes |

The checker uses monomials ordered by degree, echelon reduction and multiplication of eligible parents, cancelling repeated Boolean variables at the formal product degree. With x₀=x₇=1 appended as equations, degree two already refutes. An actual loopless three-pin NO on eleven vertices has adjacency rows (294,97,1153,208,1064,147,138,1644,513,384,148) with pins 8,9,10. The first two pins are adjacent and their free neighborhoods are masks 1,128. The third has neighborhood 148=c+1+128 in binary XOR. The pinned guards impose the two selections and one redundant linear equation, since summing mixed free guards gives c·x=0. The free guards agree with the displayed mixed system after pin substitution. Its raw linear pin system is consistent, and its actual physical refutation is of degree two. These checks directly distinguish the degree-four unconditioned projection from the conditioned graph refutation.

For c=0 every quadratic vanishing on *all* legal words is already a sum of native guards modulo the Boolean equations at degree two. Indeed the empty word and singletons remove the constant and linear terms. Every nonedge pair is legal and removes its coefficient. The remaining quadratic is an edge weighting vanishing on all induced chordless cycles. Splitting a cycle at a chord shows that chordless cycles span the cycle space. The edge weighting is therefore a cut: one may define a vertex potential by summing the weights on a spanning-tree path from the component root, and each nontree edge agrees by the cycle condition. Summing the guards with these vertex coefficients gives the polynomial. The eight-site example shows the role of mixed degree prescriptions in this exact quadratic description.

## Exposed endpoints

Drop the two guards at sites 0,7 and keep their actual selection bits a,b and their raw defects e,f, where a raw defect is the internal neighbor parity plus the local prescribed c. Six private sites remain. Enumerating the 256 words with their six private guards gives all sixteen tuples (a,b,e,f) except (1,1,0,0). Thus for independent external drifts at the endpoints the full gadget always extends except when both endpoints are selected and both drifts vanish. One may store one realizing private word per allowed tuple. This raw assertion records both defect bits even at an unselected endpoint.

Take an arbitrary loopless multigraph H. For each edge install one oriented copy of this graph, identifying its two endpoints with the corresponding vertices of H. Private sites of distinct copies remain disjoint. There is no direct edge between 0 and 7 in the gadget, so even parallel copies give a simple physical host. At a shared site prescribe the XOR of all its incident copies' local constants, with additional drift g_v. Drift may also include the contribution of additional physical edges among shared sites. The result below holds pointwise even when all g_v are affine in the exposed selection bits. It requires readouts on these exposed bits only.

For prescribed exposed occupancy a and drift g, there is a full extension unless some *whole tree component* C of H has

    a_v=1 and g_v=1+deg_H(v) modulo two at every v in C.

In particular a connected component containing a cycle imposes no restriction on its terminals in the projection. This includes a pair of parallel edges.

For a proof, on each edge whose two ends are selected, at least one of the two incident raw defect bits must be one. Complement both end bits on such an edge. At most one end can then receive a mark from that edge. For an edge with exactly one selected end, its bit at that end is free and can be counted as a separate slack incidence; its unselected end imposes no requirement. Complement also this selected-end bit so that at each selected vertex the required parity of marks is g_v+deg_H(v). A vertex requiring odd parity needs one incident resource, a marked edge or slack incidence. Conversely distinct resources for all such vertices suffice: give each exactly one mark and leave all others unmarked.

In each connected component of the graph on selected sites, a spanning tree rooted at an unrequired vertex supplies distinct parent edges to every required vertex. If every vertex is required, a slack incidence can cover its incident root, and all others use tree edges. If there is a cycle instead, orient it cyclically and extend by tree branches to cover all vertices. The remaining obstruction is precisely a fully required tree with no slack. Having no slack means it is an entire component of H. There are too few edges to cover its whole vertex set. The construction gives all needed original endpoint bits and the stored representatives reconstruct the six private sites of every copy. The [independent formula replay](merged_pair_gate_check.py) checks 411,760 occupancy/drift cases.

## Fixed-readout witness search

Assume every exposed site belongs to at least one copy and there are r affine readout equations on the exposed bits. Additional drifts may be arbitrary given affine forms of these bits. If a solution exists there is one with at most 2r selected exposed sites. For a minimum-weight solution of weight W, let K be the number of fully selected tree components. For each of those choose one vertex witnessing the required escape from the bad drift, and retain its one affine drift equation. Restrict all originally unselected bits to zero. Any solution of the r+K retained affine equations remains valid because unselecting sites cannot activate an additional full tree and each previously full tree either loses a site or retains its escape. A consistent linear system with at most r+K rows has a solution of weight at most r+K, by expressing its target using a column basis. Thus W≤r+K. Every component in question has at least two vertices and they are disjoint, so W≥2K and W≤2r. Enumerating these supports takes N^{O(r)} bit time for the supplied network and readouts. Reconstruction uses the component algorithm.

For r=2 the sharp bound is three. Discard dependent equations after testing consistency. Target zero uses the empty word and a column equal to a nonzero target gives a singleton; any singleton is feasible on the stipulated networks. In the remaining rank-two case a linear solution needs a column of each of the two other nonzero types. Select such a pair. Unless its support is exactly a two-vertex tree component it is immediately valid. If every choice of such a pair is a whole two-vertex tree, both column types occur only once at those same two vertices. They are forced to one and all remaining columns are zero. If their initial pair has bad drift, a solution exists only if one of their two escape drifts varies on the other bits. Turning on one variable with nonzero coefficient in that drift suffices; it cannot fully select another nontrivial tree by itself. For sharpness take two disjoint one-edge components ab,zc. Force a=b=1 and add an external edge az, making g_a=z, g_z=a with other additional drifts zero. Weight two fails and setting z=1 works. These conclusions concern this graph construction and its projected decision problem. They supply neither a general three-pin decision procedure nor a standard SAT reduction.
