# An affine encoder obstruction for the full rank-two table

Let a pin-free graph X have binary-plane labels u_i. Write s for the label sum of the selected vertices and t for the incoming two-bit signal. A legal selection x satisfies (A_X x)_i=u_i·t at each selected i. Suppose its table is to be encoded in the canonical gadget of three independent vertices of colors 1,2,3, preserving s,t. Write q for the selected bit at color 3. The other two bits must be s_1+q and s_2+q. At each nonzero t the canonical bits are unique: q is zero at the two coordinate signals and equals s_1 at t=(1,1).

With both input signal bits available, an affine choice of q from x,t on every legal input exists exactly when the labels in each connected component of X span dimension at most one. Indeed, taking x=0 for all three nonzero signals forces the affine term involving only t and the constant to vanish. Thus q=Lx. At the first coordinate signal q vanishes on every support with prescribed degree parities equal to the first label bit. By the [span theorem](symmetric-single-row-p3.md), on each connected component D the restriction L_D lies in the span of the first label row on D. The second coordinate signal gives the analogous condition for the second row. Finally the signal (1,1) implies L_D plus the first row lies in the span of the sum of the two rows. If the two rows are independent the first two constraints give L_D=0 and the last one fails. Conversely, when all nonzero labels within each component have the same color, send the parity of each color's selected sites to its canonical vertex. Handshaking in each component makes every one of these choices legal. They preserve the outgoing signal.

Even allowing all exterior selection bits in the affine map can fail in a physical graph. Let the first three vertices form a triangle of colors 1,2,3. Leave the next three independent with the same three colors, and join the two triples by the binary dot product of their colors. The native adjacency masks are 46,53,27,5,6,3. Both shores have full tables by empty and singleton witnesses; there are ten handshake-allowed states. The six selection masks 8,10,16,17,32,35 induce even subgraphs. In their respective order the exterior signals are 1,1,2,2,3,3 and the interior outgoing signals are 0,2,0,1,0,3. Preserving the boundary therefore requires six q-values 0,0,0,0,0,1. The six native inputs XOR to zero; there are an even number of them. The six values of any affine function on these inputs must also XOR to zero. The construction can include three isolated compulsory vertices, since their coordinates are constant.

This rules out a universal affine legal-word encoder into the canonical independent gadget with both signals preserved. It does not show a change in refutation degree or rule out degree-preserving proofs that do not encode every legal word by an affine map.
