# Independent private-edge predicates with a large shared affine input space

For each vertex of the complete graph on n vertices take a public bit x_v. For each edge e=uv take a private bit z_e and the predicate h_e=z_e XOR x_u x_v. For fixed public bits, the map from private bits to predicate values is a translation. Thus the m=binom(n,2) predicates are jointly uniform on uniform inputs. Any specified pattern occurs exactly 2^n times.

Let F_S be the conjunction of the predicates in a subset S. Its satisfying inputs require z_e=1+x_u x_v for e in S. A period translating the public and private coordinates by d and t respectively must satisfy t_e=d_u x_v+d_v x_u+d_u d_v for all public x and each e in S. Independence of the two endpoint variables forces d_u=d_v=t_e=0. These conditions suffice as well. Consequently the minimum linear space of input forms whose values determine F_S is spanned by all the private coordinates in S and all their public endpoints. Indeed the annihilator of any sufficient space consists of periods, and this particular space suffices.

In a partition S,T of the edges, let b count the vertices incident to both classes. An S-exclusive vertex and a T-exclusive vertex cannot coexist, since the edge joining them would violate one exclusivity condition. Thus one class has both endpoints of every edge among those b vertices. The sufficient affine spaces on the two sides must therefore intersect in dimension at least b, with binom(b,2) at least min(|S|,|T|). Equality in the latter bound occurs when the smaller class is a clique on b vertices and the complementary edges touch them all.

In any binary conjunction tree with nonempty disjoint factor blocks as leaves, each block of size at most m/3, some subtree contains between m/3 and 2m/3 original predicates. Starting at the root, descend into a largest child while the current node exceeds 2m/3. That child exceeds m/3, and the process ends. The affine space shared by this subtree function and the conjunction of the remaining factors has dimension at least (1+sqrt(1+4n(n−1)/3))/2. Equivalent rewrites confined to either side preserve this semantic statement. The cut compares a subtree to its full complement. An unbalanced tree can have small overlap between immediate children throughout. Global transformations can exploit the private translation directly.
