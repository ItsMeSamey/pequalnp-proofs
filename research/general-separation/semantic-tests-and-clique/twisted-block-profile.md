# A coupled multiplication bound on a matching restriction of CLIQUE

Let K_d(f) minimize the number of AND gates of a Boolean XOR–AND circuit computing f+p, over all GF(2) polynomials p of degree smaller than d. Negation and affine gates are free. A circuit of ordinary binary gates gives such a computation using at most one multiplication per nonlinear gate. This follows by expressing any binary gate as an affine function of its inputs plus a possible product. The proofs allow arbitrary fanout, cancellation, and cross-block terms in p.

## A divisibility lemma for products of block sets

Let B be a subset of the m-bit cube. Assume |B| is twice an odd number and its GF(2) indicator has algebraic degree at most m−d, for an integer d≥2. Let F on mt bits be zero exactly on the Cartesian product of t copies of B. Suppose ceil(mt/(d−1))≥t+2. Then every normalized Walsh coefficient of F+p, for every p of degree below d, has denominator exactly 2^((m−1)t−1) in lowest terms. In particular K_d(F)≥(m−1)t−1.

For every coordinate monomial M of degree smaller than d, the moment Σ_{x∈B}M(x) is even. Indeed, the product of the indicator and M has degree strictly below m, so its sum over the cube is zero in GF(2). Expand the sign of p over the integers as a product of factors 1−2M over its ANF monomials, absorbing any constant into an overall sign. A term selecting j>0 monomials has coefficient (−2)^j. Its sum on B^t factors into t local moments. At most floor(j(d−1)/d)<j blocks can have local support of size at least d. The other block moments are even. Thus every nonconstant expansion term is divisible by 2^(t+1), including when j>t. The signed sum on B^t is congruent to 2^t modulo 2^(t+1).

On the entire mt-bit cube, a term of j monomials and union support b has valuation j+mt−b. Since b≤min(mt,(d−1)j), this is at least ceil(mt/(d−1)). Twice the sum on B^t minus the sum on the whole cube equals the signed sum of F+p. Its valuation is exactly t+1. Absorbing any linear Walsh character into p gives the asserted common denominator.

An r-multiplication circuit can only produce normalized Walsh coefficients whose denominators divide 2^r. Eliminate gates in reverse topological order in the output character. If a gate appears in a term, replace its sign (−1)^(ab) by [1+(−1)^a+(−1)^b−(−1)^(a+b)]/2. Each resulting exponent is affine in the primary and surviving gate bits. Walsh averaging the final expansion selects signed affine coefficients. This proves the circuit implication directly. The Walsh-granularity lower bound for multiplicative complexity also follows from known parity decision-tree inequalities. [Chistopolskaya and Podolskii](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08668)

## Standard CLIQUE with only distinct matching edges free

Choose a power of two q≥2. In each block join 3q pairs of vertices completely across different pairs, leaving only the 3q within-pair edges variable. The clique number is exactly 3q+|x|. Take t disjoint blocks, fix all interblock edges absent, and use threshold 4q+1. This is a constant-coordinate restriction of the usual CLIQUE predicate on 6qt vertices and binom(6qt,2) adjacency bits. Each of the 3qt free bits occurs once. Its acceptance condition is the OR of t thresholds THR_{q+1,3q}.

The coefficient of a monomial of degree j≥q+1 in one threshold is binom(j−1,q) modulo two, by the partial alternating binomial identity, and all lower coefficients vanish. By binary parity of binomial coefficients, the coefficient is one exactly for q+1≤j≤2q among the relevant j≤3q. Thus the local degree is 2q. The block zero-set size is the sum of binom(3q,i) through i=q. Modulo four, (1+z)^q ≡ (1+z^(q/2))² because the two polynomials before squaring are congruent modulo two. Cubing and summing coefficients up to q gives 1+6+15 ≡ 2 modulo four.

The block lemma with m=3q and d=q gives K_q≥(3q−1)t−1. The degree of the restriction is exactly 2qt: expand one plus the product of the t block complements and use disjoint variables. Thus the degree bound gives only 2qt−1. The stronger bound survives arbitrary low-degree perturbations across the free coordinates and arbitrary coupling of all intermediate circuit gates. Any circuit for the entire CLIQUE predicate restricts to such a circuit, and any perturbation of degree below q in all graph edges remains of degree below q after restriction. Global degree on other graphs can already yield a larger numerical lower bound. Here the improvement concerns the specified restriction and its coupled profile.

## An exact family

Let A(x) denote the conjunction of d bits. On each block of m=2d coordinates compute A(x) XOR A(y), and let F be the OR of the t block results. The block zero set B has |B|=2^(2d)−2(2^d−1) and its indicator 1+A(x)+A(y) has degree d. The lemma gives K_d(F)≥(2d−1)t−1 for all d≥2 and t≥1. The 2t ANDs of arity d, followed by t−1 binary ORs, attain this bound with exactly 2t(d−1)+t−1 binary multiplications. The degree is dt. All cross-block perturbations of smaller than the local degree are covered. For this particular family the congruence also follows from classical Ax–Katz applied to the t block equations of degree d and the additional equation p=1 of degree at most d−1. Its common zero count is divisible by 2^t. [Katz, Theorem 1.0](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/old/ax.pdf)

A small literal CLIQUE representation uses a core of a vertices, for the least a satisfying binom(a,2)≥d. Encode the d x bits on distinct core edges and set its other internal edges to one. Add d independent leaves. Connect leaf i fully to the core except for one incident edge with label ¬y_i. An (a+1)-clique exists exactly when A(x)=1 and A(y)=0. Take the disjoint union with the gadget exchanging x and y, then the disjoint union across blocks. This realizes the exact family using 2t(a+d) vertices.

One can compare to the full degree of a uniform NP-complete language as well. Fix d≥3. At length (2d+1)m split the input into m formula-description bits and m blocks of 2d bits. Accept iff the formula encodes a satisfiable instance or the explicit family just defined is one; reject other lengths and treat malformed formula descriptions as unsatisfiable. The language is in NP and SAT reduces to it by appending zero blocks. At every sufficiently large valid length fix one malformed or unsatisfiable formula to retain F. Hence K_d≥(2d−1)m−1 for the full function at those lengths. Its degree is at most (d+1)m by the OR identity. The lower bound comes from the explicit easy subfamily and is linear in the input length. Establishing superpolynomial complexity for one NP language remains open.

## Audit scope

The granularity of a scalar Boolean function on n bits is at most n, by the normalized Walsh sum itself. This measure alone cannot give a superlinear circuit lower bound. The exact multiplication cost above and the matching-edge CLIQUE restriction are applications of divisibility and circuit granularity rather than new general circuit lower-bound principles.
