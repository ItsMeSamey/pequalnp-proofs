# A canonical maximum-complexity table and its sequential printing cost

Fix the complete two-input Boolean basis, counting gates with unrestricted fan-out. Write N=2^n, M(n) for the largest minimum circuit size of any n-input table, and H_n for the lexicographically first table attaining M(n). The number of ordered s-gate descriptions is at most (n+s+2)[16(n+s+2)^2]^s, giving M(n)≥cN/n for an absolute c>0 and all large n.

**Uniform transfer.** If SAT is in P, one fixed deterministic polynomial-time machine on unary input 1^N prints H_n for every power-of-two N. Given 1^N, a prefix p and s=O(N), whether p has a completion t of complexity at least s is in Σ₂^P. Guess the N-bit table and universally quantify candidate circuits of fewer than s gates. Check disagreement by scanning all N rows in polynomial time. An O(N)-gate multiplexer tree bounds the threshold range. Under SAT in P, the inner coNP test and then the outer existential test are in P. Compute M(n) by binary search and choose each next bit by a prefix query. These N calls have polynomial total time and yield one ordinary E language attaining M(n) at every length. Thus if each E language misses the exact maximum at infinitely many lengths, then SAT is outside P. Even a proof that this particular printer task needs superpolynomial time in N would suffice. The transfer applies to every polynomial exponent.

**Slicing bound.** Consider a fixed deterministic multitape printer with read-only unary input 1^N with endmarkers, or binary n, and append-only output that it cannot read. It emits at most one bit per native transition. Let T be its running time and S≥n+2 bound the bits of its working configuration, including tape contents, work and input head positions, and finite control. For every power of two B≤N and integer D≥1, the printed N-bit table has an unrestricted circuit of size

O((n+S)N/B + D(S+n) + (B+n)T/D).

In the simulation term we may cap the output counter at B, so its binary length is O(n). Represent each work tape's head in one-hot form alongside its cells, increasing S by at most a fixed factor under the standard space convention counting visited cells. A transition selects its read symbols by a linear-size OR, then updates all cells and shifts the one-hot flags with linear size. The known unary input requires only a binary position, endmarker comparisons and increments costing O(n). A binary n input also permits a linear-size lookup. These details justify the stated linear per-step simulation charge.

Split the stream into N/B aligned blocks. A block starts immediately following emission of the previous block's final bit, or at time zero. At most T/D blocks take more than D steps. Compute a high-address equality flag for each block and use those flags to select its hardwired starting configuration and an exceptional-block flag. Simulate D steps from that configuration with a capped output counter, latching the low-addressed bit when emitted. On an exceptional block, instead use its hardwired B-bit table with an O(B)-gate multiplexer on the low bits. Gate each exceptional result by its high-address flag and OR these results. The three terms above pay for configuration selection, local simulation, and exceptional output storage. No uniform construction of the circuit or lookup of unknown input symbols is required.

**Consequence.** If S=n^{O(1)} and the tables require Ω(N/n) gates at every sufficiently large n, then for each fixed δ>0 the printer needs more than N^{2−δ} transitions at every sufficiently large n. For 0<δ<1 choose a polynomial power-of-two B large enough that the first term is o(N/n), and D=ceil(N^{1−δ/2}); the other two terms are o(N/n) at every length with T≤N^{2−δ}. More generally if S=N^{σ+o(1)}, T=N^{τ+o(1)}, and τ+2σ<2 with σ<1, choose B=N^{σ+η+o(1)} and D=N^{1−σ−η+o(1)} for sufficiently small η>0. All terms are o(N/n). This proves a time-space tradeoff on unrestricted circuit complexity, for the specified sequential printer model.

SAT in P supplies an arbitrary polynomial-time printer and polynomial-in-N workspace. Neither quantity meets the slicing threshold in general. The remaining question is a superpolynomial unrestricted printing lower bound. Related work on constructing nearly maximum-complexity truth tables via explicit-construction reductions appears in [Korten, The Hardest Explicit Construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00875).
