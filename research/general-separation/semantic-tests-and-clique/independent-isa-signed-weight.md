# An absolute-coefficient lower bound for a fully interleaved signed SUM

Use the explicit [independent-address ISA product](independent-isa-positive.md). Each bank has m=2^t payload bits, P=2^floor(log₂(m/t)) pointer words and an independent primary index. There are r=t banks for the one uniform P language. F is their AND. The following estimates use independent uniform input bits and permit arbitrary binary gates in formula trees or arbitrary acyclic existential query programs with unrestricted successor sets. They concern an atom A of per-atom budget W occurrences and do not assume that A is one-sided.

Write p_a for the selected payload address, ε_(a,j)=2y_(a,j)−1, and Z=product_a ε_(a,p_a). Then E[FZ]=2^(−r), since F vanishes unless all selected signs equal +1. Put D=1+W/(rP). For every threshold L≥1 and some absolute C the individual correlation satisfies

* |E[AZ]| ≤ [D/L+C sqrt(rL/m)]^r for formula leaves;
* |E[AZ]| ≤ [D/L+CrL/sqrt(m)]^r for query nodes.

This is useful where the bracket is less than one. To prove it fix primary indices and call a bank heavy when its selected pointer word occurs more than L times in A. Pin all unselected pointer words, and pin the selected words and payloads of the heavy banks. The heavy sign factors are now constants of absolute value one. Suppose s≥1 light banks remain. For fixed light payloads, the residual accepts some mask U in [m]^s on the light selected addresses. Across all choices of payload it lies in a single dictionary of at most 2^{O(sL)} formula masks or 2^{O(s²L²)} program masks. These bounds follow from the fixed formula skeleton or fixed program-node contraction, with at most sL named occurrences left free. The actual mask can depend on every light payload bit.

For each fixed candidate mask U, the conditional average over light addresses of its signed score is the normalized sum over u in U of product_i ε_(i,u_i), with normalizer m^s. Before the signs are sampled, its coefficient array has Euclidean norm at most m^(−s/2). For moment order p≥2 the L^p norm of a multilinear sum of this type is at most (C₁ sqrt(p))^s times its coefficient-array Euclidean norm. Indeed a scalar sum of independent random signs has L^p norm at most C₁ sqrt(p) times the Euclidean coefficient norm by its subgaussian tail. Condition on the other sign banks, apply this bound, then use the triangle inequality in L^(p/2) to bound the L^p norm of the conditional Euclidean norm by the Euclidean sum of the L^p norms. Induction on s proves the estimate.

For a dictionary of size H choose p=max(2,ceil(log H)), using natural logs. The expected maximum absolute score over all masks is ≤H^(1/p) times the largest individual L^p norm. It bounds the absolute expected score of the payload-dependent mask. Thus conditional correlations are bounded by [C sqrt(rL/m)]^s or [CrL/sqrt(m)]^s, respectively, uniformly in the heavy assignments and pins. For s=0 use the trivial bound one. Bank a's heavy probability is at most W_a/(PL) with W_a its number of pointer occurrences. The heavy flags are independent because the primary indices are independent. Averaging each factor as heavy or light gives a product at most product_a[W_a/(PL)+light factor]. The arithmetic–geometric mean and sum_a W_a≤W give the claims.

## Consequences and the normalization gap

At r=t and W≤rm(log m)^b for any fixed b, D is polylogarithmic. Set L=ceil(m^(1/3)) for formulas and ceil(m^(1/4)) for programs. An atom's correlation is at most m^{−(1/3−o(1))r} or m^{−(1/4−o(1))r}. For an exact real identity F=sum_h c_h A_h, the triangle inequality and E[FZ]=2^(−r) therefore give total absolute coefficient weight at least m^{(1/3−o(1))r} for formulas and m^{(1/4−o(1))r} for programs. This is exp(Ω((log n_t)^2)) in either model. Consequently weights bounded individually by n_t^B for any fixed B require exp(Ω((log n_t)^2)) summands. Signed cancellation and full interleaving are allowed. The result also applies to a real-valued approximation G with |E[(G−F)Z]|≤2^(−r−1), by replacing the target correlation by at least 2^(−r−1). No comparable statement is made here for constant-error approximation at large r.

Arbitrary real coefficients do not obey the assumed polynomial magnitude bound. Even a fixed dictionary of simple Boolean atoms can force exponentially large coefficients for an exact Boolean output. Give each row and each atom a label (j,b) with 0≤j≤k, b in {1,2}. On a row, turn on its own atom and, for j>0, both atoms of layer j−1. The target equals one at layer zero and zero elsewhere. Encoding row labels in binary makes every atom a disjunction of at most three singleton tests; reject unused labels. Any exact representation on this dictionary must have c_(0,b)=1 and c_(j,b)=−c_(j−1,1)−c_(j−1,2)=(−2)^j. An alternative dictionary can compute this particular target easily. Thus the example blocks a coefficient cap for a given representation, not a potential change-of-dictionary theorem.

The earlier [dense-moment seed surrogate](dense-isa-moments.md) has weight m^r for a single term when d is fixed. It reproduces the indicated low-degree moments while violating Boolean range, consistently with this coefficient lower bound. Cramer's general exp(O(S log S)) coefficient allowance for S summands does not convert the present bound to superpolynomial sparsity. The target has a linear shared circuit, and no P versus NP separation follows.

The fixed-skeleton counting ingredient builds on the [Nečiporuk subfunction method](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.01932). [Klauck](https://doi.org/10.1137/S009753970140004X) develops one-way communication versions for randomized and related formula models. The signed test here uses separate independent payload banks and a sum of products of their signs.
