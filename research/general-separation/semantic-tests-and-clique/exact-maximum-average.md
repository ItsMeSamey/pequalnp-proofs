# Uniform-distribution hardness of every exact circuit maximizer

Use the complete binary Boolean basis, n variables, N=2^n, binary entropy h, and minimum gate count CC. There are absolute constants p>0 and a>0 such that for every sufficiently large n, every table f of maximum circuit complexity, and every circuit of size at most aN/n, the agreement with f on a uniform input is less than 1−p. This includes the specific lexicographically first maximizer H_n. The finite argument also applies to any table known to have CC(f)≥cN/n for a fixed c>0, with parameters depending on c.

**Sparse correction.** Fix 0<p≤q<1/2. A table e with at most pN ones has a circuit of size at most

K(1+p/q)h(q) N/n + o(N/n)

for an absolute K and sufficiently large n depending on q. Choose B the largest power of two at most n/(2h(q)), so n/(4h(q))<B≤n/(2h(q)). Treat the suffix of log₂ B input bits as addressing one of B entries of a block. Hardwire one suffix circuit for each subset of at most floor(qB) entries. The standard binomial volume bound gives at most 2^{B h(q)}≤sqrt(N) circuits. Each costs O(B) gates with a multiplexer tree, hence altogether o(N/n) for fixed q. Decode all N/B possible high prefixes by a shared prefix tree with O(N/B+n) gates. In a block with u positive entries partition its positive positions into groups of size at most floor(qB). Each group needs one AND of its precomputed suffix function with the prefix flag and one gate in the final OR. There are at most N/B + 2pN/(qB) groups in total for qB≥2. This proves the estimate with absolute constants. Empty masks and empty blocks use constant zero.

The elementary circuit-description count gives an absolute c>0 for which M(n)≥cN/n eventually. Choose q=p small enough that 2Kh(p)<c/4. For all sufficiently large n the correction then costs at most cN/(2n). If g agrees with a maximizer f except on at most pN rows, XOR the circuit for g with the correction e=f XOR g to recover f. This forces CC(g)≥cN/(2n)−1. Taking a=c/4 proves the assertion.

**Uniform transfer.** If SAT belongs to P the [prefix construction](exact-maximum-printer.md) puts the bit language of H in E. Thus the existence, for every E language at infinitely many lengths, of circuits of size o(N/n) and uniform error at most this p would put SAT outside P. The required approximations for arbitrary E languages remain unproved. No bound on the time needed to find those approximating circuits is assumed.

**Separate existence argument.** For each fixed 0<ε<1/2, a Hamming ball of radius floor(εN) has size at most 2^{h(ε)N}. Circuits of size a_ε N/n for small enough a_ε have fewer than 2^{(1−h(ε))N} descriptions, hence some truth table is outside every such ball. For fixed rational ε and suitably small fixed rational a_ε, requiring such a table extending a given prefix is in Σ₂^P by guessing the table, universally quantifying the small circuit, and explicitly counting mismatches. Under SAT in P this gives an E language hard to that error. This counting argument alone does not identify H or show that every exact maximizer has the same hardness. The sparse correction above establishes the latter for the small fixed p it supplies.

The counting and local-coding methods have classical antecedents. Shannon studied counting and synthesis for switching circuits. Lupanov developed local coding for networks of functional elements. The block estimate here is proved directly, without identifying it with an exact historical optimum.
