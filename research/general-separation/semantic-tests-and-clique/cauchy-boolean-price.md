# An exact Boolean gate bound from factorization norm, attained on every half cut

Use binary AND and XOR, with constants and unrestricted fan-out. Count only AND gates. Every binary Boolean gate is an affine function of its operands and their product, so the bound also lower-bounds the number of nonlinear binary gates in an arbitrary binary-basis circuit. For a Boolean function f use the real signed table M=(-1)^f. Across a product partition its factorization norm γ₂(M) is the infimum of max_i ‖u_i‖₂ · max_j ‖v_j‖₂ over factorizations M_ij=⟨u_i,v_j⟩. The factorization-norm approach to Boolean communication matrices is due in particular to Linial and Shraibman. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.20232)

## A gate-correct inequality despite overlap

If the circuit has g AND gates, then γ₂(M)≤2^g on every cut, including after restriction to any product of row and column domains. Order the g AND outputs topologically. After eliminating intervening XOR gates, every operand of an AND is the sum of an affine form in the original inputs and earlier AND outputs. The final answer is an affine form plus a sum of AND outputs. All nonlinear dependence on the raw inputs therefore factors through the 2g raw affine operand forms. This uses one pair per shared gate.

Strip the final affine phase by multiplying rows and columns by signs, which preserves γ₂. Row assignments with the same contribution to the 2g forms have identical rows. There are at most 2^{2g} such rows. Give each distinct row a unit basis vector and represent a column by its signs at those rows. This proves the inequality. The same list of forms works for all cuts. This is an unrestricted circuit argument with no assumption that gate operands use disjoint inputs. A gate-by-gate factorization additionally gives γ₂(A∘B)≤γ₂(A)γ₂(B), by tensoring the row and column vectors. Applying such a product recurrence naively to a DAG may count shared gates many times.

## A family saturating the bound

Let q=2^k≥4. Index q groups by t∈F_q. Each group chooses x_t∈F_q through q one-hot Boolean inputs. Fix any order of labels. Define the output bit

    f(x) = Tr_{F_q/F₂}( Σ_{s<t} K_st x_s x_t ),
    K_st = (s+t)^(q−2).

This gives reciprocal off-diagonal entries and zero diagonal. Fix arbitrary values of any groups. Split the remaining groups into disjoint A and B with a=|A|≤|B|=b. Strip row and column phases. The resulting sign matrix has entries given by the additive trace character of x_Aᵀ K_AB x_B. Every square minor of a nonempty rectangular cross block is nonsingular: for a fixed square subblock of order r, a linear dependence between its rows would give a rational function Σ_i c_i/(s_i+z) with r distinct zeros outside all its poles. Its numerator has degree <r, forcing it to vanish identically, and each residue c_i is then zero. Hence K_AB has full row rank. The difference between two distinct rows specifies a nontrivial additive character on x_B; the trace pairing is nondegenerate. Character orthogonality gives M Mᵀ=q^b I after the phase adjustments, hence also before them.

Writing R=q^a and C=q^b, all R singular values equal √C. The stable rank, ordinary rank and nuclear norm therefore attain their simultaneous maximum for R×C sign matrices. In particular γ₂=√R. For the lower bound any factorization M=UVᵀ satisfies ‖M‖_*≤‖U‖_F‖V‖_F≤√(RC) max_i‖u_i‖₂ max_j‖v_j‖₂. The matching upper bound uses the row basis. Every entrywise ε-approximation with ε<1 has rank at least (1−ε²)R, since a rank-d approximation has squared Frobenius error at least (R−d)C. These are exact signed evaluation matrices. The 0/1 encoding, sign-rank and other approximate norms are separate notions. The cross-matrix test is closely related to the finite-field graph-state characterization of absolutely maximal entanglement. [Helwig](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.2879)

On every equal partition of all groups this gives γ₂=2^{kq/4}, so the general gate bound forces at least kq/4 ANDs even for a circuit required to be correct only on the one-hot promise. This lower bound is attained by one layer of kq/4 ANDs. In characteristic two, the polynomial (s+t)^{q−2} contains precisely the q/2 even exponents in s from 0 through q−2: expand q−2 as the sum of powers of two from 2 through q/2 and use Frobenius. Evaluating these monomials on all q field elements yields a q-by-(q/2) full-column-rank matrix V by polynomial interpolation. The identity K=VJVᵀ with the invertible reversal matrix J gives rank(K)=q/2. For the Boolean quadratic form in kq decoded binary coordinates, the polar form is Tr(xᵀ K y). Its radical is exactly ker K because the trace pairing is nondegenerate, so its binary rank is kq/2. A symplectic basis decomposes the quadratic into kq/4 products of pairs of linear forms plus an affine correction. The classical quadratic multiplicative-complexity theorem gives this reduction on decoded binary inputs. Here the one-hot decoding of each bit is itself an XOR; the gate bound above proves optimality even for the restricted unary representation. [Mirwald and Schnorr](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/boolean-function-complexity/multiplicative-complexity-of-boolean-quadratic-forms-a-survey/356E403E109908619387A56F4003A651)

A polynomial-time constructor can find an irreducible degree-k binary polynomial by exhaustive search in time polynomial in q, construct the binary quadratic form, and compute its symplectic basis by elimination. Decoding and forming the linear maps use O(q²k²) binary XOR gates in total. The input length is q². Thus the largest possible factorization norm and the strongest possible flat singular spectra on all cuts and all whole-group fixings can occur simultaneously in a near-linear-size Boolean circuit. This precludes a superpolynomial lower bound from any entrywise nondecreasing aggregate of just these extremal spectral statistics calibrated against all such circuits. It leaves open gate-dependent invariants using additional information and does not settle P versus NP.

[Finite checker](./cauchy_price_check.py) verified the full reciprocal ranks at q=4 and 8; all 18 and 1,106 nonempty disjoint square minors respectively; and exact orthogonality of all 250 nonempty short-side fixed-group sign tables at q=4.
