# An odd cycle away from the pins obstructs both erased-code conversions

Let P be the pins, F their complement, and H=A[F,F]. Erasure gives (x,Hx) with A[P,F]x=0. Suppose F contains an odd cycle whose vertices have no pin neighbors and whose cycle edges have no common free neighbors at their endpoints. Neither the locally CSS nor the locally GF(4)-linear conversion of the [erased-code algorithm](erased-three-pin-stabilizer.md) can then succeed.

Write local block entries of a preserving map as a_i,b_i,c_i,d_i in row-major order. For every cycle vertex j the word with x=e_j belongs to the erased code. Applying the map and checking its graph equation gives (diag(c)+diag(d)H)e_j=(H diag(a)+H diag(b)H)e_j. At an adjacent cycle vertex i the row gives d_i=a_j since the endpoints have no common free neighbors. Trace one everywhere would force a_i+a_j=1 along the odd cycle. But both a CSS projector and an irreducible field multiplication require local trace one.

For an explicit family take pins p,q,r with only the edge pq among them. For each of t≥2 disjoint pentagons, choose consecutive a_i,b_i and a third distinct c_i. Join p to a_i, q to b_i and r to c_i by length-two paths with private middle vertices. This graph is connected with minimum degree two, is nonbipartite, and has girth at least five (a cycle exiting a pentagon uses at least two distinct length-two attachments and at least one more edge). Thus its full site-endomorphism algebra is scalar by the [girth argument](erased-three-pin-stabilizer.md). Its n=8t+3 vertices include t disjoint pentagons satisfying the obstruction.

Consider a tree branching at a free vertex u by deleting u, or locally complementing at u then deleting it. Suppose leaves may be certified by linear pin inconsistency or by either erased-code conversion, and components without pins may be discarded. On a path always taking direct deletion, fewer than t free deletions leave an entire pentagon module and all its attachments. That module lies in the component of all pins. Its pentagon remains triangle-free and outside the pin neighborhoods. Its three private neighbors at the pins give the cut full row rank three, so the linear pin checks remain consistent (set x_P=1 and solve A[P,F]x_F=A[P,P]1). The direct-deletion path therefore has depth at least t. This applies to complete trees with the specified closure rules.

The original graph has an easy witness: p,q, the two private middle vertices on their paths in one module, and its adjacent a_i,b_i induce a cycle. Pin r may be added as an isolated vertex. Thus the tree bound does not imply hardness for algorithms that construct a witness another way.
