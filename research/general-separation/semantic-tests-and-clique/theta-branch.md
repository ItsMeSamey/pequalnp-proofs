# What correctness on near-perfect graphs can force

Write θ̄(G)=θ(complement of G), with θ̄(empty)=0. It lies between clique number ω and chromatic number χ. Fix a deterministically computable upper estimate θ̄≤g≤θ̄+1/64 in polynomial bit-time. The polynomial-time theta approach to separating clique from colorability and its implications for monotone complexity are due to Grötschel–Lovász–Schrijver and Tardos. The calculations below examine explicit vertex conditioning atop this separator. [Tardos](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~eva/Gap.Between.Monotone.NonMonotone.Circuit.Complexity.is.Exponential.pdf) · [Lovász](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1979.1055985)

## A larger correctness domain for small ordinary circuits

For every set S of at most h vertices, enumerate all cliques T⊆S, including the empty clique. Let R be the graph induced by vertices outside S adjacent to every vertex of T. Accept precisely if for every S some T satisfies |T|+g(R)≥k. This costs at most poly(n) times Σ_{i≤h} binom(n,i)2^i on n vertices.

Every k-clique passes by choosing its intersection with S. If χ(G)≤k−1, then R can use only colors absent from T in a (k−1)-coloring, so every branch has |T|+g(R)<k. If deleting some ≤h vertices leaves a perfect graph, take such a set S. Every induced R in that branch is perfect and θ̄(R)=ω(R). Since ω is integral, this S rejects every k-clique-free graph in that class. This construction needs no supplied deletion set. It gives polynomial ordinary circuits for fixed h and quasipolynomial ones for polylogarithmic h. Therefore a purported quasipolynomial repair of all small ordinary circuits derived solely from these promised correct values would contradict the known monotone clique/coloring lower bound. The separator uses no assumptions about the remaining graphs.

For use below, θ̄ is additive under complete joins and takes the maximum under disjoint unions. In the usual positive-semidefinite trace-one formulation of θ, the cross block vanishes for a graph join, giving the maximum by blockwise allocation of trace. For a disjoint union, align each component's summed Gram vector in the same direction and assign trace proportional to its optimum. This attains the sum; Cauchy–Schwarz gives the matching upper bound. θ̄(C₅)=√5 since C₅ is self-complementary and vertex-transitive. Every proper induced subgraph of C₅ is perfect.

## Exact size of vertex branching on a join of pentagons

Let G be a complete join of m pentagons. It has 5m vertices, clique number 2m and θ̄(G)=m√5. Set k=2m+1 and δ=√5−2. In a vertex branch-and-bound proof, the state consists of the included clique T and the vertices explicitly queried so far. Remove excluded vertices, T itself, and nonneighbors of T to obtain residual R. A feasible state may be closed when |T|+g(R)<k. An internal node chooses an unassigned residual vertex and has one include child and one exclude child. All branch choices may depend on the state. This model uses vertex decisions and the specified upper bound; cached subproofs, integral bounds obtained by splitting independent factors, and additional cuts are different rules.

**Theorem.** The minimum leaf count is exactly 2^max(0,m−4). The least nonadaptive conditioning-set size that suffices for rejection is max(0,m−4). For the more conservative cutoff |T|+g(R)<k−1/2 the two answers instead use max(0,m−2).

Choose a maximum clique by taking one of the five edges of each C₅. Consider any partial transcript consistent with one such choice. In each factor touched by a query, the included vertices and the remaining chosen endpoints contribute at least two to |T|+θ̄(R). Every untouched factor contributes √5, since all cross-factor edges are present. With u untouched factors, the bound is at least 2m+uδ. Since 5δ>1, a terminal transcript must touch at least m−4 factors. Retain only paths compatible with some maximum clique. At the first query in an untouched factor both children retain such a completion: choose an edge incident to the queried vertex for one child and a nonincident edge for the other. These choices are independent of previous factors. After contracting nodes with only one retained child, the binary tree has minimum root-to-leaf depth at least m−4, and hence at least 2^(m−4) leaves when m≥4.

For the reverse bound preselect one vertex in each of m−4 factors (or none for m≤4). Exclusion leaves a path on four vertices with θ̄=2. Inclusion leaves two nonadjacent neighbors with θ̄=1, plus the included vertex, again contributing two. Thus all branches have |T|+g(R)≤2m+4δ+1/64<2m+1. The full binary tree achieves the bound. The same proof yields the minimum nonadaptive conditioning size. For the alternative cutoff use 2δ+1/64<1/2<3δ. These are models of a precise pruning rule even when g is exact. Detecting the join factors computes the actual clique number immediately.

## A hard slice entirely accepted by the relaxation

For k≥11 and b≥1 let J be the complete join of five pentagons. Join a shared clique K of order k−11 to the disjoint union of b copies of J, obtaining H. Its clique number is k−1 and θ̄(K joined to J)=k−11+5√5>k. Every conditioning set S of fewer than b vertices leaves one entire J untouched. Choose T=S∩K. The residual contains the other core vertices joined to the untouched J, so |T|+g(R)≥k. This works for every S and uses only k−11+25b vertices. Conversely, choosing one vertex from a pentagon in each J suffices to reject. For T empty the residual maximum across the b copies is bounded by 2+4√5. A nonempty T selects one vertex from one copy and restricts the residual there, giving the same per-copy upper bound including T. After adding K and 1/64 this is below k. Thus the exact conditioning threshold is b.

On the NP-complete input domain 1≤k≤|V(G)|, first increase k to at least 11 if needed by adjoining a universal clique and increasing k by its order. Take the disjoint union of G and H. Exact k-clique existence agrees with G. The displayed choice of T ensures every such union is accepted by the relaxation at depth h<b. For any sublinear prescribed budget h(n), choose b=|V(G)|+k sufficiently large. The new order is O(b) and eventually h(n)<b; for a fixed budget function, finitely many exceptional small inputs and trivially negative inputs can be decided directly and mapped to fixed sufficiently large accepted YES or accepted NO examples. This gives NP-hardness on an all-accepted slice. If connectivity is desired, join an extra universal vertex and raise the target by one, including that vertex in T exactly when it was queried. Disjoint-copy modules are visible in this reduction. Their purpose is to witness failure of the relaxation, not to hide a hard representation.
