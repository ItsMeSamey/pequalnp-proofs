# Asymptotic minimum algebraic degree of a partial cubing selector

Let m≥3 be odd, M=2^m−1, and work in K=GF(2^m). Require a function D:K→K to be nonzero somewhere on K* and to obey D(s)=sD(s)^4 on K*. Thus at each s it may be zero or the unique nonzero permissible value (cubing is bijective). Degree means maximum coordinate degree over GF(2). Let d_m be the minimum degree. Then

    ceil((m−1)/4) ≤ d_m ≤ m/4+O(√m).

In particular d_m/m tends to 1/4. Neither a density promise on the selected sites nor nonvanishing everywhere is imposed. With nonvanishing everywhere on K* there is only the monomial s^{(M−1)/3} on K*, of degree (m−1)/2 in its best extension.

## Exponent orbits

Restrict a univariate representation to K* and use exponent representatives 0,…,M−1. They are a basis of the functions on K* by polynomial interpolation. The defining identity carries exponent a to T(a)=4a+1 mod M and raises its coefficient to the fourth power. Thus support is a union of T-orbits. Conversely assigning coefficient 1 throughout a single orbit gives a nonzero solution. Its degree on the whole field, with the natural polynomial extension, is the largest binary weight of an exponent in the orbit. Indeed the full-field basis with exponents 0,…,M has each power of weight j of degree at most j by factorization into Frobenius powers. At each degree cutoff the number of these exponents equals the dimension over K of the space spanned by the squarefree coordinate monomials up to that degree. Hence the weight cutoff is also necessary. Restriction to K* only merges exponents 0 and M and cannot improve an extension below the maximum weight in the restricted support. This proves the minimum-orbit characterization. Since 3 is invertible modulo M, T is conjugate to multiplication by 4 via a↦3a+1, and T^m is the identity.

## Lower bound by cyclic runs

Let j=(m−1)/2. The residue c_j=(4^j−1)/3 has j isolated one-runs in its cyclic m-bit expansion. Count runs by half the number of cyclic bit transitions; both constant words have count zero, making the two representatives of zero equivalent. Adding a single power of two modulo M changes the count by at most one. To see this, rotate the added bit to the least significant position. The increment changes an initial string of ones to zeros and the next zero to one. Within that affected interval the count of transitions is unchanged when the string is nonempty; at most two boundary transitions change. A single flipped bit or a wrapped constant word also obeys the bound. Subtraction is the reverse operation. Since c_j=T^j(a)−4^ja is a sum of at most wt(T^j(a))+wt(a) signed powers of two, an orbit of peak weight d must satisfy 2d≥j.

## Upper bound by alternating blocks

Choose an even b=Θ(√m), at least four for large m. Partition the binary expansion of a, starting at the least significant end, into complete pairs of adjacent b-bit chunks and one final remainder of length <2b. Use ordinary integer chunk values 0, E+1 in each pair, with E=(2^b−1)/3; set the remainder to zero. The bits of E are one precisely at its even positions. The unmodified pair has weight b/2 since E+1 replaces its lowest one by a one at position 1.

For 0≤j≤(m−1)/2 put c_j=(4^j−1)/3. The weight of T^{−j}(a) equals that of a−c_j modulo M because multiplication by 4 rotates the m-bit word. This subtracts an even-position alternating mask over the low 2j bits. The weight of T^j(a) similarly equals that of a+4^{−j}c_j modulo M. The added mask covers the high 2j bits with ones at odd global positions, since m−2j is odd. These forward and backward iterates cover every T-orbit position. A fully covered chunk consequently subtracts E or adds O=2E.

Consider any complete modified pair and allow an arbitrary incoming carry or borrow c∈{0,1} at either chunk. With addition the zero chunk yields O+c, of weight at most b/2+1. The E+1 chunk yields (E+1+O+c) mod 2^b=c, of weight at most one. With subtraction the zero chunk yields O+1−c, again of weight at most b/2+1, and E+1 yields 1−c. Thus every fully modified pair has weight at most b/2+2 irrespective of neighboring chunks.

These estimates also apply with cyclic carry or borrow: prescribe an initial bit at the low end equal to the carry or borrow out of the high end. The final bit as a function of the initial one is monotone on {0,1}, so a fixed point exists. The resulting m output bits represent the desired residue modulo M since 2^m≡1. Replacing the all-ones representation by zero can only lower its weight. Unmodified full pairs consecutive in bit order have no ongoing carry or borrow after their first pair: either operation on the pair with incoming bit zero leaves it unchanged, and an incoming one stops by the E+1 chunk, whose value is strictly between 0 and 2^b−1. The mask is a single prefix or suffix. Hence all except O(1) unmodified pairs have weight exactly b/2. The remainder, the pair straddling the mask boundary, and a constant number of carry-affected pairs together cost O(b). Summing over at most m/(2b) complete pairs gives peak weight at most m/4+O(m/b+b). Taking b of order √m proves the bound. For large m this orbit avoids exponent zero, whose own orbit has peak at least (m−1)/2 via c_j. Thus the same asymptotic upper applies if D(0)=0 is additionally required.
