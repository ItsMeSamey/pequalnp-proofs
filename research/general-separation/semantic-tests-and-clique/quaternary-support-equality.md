# Equality of quaternary symmetric principal supports and the obstruction to gain testing

For any symmetric GF(4) matrix J on n labeled indices, let D(J) be the family of S with nonzero principal determinant and let Z_J(X)=Σ_{S in D(J)} Π_{i in S}X_i, over characteristic two. This polynomial admits polynomial-time evaluation over any explicitly constructed finite extension of GF(4). Consequently equality of two such labeled support families has a randomized polynomial-time test with one-sided error; a differing set can be checked exactly. The proof combines Frobenius norm with a standard Pfaffian minor summation. [Ishikawa and Wakayama](https://doi.org/10.1080/03081089508818403)

Form an alternating matrix B with B_ij=J_ij²+J_ii J_jj off diagonal. For even S, Pf(B[S])=det J[S]. Long cycles cancel in the determinant: reverse the long cycle containing the least index on any such cycle. Surviving transpositions contribute J_ij² and fixed points contribute J_ii. Expanding each Pfaffian edge choice pairs the selected diagonal vertices in an odd number of ways. For odd S append a dummy with edge B_i*=J_ii and take the Pfaffian on S plus that dummy. This pairs all selected diagonals together with the dummy and yields the same determinant.

If h∈GF(4), h times h² is its nonzero indicator. Hence each coefficient of Z_J is a product of the corresponding Pfaffian of B and that of its entrywise conjugate Bbar. To obtain all even-index terms simultaneously evaluate the block Pfaffian with diagonal blocks B and diag(X) Bbar diag(X) and off-diagonal identity blocks. In its matching expansion a cross edge stands for excluding the matching pair of copies and the remaining two internal index sets coincide. All signs disappear in characteristic two. To obtain the odd terms use the extended B on n+1 indices, disallow the dummy's cross edge, and set its variable to one. The dummy is then matched internally in each copy. Add the two results. Only matrix coefficients are conjugated, never the variables. A Pfaffian can be evaluated directly in polynomial time, or by taking the unique square root of the determinant in the finite extension.

Test equality by evaluating Z_J+Z_L at independent uniform elements of an extension of size at least 4n (handling n=0 directly). Its degree is at most n, so a genuine difference is seen with probability at least 3/4. Repetition and zero-restriction give a differing S: successively discard any index whose removal preserves a nonzero restricted polynomial. Use fresh tests amplified for the at most n adaptive steps, then verify both determinants. A nonzero minor family has a squarefree monomial on the final inclusion-minimal remaining set when all tests succeeded. Uniform extension construction can enumerate monic polynomials over GF(4) of degree ceil(log₄(4n)) and test irreducibility; this costs polynomial time. This is an equality test, not a polynomial algorithm for one-sided containment.

## Every nonzero base-field scalar can lose a set on the same NO instance

In the mixed two-readout problem write K=H+ωbbᵀ as in [the principal sieve](two-readout-principal-sieve.md). A genuine gain satisfies det K[S]=0 and det(K[S]+t aaᵀ)≠0 for every nonzero scalar t. A loss has the opposite direction. If independently certified that a chosen t admits no losses, the equality test for that t detects a gain and the sieve recovers a binary witness. Without this promise it may produce only losses.

Let H=diag(1,0), a=(1,1), b=(0,1), ω²=ω+1. The raw constraints force x₁=0 and the required b·x=0 then forces x₂=0, contradicting a·x=1. K=diag(1,ω) has every principal set nonsingular. For t=1 the updated matrix loses {1}. For t=ω it loses {2}. For t=1+ω it loses {1,2}: the updated full determinant is ω+t(1+ω), zero for this choice. Thus all three labeled equality tests differ on this single NO instance. Checking any or even all such differences for mere nonemptiness gives a false positive.

A scalar t outside GF(4), for example in GF(16), avoids all losses because any nonzero determinant ratio causing a loss lies in GF(4). This makes support equality equivalent to gain existence, but the support indicator of a general GF(16) coefficient is its fifteenth power. Its Frobenius factors involve four rather than two copies, and the above block-Pfaffian evaluator does not compute that product. The rank-one structure may allow another method. Neither symmetric exchange nor the equality test just proved supplies the missing one-sided test.
