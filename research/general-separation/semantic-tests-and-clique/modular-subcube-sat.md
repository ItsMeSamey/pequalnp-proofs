# Uniform SAT search for a fixed prime-power score modulus

Given m explicitly listed literal-subcube indicators C_j(x), integer weights w_j, fixed prime power M=p^a and a subset of residues A, decide if Σ_j w_j C_j(x) modulo M belongs to A for any Boolean x, and return such an x. Literal widths are unrestricted. Deterministic time is poly(n,input)·(m+1)^{O(M)}, polynomial for every fixed M. This is a direct application of prime-power digit polynomials and set-multilinear identity testing; it does not give polynomial dependence on growing M. [Raz and Shpilka](https://www.cs.tau.ac.il/~shpilka/publications/RazShpilka_PIT.pdf) · [Tzeng's span algorithm](https://doi.org/10.1137/0221017)

Reduce weights to residues 0,…,M−1, producing integer S≥0. In F_p, digit r<a of S in base p is binom(S,p^r) mod p. This follows by expanding (1+u)^S=∏_r (1+u^{p^r})^{digit_r} in F_p[u]. On Boolean cube features the same digit is the coefficient of u^{p^r} in

    ∏_j (1 + ((1+u)^{w_j}−1) C_j).

The nonconstant factor has zero constant u-coefficient. That digit is consequently a polynomial of degree ≤p^r in the C_j. The residue test for one target digit vector is the product over r of 1−(digit_r−target_r)^{p−1}. Its degree is ≤(p−1)Σ_{r<a}p^r=M−1. Sum the indicators for the disjoint acceptable residues. Compute this polynomial by truncated products and Boolean idempotence. It expands into ≤Σ_{h≤M−1}binom(m,h) subset products (up to construction factors absorbed by (m+1)^{O(M)} for fixed M). A literal-subcube product is a single subcube or zero. Scalar coefficients are in F_p. Testing for a nonzero value of the resulting polynomial P on Boolean assignments gives exactly the requested search.

For T resulting subcubes, each prefix assignment has a vector in F_p^T marking compatibility with the subcubes. Appending a bit multiplies this vector by one of two fixed diagonal zero-one matrices. Initially keep the all-ones vector. At each bit extend the current basis by both choices and retain a maximally independent subset of the resulting vectors with an actual prefix for each. By linearity every attainable prefix vector lies in the span, and the retained representatives are attainable. At full length a nonzero scalar product with the coefficient vector exists iff some retained actual assignment has a nonzero scalar product. This is a deterministic Boolean-domain specialization of standard white-box PIT, including small fields. [Finite witness checks](modular_score_witness_check.py)

For ordinary unsigned clause violations S, zero residue accepts every satisfying assignment. To certify the reverse implication, test separately for each original clause whether its bad-subcube indicator times the zero-residue polynomial is nonzero somewhere. The resulting explicit witness is a false modular positive; in the absence of any such witness, modular zero and actual satisfiability agree pointwise. The promise can therefore be checked. For M=2 one can also search for weights instead of supplying them: impose w·v(x)=1 for nonzero violated-clause incidence vectors v(x). Solve accumulated rows over F₂ and test the candidate by the per-clause test. A failed check supplies an explicit new equation excluding the current solution; it either increases coefficient rank or makes the system inconsistent. At most m+1 failed iterations occur. This finds a safe weighting if one exists.

Exact counting generally costs more than witness search. Even counting zeros of degree-three polynomials over F₂ is #P-hard. For a Boolean circuit, use quadratic constraints q_i on original and gate variables to impose gate computations and accepting output, with unique gate extensions. On adding one fresh t_i per equation, P=Σ_i t_i q_i has degree ≤3. Summing (−1)^P over all t gives 2^r on a solution and zero otherwise for r constraints. Thus the exact zero count determines #SAT by one arithmetic postprocessing step, not a parsimonious reduction. Degree-three monomials are width-three positive subcubes. [Montanaro](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.08473)

For a growing prime the unrestricted uniform decision problem already includes ordinary width-three SAT: with m≥2 original clauses choose a prime m<p≤2m and use their unsigned violation count. Its zero residue then coincides with zero violations. A prime can be located in polynomial time by elementary trial division in this range. Thus a polynomial-time solver uniform even for p≤2m would resolve P versus NP. The fixed-M algorithm above has an exponent depending on M. For a modulus with distinct prime factors, satisfying each primary-factor condition on a different assignment does not give a common witness: S(x)=3x+4(1−x) has individual zero residues modulo 2 and 3 but never modulo 6.

Finally the [disjointness rank argument](disjoint-score-tradeoff.md) also applies over finite fields and when there are merely ≤d rejecting score values on each fixed witness slice. Use the degree-d product of (S−r) over rejecting levels instead of a Boolean table. The scalar vanishes on rejecting inputs and is nonzero on accepting inputs. For each yes pair choose some accepting witness and take the finite resulting witness list. Sum these polynomials weighted by linearly independent formal indeterminates for distinct witnesses over an extension field. Every yes entry stays nonzero and every no entry zero, while the rectangle count is unchanged because the witness multipliers and slice coefficients are on the right side. This is a witness-preserving fixed-feature statement; with only decision-preserving reductions a preselected successful input could be encoded as a single cube.
