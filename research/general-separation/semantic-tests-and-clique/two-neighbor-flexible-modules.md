# Replacing entire private components by parity edges

Fix a set B of original vertices containing all mandatory vertices. Suppose each connected component D of G minus B has at most two distinct neighbors in B. Its internal order, degrees and cycles and the number of edges from D to either neighbor are unrestricted. In the quotient keep every original direct edge on B. Add one optional parity edge between the two neighbors of each component with two distinct neighbors. Ignore components with fewer. For a fixed selected U inside B, feasibility is precisely even direct cut parity in each connected component of the optional edges on U. Consequently the certified saturation and the quadratic solver on flexible components both apply, with their respective rank and running time bounds.

For any original solution sum the degree equations on the selected vertices internal to D. Only selected endpoints in B contribute. With at most one selected neighbor the contribution to it is always zero. With two selected neighbors their two parity contributions are equal. Conversely the zero contribution is always supplied by the empty hidden set. For a contribution of one at both selected neighbors, find a shortest path between them in the graph induced on D and those two vertices after temporarily removing any direct edge between the neighbors. Connectivity of D and its incidences guarantee the path exists. Its internal vertices give the required hidden selection; the shortest path is chordless in that search graph, and the possibly suppressed direct edge has no internal endpoint. Do this independently on each used optional edge of a spanning forest. The total time to form the quotient and cache one usable path per eligible component is linear in an adjacency-list representation. Recovering the original witness costs linear additional time.

The same quotient applies when each private component is already supplied with more nominal boundary vertices, provided at most two of them can be selected in a particular branch: after removing the fixed unselected boundary sites, recalculate its neighbors. A corridor obtained by replacing each edge of any base multigraph by a different connected unmarked graph with private interior therefore behaves identically to a collection of subdivided edges whenever it has exactly the two specified attachments. Mandatory vertices inside proposed private sets must first be retained in B and the resulting component condition rechecked.
