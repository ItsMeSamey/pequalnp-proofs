# Preserving cubic proofs while replacing inactive articulation satellites

Let P be three selected pins and v a free vertex with variable y. The components of G−(P∪{v}) are independent except through these four sites. For a component K let B_i∈F₂³ record the pins adjacent to i and h_i its incidence with v. Suppose the pin contribution from K is identically zero on its legal words when y=0. No condition on the rest of the graph is required.

By the [connected component span lemma](cubic-core-connected-syndromes.md), im B intersects the even plane only in zero. Consequently B=0 or im B is spanned by one odd vector b. Write the total pin contribution b z in the latter case, with z a linear form in the component bits x, and put u=h·x. Summing its native free guards cancels every internal edge twice, giving precisely z+yu=0; for B=0 this reads yu=0. If B=0 remove K, using this equation to recover the compressed v guard. If h=0 remove K, since its pin output is proved zero. In either case the inverse proof substitution sets the deleted x to zero.

In the remaining case replace K by a single vertex t adjacent to v and the pins indicated by b. At y=0 it contributes zero to the pins. At y=1 its nonzero output (b,1) to the four sites was attainable in K. Indeed the span of the four-site colors D_i=(B_i,h_i) projects onto both individual scalar coordinates z,u. Such a subspace of F₂² contains (1,1). Since b is odd, (b,1) is even. The span of legal outputs is im D intersected with the even space of four sites, by the same connected component lemma at y=1. This intersection has dimension one, so an actual legal selection d with that output occurs and can be found by the path-span construction. In particular two such satellites cannot supply two distinct rank-two planes with missing syndromes: each supplies only a complete line. Any number of independent satellites supplies the full sum subspace.

The replacement preserves native formal binary polynomial-calculus refutability at every degree D≥3. In a refutation of the compressed graph substitute t=z. The compressed guard z(1+y) follows by multiplying z+yu by 1+y using the Boolean equation of y in degree three. The compressed v guard differs from the physical v guard by yz+yu, obtained by multiplying z+yu by y. The pin rows agree. Because the new consequences themselves have degree at most two, subsequent permitted multiplications in a degree-D proof still fit the degree bound. Linear substitution transports Boolean axioms and variable multiplications at their original degree bound. For the other direction set x_i=d_i t. Pins and the v guard now agree with the compressed ones because z(d)=u(d)=1. Each substituted physical guard is a Boolean quadratic in t,y which vanishes at (0,0),(0,1),(1,1); hence it is zero or t(1+y), exactly the compressed guard, modulo Boolean axioms in degree two. This direction works also for D=2. The same substitutions transport actual satisfying selections.

All the replacement leaves at an articulation can be compressed further to at most three. Their pin colors b_j are odd. Keep a basis of these colors from the leaves themselves. In a proof on these retained leaves replace each retained bit by the corresponding linear coordinate of the total pin color Σ_j b_j t_j. The pin equations agree; applying parity to the total color gives Σ_j t_j in both descriptions, so the v guard agrees too. Each retained leaf guard becomes a sum of physical leaf guards. Conversely set every discarded leaf to zero. This step preserves all proof degrees D≥2.

In particular, if the y=0 branch is a degree-two-unrefuted NO, the connected component theorem leaves a unique carrier and every other component meets the hypothesis above. The conditional y=1 instance has only one potentially incomplete carrier table. The full unconditioned system is cubically equivalent to the carrier with the same articulation, pins, and at most three replacement leaves. This does not decide whether that remaining system always has a cubic refutation.
