# A rooted pivot certificate for three pinned vertices

Let A be alternating binary adjacency. Set y=Ax. Induced evenness means that (x_i,y_i) is among (0,0),(0,1),(1,0) at every vertex. Each of r≥1 pinned vertices requires (1,0). A witness can force arbitrarily large induced topology in degree four. There is nevertheless a normal form with at most r−1 free vertices after pivots confined to free vertices. Allowing tracked coordinate swaps on pins reduces the certificate to the r labeled pins alone.

Delete vertices outside a smallest support containing all pins. The retained set is P∪F, its witness has x=1, and A1=0. For z in ker A[F], the syndrome s=A[P,F]z satisfies 1ᵀs=0 because A[F,P]1=A[F]1. If s=0 and z≠0, toggling the free bits by z preserves every retained y and all pin values. This gives a strictly smaller support. Thus the syndrome map sends ker A[F] injectively into the even-weight pin syndromes. In particular d=nullity A[F]≤r−1.

Choose C within F giving a nonsingular principal submatrix of order rank A[F]. Such a set exists for alternating matrices: a nonzero edge permits a nonsingular two-coordinate pivot, and Schur elimination lowers rank by two at each step until the remaining alternating matrix vanishes. Write D=F minus C. Under the principal pivot on C, the roles of x_i and y_i exchange at each i in C and stay fixed elsewhere. This identity follows by solving for x_C from y_C. The constraint excluding (1,1) is invariant under exchange. The new x_C is zero since the old y_C was zero. Delete C, leaving the other pairs unchanged. The reduced graph on P∪D has the Schur complement as its adjacency, and D of size d has zero internal adjacency. A nonsingular alternating pivot is implementable by ordinary edge pivots, choosing a nonzero edge of the remaining Schur block and exchanging the pairs at its two endpoints. Each vertex in the pivot set gets swapped once.

Write N for the D-by-P adjacency in the reduced graph. The kernel isomorphism from binary vectors z_D to ker A[F] sends z_D to (A[C]^{-1}A[C,D]z_D,z_D), omitting the first component for empty C. The old syndrome on that vector equals Nᵀz_D. Thus N has row rank d. Its rows have even weight since the reduced witness has x=1 and y=0 on all survivors. At r=3 the remaining free vertices have independent two-neighbor patterns on P and no edges between them.

Take B within P giving an invertible d-by-d submatrix N[:,B]. The principal block on D∪B is invertible: its D equations in a kernel vector force the B coordinates to vanish, and the B equations then force the D coordinates to vanish. Pivot on that block and delete D, whose new x coordinates are zero. The required surviving pairs are (0,1) on B and (1,0) on the other pins. Empty pivots cover d=0.

Conversely, perform any sequence of edge pivots and deletions of nonpins, keeping each pin identity and the parity of its coordinate swaps. On a final pin-only graph set x_i=0 exactly on the pins with odd swap count, and test Ax=1+x. A positive test lifts to a valid original witness. Undo a deletion by inserting x_i=0 and computing its new y_i in the stored host. Undo a pivot by exchanging its coordinate pairs again. The constraint excluding (1,1) persists, and each original pin ends with (1,0). Thus existence is equivalent to existence of a labeled, swap-tracked pivot certificate. The construction gives O(n) operations and uses each pin in at most one pivot. Given the certificate, lifting and verification take polynomial time. Selecting a successful sequence in polynomial time is a separate problem.

On three labeled pins and no remaining free vertices the accepted forms are as follows. With no odd-swap pins, the graph is empty or a triangle. With one odd-swap pin the others are nonadjacent and the odd-swap pin has exactly one neighbor among them. With two odd-swap pins, both see the other pin and their mutual edge is unrestricted. With three odd-swap pins the test cannot pass. A three-vertex path with all vertices pinned is infeasible, though assigning its leaves swapped orientations for free passes the final test. A disjoint union of an edge containing two pins and four isolated vertices including the third pin contains an unrooted independent triple but cannot realize the original pins.

Dabrowski and coauthors give certifying algorithms for several fixed unrooted pivot-minor targets. Their input problem seeks an isomorphic target, without requiring these terminal identities and swap counts.
