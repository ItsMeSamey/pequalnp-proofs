# Counting an unrestricted circuit by absorbing products into affine coordinates

On any acyclic circuit with m arbitrary binary gates and q primary bits, deterministic polynomial preprocessing produces f quadratic consistency equations and an affine output on q+f coordinates. The count satisfying these equations with output one is the original count. An exact solver takes poly(q+m)·2^f time; f is computed from the circuit and can exceed q.

Normalize each binary gate by writing its truth table over GF(2) as a constant plus affine terms plus either zero or one input product. Give every product a formal symbol z_i in topological order and propagate other terms affinely. Each product has two affine operands on x and earlier symbols. The output is affine in x and the symbols. This costs polynomial time even with dense reads and arbitrary fan-out.

Maintain coordinates w and quadratic constraints P_j(w)=0, initially w=x with no constraints. Future operand reads and the output are affine in w and as-yet unevaluated symbols. At the next gate its operands a(w),b(w) are available. If their product is affine as a Boolean function, substitute this expression everywhere. Otherwise the two linear parts a₀,b₀ are independent.

Write every downstream read, including the output, as ℓ(w)+c z_i plus terms in later symbols. Seek a single vector v with a₀(v)=b₀(v)=0 and ℓ₀(v)=c for every read, where ℓ₀ is the linear part. For each old constraint also require that the linear part of D_vP_j(w)=P_j(w+v)+P_j(w) belong to span(a₀,b₀). This part is the polar matrix of P_j applied to v; reducing modulo the span gives ordinary linear equations. Pick a fixed elimination solution if one exists. Requiring every polar matrix to kill v is a simpler sufficient test.

If feasible, use the shear w'=w+v a(w)b(w). It is an involution since the operands are unchanged under translation by v. Each downstream read ℓ(w)+c z_i is now ℓ(w'). An old constraint becomes P_j(w')+a(w')b(w')D_vP_j(w'). The affine difference is constant, say κ_j, on a=b=1. Compute it at any solution of those two independent equations. The constraint is therefore exactly P_j+κ_j ab and stays quadratic. The span condition is necessary for this particular substitution to retain degree at most two on the full cube: in coordinates starting with a,b, every third independent coordinate in D_vP_j yields a distinct cubic monomial. Reductions modulo other constraints may permit additional simplifications.

If infeasible, append z_i as a coordinate and add z_i+a(w)b(w)=0. Each old valid state extends uniquely. Every successful change is bijective. Induction gives a bijection between final valid states with output one and satisfying primary inputs, even if their coordinate labels differ. There are exactly f extra coordinates and equations after f failures.

Include the affine output pin in the equation list. Expand each zero indicator as (1+(−1)^P)/2 and sum the resulting 2^{f+1} quadratic character sums over the final cube. Each sum takes polynomial time: when the quadratic contains a cross term ij, write it as ij+iJ+jK+R in those variables. Summing over i,j gives 2(−1)^{JK+R}. Iterate; a final affine sign has zero sum unless its linear part vanishes. The numerator needs O(q+2f+1) bits and the normalization is 2^{f+1}. This is the classical binary quadratic Gauss sum. The solver can run direct enumeration, this Gauss count, and the October width-three counter in parallel. Tseitin gives q+m variables and O(q+m) clauses for the last algorithm, taking poly(q+m)·2^{O((q+m)/√log(q+m))} under its fixed-density theorem. **Successive absorption can beat a global rank charge.** Start with q≥3 input registers. Each update replaces a target register by itself XOR the product of two other distinct registers. Cycle targets j with controls j−1,j−2 modulo q. Each next gate reads the previous target. End with a single register output after s=Θ(q log q) updates. In the normalized equations a register's linear primary part remains its original coordinate, and products are added on their target registers. At each gate the target unit vector solves the test. No constraints are created and later linear rows are unchanged regardless of tie breaking. Thus f=0 and the count is 2^{q−1}. With B the matrix of formal product coefficients in the normalized operands, rows reading the preceding target have distinct last nonzero pivots, so rank B≥s−1. If L holds primary coefficients, rank[L B]−rank L≥s−1−q. These are ordinary reversible Toffoli updates.

**Failure control.** Form z₁=x₁x₂ followed by z_i=z_{i−1}x_{(i mod q)+1} and output the last symbol. Each product has a direct later read z_i with zero current linear part, including the final output. This prevents every shear. For q≥3 the operand pairs at their turns are independent on ambient formal coordinates. Hence f=s even for s>q. Simplification using the consistency equations or a reverse analysis can solve this example quickly.

This graph algorithm has a superpolynomial saving when f≤q−ω(log q). An unrestricted worst-case saving for q log q gates remains open.
