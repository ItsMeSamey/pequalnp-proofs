# Two-edge quadratic paths suffice without linear drift

Let F be the sum over F₂ of disjoint cubic monomials covering every raw bit, together with distinct raw quadratic monomials. Require NO constant or linear terms. If each component of its raw quadratic graph has at most one edge, its exact signed gap is computable in polynomial time by the diagonal latent-link rule. If every component is an isolated vertex, a single edge or a path of TWO edges, exact evaluation is #P-hard and evaluation modulo three is #₃P-complete. The modular reduction has linear size from bipartite independent sets, and by Faben's fixed-prime construction also from a binary circuit's witness count modulo three. The exact reduction below uses one polynomial-size query from exact bipartite independent sets. All these outputs have O(q) gates at raw dimension q.

Expand each triple sign into e⊗³−2δ⊗³, e=(1,1), δ=(0,1), indexed by its latent spin s∈{0,1}. A single quadratic coupling of two raw ports has matrix ((1,1),(1,−1)). Contracted against the local vectors e and δ its latent matrix is diag(2,−1), so single edges fuse spins. A free raw port gives weights (2,1). These identities give the polynomial algorithm above by summing two alternatives per fused component and multiplying components.

Take a raw path x−y−z. Let the middle port's latent spin be s and let the end ports belong to blocks whose latent spins have already been fused to t. The resulting matrix, rows s and columns t, is ((4,2),(0,1)). For t=0 the independent sums over x,z force y=0 with factor four; this is impossible when s=1. For t=1 both endpoints are fixed to one and the two quadratic signs cancel, leaving two choices for y at s=0 or only one at s=1. Thus the exact weight is 4·2^{−s−t} times the indicator of s⇒t. This gate needs no drift.

Orient each edge of a simple bipartite graph left to right. Satisfying all resulting implications is equivalent to choosing an independent set, using s=1 for selected left and s=0 for selected right. A logical vertex v of degree d, outdegree o and indegree i requires p=o+2i external raw port slots. Give it t_v=p+U_v−2 fresh triple blocks connected in a path by clean single edges, where U_v≥max(2,3−p). The path then has t_v+2=p+U_v available slots and t_v≥max(1,p). Allocate the p used ports on distinct triples; U_v remain free. For each logical arc make its source slot the middle of a new two-edge raw path whose two endpoint slots belong to its target. Each slot is used in exactly its prescribed local component. No quadratic couples two coordinates in the same triple, and no pair of triple blocks receives a repeated quadratic link.

The path of t_v fused triples has scale 2^{t_v−1} at spin zero, with relative activity (−2)^{t_v}(−1/2)^{t_v−1}=−2 at spin one. Free slots add common scale 2^{U_v} and relative activity 2^{−U_v}. Each incident implication contributes a further 2^{−s_v}. Consequently, writing e for the number of logical edges and A=∑_v(t_v−1+U_v)+2e, the exact identity is

Z(F)=2^A ∑_{s satisfying all arcs} ∏_{v:s_v=1} (−2^{1−U_v−d_v}).

All rational denominators are powers of two. Every graph and gate list is uniformly constructible in time polynomial in its displayed size.

For the modulo-three construction, take the least such U_v with U_v+d_v even. Then U_v≤4, every relative activity is one modulo three, and the known scalar 2^A is invertible. This gives O(|V|+|E|) triples including isolates. Membership follows by producing one accepting path per positive assignment sign and two per negative sign. Faben's Theorem 3.7 uses the two-vertex edge as a fixed cancellation gadget for p=3, yielding linear size in a 3CNF description, whose witnesses can be made parsimoniously equivalent to the original circuit using constant many clauses per gate. [Faben](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0809.1836)

For the integer reduction let v count logical vertices, choose X=max_v d_v+v+4 (handle an empty source separately) and U_v=X−d_v. Write D=2^{X−1} and let C_j count feasible logical assignments with j spins equal to one. The single oracle returns 2^A ∑_{j=0}^v C_j(−1/D)^j. Multiply by D^v and divide by 2^A to obtain an integer. Modulo D the residue identifies (−1)^v C_v as the unique balanced integer because C_j≤2^v<D/2. Subtract this term, divide by D and repeat to recover all coefficients. Their sum is the desired exact number of independent sets. There are O(v²+|E|) triple blocks, and reconstruction needs only polynomial bit arithmetic. Counting bipartite independent sets is equivalent by complementation to counting bipartite vertex covers. [Provan and Ball](https://doi.org/10.1137/0212053)

This separates the known tractable subcase from counting-hard instances by actual component size in the raw quadratic graph. It is not a time lower bound for SAT. Checking if the explicit polynomial ever equals one is easy from its ANF, whereas the gap residue may vanish on an input circuit with accepting witnesses.
