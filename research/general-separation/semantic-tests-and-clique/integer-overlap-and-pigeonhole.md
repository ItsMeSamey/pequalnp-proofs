# Integer overlap consistency detects hidden charges and misses binary pigeonhole contradictions

Given explicit scopes with allowed rows, require a nonnegative rational table of mass one on each scope and agreement of projected tables on every full common overlap. Find its maximum support by testing each coordinate for positivity and averaging feasible tables. On this support require the same equations for signed integer tables of total mass one. Satisfying assignments supply compatible point masses that survive support pruning. Linear programming and integer linear system feasibility solve both tests in polynomial time in the displayed system size. This applies the support-refined BLP+AIP method of Brakensiek, Guruswami, Wrochna and Živný with full overlaps. A level-k test includes all bags of at most k raw variables, whose rows must satisfy constraints contained in the bag, as well as the primitive scopes. Its cost is polynomial for fixed k and bounded primitive scope size. [BLP+AIP](https://doi.org/10.1137/20M1312745)

## Hidden additive charges

Suppose the allowed rows at each vertex scope obey a charge equation in some finite abelian group. Each edge enters its endpoints with opposite signs and the same decoded label, any function of a shared variable block. Nonzero total charge makes the integer system infeasible. Multiply each allowed row equation by its signed integer weight and add. Agreement on the whole shared block gives identical decoded group sums at the two ends, which cancel. Total mass one preserves the nonzero total charge. The algorithm needs neither the decoder nor the group as input. The scopes must contain the displayed vertex relations, which may require enlarging bags following a CNF compilation.

For example, represent each edge parity bit by the AND of its own two bits and prescribe XOR charge at each vertex of a loopless graph of minimum degree two. Both local fibers have full affine hull in the raw bits over F₂. The zero fiber contains zero and all unit vectors. In the one fiber, activate one other edge pair and flip the chosen bit with its partner zero to realize each coordinate direction as a difference. A per-scope affine hull loses all charge information.

## Signed consistent injections

For h holes there are integral signed tables T_j of mass one on ordered injective j-tuples for all 1≤j≤floor((h+1)/2), such that deleting any coordinate gives T_(j−1) in the retained order. Set T_0=1 and choose a point mass T_1. For j≥2 use the chessboard complex Δ_(j,h) of partial injections from j rows to h holes. Orient simplices by increasing row. On faces missing row i place T_(j−1) with sign (−1)^(i−1) and sum over i to form a (j−2)-chain z. Its boundary vanishes since paired omissions have equal marginals and opposite signs. For j=2 its reduced augmentation is zero. The chessboard connectivity theorem of Björner, Lovász, Vrećica and Živaljević gives (j−2)-connectivity for h≥2j−1 and hence zero integral reduced homology in degree j−2. An integral top chain c has boundary z. Every codimension-one face misses precisely one row, so c has the required marginal on each deletion separately. Total mass follows from any margin. Sorting each pigeon subset by ID gives compatible tables. Uniform injections supply a second family of nonnegative tables positive on every possible injective row. [Chessboard connectivity](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/49.1.25)

Ciardo and Živný’s journal Theorems 2.4–2.6 construct hollow tensors with palette j(j+1)/2 and obtain BA_j acceptance for loopless graphs into that clique. The signed-table filling above uses 2j−1 holes. This compares the stated sufficient parameters. Optimality and literature-wide priority are open in this note. [Ciardo and Živný](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/Stanislav.Zivny/publications/cz25sicomp-approximate-preprint.pdf)

## One width-three family defeats both tests

Take h≥4 a power of two. Give each of h+1 pigeons log₂h raw bits. For each unordered pair of pigeons, add private XOR gates comparing matching bit positions, a private OR tree combining these difference bits and a unit clause asserting that tree’s root. Encode every gate exactly by clauses of width at most three. The size is O(h² log h). The formula is unsatisfiable. Each primary bit depends on one pigeon and each gate bit is a deterministic function of only two pigeon labels.

A bag of at most k raw or gate bits depends on at most 2k pigeons. For k≤floor((h+1)/4), push both table families above through the gate functions to every bag. Restriction and deterministic evaluation commute on the pigeons actually used by a common sub-bag, so projected bag tables agree. Primitive clause scopes require at most two pigeons even when they have more than k bits. All contained constraints hold, since injections make every participating pair unequal. Each nonzero signed projected row is positive under the projected uniform injection, establishing survival after even maximum-support pruning.

There is no identity representing the constant one as a real linear combination of nonempty products of at most floor(h/2) original bad-clause indicators. For squarefree Boolean monomials whose variables depend on at most h pigeons, define a linear functional by uniform injection on the pigeons used, followed by evaluation of their deterministic gate bits. Extend linearly in this basis. A product of d bad clauses after expansion involves at most 2d pigeons. Consistency gives it the value obtained by a single sample on those pigeons. All its bad clauses evaluate to zero. The constant has value one, proving the claim. Even before the degree cutoff the positive-product span is exponentially large, since each unit clause produces a distinct bad-cube indicator 1−z on its own private output bit and the different squarefree products of these indicators are independent on the ambient Boolean cube. The formulas have a direct counting refutation.

At the other extreme, no consistent integer injection tables on all h-pigeon subsets exist. Each such injection is a bijection. For a fixed hole let u_i be its signed singleton marginal at pigeon i. For each omitted pigeon i the remaining h marginals sum to one. Comparing the equations gives h u_1=1, impossible over the integers. This leaves a gap between the guaranteed feasible and infeasible pigeon levels.
