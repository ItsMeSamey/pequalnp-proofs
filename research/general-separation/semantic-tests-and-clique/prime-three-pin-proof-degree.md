# A three-pin NO family of exact polynomial-calculus degree three

The simple graph with adjacency masks

    6372,1572,4107,1828,640,8011,161,3665,1064,4282,6570,1185,1573

and pins 8,9,10 has ten free sites and a rank-three pin system. The [shore checker](quadratic_shore_certificates.py) enumerates 128 pin-slice points and finds no physical witness. On the 1,022 ordered nonempty proper free shores the maximum of the two physical free-shore cut ranks has distribution 128 at four, 614 at five and 280 at six. The [cut-rank criterion](quadratic-certificate-cut-ranks.md) thus excludes even iterative degree-two polynomial calculus over the binary field on this instance.

Its exact iterative degree is three and its exact static Nullstellensatz degree is four, in the following standard formal-degree convention. Include Boolean field equations, work over the binary field, allow polynomial sums and multiply a polynomial by a variable only when the formal product fits the degree bound. Static degree counts products of monomials with original generators, before Boolean reduction. The fixed pin values may be eliminated first. The [independent small linear calculation](three_pin_degree_profile.py) parametrizes the remaining pin equations by seven affine coordinates, computes the restricted guard coefficients from the 128 values by the Boolean Möbius transform, and spans all degree-bounded static multiples of the quadratic guards. Iteratively it then multiplies every current low-degree span element by every variable, retaining the resulting span until stable. Ordering monomials by degree allows ordinary echelon reduction to identify the entire subspace of polynomial degree below the cutoff. The pairs (static rank, iterative rank) at degrees 2,3,4 are (9,9), (52,64), (99,99). The constant one belongs precisely to the iterative space at degree three and both spaces at degree four.

Eliminating consistent linear pin equations preserves these degree tests. Extend an affine chart to an invertible affine coordinate change in which the extra coordinates are the pin syndromes set to zero. Every polynomial of degree at most D differing from its restricted representative is reduced using the linear pin equations with multipliers of degree at most D−1, modulo Boolean field equations. Substitution by affine linear forms preserves degree bounds and transforms a Boolean axiom to a sum of Boolean axioms. On the original graph every free guard has degree two; computing static multiples through degree D−2 on the pin slice is therefore the correct static test. Iterative multiplication on the quotient can be lifted by the same normalization after each step.

## Scaling while retaining the degree and defeating module recognition

Replace each of the ten free sites by an arbitrary nonempty simple graph, using complete joins on the old free-to-free edges and the old pin incidences on every copy. Odd selected multiplicity in a class forces its common exterior prescription to be zero by handshaking, and zero occupancy may be represented by selecting no member. Thus this operation preserves the NO answer. It also preserves the *exact* minimum formal degree for both proof models. In the forward direction substitute the parity of the class into each old variable. The old guard becomes the sum of the physical guards in that class because all internal edge products cancel in characteristic two. The pin equations are the corresponding parity substitutions and the Boolean axioms are sums of the new Boolean axioms. Linear substitution simulates a variable multiplication by a sum of single-variable multiplications without increasing the degree. Conversely keep a single copy per class and set the others to zero. Every original pin equation and retained guard specializes to the base system and all discarded guards vanish. This restriction gives the reverse degree bound.

For every nonempty proper split of the blown-up free sites, choose a left and a right representative from different original classes, then one representative arbitrarily from every class still unrepresented. Such a pair always exists since both sides are nonempty and at least two classes are present. The induced representative graph gives a nonempty proper base split. Each of the new physical cut matrices contains the corresponding base cut matrix as a submatrix. Consequently at least one of the two new shore ranks is ≥4, for every split.

Now use t vertices per class, each with a designated adjacent pair s_i,t_i. All other internal edges are independently fair. Pivot once on each designated pair using the labeled principal pivot on the two sites. Each pivot fixes the graph outside its bag because every outside vertex sees both endpoints equally and the two contributions to an outside Schur update cancel. Inside, the pivot is an involution on graphs with the designated edge, so the resulting internal bags remain independent and uniform conditional on that edge. Set the activity of the two designated vertices to one. For every other vertex v in its bag set α_v=1+A'_{s_i v}+A'_{t_i v}, using the resulting internal adjacency. The pair swaps its endpoint neighborhoods, leaving this sum unchanged. The pivot formula gives interbag edges α_v α_w times the original base edge, and pin-to-bag edges α_v times the base incidence. This follows one bag at a time, replacing its former all-one interface by α while retaining the other bag's current interface.

Every labeled physical cut rank is invariant under an edge pivot: the graph of the binary linear map is changed by exchanging its input/output coordinates at the pivot endpoints, and the rank of a cut equals the dimension of the pair projection to that shore minus its size. The pivot also preserves the two proof degrees: the new free inputs are an invertible affine substitution in the old ones after fixing pin inputs; each free guard product is preserved, and the pin outputs remain unchanged. Apply the inverse substitution for the converse.

With probability tending to one the final graph has vertex connectivity Ω(t) and has no proper nontrivial module. Each random bag is connected after deletion of a sufficiently small linear fraction of its vertices and has no nontrivial proper module with overwhelming probability. For the connectivity use the small-cut union bound from the [affine matching control](affine-matching-backdoor-control.md). For primeness, the probability that a fixed s-vertex subset of a uniform random t-vertex graph is a module is 2^{−(s−1)(t−s)}; summing binom(t,s) times this bound over 2≤s<t tends to zero. Conditioning one edge to be present changes each upper bound by at most factor two. The number of active nonendpoints is binomial(t−2,1/2), so all ten bags have both types and at least t/4 active sites with overwhelming probability. The base free graph is connected and each pin has a free neighbor. After fewer than a sufficiently small constant times t deletions, the connected remaining bags are thus joined through surviving active sites, and all surviving pins attach.

Except with exponentially small probability, no vertex has its internal adjacency on all other bag vertices either identically zero or equal to their activity vector. For a nonendpoint, test all other nonendpoints: the tested edges are fair bits independent of their respective activities conditional on the endpoint rows. For endpoint s_i, equality to activity at each nonendpoint would force every t_i-to-nonendpoint edge to be one; the converse holds for t_i. Union bound over the vertices. A global module intersects each bag in a module of its induced graph, therefore in at most one vertex or the full bag. If it meets a bag in a single site and also has an outside site, these two sites must have identical neighborhoods on the rest of the bag, contradicting the tested condition. A whole bag alone is distinguished by an active neighbor of another bag. Any module containing a whole bag must include all active neighbors in adjacent bags since they distinguish its active and inactive members; singleton intersections have already been ruled out. Connectedness of the base forces every bag and then every pin. A module of at least two pins alone is impossible because the pin incidence rows are distinct. Thus the graph is modular-prime.

Finally the high local independence argument from the [module lookahead control](linked-modules-and-affine-propagation.md) applies to these ten classes before pivoting, with 10t rather than 6t free sites: select a sufficiently small absolute ε so 10H₂(ε/10)+ε(log₂3+1)<1, and disregard both designated endpoints when checking random columns. Nonzero pin-row combinations occupy entire classes. Free pivots interchange pair coordinates and induce an affine isomorphism of the physical pin slices, so the same lower bound survives for all collections of ≤εt local pairs. On this NO family such local affine-hull lookahead does not move. The underlying rank-one cuts of the bags remain after pivoting. This is an exact low proof-degree distinction on a restricted decision problem, not a lower bound on unrestricted computation. The [bounded construction checker](pivoted_strong_blowup.py) supplies explicit modular-prime fixtures at t=8,16,32.
