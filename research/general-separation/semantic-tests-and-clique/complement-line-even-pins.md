# Fixed pinned induced-even support on complements of simple line graphs

Let the physical host be the complement of the line graph of a simple loopless B. The h pinned vertices are a specified set F of base edges. For fixed h the following polynomial-time algorithm constructs a physical support. Recover B from the complemented host by classical line-graph recognition if necessary. This proof uses Liu–Yoo Theorem 1.3 with ordinary **unoriented** path lengths modulo four. [Liu and Yoo](https://people.tamu.edu/~yyoo/stoc25.pdf)

The degree at selected edge uv is |D|−1−(deg_D(u)+deg_D(v)−2). Thus its endpoint degree parities must agree if |D| is odd, and differ if |D| is even. When all selected edges join opposite degree parities, |D| is automatically even, since it is the sum of selected degrees on the even side. Guess the degree colors on the endpoints W of F, respecting the required relation across each forced edge. Partition W into groups according to the residual components below.

**Opposite colors.** Seek disjoint trees in B−F covering the groups and no other named sites. Properly two-color each tree, respecting its guessed degree colors on W. Require that the parity of its number of color-one vertices equal the sum of forced degrees over its names. Leaf elimination then supplies residual edges whose incident parities are color(v)+deg_F(v). Adding F restores the desired degrees.

To see sufficiency of the bounded search, start with an actual solution and take components of all opposite-color edges in B−F. Handshake on D−F gives the required sum in each named component. Take a spanning tree and repeatedly prune pairs of unnamed leaves of the same color, preserving that sum. At most two unnamed leaves per tree remain. Other components can be omitted.

**Equal colors, odd total edge count.** Each group now has a constant degree color α. Its connecting motif may be a tree or a connected unicyclic graph with an odd cycle. Require α times the motif order to equal the sum of forced degrees over its names modulo two. Then residual target degrees γ(v)=α+deg_F(v) are attainable. In a tree the number of chosen residual edges modulo two equals the sum of γ over either side of the *tree bipartition*; this coloring is distinct from α. On an odd unicyclic motif either edge-count parity is possible: solve on a spanning tree and toggle the odd cycle if needed. Allow one additional unnamed motif consisting of a single edge, if desired, with both endpoints of degree color one. All motifs must be vertex-disjoint. Check that residual parities plus |F| can give an odd total, perform the corrections and return the physical names.

Given a solution, take the components of all same-color edges in B−F. In a named bipartite component choose a spanning tree. Its bipartition agrees with that of the whole component, so it gives the correct parity of the residual source edges. Delete pairs of unnamed leaves from the same bipartition side to preserve both order parity and γ-sum parity on either side. In a named nonbipartite component begin with a simple odd cycle and grow it to a spanning unicyclic graph. Delete pairs of unnamed leaves; the cycle remains and the total order parity is preserved. Both cases leave at most two unnamed leaves per motif. For unnamed components only their combined selected edge-count parity matters. If even, omit all. If odd, select any one of their selected edges and discard all other unnamed components' edges. This leaves a disjoint single edge on two freely colored odd vertices with the required contribution.

Suppress unnamed degree-two vertices, first marking three distinct sites on each retained cycle. The bounded number of leaves and cycles gives O(h+1) skeleton vertices and paths, including singleton motifs. Enumerate the abstract shapes, injective placements including all named sites, and the length modulo four of each path. With an endpoint bipartition color specified, this determines the opposite endpoint color and both parity counts among the interior sites. Add each skeleton site once to get the vertex counts required above. For unicyclic motifs the residues give cycle oddness and order parity directly. Search via the [disjoint endpoint reduction](line-graph-even-pins.md), subtracting the two known end edges on long paths. Liu–Yoo Theorem 1.3 with all edges labeled one in Z/4 and the three undesired residues forbidden supplies the paths. Reconstruct the trees or unicyclic motifs, compute residual edges, and replay the complemented line-graph degree equations. Extra base edges not selected have no physical incidence.

Three disjoint base edges with two mandatory illustrate the need for an optional odd-cardinality contribution from unnamed sites. The two arcs between suitable same-side vertices on a six-cycle have ordinary lengths 2 and 4 but identical signed gain zero if all edges are oriented from one bipartition side to the other. Thus the use of unoriented modular lengths here cannot in general be replaced by oriented gains with constant label one.
