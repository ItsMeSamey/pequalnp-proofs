# Assessment of the near-Shannon E route

The deterministic canonical starting point is known. Miltersen, Vinodchandran and Watanabe, Lemma 5 and Figure 1, generate a maximum-circuit-size table by extending a prefix with the lexicographically smaller possible bit, using a second-level polynomial-hierarchy oracle on the table scale. SAT∈P putting the canonical bit language in E is a direct corollary. Kannan had an earlier third-level exponential construction. [MVW](https://www.brics.dk/RS/99/46/BRICS-RS-99-46.pdf) [Kannan](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0019995882903825)

Chen, Hirahara, Li and Ren give near-maximum 2^n/n deterministic size in symmetric exponential time, and their Theorem 4.8 gives exponential average-case hardness with the tradeoff δ₁+2δ₂<1. Their single-valued range avoidance in Corollary 4.5 also strengthens existence of a hard language for the [sequential controller model](sequential-stream-avoidance.md) to S₂E without assuming SAT∈P. Recover a bounded controller's table in poly(N) time under the specified time or linear-state limits, mapping invalid descriptions to zero. Supply as extra input the rank of an error mask of weight at most pN. Enumerative decoding by exact binomial counts takes poly(N) time. There are at most 2^(H₂(p)N) masks. With the small description constant of the linked note, the combined input is shorter than N. Compile the uniform polynomial-time map to a polynomial-size circuit with N outputs, padding the input to N−1. A string avoided by this circuit is far from every valid controller. Their single-valued canonical Avoid procedure yields an S₂E bit language by the argument of their Theorem 4.8; it need not select the lexicographically first avoided table. Their Theorem 4.9 separately handles fixed-time exponential machines with close-to-full advice. The controller accounting and silent-delay limit are specific to the linked formulation. General high-class range avoidance is prior work. [Chen, Hirahara, Li and Ren](https://hanlin-ren.github.io/files/pdf/CHLR_S2E_journal.pdf)

The hypothesis that every E language has infinitely-often constant-error approximators below a fixed a·2^n/n, or an appropriate recurrent representation, has no construction covering arbitrary E algorithms here. Treat it as a sufficient condition with restricted tests. Generic synthesis alone cannot prove it: Andreev, Clementi and Rolim give matching worst-function bounds on prescribed approximation advantage. Impagliazzo and Wigderson prove E hardness 2^(εn) for some fixed ε>0 suffices for P=BPP. A bound a·2^n/n remains much bigger than 2^(εn) at any fixed ε<1; their assumption and conclusion do not yield the desired improvement. Miltersen and Vinodchandran's AM derandomization uses exponential hardness for single-valued nondeterministic circuits with a success flag, distinct from one-sided existential acceptance. Ordinary existential hardness implies the requisite single-valued hardness up to constant gate overhead by testing for flagged one outputs. Under P=NP, P=BPP and AM=NP already follow from the polynomial-hierarchy collapse. These consequences supply no contradiction. [Andreev, Clementi and Rolim](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/1995/041/) [Impagliazzo and Wigderson](https://www.math.ias.edu/~avi/PUBLICATIONS/MYPAPERS/IW97/proc.pdf) [Miltersen and Vinodchandran](https://www.brics.dk/RS/99/47/BRICS-RS-99-47.pdf)

The easy-witness method searches for proofs whose bits are computable by small circuits. IKW's equivalence between NEXP⊆P/poly and NEXP=MA uses a strong nonuniform collapse on the former side. P=NP alone does not supply their small-circuit hypothesis. The [hard-history test](exact-maximum-hard-histories.md) instead derives, conditional on P=NP, a quantitative obstruction for a specific canonical E language: for each decider, a positive fraction of each label measured against all raw inputs has histories with large circuit addressing complexity. An E-time computation can take 2^(cn) steps with arbitrarily large fixed c. No examined argument compresses all such computations below the proved scale. A requirement on every decider of even a trivial language would be too strong: an accept-all machine can append the entire table of an unrelated E language as irrelevant work. The quantifier in the proposed sufficient condition is some useful decider per language. [Impagliazzo, Kabanets and Wigderson](https://www2.cs.sfu.ca/~kabanets/papers/exp_journal.pdf)
