# Pinned even supports as nonsingular diagonal completions with fragile named choices

Let A be a symmetric loopless binary adjacency matrix. An induced-even support contains a named set P if and only if some binary d makes M=A+diag(d) nonsingular with diag(M⁻¹)_p=1 for every p in P. Given a physical witness one can find such a d in polynomial time while retaining its entire support. Given d one obtains a genuine witness by inversion. Finding such a d without a witness is a separate search problem.

Write x for an actual witness. Choose d_i=(Ax)_i wherever x_i=0, and freely at selected sites. Then Mx=d. Conversely every solution of Mx=d gives (Ax)_i=d_i(1+x_i), so induces even degrees on its support. If M is singular, take a nonzero kernel vector v. If v avoids all selected sites, first replace x by x+v, enlarging the support and preserving Mx=d. Now choose i with v_i=x_i=1 and flip d_i. Both sides of Mx=d change by e_i. This unit vector is outside the image of M because vᵀe_i=1. Any kernel vector u of M+e_i e_iᵀ must have u_i=0 (otherwise Mu=e_i), and that new kernel is exactly ker(M) intersected with u_i=0. Rank rises by one. At most n flips reach a nonsingular completion.

For nonsingular symmetric M with diagonal d one has M⁻¹d=diag(M⁻¹): with z=M⁻¹e_i, symmetry in characteristic two gives dᵀz=zᵀMz=z_i. The diagonal-range and inverse-diagonal facts and loop rank updates have prior literature. Their use here retains the physical pins and constructs a certificate. [Filmus](https://yuvalfilmus.cs.technion.ac.il/Manuscripts/Range.pdf) [Aliabadi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11056)

Equivalently choose one column in each labeled pair {A_i,A_i+e_i} to form a basis such that switching any one named choice destroys the basis. This follows because det(M+e_i e_iᵀ)=det(M)(1+(M⁻¹)_ii). For three pins this is exactly an isolated one relative to the three named directions in a fiber of the Boolean function d↦det(A+diag(d)). Contracting the chosen free columns leaves a three-dimensional space in which the chosen named triple is independent and the alternate at i lies in the span of the other two. All three conditions must hold for the same free transversal.

## A fixed exterior and its complete three-pin boundary test

Fix the n−3 free diagonal sites. Let D be the free principal block and t its nullity. By a free-coordinate congruence split off a nonsingular part and eliminate it by Schur complementation. What remains is [0,L;Lᵀ,C+diag(z)] with zero block t-by-t and L of size t-by-3; translate z to absorb the diagonal of C. The dropped determinant is one. A rank deficiency in the rows of L makes the entire fiber zero, so assume full row rank and t≤3.

Write b_ij for the off-diagonal entries of C. For t=0 the polynomial is z₁z₂z₃+b₂₃z₁+b₁₃z₂+b₁₂z₃. Testing its eight inputs gives isolated ones only for b=000 (one) or b=111 (three). For t=1 let λ be the nonzero row of L. Then the polynomial is λ₁z₂z₃+λ₂z₁z₃+λ₃z₁z₂+s, where s=λ₁b₂₃+λ₂b₁₃+λ₃b₁₂, by determinant expansion in characteristic two. The λ-weighted sum of the three first differences is zero. At an isolated one all differences are one, hence λ has weight two. For λ=110 up to permutation, the differences all equal one exactly for z₃=z₁+z₂=1; the determinant there is 1+s. Thus there are two isolated ones when s=0, and none otherwise.

For t=2 the determinant is affine with z_i coefficient equal to the square of the minor of L on the two other columns. The three coefficients form the nonzero kernel vector of L. There are four isolated ones if that vector is 111 and none otherwise. For t=3 the full-rank determinant is the constant det(L)²=1 and has none. This evaluation is for a supplied exterior; it does not find the exterior. In particular, counting isolated ones modulo two is unsound here.

## A sparse chart need not contain any witness

Start with a cycle of length 4t+3, t≥1, and three consecutive pins. Their unique induced-even extension is the full cycle, by propagation from an adjacent selected pair. Its free path consists of consecutive segments U,V on 2t sites each. Even path adjacency is nonsingular by determinant expansion at a leaf. Principal-pivot on U only. This swaps the actual pair (x_i,y_i) for y=Ax on U and fixes other pairs. It produces another symmetric loopless graph B: the graph space is still isotropic with xᵀy=0, and the invertible principal block makes it a graph over the new input. The pins remain unchanged and local forbidden state (1,1) is invariant. In the unique transformed witness the free input has support V and the free output has support U, both of size 2t.

Its free block B[F,F] is nonsingular as well. With zero pin inputs and zero transformed free output, the original x_U=0 and y_V=0. Since H[V,V] for the original cycle H is nonsingular, x_V=0 and hence y_U=0. Thus all transformed free inputs vanish. Although the pinned linear parity equations by themselves admit a vector of weight at most three on either of the free input and free output charts (at most three linear equations, with an invertible change of free chart), no physical witness is sparse on either. This disproves a bounded-weight rule for a fixed chart, not adaptive choices of diagonal completion.
