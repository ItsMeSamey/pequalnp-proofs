# Tight score-flattening cost for a linear-size 2-CNF

Let DISJ_n(x,y) be the conjunction of (NOT x_i OR NOT y_i) for i=1,…,n. A witness-preserving score representation has m fixed subcube indicators G_j on all of (x,y,z), with arbitrarily many auxiliary bits z. Each subcube fixes any set of literals, without a width limit. For each z allow arbitrary weights w_j(z) and a Boolean table on the attained values of S_z(x,y)=Σ_j w_j(z)G_j(x,y,z). Suppose each z slice has at most d+1 attained values. The predicate is true iff some z accepts. For 1≤d≤n the least possible m is Θ(d·2^{n/d}) with absolute constants, even with these freedoms. The upper bound uses ordinary unweighted CNF violation scores with no auxiliaries.

For a fixed z interpolate its table by a polynomial of degree ≤d in S_z. Boolean idempotence gives a sum over ≤Σ_{j=0}^d binom(m,j) subsets of features; coefficients may depend on z. Each product of fixed subcubes factors as a function of x times a function of y times a function of z. Its x and y factors do not change across witnesses. Summing the nonnegative acceptance bits over z gives the witness-count matrix M, of real rank at most the binomial sum, because all coefficients and witness counts combine on each fixed rectangle. It suffices even to sum a finite list covering all yes pairs.

M is nonzero precisely on DISJ_n. Complement the column subset to T and order row and column subsets by increasing cardinality. The support is inclusion x⊆T, yielding an upper triangular matrix with positive diagonal. Its rank is 2^n, the classical maximal nondeterministic rank of disjointness. Hence Σ_{j≤d}binom(m,j)≥2^n, so m≥n≥d and (em/d)^d≥2^n. This gives m≥(d/e)2^{n/d}. [de Wolf](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539702407345)

For the converse split n coordinates into d nonempty blocks of length ≤ceil(n/d). Partition the bad assignments of one length-ℓ block into disjoint subcubes. Choose the first i with x_i=y_i=1 and impose that pair. For each earlier j choose one of the disjoint cases x_j=0 or x_j=1,y_j=0. Later coordinates are free. There are Σ_{i=1}^ℓ2^{i−1}=2^ℓ−1 subcubes. Negate each to get a clause violated there. The total violated-clause count equals the number of bad blocks, has exactly the d+1 values 0,…,d and is zero precisely on DISJ_n. There are m≤d(2^{ceil(n/d)}−1)≤2d·2^{n/d} ordinary clauses. For m=n^{c+o(1)} with c>1 fixed, the minimum d has sharp leading order (1+o(1))n/((c−1)log₂ n) in the polynomial-exponent regime. [Finite rank and pooling checks](disjoint_score_pool_check.py)

For general Boolean features with ranks r_j across the single global partition x | (y,z), the same proof bounds rank(M) by Σ_{|T|≤d}∏_{j∈T}r_j. Factor each feature into r_j products of an x-function and a (y,z)-function, expand each Hadamard product and sum over z on the right. The slice polynomial may depend on z but not separately on x or y. A rank bound only on individual z slices fails: guessing z=x and requiring y_i=0 wherever z_i=1 uses a single subcube on (x,y) per z, while its global feature rank is 2^n. The theorem excludes a witness-preserving low-score flattening of this easy 2-CNF, not algorithms deciding SAT.
