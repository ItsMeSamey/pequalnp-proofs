# Three pinned vertices on bipartite graphs and on graphs reducible by an independent set of local complementations

Three specified vertices in an induced all-even subgraph admit a deterministic polynomial-time witness algorithm on bipartite graphs of arbitrary degree. The algorithm uses the project's binary trace theorem in ambient boundary dimension three. It also applies when any subset of a supplied independent set of unpinned vertices can be locally complemented to obtain a bipartite graph. Recognizing this latter condition and recovering a successful schedule are polynomial even if neither the subset nor the bipartition is given.

## Bipartite trace

Write V=L∪R for a bipartition, and C for the binary L-by-R adjacency matrix. Write x and y for the selections on L and R respectively. Form an ordinary binary vector family in GF(2)^L with one column e_i per left position and C_j per right position. All positions retain identities, even for repeated or zero columns. If the three pins are on one side, taking those vertices already solves the problem. Otherwise exchange sides if needed; denote the two pinned left positions by p,q and the pinned right position by t.

Remove their three columns from the ordinary selection and take the named space W=span(e_p,e_q,C_t), of dimension at most three. Prescribe the functional lambda on W by lambda(e_p)=lambda(e_q)=1 and lambda(C_t)=0. If these assignments do not define a linear functional, reject. Query the trace with target C_t: it asks for an ordinary subset summing to C_t and a functional x on the entire ambient space extending lambda and vanishing on every chosen column. This is exactly the named three-dimensional trace of the earlier project result. That theorem uses the binary three-element circuit algorithm, interface replacements and ordinary disjoint paths in a graphic terminal case. The circuit-through-three-names theorem alone need not answer the trace. [Three-dimensional trace proof and algorithm](../../three-dimensional-trace/README.md) [Geelen, Gerards and Whittle, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20960)

From a trace witness, include the right indices of its selected columns and t in y. The indicator of the selected left unit columns equals Cy by the sum equation. This vector vanishes at p,q. Since x annihilates selected unit columns, x and Cy have disjoint supports. Since x annihilates every selected right column and C_t, y and Cᵀx have disjoint supports. These are precisely the induced evenness conditions, with x_p=x_q=y_t=1. Conversely, from an actual pinned support take the right columns where y_j=1 except j=t, and the left unit columns where (Cy)_i=1. The units p,q are unused by induced evenness. The sum is C_t, and x gives the required functional. Zero, dependent and parallel named columns are covered by the consistency check and the lower-dimensional trace. This decision and witness result does not count all supports.

The bipartition supplies distinct physical roles to vertices. Dividing a nonbipartite input into left and right copies and simply forgetting to synchronize them fails. For example, put the endpoints of an edge on the pinned left and an isolated third vertex on the pinned right in the ordinary bipartite double cover. Selecting these three copies alone gives an even support; the original graph on three pinned vertices has none.

## Finding a bipartite graph by neutral local complementation

Let U be a supplied independent set containing no pin. Local complementation at u toggles each edge between distinct neighbors of u. No such operation changes any adjacency incident to any vertex of U. The neighbor sets at all available operations therefore stay fixed, and the operations commute. Define E* as the pairs of distinct vertices with a common neighbor in U. For each pair i,j in E*, any final bipartite coloring must give them the same color because both have the same unchanged neighbor u. Thus for every such pair impose the binary linear equation

Σ_{u∈U} A_iu A_ju t_u = A_ij.

These equations say that every edge on E* is absent after toggling the vertices selected by t. All pairs outside E* are immutable. Test whether the graph of immutable edges is bipartite. The toggling system is consistent and the immutable graph bipartite exactly when a successful subset exists: a solution erases every eligible edge, and the remaining graph consists precisely of the immutable edges. Conversely every successful sequence must erase precisely those eligible edges originally present and keep the other eligible pairs empty. This gives the schedule by elimination. The total cost of forming these equations from dense adjacency is polynomial in the displayed graph size. No enumeration of 2^{|U|} schedules is necessary.

For witness preservation under a local complementation at u, let h be the original adjacency column at u and y=Ax. The new adjacency is A′=A+hhᵀ+diag(h). Define x′ by changing only x_u to 1+x_u+y_u. Then y′_u=y_u and y′_i=y_i+h_i(1+x_i) elsewhere. At u this permutation fixes the forbidden pair (1,1) and exchanges two allowed pairs. At a neighbor it also fixes (1,1) and exchanges two allowed pairs. Every other site is unchanged. The pinned x coordinates stay fixed, and applying the same operation again gives the inverse witness map. This is an affine transformation on actual binary assignments. Apply the bipartite trace after the computed schedule and undo the operations in reverse order. For an explicit unbounded schedule, disjoint triangles each with one permitted neutral vertex require every such vertex to be complemented; the resulting components are three-vertex paths.

Without an independent-set promise no bound on finding a successful unrestricted schedule follows from these linear equations. Fixed labeled graph-state vertex-minor algorithms allow other operations and can require bounded host rank-width. The present trace uses the actual bipartite adjacency and recovers the prescribed physical pin values. [Dahlberg and Wehner](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.05305)
