# A linear lower bound for arbitrary parity checks on the merged guards

For each integer k≥3 there is a connected subcubic original common-hole NO graph with n=388k−156 vertices, named nonadjacent degree-two terminals x,y, and k independent binary vectors d₁,…,dₖ on its original vertices. Every nonzero vector in their span occurs as the original degree-error vector in a legal XOR-merged witness. In each witness all midpoint guards hold and the original flow-error vector equals the degree-error vector, including the unit demands at x and y. Consequently fewer than k homogeneous linear checks of these original errors, even checks tailored to the instance, accept a false witness: their restriction to the k-dimensional span has a nonzero kernel vector.

Use k input variables and a chain of k−2 three-literal clauses encoding their disjunction. For each input Xᵢ add four clauses (¬Xᵢ ∨ ±aᵢ ∨ ±bᵢ), using the four sign choices on fresh variables. These force Xᵢ false; the complete formula is unsatisfiable. Every clause uses three distinct variables, and every variable occurs with both signs. For each nonzero input word, set the chain variables to satisfy all the disjunction clauses and set all aᵢ,bᵢ false. The only unsatisfied clauses are (¬Xᵢ ∨ aᵢ ∨ bᵢ) at the selected coordinates.

Apply the original degree-three common-hole construction of Lévêque, Lin, Maffray and Trotignon. Their Lemma 2.6 says that the resulting graph has no induced cycle through both terminals. Its forward direction describes a vertex selection using the truth assignment and one chosen true occurrence per clause. Use that selection here with one change: at an unsatisfied clause choose its first occurrence, ¬Xᵢ. [LLMT, Section 2.4 and Lemma 2.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.0971)

The selected subgraph remains connected. Both occurrence rails run from x to their far ends; the variable and clause corridors join them to y. Every chosen corridor segment still connects its designated ends even if some of its middle vertices already belong to a rail. At a chosen true occurrence, the negative middle pair on the alpha rail was available for a separate detour as in LLMT. At a chosen false occurrence both negative rails are already selected, so the positive and negative clause attachments each meet **two** selected middle rail vertices. The four middle rail vertices, in positions 2 and 3 on both rails, each change from degree two to degree three. Each of the two chosen clause attachment vertices also has degree three. All six therefore have odd degree. The variable detour for a false occurrence uses the separate positive middle pair; its selected degrees stay two. At the remaining sites the paths and degrees are as in a satisfying selection. The six exceptional sites form dᵢ and are disjoint across the designated clauses. Thus the odd-degree set is precisely the sum of the directions selected by the input word.

Select every midpoint internal to the resulting vertex set, and then toggle the midpoints on one selected x–y path. Each selected midpoint has two selected endpoints. The vector of midpoint incidence parities on original vertices equals the degree-error vector plus the two terminal units. Subtracting the required terminal demands gives exactly the degree-error vector. This real midpoint selection witnesses the claimed collision for the merged local XOR and preserves the original site identities.

The checker constructs the complete LLMT graph and checks every nonzero input for k=3,4,5,6. It verifies connectivity, all odd degrees, all selected midpoint endpoint checks, and both original terminal demands. The asymptotic lower bound follows from the family, independently of this finite replay. [Executable checker](llmt_punctured_residual.py)
