# Block interpolation with fully matched cubic phases

Start with a signed cubic phase G on n=O(N) bits whose cubic monomials are pairwise disjoint. Let the k≤n spare bits be ALL variables outside the cubic matching. To preserve quadratic maximum degree four after inserting chains, require every spare to have initial quadratic degree at most three and every remaining bit to have initial quadratic degree at most four. The port preparation below guarantees this. In the [linear-size port preparation](perfect-cubic-matching-count.md), N is the original number of primary bits plus gates plus one, and the desired #CircuitSAT answer is Z(G) divided by a known power of two. Here Z denotes the sum of the phase signs. We implement Curticapean's block interpolation with physical signed unary chains of logarithmic length. [Block interpolation](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ic.2018.02.008)

**Unary nodes.** A terminal triple uab yields the conditional-weight pair (4,2) on u. Given a suffix with weights (A,B), put a fresh front triple uab and couple u to its suffix port by a quadratic term. Its weights become (4(A+B),2(A−B)). Adding the linear term u negates the second entry. Thus the unsigned update sends r=B/A to f(r)=(1−r)/(2(1+r)). Four successive updates followed by optionally negating the last new port give g₊=f⁴ and g₋=−f⁴. On I=[−1/2,1/2] these maps are injective with disjoint images contained in I. Indeed the successive endpoint images are obtained by monotonicity on each pole-free intermediate interval: starting with I the first image is [1/6,3/2], the second [−1/10,5/14], and the final image after four steps is [7/58,29/94]. All intermediate denominators are positive. Starting from 1/2, words of the same length j give 2^j distinct physical ratios by disjointness of the last images and injectivity backwards. With j=ceil(log₂(b+1)) choose b+1 of them. Each chain has 1+4j triples, its integer weights have O(log(b+2)) bits, and its first weight is nonzero. Connect the external old spare bit as the final front port. The degree bound of four on quadratic edges from the port preparation is preserved, since sign changes are linear terms.

**Queries.** For 1≤b≤max(1,k) partition the spares into d=ceil(k/b) groups of sizes bₕ≤b. Define the polynomial

    P(X₁,…,X_d)=Σ_x (−1)^{G(x)} ∏ₕ Xₕ^{wt(x on group h)}.

In each group independently choose one node j with physical pair (Aⱼ,Bⱼ), and install a copy on every spare in that group. The fully matched oracle returns exactly P evaluated at Xₕ=Bⱼ/Aⱼ, multiplied by ∏ₕ Aⱼ^{bₕ}. All (b+1)^d queries are nonadaptive; they contain O(N log(b+2)) variables and gates each. If k=0 one query suffices unchanged. Evaluate each univariate Lagrange basis of the b+1 nodes at 1; denote the resulting weights by lⱼ. The required P(1,…,1) is the sum of the raw oracle values multiplied by ∏ₕ lⱼ/Aⱼ^{bₕ} for the respective group choices. The same interpolation formula is valid on a short final group.

For exact bit time, each lⱼ is a product of b rational factors of O(log(b+2)) bits apiece. A common integer denominator D₀ is the product of all denominators of lⱼ and all |Aⱼ|^b. It has O((b+1)²log(b+2)) bits, and D₀^d is a common denominator of every summand. Scaled numerators, the running sum, and all oracle values have polynomially many bits in N, since log₂(number of queries)=d log₂(b+1) is polynomial too. Thus ordinary integer arithmetic adds polynomial overhead per query, without a growing product of query-dependent denominators.

If fully matched gaps on Q variables and O(Q) gates take poly(Q)2^{O(Q/√log(Q+2))} deterministic time, choose b=min(k,ceil(√log₂(N+2))) for k>0. The total cost is

    2^{O(N loglog(N+4)/√log(N+2))}.

When k is smaller than the proposed block size there is just one group. This is a class-restricted solver transfer using a deterministic nonadaptive grid. The direct fixed-density October count applied to a Tseitin encoding can already achieve the better exponent O(N/√log N) in the original circuit-size parameter. [Direct clock](october-next-gate-scale.md)

**A separate tractable signed model.** Suppose all between-triple interactions of a matched phase are quadratic and use one designated bit per triple; allow arbitrary lower-order terms within each triple. Sum its other two bits at each fixed designated value to obtain a signed integer unary pair. A zero pins the opposite value (two zeros give zero total); propagate pins into the remaining quadratic Q and absorb linear signs into unary weights. On the remaining coordinates let ρ be the GF(2) rank of Q's alternating polar matrix. The exact total takes poly(N)2^ρ bit time, regardless of graph treewidth. To see this, binary symplectic elimination expresses Q as a sum of ρ/2 products of linear forms plus an affine function. Fourier-expand each two-bit sign (−1)^{uv} into four affine signs with coefficients ±1/2. For each resulting global affine sign, the weighted sum factors into one two-entry sum per designated bit. The common denominator is 2^{ρ/2}, and all numerators have polynomial bit length. This yields polynomial time at ρ=O(log N), including dense interactions (a complete bipartite quadratic has ρ=2). It does not bound ρ for general matched phases or when several ports of a triple have external neighbors.


**A precise unrestricted-circuit implication under a stronger counting hypothesis.** Suppose a single uniform deterministic bit algorithm computes the exact gap on every presented perfect cubic matching plus quadratic maximum degree four in time poly(Q)·2^{C Q/(log Q loglog Q)} for a common constant C, where Q counts variables and the O(Q) gate or monomial descriptions have their usual polynomial encoding. The standard deterministic tape-model simulation cost must be included in this hypothesis. For an arbitrary binary circuit on q inputs and at most B q log q gates, N≤q+B q log q+1. Take b=min(k,ceil(log²(2+N))) when k>0; if k=0 use the single original matched instance. The log of the number of calls is O((N/log²N+1)loglog N)=o(q). Each output has Q≤C₀N loglog(4+N) for an absolute C₀. Since the rate function is eventually increasing and N≥q, its exponent is at most O(C) N/log N = O(C) Bq+o(q), with absolute constants independent of B. For k=0 the same upper bound holds. Choosing one sufficiently small fixed B>0 gives total time at most 2^{q/2} at large q, including polynomial input encoding, generation per call and interpolation. Thus the quoted hypothesis suffices for the next-gate-scale decision hypothesis and its E^NP infinitely-often Ω(t log t) gate consequence. The given October bound on matching counts does not establish this stronger hypothesis. [Gate-scale quantifiers and hierarchy transfer](october-next-gate-scale.md)
