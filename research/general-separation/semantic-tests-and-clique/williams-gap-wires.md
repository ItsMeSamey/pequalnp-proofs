# Witness lower bound for signed counting circuits with two output values

**Prior result.** Bhattacharjee's November 19 preprint already proves the lower bound below in Theorem 7 for integer tensor networks and arbitrary positive integer scales, for every fixed high-arity exponent below one. Its proof uses the same fourth-, third- and second-moment identity and signed clause test. Negating the output converts a negative global scale to a positive one. Corollaries 7–10 include unambiguous and signed gap circuits. The derivation here verifies a special case directly from the October contraction theorem. [Bhattacharjee, Theorems 7–8](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202611.0576)


Let a circuit on t input bits and u guess bits have deterministic acyclic gates of arbitrary truth tables and fan-in at most floor(t^{9/10}). Its output assigns each guess a sign in {−1,0,1}, encoded by two bits. Write z(i) for the sum of these signs over all guesses on input i. At a given length a circuit represents a Boolean function f if there is one nonzero signed integer h with z(i)=h f(i) for every i. The scale h may depend on the circuit. Charge input incidences at gates and the two output designations as wires. Discard unused gates and absorb constant gates into their consumers. Unused guesses can be dropped by dividing z and its nonzero scale by the common factor two, except for an identically zero function, where scale one suffices. Thus u is bounded by the charged size. This representation permits exponentially large counts of both signs and arbitrary cancellation.

**Claim.** Under the pinned October Boolean tensor contraction theorem, a single W in E^NP requires at least c t sqrt(log t) wires in this model for infinitely many input lengths, for an absolute c>0. This extends the model in the October quantitative circuit corollary. The class containing W and the asymptotic growth rate are those of that corollary. [October witness proof](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/92b516ef8e/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/quantitative-circuits.tex) · [Contraction hypotheses and bound](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/92b516ef8e/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/quantitative-reduction.tex#L15-L48)

Use the October proof's unary H in NTIME(2^n) outside NTIME(2^{n/2}), t=dn for a fixed integer d, and q=n+O(log n) bits indexing its local 3-CNF clauses. The linear-size decoder outputs three signed t-bit variable labels. W(i) is the bit indexed by i of the lexicographically first satisfying assignment on yes lengths, and zero on other lengths. Repeated NP prefix-extension queries construct it in E^NP. The same decoder and W work throughout. [Local Cook–Levin reduction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.3171) · [Williams witness-circuit argument](https://doi.org/10.1137/10080703X)

Suppose circuits of size at most w=floor(η n sqrt(log n)) existed at all sufficiently large lengths t=dn, for η>0 to be fixed. On unary input guess the topology, truth tables, and nonzero h with |h|≤2^u. For the zero function one can always take h=1. Scan and reject malformed descriptions. The total description length is at most poly(n) 2^{O(t^{9/10})}.

First enforce the two-value promise even for adversarial guesses. Compute the following integer exactly and require it to vanish:

    Σ_i z(i)^4 − 2h Σ_i z(i)^3 + h² Σ_i z(i)^2 = Σ_i [z(i)(z(i)−h)]².

Each moment is a contraction with one common t-bit input and k independent sets of guesses and internal wires. The equality test over integers implies that z(i) is zero or h on every label. No assumption about z on guessed descriptions enters the test.

For each indexed clause and each of its three literals, use false-literal factor h−z(i) for a positive literal and z(i) for a negative literal. Sum the product of the three factors over all clause indices. After the first test this sum is exactly h³ times the number of violated clauses, including for negative h. Require that sum to vanish. It is one signed contraction: the literal sign is decoded locally and selects z or h−z. Implement the latter by a local selector between h and −z, pinning all internal and guess bits of the unused branch to a unique extension, or equivalently use the following constant expansion. Split by the eight patterns of literal signs; expand each product into at most eight terms and perform a separate contraction per term. A term includes only copies actually used and multiplies their result by its signed power of h outside the contraction. Repeated labels still use independent guesses. If H accepts, the representation of W passes both tests. Otherwise every Boolean assignment violates some decoded clause and no guess passes.

A gate is a 0/1 relation on its input ports and one output. For each emitted bit use a copy chain to distribute it to all consumers; duplication costs linear in total incidences. An unused shared input contributes a free summed bit, by an explicit unconstrained wire or an external power of two. Encode the sign using one local signed output tensor. Subdivide wires with degree-two equality tensors to obtain a bipartite multigraph. The decoder, a constant number of witness copies, ties and branch-free term construction have O(n+w) binary edges. The maximum degree is at most max(t^{9/10}+1,O(1)). Choose the contraction parameter s proportional to n+w, with fixed coefficient. For all sufficiently large n it satisfies the exact degree cap ceil(s^{9/10}), since w/n grows without bound at any fixed η>0. Literal-sign restrictions have constant cost. Vertex count is polynomial in s, and sequential table lookup takes polynomial time and space in total description length L plus s.

For all moment terms k≤4 the sum before scalar multipliers has absolute value at most 2^{t+4u}. All absolute expanded moment terms together are at most 4·2^{t+4u}; the absolute clause terms together are at most 64·2^{q+3u}. Thus P=max(t,q)+4u+10 bits of two-adic precision suffice even for signed recovery of the final expanded integers. Set the truncated polynomial dimension to one. P≤Cs for an absolute fixed C. The contraction theorem's time is (s+L)^{O(1)} 2^{O(s/sqrt(log s))}, with (s+L)^{O(1)} space. The description-length factors are 2^{o(n)}. A multitape machine can simulate indexed bit access by scanning this workspace, adding only another such factor. The coefficient of ηn in the exponent is bounded by a fixed constant independent of η: take the theorem's constants and the linear wiring multiplier first, then choose η sufficiently small. With that choice all tests, guesses and construction take less than 2^{n/2} time for sufficiently large n. Finitely many exceptional lengths can be hardwired. This contradicts the unary hierarchy separation, giving the stated infinitely-often lower bound after t=dn rescaling.

This proof uses the full binary tensor contraction interface, including its succinct entry descriptions; the fixed-width SAT corollary by itself does not supply the high-arity simulations.
