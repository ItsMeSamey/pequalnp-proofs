# Directional derivatives under a binary-gate budget

For every integer t≥3 set q=3t^t. The following explicit cubic uses O(qt) two-input gates and has derivative bias at most 2^(−2^(t−1)) on every affine subspace of dimension at least q−2^(t−1), in every nonzero direction. Bias means the absolute expectation of the sign. Thus the gate count is o(q log q) and the codimension grows faster than every fixed power of log q.

Let P stack the t-dimensional identity over an all-ones row, and C=P tensor-powered t times over the binary field. In three independent q/3-bit inputs define f(x,y,z) as the parity of (Cx)_i(Cy)_i(Cz)_i over all i. Encode dimension by dimension. Along each fiber the old coordinates pass through and one parity of t coordinates is appended. At each stage fewer than (t+1)^t≤e t^t binary XOR gates suffice. The final layer takes two binary ANDs and at most one XOR per coordinate.

At tensor level h, contract the first slot with any nonzero a. The resulting bilinear matrix M_a has rank at least 2^h. At level zero the claim is scalar. Write a in t slices and denote their previous-level contractions by A_i. The new matrix has block form diag(A_i)+J tensor S with S equal to the sum of the A_i. Nonzero slices have nonzero matrices by induction. If S vanishes there are at least two nonzero diagonal blocks. Otherwise choose A_i nonzero, and j,l such that i,j,l are distinct. The block minor on rows j,i and columns l,i has rows (S,S) and (S,S+A_i). Elementary block operations give diag(S,A_i). The nonzero S is itself a previous-level contraction, by linearity. Both cases double the lower bound. It is exact for a pure tensor of coordinate vectors, since each one-coordinate base contraction has rank two.

For any nonzero direction v, some slot has a nonzero shift a. On the other two slots the quadratic polar form of the derivative has principal block matrix with zero diagonal blocks and off-diagonal M_a and its transpose. Its rank is at least 2^(t+1). Restricting a bilinear form to codimension c loses at most 2c rank (restrict rows, then columns). A quadratic sign with polar rank R on an affine space has bias zero or 2^(−R/2), by averaging its squared bias over shifts. Taking c≤2^(t−1) proves the claim. The construction therefore supplies an infinite sequence of full-width examples for the directional quadratic-frontier inequality. It establishes no logarithmic entropy threshold.

## Cost of the Li–Zhong random front end

For constant error the CCC 2024 Theorem 14 proof samples m=q(k+2) independent (k+3)-by-q matrices with k=log₂q+log₂log₂q+O(1), then samples independent Boolean truth tables on k+3 input bits and XORs the m table outputs. The published size bound is O(q²log³q) for XOR–AND–XOR and O(q⁵log³q) for De Morgan formulas. The appendix explicitly gives the random matrix and table construction. [Li–Zhong, CCC 2024](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2024.10) [Appendix A, Lemmas 72–73 and Theorem 74](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2023/058/revision/2/download/)

Here is a direct binary-gate compilation improving the upper bound for unrestricted depth, with nonuniform constants. Let R=m(k+3). Split q inputs into blocks of floor(log₂q) bits. Precompute all parities in each block with one XOR per additional parity. Each requested row needs O(q/log q) further gates, giving O(q²log q) for all rows. Each d=k+3 bit table admits O(2^d/d) binary gates. To see this split its inputs into u=floor(log₂d)−1 low variables and d−u high variables. Compute every high assignment indicator with O(2^(d−u)) gates via a shared prefix tree. All possible low functions together cost O(2^u 2^(2^u)) gates by naive table lookup per function. Group high indicators by their low function, OR within groups, AND with the corresponding low function, and OR the results. This fits O(2^d/d). The m separate tables thus cost O(q²log q) altogether.

Conversely the sampled front end contains qR independent random bits. A binary-gate circuit with s gates and R named output wires has at most (s+1)[16(q+s+2)^2]^s(q+s+2)^R descriptions. Distinct matrices give distinct linear maps, so for a sufficiently small fixed c the probability that the full named front end has a circuit of size ≤c q²log q is 2^(−Ω(qR)). The injector succeeds with probability bounded below by a positive constant by Lemma 72, hence conditioning on success preserves the high-probability lower bound. This argument covers implementations exposing the sampled rows. It supplies no corresponding lower bound on the composed one-bit function, on structured matrices, or on other directional dispersers.

A separate padding consequence follows from the compilation. At width q use only m=floor(η√q) variables for the Li–Zhong scalar, choosing constant η>0 sufficiently small for a given Bqlog q budget. Against polynomially many point errors, some setting of the q−m unused inputs leaves a clean restriction. On that slice the original logarithmic-entropy obstruction applies, yielding Ω(√q) quadratic features under the directional quadratic-frontier lemma. The padded function has constant derivatives along its unused coordinates. The full-width cubic above gives a weaker width bound with a guarantee on every direction.
