# Directed Hamiltonicity with optional two- and three-cycles

Let σ be a permutation on n≥1 labels with a list of pairwise disjoint permutation cycles of lengths two or three. Whether multiplying σ by the product of a sublist gives one cycle, and a choice achieving this when possible, can be found deterministically in polynomial time.

Use n directed tracks. An optional transposition is a crossbar, a vertex with two incoming and two outgoing arcs admitting a straight and a swapped pairing. Implement a three-cycle by two successive transpositions, in the order giving the prescribed cyclic orientation. Its two crossbars must have equal toggle bits. Feed the ends back to the starts according to σ. One pass realizes the permutation product. The full products σC and Cσ for the combined selected switch permutation C are conjugate. The order of the two elementary swaps inside a three-cycle is fixed to implement its prescribed orientation. The directed circuit partition has precisely as many circuits as the product has cycles.

Suppress directed wire segments, retaining any switchless closed component. Such a component and any other component preclude a single circuit; a sole such component succeeds. More generally, a disconnected network cannot have a single circuit. With at least one switch and a connected network, Hierholzer's algorithm gives an Euler circuit when the equality constraints are set aside. Let B be its physical set of swapped switches.

Read the cyclic double-occurrence word of visited crossbars. Over the binary field let A be the zero-diagonal symmetric matrix with a one exactly when the occurrences of the two switches alternate. The directed Cohn–Lempel equality states that changing the Euler transition at switches S gives exactly 1+nullity(A[S]) circuits. Hence the physical toggle sets giving one circuit are the symmetric differences B△S with A[S] nonsingular, including S empty. These sets form the twisted even linear delta-matroid D(A)*B. [Circuit-nullity equality](https://arxiv.org/abs/0903.4405)

Take two disjoint copies of D(A)*B, represented by the binary alternating matrix diag(A,A) twisted by B in both copies. Partition their entire ground set into pairs. Within each copy, pair the two serial crossbars belonging to each three-cycle. Across the two copies, pair each free transposition switch with its twin. If a legal one-circuit switch set exists, use the identical feasible set in both copies: their union is a union of these pairs. Conversely any feasible union of pairs restricts in its first copy to a one-circuit switch set satisfying every required three-cycle equality. The second copy adds no restriction on which free transpositions may be chosen in the existence test. This handles zero singles, zero triples, and either parity of the Euler baseline without separate cases.

The deterministic augmenting-path algorithm of Geelen, Iwata and Murota minimizes the number of split pairs over feasible sets. Zero is the desired condition and the minimizing set supplies a witness. Section 5.2 gives O(t⁴) field operations on t elements, here over the binary field with t=O(n). The graph and its matrix can be constructed in polynomial time. [Linear delta-matroid parity, including Eulerian delta-matroids in §4.3](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~jfgeelen/Publications/deltamatroidparity.pdf)

**Application to maximum outdegree two.** Form the bipartite successor graph with one left and one right copy per vertex. Find a perfect matching or reject. Keep only edges appearing in some perfect matching, for example by forcing each edge and checking the remainder. A degree-one vertex then belongs to an isolated forced matched pair, since no other edge at its partner can occur in a matching. Remove those pairs from the choice graph while retaining their successor assignments. Each residual component has equally many left and right vertices, minimum degree two and left degrees at most two. Thus all degrees equal two. Switching a component between its two possible matchings multiplies the reference permutation by a cycle on the corresponding left labels, of length half the component length. The cycle supports are disjoint. The solver applies when each residual component has length four or six. With h longer components, enumerating their choices first adds a factor of 2^h.


**Costs.** If the reference permutation has s two-switches and g three-switches, there are s+2g real crossbars and t=2(s+2g) parity elements. The initial bipartite graph has O(n) edges; matching and allowed-edge tests are deterministic polynomial preprocessing, independent of the branch choices. For each of the 2^h choices on longer components absorb their cycles into σ and apply the solver to the remaining disjoint supports. Direct pairwise comparison of Euler-word positions builds the matrix in O(t²) time. The resulting bound is O(poly(n)+2^h(poly(n)+t⁴)), including witness construction. Every longer component uses at least four disjoint left labels, so h≤⌊n/4⌋ and the decision algorithm takes O*(2^(n/4)) time and polynomial space. The matching preprocessor assumes a simple directed input, or that repeated parallel copies have been collapsed when only existence matters.

**Controls.** For free transpositions alone, contract the cycles of σ and draw an undirected edge between the two cycles touched by each optional switch. Connectivity is necessary. It also suffices: choose switches of a spanning tree of the contracted graph and apply them in a forest order, merging two current cycles each time. For triples, connectivity alone fails. With σ=(12)(3) and the optional switch (123), the two possible products both have two cycles; the base and the triple label together connect the support. This example also exhibits the sign obstruction: a three-cycle preserves parity while a single cycle on three vertices is even.

Connectivity plus the necessary sign still fails. On six labels take σ=(1)(234)(56) and the disjoint optional switches (123),(456), composing right-to-left. Their supports link the three base cycles into a connected graph; σ and every switched product have the odd sign required by a six-cycle. With neither switch the cycles are those of σ. With only the first they are (13)(24)(56), with only the second they are (1)(2346)(5), and with both they are (13)(246)(5). All four choices have three cycles.
