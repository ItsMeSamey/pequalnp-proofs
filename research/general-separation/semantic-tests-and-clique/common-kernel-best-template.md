# Searching relative to the best single loop completion

Let R be the span of all raw private vectors for the three named pins, namely the w with w_P=0 for which (A+diag d)w=0 at some exterior choice. For any template b on the exterior define U_b to be the entire kernel with those loops and w_P=0. Pin diagonal values can be fixed to zero. Put

    k* = dim R − max_b dim U_b.

There is a deterministic original-witness algorithm using 2^{O(k*)} n^{k*+q+O(1)} time and polynomial space, where q=16 is the field size of the [common cap](even-odd-extension-cap.md). Neither R, the optimum template, nor a bound on k* is supplied. This is an XP bound in the residual dimension. In particular the case k*=0 takes polynomial time even when dim R is linear.

## Fitting a template on a known subspace

Maintain a matrix W whose independent binary columns are genuine private vectors, or linear combinations of such vectors. Testing if some b gives rank((A+diag b)W)≤k is fixed-parameter tractable in k. Each exterior row gives two vectors (AW)_i and (AW)_i+W_i; pin rows are zero. Start with a trial row space L=0. If every site has some option in L, select such an option in each row to construct b. Otherwise find a site with both options outside L and branch on adjoining either. Each branch raises rank by one. Discard branches exceeding k. Every feasible choice of rows yields a successful branch, by always choosing its option at the current site. The search uses 2^{O(k)} polynomial-time linear algebra operations and depth at most k+1.

Fix a successful b and add a basis of U_b to the known span, discarding dependent columns. These genuine private vectors do not increase its residual rank. Write W again for the augmented span, H=A+diag b, and D=H W for the image subspace with dimension at most k. Every vector in W vanishes at the pins and has zero pin output. The set of actual exteriors vanishing in both channels is characterized by the raw private kernel as in the [parameter-r proof](common-radical-fpt.md).

## Monotone search after fixing residual syndromes

Write N for exterior coordinates at which the raw candidate d differs from b. A nonzero w∈W is a known common-kernel witness at N exactly when z=Hw is supported on N and w restricted to N equals z restricted to N. This is the binary identity Hw=diag(1_N)w. At fixed N the presence of such a w is directly decidable by linear algebra.

Enumerate every set O of at most dim D exterior coordinates, constrained to be outside N. Compute Z={z∈D : z|_O=0}, and force every coordinate in the union of supports of Z to lie in N. These forced sets are disjoint. On this face, the vectors in D supported on N are exactly Z: every element of Z is included and each z outside Z has a nonzero coordinate in O. Every possible N belongs to at least one such face. Indeed the coordinate functionals on D at indices outside N have a basis at some O of size at most dim D; the resulting Z comprises exactly the syndromes supported on this N.

Within a face the condition that no nonzero w in W is a private witness is upward closed under enlarging N. The eligible z=Hw are fixed in Z, and each added site imposes another equality on w. Begin at the maximal point N₀=F minus O. If a known witness exists there, skip the entire face. Otherwise test both cap channels at N₀. If either is missing, return that exterior. When both are feasible there, search every legal reduction from N₀ by at most q sites, testing the two channels and checking that no known kernel survives. This suffices: if a channel is missing at some N where no known witness survives, pivot its symmetric matrix at the feasible base for N₀, keeping mandatory border sites in the base. The change set N₀ minus N contains a singular principal subset of size at most q by the [short singular obstruction theorem](minimal-singular-principal-search.md). Removing just those coordinates preserves the forced conditions and remains in the upward closure of N. Thus an examined point is missing and has no known witness. The bit translations from raw diagonals to subsets merely change which labels enter the pivot base.

If no face returns a point and the known vectors are genuine, the two channels agree everywhere. Any distinguishing exterior would be missing from one channel and lack a private kernel, so the search would have returned a candidate. A returned candidate missing on just one side yields the original witness by pin replay. A candidate missing on both yields a genuine private vector outside W by solving the raw linear system.

## Learning and the uniform clock

Increase k starting at zero. Keep the learned subspace when changing budgets. At each round fit a template at the current budget, add its private kernel, and run the face search. If fitting fails, advance k. If a new private vector is returned, adjoin it and repeat. A failed fit is impossible at k≥k*: restriction to the learned subspace of an optimum H has rank at most dim R−dim U_b for that optimum. Every nonterminal face search at a successful fit adds a linearly independent vector, so at most n of these occur across all budgets. The enumeration of n^{dim D+O(1)} faces and at most n^{q+O(1)} neighbors on each uses polynomial space. Matrix and field operations are polynomial for fixed q. This proves the displayed bound.

As an example, blow up each of the four exterior sites of either [seven-site fixture](physical-cap-field-obstructions.md) into t independent vertices with its original external neighbors replaced by complete joins. Keep the three original pins. Original feasibility is invariant under replacing selected counts in each twin class by their parity. Pin cut rank remains three, and for t≥2 vertex connectivity is at least t: both free graphs are connected, every pin touches the free graph, and fewer than t deletions leave a representative of each exterior class. Each within-class even vector lies in U₀. Their direct sum has dimension 4(t−1). The class-parity projection of every private vector satisfies the original three homogeneous pin equations, whose kernel on the four free coordinates has dimension one. Hence dim R≤4(t−1)+1 and k*≤1 for both answers, while r=dim R grows linearly. This comparison describes applicability of the algorithm; these cloned examples also admit direct compression.
