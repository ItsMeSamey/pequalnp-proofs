# Recovering cubic blocks and counting through their signed cut matrices

Let f be an explicit squarefree Boolean polynomial of degree at most three on q input bits, or derive it at polynomial bit cost from a supplied circuit. For example, propagating exact gate polynomials and declining any intermediate gate above degree three is sound and polynomial, since each binary product tested has degree at most six. A circuit with larger intermediate degrees needs a different certificate. Write Z=∑_x (−1)^{f(x)}. The count of accepted inputs is (2^q−Z)/2.

## Recognizing the cubic blocks

The homogeneous cubic coefficients define an alternating trilinear tensor T, equivalently the third finite difference. It transforms by pullback under affine coordinate changes. Compute its radical R and a linear complement by elimination. On this complement recognize direct sums of distinct three-dimensional volume forms, including the empty direct sum. The center method for multilinear forms is classical; the following proof works in characteristic two. [Huang, Lu, Ye and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07915)

Solve T(Au,v,w)=T(u,Av,w)=T(u,v,Aw) on coordinate basis triples for the matrix A. In a volume direct sum every solution is block diagonal: one argument in one block and two in another detect every off-block image by nondegeneracy. Within a volume block T(Au,u,w)=T(u,u,Aw)=0 forces Au∈span(u). Linearity makes the map scalar. Conversely independent block scalars are solutions. Split the space successively by the 0 and 1 eigenspaces of a basis of solutions, requiring the two intersections with each current subspace to sum to that subspace. In the target class the basis spans all block scalars and separates all distinct blocks. Verify that every final block has dimension three and nonzero volume, and all mixed entries vanish. Otherwise decline. Choose block bases, substitute in f and subtract one triple product per block. The remainder is an explicit quadratic. Dense matrix and coefficient work is polynomial in the supplied description, without a nearlinear gate-size assumption.

Let d=dim R, y denote the triple coordinates, and z the radical coordinates. Symplectic elimination on the radical-only quadratic polar gives ℓ disjoint pair products, leaving d−2ℓ kernel variables. Linear z coefficients are affine in y. For a pair u,v with drift Au+Bv the sign sum is 2(−1)^{AB}; a kernel variable with drift L sums to 2·1[L=0]. Consequently

Z=2^{d−ℓ} ∑_y 1[E y=t] (−1)^{∑_i y_{i1}y_{i2}y_{i3}+Q(y)}.

Here Q is the corrected quadratic and E,t form an explicit affine system. Inconsistency gives Z=0. This integration permits unrestricted quadratic coupling to the radical.

## The cut matrix and the dynamic program

Fix any supplied block order, or use the recovered order and charge its measured width. Across a prefix P and suffix S write uᵀJv for the cross part of Q. Set c=rank E_P+rank E_S−rank E and r=rank(J restricted to ker E_P × ker E_S), computed over F₂. If E y=t is consistent, the REAL flattening rank of the corrected signed tensor is exactly 2^{c+r}. Local phases multiply rows and columns by signs. Feasible past syndromes h lie in im E_P ∩ (t+im E_S), a nonempty affine space of dimension c. Each h gives one Cartesian block of past and future assignments, each a translate of its respective kernel. Translating to the kernels contributes only row and column signs and a constant. The character matrix on these kernels has 2^r distinct rows and they are pairwise orthogonal. The syndrome blocks have disjoint row and column supports, so ranks add. This recovers in explicit coordinates a known stabilizer-state rank phenomenon for the signed quadratic part; block-local cubic signs preserve each cut rank. [Dehaene and De Moor, Theorem 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0304125) and [Fattal et al., Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0406168)

For every feasible h choose an affine-linear right-inverse solution v_h to E_S v_h=t+h. Index states by (h,σ), where σ is the linear functional uᵀJ restricted to ker E_S. For fixed h its possibilities form an affine space of dimension r. Every feasible past u in a state has the same signed interaction profile with the future after normalization by (−1)^{uᵀJv_h}. Accumulate the past-only phase times this normalization in one signed integer per state. Keep the global constant consistently in the initial past weight.

To extend by a new whole block b, try its eight possible values. Use a representative u found by linear equations for (h,σ). At the new cut form h′=E_Pu+E_b b, discard if it lacks a completion, and calculate the new functional of (u,b) on ker E_{S′}. It is independent of the chosen u: two representatives differ by a vector invisible to the old kernel, which includes every (0,v) with v in ker E_{S′}. Multiply the old weight by the new block's local sign, its interaction with u, and the ratio of the new and old normalization signs. Including the cross term to b and the two normalizations, the exponent is

uᵀJ_{P,b} b + (u,b)ᵀJ_{P′,S′} v′_{h′} + uᵀJ_{P,S} v_h.

For two representatives, the difference of this expression pairs their difference with (b,v′_{h′})+v_h in the old kernel. Thus the multiplier applies to the aggregated weight. New signed weights add. At the end the only feasible syndrome is t, the suffix is empty and normalization equals one. Starting at the empty prefix gives the signed sum. Each unnormalized or normalized partial aggregate uses O(q+1) bits. At most 2^{c+r} states at each cut occur, and the exact bit time and space are poly(q,description)·2^{max(c+r)} including handling of zero weights. This bounded-width result concerns a supplied or recovered order. Finding an optimal order is unnecessary for the stated clock.

To find a satisfying original input, maintain the complete computation in the recovered y,z coordinates. Pin consecutive y blocks and use the same signed counter. A pin within its block is a local zero-one weight; the affine support used for the state description can be kept unchanged. In the raw-count formula replace 2^q by the number of unconstrained complete y,z assignments after those pins. The block-pinned sign sums still include the radical variables through their same integration. Once y is fixed, pin z coordinates by direct quadratic Gaussian counts on the remaining z. The invertible original linear change gives the raw witness. Also available for this class: pin one coordinate per cubic block and sum a quadratic on each of the 2^m cells for m blocks, with polynomial space. Dovetail the methods after the common preparation.

## What the order can cost

On m disjoint cubic monomials every affine flat making the phase quadratic has codimension at least m, even after adding any quadratic. Thus every complete adaptive partition into single-quadratic leaves has at least 2^m leaves. Fixing one coordinate in every triple attains equality. The degree-drop theorem already establishes this statement about the monomials. With E absent and Q block-local the block contraction above is polynomial, including after a certified invertible change of coordinates. [Carlet, Feukoua and Sălăgean, Theorem 9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-025-01702-z)

The companion cubic-block-expander-width.md puts an ordinary quadratic interaction of bounded degree on one coordinate per cubic block and makes the transfer width exponential in every order, even though an alternative local sum computes the answer in polynomial time. The rank calculation describes the exact size of a linear representation of the entries across a cut, not the scalar signed sum's unrestricted complexity. The bounded checker cubic_phase_cut_check.py compares the binary formula with rational matrix ranks on all right-hand sides of 250 seeded systems and seven structural controls. It does not implement the centroid recognizer or full counter.
