# The unrestricted row interior with two neutral columns

Every original instance with a named universal vertex z and two other named sites s,t reduces parsimoniously to another such instance with two new named sites p,q. In the reduced instance p and q have disjoint neighborhoods in G'−z, with a complete join between them, one neighborhood is a singleton, and precisely two unnamed neutral vertices remain, forming an independent set. The first neighborhood may induce an arbitrary graph. This complements the sharp seven-site test when both row neighborhoods are homogeneous. It identifies the complexity of allowing an arbitrary first neighborhood, even with the other displayed parts fixed in size.

Delete z from the input to obtain K. Retain as A the graph induced by V(K)−{t}, including the original vertex s. Outside A use new p,q,b,d,e. In K' join p to every vertex of A and q only to b. Join b completely to A and additionally to e. Join d only to s. The remaining neighbors of e are the vertices of N_K(t) in A, with membership of s toggled. Include no other edges. Reattach the named universal z to everyone; the three new names are z,p,q. The named vertices in K' have distance three by the path p–s–b–q. The transformation increases the order by four, uses only O(|V(K)|) additional edge or incidence operations with the old induced graph retained, and is uniform in the original adjacency representation.

After removing a named universal vertex the remaining support must induce all odd degrees, which automatically enforces even cardinality by handshaking. Write X for its selected sites in A. In any solution on K', q forces b and p forces |X| odd. The two contributions at b from q and X cancel, so b forces e. The selected support so far has size |X|+4, odd, forcing d by handshaking. Vertex d forces the old s. At any selected site of A, p and b are two extra neighbors and do not alter degree parity. The selected e supplies exactly the original incidence of t, except for the toggled incidence at s, where d cancels the change. At e, the selected b and the toggled s cancel, so its degree parity equals the old degree of t in X∪{t}. Thus X∪{t} induces all odd degrees in K with both original names.

Conversely an original all-odd support X∪{t} contains s and has even cardinality, so |X| is odd. Take X∪{p,q,b,d,e}. The same degree equations give all odd degrees on the retained sites and on the new ones. With z restored these operations are inverse on individual original-label witnesses, regardless of disconnected components or the original adjacency of s,t. Both the general universal-pin branch and this two-column subclass belong to NP and are polynomially interreducible; their classification as polynomial-time solvable or NP-complete is not established here. The reduction makes no claim about original instances without a named universal vertex.
