# What collapse would do to one canonical hierarchy witness

Let H, the indexed clauses, and W be those of the October proof (quantitative-circuits.tex). H is unary in NTIME(2^n) outside NTIME(2^(n/2)); each clause has a polynomial-time decoder on n+O(log n) index bits and three dn-bit variable labels. W at length dn is the lexicographically first satisfying assignment at YES lengths and zero at NO lengths. The source puts W in E with an NP oracle by expanding the clause list and asking prefix-extension questions of exponential length.

The following disjunction is unconditional: NP differs from coNP, or there is a δ>0 such that at infinitely many YES lengths n every satisfying assignment has circuit size greater than 2^(δ n). In particular the same single W meets that lower bound infinitely often in the second branch. Given a circuit C, existence of a violated clause is in NP in the total input size of (1^n,C). Guess its index and evaluate the circuit on its three labels. If NP=coNP, checking the complement uses a single nondeterministic polynomial clock, with exponent c independent of n and circuit size. Guess a circuit with at most 2^(δ n) gates, using O(2^(δ n)(n+1)) encoding bits; check its clause condition. For sufficiently small fixed δ, the guess and check together fit 2^(n/2) time. On NO lengths no guess can succeed. If all sufficiently large YES lengths had a small satisfying assignment this would decide H under the excluded time bound. The hypothesis NP=coNP also puts W in NE∩coNE by guessing the correct answers and certificates (of both polarities) for the exponentially many exponential-size prefix queries, within total time 2^O(n).

If P=NP, W belongs to E and has exponential circuit complexity infinitely often, which in that case also means W is outside NP. An independent counting check shows why an unrestricted easy-witness assumption is incompatible with this branch. For N=2^n, some truth table has circuit complexity greater than 2^(n/2) by counting gate descriptions. The question whether a prefix of a truth table extends to such a hard table lies in the second level of PH when the input supplies unary N: guess all remaining entries and universally quantify the circuit descriptions; direct evaluation on N positions costs polynomial in N. Under P=NP one can find the lexicographically first hard table by N polynomial-time prefix questions, giving one E language with exponential circuit complexity at all sufficiently large lengths. The acceptance verifier on 1^n that computes this table and accepts it as its sole witness has exponential time and a trivial underlying unary language. Williams's easy-witness theorem assumes NEXP is contained in P/poly.

Padding the first W construction to length M approximately 2^(ε n) for fixed ε converts its lower bound to only M^(δ/ε+o(1)). The deterministic upper exponent for the padded language also scales with 1/ε. Changing ε with the desired exponent changes the language. Taking ε tending to zero inside one language removes the fixed simulation clock. No conclusion about one fixed NP language being hard for all polynomials follows.
