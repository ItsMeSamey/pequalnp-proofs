# Independent audit of the conditional single-language criterion

Let mu=NK(N)/R and let T=mu/(A log(SN+2)). In one bottom atom, at most NK(N)/T raw positions are heavy. Independently choosing one of R slots in each block therefore selects at most A log(SN+2) heavy positions in expectation. A fixed Chernoff multiple of this bound fails with probability at most (SN+2)^(-3) for sufficiently large A. The indicators are independent across blocks; no independence across atoms is required. The light mass is a sum of independent [0,T]-valued variables whose total mean is at most mu and total variance at most T mu. With fixed eta>0, Bernstein gives failure probability at most exp(-c_eta mu/T) for the threshold (1+eta/2)mu. Union bound over all S atoms gives a single embedding for each ambient length. If S=0 the represented function is zero directly.

For atom j split **only** its own H_j selected heavy coordinates into at most 2^H_j disjoint guard assignments. Under each, constant folding or fixed-query endpoint closure produces the appropriate residual on the light labels. Guard cost is O(H_j+1) leaves or query nodes, including a possible fresh dummy start for multiple BP endpoints. A fixed input satisfies exactly one guard for each original atom. Hence its coefficients are copied without averaging or renormalization, and total sparsity is at most S(SN+2)^A_eta. Because N=nR+o(nR), the resulting bottom cap is (1+eta)nK(N)+O_eta(log(SN+2)). This is nonuniform deterministic representation existence; an algorithm for finding the slots is unnecessary.

Choose a single universal language U on a fixed self-delimiting machine description plus n,R and nR parity bits. Reject invalid headers and dimensions; sizes can be checked against the physical input before allocating arrays. Take n parities and nondeterministically simulate at most N steps of the described machine. A fixed interpreter for a fixed TM encoding uses poly(N) total time with an exponent independent of the machine, even though direct acceptance of the hard language originally had a machine-dependent clock. For any j choose one machine for its language but do not put a list of those machines into U. Choose integer p_j>=max(1,ceil(t_j)+1) with p_j=O(t_j+1); this also absorbs any fixed multiplicative running-time constant for all sufficiently large n. With R=n^(p_j-1), hardwire the machine and n,R and obtain N=n^p_j+O_j(log n). Thus the N-step cutoff covers all computations of that machine on sufficiently large input lengths.

Assume U eventually has sparsity N^d at every length with the stated smaller density exponent a. For fixed j, the depadded sum has at most n^{(d+(d+1)A_eta)p_j+o(1)} terms and bottom cap (1+eta)n(loglog N)^a+O_{j,d}(log n) up to rounding. Since p_j is fixed, loglog N=loglog n+O_j(1). Thus a<a' permits the stronger budget n ceil((loglog(n+16))^a') for all sufficiently large n. For every fixed d, unbounded s_j/(t_j+1) allows choosing one j with a strict exponent margin before taking n to infinity. Every sufficiently large hard length for that j gives a contradiction. The same U therefore escapes every fixed d infinitely often, with length sets allowed to depend on d. Choosing j uses no effective enumeration; its description is hardwired in the mathematical specialization.

[Murray–Williams, Theorem 1.1](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/easy-witness-nqp.pdf) assumes a same-epsilon nondeterministic gap algorithm and gives ordinary n^k lower bounds in NTIME[n^(c k^4/epsilon)]. This is an upper allowance on the time of some hard language; it supplies no sublinear-in-k verifier bound or unbounded hardness-to-clock ratio. The previous restricted SUM family offers an arbitrary exponent but no quantitative verifier control of this kind. A purely ordinary-circuit improvement giving unbounded k/(t+1) would by simple ordinary padding itself imply NP is not contained in P/poly. A ratio improvement for restricted exact SUM is a weaker sufficient target. The ratio condition is sufficient for this specific packing method, not established as universally necessary.
