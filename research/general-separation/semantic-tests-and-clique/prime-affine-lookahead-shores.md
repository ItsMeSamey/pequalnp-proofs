# A modular-prime NO instance with linear affine lookahead

Take two sets L,R of m vertices each, m even. Give exactly half of each shore the label 1 and the others label 0, with label vectors a,b respectively. Choose all edges within each shore by independent fair bits. Join i in L to every vertex of R precisely when a_i=1. Add three compulsory vertices p,q,r. The only pin edge is qr. Join q to L, r to R, and p to the label-one vertices of both shores.

Every choice of the random edges gives a NO instance. The checks at q and r force both selected shore sizes to be odd. Summing the selected L degrees cancels every internal edge twice. Writing x and y for selected shore indicators, the sum modulo two is

    |x| + a·x + (a·x)|y| = 1.

The condition at p is unnecessary for this contradiction. For a YES control one may replace qr by pq and select the three pins together with a single label-one L site. They induce a triangle and an isolated vertex.

With probability tending to one exponentially in m, each graph is prime for ordinary modular decomposition. A module is a set on which each outside vertex has constant adjacency. Each induced shore is itself prime with exponentially high probability. For a proposed proper module of size j≥2 in a random shore, the probability is 2^{−(j−1)(m−j)}. Summing over subsets gives exponential decay: sizes 2 and m−1 have only polynomially many choices, and for 3≤j≤m−2 the exponent is at least 2m−6. Also, no vertex agrees on the other m−1 vertices of its shore with the adjacency pattern of any one outside vertex, except with probability O(m² 2^{−m}). The external patterns are fixed independently of all internal edges.

The intersection of any host module with a shore must be a module of that shore. If each intersection has size at most one, a module with a free vertex and a second member contradicts the row-agreement exclusion. Each pair of pins is distinguished on the shores. If a module contains L, then p and every R vertex distinguish two L vertices. Both q and r then distinguish L from R. If it contains R, p distinguishes two R vertices, and r distinguishes R from p. Every label-one vertex of L distinguishes R from r, forcing at least two L vertices and hence all of L. The proof also works when qr is replaced by pq, since r still distinguishes R from p.

The pin normal space is generated by the two shore indicators and a+b (using disjoint supports). Each nonzero vector has weight at least m. For s≤m/16, a relation on s free guard groups modulo that space selects one of e_i,A_i,e_i+A_i per group. With units only there is no such relation. Otherwise choose a shore containing a selected adjacency row; on each of its ≥m−s uninvolved coordinates the relation exposes an independent fair internal edge parity. Union bounding gives at most

    8 Σ_{1≤s≤m/16} binom(2m,s) 3^s 2^{−m+s} = 2^{−Ω(m)}.

All those guard pairs are consequently jointly independent modulo the pin equations. On the consistent pin slice, every tuple of up to m/16 local pairs is onto; allowing three corners per pair leaves its affine hull unchanged, including the kernel fibers. The [original affine closure argument](linked-modules-and-affine-propagation.md) therefore stalls on both signs.

Each random shore also survives deletion of m/20 sites connected with exponentially high probability by the [elementary cut union bound](affine-matching-backdoor-control.md). A surviving label-one L site connects both shores. Each surviving pin still has a shore neighbor. Thus the examples have linear vertex connectivity.

There remains a visible pin-free rank-two cut: across R the outside adjacency vectors restricted to R are zero, the all-ones vector, or b. This family excludes ordinary module compression followed by bounded-family affine closure; it does not exclude rank-two replacement or prove a decision complexity lower bound.
