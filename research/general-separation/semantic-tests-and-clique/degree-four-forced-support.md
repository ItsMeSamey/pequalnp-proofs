# Three pins can force a large induced even support at maximum degree four

For every n≥8 take vertices 1 through n. Join vertices at distance one or two in the ordinary path order, and add the edge from 2 to n−1. Require vertices 1,3,n−1 to be selected. These pins induce only the edge 1–3. The graph is connected and every degree is even. Vertices 1,n have degree two, and the other n−2 vertices have degree four.

In an even-degree host, whenever an already required vertex has exactly one neighbor whose selection is still unknown and all the others are required, its last neighbor must also be selected. This is the usual zero forcing color-change rule applied to required membership. Vertex 1 forces 2 here. Vertex 2 sees the required vertices 1,3,n−1 and forces 4. From vertex 3 onward in increasing order, the selected prefix forces the next vertex at distance two whenever that vertex is still unknown. The known vertex n−1 may be skipped as a new assignment; vertex n−2 forces n. All vertices are therefore required and the full graph supplies a solution. Classical zero forcing also bounds the nullity of any symmetric matrix with this off-diagonal edge pattern by three, over any field: a kernel vector vanishing on the pins vanishes along the same forcing sequence. [AIM Minimum Rank – Special Graphs Work Group](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2007.10.009)

Consequently the unique witness contains n−2 vertices of degree four. Its 2n−2 edges give cycle-space dimension n−1. Its adjacency rank over GF(2) is at least n−3. This is a sparse obstruction with the same single-edge pin pattern used in the [mixed two-readout reduction](three-pin-even-reductions.md). It rules out imposing a uniform bound on the number of branching vertices of a minimal witness even at maximum degree four. In this family ordinary propagation actually constructs the witness in linear time. In the [dense complement-cycle example](three-pinned-even-support.md), taking even n≥8 leaves every pin with at least two unpinned neighbors initially, so the same local propagation stops immediately even though the full graph is again the unique witness.

The examples also distinguish three related existence questions.

- A four-cycle with the extra chord joining opposite vertices u,v has an ordinary cycle through u and the two vertices other than u,v. Pin those three vertices. The set consisting of the pins induces a two-edge path, and the full graph gives u,v degree three. Neither is an all-even support.
- A chordless cycle with two adjacent vertices among the three required pins forces the full cycle. Every selected vertex is then in a single component. At the other extreme, requiring the three leaves of a claw in a connected host forces three isolated components, since selecting their center gives each leaf degree one.
- The degree-four family above forces a connected graph of unbounded cycle-space dimension, so neither a search for a single induced cycle nor a search for disjoint induced cycles can be complete in general.

Huynh's fixed-group linkage theorem concerns vertex-disjoint paths with prescribed gains in an oriented group-labelled graph. It does not require the subgraph induced by the union of their vertices to equal the union of their edge sets. The four-cycle with a chord already exhibits the missing degree-parity check. The hardness theorem for a single induced cycle through two nonadjacent degree-two vertices in a subcubic graph likewise concerns a stronger connectivity demand than the three-pins problem; the polynomial subcubic pin algorithm uses the option of selecting a separate isolated pin. Existing large induced parity-subgraph results instead bound the size of an admissible support and do not require a designated set of vertices.
