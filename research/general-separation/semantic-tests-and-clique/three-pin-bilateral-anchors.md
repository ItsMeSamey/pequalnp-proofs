# What a doubled graph preserves about three named pins

Let A be a loopless symmetric binary adjacency matrix and P a set of three named vertices. A physical witness is z with z_P=1 and z_i(Az)_i=0 everywhere. It selects an induced subgraph all of whose degrees are even. With columns K=[A|I], the word (x,Ax) is an actual dependency. With zero site tags, the matching chart g covers this dependency precisely when both products x_i(Ag)_i and g_i(Ax)_i vanish at every site i.
Also require x_P=g_P=1. Call these pairs compatible. They are exactly the induced-even supports on the bipartite double cover with both copies of each named pin selected. The diagonal compatible pairs (z,z) are exactly the requested witnesses. The [supplied-word completion theorem](../eth-review/fixed-circuit-completion.md) instead asks for two physical dependencies with a prescribed alternating pairing on jointly matched sites. We have given a correspondence for coverage of one word and have not constructed such a pairing. Its balanced-basis counterexample therefore does not by itself transfer.

## A connected obstruction with three independent pin rows

Start with the pinned path u,c,v. Attach a separate odd cycle of length at least five at each of u and v, identifying only its root with the respective pin. Attach another disjoint odd cycle of length at least five to c by a length-two path from c to one cycle vertex. There are no other edges. The three rows A[P,F] have disjoint nonempty supports and are independent. The whole graph is connected, has minimum degree two, girth at least five, and is nonbipartite. Thus its global algebra of site-preserving endomorphisms consists only of scalars by the [girth argument](erased-three-pin-stabilizer.md). Its third cycle is disjoint from the pin neighborhoods and none of its edges has a common neighbor, so its erased code also fails the [local CSS and field-linearity tests](erased-odd-cycle-obstruction.md).

A physical witness would have to select exactly one cyclic neighbor of u, since it selects c. Starting from that selected neighbor, each selected degree-two vertex must also select the next neighbor around the cycle. This forces the other cyclic neighbor of u and proves infeasibility. In the double cover the cycle rooted at u gives two length-equal paths from its left copy to its right copy because the length is odd. Select just one of these paths. Do the same at v, and include both copies of each vertex of u,c,v without selecting any other vertex. In the induced subgraph every selected vertex has degree two. The unused arc in each lifted cycle contributes no chord, since it has internal vertices. Both named syndromes agree, and the set D of coordinates on which x and g differ induces just the two paths obtained by deleting their roots from the first two cycles. Consequently even a disagreement forest and full pin row rank do not force a diagonal witness.

## Exact search on the disagreement forest

For any supplied compatible pair, let T be the agreed-one coordinates and D the differing coordinates. Fix the agreed zeros to zero and T to one. Put B=A[T,D], d=A[T,T]1 and a=A[D,T]1. An actual rounding on this face is exactly an assignment b on D satisfying Bb=d and, at each i in D with b_i=1, the selected-neighbor parity in D equals a_i. These conditions follow by substituting the fixed coordinates in the original equations.

If G[D] is a forest, write h=rank B. Gaussian elimination either certifies inconsistent linear rows or selects an equivalent independent system B'b=d' of h rows. Its indicator is the integer character average over s in the binary h-cube of (-1) to the power s·(B'b+d'). For fixed s the vertex factors on D are signs depending only on b_i. Root each tree. For each vertex and each choice of its own bit and its parent's bit, combine the child tables by a two-entry convolution collecting the parity of the chosen child bits. When the vertex is selected retain the entry whose parity plus the parent bit equals a_i. Otherwise add both entries. Multiply by the vertex factor. At a root use parent bit zero and sum over its own bit; multiply the tree values. This gives the integer signed sum for s. Multiply by the constant sign from d', sum over s and divide by 2^h. Intermediate integers have O(n+h) bits. This computes the exact original witness count on the face in 2^h poly(n) bit operations and polynomial space. Additional unary restrictions in D merely suppress table entries, so bit-by-bit self-reduction constructs a physical witness at the same exponential scale. In the counterfamily the doubled solution has T=P and h at most three, so this test correctly rejects its face. Failure on an arbitrary restricted face does not in general decide the full input.

## Recovering the diagonal enumerator

Over a field of characteristic two, give both copies of site i the same indeterminate t_i. Let E(t) be the sum over compatible pairs of the monomials with exponents x_i+g_i taken as ordinary integers. Let Z(t) sum the squarefree support monomials of the original physical witnesses, including pin factors. Swapping the two copies cancels every nondiagonal orbit, hence the formal identity E(t)=Z(t)^2. Distinct original supports have distinct monomials. This proves an oriented witness extraction conditional on a polynomial-time evaluation oracle for this weighted doubled count over explicitly represented binary extension fields. The total degree is at most 2n. Independent evaluations over a field of size at least 8n return a nonzero value with probability at least 3/4 for a nonempty family by Schwartz–Zippel. To forbid a vertex set its weight to zero, leaving all pin weights unrestricted. Delete a free vertex whenever the restricted oracle test detects a surviving support. Amplifying each of at most n+1 tests gives any inverse-polynomial failure bound. With all answers correct the remaining minimal allowed set is itself a witness. Replay its original equations, so the search never outputs a false witness. A polynomial deterministic hitting set for these restricted polynomials would give deterministic search by the same proof. Field representations of O(log n) bits can for example be found by enumerating monic degree-O(log n) binary candidates and testing irreducibility. No such efficient general evaluation oracle is provided by the existential chart algorithm. The specialization with all weights one records only parity and can vanish on YES graphs, for example three isolated pins and an isolated optional vertex.
