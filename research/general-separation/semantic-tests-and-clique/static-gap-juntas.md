# A rejection gap on every invalid original assignment

Suppose a static weighted family of tests on original bits x and auxiliary bits z has nonnegative weights of total W>0. Every test has a fixed scope of at most k original coordinates (it may also have auxiliary inputs). On every x with F(x)=1 there is a z with violated weight at most αW. On every x with F(x)=0 all z have violated weight at least βW, with δ=β−α>0. Then every relevant original bit belongs to tests of total weight at least δW, and F depends on at most floor(k/δ) original bits.

Indeed a relevant i has two neighboring original assignments with distinct outputs. Use the successful z on the yes side on both. Only the tests incident to i can change, while the rejection weight increases by at least δW. Summing all incidences of original coordinates counts at most kW. More generally the numerator k can be replaced by the weighted average original scope size. If there are no auxiliary witnesses, compute the heavy set, enumerate its assignments with all other bits fixed to zero and evaluate F by a threshold between α and β. Multiply the resulting accepting count by 2 raised to the number of ignored bits. This takes poly(input)·2^{k/δ} time. With auxiliary witnesses the relevance bound alone gives no procedure for finding a proof. A conjunction of m disjoint width-k clauses attains equality at δ=1/m with perfect completeness.

For an adaptive binary test whose sequence of original and auxiliary bit queries has maximum depth k, the union of its possible original coordinates has size at most 2^k−1. This gives the analogous bound (2^k−1)/δ. It is tight: take one full depth-k binary decision tree with a different variable at every internal node, arranging each bottom test to return its queried bit. Every internal variable is relevant, since its two child subtrees on disjoint variables can take opposite outcomes. Taking m such disjoint trees and requiring them all to accept realizes δ=1/m. If proof bits can select queries without being charged in depth, this union bound does not apply.

There is also a pointwise restriction for genuinely adaptive input queries. On any yes assignment x fix its good proof. Couple the test choices and running paths on x and each no assignment y. With probability at least δ the two outcomes differ, which requires querying at least one coordinate on which x and y differ before their paths diverge. If p_i is the probability of querying i on x, then Σ_{i:x_i≠y_i}p_i≥δ for every such y. If at most k original bits are queried on a run, Σ_i p_i≤k. Thus p_i/δ gives a fractional certificate of weight ≤k/δ for that yes input, and at most k/δ pairwise disjoint sensitive blocks can be present there. This is an elementary application of the randomized-certificate viewpoint. [Aaronson](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0210020)

The assumption imposes a soundness gap at every original rejecting assignment, including assignments one bit from a satisfying one. A usual PCP statement compares yes and no instances of a language; a proximity verifier may only provide the specified rejection probability far from the accepted set. Neither distinction permits this junta bound to be applied to a general PCP reduction. [Ben-Sasson et al.](https://www.tcs.tifr.res.in/~prahladh/papers/BGHSV2/BGHSV2005.pdf)
