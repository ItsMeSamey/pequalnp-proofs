# Deterministic recovery of odd majority in a hidden affine basis

Let q be odd and N=2^q. The clean sign oracle is f(x)=sign(Σ_i σ_i (−1)^{a_i·x}), where a_1,…,a_q form a basis of the binary dual space and σ_i are signs. An observed membership oracle may disagree on any polynomial number of points. For an absolute ε>0 there is a uniform deterministic algorithm recovering a basis and signs for the *entire clean function* in poly(q)2^{(1−ε)q} operations and queries. The recovered presentation need not match the original ordering or omitted parity. Evaluating a size-s input circuit costs an additional poly(q,s) factor. The argument below covers sufficiently large q; bounded odd q can be handled by the full transform when the noise bound permits identifiability. Asymptotic polynomial corruption is understood eventually for each fixed polynomial.

## Spectrum

Use normalized real Fourier coefficients and first take the ordinary majority in its own coordinates. Even levels vanish by odd symmetry. Given a set at odd level l containing a coordinate i, average over the sign of i. Its derivative is one precisely when the remaining q−1 signs balance, and otherwise zero. Thus the coefficient at level l equals α=binom(q−1,(q−1)/2)/2^{q−1} times the (l−1)-fold product moment of a uniformly random balanced sign vector. Its even moments obey M_0=1, M_j=−(j−1)M_{j−2}/(q−j), by multiplying the zero sum of all q−1 signs by a product of j−1 distinct signs and averaging. Also |M_j|=|M_{q−1−j}| since the product of all balanced signs is fixed. Consequently the largest absolute coefficients, all α=Θ(q^{-1/2}), are the q singletons and the all-ones vector for q≥5. The next largest is α/(q−2). For odd 3≤l≤(q+1)/2,

    |coefficient(l)| / α ≤ (2l/q)^{(l−1)/2}.

The magnitudes are nonincreasing up to the middle and symmetric under l↔q+1−l. The bound follows termwise from the recurrence (each denominator is at least q/2). An affine basis change permutes frequencies by an invertible linear map and multiplies coefficients by signs. All references to weights below are in this hidden frequency basis, used for analysis only.

## Restriction and alien coefficients

A d-dimensional kernel W in the frequency domain corresponds to the primal query subspace U=W^⊥. At a fixed translate t+U, Fourier transform the sign table using coordinates on U. The coefficient labeled by a coset v+W is exactly Σ_{w∈v+W} fhat(w)(−1)^{w·t}. Thus a sufficient property of W is that each bin contain at most one top frequency and the absolute sum of all non-top coefficients in any bin be <α/4. This property suffices on every translate, even when the hidden signs are arbitrary.

Choose K=1024, a sufficiently small fixed δ>0, d=floor(δq), and h=ceil(K d²/q). Require that W have no nonzero vector of hidden weight ≤2h or ≥q−2h. Distinct singleton tops differ at weight two, and a singleton and the all-ones top differ at weight q−1. In every coset there is at most one vector of weight ≤h and at most one vector whose complement has weight ≤h: any two in the same group would have difference of weight at most 2h. Apart from tops, their total contribution is ≤2α/(q−2).

For the remaining tail, let l denote the smaller of the level and its reflected level q+1−level. At most two levels have the same l. An information set of d coordinates for W projects a coset bijectively onto the d-cube. Points of weight l restrict to patterns of weight at most l, and points at level q+1−l have complements of weight l−1 and obey the same count bound. For h<l<d/2 each of the two level counts is at most Σ_{j≤l}binom(d,j)≤(ed/l)^l, with e the base of natural logarithms. Its total relative contribution per level is at most sqrt(q)(4d/sqrt(ql))^l, since e sqrt(2)<4. With K=1024, this is at most sqrt(q) 2^{−l}. Summing from h gives o(1) for any fixed δ>0. A vector of reflected level h+1 whose complement has weight h may be counted twice, which only increases the bound.

For central levels l≥d/2, all remaining coset points together number at most 2^d. Choose the largest odd l_0≤d/2. Monotonicity and reflection bound their combined relative contribution by 2^d(2l_0/q)^{(l_0−1)/2}≤2^d δ^{d/4−2}, which tends to zero for sufficiently small fixed δ. This proves the stated α/4 tail bound eventually.

## Uniform family and decoding

Put V=Σ_{j=0}^{2h}binom(q,j). At most 2V nonzero ambient vectors have forbidden weight. Any family of 4V+1 kernels with pairwise intersection {0} therefore has a strict majority of good members, for any hidden basis. If H₂ is binary entropy, log₂ V≤qH₂(2h/q). Choose a single positive δ so small that 2δ<1, 2Kδ²<δ/4, H₂(2Kδ²)<δ/3, and 2δ^{1/4}<1. Such a constant exists because H₂(2Kδ²)/δ tends to zero. Then 4V+1<2^{d/2} eventually.

Identify the first 2d coordinates with two copies of the binary field of order 2^d. Take graphs of multiplication by θ for the first 4V+1 distinct field elements θ, padding the remaining coordinates by zeros. Their kernels intersect trivially in pairs. A degree-d irreducible polynomial can be found in 2^d poly(d) operations by enumerating monic candidates and using polynomial-time irreducibility testing. This preprocessing occurs once. The much larger collection of all possible kernels need not be transformed.

For each W, compute the transform on U and on d further translates. Choose a primal basis of U and d vectors extending it to a full primal basis, using Gaussian elimination. On U select coefficients with absolute value >α/2. On each other translate the sign for a selected bin records the missing parity of its unique top frequency, by comparison with the sign on U. Its bin label already gives its pairings with the basis of U. Thus Gaussian elimination reconstructs the frequency in ambient coordinates, and the original sign on U gives its coefficient sign. A bad kernel may produce anything and can be rejected if it returns the wrong cardinality. On every good kernel, corruption at E_bad input points perturbs each normalized transform coefficient by at most 2E_bad/2^{q−d}. This is <α/8 for every polynomial E_bad eventually. Consequently all good kernels return precisely the same sorted list of signed top frequencies. Return the majority list. Each translate requires O(q2^{q−d}) arithmetic operations on O(q)-bit integers via a fast Walsh transform; normalized comparisons can use exact integer arithmetic. Total cost is poly(q)[2^d+(4V+1)2^{q−d}]≤poly(q)2^{(1−δ/2)q} eventually.

To recover the clean function, any q of the q+1 recovered frequencies form a basis. Their q+1 signed parity values have product (−1)^{(q−1)/2}, using the sign of M_{q−1} and the fact that the added frequency is the sum of the original q. A tie of q+1 signs would instead give product (−1)^{(q+1)/2}. Hence the sum is never zero, and deleting *any* one term preserves its sign. Deleting the original all-ones term gives exactly the hidden majority; every other deletion gives the same function.

## Noise and prior methods

The proof actually handles E_bad<α2^{q−d}/16. In a black-box model any deterministic exact recovery algorithm tolerating E_bad corruptions needs more than E_bad distinct queries in the worst case: run it on one clean function and alter the answers of its negation at the queried points to mimic that transcript. Both functions belong to the promise, and the same transcript cannot recover both. This lower bound concerns membership queries and does not exclude randomized algorithms or inspection of a circuit description.

Randomized recovery of large Fourier coefficients from membership access is classical. The specific deterministic improvement above uses the structured coefficient levels of hidden odd majority and the family of restricted transforms. No general deterministic heavy-Fourier-coefficient recovery theorem is asserted. [Kushilevitz and Mansour, Learning Decision Trees Using the Fourier Spectrum](https://doi.org/10.1137/0222080)
