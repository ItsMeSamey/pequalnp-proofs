# One readout stays easy at fourteen states; two can require long witnesses

Use the [two-port network model](fourteen-port-uniform-boundary.md), with loopless overlap graph H having no isolated exposed site. Every edge admits at least fourteen of its sixteen raw tuples. Its two defect bits enter the occupied endpoints' XOR equations with arbitrary given affine drifts. Readouts use exposed occupancies.

With one affine readout, every linearly consistent system has a witness of exposed weight at most two. For target zero select no sites; each edge admits at least two defect pairs at occupancy 00 and there are no occupied site equations. For target one choose any v of readout coefficient one. Try the singleton v, using the fixed-occupancy extension algorithm. If it fails, every incident box must force its defect at v when v alone is selected. Otherwise an edge allowing both values at v supplies the required XOR correction; all its other ends are unoccupied. For a box to force that bit both omitted tuples must have precisely this same occupancy pattern, that bit fixed to its missing value, and both values of the unoccupied end bit. Hence at any other occupancy pattern this box admits all four defect pairs.

Take a neighbor w of v. With just w selected, the edge vw has all four defect pairs and lets w correct its parity. If w also has readout coefficient one this singleton suffices. If w has coefficient zero select both v and w. The same edge now gives independent corrections at both occupied sites. All other edges have at least one allowed pair for their specified occupancy and none can stop the independent correction. This proves the claim with original witness reconstruction. Two are sometimes necessary: in the specified fourteen-state box constrain a=1 by the readout and require additional external drift zero at a. The selection a=1,b=0 fails, while a=b=1 allows correction.

With two affine readouts the exposed weight of a smallest witness is already unbounded, even for the single six-site box type of the previous result. In the star simulation use original mixed graph the m-vertex path for m≥2, prescribe odd degree at its two endpoints and even degree at every interior vertex. Require the hub to be zero and require the first exposed path vertex to be one. With hub zero the proved projection is exact. The first selected endpoint forces its only neighbor. At each successive selected internal vertex, its predecessor contributes one, forcing its successor. All m path vertices must therefore be selected; selecting them does meet the requirements. This is a linear-size graph with two readouts. It disproves any weight bound depending only on the number of readouts at this density. The established one-readout mixed-graph algorithm solves the displayed path family, and this example gives no lower bound on deciding it.
