# A component test for an unbounded optional port

Let three mandatory vertices induce the path a–h–c, where h has precisely four neighbors a,c,u,v. Assume that u has ambient degree at most three. The other port v may have unbounded degree. After a single shortest-path test the following exact decomposition produces at most two component branches. Every branch has at most one remaining mixed graph with two specified selected sites; all its other components have polynomial constructive solutions.

First try a–c connectivity in G minus h,u,v, as in the [middle-port theorem](degree-four-middle-port-threshold.md). If it fails, a,c are in different components there, and every witness selects all four neighbors of h. After deleting h the selected degree of u must be exactly one. Enumerate its unique selected neighbor beyond h, called w. When uv is an edge, only w=v is possible; otherwise try each neighbor of u besides h. If none is available the branch fails. Exclude every other neighbor of u besides h,w and reject contradictions with required occupancies. There are at most two choices.

Fix h,u,v selected. On the remaining induced graph, require a,c and also w if it was not v. At a selected remaining vertex i the internal prescribed parity is its adjacency to h plus its adjacency to u plus its adjacency to v, counted modulo two. Thus on remaining vertices the prescription is the sum of e_a, e_c, the optional e_w (only for w distinct from v), and the neighborhood indicator of v. The fixed h,u guards already hold. If all remaining guards hold, the guard at v follows by the handshake identity on the full selected graph. Hence components can be solved independently; no separately optimized channel at v has to match another witness.

A component with no mandatory site can be left vacant. With one mandatory site, use the [symmetric one-mark algorithm](symmetric-single-row-p3.md). Sites a,c remain in distinct components after the further deletions. Only when w is distinct from both and lies in the same component D as one of them, say p, can two mandatory sites remain together. In D, write J for the neighbors of v. Its exact problem is to select p and w, with internal prescribed vector e_p+e_w+1_J. Handshaking in D forces an even number of sites in J to be selected.

If J is empty or a singleton, remove J, rejecting if a required site was removed. The only required odd sites in D are now p,w. They can be realized precisely when they are connected after the removal. Necessity follows by component parity, and a shortest path suffices. Therefore decision and original witness recovery are polynomial whenever every surviving branch has at most one high-port neighbor in its two-mark component. The total degree of v and its incidences in the other components are unrestricted. With uv present, or with w never sharing a component with a different mandatory site, no such restriction is needed.

The [constant-size reduction](degree-four-middle-port-threshold.md) already uses just two incidences into the potential hard component: its high port v is adjacent beyond h only to t,p,q, where the mandatory t is isolated after h,u,v are removed. Thus allowing at most two such incidences on every branch includes all unrestricted three-pin inputs up to a parsimonious reduction. If the two incidences are not both present in the same relevant component on a particular input, the easier test applies. The boundary distinguishes these incidence classes without proving hardness of the unrestricted language.
