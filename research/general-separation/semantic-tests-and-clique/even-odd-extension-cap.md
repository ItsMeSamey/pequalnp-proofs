# A single GF(16) determinant encodes both parity projections on three named pins

Let K be a symmetric binary matrix indexed by free elements F and the three labelled terminals P={1,2,3}. For each S⊆F write m_T=det K[S∪T]∈GF(2), for T⊆P. We seek an exterior S for which at least one m_T is one and all nonzero m_T have the same |T| parity. Such a slice is exactly an even nonempty minor on the retained labels. Twisting those labels may swap the names of the two parity classes but preserves their exclusivity. The [rooted fiber criterion](even-rooted-fibers.md) gives the physical three-pin interpretation in its diagonal chart.

Work in GF(16)=GF(2)[α]/(α⁴+α+1). Add a cap with zero diagonal supported on the three terminal edges; its entries on 23,13,12 are respectively v=(α,α²,α³), added to the corresponding entries of K. Add one dummy element e, with zero diagonal and no free neighbors, and entries to 1,2,3 respectively d=(1,α,α²). Denote the enlarged symmetric matrix by H. For every exterior S its two determinants are

    det H[S∪P]     = m₁₂₃ + v₁²m₁ + v₂²m₂ + v₃²m₃,
    det H[S∪P∪{e}] = d₁²m₂₃ + d₂²m₁₃ + d₃²m₁₂ + (d·v)²m∅.

Indeed in characteristic two the determinant of a symmetric matrix is the sum over matchings covering vertices with pairs or diagonal singletons. Longer permutation cycles cancel by reversal; every selected pair contributes the square of its entry. Addition of a cap edge therefore permits choosing its squared weight or the old squared weight with no mixed term. Without e there is either no selected cap edge or one selected pin edge. With e the dummy must pair to a pin, after which the opposite cap edge may also be used. This proves both identities, including when original pin edges or loops are present.

The first four coefficient values are linearly independent over GF(2), since 1,v₁,v₂,v₃ are a basis and squaring is a field automorphism. For the second four, d·v=α+α³+α⁵=α³+α², using α⁴=α+1. Thus d₁,d₂,d₃,d·v are also independent. Nonvanishing of each determinant consequently gives exactly the existence of an odd or even feasible pin extension, respectively. This is deterministic with one constant field and one extra element, for all exteriors simultaneously.

The full included-P slice is nonempty: take S=∅ and include e; the coefficient of m∅=1 cannot cancel. Hence contracting all three pins in the symmetric principal-minor delta-matroid of H realizes this exact slice on F∪{e}, without forced-exclusion ambiguity. In this contracted system a successful exterior is precisely a failure of toggling e in a feasible set. Equivalently the answer is NO iff the set system factors as the direct product of its projection on F and the full one-element family {∅,{e}}. Separately taking the two e slices yields explicit extension-field contraction representations of the odd and even projections (one may be empty). A contraction representation can be queried directly using its mandatory border. If a normalized representation is needed, a feasible set extending the mandatory border can be found whenever no radical vector of the border is annihilated by all external rows, by choosing external rows forming a basis on the radical. Pivoting there and retaining the respective twist normalizes it. [Principal-border argument](p3-rooted-matroid-readouts.md)

A nonzero off-diagonal entry at e is insufficient to disprove this product property over GF(16). For example the 2-by-2 matrix with diagonal entries both one and off-diagonal α has all four principal minors nonzero (the pair determinant is 1+α²). Its nonzero-entry graph is connected but its feasible subsets factor completely. In contrast, over GF(2) ordinary determinant support records the entire determinant value. The cap encodes four independent *binary* coefficients in one field element; comparison must distinguish zero from nonzero in that larger field. Comparing the two nonzero values themselves can give a false difference even when both parities extend. These observations neither give a polynomial algorithm for the one-dummy toggle search nor imply hardness. The cap resolves representation, not the remaining support comparison.
