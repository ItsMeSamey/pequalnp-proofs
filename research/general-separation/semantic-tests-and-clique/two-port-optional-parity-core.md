# Completing a three-pin witness through optional two-port paths

Let G be a simple undirected graph and P its three required vertices. Supply a core K containing P such that each connected component of G−K has at most two distinct neighbors in K. This hypothesis can be checked directly. A canonical candidate K consists of the pins and every vertex of degree other than two. All remaining components are paths or cycles with at most two core neighbors. The theorem also applies to any other supplied K meeting the condition.

Let H=G[K]. Form a graph F on K by placing an optional edge uv when some outside component has precisely the two distinct neighbors u,v. Keep one such component and a shortest u-v path through its interior for lifting. When uv is an actual H edge, exclude it for this shortest-path search. Supporting outside components of different optional edges are distinct. Selecting the path when both endpoints are selected gives even degrees at the interior sites and contributes one incidence at each endpoint. The shortest path has no chord except possibly the separately accounted actual H edge uv. Skipping a component contributes zero.

For fixed S in K containing P, actual extendibility is equivalent to the following condition: each component of F[S] receives even total degree from H[S]. The handshake lemma on an outside component shows that its boundary contributions at selected ports are either all zero modulo two or one at both distinct selected ports. Therefore the attainable corrections belong to the span of incidence vectors of optional edges with both endpoints selected. These incidences span exactly the vectors with even weight in each component of F[S], by elimination on a spanning forest. The span is attained by taking a saved path for each chosen forest edge and leaving other exterior vertices absent. This also proves sufficiency and constructs the physical lift. Multiple lifts need not have the same size or multiplicity.

## The deterministic algorithm

Let C₁,...,Cᵣ be the components of F on the whole core. In core bits b with the pins fixed to one, define the binary quadratic d_j(b) to be the parity of selected H edges crossing between C_j and its complement. Internal H edges cancel in the aggregated degree of C_j. Every true witness has d_j=0 for all j because it satisfies the finer selected-component condition. Substitute omission bits y_i=1+b_i at nonpins. By coefficient-matrix elimination choose a basis q₁,...,qₖ of the span of the d_j as squarefree polynomials, including the constant coordinate. All vanish exactly when the basis vanishes. Since the sum of the d_j is zero, k≤r−1. Let h_j be the number of basis polynomials actually depending on some free coordinate in C_j. Every monomial in a basis polynomial has degree at most one in any individual component: each quadratic part comes from crossing edges between different components.

Assume that deleting at most h_j nonpin vertices from F[C_j] leaves it connected or empty, for every j. A simpler sufficient condition uses r−1 in place of each h_j. This is polynomial-time recognizable even with variable thresholds. For each nonadjacent vertex pair compute a minimum vertex separator with unit capacity on nonpins and capacity greater than |K| on protected sites, including the endpoints, by vertex splitting and max flow. A forbidden disconnection exists precisely if some surviving pair can be separated using the permitted deletions. Singleton components need no test.

On this class, exact feasibility and a physical witness take deterministic 2^k poly(|V|+|E|) bit operations and polynomial space. Define the Boolean indicator Q(y) as the product of the k factors (1+q_ℓ(y)). If it is identically zero report NO. Otherwise find an inclusion-minimal set T of allowed omission coordinates on which Q, with all other omission bits set to zero, remains nonzero. Initially allow them all, then restrict each y_i to zero permanently whenever some solution persists. A previously rejected restriction stays impossible after further restrictions. The final restricted squarefree polynomial is the single monomial on T: any different surviving monomial would omit a still-allowed coordinate, which could have been eliminated. The empty monomial is included. Therefore Q(1_T)=1, |T|≤2k and |T∩C_j|≤h_j. These degree bounds follow by multiplying k factors of degree at most two, with at most one variable per component in each term of each factor, and then reducing repeated powers. Select S=K−T. Its occupied part in each C_j is connected or empty, so the d_j=0 tests suffice for the finer actual condition. The spanning-forest construction yields an original witness.

Each nonemptiness test on the restricted inputs is deterministic. The number solving q₁=...=qₖ=0 equals 2^(−k) times the sum over all k-bit selectors s of the signed quadratic sums for the phases Σs_ℓq_ℓ. A phase with a cross term xy can be written xy+xU+yV+W with U,V affine on the other coordinates. Summing over x,y gives a factor two and replaces W by W+UV, still quadratic. Once no cross term remains a nonconstant affine phase sums to zero and a constant phase has its known signed power of two. Outer accumulations need O(|K|+k) bits. Workspace is reused for selectors and restrictions. In particular k=O(log |V|) gives polynomial time. The algorithm allows arbitrary dense physical H edges subject only to the measured conditions.

## Why the finer component checks matter

Take core vertices u,v,p,w with pins u,v,p. Put physical edges uv,pw in H and realize optional edges uw,wv using one fresh degree-two internal vertex each. The whole optional components are the path u-w-v and isolated p. Aggregation permits w=0. But w=1 violates the degree at p, and w=0 separates u and v in the selected optional graph while physical uv gives both odd degree. There is no original witness. The path fails the required one-deletion test. For K=V the procedure can have k of order |K|, so it gives no universal polynomial clock.
