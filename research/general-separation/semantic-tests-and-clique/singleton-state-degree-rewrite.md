# Excluding one fully fixed physical state saves a proof degree

Fix D≥3 and three literal pins. At a free site v write x for its binary selection and y for its neighbor parity. The native guard xy=0 gives three allowed states. Each indicated affine form is the indicator of its state within the legal set.

| State | Indicator I | Complementary line I=0 |
|---|---|---|
| (1,0) | x | x=0 |
| (0,1) | y | y=0 |
| (0,0) | 1+x+y | x+y=1 |

If the graph equations together with BOTH defining linear equations of a single state refute in formal binary polynomial-calculus degree D−1, then the graph derives I=0 in degree D. Each original generator may be multiplied by I within degree D. For each appended state equation L=0 its product IL is zero modulo the Boolean field equations and the native guard, with degree at most two. In the third case the two products are x+x²+xy and y+y²+xy. Thus multiplying all lines of the refutation by I is a legal degree-D derivation after these replacements. A sum or admissible variable multiplication lifts directly. This proof uses the guard at the actual site and applies even when its two affine coordinates are dependent on the pin slice.

The complementary line can be represented by removing just v while retaining the three original pins:

* For x=0, delete v.
* For x+y=1, locally complement at v, then delete it. The new input is 1+x+y and all other inputs stay fixed. The changed guard at v differs only by y²+y. Each neighboring guard differs by its own Boolean equation, and each named pin output correction vanishes at its fixed input one.
* For y=0 use any free neighbor u. Apply the labeled principal pivot transform on the nonsingular edge vu, then delete v. This swaps each selected pair (input,output) at u and v and fixes both coordinates at other sites. To obtain the matrix, swap the exterior adjacency rows of u and v, keep their mutual edge, and toggle the exterior adjacency by the sum of the two cross outer products of the old exterior adjacency rows. This is a symmetric loopless graph. Its new input at v is y. If no free neighbor is available the third move can be skipped.

The graph chart is an invertible affine change of original inputs, with degree-preserving transport of the guards. The derived I=0 becomes the new input equation x′_v=0. A child refutation of degree D therefore imports with no additional degree: specialize each generator at x′_v=0 using this derived input equation with affine multipliers, then use the child proof unchanged. In the opposite direction restrict a graph refutation in the changed chart to x′_v=0. Thus this rewrite preserves degree-D refutability in both directions. It also preserves true decision and lifts witnesses by inserting zero then undoing the chart, since the omitted fully fixed state has a sound refutation.

A polynomial-time partial solver at D=3 repeatedly computes quadratic refutability of the fully fixed states, applies an available rewrite, and checks the residual including the three-pin base case. There are at most n−3 removals and at most 3n tests per removal. A failure to find a move gives UNKNOWN. The [implementation](singleton_state_degree_rewrite.py) retains the labeled inverse transformations. The supplied thirteen-site cubic NO has, among its ten free vertices, respectively 8, 7 and 8 quadratic state refutations in table order. The procedure returns NO on this instance. On 210 seeded small graphs it returned 46 verified original witnesses, 77 verified NOs and 87 UNKNOWNs. The checker also tests the pivot equations on the actual input and replayed inverse.

Suppose there is any degree-D-unrefuted NO. Choose one of minimum order. By the [component criterion](cubic-core-connected-syndromes.md) its free graph is connected and its physical pin adjacency has rank three. In particular every free vertex has a free neighbor. All three complementary-line children of every free vertex refute at degree D, since they are smaller NO graphs. If any one fully fixed state refuted at D−1, the degree-preserving rewrite just proved would contradict the original nonrefutability. Conversely any fully fixed state is contained in a complementary line of one of the other states. Import that child's degree-D proof after adding the two affine equations of the desired state, which entail the corresponding new input equation. Consequently every one of the 3|V−P| fully fixed states has minimum refutation degree exactly D. A global counterexample to cubic completeness must therefore yield a physical core with degree exactly three on every one of its fully fixed single-site faces. General graphs with no such easy face are still possible, and the implication supplies neither a universal cubic proof nor a reduction from general SAT.
