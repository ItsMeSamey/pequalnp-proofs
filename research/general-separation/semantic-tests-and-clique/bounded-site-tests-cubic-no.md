# Branching on several physical states before a quadratic refutation

Use the [dense-module family](dense-module-cubic-lower.md) on N=10t+3 vertices, and put d=floor(t/20)+1. It is a genuine three-pin NO with a degree-three refutation, linear vertex connectivity and no nontrivial module. Every assignment of allowed states to fewer than d free sites admits an affine proof transport from the thirteen-site NO unrefuted at degree two.

Consider a branching proof whose node outcomes are arbitrary predicates on at most w≥1 named free sites. Its children cover all the possibilities remaining from earlier outcomes on the abstract ternary site alphabet. The tested sites and predicates may depend on all previous answers, and distinct outcomes may overlap. At a leaf grant even all quadratic equations valid on the *whole leaf relation* on independent legal site symbols. Substitute the actual physical coordinates (x_i,(Ax)_i) in those equations, add them to the native graph system, and require a formal degree-two refutation. Then the number of leaves is at least

    (1−3^(−w))^(−ceil(d/w)).

For fixed w this is exponential in N. At w≤(1−ε)log₃N for fixed ε>0 it is at least exp(Ω(N^ε/log N)). Tests of bounded-support parity are included. The virtual alphabet is essential: a premise valid only because the entire underlying graph is unsatisfiable cannot be installed as a free transcript equation.

## Why a positive leaf cannot have a short certificate

Take any fixed root-to-leaf path and the resulting conjunction of tested outcomes. Suppose specifying fewer than d full site states gives a nonempty subcube entirely inside that conjunction in the product ternary alphabet. Every granted quadratic then vanishes on this subcube. To see what this means for degree-two proof transport, in each fixed site replace both coordinates by their specified constants. On any remaining site reduce squares as Boolean and eliminate its product xy using the local guard xy=0. These are degree-two identities or multiples of the fixed linear equations within degree two. The remaining monomials form a subset of the product basis obtained by choosing 1,x,y at each free site, since the three functions are independent on (0,0),(1,0),(0,1). No nonzero linear combination of these basis monomials vanishes on the whole free ternary product. Thus every granted quadratic follows in degree two from the fixed coordinates, Boolean equations and local guards alone. This reasoning allows quadratics relating symbols from different tested sets or involving other free sites.

Under the dense module's affine section for these fewer than d observations (transported through the free edge pivots), fixed state equations vanish identically and each physical guard reduces to zero or an old guard modulo the old Boolean axioms. The granted quadratic equations consequently also transport at degree two. A quadratic leaf refutation would contradict the original thirteen-site lower bound. Hence no such nonempty subcube can certify that a virtual assignment reaches this leaf.

## Measuring one leaf without an independence assumption on its tests

Draw an independent uniform symbol at every original free site. For the fixed conjunction, keep a partial assignment of revealed symbols. If any outcome is impossible, stop with failure. If every outcome is forced by the partial assignment, declare acceptance. Otherwise pick a predicate of this leaf that can still fail and reveal all its as-yet unread sites, at most w of them. Conditional on the previous values, at least one tuple of the new independent symbols fails it. The conditional probability of surviving this round is at most 1−3^(−w). Check the revealed predicate before continuing. Each surviving accepting run needs at least ceil(d/w) rounds, because earlier acceptance would exhibit the prohibited subcube. Multiplying these conditional upper bounds gives leaf probability at most (1−3^(−w))^ceil(d/w). This verification procedure is applied only for bounding a particular leaf's volume; it may read predicates in a different order from the original proof tree. Since every virtual tuple enters some leaf by the covering rule, a union bound proves the assertion.

To see the width dependence in the volume argument, a conjunction of k disjoint w-site predicates, each excluding a single tuple, has positive certificate complexity k and uniform probability (1−3^(−w))^k. This example concerns the abstract alphabet and does not supply a physical quadratic refutation. For unrestricted global tests, complete parities of the ten source bags can be read in ten queries before the recorded pivots, with corresponding linear forms after the pivots. The local width theorem supplies no bound for those queries or for unrestricted graph algorithms.
