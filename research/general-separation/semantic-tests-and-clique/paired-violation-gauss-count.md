# Pairing violations in signed 2-CNF counting

Let F be a 2-CNF on n raw variables, A an explicit system of binary affine equations on them, and Q any binary quadratic polynomial. There is a polynomial-space deterministic algorithm computing the exact integer sum of (−1)^Q over solutions to F and A in O*(ρ^n) bit time. Here ρ=1.38027757… is the positive root of ρ⁴=ρ³+1 greater than one. O* suppresses a polynomial in the full input length, which includes all dense rows and phase terms. Consequently testing affine-constrained 2-CNF satisfiability admits the same running time by setting Q=0. Nagao, Seto and Teruyama report a deterministic O*(2^{n/2}) algorithm for that decision problem. The exponent comparison is with that specific result. [Nagao, Seto and Teruyama, COMP2026-5](https://ken.ieice.org/ken/paper/20260521McuY/eng/) [Arvind and Guruswami, IPEC 2021](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.IPEC.2021.5)

## A matching costs √3 per edge up to one global factor

The violation indicators f,g of two ordinary 2-clauses are products of two affine literals, valued in {0,1}. Pointwise over the rationals,

[f=g=0]=(1+(−1)^{f+g})/2 − [f=g=1].

The last factor pins every involved literal to its violating value. Any conflict makes it zero. For an unpaired clause use [f=0]=(1+(−1)^f)/2. With j residual clauses this expands to at most 3^{floor(j/2)}·2^{j mod 2} leaves. Each leaf has affine constraints, a single quadratic phase and a rational power-of-two coefficient including its sign. Consistent affine rows have a bijective parametrization. A term uv+uB+vC in the pulled-back phase can be eliminated by summing u,v, giving the factor 2(−1)^{BC}; iteration ends at an affine character. This evaluates a leaf in polynomial time. The pairing identity also works when clauses share variables. In the algorithm below we postpone all isolated edges and pool them into one expansion. This makes its overhead at most a single factor 2/√3 relative to (√3)^j across all components.

Delete tautologies, move unit clauses into A, and group clauses with the same pair of underlying variables. If a pair forbids two or more different assignments, its feasible subset has size at most two and is therefore an affine set or empty. Move that restriction into A, or return zero if empty. The remaining simple graph has one clause per edge with arbitrary signs. A variable pinned during branching is substituted in the local clauses, producing at most unit clauses in its neighbors; it is recorded or substituted in A and Q. Variables which become locally unconstrained can be retained only in A,Q. They cost no further branching, and all their interactions survive to the final Gauss step.

## Graph of degree at least three

If v has d≥3 distinct neighbors, branch on its value. In one branch p of its incident clauses force their distinct neighbors. In the other branch d−p force theirs. Substitute the forced values and bank all resulting units in A. Inconsistent branches contribute zero. Treating n in this recurrence as the number of active variables, the branch budget is at most

ρ^{−1−p} + ρ^{−1−d+p} ≤ ρ^{−1}+ρ^{−4}=1.

This uses convexity in p and d≥3. Recording an inherited restriction in A instead of propagating it immediately may create empty leaves but does not increase the stated budget. A closed child requires only polynomial work, charged to the current node.

## Paths and cycles

Write b=√3. We measure a branching plan by summing b^j over its leaves, where j counts postponed, globally pooled edges. On a k-vertex path the cost is bounded by p₀=p₁=1, p₂=b, and p_k=p_{k−2}+p_{k−3} for k≥3. Branch on the second vertex. The endpoint is now either forced or an unconstrained Gaussian variable and can be removed from the clause graph in both branches. In exactly one branch the third vertex is forced as well. After removing it, any new unit on the fourth vertex can be banked. Additional conflicts can only close a branch. Paths with k≤1 contain no clause, and the single edge k=2 is postponed.

On a cycle of k≥4 vertices split on one vertex. If its two occurrences have the same sign, one branch leaves a path on k−1 vertices, the other forces both its neighbors and leaves k−3. Opposite signs leave paths on k−2 in both branches. Thus set c_k=max(p_{k−1}+p_{k−3},2p_{k−2}). A triangle with a mixed-sign vertex gives at most two edge-free branches. If no vertex is mixed, complement local variables so the allowed patterns are 000,100,010,001. These partition into two affine lines, {000,100} and {010,001}. Both substitutions can simply be banked in A. Therefore every triangle costs at most two.

All p_k≤ρ^k by the recurrence and ρ^{−2}+ρ^{−3}<1. Both expressions defining c_k obey the same linear recurrence as p_k for k≥7, and hence c_k≤c_{k−2}+c_{k−3}. The initial values for k=4,5,6 are at most max(3,2b), max(1+2b,4), and max(4+b,2+2b), respectively; each is below ρ^k (already using ρ>1.38). This proves c_k≤ρ^k. Independent clause components multiply the weighted costs because their plans can be executed successively, retaining all restrictions and quadratic couplings. No numerical factorization of the target sum over components is assumed. After the final matching expansion the full bound is at most (2/√3)ρ^n times polynomial work. Depth-first traversal uses polynomial work space. Coefficient denominators divide 2^{O(n)} since only matching clauses remain in each final expansion; numbers of terms, absolute phase sums, integer numerator sizes and accumulated bit lengths obey the same polynomial-bit bound.

## Other parameters and scope

On the affine solution space of dimension r, another deterministic bound is O*(φ^r), with φ=(1+√5)/2. After pullback each clause forbids simultaneous affine conditions a=b=1. Nonconstant independent a,b partition its feasible locus into a=0 and (a=1,b=0), reducing dimension by one and two. Dependent affine pairs give either a trivial clause, an affine restriction or a contradiction. Repeat and use Gauss evaluation at the leaves. With m original clauses one can also apply the paired expansion directly in O*(3^{m/2}), regardless of r. Choose the smallest of these bounds up to a global constant. On the drift-free perfect cubic matching with quadratic components of at most two edges, linear-time factor identities give signed implication counting modulo three, so the ρ^V algorithm applies to V surviving fused and strongly connected selector classes. The factor identities and prior comparison to the fixed-density October theorem are in [the affine partition note](quadratic-2cnf-affine-partition.md).

An AND gate z=xy admits an affine-constrained 2-CNF description. The three affine outputs x+z,y+z,z must have Hamming weight at most one, enforced by three binary clauses on explicit ports tied to those forms. Their four legal patterns map bijectively to the AND truth table. Combining such ports with affine equations for circuit wires works for arbitrary fanout and costs O(q+g) raw coordinates at q inputs and g binary gates. At g=Θ(q log q), this bound does not certify a saving in q. The general CircuitSAT and standard P versus NP questions remain open.
