# Exact counting with read-bounded activation of expensive gate groups

Let a binary Boolean DAG have q primary bits and polynomial description size s. A designated set of a≤αq controls has fixed α<1. Suppose there are m=Θ(q/loglog q) activity predicates of these controls. Their declared supports have maximum input load d≥1, and their individual densities under uniform controls are at most 1/log₂ q. The following gate accounting is a sufficient hypothesis. For each fixed control string, a deterministic polynomial-time sound specialization yields an equivalent residual DAG on the remaining raw bits with at most

C₀q + C₁ K log₂(q) log₂log₂(q)

binary gates, where K is the number of active predicates. Fix the constants independently of q and d. A syntactic implementation groups gates into m data cones of size at most C₁ log q loglog q. Each cone feeds the remaining O(q) core through outputs masked by its guard. Charge every interior fanout that crosses a group, or verify the residual bound directly by specialization. Exposing all raw bits as additional core inputs is allowed. Guard evaluation and gate deletion are charged polynomial work per control string. All logarithms in clocks can be truncated below by a constant.

Under the pinned October fixed-density counting theorem the exact count on original inputs is computable deterministically in polynomial space and

poly(q,s) · 2^{q−Ω(q/(d√log q))}

bit operations. This gives a superpolynomial saving over enumeration when q/(d√log q)=ω(log q). Both constants and the sufficiently large threshold may depend on α and on the two-sided constants of the gate accounting and m.

For independent uniform *control coordinates*, write X_i for the guard indicators, μ=∑ E X_i. Product-space generalized Hölder gives E exp(tK) ≤ ∏(E exp(td X_i))^{1/d} ≤ exp((μ/d)(exp(td)−1)). Thus for h>μ the fraction of assignments with K≥h is ≤(eμ/h)^{h/d}. When μ=0 everything is inactive. This bound is a standard read-d Chernoff argument. [Gavinsky, Lovett, Saks and Srinivasan, Theorem 1.1](https://users.math.cas.cz/~gavinsky/papers/Tail-read-k.pdf)

Choose h=⌊γm/√log q⌋ with a sufficiently small fixed γ>0. Its ratio to μ is Ω(√log q) for μ>0. The exponent (h/d)log₂(h/(eμ)) is Ω(q/(d√log q)). For K<h the residual's parsimonious Tseitin encoding has q−a declared primaries and one new variable per remaining binary gate. At most four width-three clauses define each gate and a unit forces its output. Thus n'≥q−a≥(1−α)q and n'≤O(q)+O(γq√log q), with fixed clause density. The October counter takes polynomial bitspace and at most a polynomial factor times 2^{A n'/√log n'} at fixed width/density. Taking γ small makes the good-branch exponent at most (1−α)q/2 for all large q. Summing over ≤2^a good branches costs at most a polynomial times 2^{(1+α)q/2}. [October fixed-density theorem, pinned version](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/92b516ef8e/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/main_full_version.tex)

Each bad branch takes at most poly(q,s)2^{q−a} by exhaustive evaluation. Its total contribution to the clock is bounded by 2^q times the bad fraction up to a polynomial. Separately sum the integer counts, using q+1 bits. Dovetailing the two residual counters on every branch removes any need to know the October constant. To recover a witness impose prefix units on the original primary bits and rerun. Keep the same full control enumeration, returning zero on inconsistent assignments, so the unconditional tail bound remains valid. No satisfying assignment is discarded.

As an example, take L=⌈log₂log₂q⌉, m=⌊q/(2L)⌋ disjoint L-literal guards and arbitrary private gate groups of the stated sizes sharing the raw data inputs. Aggregate each guarded output in an O(q)-gate core, optionally also using the raw bits there. At size Θ(qlog q), direct Tseitin only certifies an exponent O(q√log q). The new bound with d=1 saves Ω(q/√log q) in the exponent of direct enumeration. It uses the supplied structure. Six copies of one two-literal guard are all active with probability 1/4. Even the event that contains every witness can be this correlated. More generally repeating each read-once guard d times scales the number of independent guard trials down by d. Inferring the independent-trials estimate from only the individual marginals would give incorrect branch costs. The bounded checker read_d_guard_check.py includes such a single-witness circuit and recomputes exact counts on 42 small DAGs. No unrestricted CircuitSAT bound follows.
