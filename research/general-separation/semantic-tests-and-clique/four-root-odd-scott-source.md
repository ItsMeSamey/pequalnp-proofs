# Scott's private-neighbor proof specialized to four named vertices

Scott's proof of Theorem 4 in *On Induced Subgraphs with All Degrees Odd*, pp. 8–9 of the author's version, starts with an independent set W of nonisolated vertices. It chooses an inclusion-minimal exterior set X whose neighborhoods cover W. Each x in X has a distinct private witness y_x in W with N(y_x) intersect X equal to {x}. Scott uses these private witnesses either as leaves on an all-even subset of X, or to repair the parities of selected centers after adding vertices from W outside the private witnesses. His stated intermediate bound is at least |W|/4 named vertices. The four-vertex conclusion below follows by specializing the construction, with a separate choice in the case of two centers. [Scott, pp. 8–9](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/scott/Papers/odds2.pdf)

Take four distinct nonisolated named vertices. An edge among them already gives a witness of size two. Otherwise take W to be all four. The private witnesses imply 1≤|X|≤4.

- With at least three centers, use two nonadjacent centers if available, and otherwise three centers forming a triangle. Either choice induces only even degrees. Append their respective private witnesses, obtaining an odd induced subgraph on four or six vertices that contains at least two members of W.
- With two centers, let p,q be the two vertices of W outside their two private witnesses. Their two neighborhood rows on X are nonzero. Some selector s has dot product one with both rows: equal rows give one consistent equation, and distinct nonzero binary rows are independent. Take p,q and the selected centers. Each of p,q has odd degree. For each selected center currently of even degree append its private witness. The result has at most six vertices and all degrees odd.
- With one center, that center and any three members of W induce a claw.

The two-center step also gives a sufficient test for a fixed nonadjacent pair p,q. Guess one or two centers and one private witness per center, with the named vertices and private witnesses distinct and jointly independent. Check that each private witness sees exactly its own center among the guesses and each named vertex sees at least one center. The same linear selection and private-witness repair give an induced odd support through p,q of at most six vertices. The four-name theorem can choose the pair, so it gives no guarantee for two names fixed beforehand.
