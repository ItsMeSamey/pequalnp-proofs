# A shared three-pin bus cannot merge independent original drift checks

Consider a reusable undirected graph with arbitrarily many helper sites and p named selected scaffold pins. It has two distinct terminal sites H_i,F_i per data index i, with arbitrary global edges between or incident to these terminals. A data layer adds its own prescribed drift bit at the corresponding terminal; the actual terminal checks use the sum of that bit and the defect contributed by the bus. The intended horizontal projection is the source occupancy x=H. The bus may transmit nonzero defects into either layer. Its graph, hidden prescriptions and scaffold pins stay fixed as the data constants and the optional single marked horizontal index vary.

Even with no data edges at all, such a design fails as a universal decision reduction with an externally named horizontal index for all but at most p choices of that index. It also fails as a universal original-witness-preserving relation compiler without any extra readout as soon as n>p. The source requires each selected x_i to meet BOTH single-site zero-neighbor tests, with independent input drift constants. Fixing the external horizontal index i, when used, means requiring H_i=1 in the native graph and x_i=1 in the source.

For the witness statement assume the zero-data source word x=1 at all n indices has at least one native extension with H all ones. For the decision statement this completeness obligation can instead be imposed as a separate transparent-bus hypothesis (for instance both clones all ones with neutral defects at zero data). Pure decision completeness on unmarked zero data alone does not force that word.

Now temporarily omit the terminal guard checks, retain the bus and pin guards, and take the affine slice H=1. The [open-scaffold flat theorem](three-pin-open-scaffold-flats.md) gives an affine flat of flow occupancies F of dimension at least n−p, every point with an actual hidden extension. At most p distinct coordinates can be identically one on this flat. At each other index i choose a point with F_i=0, together with its hidden lift. Compute the actual bus defects at all selected H and F terminals. Choose their data constants equal to these defects, ensuring every selected native guard passes. At the unselected terminal F_i set the flow-layer constant to one. This cannot disturb the native assignment. Other unselected constants may be arbitrary.

The projected source word H=1 fails its flow-layer test at i. With the additional named index i the source is a NO instance, since every legal word would have to select i while its zero-neighbor equation has right side one. The native output is YES on the supplied assignment. The construction explicitly allows the two data layers to cancel the bus's nonzero output defects; it does not require per-site zero transparency on this bad instance. From one neutral all-horizontal witness, the flat and this family of adversarial inputs are constructible by Gaussian elimination.

The result concerns a common data-independent bus with independently settable local constants and, for decision, a mark attached without changing the bus. A compiler may inspect the data when building its graph. The common-hole midpoint layer has a fixed special drift pattern and incidence matrix, so this result alone does not rule out a bus tailored to that source.
