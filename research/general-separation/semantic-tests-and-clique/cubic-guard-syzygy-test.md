# A sufficient exterior-rank obstruction to degree-three polynomial calculus

Work over the binary field in squarefree Boolean coordinates on a consistent physical pin slice. Let V denote its linear coordinate forms and W the binary span of the restricted guards. Suppose W contains no nonzero affine polynomial. Write each physical guard g_i=l_i m_i as a product of two affine local coordinates. Let ω(w) in Λ²V denote the homogeneous quadratic coefficients of w, and define

    μ : V⊗W → Λ³V,     λ⊗w ↦ λ∧ω(w).

This map involves only the leading homogeneous coefficients. Define U to be the span of lin(l_i)⊗g_i and lin(m_i)⊗g_i for all sites. If dim ker μ = dim U, then iterative degree-three polynomial calculus cannot refute this pin system. The test requires no satisfying assignment; it also applies to satisfiable inputs. All spaces here can be obtained by ordinary binary elimination.

Indeed the degree-three coefficients of the Boolean-reduced product λw are precisely the displayed exterior form. Both tensors associated to a site therefore lie in ker μ. In the Boolean ring l_i g_i=g_i and m_i g_i=g_i, so the product of either linear part with g_i is zero or g_i. Thus all elements of U map into W under actual multiplication followed by Boolean reduction. If U spans the kernel, then W+VW has W as its entire degree-at-most-two part. This space contains all input guards, is closed under sums, and is closed under every allowed degree-three PC multiplication: a multiplicand of formal degree at most two reduces into W, whose products are in W+VW. Boolean axioms reduce to zero. Since 1 is affine and W contains no nonzero affine element, no refutation occurs.

More generally the same conclusion holds if a basis of ker μ modulo U is supplied and its Boolean-reduced products all belong to W. Conversely, when W contains no nonzero affine polynomial, failure of this latter condition is exactly the existence of a first new consequence of degree at most two at the cubic round. The additional consequence need not be a contradiction. This identifies the required syzygy for a universal completeness proof and gives a sufficient test for a counterexample. No NO instance passing the obstruction has been established here.

One elementary positive case follows from splitting on one affine form L. If each binary branch L=b has a degree-two PC refutation, then the unsplit system has a degree-three refutation. Multiply all equations of the b-branch derivation by its affine indicator 1+L+b. The additional branch axiom has zero product with this indicator by the Boolean axioms; all original generators can legally be multiplied by the affine indicator within the increased degree bound. Linear combinations and allowed variable multiplications in the old proof lift within the new bound. The lifted final equations assert that each branch indicator vanishes, and their sum is one. The statement assumes degree-two proofs with the linear branch axiom included; the usual affine pin-slice substitution transfers such proofs back to that presentation.
