# Exact Boolean matrix powers with binary exponents

Let A be a Boolean adjacency matrix on w≥1 vertices. For a nonnegative integer T given by at most K read-only binary digits, the Boolean-semiring entry (A^T)[s,t] can be computed deterministically in poly(w,K) time, O(log(w+2)+log(K+2)) clean bits and O(w log²(w+2)) borrowed bits restored on every input seed. Read-only matrix entry access is assumed. Generating an implicit matrix or exponent must be charged separately.

For T≥2w², the entry is one precisely when some vertex v and integers 1≤d≤w and 0≤r≤ℓ<2wd satisfy ℓ≡T mod d and there exist walks

    s → v of length r,    v → t of length ℓ−r,    v → v of length d.

Indeed, among the first w+1 visited vertices of an accepting walk two agree. Their intervening nonempty closed walk has length d≤w and base v. Split the whole walk at its first chosen occurrence of v. Shorten each piece separately while preserving its length modulo d, by taking a shortest path between the corresponding endpoints in the product graph of vertices and residues modulo d. Each shortened piece has length at most wd−1. Their concatenation still visits v. This also covers an empty piece. Conversely, insert (T−ℓ)/d copies of the certified closed walk at v; the assumed threshold ensures this integer is nonnegative. The product graphs are used only in the proof. For T<2w² test the requested short length directly; length zero is the identity.

Enumerate the polynomially many tuples with logarithmic counters. A streaming scan maintains the exponent saturated at 2w² and, on demand, its residue modulo d. Test each short-walk condition with integer adjacency matrix powering, Theorem 3.1 at tradeoff parameter 1. Its Algorithm 2 computes small-prime residues and reverses the register computation before returning. For a positive tested length a≤2w² the count is at most w^a. Testing the first 1+ceil(a log₂(max(2,w))) primes suffices, since their product exceeds this bound. These primes have polynomial magnitude and can be enumerated by trial division with logarithmic work bits in polynomial time. Equivalently, stream the exact integer output of Theorem 3.1 and record whether it has a nonzero bit. Calls reuse the same catalyst sequentially. [Chakraborty, Datta and Shandilya, Theorem 3.1 and Algorithm 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.41723v1#S3.SS1)

For a controller producing a table of N=2^n outputs with K≤N, w log²(w+2)=o(N/n) leaves the restored tape below every fixed positive multiple of N/n eventually, and only O(n) clean bits are needed. Time over all N addresses is polynomial in N provided each input-bit and adjacency query has a common polynomial bound. A small *tick circuit* additionally requires those queries be implemented within the gate and state budget, for instance by sequential calls to supplied small query circuits. An arbitrary length-N exponent ROM or arbitrary w²-entry matrix ROM cannot be presumed free under a circuit description budget. The quantitative application combines the unary-walk certificate with short powering on w vertices. The underlying unary-automata progression phenomenon is classical. [Chrobak](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(86)90104-7) and [To’s correction](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.1291)
