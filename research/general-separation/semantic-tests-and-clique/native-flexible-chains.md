# Selecting the branch vertices of subdivided graphs with mandatory vertices

Let G be a finite undirected simple graph and P a specified set of p mandatory vertices. A solution is any set containing P that induces only even degrees. The following algorithm decides the problem and constructs a solution in O(4^p (|V|+|E|+1)) time whenever no two vertices of degree different from two are adjacent. In particular it covers arbitrary positive subdivisions of every base edge, with independent lengths and mandatory vertices anywhere along the paths. A further test for saturating part of an arbitrary graph permits arbitrarily many unsubdivided edges.

Let B contain precisely P and all vertices with degree different from two. A connected component with no vertex of B is an unmarked cycle and can be left vacant. The rest decomposes into direct edges between vertices of B and maximal paths with ends in B and at least one interior vertex, all their interiors unmarked and of degree two. Use these paths as edges of a multigraph L on B. A returning path with coincident endpoints can be left vacant. Edges with distinct endpoints may have parallel alternatives in L. Denote the direct edges by H.

For a fixed choice U of occupied vertices of B containing P, exactly the following parity conditions determine feasibility. For each connected component C of L[U], the number of direct edges of H[U] leaving C must be even. To see necessity, sum the guards on the selected interior of a single path. Its incidences with selected endpoints have even total. If both distinct ends are selected their incidence bits agree, and if only one end is selected its bit is zero. A returning path contributes zero in aggregate at its sole endpoint. Thus the long paths act as optional parity edges on L[U]. For sufficiency the required right-hand side on U is its degree in H[U] modulo two. On a spanning forest of L[U], take the parent edge at a vertex exactly when the sum of right-hand sides in its descendant subtree is odd. Each used path is selected in full, and all other path interiors are left vacant. This gives a solution in original vertices.

More generally fix a supplied K between P and B. Enumerate U0 between P and K, and let U* be U0 union (B minus K). Compute the components of L[U*]. Call this assignment certified when every edge of H[U*] between distinct such components has both endpoints in K. For a certified assignment, feasibility with U intersect K equal to U0 is equivalent to feasibility of U*. Indeed suppose U is any feasible choice in that slice. Each component of L[U] lies in a component C of L[U*]. Every direct edge leaving C in H[U*] has its endpoints already selected in U0. Thus the parity of all direct edges leaving C in H[U*] equals their parity in H[U]. The latter is even by summing the feasibility equations on the components of L[U] contained in C. This proves the criterion and the preceding forest construction lifts its positive answer to G. The test costs O(2^{|K minus P|} (|V|+|E|+1)). A positive saturated choice always certifies YES. If every negative saturated choice is certified the test decides the instance. Other branches may be searched further.

Under the hypothesis in the first paragraph every direct edge has a mandatory endpoint. Add to P every other endpoint of a direct edge and use the resulting K. A mandatory vertex with degree different from two can touch an unmarked vertex of B by a direct edge only if that unmarked vertex also has degree different from two, which the hypothesis excludes. The remaining mandatory vertices have degree two, hence contribute at most 2p unmarked endpoints. All assignments are certified and the stated uniform running time follows in the adjacency-list model. More generally k unmarked endpoints of direct edges suffice in O(2^k (|V|+|E|+1)) time, even without the positive-subdivision condition. The certificate can use a smaller K, since arbitrarily many direct edges may be internal to a flexible connected component.

A negative saturated test can err without its certificate. Take a triangle on mandatory p,q and optional z with a private pendant leaf at each triangle vertex, and a mandatory isolated r. The triangle and isolated r are a solution. All vertices have degree different from two, so keeping all of B fails the parity test. For general hosts with no unmarked degree-two sites the exact description above retains the original selection problem on H.
