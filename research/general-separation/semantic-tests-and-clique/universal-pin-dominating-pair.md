# Three named vertices with a universal pin and an apex-minor-free complementary remainder

Let G be a simple undirected graph with distinct mandatory vertices z,p,q, where z is universal. Fix an apex graph A (planar after deletion of one vertex). If the complement J of G−z excludes A as a minor, there is a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm returning an induced all-even vertex set of G through the three original pins or certifying that none exists. The planar case permits unbounded degree. It suffices that H=J−pq excludes A when pq exists.

The guard at z requires even cardinality of the remaining selected set S. For v in S, its degree in the original support is |S|−deg_{J[S]}(v). Thus S must contain p,q and induce even degrees in J. Nonadjacent p,q give S={p,q}. Otherwise remove pq to form H. If H has no p–q path, reject, since pq would be a bridge in any proposed even support. If some vertex v has a p–q path in H avoiding its closed neighborhood N_H[v], take a shortest such path. Its vertices induce a cycle in J after restoring pq. When that cycle is odd, append v as an isolated selected vertex; otherwise take the cycle alone. The closed neighborhoods of p or q cannot witness this test, and all other closed neighborhoods agree in J and H. Ordinary graph searches suffice.

In the residual graph H every p–q path dominates H, and H is connected. This is the path definition of a dominating pair. Corneil, Olariu and Stewart use this notion in their algorithms for AT-free graphs. https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539795282377

**Strip lemma.** Suppose a connected graph H with a dominating pair p,q excludes the complete bipartite minor K_{t,t}, t≥2. Write L_i for BFS layers from p and P_i for the i-th vertex of a shortest p–q path, with q=P_d. This path dominates, so every layer has index at most d+1. Put b=a+t+5, where a≥0 and b≤d. In the induced slab of layers a through b there is a vertex separator of size <2t between L_a and L_b, with both endpoint layers deletable.

Indeed, consider pairwise vertex-disjoint crossings between the two layers. Extend each to a walk from p to q by using a BFS path to its left endpoint, in layers ≤a, and connecting the right endpoint to the spine (distance ≤1) then following it to q in layers ≥b−1. For each of the t distinct spine vertices P_{a+2},…,P_{a+t+1}, the extensions avoid its closed neighborhood by layer distance. Hence this neighborhood hits the crossing, for otherwise a p–q path would avoid it. Given 2t crossings, discard paths containing any of the t named spine vertices. At least t disjoint connected crossings remain, each adjacent to every one of the t singleton vertices. Contraction yields the excluded minor. The vertex version of Menger's theorem gives the separator, constructible by unit vertex-capacity flow.

**Bounded treewidth on the residual.** Choose t=max(3,|V(A)|). A perfect matching contraction gives a K_t minor in K_{t,t}, so H excludes K_{t,t}. Choose full slabs [a_i,b_i] starting a_1=0, with b_i=a_i+t+5≤d and a_{i+1}=b_i+4. Retain their separators X_i. After deleting their union, no component can meet levels strictly below a_i and strictly above b_i, since a joining path contains a crossing wholly in that slab. Its span of layer indices is therefore at most 3(t+9)+3, including the end bands. If no whole slab fits, d<t+5 suffices. Each component touches at most two separators, necessarily consecutive: contact-neighbors for X_i and X_{i+2} would lie on opposite sides of the intervening slab. There are no edges directly between separators.

For a band of BFS layers of span h, contract all earlier layers, when nonempty, to one vertex (their union is connected), and discard later layers. The resulting minor has radius at most h+1 from that vertex or from p. Eppstein's diameter–treewidth theorem bounds its treewidth by a constant depending on A,h. Each component therefore has bounded treewidth. Take a path of bags X_i∪X_{i+1}, using empty endpoint separators. Add each component's boundary to all bags of its decomposition and attach it to a path bag containing that boundary. All edges are covered and bags for any vertex form a subtree. When no separators exist use the single residual decomposition. This bounds tw(H) by a constant depending only on A. For planar H one can use t=3. Obtain a decomposition directly for H by Bodlaender's constructive fixed-width algorithm. Trying successive widths needs only constantly many trials on this class, even without a supplied numeric diameter–treewidth bound. https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9907126 and https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539793251219

Add p to every bag to restore pq. Store one occupancy bit and the accumulated neighbor parity per bag vertex, enforcing the named occupancies. Assign each edge to one node and update parities from both occupancies. At a forget node require even accumulated parity for a selected vertex and toggle global cardinality parity by its occupancy. The global bit counts forgotten vertices, so joins xor child parities and global bits at common occupancy. An empty root accepts even global parity. Recorded decisions return S then S∪{z}. This accepts disconnected and higher-degree witnesses. Complexity is polynomial on the stated classes.

A minimum counterexample to using only an even cycle or odd cycle with an isolated vertex has six vertices in J. Use the four internally disjoint u–v paths uv,uav,ubv,ucdv and require p=u,q=a. All six vertices work, the only induced cycle through ua is triangle uav, and each remaining vertex touches that triangle. On at most five vertices an even-cardinality all-even set through an edge must have four vertices and be a four-cycle or a triangle with an isolate. The bounded-degree companion implementation tests this fixture and reconstructs its raw original witness.

Excluding just some fixed minor is insufficient for the structural width conclusion. Take a planar grid with a new p adjacent to every grid vertex except the corner q. This H is K_6-minor-free: deleting the branch set containing p from a K_6 model would leave a K_5 in the planar grid. Every p–q path dominates H, which contains those unbounded-width grids. Adding pq gives J with universal p, so an even-cardinality all-even support containing p is impossible there. This example limits the width argument without implying decision hardness.
