# Exact SAT counting for few attained violation scores

Let S(x) on n Boolean variables be a signed rational weighted sum of m clause-violation indicators, each involving at most k bits. For ordinary CNF use one positive unit weight for each clause; score zero then means satisfiable. The bounds below apply to any chosen score level for the weighted version. Write B for a proposed upper bound on the number of different attained scores, irrespective of their numerical sizes. For example, the distinct clauses (x OR z_j) and (x OR NOT z_j) for each j=1,…,h give exactly the two scores 0,h. This differs from the established hitting-k promise bounding how many clauses a single assignment can falsify. [Filmus et al., Hitting Formulas](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2024.48)

## Two uniform counters

Each clause-violation indicator is a multilinear product of the prescribed literals; the score has real degree at most k. If there are r attained scores, interpolation gives the indicator g of any desired subset of levels as a Boolean polynomial of degree ≤d=min(n,k(r−1)). The radius-d Boolean Hamming ball around zero determines every multilinear polynomial of degree ≤d by Möbius inversion. In particular every attained score occurs within the ball. Given the bound B, enumerate the ball of radius min(n,kB) and collect its score set A. If |A|>B the promise fails. Otherwise the polynomial ∏_{a in A}(S−a) has degree ≤kB and vanishes on that ball, hence everywhere after multilinearization; the collected scores form the entire spectrum. The same argument works around any center by complementing coordinates.

For exact counting use r=|A| and the smaller radius d after the test, or immediately use min(n,k(B−1)) on a promised input. At each 1_T with |T|≤d evaluate its acceptance bit g. Its contribution to the total count has coefficient

    c_t = Σ_{j=0}^{d−t} (−1)^j binom(n−t,j) 2^{n−t−j},     t=|T|.

Indeed the coefficient of a multilinear monomial indexed by U is Σ_{T⊆U}(−1)^{|U|−|T|}g(1_T), and its sum over the cube is 2^{n−|U|}. Collecting equal T gives the displayed coefficient. Thus counting and finding an accepting input take poly(input,n) times the size of the applicable ball. For fixed k,B this is polynomial, and for kB=o(n) it is subexponential. All bit arithmetic is polynomial in the explicit input, the rational data and n. A promised accepting level occurs in the ball.

Alternatively compute the moments M_j=Σ_x S(x)^j for j=0,…,2B, with M_0=2^n. Expand into ordered tuples of at most 2B clauses. An inconsistent union of falsifying literals contributes zero; a consistent union fixing h distinct bits contributes 2^{n−h} times its coefficient. The runtime is poly(input,B)·(m+1)^{O(B)} even for large clause width when literal unions are explicit. Negative rational weights cause no problem since the measure on score values has positive multiplicities.

For a trial degree t fit the next moment recurrence via the Hankel Gram matrix (M_{i+j}) for 0≤i,j<t. It is positive definite as long as there are at least t distinct scores. Starting at t=1, solve for the coefficients of the monic P(T)=T^t+Σ_{j<t}p_j T^j orthogonal to 1,…,T^{t−1}. The quantity M_{2t}+Σ_{j<t}p_j M_{t+j} equals Σ_x P(S(x))². Its first zero certifies the minimal support polynomial, with all distinct attained scores as its simple roots. If no zero occurs by B the promise fails. If P(0)≠0 the zero-level count is zero; otherwise P(T)/(T P'(0)) is its level indicator and its moments give the count without factoring. The rational linear systems have polynomial bit complexity in the data and B. This is classical finite-moment reconstruction applied to clause sums. ## A sharp obstruction to witness-preserving flattening

Suppose a formula F on the original bits x has a representation F(x)=1 iff some auxiliary string z makes H(x,z)=1. Assume H is a Boolean table on a score of local real degree ≤k with ≤B distinct values. Even a separate B-value bound on x for each fixed z suffices. On every Boolean face R having T>0 solutions of F,

    k(B−1) ≥ dim R − floor(log₂ T).

Fix a successful (x,z) on the face and hold that z fixed. The resulting H is a nonzero Boolean function supported on at most T points of R, and its degree there is ≤k(B−1) by interpolation. A nonzero multilinear polynomial of degree d on t bits has at least 2^{t−d} nonzero evaluations. For example fix a degree-d monomial with nonzero coefficient and each assignment to the complementary bits: the restricted polynomial keeps that coefficient, so at least one of its evaluations survives. This proves the stated bound independently of the number or multiplicity of auxiliary witnesses. For a direct Boolean level indicator of degree d, the count is also divisible by 2^{max(0,n−d)} because its multilinear coefficients are integers. [Nisan and Szegedy, Boolean degree](https://doi.org/10.1145/129712.129757)

In particular a unique original solution forces k(B−1)≥n. The bound is attained in ordinary width-k CNF when n=k(B−1): split the bits into B−1 blocks and include, for each block, one width-k clause excluding each local word except all ones. Exactly one clause fails per bad block and none per good block, so the spectrum is 0,…,B−1 and only the all-ones word satisfies. Even a dense solution set can have a hard face: x₁ OR (x₂ AND … AND x_n) has a unique solution on x₁=0. The obstruction concerns preserving original witnesses under existential projection. A general equisatisfiable reduction that discards the original witness coordinates is outside its scope.

As a positive composition rule, suppose the constraints are grouped into blocks with private variables and shared interface bags I_j. Assume the bags form a join tree (bags containing each shared variable are connected) of size ≤r, and each block has ≤B private-variable score levels on every fixed assignment of its interface. Obtain each block's conditional count by the moment algorithm. Sum the product of these counts over consistent interface assignments by ordinary join-tree messages in poly(input)·2^{O(r)}(m+1)^{O(B)} time, including a separate factor of two per completely unused variable. This admits unbounded global score spectrum and multiple independent low-score constraints. It requires the supplied block and interface structure.
The composition rule takes the conjunction of the block predicates. Each shared-variable bag has at most r variables.
