# Three specified vertices in an induced all-even subgraph

The problem asks whether a finite simple graph has a vertex set containing three specified vertices and inducing only even degrees. Isolated selected vertices are allowed. On arbitrary graphs this literal-pin case is equivalent for decision and witness reconstruction to the all-even problem with three binary linear readouts, and to the symmetric two-readout problem with arbitrary degree parity prescriptions. On subcubic graphs, three literal pins admit a deterministic polynomial-time construction. The conversions from arbitrary readouts can raise the maximum degree.

## Replacing linear readouts by literal pins

Write T for an r-by-n binary readout matrix, A for loopless symmetric binary adjacency, t for the desired output. First test linear consistency, eliminate dependent rows, and absorb affine constants into t. If t=0 the empty support solves the all-even decision question. For full row rank r≥2 and t≠0, change row basis to arrange t=e₁+e₂. Put j equal to the all-ones r-vector and v=e₁. The symmetric matrix M=jvᵀ+vjᵀ has zero diagonal, rank at most two, and Mt=j because jᵀt=0 and vᵀt=1.

On the original vertices use adjacency A′=A+TᵀMT. Its diagonal is zero. Add r new vertices, all required to be selected. Pin i is adjacent to the original vertices specified by row i of T. Add the single edge between pins 1 and 2. The pin equations are precisely Tx=t. Under those equations, all r pins together contribute Tᵀj to the original degree-parity vector, which exactly cancels the term TᵀMTx. Thus selecting the pins gives a bijection between original satisfying assignments and pinned all-even supports, including singular cases. The adjacency update may toggle quadratically many edges. A dense adjacency matrix can be formed in polynomial time using the two outer products. The pin-original incidences cost O(rn). For an all-zero target or rank below two the elementary decision cases may be handled separately (a nonzero rank-one target is attained by a singleton). No count preservation is claimed for those shortcuts.

For symmetric mixed prescriptions write G for the loopless adjacency, c for the prescribed parity on each selected vertex, and normalize two independent readouts to aᵀx=1, bᵀx=0. Attach three required vertices to the originals using the rows aᵀ,bᵀ,(c+a+b)ᵀ. Add just an edge between pins 1 and 3. The pins require outputs (1,0,1) and the original vertices see additional parity c. The third equation is equivalent to cᵀx=0 in the presence of the first two, and that equality holds on every satisfying original support by the handshake lemma. No old adjacency changes in this construction. Again this is a bijection on vertex supports. A zero readout target is realized by the empty mixed support. In rank-one or dependent cases use consistency and the [single-readout construction](symmetric-single-row-p3.md).

For the converse direction three pins are themselves three coordinate readouts. Convert an all-even problem with three independent rows to a mixed problem with two by changing the target to (1,1,0). Write a,b,d for these three rows viewed as columns. Toggle adjacency by abᵀ+baᵀ, prescribe c=a+b, and require aᵀx=1 and dᵀx=0. The handshake lemma on every mixed solution gives bᵀx=aᵀx. Under those target values the changed adjacency contributes a+b and the mixed constraints agree with the original even checks on every selected vertex. This preserves each support. The distinct favorable directions of the resulting equivalence concern unrestricted graph degree. [Two-row twist](symmetric-two-readout-twist.md)

## Three literal pins in a subcubic graph

In maximum degree three, every nonisolated component of an all-even induced support is a chordless cycle. Among the pin triples, an independent triple or a triangle is already feasible. If the three pins form an induced path a-b-c, remove b and all its neighbors other than a,c. Accept precisely when a and c are joined in the remaining graph. Any shortest such path closes through b to an induced cycle. Conversely the unique cycle component containing both prescribed edges supplies such a path.

In the remaining case ab is the only pin-pin edge, and c is the other pin. Put H=G−ab and let H₀=H−N_H[c]. If a,b are connected in H₀, a shortest such path closes through ab to an induced cycle in G and c may be selected as an isolated vertex. Otherwise let A,B denote the distinct components of H₀ containing a,b. In any feasible support the edge ab lies on an induced cycle. If c were outside that cycle, no selected cycle vertex could be adjacent to c, since different induced components have no edges between them. The cycle minus ab would then connect a,b in H₀. Hence c lies on the cycle.

Enumerate ordered distinct nonadjacent vertices u,v in N_H(c) as the two neighbors of c on that cycle in the order from a to b in H. Test reachability from a to u in H[A∪{u}] after deleting N_H(v), and from b to v in H[B∪{v}] after deleting N_H(u). A deleted endpoint makes the test fail. On any witnessing cycle the corresponding two path interiors before u and after v lie in A and B and avoid the stated neighborhoods by inducedness. For the converse, take shortest paths under both restrictions. Edges between A and B are absent in H. The deletions rule out edges from u or v across to the wrong path. The neighbors of c in the assembled paths are precisely u,v. Restoring ab therefore gives a chordless cycle with all three pins. This takes O(n+m) time in adjacency-list subcubic graphs since the number of ordered pairs is at most six. The returned vertex list is an actual witness.

Lévêque, Lin, Maffray and Trotignon establish hardness of finding a single hole through two specified nonadjacent degree-two vertices in graphs of maximum degree three. The present question permits multiple components including isolated pinned vertices. The H₀ connectivity test exploits exactly that permission. [Primary theorem on holes through two vertices](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.0971)

## An obstruction to vertex-local degree reduction

A proposed replacement for a degree-d vertex uses a subcubic graph with d external incidences or ports. Each incidence joins a designated gadget vertex to its original external neighbor. Consider the local boundary experiment with every external neighbor selected, while its own local degree check is imposed by the external graph. At a port the outgoing contribution bit is whether its gadget endpoint is selected. All selected internal vertices must have even total degree including their external incidences. A set of internal vertices may be explicitly forced to be selected. The required local signatures in this experiment for a single original vertex of even degree d are the all-zero and all-one d-bit vectors.

For even d≥4, realizing the all-one signature and excluding every mixed signature needs at least d/2 forced internal vertices in this model. Take a realizing assignment for the all-one signature. Append a distinct degree-one stub to each of its selected port vertices for each incident external edge. Every selected internal vertex in the resulting graph has degree zero or two, including its stubs. Exactly d/2 components are paths pairing the stubs; each contains at least one internal vertex. If one such component contains no forced vertex, delete its internal vertices from the assignment. No surviving selected internal vertex had an edge to that component, and its external neighbors are still selected. All surviving checks still hold but the outgoing vector has exactly two zero entries and d−2 ones. This contradicts local signature equality. The argument also covers ports at the same internal vertex. It assumes one physical incidence per old neighbor and exact local outgoing contributions. It does not rule out global reductions with encoded ports or changed boundary semantics.

Three literal pins in unbounded-degree graphs can force a unique high-degree support even when that support has quadratic cycle-space dimension. [Explicit complement-cycle construction](three-pinned-even-support.md)
