# A semantic obstruction to a superlogarithmic saving from quadratic frontiers

For Boolean f on q primary bits, suppose that for every nonzero v and every affine subspace W of dimension at least k, the proportion of x in W with f(x)≠f(x+v) is at least γ>0. A one-bit directional affine extractor of error ε<1/2 satisfies this with γ=1/2−ε by testing inequality of its two output coordinates. An affine non-malleable extractor also qualifies, taking translation by v as the fixed-point-free tampering.

Let Q=(Q₁,…,Q_r) be any list of degree-at-most-two GF(2) functions with r≤q−k, and let H=h(Q) for an arbitrary Boolean h, with no circuit-size bound on the list or h. For a fixed nonzero v the collision condition Q(x)=Q(x+v) is a system of r affine equations in x. If its solution set L_v is nonempty, its dimension is at least q−r≥k. H is equal at paired points of L_v, whereas f differs on at least a γ fraction. Since r<q when k≥1, at least one nonzero shift has a collision. Thus no such representation of f is exact, regardless of circuit rewrites or selected wires.

**Sparse corrections do not remove the obstruction.** Write N=2^q, M=2^r, n_y=|Q^{-1}(y)|, and L=max_y n_y. The number of ordered pairs of *distinct* primary inputs with equal Q image is C=Σ_y n_y(n_y−1). Counting collisions by their difference v shows at least γC ordered pairs where f differs and H agrees. An error at x can explain at most 2(n_{Q(x)}−1) such pairs. Consequently the number E of errors satisfies

    E ≥ γ C/[2(L−1)] ≥ (γ/2) sqrt(N²/M−N).

For the last inequality C≥N²/M−N by Cauchy–Schwarz and C≥L(L−1) from the largest fiber. Thus C/(L−1)≥max(L,(N²/M−N)/(L−1))≥sqrt(N²/M−N). The denominator is positive since M<N. For r≤q−1 this forces Ω(γ·2^{q−r/2}) errors. This is an absolute disagreement bound; no constant-error uniform approximation claim is needed.

**Existing functions with the derivative property.** Li and Zhong give explicit polynomial-time affine non-malleable extractors at entropy c log q with any chosen sufficiently small constant error. Fix ε=1/8 and choose the construction's constant c accordingly. Translation by any v≠0 has no fixed point. Thus one explicit P language requires more than q−ceil(c log q) quadratic features at every sufficiently large length, even if a polynomial number of disagreements are allowed. Their small-entropy result is a primary theorem about extractors; the quadratic-representation calculation above is a direct application. [ICALP 2024, Theorem 8](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2024.108) · [Full proof, Section 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.17013v1)

Separately, their CCC 2024 Theorem 14 supplies a nonuniform function with an XOR–AND–XOR polynomial-size circuit and directional extraction at k=log₂(q/ε²)+log₂ log₂(q/ε²)+O(1), as well as a polynomial-size De Morgan formula. This gives the same obstruction at k=log₂ q+log₂ log₂ q+O(1) for one family of polynomial-size functions. One need not infer uniformity of this probabilistic construction or linear fan-in-two size. [CCC 2024, Definition 2 and Theorem 14](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2024.10)

A counter enumerating 2^r rows on one exact quadratic frontier can therefore lose its superpolynomial saving on polynomial-size circuits even with arbitrary semantic preprocessing. The cited size bounds do not place those functions inside Bq log q binary gates for fixed B. Decision-only reductions, branching among different maps, and exploiting the internal structure of h without enumerating its whole table remain outside this conclusion. The obstruction is compatible with f itself being polynomial-time computable.
