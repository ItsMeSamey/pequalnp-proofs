# Eliminating private variables by certifying attainable quadratic outputs

Suppose a supplied gate DAG factors as H(z,Q(u,z)), where the p bits of u reach the outer DAG only through the r listed Boolean quadratic outputs. Structural separation is checked by cutting the listed wires and checking that u no longer reaches the sink. Each quadratic identity needs a certificate, e.g. propagation of verified polynomial labels. If Q(·,z) attains every r-bit y for every z, SAT reduces exactly to SAT of H(z,y) on q−p+r primary bits, with q=|z|+p. This is valid even when only one value of y at one z satisfies H. Unweighted model counts generally change.

**Polar-rank certificate.** For a nonzero λ in GF(2)^r let Rλ be the rank of the alternating polar matrix of λ·Q on u. Since total degree is at most two, this matrix is independent of z. Its normalized additive bias on any z slice has magnitude zero or 2^{−Rλ/2}. Indeed square the character expectation and substitute a uniform difference h; averaging over u kills all h outside the polar kernel. On the kernel the residual sign is a character times a fixed normalization, so the squared bias is zero or 2^{−Rλ}. Fourier inversion gives for every z,y

    |{u: Q(u,z)=y}| ≥ 2^{p−r}(1−Σ_{λ≠0} 2^{−Rλ/2}).

In particular Rλ≥2r for every nonzero λ guarantees at least 2^{p−2r} preimages per output. Computing all ranks costs poly(input) 2^r; even followed by full enumeration of the reduced instance, the clock is poly(input) 2^{q−p+r}, since q≥p. A drop of two in the rank hypothesis already fails in general at r=2: Q=(ab,(a+1)c) has rank two for each of its three nonzero pencils, yet 11 is unattainable.

**A polynomial certificate for a coupled pencil of dimension r.** Use p=2m private bits (a,b) as coordinates of K=GF(2^m), m≥r. Let π:K→GF(2)^r be a supplied full-rank linear map. Consider

    Q(a,b,z)=π(ab)+R(a,z)+L_z(b),

where R is vector-valued quadratic, L_z is GF(2)-linear in b on each slice, and there are no extra a-b or b-b products. These conditions can be verified on coefficient tables in polynomial time using multiplication in the supplied checked field presentation. An additive constant in b is absorbed into R. For λ≠0 the functional λπ is nonzero. The bilinear pairing (a,b)↦λπ(ab) is nondegenerate since multiplication by nonzero a permutes K. The entire polar matrix has blocks [P B; Bᵀ 0] with B nonsingular, so has rank 2m independently of P. Thus the stronger lower bound is 2^{m−r}(2^m−2^r+1). This covers all outside slices, without enumerating λ or z. For π the identity and Q=ab, zero has 2^{m+1}−1 preimages and each nonzero output has 2^m−1. An implementation must charge the supplied field arithmetic; this family alone does not give a near-linear gate bound for arbitrary field presentations.

**A near-linear DAG with a single original satisfying assignment.** A second sufficient certificate composes private pairs in order. If the next output after previous private pairs have been fixed has the form aᵢbᵢ+αaᵢ+βbᵢ+γ, with α,β,γ depending only on z and earlier pairs, both desired bits are attainable: this is (aᵢ+β)(bᵢ+α)+γ+αβ. Choose a pair attaining the requested bit and continue. An invertible affine remapping of all output coordinates preserves surjectivity. These are structural conditions and give a deterministic polynomial-time witness lift when their coefficients are evaluable. Inputs to later equations may reuse earlier nonlinear outputs.

For an explicit 2r-input example compute gᵢ=aᵢbᵢ and output yᵢ=g₁+⋯+gᵢ with prefix XOR gates. The r homogeneous quadratic parts are independent. Every private input affects an output, the final output depends on every gᵢ, and the entire map uses 2r−1 binary gates. The y corresponding to g=1^r has exactly one primary preimage; y=0 has 3^r. An outer equality test for that rare y accepts exactly one original assignment. The replacement reduces the decision search dimension by r without enumerating either set of fibers. This triangular certificate works even though the minimum polar rank is two.

If a retained solver with known clock T(v,s) is used, charge the actual residual gates s of H and v=q−p+r, plus certification time. For any fixed B, a polynomial-time certificate with p−r=ω(log q) and polynomial original size already gives a superpolynomial exhaustive-search saving on this supplied subfamily of Bq log q gate instances.
