# Sparse cubic hardness on simple incidence graphs and its affine rigidity

Consider m disjoint cubic triples on all 3m raw bits and a perfect matching of quadratic terms. If every quadratic joins distinct triples and each unordered pair of triples has at most one quadratic link, the interaction graph on triples is simple and cubic. Computing the signed gap modulo three is #₃P-complete even within this class, with at most one linear term in every triple and at most one drifted endpoint per quadratic edge. Furthermore any invertible affine change mapping one such scalar Boolean polynomial to a polynomial with a perfect cubic matching and a perfect quadratic matching is a coordinate permutation with zero translation (allowing an independent output constant). Thus affine normalization cannot generally turn the mixed-drift cases into zero-drift or all-one-drift cases. This statement concerns the actual ANF and raw bits, not adding auxiliary inputs or general equisatisfiable rewriting.

## A simple port closure

Use the directed bipartite independent-set construction in paired-cubic-mod-three.md. At a logical vertex of degree d and outdegree o choose t≥max(1,d−2,o) with t+2−d+o even, minimal under those conditions. The t-triple path of internal clean edges has at least one free port on every triple. Reserve its o outgoing arc ports on DISTINCT triples, then allocate the other d−o logical incidences from the remaining t+2−o ports. Arc endpoints at the source have drift one; all other base ports have zero drift. The base graph stays simple for a simple bipartite source graph. Its logical signs and component equalities are as in the companion proof.

For each unused base port add a fresh copy of K₄ with one edge subdivided. The four original vertices and the subdivider are five new triple blocks. Couple the unused port to the subdivider by a clean edge, using its last available port. The seven internal edges of this graph are assigned one distinct raw port per incidence. Designate one nonbridge internal edge mixed with drift at just one endpoint; all others are clean. For example take an edge of a triangle not incident to the subdivider. Removing this mixed edge leaves the gadget connected, so all five latent spins agree with the attached base spin. Modulo three the seven internal and one external link give the constant factor (−1)^8, and the designated source drift contributes (−1)^s. Every intrinsic spin-one coefficient −2 is one modulo three. Thus the gadget has exactly the required sign (−1)^s. This again cancels all vertex-dependent unary factors by the chosen parity. Each gadget uses at most one drifted raw bit in a triple, and every link is clean or mixed. All interactions in the joined graph are between distinct blocks, with no repeated pair. Size remains linear in the input graph's vertices and edges. The reduction and modulo-three membership follow from the companion proof, credited there to Faben for the hardness of the source.

## Rigidity of raw affine changes

The third finite difference is the direct sum of the three-dimensional volume tensors on the coordinate triples. Its center is the algebra of independent scalar matrices on these blocks. To verify this, moving a center map between an argument in one block and two in another forces all off-block components to zero. Within one block T(Au,u,w)=T(u,u,Aw)=0 for all w forces Au proportional to u, hence A scalar. The primitive idempotents of this algebra are precisely the individual block projections, distinguished intrinsically as its nonzero idempotents that do not split as a sum of two nonzero orthogonal ones. Under any invertible linear change the center conjugates. Therefore if the transformed cubic terms form a perfect matching, the linear part carries the original triple spaces to the new ones, up to permutation. This applies to all invertible affine changes since translation leaves the top tensor fixed. [Center decomposition background](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07915)

After aligning these block permutations, consider the cross-quadratic coefficient matrix between two blocks. Cubics and lower terms within a block can change only within that block. Every nonzero cross matrix remains rank one under independent invertible within-block changes, which may introduce additional nonzero entries but cannot annihilate the matrix. Each of the original 3m/2 distinct block pairs must therefore contribute at least one quadratic monomial in the transformed ANF. A perfect quadratic matching has exactly 3m/2 monomials total. Each original cross matrix must have exactly one nonzero entry and there are no intra-block quadratics in the transformed polynomial. A rank-one outer product of two nonzero binary vectors has a single nonzero entry exactly when both vectors have weight one. Hence at each block all three original coordinate forms must map to singleton coordinate forms, since each participates in its own edge to a different neighbor. The linear part is a permutation at each block.

After that permutation, translating the three coordinates of a triple by c₁,c₂,c₃ creates the quadratic terms with coefficients c₁,c₂,c₃ on the complementary pairs. No between-block quadratic or linear term can create an intra-block quadratic under translation. The absence of intra-block quadratics forces all three constants to vanish in every block. The remaining linear drift is carried just by coordinate permutation. In particular the property of having a drift on a link (and how many) is invariant. The hard scalar tasks here are gap residues of a supplied circuit; the rigidity statement alone is no lower bound on an unrestricted SAT solver.
