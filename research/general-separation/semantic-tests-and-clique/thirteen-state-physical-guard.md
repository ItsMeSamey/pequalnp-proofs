# Abstract thirteen-state tables and physical guard caps

A physical box is any finite undirected graph with two terminals a,b, private vertices, and arbitrary constant prescribed degree bits. At a selected private vertex its neighbor sum must equal its prescribed bit. A terminal defect is its neighbor sum plus its prescribed bit, whether selected or unselected. The raw table records all attainable occupancies and defects.

The abstract table omitting exactly (a,b,e,f)=(1,0,1,0),(1,0,1,1),(1,1,1,0) has thirteen states. Give each original mixed graph vertex its own copy, with endpoint a and private head b. Set b's exterior drift to zero and keep both source readouts and all original symmetric affine drifts on the a sites. A selected a with b=0 has e=0. At a=b=1 the guard on b forces f=0, again giving e=0. Thus the original selected-site guard holds at a. Every solution lifts by b=0 and e=f=0. Two readouts on unrestricted abstract thirteen-state tables therefore contain the generic native three-pin graph problem, even on overlap matchings, via the [native-to-mixed reduction](three-pin-even-reductions.md).

If the defect e of a physical box is fixed at occupancy (1,0), its defect fiber at occupancy (1,1) is an affine subspace of the binary plane. Terminal constants and a direct terminal edge just translate each fiber.

Indeed, let u,v be the two neighborhood vectors on a connected component of the private graph, with prescribed bits d. At occupancy (1,0) the prescription there is d+u. Components vary independently and contain the empty legal support. Thus u pairs to zero with every legal private support. If u is nonzero the [connected one-mark span theorem](symmetric-single-row-p3.md) gives d+u=u, hence d=0. At occupancy (1,1) the prescription is now u+v and handshaking gives (u+v)·x=0. The two contributions then lie on the diagonal line and include zero. Components with u=0 contribute only on the vertical line and also include zero. Each of these subsets of a binary line is a subspace. Translating the sum yields the stated affine fiber. The proof works for arbitrarily many private vertices.

In particular the proposed abstract table, with three points at (1,1), has no physical realization. In fact, for a strict guard at a=1 formed by privatizing b, b=0 leaves its defect unchecked and hence e must already be fixed at (1,0), using two omissions. At physical table size thirteen or above the (1,1) fiber must then have at least three points and must be full by the theorem. At b=1 any affine prescription on b selects one defect coordinate, leaving both values of e possible. Affine shifts of either defect depending on a,b only translate a fiber. This upper bound concerns a single cap with arbitrary private graph and one private terminal b.

Five sites attain twelve states. Take adjacency masks (4,24,1,2,2), prescribed-bit mask 16, terminals 0 and 1. At the four occupancies of integer masks 0,1,2,3 the allowed defect masks e+2f are respectively {0,1,2,3}, {0,2}, {0,1,2,3}, {0,2}. Every selected a has defect zero. The [native checker](physical_tail_fiber.py) provides representatives for every row. Thus mixed symmetric guards with two readouts embed even on disjoint copies of this twelve-state physical box.
