# Linear-wire tensors for planar switching connectivity

A finite undirected planar multigraph has two terminals s,t. Each edge is enabled by one input literal or a constant. Variables may repeat without restriction. Consider either the two-terminal predicate or the predicate that the enabled spanning subgraph on all the vertices is connected. For both there is a uniformly constructible bounded-arity Boolean tensor with O(m+v) wires including its edge-test ports. For every fixed edge pattern its contraction is the indicator over the integers, and therefore modulo every power of two.

The two-terminal loop observable has direct prior in Cardy, who identifies connection across the boundaries of an annulus by suppressing winding loops. The planar random-cluster to vertex-model correspondence goes back to Baxter, Kelland and Wu. We give finite, pointwise tensor implementations, including a denominator-free three-state specialization at component weight zero, and derive the restricted representation consequence. No continuum statement is used. [Cardy, sections 2 and 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math-ph/0604043) · [Baxter, Kelland and Wu](https://doi.org/10.1088/0305-4470/9/3/009)

## Boundary curves and local data

Put a small closed polygonal disk about each vertex and a narrow strip along each enabled edge. Distinct strips are disjoint outside the disks. The connected components of their union, including isolated disks, are exactly the enabled graph components. Its boundary is a disjoint family of simple polygonal loops. Choose p_s,p_t in the interiors of the original terminal disks. Their disks belong to the same component precisely when no loop separates the points. The regions of the complement of the boundary curves are determined by which side of each curve contains the point. Distinct black interiors are distinct such regions.

Delete self-loops, which affect neither predicate. Subdivide parallel edges for a simple straight-line grid drawing using O(m+v) pieces, treating subdivision points only as bends of each original strip. In particular they do not become isolated vertex disks on disabled edges. Choose disjoint small rational squares about original vertices and thin polygonal strips along edges, each with its own attachment interval at each end. Polynomial-bit clearances suffice: coordinates of the underlying grid drawing have polynomial bit size, and distances between disjoint straight segments and directions at incidences have polynomial-bit determinant bounds. Generic perturbations avoid square corners, collinear reversals and coincident attachments. Each edge end creates a constant number of corners and attachment interval ends. In total there are O(m+v) possible boundary pieces and corners, including subdivisions of square sides between consecutive attachments. [de Fraysseix, Pach and Pollack](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02122694)

A disabled edge keeps the two attachment intervals on the disk boundaries. An enabled edge replaces them by the two sides of its strip. Constant-arity local switches enforce these transitions on the segment states, with one dummy state on inactive pieces. Independent states run around exactly the resulting loops. Large degree disks are represented by segmented polygon boundaries, not high-degree tensors. Finite states have constant-bit codes with invalid codes rejected. For an oriented loop let R be its signed rotation (+1 counterclockwise, −1 clockwise), and W_p its winding about p. The local integer contributions to R count signed crossings of a fixed generic tangent direction during each corner's principal turn. Those to W_p are signed intersections with a generic ray from p to infinity. Avoid all potential vertices, corners and parallel directions. Each possible straight segment meets each ray at most once. Signs can be precomputed by rational comparisons in polynomial time.

## Two prescribed terminals

Let ζ be a primitive twelfth root, z=ζ² and b=ζ. Give each boundary loop either orientation. With windings W_s,W_t about the marked points assign weight

    z^(R−2W_t) b^(W_s−W_t).

For a fixed loop put ε=R. Then W_p equals ε inside the loop and zero outside. If neither point is inside, the weight is z^ε; if both are inside it is z^(−ε). The two orientations sum to z+z^(−1)=1 in both cases. If s alone is inside, the weight is (zb)^ε; if t alone is inside it is (zb)^(−ε). Their sum is i−i=0. Orientations on distinct loops are independent. The product of these sums is the desired indicator. The correction −2W_t accounts for the changed bounded side when t is taken to infinity.

For ordinary integer or modular tensors, accumulate the sum of all active local ζ exponents in a twelve-state chain modulo 12 starting at 0. At its last state j output the constant coefficient of X^j modulo X^4−X²+1. Linearity extracts the coefficient of 1 of the cyclotomic sum, already equal to the Boolean value. All entries have bounded size and no complex entries are required. [Two-terminal checks](plane_medial_loop_check.py)

## All vertices in one component

Orient the boundary canonically with the black neighborhood on its left. Fix any vertex point p_t. Taking p_t as infinity swaps which side of an enclosing loop is bounded, so its corrected rotation is R'=R−2W_t. The one unbounded black component has only boundaries with R'=−1. Each other black component has one exterior boundary with R'=+1, and any holes have R'=−1. Thus exactly κ−1 loops have R'=+1 for κ connected components. This argument includes all nested loops and isolated disks.

For integer u take a companion matrix A of X³−uX²+X−1. Its determinant is 1, trace A=u and trace A^{-1}=1. Carry a three-state index around each canonically oriented loop with local matrix A^e, where e is the local turn contribution minus twice the local crossing contribution for p_t. The trace around a loop is trace(A^{R'}). The full tensor evaluates to u^{κ−1}. At u=0 this is exactly the spanning connectivity bit with 0^0=1. All local entries then have constant bit size. The empty graph can be dealt with by the chosen connectivity convention. This gives an integer specialization of a planar random-cluster observable. [Spanning checks](spanning_switch_check.py)

## Pinning and the hierarchy consequence

Count graph vertices and edges in the size. Under pinning, contract fixed-enabled edges, delete fixed-disabled edges, and discard self-loops. Both predicates on the contracted graph equal their original values pointwise on the remaining inputs. For spanning connectivity, a disconnected graph of all possible remaining edges is identically false. Otherwise q remaining edge tests support at most q+1 vertex classes. For the two-terminal predicate, merged terminals give true, disconnected support terminals give false, and other support components can be discarded. These graph operations preserve planarity. Each residual tensor therefore has O(q+1) wires. Descriptions may include plane embeddings or be checked by polynomial-time planarity testing. Ordinary polynomial-size Boolean circuits evaluate both predicates by reachability.

For an absolute c>0 and each fixed d≥1 some language L_d in NP lacks, at infinitely many lengths M, an exact real SUM of at most M^d mixed atoms of the following sizes. Binary formulas, unrestricted cyclic deterministic one-bit query programs and both planar switching predicates may each have size at most M ceil(c sqrt(loglog M)). Acyclic nondeterministic one-bit query programs with arbitrary ambiguity may each have size at most M ceil(c sqrt(loglog M)/logloglog M) at large M. Size means leaves, query nodes or graph vertices plus edges respectively; use positive cutoffs at small M. Apply the per-model common-slot projection and heavy-bit branch in the [mixed hierarchy proof](mixed-dense-sparse-tensors.md). At most O(n+n C c sqrt(loglog M)) labeled edges per planar atom survive. Share the unbranched x bits across all factors with equality trees. The signed scalar graphs have the same wire, degree, precision and Boolean-value guarantees as the sparse factors in that proof, so its clock and quantifiers apply unchanged. The standard unrestricted P versus NP question remains open.
