# Independent audit of canonical two-cap host search

**Verdict.** The polynomial-time decision and original-label witness algorithm in degree-five-common-neighbor-host.md (1f5dcd2c05b4a5189adb91e35b25b72017311dd7df479f9a89b28e026b775ced) follows from the earlier minimal-cap classification on the stated host promise. Degree is at most five, and a pair with three common neighbors may not contain a degree-five vertex. Finite-window soundness for a syntactically valid paired/singleton-triangle word needs only the degree bound; the common-neighbor condition is used for completeness.

**Language and complexity.** Starting from a bounded, genuinely minimal, ordered terminal, apply only the two inverse cap rules. Both old child pins are mandatory and gain exactly two selected edges; in a triangle the new mandatory leaf forces its parent, which already sees that leaf and the retained old pin and thus forces the other old pin. Hence these are actual support bijections including for the entire word. The abstract pattern underlying any accepted word is minimal on abstract distinct vertices, even when the physical labels collide. A child pin used in an inverse cap must have degree ≤3 before its increment of two. A vertex can have at most two such uses. Once no longer an active pin, it cannot again become one. Therefore its creation, uses and retirement occupy at most four neighboring layers. Vertices never active except within a terminal or discarded local cap occupy only that bounded piece. All template edges span at most four introduction layers. The finite checker need store only current pin degrees, order, cap type, locally named endpoints/ages and a bounded terminal. Equivalently use reversal of this finite inverse grammar. New abstract labels are fresh by construction; only physical images can repeat. Each introduced physical site has a required earlier neighbor: parents of paired leaves; u adjacent to p in a triangle and z adjacent to u; and in the terminal a rooted order in each component from its designated pin(s). The first two initial pins require no predecessor and are distinct by input. One may bound the accepted word length by n noninitial steps without loss since a genuinely induced embedding uses at most n distinct sites. Enumeration of reachable finite annotations and at most 128 layers of host labels, with one predecessor per state (and depth if bounded), is deterministic n^O(1).

**True witness completeness.** Select an inclusion-minimal witness in the host. A triple toggle before any such toggle would occur in a graph made of physical vertex/edge deletions: its degree-five vertex must still have degree five in the host, and its three shared neighbors remain physically shared. This contradicts the promise, so only the two enumerated caps and an ordinary terminal are needed. Deleted pin edges do not accumulate inside the surviving graph because at least one end of the currently erased pin pair disappears at the next reduction. The intended physical adjacency, including the current erased edge on restoring a child, is therefore determined by bounded annotations.

**Why a remotely forgotten edge can be repaired.** Scan the complete abstract word mapped to original sites and choose the first bad introduced site X. A far repetition is impossible: a required earlier neighbor U would already have an unexpected physical edge to the old occurrence of X, since that occurrence and U are separated by more than four layers and the earlier prefix was clean. Recent repetitions and all missing required edges are tested locally. Choose the earliest *deletion* step i among earlier unintended neighbors. Every available older remote introduced site has such a deletion: until the terminal a presented site is either removed immediately or one of the two active pins. Its lifetime is at most four layers; oldest untested neighbor precedes X by ≥127 layers. All sites of the repair within graph distance four of the cap have already been introduced in the clean prefix (≤16 additional layers along these edges); U is at most four layers before X and has no edge to those protected sites or prior removed sites. Work in the abstract residual rather than assuming minimality of a corrupted physical suffix.

If X hits the removed leaf p, the claw solves when it also hits q. Otherwise component Q of q after deleting p and its parent u has at least |H|−3 sites. Any other component only attaches at u. If its contribution there is even it can be omitted; if odd it can be replaced by one neighbor. Minimality forces a singleton leaf, and there is at most one. Choose a connected five-site set in Q from q. Four other sites compete with at most three available X-neighbor slots (p,U already adjacent). A shortest path in that set from q to a different X-nonneighbor is induced; every interior site has an X edge, making its two path edges odd in total. Both ends have odd degree. Select p and X, then toggle U solely to correct X.

Otherwise the first contact is the parent u of a triangle. If X misses q use its claw with u,p,q. If X hits q, the component of q after removing p,u and the common site v has at least |H|−4 sites: off-q components attach only at v because p,u have no other neighbors, and the same single-leaf replacement applies. Grow a connected four-site set in this Q. Only two further X neighbors are available, since u,q,U already meet X. The induced shortest path from q to another X-nonneighbor again works; q now sees u,X and its path neighbor, u sees p,q,X, p sees u, and optionally U corrects X. A physical edge between the two current pins erased in the abstract residual is restored only when undoing the preceding exact cap, along with its two balancing incidences.

All original earlier deletions are lifted: auxiliary path sites are original clean-prefix sites in the residual; X has no unexpected contact with any earlier deleted site by the earliest-deletion choice; U has none by clean prefix and layer separation. The derived support keeps the specified original pins. This is an actual rerouting argument rather than a claim that forgotten edges do not exist.

**Physical tests separate from proof.** canonical_repair.py constructs genuine 150- and 151-cap paired/triangle templates on 308 and 310 sites, introduces contacts ≥130 canonical layers apart under the degree-five cap, runs the four repairs and recomputes each selected degree from the original augmented adjacency after undoing all earlier caps. It checks 646 and 1158 labeled configurations, including the triangle case X meeting u and q. The finite runs test particular inputs; the general proof is the first-conflict argument above. The published supplied-layout NO counterexample has a noncanonical interleaving, while its 16-site seed fits entirely in a single 128-layer window. Neither refutes this theorem.
