# A deterministic host algorithm when degree-five vertices have limited common neighbors

Let G be a finite simple graph of maximum degree five. Assume every pair of distinct sites having at least three common neighbors consists of vertices of degrees at most four. Given two distinct original pins, a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm returns an induced all-odd support through them or decides none exists. This includes every maximum-degree-five graph with no K₂,₃ subgraph. One can check the hypothesis by listing length-two walks and counting their distinct middle vertices for each pair.

## A finite presentation

In the [cap classification](degree-five-leaf-cap-pathwidth.md), a triple toggle needs two sites sharing three neighbors with at least one site of degree five. Before the first triple toggle every graph change is a deletion of sites or edges. Thus no minimal support in the promised host needs such a move. Only paired and singleton-common-neighbor triangle reductions precede the terminal of at most eight sites. Each nonterminal reduction erases just the edge joining the two next pins. At least one of these pins disappears at the next reduction. Consequently the intended residual at any depth is the physical induced pattern on its live labels with just its current pin edge erased, except at the root.

Take as terminals the constant finite list of ordered rooted graphs on at most eight sites passing the literal degree and minimality tests. In reverse, require the two existing pins y,z to be nonadjacent and of degrees at most three. A paired extension inserts yz and two new vertices, one leaf at each old pin, and names the new leaves. A triangle extension inserts yz and two new sites u,p with edges uy,uz,up. The new pins are p and either designated old pin y. Both operations raise the degrees of both old pins by two. The forcing bijections prove that every generated abstract pattern is inclusion-minimal. A generated pattern violating the host promise causes no difficulty: a genuinely induced embedding would still provide a witness.

Present a pattern from its root toward its terminal. Start with the ordered pins and expose two new names at each cap. At a paired step introduce the two parents, each adjacent to an old pin and joined to the other parent. At a triangle introduce u and then z, emitting the four cap edges given above. At the terminal introduce the remaining sites in an order in which each has an earlier neighbor. Such an order exists since each terminal component contains a pin. The only terminal edge on already introduced sites can concern the two current pins, whose physical adjacency is already known after a cap.

Legality of the abstract word uses finite state. Guess the two residual pin degrees and order at every step and verify the inverse update between adjacent annotations. Require a terminal consistent with the last annotation, including nonadjacency of its two pins after a cap. Once a name ceases to be active it cannot recur in the abstract presentation. Each use of a site as an inner pin adds two to its physical degree, so it participates in at most two such uses. Including introduction and deletion, appearances of each name in cap bags lie in at most four consecutive layers. Every template edge has endpoints introduced within four layers, and current pins have ages at most four. The same bounds apply to the single final layer of at most eight sites.

## Host reachability

Map the initial pins to their given original labels. Guess an image for each new name and remember the last 128 layers with their active pins and annotations. Require distinct labels and exactly the prescribed physical adjacencies throughout this window, also within a partially exposed layer. Expected edges between a new site and earlier sites are known at introduction from the cap or terminal. Test acceptance by ordinary directed reachability and store one predecessor per reached state. There are constantly many possible abstract annotations per layer and constantly many retained sites. Thus a uniform n to an absolute constant power bounds time and space, including graph indexing. Every genuine embedding from the classification passes these tests.

## The first remote conflict

Read a reconstructed accepting walk in presentation order. A globally injective induced walk solves the instance. Otherwise take the first new name X that violates either condition. Choose a previously presented template neighbor U of X, within four layers. If X repeated a remote label, U would already have had an unexpected edge to that label across more than four layers in the clean prefix. Recent repeats and missing required edges are ruled out by the window. Thus X is fresh and has at least one spurious physical edge to an earlier site.

Choose such a neighbor with earliest cap deletion index i. It was introduced at least 127 layers before X, and its deletion follows within four layers, so many subsequent template layers remain. No earlier deleted site has an unintended edge to X. Work in the abstract residual H at i, including the current pin-edge erasure when needed. This H is minimal and connected with more than eight sites: a disconnected minimal support has just two edges. A connected set grown from a current pin within radius four lies in the clean prefix since each physical edge spans at most four layers. U is remote from the current cap, every such protected site, and all earlier deleted sites. No incidences with U among them are possible by the clean-prefix test and the span bound. These arguments apply to the abstract minimal template even when the whole mapped walk fails to be an induced copy.

If X meets a pinned leaf p being removed now, let u be its unique neighbor in H and q the other pin. This handles either removed leaf of a paired cap. If X meets q, take the claw on p,q,X,U centered at X. Otherwise consider the component Q of q in H minus p,u. Any other component has only u as a contact with the rest. Such a component contains no pin. It can be omitted if it sends even parity to u, and replaced by one neighbor of u if it sends odd parity. Minimality implies it is a single leaf. Two such leaves could be removed together. Hence Q contains at least |H|−3 sites. Grow a connected set of five in Q starting at q. Since X already sees p,U, at most three of the four other sites meet X. Take a shortest path within the set from q to a different nonneighbor of X. Its internal sites all see X and the path is induced. Keep the path and p,X. Vertex p sees only X, and every path site has odd degree. Include U exactly when X otherwise has even degree.

The remaining possibility is a triangle step where X meets the removed parent u and misses its removed pinned leaf p. The neighbors of u in H are p,q,v, with singleton common neighbor v of u,q. If X misses q, the claw on u,p,q,X suffices. Otherwise let Q be the component of q in H minus p,u,v. Any other component attaches to the rest only through v, since p only sees u and u has no other neighbor than p,q,v. The previous thinning argument leaves at most one site outside Q and p,u,v. Grow a connected four-site set from q in Q. Vertex X sees u,q,U, so some other site in the set misses X. Take a shortest path from q to a different nonneighbor, whose internal sites all meet X. Keep p,u,q,X and the path. Now p sees u, u sees p,q,X, and q sees u,X and its single path neighbor. The other path sites have odd degree, and U can again correct X. No path auxiliary sees p or u. These cases exhaust the two sites removed at the earliest affected cap.

Undo the i earlier reductions on their recorded original labels. On the clean prefix their support bijections apply to any selected subset with the child pins. X,U have no extra incidences to earlier removed sites by the earliest-deletion choice and the clean prefix. Thus the lift restores all original guards, including each earlier physical pin edge. Directly validate the final word. This procedure can change earlier choices; preserving only an aggregate future parity would not certify the proposed full walk. Previous one-bag counterexamples do not give a 128-layer canonical presentation satisfying the hypotheses. The algorithm leaves open degree-five hosts containing a degree-five site sharing three neighbors with another vertex, where triangle triple-toggle steps need additional analysis.
