# Disjoint pin neighborhoods in a minimal odd support

Let H be a finite simple inclusion-minimal induced all-odd support through two nonadjacent pins p,q. If every degree in H is three or five, then the pins have a common neighbor.

Suppose their neighborhoods A and B are disjoint. The pins and their neighborhoods already contain at least eight sites. Every a in A must meet every b in B, since otherwise p,a,q,b induce two disjoint edges. Both odd neighborhood sizes are at least three and at most four, since every vertex on the opposite shore needs an edge to its pin as well. Thus each size is three. Each of the six sites has degree five since it already sees its pin and the opposite triple. Remove the two pins and all nine cross edges. The six marked sites now have degree one and every unmarked site retains its original degree three or five. All sites together satisfy all-odd and the two readouts requiring an odd number selected from each marked triple. Any satisfying word lifts by restoring the two pins: each selected mark gains one pin and an odd number of opposite marks, and both pins see an odd number of marks. A proper word remains proper. Hence no proper word can satisfy these requirements.

Consider any such residual graph with two disjoint triples of marked leaves, all other degrees three or five, and no proper satisfying word. An isolated edge whose two marks have the same color can be deleted in full. One with opposite colors is by itself a proper satisfying word. Thus all parents are unmarked. Two same-color leaves cannot share a parent, since they could be deleted together. A common parent of opposite colors has another neighbor since its degree is at least three. That neighbor is unmarked because the common parent already has one marked leaf per color and cannot have two of either color. Those two leaves, their parent and its extra neighbor induce a proper satisfying claw. Thus the six parents are distinct. Any nonadjacent pair of opposite-color parents gives a proper satisfying word on two disjoint leaf edges. Hence the new triples of parents also form a complete bipartite graph between them. Each parent has degree five because it sees its own leaf and all three opposite parents.

Delete the old leaves and erase the nine new cross edges, leaving their parents as the two marked triples. These newly marked vertices have degree one, and every other degree is unchanged. A word with odd occupancy in each new triple lifts by selecting each old leaf exactly when its parent is selected. Each selected parent regains one leaf and an odd number of opposite selected parents. The old readouts hold and a proper word stays proper. The smaller graph therefore satisfies the same assumptions, including absence of a proper satisfying word. Repetition removes six vertices at every step, which is impossible in a finite graph.

All degree assumptions refer to the minimal selected support. No assumption on the degree of vertices outside it or on its connectivity after deletions is needed.
