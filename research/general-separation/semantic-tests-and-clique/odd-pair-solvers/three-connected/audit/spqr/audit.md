# Two-boundary projection and the SPQR leaf argument

Source dd435f5a. The projection argument is sound when neither pin, exactly one pin, or both pins are interface vertices.

Form K on E∪I, omitting ab. Exterior vertices have all their original neighbors in K and have odd degree ≥3. Fix columns at both pins and leave the two boundary rows unchecked; then |E|=|V(K)−P| independently of overlap. A binary vector on the remaining columns means delete its support X. To keep each surviving exterior vertex odd requires even adjacency to X only at rows outside X. A diagonal modification on common row/column labels enforces an unnecessarily stronger linear system but changes no surviving row equation. Any nonzero null vector gives a strict thinning because X is removed on the K side, where no reintroduced W vertex can replace it.

For nonsingular M let j be a nonpin exterior vertex. A row j of the inverse which vanished on all nonpin exterior columns would express the row unit at j as a sum of exterior-pin rows of M. With both pins at I there are no such rows. With one exterior pin its row has weight ≥2: removal of the other pin's column loses at most one neighbor and the pin has no loop. With two exterior pins either individual row still has weight ≥2; their sum has even weight, since deleting their mutual edge loses equally from both rows. Thus the inverse submatrix on the common labels has no zero row. A shortest directed cycle has no internal chord (including a loop in a longer cycle). Its principal inverse submatrix is a permutation with eigenvalue 1, so the corresponding diagonal toggle in M is singular by the determinant lemma. This proves existence without bounding the degree from above.

If E has one vertex it has at most two neighbors, impossible. With |E|=2 and the minimum degree constraint, each exterior vertex meets both ports and the other exterior vertex. Deleting either one or both available ports is not always allowed if pinned, but the matrix argument still works whenever U is nonempty. When U is empty the two exterior vertices are exactly the pins: deleting both boundary vertices leaves the pin edge. An independent direct exhaustive deletion search on all simple K with up to four exterior sites verified 4,871 valid graph/pin placements (6 + 140 + 4725), including both-, one-, and zero-boundary-pin cases. No three-connectivity assumption was made in this check.

To lift, pass to the table the actual occupancy at a,b and the incoming parity from *surviving E vertices*. Include a real ab only on the W side; include no physical ab on either side when virtual only. Changing the ab bit simply permutes drift entries for a,b both occupied and has no effect on occupied checks otherwise. Hence universality is invariant under this bit toggle. Changed occupancy on either boundary cannot affect an outside retained pin except by an exterior row equation, which the projection already checked. Direct subset enumeration verified both ab states on the actual order-six and order-eleven examples.

For a reduced Q-free SPQR decomposition, a leaf S skeleton has a private cycle vertex of physical degree two. A leaf P skeleton has at least two parallel real edges. Neither can occur in a simple biconnected all-odd support. A leaf R has only its one tree incidence virtual: every edge incident to a private vertex is real, and such a vertex has no occurrence on another skeleton. Any further real ab can only add the same edge suppressed in forming the simple augmented torso. Another leaf exists in a nonsingleton tree and is also R, hence provides a private vertex strictly across the separation. Biconnectivity gives each boundary endpoint an exterior neighbor, paying for the possible added ab when bounding Δ(J)≤5. Uniqueness of the full selected interior at fixed full-port parities follows by physical substitution with all E retained; toggling ab changes both boundary parities equally in both selections. Thus the enumerated torso alternatives apply. When their tables are all universal, no R leaf can be pin-free, and disjoint private interiors give at most two leaves. A finite tree with at most two leaves is a path.

The projection and tree reasoning are independent of the distinct mixed-torso finite classification. The two explicit physical table checks do not replace the released all-presentations census for that premise. The resulting path theorem concerns the reduced SPQR tree of the selected support; it does not give a bound on internal skeleton size.
