# Where a large minimal odd support can lie

Let H be an inclusion-minimal induced all-odd support through distinct physical pins p,q. If H is disconnected, every component contains a pin, since otherwise that component can be deleted. Then each of the two components must be one edge: choose a neighbor of its pin and keep just those two vertices.

For connected H use the block-cut tree, counting bridges as two-vertex blocks. For a leaf block with attachment v and no pin in its interior, let t be the parity of the number of interior neighbors of v. If t=0 delete the interior. If t=1 keep just one interior neighbor of v. Its degree becomes one, the contribution to v is unchanged, and the interior has no edges elsewhere. Therefore every such block is a pendant edge. The same argument applies to an entire pin-free component of H−v. At most one such component can hang at a fixed v, since two singletons could be deleted together.

Place each noncut pin at its unique block node and each cut-vertex pin at its own articulation node. Take the tree path between the two placements, allowing a path of length zero. At a route articulation, every off-route branch belongs to a pin-free component of H minus that articulation. If a branch diverges at a route block instead, its first off-route tree vertex is an articulation belonging to that block; each continuation away from the block belongs to a pin-free component at that articulation. Thus all blocks off the route are single edges to leaves. The [optional-leaf theorem](optional-leaf-pin-bound.md) bounds the total number of unpinned leaves by two even when their parents differ.

Consequently pw(H) ≤ 3 + max_B pw(B), over the blocks B. Concatenate path layouts of the blocks on the route, holding the at most two sites shared with preceding or following blocks throughout the corresponding segment. Handle each off-route leaf at an occurrence of its parent by duplicating the bag and adding that leaf alone. This covers each edge and gives an interval per vertex. Hence unbounded pathwidth requires unbounded pathwidth within individual biconnected blocks of the actual supports.

## Composition at two original sites

Suppose W is disjoint from both pins and all its outside neighbors belong to two sites a,b. For each occupancy of (a,b) record the achievable pairs of degree contributions at the occupied boundary sites and one actual realizing subset of W per pair. All selected interior sites must have odd degrees using the indicated occupancies. For disjoint attachments with no edges between their interiors, hold the *same* occupancies, XOR their contributions, and add the physical edges on or beyond the boundary once. This tests the original rows at every retained vertex. Union of the stored representatives with the selected retained sites gives a native witness. With no occupied boundary site use the empty interior. With only a occupied, zero is achievable by the empty set and one is possible exactly if a has a neighbor in W (take that neighbor alone). For both sites occupied, the zero vector again comes from the empty set. Computing contribution (1,1) for two occupied nonadjacent sites in the general case is precisely the original two-pin problem on the limb plus those sites. This composition rule presupposes we can obtain the entries.

Suppose now the actual support H is biconnected with maximum degree at most five, and a,b separate W from a nonempty other side. Suppose J=H[W∪{a,b}]+ab is 3-connected, adding ab only if absent. Biconnectivity makes each endpoint incident to the other side, so J has maximum degree at most five. Let σ be the degree parities at a,b in full J. Its full set is the unique word containing both endpoints with those boundary parities and odd interior degrees. Any proper alternative would thin H upon reinsertion with its original exterior: the boundary edge has the same contribution for both words, even if virtual.

If σ=(1,1), ab alone realizes it, which is impossible. If σ=(0,0), delete ab, making both endpoint degrees odd. Both pin degrees are now three since they were even and between three and five. Every other site has odd degree. If the order exceeds four, any exclusive a-neighbor missing an exclusive b-neighbor gives a proper all-odd pair of edges. Therefore all exclusive cross edges exist. There are three internally vertex-disjoint a–b paths: one via each common neighbor and one through each matched pair of exclusive neighbors. Thus J−ab remains connected after any two deletions. If neither endpoint is deleted, ab can be replaced by a surviving path; if either is deleted, the edge is immaterial. J−ab is genuinely 3-connected. The [six/eight theorem](degree-five-three-connected-shells.md) applies to its actual minimal full support. All vertices lie in the two pin neighborhoods together with the pins, and there is at least one common neighbor. These pin degrees are three, so the order is at most seven and the only possible value is six. At order four the only 3-connected J is K₄, which cannot have σ=(0,0). This exact reduction concerns a selected shore with the stipulated connectivity of J. Unequal parities admit larger examples, and the bound does not compute the response table of an arbitrary large host torso.
