# Biconnected minimal two-pin odd supports of degree at most five

Every simple biconnected inclusion-minimal two-pin all-odd support H of maximum degree five, on at least three vertices, is 3-connected. Consequently, the complete list consists of the same two six-vertex and nine eight-vertex unordered pin types as in the [sharp 3-connected classification](degree-five-three-connected-shells.md).

Indeed, suppose the reduced SPQR tree has more than one node, omitting Q leaves and leaving the true edges in the skeletons. By the [universal two-port theorem](universal-pair-shore-spqr-path.md), every leaf is an R leaf and contains one of the two pins strictly in its exclusive interior. There must therefore be two different leaves, with one pin exclusive to each. A pin has odd degree at least three in biconnected H, and its leaf has only two contact sites. Thus it has a neighbor in the same exclusive interior. There are no edges between the two leaf interiors; each can meet the rest only through its own contact sites, and a site exclusive to another leaf cannot be such a contact site. Select each pin and its chosen private neighbor. These four original sites induce two disjoint edges, a valid odd witness. It is proper because H is connected. This contradiction makes the reduced SPQR tree a single node. It cannot be a bond in a simple graph with at least three sites, or a cycle since all degrees are odd. It is therefore an R node and H itself is 3-connected.

The [original-host finder](three_connected_odd_pair.py) thus also decides existence of a biconnected inclusion-minimal witness of order at least three with maximum induced degree five, returning literal vertex labels. A biconnected host may contain only disconnected or articulated minimal witnesses. A failed search retains UNKNOWN for unrestricted two-pin existence. Conventions that count a single edge as a block add that immediate case separately.

More generally, in any inclusion-minimal two-pin odd support different from the two-edge disconnected witness, a vertex separator disjoint from the pins that places them in different components has size at least the smaller of their physical degrees. If both degrees exceed the separator size, each pin has a neighbor in its own component and their two edges form the same four-site contradiction. This observation also applies when the whole support has articulations, without changing the scope of the classification.
