# A mixed two-site parity limb with an added separator edge

Consider a simple limb with required boundary sites p,q. Every selected interior vertex must have odd induced degree, and the two boundary degree parities are specified. Assume the full limb is inclusion-minimal for these requirements, and adding the edge pq if absent gives a three-connected torso. Internal degrees are at most five and each boundary site has at most five actual neighbors in the interior. The following statements concern mixed boundary requirements. Adding or removing pq toggles both boundary parities on every feasible word; thus the required contributions from interior vertices are invariant. Name the pins so that these contributions are one at p and zero at q.

An interior neighbor of p alone gives a feasible word on three sites. Thus in a larger minimal limb there are only three classes: C sees both pins, B sees q alone, and D sees neither. Both |C| and |B| are odd by the two full boundary requirements. Also |D| is odd by handshaking on the full limb. Since p has degree at least three in the augmented torso, |C|≥2. Since |C|+|B|≤5 and this sum is even, |C|=3 and B={b}. Every feasible word selects b and an odd number of C.

For c∈C and d∈D, denote cb, bd and cd by their zero-one edge indicators. The word consisting of the two boundary sites and c,b,d is feasible precisely when the triple of indicators is (0,0,1) or (1,1,0). Indeed the required parities of the internal edges on c,b,d are one, zero and one respectively. Therefore in every minimal limb larger than five, writing S=N_D(b), either cb=1 and S⊆N_D(c), or cb=0 and N_D(c)⊆S. If b meets none of C and has degree three, |S|=2 and no edge joins the pins or C to D outside S. Three-connectivity forces D=S, contrary to its odd cardinality.

## Exact change to ordinary odd-degree witnesses

Delete p and q, insert a new compulsory site s adjacent exactly to C, make b compulsory, and toggle the three potential edges from b to C. Keep all other interior adjacencies. This ordinary graph requires every selected site to have odd degree. Its witnesses correspond bijectively to the mixed limb's witnesses by replacing s with p,q. In the original b was forced; in the new graph the row of s requires odd occupancy of C. At any selected c the two old pin incidences cancel, and the new incidence with s cancels the changed edge to the selected b. At b the old q incidence is replaced modulo two by toggling the edges to an odd number of selected C sites. All remaining sites have unchanged rows on the selection. Replacing the fixed boundary sites recovers a valid original witness, with the correct boundary parities whether pq was present or virtual. The map preserves strict containment.

The new degree of b is its old degree plus 2−2|N_C(b)|. It can be seven when b had degree five and missed C. The three C sites each retain their old degree or lose two. Even when all new degrees are at most five, three-connectivity of the rewritten graph requires separate verification before applying the three-connected ordinary classification. These statements give a reduction and an excluded boundary profile; they leave the general mixed limb order open.
