# Bounded order of three-connected minimal odd supports of maximum degree five

Let H be a finite simple 3-connected graph of maximum degree at most five, with distinct named vertices p,q. Suppose the full vertex set is all-odd and no proper vertex subset containing p and q induces all odd degrees. Then H has at most 30 vertices. This is an upper bound obtained without enumerating the final layer. Both the minimality test and the conclusion concern the literal support, which may sit inside a larger host.

Every vertex has degree three or five. Adjacent pins give a proper two-vertex witness. For nonadjacent pins put A=N(p)∖N(q), B=N(q)∖N(p) and C=N(p)∩N(q). The [disjoint-neighborhood lemma](five-disjoint-pin-neighborhoods.md) proves C nonempty. Every A–B edge must be present, since any nonedge yields two disjoint odd edges with the pins. A vertex of C has no neighbor outside the pins and their neighborhoods, since such a neighbor yields an odd claw with the pins. These obstructions apply because every graph under consideration of order greater than four has more vertices than the displayed witnesses. Any graph on at most four already satisfies the bound.

**Generate the initial graphs.** Use the two pins and A,B,C as the prefix. If this is the entire graph, its order is at most eleven. Otherwise enumerate c from 1 to 5, and let each of a,b be a nonnegative number of the form 3−c or 5−c, interchanging pins so that a≤b. For each admissible triple enumerate all simple edges among the three sets, starting from the required A–B edges. Skip degrees above five, require final degree three or five at each C site and reject a prefix that already contains an odd support through both pins. Only A and B sites with prefix degree below five may have future neighbors. There must be at least three of them: their deletion separates the two pins from the nonempty exterior. Likewise after deleting any at most two prefix sites, every surviving component must contain some surviving eligible site, since the exterior is still nonempty. Relabeling within A,B,C or swapping equal-size A,B along with the pins preserves extension. The [root enumerator](degree_five_common_core_census.py) gives 72 root orbits before the latter cut test and [60 afterwards](degree_five_common_core_census.json).

**Exact physical prefix table.** Process the remaining vertices in distance layers from this initial prefix. A vertex in the next nonempty layer can meet only the current last layer among processed vertices. List as ports all eligible sites there. For an ordered port set of size k, T[v,u] is the number, truncated at two, of pinned prefix selections whose port occupancy is u, whose selected internal sites are odd, and whose selected port parities become odd after adding the parity vector v. This table can be recomputed directly from the original graph. The ordering is shared by incoming parities, occupancy and current degrees. It records proper alternatives to the full prefix even when they give exactly the same external behavior.

Write the nonzero bitmasks t₁,…,t_l for the port neighborhoods of the next layer, sorted to remove only permutations of the new vertex labels. Repetitions are allowed. At each old port j require its present degree plus the number of masks containing j to be three or five. All incidences between old and new vertices are now fixed. Internal edges of the new layer run over all simple graphs subject to the degree cap. For occupied subset S of the new layer, the old prefix receives v=⊕_{i∈S}t_i. For each old u counted by T[v,u], each selected i in S receives parity t_i·u from the old prefix, plus its induced parity within the new layer. Future parity is required to correct precisely the even selected sites. Forget new vertices already at degree five, requiring that no correction be needed there, and sum the counts into cells at the remaining ports. Truncation at two commutes with this sum of nonnegative integer counts. These transitions give exactly the physical table, without an assumption about how vertices outside the prefix will later connect.

A nonterminal new layer has at least three sites, and at least three old ports meet it. At least three new sites must still have degree below five. Removing any such separating set of at most two would disconnect an earlier interior from a surviving exterior vertex. A pinned odd word anywhere inside the new prefix disproves minimality since the further layer is nonempty. Thus type t cannot occur if T[t,u] counts a u with t·u=1. Distinct vertices of types t,h cannot have edge bit e if T[t⊕h,u] counts a u with t·u=h·u=1⊕e. This permits repeated types and constrains only the pair under test.

Fix all later vertices occupied. With all new sites selected the old prefix receives the XOR of all types. Its full-port cell must count exactly one old word: the whole graph was all odd, and any other completion with the same u would leave outside parities unchanged. A second u differing from the full mask by a vector orthogonal to every t_i would also preserve every outside parity. After forming the new table with port degrees d_j its full-port cell at incoming v_j=1⊕(d_j mod 2) must likewise count one word. For a true all-odd graph this is the actual incoming parity. Surviving prefixes need not have genuine extensions.

The [nonterminal enumerator](degree_five_active_shells.py) retains every actual graph history. With option `--check-raw` it recomputes every cell of every child table by scanning the original prefix adjacency.

The last column uses prefix size plus the sum of 5−d_j over its ports. Each final vertex needs a distinct incidence from an old port, even when several sites have identical neighborhoods. This bound covers final layers of size one or two, for which nonterminal singleton and pair rejections would need modification. An [independent raw sweep](degree_five_raw_shell_check.py) builds the layer site by site and reproduces the same sequence of distinct tables.

The theorem concerns minimal supports that are themselves 3-connected. A 3-connected host can contain a witness of smaller connectivity. This result gives no bound for such a witness or for a 3-connected torso obtained by introducing virtual edges.

## Sharp terminal classification

In fact H has order six or eight and lies in N_H[p]∪N_H[q]. Up to isomorphism preserving the unordered pins there are two types of order six and nine of order eight. A 3-connected graph has at least four vertices. The only 3-connected graph on four vertices is K₄, whose adjacent pins give a smaller witness. For larger graphs the earlier reductions apply without exceptions.

First enumerate all closed initial graphs, omitting the exterior-port requirements and the rejection of a full prefix. Require degrees three or five, connectivity after each two-vertex deletion, and no odd word on any proper pin-containing subset. There are four labeled presentations of order six and 216 of order eight in the fixed pin-neighborhood sectors with a≤b. Every other order gives zero. Next take every one of the 60, 93, 100 and 32 physical prefixes listed above, and separately complete it by a nonempty final layer. A final layer of one or two sites may itself finish the unique full word. Apply singleton or pair rejection only when the word it exhibits is proper. Require no subsequent layer or outgoing ports. Finish all degrees at three or five and sum the actual parity tests over all old prefix responses and all final occupancy masks. Require the full word, exclude every proper alternative and check physical 3-connectivity. No nonempty exterior passed.

The [terminal sweep](degree_five_terminal_sweep.py) and [independent terminal check](degree_five_raw_terminal_check.py) agree. The first enumerates neighbor-type multisets and compatible internal edges. The second builds terminal vertices incrementally using literal mask tests. The two terminal implementations share the raw prefix generator. The capped-table program independently verifies those nonterminal transitions. The [canonical enumeration](degree_five_terminal_types.py) tries within-sector permutations and both pin orders. For a sharp example take the complement of an eight-cycle and pin consecutive cycle vertices. An odd word has even size. Its selected sites must therefore have even induced degrees in the cycle, forcing both neighbors at every selected end of a cycle edge. The pinned edge forces the whole cycle. The complement remains connected after two deletions: the six remaining vertices have minimum degree at least three.

On a host of maximum degree five, the union of the two named closed neighborhoods has size at most twelve. Enumerate pin-containing subsets there, checking induced odd degrees, 3-connectivity and absence of proper pinned odd subsets. A successful check returns original vertex labels. The work is constant after bounded-degree adjacency indexing. For a host with arbitrary degrees but a requested 3-connected minimal witness of maximum degree at most five, enumerate at most six additional sites from the same union and test the degree cap inside the subset. Host edges to outside do not affect any induced test. A failed search excludes the specified type of witness; other witnesses may remain.
