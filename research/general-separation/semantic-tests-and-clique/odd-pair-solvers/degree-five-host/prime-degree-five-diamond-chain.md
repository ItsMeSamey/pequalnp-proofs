# A module-prime family with unboundedly many degree-five sites

For every r≥2 there is a module-prime simple graph on 4r+4 sites of maximum degree five whose unique odd support through two named vertices is the full vertex set. Exactly r selected sites have degree five, spread across r blocks. The examples have pathwidth at most three and a reducible end. They refute a bounded-total-exception hypothesis, without settling bounded width.

Begin with two disjoint edges and require one leaf S,T of each, forcing the full graph. Add ST and fresh U,V with U adjacent to S,T,V, and require V,S. Then V forces U, and U sees V,S, so it forces T. Deleting U,V and ST recovers the old solution; on lifting, S,T each regain two selected incidences, U,V have degrees three and one. Now add VS and fresh leaves S',T' at V,S respectively, and require S',T'. Both old pins are forced and regain two selected neighbors. Repeat with S',T'. The two transformations are bijections, and every added edge was absent. In one round S grows from degree one to five, T to three; U,V finish at degree three. Thus the unique full word persists.

Block i is a diamond on S_i,T_i,U_i,V_i missing only T_i V_i. Consecutive diamonds are joined by the matching V_i S_(i+1), S_i T_(i+1). Initial leaves attach separately to S_1,T_1; terminal leaves attach separately to V_r,S_r. For r≥2 no site has two pendant neighbors. The core of diamonds is 2-connected (a single deletion leaves each block connected and retains a link between each consecutive pair). Codegree is at most two. Vertices of one block have no common outside neighbor, and within a diamond codegree is at most two. For two sites in consecutive blocks, each of their own blocks supplies at most one common neighbor and no other can supply any. For two nonconsecutive blocks there is at most one common neighbor in their sole possible intermediate block. Leaf pairs or pairs involving a leaf satisfy the same bound.

A proper module on at least three sites has an external neighbor by connectedness; by the codegree bound it has exactly one such neighbor a. Its size is at most five, and it is a union of components after deleting a. The remaining core vertices form part of a component on at least 4r−1>5 vertices, so none can lie in the module. At most one detached pendant leaf is available at a. There is no such module. There are no twins either: leaves have distinct neighbors, core degrees are at least three, every core vertex has at least two neighbors in its own block whereas another block's vertex has at most one there. Interblock edges lie in no triangle, precluding interblock true twins. Within a diamond the only nonadjacent pair T_i,V_i has different external neighbors; S_i differs in degree from every other block vertex and U_i has no external neighbor whereas T_i and V_i each have one. This proves full module primeness.

Take one bag per diamond, inserting the four endpoints of their two linking edges between consecutive block bags. A bag at each end contains its two leaves and their neighbors. This is a path decomposition with bags of size four. The interblock distance bound also rules out covering all exceptions by a fixed number of balls of bounded radius. The [independent finite checker](degree_five_periodic_prime.py) checks uniqueness up to r=4 and all distance-two pair closures through r=14.
