# Disjoint pin-neighborhood descent with unmarked leaves

This supplements the [one-leaf shell proof](single-leaf-high-pins-eight.md). At any step of the [two-triple descent](five-disjoint-pin-neighborhoods.md), a marked leaf may have an unmarked degree-one parent. The two sites then form an isolated edge. Choose any marked leaf of the opposite color and its parent. If its parent is unmarked, these two isolated-from-the-first-edge pairs induce two disjoint edges with precisely one mark of each color. This remains true when the second parent has degree greater than one, since no other neighbor of that parent is selected. The resulting word is proper because four other marked sites are omitted. If instead its parent is marked, that isolated marked–marked edge either has opposite colors and alone solves the instance, or has equal colors and can be deleted from the full word. These arguments apply symmetrically to either color, and exhaust possible isolated edges meeting a mark.

After those cases have been excluded, a shared unmarked parent of opposite colors has at least two neighbors and therefore at least three by oddness. Its third neighbor is unmarked, since any same-color pair sharing a parent can already be deleted together. A parent meeting its leaf and a triple of opposite-color parents has degree five. Thus every induction step used in disjoint-neighborhood descent remains valid with unmarked degrees one, three, or five. Strictly smaller witnesses lift exactly as in the original two-triple argument.
