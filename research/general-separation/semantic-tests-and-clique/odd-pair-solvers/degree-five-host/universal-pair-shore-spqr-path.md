# The SPQR tree of a biconnected minimal two-pin odd support of maximum degree five is a path

Let H be a finite simple biconnected inclusion-minimal all-odd support containing two distinct pins P. All its degrees are odd and at least three. Let W be a nonempty pin-free shore with boundary sites I={a,b} and E=V(H)∖(W∪I) nonempty. There are no W–E edges. For every occupancy of I and every incoming pair of parities from E, the table of W states whether a W selection makes every selected site in W∪I odd. Any real edge ab is included in this table.

**Universal-table obstruction, with no degree upper bound.** The table cannot accept all sixteen entries. Otherwise use K on E∪I without ab and leave its rows at I unchecked. Any strict thinning of K fixing P and preserving oddness at retained E sites thins H by restoring a realizing W selection for its boundary state.

Let M be the adjacency submatrix of K with rows R=E and columns C=V(K)∖P over the binary field. It is square even when I,P overlap. For any diagonal change on common indices U=R∩C, a nonzero kernel vector gives nonempty X⊆C to delete. Every retained row of R sees even parity into X, since its diagonal change acts on a zero bit. If U is empty, E⊆P. A lone exterior vertex cannot have three neighbors in I, and when E=P has size two the pins must be adjacent to reach degree three. Their edge already thins H. Hence assume U nonempty.

If M is singular use it directly. Otherwise N=M⁻¹ has rows C and columns R. Regard N[U,U] as a directed adjacency matrix with loops. No row j there is zero: from NM=Id such a row would express e_jᵀ as a sum of rows of M indexed by R∖U=P∖I. A single such pin has degree at least three in K (it has no W neighbors), and deleting the pin columns loses at most its edge to the other pin. Its remaining row has weight at least two. If two rows are available, both pins lie in R and have odd degrees. Omitting their columns removes the same mutual edge from each, so their sum has even weight. The empty combination has weight zero. None gives a singleton.

Choose a shortest directed cycle S in N[U,U], possible because every row has a nonzero entry. The induced matrix on S is a cyclic permutation matrix: any extra arc would give a shorter cycle. Adding ones to diagonal positions S of M now gives determinant det(M)det(Id+N[S,S])=0. Gaussian elimination finds the deletion vector. The matrix and shortest-cycle steps take polynomial time; the saved W representative lifts the smaller witness to original labels. This inversion and shortest-cycle certificate is related to the balanced open-graph internal-set criterion of Mhalla, Murao, Perdrix, Someya and Turner. The no-zero-row argument uses the degrees of the physical pins. [Mhalla et al., Theorems 4.1 and 5.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1006.2616)

**SPQR application.** Use the reduced SPQR tree without Q leaves, retaining real edges in the S, P and R skeletons. Each tree edge gives one virtual edge in each incident skeleton. An S skeleton is a simple cycle of length at least three, a P skeleton is a bond with at least three parallel edges, and an R skeleton is a simple 3-connected graph. [Gutwenger and Mutzel, A Linear Time Implementation of SPQR-Trees](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-44541-2_8)

Suppose the tree has at least two nodes. A leaf S has one virtual edge, and therefore an interior cycle vertex of physical degree two, contrary to oddness. A leaf P would have at least two parallel real edges, contrary to simplicity. A leaf R has one virtual edge ab and its only contacts with the rest are a,b. Its exclusive interior W is nonempty. The other side must contain at least one exclusive vertex: an empty side could supply only a single real ab in a simple graph, and there is no Q leaf; a side consisting entirely of bonds would have a forbidden P leaf. Equivalently, choose another tree leaf: its skeleton is R and it has private vertices. The actual leaf skeleton equals H[W∪{a,b}]+ab.

If the interior W of an R leaf has no named pin, its full selection must uniquely realize its own boundary parities with a,b on. The augmented edge ab is fixed for that comparison. If both endpoint degrees in the augmented skeleton are odd, ab alone contradicts minimality. If both are even, [deleting ab gives one of the degree-three-pin six-site supports](odd-block-path-and-edge-limb.md). If exactly one is even, the [mixed classification](mixed-rooted-three-connected-limb.md) yields seven or eleven sites. The [raw table test](all_pendant_torso_tables.py) enumerates all literal interior masks, both boundary occupancies at each port, and both possibilities for the true edge ab. It checks positivity in all 16 entries for all four order-six presentations, all twelve order-seven presentations and all 48 order-eleven presentations. The universal-table obstruction excludes every case.

Every leaf of a nontrivial SPQR tree must therefore contain a pin among its exclusive vertices. Distinct leaf interiors are disjoint, so there are at most two leaves, and the tree is a path. A singleton tree satisfies the same conclusion. Internal skeletons on the path can have two virtual edges. This proof gives no bound on their size and supplies no algorithm for identifying an unknown induced support inside a larger host. A block of a graph with articulations can have different degree parities and is outside the theorem.
