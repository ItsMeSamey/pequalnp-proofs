# A two-pin induced odd-support algorithm at maximum degree five

For a finite simple graph of maximum degree five and any two distinct original pins, a uniform deterministic polynomial-time algorithm finds a set through the pins inducing odd degree at every selected vertex, or reports that none exists. The proof uses the [four exact reductions and small terminals](degree-five-leaf-cap-pathwidth.md). Its bounded window concerns their specified order. An arbitrary bounded-width layout cannot replace it.

## Complete minimal patterns

Use the two inverse operations in the [paired and singleton-triangle algorithm](degree-five-common-neighbor-host.md) and two triple inverses. Child pins q,v must be nonadjacent and N(q) must be a triple C. Introduce p,u. In the first operation add pu,uv and all three uC edges, and toggle all vC edges. In the second add pu,uq,qv and all uC edges, and again toggle vC. Name p,q. Require that the resulting graph has maximum degree at most five. In both operations the child row at q requires odd occupancy of C, yielding the exact support bijection in the four-cap theorem. Inverse paired and singleton operations require two nonadjacent child pins of degrees at most three.

The terminal is any rooted minimal graph of maximum degree five on at most eight sites, or a ten-site graph with the exceptional nested seven/three pins as in the [exceptional cap](exceptional-seven-pin-ten.md). Check degrees and all proper subsets directly on the finite list. The latter terminal is allowed only directly below a triple move restoring maximum degree five, and the original root must satisfy this degree bound. Every generated pattern is minimal. Every minimal support of the original host has such a presentation. Every nonterminal with another move has a pinned leaf; a stage with two high pins is necessarily a terminal.

Let B_i consist of the current pins and their neighbors immediately before forward move i. It has at most twelve sites. Every edited site belongs to B_i or B_(i+1), and all surviving members of B_i belong to B_(i+1). In reverse it suffices to retain the actual adjacency on B, total degrees of its members, and the ordered pins, using a fixed local palette of names. A cap record specifies the move, the two fresh vertices, the new active slots and slot correspondence. Each reverse operation changes only active or new sites and yields the next boundary from this information. Slots retired in reverse can be used later for new sites. No retired site can be changed or retrieved. This defines a finite automaton from terminal to root. Reverse its accepted record language to read complete traces from root to terminal.

## Temporal extent

A surviving named site is q in a leaf/high move. Only a nonadjacent triple preserves its degree, and it then keeps precisely the same neighbors C. Every other retaining move lowers its degree by two. In a consecutive block of nonadjacent triples at q, each removed parent u_j already meets every c of C at block start. Earlier changes in this block concern C and earlier transferred pins v_k. Every v_k is the other pin and disappears as the removed leaf in the next move of this block. No u_j is in C or among the earlier v_k. The distinct parents therefore already occupied one slot each at every c at block start. Since c has at most five neighbors, including q, the block has length at most four. A degree-five q can decrease to three, persist in one such block, and then decrease to one, when the next move removes it. There are at most six consecutive named survivals.

An unnamed member w of B_i that survives without promotion stays adjacent to the same surviving q. In a paired move the only survivors are the promoted parents. In a leaf/high move the only neighbor of the leaf is the removed parent. An old q-neighbor lost at this move is removed or is the promoted v. If q is eventually removed as a leaf while w is still attached to it, w is its sole parent and is removed or promoted. Thus an active site has at most one unnamed period bounded by a named tenure, and then at most one named tenure of its own. Including the terminal and the record introducing or retiring each site, all its activity fits within 24 consecutive layers. Every original or residual template edge connects sites whose layers of first exposure differ by at most 24: its introduction or edit in reverse occurs with both endpoints in adjacent active bags. This bound includes the bounded terminal.

## Exposure and host reachability

Expose the original pins first and then the rest of B₀, each incident to a pin. At a move expose the new members of B_(i+1). In a nonadjacent triple a potentially fresh v meets the exposed u in the pre-move graph; expose it first. All other fresh members meet a new pin by a pre-move edge: changed vC edges concern the already exposed C, and q obtains no new ordinary neighbors. At a terminal grow from the pins in each component. No incidence to a newly exposed site was modified in an earlier move, since earlier edited endpoints were exposed with their record. Each new site after the initial pins therefore has an earlier physical neighbor within 24 layers.

For two names exposed within 24 layers, their original edge was initialized in reverse at creation of an endpoint (or on the terminal); each subsequent toggle of that edge mentions both names while active. All pertinent records lie within 24 layers on either side of the exposures, allowing an extra cap layer. Pairs never present together at a cap or terminal have no edge. The finite trace can consequently be annotated with its physical adjacencies and checked with a finite 64-layer lookahead buffer; guess its records and later verify them. This includes vC edges toggled several times.

Map the root to the prescribed indices and guess the labels for each new site. Require distinctness and exactly the prescribed physical edges throughout the most recent 1024 layers, including partial layers. Carry the finite annotations and perform directed reachability with one predecessor per state. Even retaining 1100 layers with sixteen slots per layer bounds the states and transitions by n to an absolute constant power. All finite control tables are uniformly enumerable. Every genuine minimal support gives an accepting walk.

If the reconstructed accepting walk is globally induced and injective, return it. Otherwise take its first bad new name X and an earlier template neighbor U as above. A repeat of a remote label would imply a previous extra edge at U to that earlier occurrence, spanning more than 24 layers. Recent repeats and missing required edges are checked locally. Thus X is fresh with a spurious physical edge to an earlier site.

Choose the earliest sensitive cap index i at which an unexpected X edge hits a site deleted at that cap or the surviving q at a triple. Every unexpected neighbor was exposed over a thousand layers before X and is removed within 24 further layers, so i exists. The current cap and connected sets of radius four from its pins in the abstract residual H are present in the clean prefix: each residual edge has span at most 24. H is connected and larger than ten since many layers remain and a disconnected minimal support has only two edges. U is within 24 layers of X, and is anticomplete to the cap, these protected sites and all earlier removed sites by prefix cleanliness.

Take the induced physical graph on the distinct clean prefix labels and X, and execute the earlier specified edits and deletions to reach i. All required earlier endpoints were already exposed. Among the clean labels the resulting adjacencies agree with their abstract residual. X is remote and new, so its incident edges were never toggled in these earlier moves. No spurious neighbor of X was previously deleted or was the retained q at an earlier triple; U also has no earlier sensitive incidences. These facts will be used in restoring previous rows.

## Exhaustive local repairs

If a removed pinned leaf p sees X, use the leaf repair from the [single-common-neighbor proof](degree-five-common-neighbor-host.md). It only needs p's unique neighbor u and the other pin q. If q sees X, the claw p,q,X,U works. Otherwise in H minus p,u the component at q has at least |H|−3 sites, since any other component attaches only to u and minimality reduces all such components together to at most one leaf. On a connected five-site set there starting at q, find a shortest path to the set of other X-nonneighbors; this set is nonempty since X also sees p and U. The path's interiors see X. Add p,X and optionally U to make X odd. This produces odd degrees using actual residual edges. It covers either leaf of a paired step.

At a singleton triangle, if the removed parent u sees X but the removed leaf p does not, take the claw u,p,q,X when q misses X. Otherwise remove p,u and their sole common cap neighbor v of u,q from the abstract residual. All components apart from q's can attach only to v, hence together consist of at most one optional leaf. In a connected four-site set starting at q find a shortest path from q to another nonneighbor of X, with all interior sites adjacent to X. It exists since X already sees u,q,U. Add p,u,X and optionally U to the path. Vertex q sees exactly u,X and its first path neighbor, u sees p,q,X, and all path sites are odd.

Finally consider a triple. Every nonpin has degree at most five in the materialized physical residual, including any extra incidence to X. Originally this follows from the host bound. Only C nonpins are toggled, and each loses its actual neighbor u while possibly gaining v. Any exceptional gain is confined to a new pin v; a named site is never demoted to ordinary status before deletion. Thus the removed nonpin u, already with five distinct intended neighbors, cannot see the new X. At a nonadjacent triple where q sees X and p misses X, the two edges pu and qX suffice since uq,pq,uX are all absent. The case pX was handled above.

At an adjacent triple the surviving q already has five distinct intended neighbors. In a long continuing prefix its physical degree remains at most five. Indeed, while a pin survives as q, its degree cannot rise in forward moves. A site first made an inner pin by a paired or singleton move was an ordinary site before its degree decreased. If newly made the transferred pin v by a triple, the other inner pin then has intended degree three. If v had high intended degree that child would immediately be a bounded terminal. Otherwise v had intended degree one and can only be removed at the very next move, never surviving later as a high q. The initial named sites have degree at most five. These are all possible ways the current q first became named. Therefore q cannot see X at this adjacent triple. The leaf case, two-edge repair and saturated cases exhaust all first sensitive steps.

Lift the repaired local support by reversing the earlier moves on the original indices. X,U have no unexpected incidences to any earlier removed site. At an earlier triple the surviving child q still has no additional selected neighbor outside its intended three C, because early qX edges were included in the choice of i and U is remote. Every other selected site in the repair belongs to the clean prefix. Consequently the child q guard implies odd occupancy of C exactly as required by the inverse. Other surviving sites (including v or members of C) may see X; the same unchanged incidence appears on both sides and cancels in their row comparison. The nontriple inverses need only the two forced pin incidences. Hence induction restores both original pins and all original odd degrees. Directly check the reconstructed output in the full host. If no accepting state exists, completeness of the generated pattern language proves NO.

The running time includes finite automaton construction, uniform reachability and witness recovery. It is a polynomial-time theorem, with very large constants in this direct translation. The earlier, separately implemented solver handles the common-neighbor subclass. This graph theorem alone makes no classification of the unrestricted-degree two-pin language or of P versus NP.
