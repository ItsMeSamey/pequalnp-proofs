# An unbounded-degree class for two prescribed odd vertices

Call an edge crowded if it belongs to at least three ordinary four-cycles, with chords allowed. Suppose (i) every vertex on a triangle has degree at most four, and (ii) every vertex on a square containing a crowded edge has degree at most four. Other degrees are unrestricted. There is a deterministic polynomial-time native search for an induced all-odd support through two original names p,q. Recognize the class in O(n^4) indexed operations by enumerating all triples and quadruples (including all three possible cyclic orders per quadruple), recording triangle participants and counting squares per edge, then rescanning the squares.

First test all two-, four- and six-site words through the pins. Within an inclusion-minimal successful induced support H, sites of original degree at most four have induced degree at most three. If some edge uv lies on at least three squares in H, both endpoints and every site on those squares satisfy this bound by (ii). As in [the subcubic calculation](subcubic-two-odd-pins.md), each endpoint has at most two other H-neighbors, and three distinct ordered square paths use every position of the two lists. The shared-site cases are excluded by the same degree bounds, and the complete or one-edge-deficient six-site bipartite pattern has a saturated twin pair on each shore. Nonadjacent pins cannot hit both pairs; deleting both selected vertices of an unpinned pair preserves all remaining parities. Thus H has at most two squares per edge. Adjacent pins are immediate, and otherwise the [initial two-square classification](two-square-odd-pair-walk.md) gives a paired or triangle initial relay in H whenever the bounded test failed. Enumerate all three-edge candidate paths (O(n²) with fixed ends) and apply the verified exact first reductions.

At any subsequent state its nonadjacent pins s,t were joined by an original edge just erased, and each has lost at least one other original incidence through that preceding step. If the erased edge was originally crowded, both endpoints have original degree at most four, hence residual degree at most two. After the four-site test only paired relays are then required. Otherwise at most two residual three-edge paths between the pins are possible, since each closes a distinct original square. In any minimal residual witness lacking a four-site solution the exclusive pin neighborhoods (sizes a,b) are completely joined and their common neighborhood C has no neighbor outside the closed pin neighborhoods. The path count is at least ab+|C|, while a,b have equal parity and a+|C| is odd. With a,b positive this forces a=b=1 and C empty. If either side is empty there is exactly one common site c, with exactly one further selected neighbor v in an exclusive neighborhood (the number is positive odd and bounded by two paths). Then c,v and the pin t on that side form a triangle; its odd selected degree is at least three since it sees both c,v. The two previously lost original incidences give original degree at least five on a triangle, prohibited by (i). Consequently only the paired case survives after a failed four-site test. The four-site cap at nonadjacent pins is necessarily two disjoint edges or a claw with both pins as leaves. Thus a successful residual has a clean paired ladder with one of those caps, by repeated choices inside a minimal support.

Use the twelve-rung finite-state search in [the first-conflict theorem](quartic-ladder-first-conflict.md), omitting the raw maximum-degree-four assertion. Its only saturation step concerns a fresh X already adjacent to four distinct sites s_i,t_(i+1),t_(i+2),U. Since t_(i+1)t_(i+2) is a genuine rail edge, X lies on an original triangle. By (i) X misses t_(i+3) as required. All other repairs use only the globally clean prefix. The same reconstruction therefore returns an original native witness. Without a global degree bound there are O(n^24) ordered twelve-rung suffixes and only polynomially many next pairs or cap candidates at each; both time and space are polynomial. This does not claim the smaller linear quartic implementation bound.

For connected examples beyond either previous class alone, take the four-cube Q and the graph T of d≥5 internally disjoint length-three paths between two ends. Replace one edge in each by two crossing links matching their original endpoints. A cycle using a link uses both links and has length at least 2+3+5=10 by the original girths. The T portion has neither triangles nor squares, whereas Q has degree four and retains crowded edges (a parallel cube edge obtained by flipping all three coordinates perpendicular to the removed edge shares no square with it). T retains vertices of arbitrarily large degree d. The output is biconnected: after deleting a nonattachment vertex on one shore, any lost internal attachment-to-attachment edge can be simulated via the opposite intact shore; deleting an attachment itself leaves its original shore connected and the other link active.

For comparison, the [Belmonte–Sau rank-width algorithm, Section 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.06078) permits arbitrary degrees on a supplied bounded-width decomposition, requiring the two pins at their leaf states. The present algorithm checks its structural conditions directly on the raw graph; the class contains all graphs of maximum degree four. Unrestricted degree five remains open here.
