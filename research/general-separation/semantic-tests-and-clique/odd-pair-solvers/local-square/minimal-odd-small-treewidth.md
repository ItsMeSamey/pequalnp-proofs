# Bounded pathwidth of minimal two-pin supports of treewidth two

Let H be an inclusion-minimal induced all-odd support containing two named vertices. If H has treewidth at most two, it has pathwidth at most eight. This holds with no degree cap, including disconnected supports. Thus one cannot force unbounded pathwidth by composing series-parallel pieces while keeping the entire native support series-parallel. The conclusion concerns the actual support, not the treewidth of the SAT host or the complexity of identifying an embedded minimal support.

Take any finite width-two tree decomposition (T,B). If bags at adjacent nodes are comparable by containment, contract their tree edge retaining the larger bag. All vertices and edges remain covered, and each vertex's occurrence subtree remains connected. Repeat until there is one bag or adjacent bags are incomparable. If there is one bag the bound is immediate. Each leaf bag has a vertex missing from its parent. By connected occurrences that vertex appears nowhere else, so all its neighbors are in its leaf bag and it has degree at most two. Since it is selected in H its degree is odd, hence exactly one. Different leaf bags give distinct private degree-one vertices. The [constructive optional-leaf theorem](optional-leaf-pin-bound.md) bounds unnamed leaves by two, hence H has at most four leaves in total. Consequently T has at most four leaves and at most two vertices of degree at least three, by the degree sum for trees.

Choose a path in T containing those branch vertices, extending between leaves (or use T itself if it is a path). Every component outside this path is a path attached to one spine vertex. A width-two path decomposition of T processes spine vertices consecutively. At each spine site hold it present while processing the attached paths one at a time, keeping two consecutive sites on the current attached path. Include consecutive spine vertices together between stages. If the attachment lies internally on an off-spine path it would have a third tree neighbor and hence would be a branch vertex, already on the spine. All bags have at most three sites.

Replace each tree path-bag by the union of its original graph bags. The new bags have size at most nine and cover all graph edges. For each graph vertex the tree nodes whose original bags contain it form a connected subtree, so their intervals in the path decomposition of the tree have connected union (adjacent nodes' intervals overlap by edge coverage). This proves the claim and gives a constructive layout from a supplied decomposition.

More generally a minimal support through h pins has at most 2h leaves in total. In a reduced width-two decomposition this also bounds the number of leaf bags, so the decomposition tree has at most max(0,2h−2) branch vertices. Keeping them in every bag and scanning the remaining disjoint paths gives pathwidth O(h) for that tree; taking bag unions gives the same O(h) bound for the actual support. No comparable leaf-bag argument for width three is asserted: a private site in a four-site bag may have degree three.
