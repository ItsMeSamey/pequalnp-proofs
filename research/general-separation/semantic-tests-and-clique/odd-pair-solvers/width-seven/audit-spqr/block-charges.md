# Distinct charges in a nontrivial block

Audit of df4e5683c7c427e606e0f1d8a583c7f90215a54ca4e6129757c6b1f2caf08023. Let H be the actual selected connected simple graph with two distinct pins, all degrees odd and at most five, and no proper induced all-odd subset containing both pins.

**Simultaneous completion.** For pin-free shores with disjoint interiors, no cross edges and a universal parity table on their actual incident boundary vertices, remove their interiors. Original degrees at all sites outside the union I of the boundaries are still odd. A nonzero kernel vector for the adjacency matrix with rows outside I and columns outside the pins deletes a nonempty set in this remainder, preserving oddness at all retained checked sites. More columns than rows holds exactly when |I| exceeds the number of pins, including when pins themselves are boundary sites. At every occupied vertex of I allocate its required parity to one incident shore and zero to all other incident shores. Every shore can realize its assigned vector simultaneously even when its ports overlap another shore's. Boundary edges stay in the remainder and are counted once. An originally deleted remainder site cannot be restored by any interior selection. Equal boundary pairs require the separate move of emptying the first shore, keeping the entire remainder, and assigning the combined old contribution to the second. This handles equality in the dimension count.

**Physical token injection.** Root the block-cut incidence tree at B. Each component of H−V(B) attaches to a unique B vertex: two contacts induce an external path between distinct B vertices, an ear enlarging the biconnected block. Conversely each H articulation belonging to B has a nonempty off-block branch there, since B−v is connected. Thus branches chosen at different B vertices are disjoint and each contains no B vertex. Distinct SPQR leaf interiors are private even when the separators coincide: a private vertex occurs only in its leaf skeleton, whereas a separator vertex also occurs in a neighboring skeleton.

Charge a leaf containing a private pin to one such pin, even if it is an articulation. Otherwise take a private articulation v and one off-block component there. A pin in the component cannot be another leaf's private B pin, and the component cannot be chosen at another leaf's distinct private articulation. If it has no pin then the parity it supplies at v must be odd (otherwise delete it), and keeping one of v's neighbors instead gives the same parity, hence by minimality the component is just that single physical unnamed leaf. There is no charge to v merely because it is adjacent to a pin. A pin sitting on the boundary of two leaf skeletons is private to neither, so it cannot create a double charge. A pin at a different B articulation also cannot appear in an off-block component. These facts prove the claimed injection into two physical pins and at most two physical unnamed leaves. For this last bound, distinct optional leaves cannot share a parent (delete the two), and two adjacent optional leaves would be a deletable component. Thus each optional leaf is a universal singleton shore with distinct boundary vertex. The simultaneous completion lemma already gives the bound two.

**Clean case.** Every private non-articulation has its entire physical star inside B. This precludes a clean S leaf (private degree two); P leaves are impossible in a simple graph with Q leaves omitted. For a clean R leaf the sole virtual pair has another side in B which meets both endpoints. Adding ab to the physical induced shore respects the five-degree cap. Oddness at boundary sites in B itself is not used. The prior rooted torso and universal-table classification applies at the full physical boundary parities. Two clean leaves have no edge between private interiors; either their interface union has at least three vertices, or the equal-boundary replacement applies. Thus there is at most one. If a different leaf is charged to an unnamed pendant, its parent is private to that leaf and cannot be in the clean leaf's boundary pair. These two adjustable shores have three distinct ports, contradiction. At most two pinned charges then remain. The tree has at most four leaves in general and at most three with a clean leaf.
