# Every minimal two-pin odd support of maximum degree five has pathwidth at most seven

Let H be a finite simple graph of maximum degree five, with every degree odd and two distinct named sites p,q. Suppose no proper subset containing the pins induces odd degree at every selected site. Then H has pathwidth at most seven. Given this minimal H, the proof constructs a path decomposition and exact reductions on the original labels. Extra vertices of a host still matter to the task of finding such a support.

## Terminals with two nonleaf pins

A disconnected minimal support must be two disjoint edges. A component without a pin can be dropped, and any incident edge in a component with one pin suffices for that pin. Thus two nonleaf pins require a connected graph. If it has a leaf, the [two-leaf exclusion](high-pins-forbid-two-optional-leaves.md) and [single-leaf census](single-leaf-high-pins-eight.md) give order eight. Otherwise it is biconnected. At a cut vertex, a pin-free component can be omitted if it sends even parity to the cut, and replaced by one neighbor of the cut if it sends odd parity. Minimality would make it a single leaf. If the cut instead separates the two pins into different components, each pin has a neighbor in its own component even after excluding the cut. Those two edges alone give a proper support. A cut equal to a pin always has a pin-free component. Thus the [biconnected classification](biconnected-odd-six-eight.md) gives order six or eight in the leaf-free case.

## The four physical reductions

Adjacent pins give their own edge. Otherwise let p be a pinned leaf with parent u. If q is also a leaf, their parents can coincide only in a four-site claw, since the parent must have a third neighbor and that claw suffices. Distinct nonadjacent parents give two disjoint edges. Otherwise the two parents u,v are distinct and adjacent. Delete p,q, erase uv, and name u,v. Each old pin forces its parent. On any selection containing both parents each loses two incidences. This gives a bijection of supports, and the child pins have degrees at most three.

Now q has degree three or five, and differs from u.

* If uq is absent, every neighbor in C=N(q) meets u. Otherwise the pins and their respective neighbors give a proper pair of edges. The degree of u is odd, includes p and the odd-sized set C, and is at most five. Hence C has size three and N(u) consists of p,C and one further vertex v. Here qv is absent. Delete p,u, toggle the three v-to-C edges, and name q,v. In every old support u is forced, q sees an odd number of C, and u then forces v. In either direction each selected site in C exchanges u for the toggle at selected v. Vertex v loses u and changes parity by an odd number of C incidences. The row at q is unchanged.
* If uq is present, any further neighbor of u other than p outside N(q) gives a proper claw with leaves p,q and that neighbor. Write C for the common neighbors of u,q and E for the neighbors of q other than u and C. Vertex u requires odd occupancy of C since p and q are selected. Vertex q then requires odd occupancy of E. Their full sizes are odd with sum two or four. If C is a singleton v, delete p,u, erase qv and name q,v. The forced site v loses two selected incidences at u,q. Vertex q loses two at u,v. Its remaining neighborhood is E, and the inverse restores the two deleted sites.
* In the other adjacent case C has size three and E is a singleton v. Delete p,u, erase qv, toggle the three v-to-C edges and name q,v. The child q now requires odd occupancy of C. Restoring the forced v and the two deleted sites makes the old rows at u,q odd. Each selected site in C exchanges u for its toggle at v. Vertex v loses q and changes parity by an odd number of C incidences.

All toggles use literal adjacencies, including previously present toggle edges. Each map preserves every support through its pins in both directions and sends the full set to the full set. Consequently each child is minimal. Each step removes two vertices. Application to a larger host would require separate justification or branching on those incidences.

## The exceptional child

In a triple-toggle case let k count the neighbors of v in C before the move. Its new degree equals its former degree plus 2−2k. All other surviving sites stay within the degree-five bound: each site in C loses u and either gains or loses v. Thus a degree above five requires former degree five and k=0. The child has pins q,v of degrees three and seven, qv absent, and all three neighbors of q also adjacent to v. The [exceptional census](exceptional-seven-pin-ten.md) gives order ten for zero or one optional leaf. The two-leaf exclusion holds without a maximum-degree restriction. A disconnected exception cannot be minimal with both pins nonleaves. Recursion therefore reaches an ordinary terminal or this exceptional cap.

## End bags and original labels

For an ordinary instance require an end bag containing both pins and all their current neighbors. A base graph of order at most eight fits in one bag. For the paired reduction append the four old pins and parents as a bag. The previous end bag contains both parents. In a step deleting p,u with other pin q, append the bag consisting of p,u,q,v and the old neighbors of q other than u,v. It has size at most seven. It contains both old pins and all their neighbors, every edge restored at p,u and every toggled surviving edge. Every surviving member lies in the previous end bag, since the neighbors in question remain adjacent to q. This preserves the interval condition for each vertex. Extra edges in the child that are removed while lifting impose no requirement on the parent decomposition.

An exceptional child occurs only directly below a triple toggle. Its needed handoff is therefore q,v,C. Let A be the four neighbors of v outside C. The sole remaining site d sees neither pin and meets no site of C, since such an edge would produce a proper claw through both pins. Use consecutive bags A∪{d}, A∪C∪{v}, C∪{q,v}. They cover every edge, have size at most eight, and the last covers the required handoff. In all cases width is at most seven. Back-substitution appends original vertices with their recorded labels.

On a promised minimal support choose a reduction or a terminal by inspecting the current pins and their constant-size neighborhoods. Store mutable indexed adjacency lists of capacity seven, active flags and one record per move. Every move costs constant time, and the bounded-size terminal can be inspected directly. Reading the input, constructing the reduction sequence, and writing the path bags in reverse take O(|V|+|E|) on an indexed adjacency-list RAM. This is a structural and constructive result on the selected graph. It does not decide the unrestricted induced-support search on an input host of maximum degree five.
