# Branches of a minimal induced all-odd support

Let H be the graph induced by an inclusion-minimal all-odd support containing specified pins p,q. Every vertex of H is selected and has odd degree. All boundaries and degrees below are taken in H. Replacing pin-free components is legitimate if the retained vertices are odd and the replacement sends the same neighbor parities to the rest of H.

For any vertex set K, let d_B be the vector indexed by K of parity contributions from each pin-free component B of H−K. These vectors are independent over GF(2). Otherwise delete the union of the components in a nonempty zero-sum collection. Each remaining degree stays odd and both pins remain. Thus there are at most |K| such components.

**One physical separator vertex.** Every pin-free component of H−v is a singleton, and there is at most one. An even contribution is removable. An odd contribution can be realized by retaining just one neighbor of v in the component, which then has degree one. In particular every nontrivial branch attached at only one vertex requires a pin of its own.

**Two physical separator vertices.** At most one pin-free component of H−{u,v} has more than one vertex. Otherwise two nonsingleton components B,C have distinct nonzero contributions. At least one contribution is a unit vector, say d_B=10. No contact vertex of B has neighborhood 10 on {u,v}, since its singleton would replace B. Hence the sum 10 requires both a vertex of contact type 11 and one of type 01. The latter singleton is odd and contributes 01. If d_C=11, the singleton replaces both B and C as their combined contribution is 01. If d_C=01 then the symmetric argument in C supplies a singleton of type 10, and the two swapped singletons replace both components. The components have no edge between them, so the new selected degrees at both singletons are one.

**Rank-two version.** Let U be any pin-free shore and assume the matrix of incidences from U to its exterior has rank at most two over GF(2). At most one component of H[U] has more than two vertices. Moreover, if exterior incidence has odd row sum somewhere then at most one has more than one vertex. Encode the row vectors injectively and linearly in GF(2)², writing z_x for the code of a vertex x. Let β be their row-sum functional. The component vectors d_B are sums of codes and are independent by the same removal argument. In rank zero or one this alone bounds the number of components by one. At rank two and β nonzero choose as basis the two distinct codes with β=1. Both singleton types are odd in the external environment. The two-vertex-separator proof applies verbatim using codes instead of physical neighbor sets.

If β=0, the selected vertices of every component B have odd degrees internally. Its sum d_B is the sum over its edges xy of z_x+z_y, by counting each vertex once per incident edge. Keeping just the endpoints of any such edge is a valid replacement candidate because each has even external contribution and one selected internal neighbor. If B has more than two vertices no edge sum can equal d_B, since this would give a strict reduction. For two such components their d_B,d_C are independent, and for each component its edge sums must include both other nonzero codes to generate its own code. In particular B has an edge contributing d_C and C has one contributing d_B. Retain just these two edges, a strict reduction with unchanged total contribution.

These lemmas allow other components of the graph and separators containing pins. They apply to multiple branches sharing the specified interface. Attachments to different pairs of a common torso, and a single large branch, require further arguments; the results by themselves give no global bound on pathwidth.
