# Two nonleaf pins permit at most one optional leaf

Let H be a finite simple inclusion-minimal induced all-odd support through two distinct sites p,q. If both pins have degree at least three in H, there cannot be two unpinned leaves. The bound is independent of maximum degree.

Suppose l₁,l₂ are two such leaves. They cannot be adjacent (delete their isolated edge) and cannot have a common parent (delete both). Remove them and call the remaining vertex set V. Write I for their two distinct parents, P={p,q}, E=V∖I, C=V∖P, U=E∩C. The binary matrix M=A[E,C] is square, with A the adjacency matrix on V. For each common index j in U its diagonal entry may be changed independently. A nonzero kernel vector of any such matrix is a deletion mask on C. Every retained row in E sees an even number of deletions, since a changed diagonal only contributes when its own site is deleted. For a deleted parent delete its leaf, and for a retained parent delete its leaf exactly when its other neighbors suffer an odd number of deletions. This preserves all retained degree parities and both pins, giving a strict thinning.

Here is the square-matrix argument from the [universal two-port lemma](universal-pair-shore-spqr-path.md) applied to the two leaf parents. If U is empty then E⊆P. Each vertex in E has all its neighbors in V because neither leaf is its neighbor. If E contains just one site, that pin has degree at most two. If E contains both pins, they must be adjacent for their degrees to exceed two, and their own edge is a proper witness. If E is empty, V consists only of the two parents, both pins, of degree at most two even counting their leaves. Thus assume U is nonempty and M invertible. Its inverse N has rows C and columns E. No row j of N[U,U] vanishes: otherwise NM=Id writes a singleton e_j as a sum of rows from E∩P. A single such row has weight at least two, since among the original neighbors of a pin in E, deleting the two pin columns loses at most one neighbor. If both pin rows are available, their weights have equal parity, because their original degrees are odd and the edge between them is omitted equally. Their sum cannot be a singleton either.

The digraph of N[U,U] therefore has a directed cycle. A shortest one, on sites S, induces a cyclic permutation matrix, with a loop allowed. Flipping the entries M[j,j] for j in S multiplies its determinant by det(Id+N[S,S])=0, by the determinant lemma. This produces the kernel vector above. All operations construct a smaller witness in polynomial time.

For exactly one leaf the same correction eliminates one row but leaves one more row than free columns. The square argument does not establish an order bound in that case.
