# Completeness audit of the three-connected degree-five pinned support census

**Verdict.** The finite classification proves the stated result for a finite simple graph H that is itself 3-connected, with distinct pins, all degrees odd and at most five, and no proper induced odd set containing both pins. Thus every vertex belongs to N[p] ∪ N[q] and the order is six or eight. The finite step uses the enumerated outputs for terminal sweep 357940285c267afb24380c672588dd7082fe05b118eceaf182c82cb40b5aa4f4 and the independently reproduced physical history counts. This is a computer-assisted proof.

1. The minimum degree of H is three. If the pins are adjacent, their isolated two-site selection is a proper witness since a 3-connected graph has at least four sites. Otherwise partition the pin neighborhoods into the two exclusive classes A,B and the common class C. Every missing A–B edge supplies two disjoint pin edges on a proper subset. A C vertex with an exterior neighbor supplies an induced pin-containing claw. These witnesses have four sites; the only 3-connected four-vertex graph is K4, already excluded. All these tests use induced edges of H itself.

2. C must be nonempty. If empty, each exclusive size is odd, at least three, and at most four by complete cross adjacency and the reserved pin edge at every opposite vertex. Both equal three. Delete the pins and erase the nine cross edges: six marked vertices now have degree one (previously five); the remainder still has degree three or five. Require odd selection in each marked triple. A proper odd word satisfying these two readouts lifts to a proper pinned odd set by restoring pins and cross edges (one pin and an odd number of opposite neighbors added at a selected mark). In the residual graph an isolated two-leaf edge of equal colors can be removed, and an opposite-color isolated edge is a proper satisfying word itself. Two marked leaves of one color sharing a parent can both be removed. A parent shared by opposite colors has an unmarked third neighbor, since the same-color case has been excluded; the two leaves, parent and that neighbor give an induced claw. Hence all six parents are unmarked and distinct. Any missing opposite-color parent edge gives two disjoint leaf edges. Thus the six parents themselves form cross-complete triples of degree five. Delete old leaves and erase those nine cross edges. They now give smaller marked triples of degree one satisfying the same no-proper-word condition: copy the selection of each parent to its old leaf for lifting. This descends by six vertices, contradiction. This argument does not require residual connectivity.

3. If the root R={p,q}∪A∪B∪C is proper, only A,B vertices of degree below five have possible edges out. Removing at most two sites cannot disconnect two surviving sites. In particular at least three such ports are needed. More generally after any root deletion of at most two vertices each surviving root component must be able to reach a surviving port, because a path to any exterior vertex has to leave R. This holds even when the exterior itself is disconnected. The root generator tries all optional edges among A,B,C (only fixed A–B and pin edges), rejects any odd pinned selection in the proper prefix, and requires C already at final degree. Permuting vertices within each class, or interchanging the pins and equal-size exclusive classes, preserves all requirements. The decoder's exterior port test d<5 is a permissible superset; actual final counts may be zero. For c=1,…,5 and each a+c,b+c in {3,5}, every possible R is represented. The degree screen and root-cut test leave 60 physical representatives.

4. For any putative exterior, take actual breadth-first distance layers from R. There are no edges skipping a layer. Each new site meets the prior boundary in a nonzero mask t. Multisets of these masks sorted by value retain all possibilities up to relabeling; equal types are repeated and all edges among their individual sites remain choices. At an old port j the sum of its old degree and the multiplicity of incident mask bits must be 3 or 5, including ports with zero new neighbors. A nonlast layer has at least three vertices, with at least three able to reach a future layer; likewise at least three used old ports are necessary even for a singleton last layer. Removing fewer separates a surviving pin-side vertex from the exterior. Distance layers and intentionally permissive port sets require no equivalence of 3-connected graphs under a table merge.

5. For a pinned subset W of the old prefix and selected new sites S, write u for W's occupancy on the old boundary, v for the XOR of t_i over S, and E(W) for the even-degree selected old vertices. Old feasibility is exactly E(W) supported on ports and E(W)=u&v in port coordinates. Unselected sites impose no parity condition. For any newly selected i the parity is dot(t_i,u) plus its number of neighbors in S, modulo two. These are exhaustive identities, with every literal cross edge counted once at each end. Therefore propagating old raw masks across **all** subsets S recovers all feasible physical prefix words on the larger graph. Counts truncated at two preserve existence and uniqueness because sums are nonnegative and every (W,S) gives a distinct resulting word. Crucially the terminal algorithm uses actual words and later keeps the flag W==full alongside u; it does not mistake two identical boundary rows for one unique full word or infer connectivity from truncated counts.

6. In a nonterminal prefix any odd selection there is proper, even if it uses the entire prefix. This justifies single-site vetoes and the more general newly introduced site plus any preceding sites in its layer. At a full old/new occupation the real H witnesses old feasibility. An alternative old W with dot(t_i,u)=dot(t_i,fullports) for **each** new i extends to all exterior sites selected: it leaves every new degree unchanged, and sites at greater distances see none of the altered old vertices. This is a proper witness unless W is full. This proves the full-mask replacement cut used before selecting within-layer edges. After actual new edges are chosen, the corresponding incoming bit for each new port equals 1 xor (its prefix degree mod 2). A second word with this incoming vector and all ports selected similarly contradicts minimality. There is no assumption about distinct parents or acyclic shells.

7. In a terminal layer, properness cannot be inferred from having selected one/two new sites if that exhausts the layer. Accordingly singleton vetoes are used only for m≥2, pair vetoes only for m≥3. For a selected pair with types t,h and its mutual edge e, a forbidden induced word exists precisely when some old feasible W at incoming t xor h gives dot(t,u)=dot(h,u)=1 xor e. At the final test, enumerate every S and old compatible occupancy/old-full flag. A choice witnesses nonminimality exactly when (S is not full or old-full is false) and all selected new sites have odd parity. This criterion also handles S empty. The code then reconstructs both directions of each old-to-new edge, adds the literal chosen within-layer edges, checks every new final degree, and tests connectivity on the physical graph after every pair deletion. For a graph with at least four vertices and minimum degree three, this last test is equivalent to 3-connectivity. Thus no bound on connectivity of a contracted graph is used. The terminal traversal iterates lists of physical histories, not the table-deduplicating diagnostic in the helper's main.

8. At depths 0 through 3 the verified finite enumeration has 60, 93, 100 and 32 physical proper prefixes; none has a terminal completion. Depth four has zero nonterminal prefixes. By the extension argument every exterior would reach exactly such a terminal completion or a missing next prefix, so no exterior is possible. For a closed root, order even implies c even since a+c and b+c are odd. Thus exactly four color-size triples remain. Our separate closed.py generates completions by choosing each vertex's complete remaining neighborhood to achieve degree 3 or 5, then checks every proper even pinned subset directly and all pair deletions. In the order (1,1,2), (1,3,2), (3,3,2), (1,1,4), its all-odd counts are 4,112,140,404 and its final counts 4,48,0,168. This yields 4 order-six and 216 order-eight representatives in the fixed root-color labeling convention. These are not counts of all arbitrary vertex labelings or of nonisomorphic graphs.

Only the degree of a selected vertex **inside H** enters the proof. Adding unselected ambient neighbors does not alter any induced witness. Minimality is inclusion minimality among original pinned odd subsets, rather than minimality among just 3-connected subsets.
