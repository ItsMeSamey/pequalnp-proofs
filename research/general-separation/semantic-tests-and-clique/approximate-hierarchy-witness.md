# Uniform-error correction for a hierarchy witness

Keep the original October clause decoder for the unary H outside NTIME(2^(n/2)) and the dn-bit addresses of witness variables. Pad the 2^(dn) assignment bits with zeros to a grid of h^m entries, where h=2^ceil(log₂ n) and m=ceil(dn/log₂ h) for n≥2. Group the binary address (with leading zeros as needed) into m blocks of log₂ h bits. Let F have q elements, where q is the least power of two at least max(32,16D) and D=m(h−1). The low log₂ h coordinate bits identify h designated distinct elements H₀ of F; they need not form a subfield. Use the polynomial basis for the lexicographically first irreducible binary polynomial of degree log₂ q. Searching all candidates and trial-dividing is polynomial in n because q is polynomial in n.

Interpolate the unique polynomial P of degree <h per variable and total degree at most D with the padded table values in {0,1} on H₀^m. Encode it on independent uniform addresses (x,b) in F^m times the binary vector space of log₂ q bits by the binary dot product b·coord(P(x)). Every such pair uses exactly (m+1)log₂ q=O(n) bits. Encoding the full word is an operation on the exponential truth table; computing an individual encoded bit is not promised to be in polynomial time from local access to W.

Suppose one fixed PH predicate B on (1^n,seed,x,b) with a common 2^o(n)-bit seed at each sufficiently large YES length agrees with this encoding of some satisfying assignment on at least 99% of its addresses. A randomized oracle algorithm recovers the original bit at *each* requested grid point u with probability at least 9/10. Nearest-neighbor decode each encountered inner Hadamard block (enumerate all q symbols and q bits, with deterministic tie-breaking). At most .04 of all field points can be decoded incorrectly, since a wrong block costs at least 1/4 of its bits. Choose a uniform v in F^m. For every nonzero t in F the point u+tv is uniform. Hence the expected bad fraction among those q−1 positions is ≤.04 and is at least .4 with probability at most .1. On its complement decode the resulting punctured Reed–Solomon word of degree ≤D uniquely to recover P(u) at zero. The relative unique radius is above .4 because q≥16D and q≥32. The argument also allows v=0, in which case the genuine restriction polynomial is constant. Use a fixed default on decoding failure and a fixed Boolean projection that agrees on field elements 0 and 1. Runtime and oracle queries are polynomial in n and the explicit tuple length.

By amplified Sipser–Lautemann relative to B, a single total PH predicate outputs the correct bit on the high-confidence YES seed at every address. On other seeds it still defines a total Boolean assignment. Under NP=coNP the predicate GOOD asking whether that assignment satisfies every indexed clause belongs to NP in the explicit length of (1^n,seed). Guess the good seed, then guess the certificate for GOOD. If its fixed clock is O((n+|seed|+2)^c), guess up to 2^floor(n/(4c+4)) bits. This fits the hierarchy bound and covers all sufficiently large promised YES lengths. NO lengths admit no satisfying assignment regardless of recovery accuracy. Thus the seed hypothesis implies NP≠coNP. A circuit of size 2^o(n) describing approximate bits could supply a common seed by encoding its gate list; no uniform circuit generator is required. Under NP=coNP there is a δ>0 such that infinitely many YES lengths admit no circuit of size 2^(δn) attaining 99% agreement with the encoded version of *any* satisfying assignment. Otherwise the minimum successful description has log-size o(n) along YES lengths, contradicting the seed argument.

## Tests of the missing generator

For two distinct padded assignments, Schwartz–Zippel bounds the coincidence probability of their multivariate extensions by D/q. Their binary encodings agree on precisely one half plus half this probability, at most 17/32. Thus substituting the encoding of a different assignment or the zero template cannot get 99% agreement unless the chosen template is the assignment. These observations by themselves concern the possible encodings, not alternative arbitrary bit predictors.

There is a separate black-box obstruction to turning cheap access to original bits into cheap encoded bits. On random x, the chance all coordinates avoid H₀ is at least 1−mh/q, exceeding .87 for all large n. On such points every multivariate Lagrange basis coefficient is nonzero. For a uniform inner mask, on average half the 2^(dn) original bits have nonzero binary coefficient in the queried encoded parity. Hence with probability at least 1/3 its support contains at least a quarter of the original positions. For a uniform random original table, any algorithm making fewer than a quarter-table queries then leaves the required parity unbiased even conditioned on its adaptive transcript. Its overall success is bounded away from .99. This argument is for oracle access without advice depending on the hidden table; syntax-aware algorithms and hierarchy-specific structure are not covered.

The approximate common-bit assumption for the encoded table implies the exact-bit assumption for the original assignment. The reverse implication has not been established. Exact original bits can contribute to exponentially large parities in the encoding. Classical worst-to-average hardness amplification and local list decoding give stronger general coding theorems, and no priority claim for this code is intended.
