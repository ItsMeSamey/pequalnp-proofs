# The cut ranks forced by a quadratic contradiction

Let P be any fixed set of pinned vertices in a simple graph, all required selected. The free vertex bits satisfy the pin equations Bz=d with d=A[P,P]1. Assume this affine slice is nonempty, write k=rank B, and observe 1ᵀd=0. For free site i let f_i=z_i((A_F z)_i+B_iᵀ1), its Boolean zero-product constraint. All polynomial identities here are over the binary Boolean cube.

Take an arbitrary subfamily of the free guards, on shore L, and call the other free vertices R. Write z=(x,y), B=[U V] and D=A[L,R]. Its sum is

    p_L(x,y)=Σ_{i∈L} f_i = 1ᵀUx + xᵀDy.

Internal free edges cancel. If p_L is affine on the pin slice, both physical cut ranks ρ(L),ρ(R) are at most k. If p_L is identically one there, both cut ranks are at most k−1, and both shores are nonempty. In particular, for three pins, every such contradiction exposes a pair of pin-free rank-at-most-two shores. A graph with all pin-free rank-two sides of size at least four eliminated and more than six free sites has no such certificate.

To prove the affine statement, take u∈ker U and vary x to x+u in the pin slice. The resulting change uᵀDy must be constant over all feasible y. Their affine space Y={y: d+Vy∈im U} has codimension k−rank U, since V onto the quotient (im U+im V)/im U has that rank. Hence Dᵀ(ker U) annihilates the direction space of Y. The rank of x↦(Ux,Dᵀx) is at most rank U+codim Y=k, precisely the promised bound on ρ(L). Switching the two shores works because the sum over all free guards is 1ᵀBz=0 on the slice.

For the constant-one case, x=0 cannot be feasible since p_L(0,y)=0, and y=0 likewise since then p_L(x,0)=1ᵀd=0. The nonempty Y omits zero; its linear span has one dimension more than its direction space. Each uᵀDy is now zero, so Dᵀ(ker U) annihilates this larger span, improving the bound by one. The same argument applies to R.

This covers all static zero-equals-one identities given by constant coefficients on the quadratic free guards and affine multipliers on pin equations (with Boolean squares reduced). In fact a quadratic polynomial vanishing on a consistent affine slice is generated in degree at most two by the defining linear equations modulo Boolean squares: switch affine coordinates to make the first k coordinates the pinned syndromes minus their required values, then each surviving monomial vanishing on that coordinate slice has a factor among these k variables. One can search deterministically for any such contradiction by parametrizing the pin slice, expanding each f_i in its squarefree monomials of degrees zero through two, and solving a linear system for coefficients λ_i with Σ λ_i f_i=1. Similarly imposing zero quadratic coefficients alone computes all affine consequences obtainable by these single sums.

A limited conclusion extends to iterative degree-two polynomial calculus. Suppose every nonempty proper free shore L has either ρ(L)>k or ρ(R)>k. On the pin slice no such p_L can be affine. The empty sum and the full sum are zero there. Hence the span of the restricted free guards contains no nonzero affine polynomial at all. For the usual degree-two polynomial calculus with Boolean axioms and one-variable multiplication permitted only when the parent has formal degree at most one, this prevents a refutation. Indeed all derivable polynomials of degree at most two restrict to the span of the guards: initial axioms do; sums preserve it; and any derivable affine parent restricts to zero since this span has no nonzero affine member. Multiplying that parent again gives zero on the slice. The statement does not cover rules that multiply a quadratic first and count only the degree of its reduced product. This is an implication under a cut-rank hypothesis, not a claim that a NO graph with that hypothesis exists.

For the [modular-prime two-shore construction](prime-affine-lookahead-shores.md), the explicit NO contradiction is exactly such a shore sum. Its exhibited rank-two cuts are thus mandatory for this kind of certificate. Neither a lower bound for unrestricted proof systems nor an all-host graph solver follows.
