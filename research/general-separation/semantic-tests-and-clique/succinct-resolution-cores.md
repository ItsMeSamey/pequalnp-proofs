# Compact NO refutations as an alternative hierarchy hypothesis

Use the fixed unary H and indexed formula φ_n from the October reduction. A description of budget K includes the unary arity a≤K of proof-line addresses, width cap w≤K, a binary final index, and a Boolean circuit producing one annotated line on input j of a bits. Budget the entire encoded description, including widths, address specifications, and circuit records. Actual proof length is at most 2^a. A line lists at most w signed global variable labels with validity flags, and gives either an original clause index, a weakening parent, or two resolution parents and a pivot. Parents must be strictly earlier. The final designated line must be empty. Unused later indices are ignored. Use ordered normalized clauses or compare their sets explicitly. A syntactically valid seed with no locally defective line describes a sound finite refutation by induction on the integer line index.

Given (1^n,1^K), the existence of such a refutation is a fixed Σ₂P test. Existentially guess the description, universally select the index to inspect, and use deterministic polynomial time in n+K to reconstruct the line, its indicated parents, or the original axiom. This is circuit-only verification with the stated annotations. Krajíček's implicit proof systems also require a formal correctness proof; those are different objects.

If all sufficiently large NO instances had such descriptions of length 2^o(n), their existence would imply NP≠coNP. Under the contrary collapse the complementary compact predicate is in NP with fixed clock (n+K+2)^c. On YES there is never a valid proof. On a NO satisfying the hypothesis choose K=2^floor(δn) for fixed positive rational δ<1/(4c+4). Thus the verifier for nonexistence decides H under its excluded time bound, after hardwiring finitely many small lengths. Equivalently under NP=coNP there are infinitely many NO lengths whose minimal budget exceeds 2^floor(δn).

A core of K bounded-width clauses on v≤3K variables gives a description of polynomial size in n+K. Store the core indices and its variables. First output the core axioms, then derive a blocking clause per partial assignment in a complete decision tree. For a full assignment find the first falsified axiom and weaken it to exclude precisely that assignment. Resolve sibling clauses at the split variable to derive the parent clause; the root is empty. Reverse heap order places children before parents, with O(v+log(K+2)) address bits. Scanning the core, computing parent indices, and generating signed labels all have polynomial-size circuits. Multi-literal weakening is allowed. In the opposite direction the standalone false heap tree in hierarchy-small-cores.md has exponential minimum core but a generator of polynomial size in n: derive the negative unit for every internal node by resolving its transition clause with both child units and resolve at the root with the positive pin. This refutes the standalone tree, not a guarded disjunction whose other branch is an arbitrary unsatisfiable φ_n.

## Same-language reduction where even succinct resolution fails

Let M=2^m and m=floor(n/2). Vertices are strings v of length m. Give each v one undirected rotation edge indexed by v joining it to cyclic rot(v); add one flip edge per pair differing only in the first bit. The resulting cubic multigraph counts self-loops twice and allows parallel edges. Flips and rotation conjugates connect all hypercube neighbors, so it is connected. Each hypercube edge in coordinate i is routed by at most 2m+1 rotation/flip/inverse-rotation steps. At each fixed coordinate i and step of that route, the current vertex is a permutation of the starting vertex. Congestion on any graph edge from all these routes is O(m²). A hypercube cut with both sides of size at least M/3 has Ω(M) boundary edges, so the cubic graph's corresponding cut has Ω(M/m²) boundary edges.

Impose parity one at the origin and zero elsewhere on incident edge variables, encoded by constant-width clauses excluding disallowed triples. Loops cancel in parity and do not cross cuts. The vertex system is inconsistent; dropping any vertex parity admits a solution by fixing all others on a spanning tree. Ben-Sasson and Wigderson's width lower bound gives Ω(M/m²) balanced-cut width, and their size–width theorem with O(M) edge variables and width-three axioms gives at least exp(Ω(M/m⁴)) proof lines. One can see the width argument directly: assign a clause the minimum number of vertex equations implying it. Resolution is subadditive for this measure, which goes from at most one to M. A balanced minimum implying vertex set U must have all its boundary edges in the clause. Otherwise omitting an endpoint equation gives a falsifying assignment that can be repaired at that endpoint by flipping the omitted cut edge. Weakening preserves the lower bound.

The width-three decoder can generate even vertex constraints for precisely the nonzero v with constant dependence per output bit. On two m-bit strings b,c, output v_i=(b_i xor b_(i+1) xor [i=1]) OR c_i, cyclically indexed. The inner map ranges over all odd words; every desired nonzero v contains a singleton that can be filled to v by c. Generate the origin separately with its odd charge. Each incident rotation, inverse rotation, and canonical flip edge label uses fixed wiring. A constant tag picks the parity-forbidden row. Thus q≤n+O(1) bits suffice for this component.

Guard φ_n and this false Tseitin formula by opposite literals to implement OR. Split (guard OR l₁ OR l₂ OR l₃) into (guard OR l₁ OR a) and (¬a OR l₂ OR l₃), with one private a per guarded clause. In the restriction where φ_n is disabled, take a=0 on each of its split clauses. On the active Tseitin side substitute a=¬l₁. The first split clause becomes tautological and the second is the original Tseitin axiom. Literal substitution and restriction of a resolution proof give a proof no longer than the original after omitting tautological lines and retaining stronger subclauses when a resolution degenerates. Thus the guarded formula on each NO length inherits the line-count lower bound. Any proof descriptor in the specified model needs at least Ω(M/m⁴) address bits, already exponential in n. This is an arity bound; it is not an unconditional gate lower for a model that permits large free index arity without charging it.

The guarded decoder still recognizes the same H with q=n+O(log n), O(n) total gates and constant per-bit locality, but differs from the literal reduction. JMV and the October witness argument supply no conclusion about proof budgets of the unmodified NO instances.
