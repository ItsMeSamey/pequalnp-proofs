# GO for the growing-density ambiguous branching-program extension

This checks the extra step beyond the fixed-density audit. For any fixed real 0<=a<1/2 and positive integer s, the argument produces an NP language that lacks, infinitely often, an exact real SUM of at most N^s acyclic existential query branching programs each of at most N ceil((log log(N+16))^a) query nodes. The exponent can be bounded above by a fixed rational below 1/2 when choosing the verifier's clocks. The integer d below is a selector dimension, independent of s.

After the common slot restriction and heavy-bit branching, a product of at most four BP atoms has b<=n remaining original variables and q=O(n(log n)^a) live query nodes. First-hit closure through fixed nodes preserves existential acceptance. Define the truth of every live node by its selected outgoing disjunction, in reverse topological order. The forward implications use O(q²) width-three clauses. The reverse implications plus the start acceptance tests give at most 2q+4 possibly long clauses. There is exactly one vector of node values satisfying the definitions for each input, even on unreachable nodes. These statements are proved separately in ambiguous-verdict.md.

Let k=ceil(log_2(n+2)). Apply width reduction only to clauses longer than k, first deleting duplicate literals and tautologies. On each branch with first k literals of a long clause simultaneously false, k previously free variables are fixed. On the other branch the long clause is replaced by the disjunction of those k literals. The branches are disjoint and exhaustive on satisfying assignments. For a uniform upper limit V=O(n(log n)^a) on both variables and possibly long clauses, the number of leaves is at most binom(V+floor(V/k),floor(V/k)) times a harmless constant adjustment of V. Hence its logarithm is O(V log(k+1)/k)=o(n). The original width-three clauses persist without growing in width. At a leaf there remain O(V) marked clauses of width at most k to encode. Variables fixed on a branch contribute no multiplicity; untouched, disappearing declared variables remain free.

**First-true encoding.** Order the literals l_0,...,l_(h-1) of one marked nonempty clause, h<=k. Fix an integer d>=1, let U be the least positive integer with U^d>=k, and introduce d groups of U fresh one-hot digit bits. In each group, impose pairwise at-most-one clauses. To impose at least one, use an OR chain with bidirectional gate definitions and force its final output to one. The chain uses at most U−1 auxiliary bits and clauses of width at most three, with unique values given the digit bits. For each tuple t in [U]^d, let z(t) be its zero-based base-U index in [0,U^d−1], and let G_t denote the conjunction of its d selected digit bits. For z(t)>=h add the width-d clause negating G_t. For valid z=z(t), add separately the guarded unit requirements G_t => l_z and G_t => not l_j for every j<z. Each has width d+1. Compute the radix by integer arithmetic; unused padded indices have exactly the same prohibition when h<k.

An unsatisfied clause has no admissible tuple. For a satisfied clause its least true position supplies a tuple meeting every guarded requirement; any other tuple either names a false literal, skips an earlier true literal, or is invalid. Exactly one digit assignment and one assignment to its OR-chain bits survive. Empty clauses reject immediately. Constants and clauses of width at most three can bypass the gadget. Distinct gadgets use private fresh variables, preserving parsimony after conjunction with all other clauses. Per gadget there are at most 2dU variable bits. The tuple count U^d is at most 2^d k for fixed d; the valid-index requirements number at most h(h+1)/2. The pairwise digit constraints contribute O_d(U²). Thus each gadget uses O_d(k²) clauses and the entire instance still has polynomially many clauses. Selector dimension d is fixed throughout; the growing cutoff k is never passed as the width to the October theorem.

The final width is max(3,d+1) and the final variable count u is at most O_d(n(log n)^(a+1/d)). Apply the arbitrary-density bound in the [pinned October 25 theorem, lines 70–77](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/92b516ef8e/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/main_full_version.tex). For fixed d the per-leaf counting exponent is O_d(n(log n)^(a+1/d−1/2) loglog n)=o(n) when d>1/(1/2−a); small u are handled directly. For a=1/3, d=8 works and yields width nine. Combining this with the truncation gives an o(n) inner exponent; the outer heavy assignments and raw scans retain the clock 4delta n+O_s(epsilon n)+o(n). This proof imposes no path-ambiguity bound.

**Smaller width option.** With cutoff k=ceil((log n)^beta) the two inequalities needed are a<beta and a+beta/d<1/2. Taking beta=d/(2(d+1)) balances both powers. For a=1/3, d=3 and beta=3/8 give width four and both exponents are O(n loglog n/(log n)^(1/24)). This refinement is optional.
