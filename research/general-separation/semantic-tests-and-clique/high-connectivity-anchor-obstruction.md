# Linearly many misleading anchors on highly connected scalar-only hosts

For every t≥1 there are simple graphs with three named pins and Θ(t) vertices and vertex connectivity Ω(t). On each graph the pin cuts have binary rank three, the global site-endomorphism algebra is scalar, and the erased stabilizer admits neither a local CSS structure nor a local field structure over GF(4). One graph is NO and has t mutually [cross-checked off-diagonal anchors](three-pin-many-anchor-bound.md). A second graph is YES with the same t anchors, each individually blocking every true witness in any common-chart extension. Both claims concern the original chart, using a dependency (x,Ax) of [A|I] on the same indexed vertices as the chart evaluating the other vectors.

For the construction make two disjoint sets L,R of m=5t+7 vertices each. Inside each set start with a clique on t marked vertices. At each marked vertex identify one vertex of a fresh pentagon with that vertex. Also add a disjoint ordinary seven-cycle inside each set. Use all edges between L and R. Denote the three pins by p,q,r and join q to r, q to all of L, and r to all of R. Join p to all of L and to all of R except a site h on its seven-cycle. There are no further edges in the NO graph. Its pin-cut rows, restricted to L and R, are 1_L, 1_R and 1_L+1_R+e_h, hence independent. After at most m deletions, if both shores survive then their complete join is connected and every surviving pin has a neighbor in it (for q or r the respective shore survives). If one shore is deleted completely, all pins remain, and the other shore is joined through its adjacent pin and p. Thus connectivity is at least m+1; pin q has exactly that degree.

In any physical support q requires an odd number of selected L vertices, and r the same on R. Sum the degrees of the selected L vertices modulo two. Internal edges cancel. Each selected L vertex sees the two selected pins p and q, and the complete join contributes odd times odd, which is odd. This disproves feasibility. Index the marks within both cliques by 1 through t. The i-th guess selects the pins and the i-th mark in each shore, so each pin has even degree. When checking against a different guess, each marked vertex sees its clique neighbor, the opposite-shore marked vertex, and its two adjacent pins. All these parities are even. Thus the t guesses actually cross-check pairwise.

For the YES graph add one vertex s adjacent to q,r,every vertex in L, and h. It has more than m neighbors, preserving the lower bound on vertex connectivity; retaining the previous columns preserves the pin-cut rank. Selecting p,q,r,s gives a triangle on q,r,s and the isolated p. Every true selection requires s because fixing it to zero restores the NO graph. But s sees three selected neighbors under each old guess, so its odd parity forces it absent in any chart compatible with that guess. The old guesses remain mutually compatible. This proves the claimed gap even against a greedy common-chart search that reads all cross equations.

## Local algebra checks

For adjacency H write the four coefficients of any sitewise preserving binary linear map as a_i,b_i,c_i,d_i in row-major order. Preservation of the graph space {(x,Hx)} is precisely diag(c)+diag(d)H=H diag(a)+H diag(b)H. At nonadjacent distinct vertices i,j, the sum of b over their common neighbors therefore vanishes.

Inside L one can find a pair in different internal components with no common internal neighbor. For every site w in L there is also a nonadjacent pair whose only common internal neighbor is w. Use the two neighbors of w on its own private pentagon or seven-cycle. This remains true for clique sites and for the private neighbors of clique sites. The external common-neighbor set is identical for every such pair: R,p,q and, on the YES graph, s. Subtract the baseline equation to obtain b_w=0 for every w in L.

For any j in R other than h the nonadjacent pair q,j has common neighbors precisely L and r, giving b_r=0. On the YES graph q,h additionally have s in common, giving b_s=0. Take two R vertices in different internal components, both different from h. Their common neighbors are L,r,p, so b_p=0. Using the private-cycle pairs on the R shore now gives b_w=0 for every w in R; their external common neighbors all have zero coefficient. The L baseline gives b_q=0. With all b zero the graph preservation equation enforces c_i=0 and d_i=a_j along each edge ij. Connectedness and an odd cycle force all a and d to be the same scalar.

Finally erase the three pin sites in the stabilizer, requiring both coordinates there to vanish before puncturing. Vectors x supported on L with even weight satisfy the pin rows. Their graph words vanish outside L: on R the complete join sees even parity; on s in the YES case the same is true. Their restriction to L has ordinary internal graph matrix H_L. Any preserving sitewise map takes these words to words still zero outside L, with the same internal graph equation. Consider an edge ij of the separate seven-cycle inside L. Test that equation on x=e_j+e_f where f is any vertex in the other internal component of L. Contributions of f at i vanish in all terms of the graph preservation equation since it has no internal path of length at most two to i. The endpoints of ij have no common internal neighbor, so d_i=a_j. If all local matrices had trace one, adjacent a-values on this odd cycle would be different, an impossibility. Both a local CSS projector and a local GF(4)-multiplication require a preserving map of trace one at every site by the [erased stabilizer criteria](erased-three-pin-stabilizer.md). Thus neither recognition applies. No inference about alternative graph-aware search rules follows.
