# A uniform E oracle against polynomial-clock parity predictors with logarithmic advice

Fix positive integers b,c and an integer D>b+c+1. Put N=2^n. There is a deterministic E-computable oracle A with at most one positive entry in each (n,x)-block such that the parity table

F_n(x) = XOR over y of length Dn of A(enc(x,y)), for |x|=n,

has the following property. For every fixed deterministic oracle predictor, at every sufficiently large n and for every advice word of at most cn bits, the predicted N-bit table has more than floor(N/2)−ceil(sqrt((c+3)nN)) errors, or is malformed. The predictor may either output the entire table on unary input 1^n within N^b native transitions, or output one bit separately for each x in time N^b per row with the same advice. A timeout or malformed row invalidates its table. Oracle calls to arbitrary finite words are allowed, with at most one membership query per native transition. Work and output tapes use the ordinary serial bit model. The theorem addresses fixed b,c and D; the E-computability exponent of the resulting A may depend on them.

**Construction.** Use the injective encoding enc(x,y)=1^n 0 x y with n=|x| and |y|=Dn. At stage n enumerate the first n machine descriptions in length-lexicographic order for each of the two output conventions, and every advice word of length at most cn. Simulate each clocked predictor, independently on all N inputs in the row convention. All simulations answer yes precisely on the positives placed by previous stages; record every queried word. Maintain cumulative lists of positive entries and all query words, regardless of their length and even if they address a future block. The number of simulated tables is at most 4nN^c. There are at most 4nN^{b+c+1} queries at this stage. Through stage n the count is at most 8n²N^{b+c+1}, which is less than N^D for all sufficiently large n. Small stages may assign no positive entries.

For each x, choose a Dn-bit y whose encoded address is absent from the cumulative query list. Each block has N^D entries and the count bound is global, so such a choice exists independently in every block. Use the first available y. The chosen address is fresh even relative to earlier algorithms that queried far-future lengths. No positive placed earlier belongs to the present block.

For every complete table from these simulations, consider the Hamming ball of radius floor(N/2)−ceil(sqrt((c+3)nN)). For a uniform N-bit word, the probability of membership in any one ball is at most exp(−2(c+3)n) by the fair-binomial tail bound. Multiplying by 4nN^c gives less than one eventually. Construct a word f outside every ball deterministically: for each next bit choose the extension minimizing the conditional expected number of containing balls. For a remaining suffix of length k and a known partial disagreement count e, the conditional ball mass is the sum of binomial(k,j)/2^k over allowed j≤radius−e, truncated to [0,k]. Compute both candidate sums exactly with a common denominator. Their average is the previous conditional expectation; the integer final count is zero. Empty lists cause no problem. Set the chosen block address positive exactly when f(x)=1. Every recorded query answer is stable in all later stages by the address avoidance rule and by retaining old positives. Thus all completed simulated tables are exactly their final-oracle tables within the clocks. Each fixed predictor is included at every sufficiently large stage.

**Uniform resources.** At stage n there are N^{O_{b,c}(1)} native transitions, and every queried word has length at most N^b+O(n) on the standard initially blank query tape. A universal interpreter for machines with descriptions of length O(log(n+2)) has polynomial overhead (even rescanning the whole simulated configuration per step suffices). Linear scans of the cumulative lists answer queries and check candidate addresses in polynomial-in-N time: the list has polynomially many entries each of polynomial length, including those from earlier stages. Enumerating up to all N^D candidate suffixes for each x costs polynomial time for fixed D. Storing all output rows and evaluating both conditional expectations per bit, even recomputing binomial sums with O(N)-bit integers, likewise costs polynomial time. On input z reject malformed encodings directly. For a valid encoding parse n, replay stages 1 through n once while carrying the explicit cumulative data, and test the indicated address. Total time is at most n 2^{O_{b,c,D}(n)}, hence 2^{O_{b,c,D}(|z|)}. This reconstruction invokes no oracle for its own semantics. Finally F belongs to E: one may replay the same stages and read the selected f(x), or evaluate each block bit directly with the fixed E algorithm and take parity.

**Limits.** The public construction can itself be run with its larger time exponent. The advice allowance cn is logarithmic in table length N. At κN bits the number of descriptions is doubly exponential in n, and the simultaneous transcript freezing and polynomial-time enumeration above no longer apply. The proof diagonalizes against fixed polynomial clocks and bounded advice; it does not separate standard P from NP. For the classical background on oracle diagonalization see [Baker, Gill and Solovay](https://doi.org/10.1137/0204037).
