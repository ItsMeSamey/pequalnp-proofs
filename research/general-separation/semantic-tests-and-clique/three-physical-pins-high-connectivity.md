# Three physical pins, one pin edge, and highly connected inputs

For any fixed ε>0, deciding whether three prescribed vertices lie in an induced subgraph with all degrees even is polynomial-time Turing equivalent to the same question restricted to hosts of order N with vertex connectivity at least N^{1−ε} and exactly one edge among the three pins. For ε≥1 one may use a smaller positive exponent parameter. The algorithm below reconstructs an original witness from witnesses to its positive queries. It allows disconnected supports. The result gives a transfer of the decision problem, without asserting hardness or a general polynomial algorithm.

## Isolating the three-bit interface

Remove the three pins P and form the connected components D of the remaining graph. Let B be the three-by-|D| binary incidence of edges from P to D. With all pins present, a vector x on D is admissible locally exactly when every selected j has (A_D x)ⱼ=(Bᵀ1)ⱼ. Its boundary signature is Bx. Every such signature has even weight by the handshake identity on the selected vertices of D. The zero signature is available via the empty support. Signatures from separate components combine by XOR. The target is the vector of degree parities in G[P]. A four-state dynamic program combines the possible signatures and stores predecessor witnesses.

For each D test each of the three nonzero even signatures eᵤ+eᵥ. Use a graph with D and the same three mandatory singleton vertices, retaining the inherited incidences B and putting uv as the *only* edge within the pins. Because all pins are present, this precisely asks for the specified boundary signature and the required local degrees. If a missing pin-to-D neighborhood belongs to u or v, that endpoint has forced odd degree, so reject. If only the third pin lacks neighbors, it is isolated, and feasibility is equivalent to uv lying on a cycle: a shortest alternate u-v path induces the required cycle. A one-vertex D can always be checked directly. Therefore the only queries needing a solver have |D|≥2, connected D, and a neighbor in D for every pin. There are at most 3(n−3) such queries over a host of order n.

## Raising vertex connectivity while retaining actual pins

In each query replace every vertex of D by t independent false twins. Join adjacent groups completely and connect each pin to all twins of each old neighbor. Leave the three pin vertices and their one mutual edge unchanged. A base witness lifts by selecting a single representative per selected group. Conversely, in any witness of the enlarged graph retain a base vertex precisely when its group has odd occupancy. Contributions of an occupied even group to every neighboring degree vanish modulo two. A retained group had an actual selected twin, and its degree parity in the compressed support is that twin's degree parity, including all incident pin edges. The same parity comparison applies at every pin. This gives a witness in both directions, including at the zero-occupancy groups, and even preserves the minimum witness cardinality.

Deleting fewer than t vertices leaves a representative of every core group. Those groups together induce a connected graph: D is connected with at least two vertices, so twins in the same group can also connect through a neighboring group. Every surviving pin attaches to a surviving group. The enlarged graph hence has vertex connectivity at least t. For 0<ε<1 choose t=ceil((n+3)^{1/ε}); then N=t|D|+3≤t(n+3), and t^ε≥(n+3)^{1−ε} gives t≥N^{1−ε}. The output and its explicitly listed edges have polynomial size for each fixed ε. The reverse direction is simply restriction of the original problem.

For a concrete NO family use a two-vertex connected core with both its vertices adjacent to all three pins, and let uv be the only pin edge. The same core parity must be one at u and zero at the isolated-in-P third pin. This contradiction persists under every blowup, including ones with connectivity linear in N. Thus high connectivity does not imply that every pin triple can be satisfied.

## Compiling three affine readouts into exactly three actual pins

For completeness let a loopless base adjacency A and three binary rows L specify the target Lx=t with all selected degrees even. The zero target has the empty witness; inconsistent dependent rows can be rejected. For a nonzero target an invertible change of the three row coordinates makes t=(1,1,0), whether or not the rows are independent. Put K₁₂=K₂₁=K₁₃=K₃₁=1 and all other entries of K zero. Then Kt=1 (the all-ones column) and K is alternating. Use interior adjacency A+LᵀKL, attach three new pins by incidence L, and put exactly edge 12 among the pins. There are no loops because the update is alternating. The pin checks force Lx=t. On precisely that target, the interior update LᵀKLx cancels the fixed degree contributions Lᵀ1 of the selected pins. The original interior parity checks are thus recovered at every selected site, with no requirement on the unselected sites. Witnesses lift and project by simply adding or removing three actual pinned vertices. [Mixed two-readout equivalence](symmetric-three-terminal-p3.md)

## Constraint on forcing replicas by a tree of gadgets

In every feasible induced graph, each biconnected edge block is itself even: decompose the edges into cycles, each of which lies in a single block. Consequently in an inclusion-minimal feasible support each leaf block of a component with at least two edge blocks contains a required pin outside its attachment vertex. Otherwise remove the leaf block's nonattachment vertices. All surviving induced edges at the articulation lose an even number of incidences, and no pin was removed. Distinct pendant blocks consume different pins, so at most three such blocks can be essential. This excludes a forcing scheme whose correctness requires arbitrarily many distinct terminal-free pendant blocks in every witness. It does not rule out replication within a biconnected block or through interfaces of size greater than one.
