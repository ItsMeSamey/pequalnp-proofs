# Stretched-exponential scales for independent lookups

Keep m=2^t, P=2^floor(log₂(m/t)), and permit all per-atom budgets W≤n(log n)^b for any fixed b, where n=r(log₂P+Pt+m)=Θ(rm). The unsigned [sound-cover](independent-isa-positive.md) and [signed-correlation](independent-isa-signed-weight.md) single-atom estimates hold for every r. Increasing the number of independent banks changes the hardness as a function of the actual input length.

Take t≥16. In the formula schedule set r=2^(t−ceil(sqrt(t))). In the program schedule set r=2^floor(t/2−sqrt(t)). These positive integer functions are nondecreasing, while m doubles at each t, so the displayed input lengths increase strictly. For each schedule the AND or balanced XOR predicate from the companion proofs, rejecting all other lengths, is a single explicit P language with linear shared circuits. The program schedule also provides a valid formula lower bound at the program rate. The o(1) terms below allow dependence on the chosen fixed budget exponent b.

Put D=1+W/(rP)=t^{O_b(1)}. In the formula correlation bound choose L=ceil((D²m/r)^(1/3)). In the program bound choose L=ceil(sqrt(D sqrt(m)/r)). These unrounded choices tend to infinity at the respective schedules, so rounding costs a constant factor. The per-bank brackets of the Rademacher correlation bound become respectively O((rD/m)^(1/3)) and O(sqrt(rD/sqrt(m))). In their schedules each bracket is ≤2^(−c sqrt(t)) eventually for an absolute c>0 and every fixed b. The corresponding per-bank brackets CrD/m and CrD/sqrt(m) for sound covers satisfy the same estimate.

For formulas log₂n=2t−ceil(sqrt(t))+O(1), giving r sqrt(t)=n^(1/2−o(1)). For programs log₂n=t+floor(t/2−sqrt(t))+O(1), giving r sqrt(t)=n^(1/3−o(1)). In particular, for each fixed ε>0 and large hard lengths, the following statement holds with β=1/2 in the formula schedule and β=1/3 in the program schedule:

* An individual atom’s advantage in predicting the balanced XOR output on uniform inputs is at most 2^(−n^(β−ε)).
* A sound cover of the AND predicate requires at least 2^(n^(β−ε)) terms. The uniform accepting probability 2^(−r) changes the logarithm by r, absorbed by the r sqrt(t) bound.
* Any exact signed real SUM for AND, or a real SUM for XOR with uniform average absolute error bounded away from 1/2 on the smaller side, requires total absolute coefficient weight at least 2^(n^(β−ε)). With individual weights ≤2^(n^(β−η)) for fixed η>0, one still needs at least 2^(n^(β−o(1))) terms by subtracting n^(β−η) from the log weight lower bound. The same conclusion holds for integer-weight threshold classification with these weight caps and the half-integer normalization.
* The normalized margin of any correctly classifying real threshold of the atoms on balanced XOR is at most 2^(−n^(β−ε)). This does not assume its inputs are independent after evaluation.

These are methods for restricted representations. At the formula schedule a gate can still branch and reuse a primary multiplexer in the ordinary linear circuit. Also every Boolean function on n bits is the sum of at most 2^n width-n singleton indicators. Thus a general ℓ₁ lower bound coupled only with Cramer's exp(O(S log S)) upper bound in terms of sparsity S could not prove superpolynomial sparsity by this route.

The AND target itself has a small integer threshold representation at the advertised near-linear budget. For each bank and primary row, conjoin that row guard with the corresponding payload multiplexer. At most one term per bank is true, and the entire AND is the threshold “at least r” on all rP unit-weight terms. This is compatible with the AND results, which concern sound covers and exact signed linear equality. The margin and threshold-count lower bounds above are for balanced XOR.
