# A sharp density threshold for deterministic witness testing

For A⊆{0,1}^m and a bit prefix u let d_A(u) be the fraction of its length-m completions in A. The prefix density condition at δ requires each prefix to have density either zero or at least δ, including the empty prefix. A universal hitting set H must meet every nonempty such A. Write h_m(δ) for the smallest size.

* If 1/2<δ≤1 then h_m(δ)=1. A nonempty node cannot have an empty child, since its density would be at most 1/2. Induction makes every leaf present.
* At δ=1/2 one has h_m(δ)=m+1. The explicit witnesses are the all-zero string and the m strings containing exactly one one.
* For 0<δ<1/2 one has h_m(δ)≥[2(1−δ)]^m. Every fixed density below one half permits an exponential requirement.

For the half-density upper bound let A avoid the displayed strings. Its zero child avoids the corresponding list at depth m−1 and hence is empty by induction. A nonempty root would require the one child to be completely full, contradicting the absence of 10…0. At m=0 the list contains the empty string. For optimality, if neither child of H hits all nonempty half-dense suffix families, choose an avoiding positive family in each child. Their combination has root density at least one half. Thus some child hits all such families, needing at least m strings at depth m−1. The other child needs a marked string since a full family in that sole child has root density one half.

For the below-half bound remove any proposed H from the full cube. Process each depth from m−1 up to the root. At every node with positive remaining density below δ delete its remaining descendants. Previously verified descendants which survive keep their densities. If a node is pruned, its previously erased fraction was greater than 1−δ and is now one. Else its erased fraction stays fixed. Summing across equal-sized nodes of one depth, the erased fraction increases by a factor at most 1/(1−δ). It started at |H|/2^m. If |H|<[2(1−δ)]^m the final erased fraction is less than one. The surviving family avoids H and meets the condition. We do not claim a matching exponent below the threshold.

For an explicit forbidden list H and rational δ, the pruning can be carried out using just its prefix trie with O(m|H|+1) nodes. Absent children contain no forbidden leaves. At a marked leaf the deleted count is one. At every internal trie node add child counts, compare the remaining count against δ times its capacity, and record whether the whole subtree is deleted. These integers have O(m+1) bits. Membership follows a single path, rejecting at a deleted ancestor and accepting when it exits the trie. Polynomial-size explicit lists yield polynomial-size membership circuits. A polynomial-time list constructor yields polynomial-time uniform avoiding families when the growth bound permits. This statement takes H as input and does not produce a single easy language hard for arbitrary polynomial-time algorithms.

In particular if a language has a deterministic polynomial-time verifier V(x,w), polynomial-time computable witness length m=poly(|x|), existential acceptance, and the half-density condition in the raw binary witness for every x, the language is in P. Evaluate V on the m+1 displayed strings. More generally a computable input-dependent binary prefix ordering can be used if the verifier and path decoder both run in polynomial time and give bijective leaf coordinates. This sufficient test applies to SAT only if an appropriate uniform verifier is supplied. A conjunction fixing all m bits has classical witness density 2^(−m) at the root. Appending unrestricted dummy witness bits preserves that fraction. A fixed density below half still gives a one-sided randomized test by sampling witnesses. The deterministic hitting lower concerns a list common to all allowed witness sets. It does not exclude a new algorithm using a verifier's code.

The strength of requiring density at every viable prefix differs from the next-bit probability bounds in [Santha and Vazirani](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~vazirani/pubs/quasi.pdf). The finite checker enumerated all nonempty families through m=4. At depth four the exact hitting numbers at densities 1/4, 1/3, 3/8, 1/2 and 2/3 are 12, 8, 8, 5 and 1.
