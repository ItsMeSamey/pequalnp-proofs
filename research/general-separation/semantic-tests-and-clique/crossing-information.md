# Repeated visits and broadcast cost for Hamiltonicity

Write the adjacency matrix of an N-vertex directed graph as a word of m=N² bits, in any fixed order. Let W be uniform on the (N−1)! inputs consisting of a single directed Hamiltonian cycle. The estimates also hold for bipartite perfect matching with W uniform on the N! permutation matrices. The sole combinatorial input is the following bound from [the balanced rectangle proof](balanced-tour-rectangles.md). For every partition of the m input bits with both sides of size at most 2m/3, an accepting rectangle sound on inputs with at most N ones has W-mass at most 2^{−(b−o(1))N}, where b=log₂(3/2). The error is uniform over the partitions.

## Every accepting run for almost every tour

Fix a deterministic or nondeterministic multitape Turing machine with a fixed finite alphabet and control. One primary tape, initially holding the adjacency word, has a single head that moves by at most one cell per step and starts at the left end. Every other tape is an initially blank work tape with a unit-speed head starting at its origin. The machine accepts positive inputs and has no accepting run on promised negative inputs. For a run define S_t as the total number of work-tape cells in the intervals visited through step t, excluding the primary tape. This charges the position of a head even when the scanned work tape is blank. Nondeterministic choices are local machine transitions. The primary tape may be read-only or writable.

With W-probability 1−2^{−Ω(N)}, every accepting run satisfies

    Σ_{t at which the primary head moves} (1+S_t) ≥ Ω(N³).

Fix a cut j between adjacent primary cells with m/3 ≤ j ≤ 2m/3. Immediately after each crossing record the finite control and a snapshot of every work tape, including its head and origin. The primary head position and the direction are determined by the cut and crossing parity. A snapshot is self-delimiting in O(1+S_t) bits. Use a fixed alphabet of marked cell symbols with tape delimiters, and include the control and an end-of-list token. The encoding length of the accepting transcript is at most O(1)+C Σ(1+S_t) over crossings of this cut, for a constant C depending on the machine.

For a fixed transcript, the set of inputs admitting an accepting run with that transcript is a rectangle. The left and right segments can be chosen independently to match the prescribed snapshots at both ends of each visit. Each side retains the changes to its own primary cells across visits. Choose compatible local segments and concatenate them at the prescribed crossings. Fresh nondeterministic choices in the joined run are permitted. The already-visited intervals in a snapshot can be regarded as marker tracks updated locally with each work-tape move. Machine soundness makes this an accepting rectangle on the promise, including when the computation on inputs outside the promise is unconstrained.

For fixed 0<α<b, fewer than 2^{αN+1} transcripts have length at most αN. The chance that the input admits such an accepting transcript at some balanced cut is at most m·2^{−(b−α−o(1))N}. Outside this exceptional set every accepting run pays Ω(N) summed snapshot cost at every balanced cut. A unit primary-head move crosses at most one cut. Summing over the Θ(N²) balanced cuts proves the assertion. In particular a machine using at most S visited work cells on its accepting runs requires Ω(N³/(S+1)) time on almost every pure witness. The proof permits adaptive rereads and variable running time. In terms of the word length m the cumulative bound is Ω(m^{3/2}). Hennie introduced crossing sequences and proved the segment-splicing property for one-tape off-line Turing machines. [Hennie, §II](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0019-9958(65)90399-2)

## Circuit signals with arbitrary reuse

Place the m distinct primary input nodes of a Boolean circuit on a line, one at each integer position from 1 to m. Other gates may be placed arbitrarily. Choose a cut in each open gap between adjacent inputs, avoiding gate positions. For each producer v (input or gate), let L_v count the balanced gaps whose chosen cut separates v from at least one consumer. A signal with several receivers across one cut contributes just once. Write Y_v for the output of v under W and H for Shannon entropy in bits. For every circuit computing the predicate on the sparse promise,

    Σ_v L_v H(Y_v) ≥ (b−o(1)) N · #{balanced gaps}.

At a fixed cut, assign values to all distinct producer signals that cross. Each side checks the gates and primary bits located on its own side, substituting the assigned bits for remote operands, requiring its own crossing producers to have their assigned values, and requiring the circuit output to be one on the side containing it. The conjunction defines an accepting rectangle. Acyclic evaluation makes every consistent accepting assignment genuine. Thus every value of the joint crossing vector has W-probability at most 2^{−(b−o(1))N}, regardless of correlations. Its Shannon entropy is at least (b−o(1))N. Subadditivity bounds this by the sum of H(Y_v) over crossing producers. Sum over cuts. Even unweighted broadcast span is Ω(N³). The argument permits arbitrary Boolean gate functions and unrestricted reuse. The cost depends on the placement. A long broadcast contributes at many gaps, so this bound gives no superpolynomial gate lower bound. Thompson used communication across geometric bisections to bound VLSI area and time in a different physical model. [Thompson](https://doi.org/10.1145/800135.804401)

## Sharp example and limits

For row-major exact permutation recognition, a multitape machine scans each row while scanning an N-cell writable column-seen array. It rejects duplicate columns and rows with other than one occupied cell. Rewind the work head once per row. This costs O(N²) time and O(N) space and attains the cumulative order. The format can omit headers. Count the input length in a preliminary scan using amortized constant-time binary increments, compute its integer square root, reject nonsquares and rewind. The same checker unrolls into an O(N²)-gate circuit of broadcast span O(N³). At each input position combine the column flag from the previous row with the new input and update the local row count and rejection flag. Column connections span N gaps, row and rejection updates span O(1). All gates for a cell can be placed next to that input.

Sparse-promise Hamiltonicity itself has a deterministic polynomial algorithm using O(log N) writable cells in row-major order. Follow the unique outgoing neighbor from vertex 1 for N steps, rescanning the input to find and validate the outgoing edge at each step. Reject a missing or duplicate successor, an early return, or failure to return on step N. In a deterministic successor walk whose first return is at N, the first N vertices are distinct. The matching and HC bounds therefore coexist with polynomial-time decision on the very inputs used in the proof.

The single physical input head is material. An oblivious branching program checks permutation matrices with constant width and two differently ordered scans, checking rows in row-major order and columns in column-major order. One random-access jump can traverse many physical cuts at once. Multiple independent input heads also invalidate the local-segment simulation. The proof supplies neither a bound for those access models nor a separation of P and NP.
