# Counting grouped Euler switches at small cutrank

Use the binary alternating matrix, bundle order, and cut profiles from [the decision proof](./switch-cutrank.md). Each physical switch assignment is counted once. Relative reference masks for the two choices of one bundle remain distinct.

**Characteristic-two generating polynomial.** Label the two choices in bundle i with 1 and an indeterminate z_i. At each cut retain a profile basis consisting of actual prefix assignments. Express each extended candidate as a binary linear combination of that cut's basis. If a candidate has weight p, add p to the weight of each basis member used in its expression. The scalar product identity preserves the resulting polynomial against every fixed remaining suffix. This invariant holds before and after extension by a legal bundle mask, since extension restricts the suffix and multiplies by its label. The final result is the sum, over valid assignments, of their distinct selector monomials over the binary field. This gives a layered algebraic branching program of width at most 2^r with O(h·4^r) edges, constructible within the binary decision preprocessing bound. Setting every selector to one gives the parity of the number of witnesses.

**Exact integer counts.** At each cut, profiles of length 2^r have a succinct description. Write K=A[H] and E=U[H,:]. Change the basis on H by binary congruence to split K into an invertible alternating block D and a zero block of dimension d. Write E₁ and C for the two corresponding parts of the transformed E. If C lacks full row rank then a radical vector survives in every augmented principal matrix and the profile is identically zero. Otherwise d≤r. Eliminate D by a Schur complement; its determinant is one. The T entry of the profile is the determinant over the binary field of

\[
\begin{pmatrix}0_d&C[:,T]\\C[:,T]^\mathsf{T}&J[T]\end{pmatrix},
\qquad J=E_1^\mathsf{T}D^{-1}E_1.
\]

The empty D contributes J=0. The matrix J is alternating. The key (d,C,J), plus a zero key, thus gives at most 1+(r+1)2^{r²+binom(r,2)} possible representations. Equal keys suffice to merge states; canonicalization across unequal keys is unnecessary. Store one actual prefix per key together with its integer multiplicity. Identical profiles give identical acceptance against every suffix, so replacing prefixes by the representative with their summed multiplicity preserves the total witness count even if equivalent prefixes would yield different keys at later cuts. The algorithm uses h·poly(n)·2^{O(r²)} time and poly(n)·2^{O(r²)} space, with at most h+1 bits per count. This is polynomial when r=O(√log n).

**A residual-count obstruction.** For general binary alternating matrices, one cut of rank r (r≥2) can have 2^{binom(r,2)} distinct Boolean residual functions. Use r suffix probes with V=I and no suffix edges. For each i<j put an isolated left edge uv in a two-element bundle, allowing the empty set or both vertices. Its U rows are the i-th and j-th coordinate vectors, respectively. When chosen, this invertible edge contributes a single symmetric elementary off-diagonal pair to the Schur complement J. All alternating J occur. A suffix selecting exactly probes i and j accepts iff J_ij=1. Thus all these residual functions differ. The construction concerns general alternating matrices; a native Euler interlacement realization would require a separate proof.
