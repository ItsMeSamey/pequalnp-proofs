# Reachability under a single linear switch

Over F₂ consider the autonomous map U(x)=M_{ℓ(x)}x on s bits, where M₀,M₁ are supplied binary s-by-s matrices and ℓ is a supplied linear functional. From a designated state ask whether a designated linear flag is ever one. This problem is PSPACE-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions, even when both matrices and the resulting piecewise map are invertible. On the same restricted inputs, returning one designated linear bit at the first hit of a different linear flag is PSPACE-hard under the promise that this first hit exists. Every description in the reduction has polynomial bit length. The construction below gives a direct proof. Related results on prescribed iterates of succinct reversible circuits and piecewise-linear transformations appear in [Eppstein, The Complexity of Iterated Reversible Computation](https://theoretics.episciences.org/12313).

**Simulating a reversible step circuit.** Take a reversible circuit W on w bits, presented as a list of NOT, CNOT and Toffoli gates with distinct target and controls. Use one bank of w+1 bits per gate. Each bank includes a homogeneous unit marker. Initially exactly one bank contains the intended state and a marker one, and all other banks are zero. A tick simultaneously applies a gate within each bank, then shifts the banks cyclically. NOT toggles its target by the marker and CNOT toggles its target by the local control. Take ℓ equal to the sum of the first control coordinates in the Toffoli banks. In a Toffoli bank, toggle its target by its second control if the global selector is one. Under either fixed selector each local operation is an invertible linear transvection or the identity. The common selector is unchanged by every within-bank operation, because every first control contributing to ℓ stays fixed in its own bank. Hence the combined within-bank piecewise operation is an involution on the full cube. The shift is invertible, so U itself is bijective even on states with many simultaneously populated banks. In the one-active-bank domain, every operation implements the intended local gate and leaves inactive banks zero. One full cycle computes W. Monitoring just one coordinate in the initial bank tests a macrostep-boundary bit, since that bank vanishes at all other phases. A dummy identity initial phase allows a distinct monitored bit and a nonzero selector with disjoint support. A redundant pair of identical Toffolis on additional fresh wires can ensure nonzero ℓ. A linear change of basis can make the selector and the flag two designated coordinates.

**Building a reversible step with a known observation time.** Fix a halting deterministic polynomial-space decider for TQBF. On instance v let f be its transition on p-bit configurations, computable by a polynomial-size circuit constructed in polynomial time from v. Include a decision bit in each halting configuration and make these configurations fixed points. From the initial configuration a, the state f^{2^p}(a) is therefore halting. Denote that state by a*. We describe a reversible circuit W of polynomial size and a schedule of exponentially many macrosteps. The full schedule returns all data to their initial values. This prevents spurious yes answers after an unsuccessful first pass.

On designated disjoint p-bit registers A,B, a leaf operation is B ← B XOR f(A). It is an involution on all A,B. Implement it by computing f with reversible gates into clean scratch, XORing into B, then reversing the computation. Such clean-scratch circuits follow directly from reversible evaluations of binary Boolean gates into fresh target bits. At depth d use d additional p-bit work registers, initially zero, to implement B ← B XOR f^{2^d}(A). At depth d+1 use a fresh zero work register C and execute in order the depth-d subroutines on (A,C),(C,B),(A,C), with the d lower work registers shared. The first call computes the midpoint in C, the second updates B, and the third restores C. Every completed subroutine preserves its source and clears all its own work registers. The depth-p circuit takes L=3^p leaf operations. This is the reversible checkpoint technique of [Bennett](https://doi.org/10.1137/0218053).

A leaf numbered j from zero to L−1 is addressable in poly(p) time. Descend through the p most-to-least significant base-three digits of j. At a node with active endpoint pair A,B, let C be the work register reserved for that depth. Digits 0 and 2 select (A,C), and digit 1 selects (C,B). Thus the endpoint indices depend only on j. Ordinary uniform polynomial-size arithmetic and selection circuits compute j's digits and perform the selected reversible leaf operation. Explicitly compute endpoint indices from j into clean scratch, copy the source register selected by those indices, evaluate f and XOR its output into the selected destination by controlled gates, then undo the computation and the source copy. The indices are distinct at each leaf. All scratch returns to zero.

Use a w₀-bit clock j modulo a power of two strictly exceeding 2L, with w₀=O(p), initially zero. A macrostep performs the leaf numbered j when j<L, the leaf numbered j−L when L≤j<2L, and the identity otherwise. Then increment j modulo 2^{w₀}. This clocked step is a reversible circuit. Each controlled leaf is bijective, and the increment is bijective. Its description is polynomial in p and the size of f. A complete clock cycle applies the depth-p XOR operation twice and resets the data, including its zero work registers. At exactly j=L the designated destination, initially zero, holds a*. Conjugate the macrostep by the reversible map toggling an otherwise untouched bit h by [j=L] times the answer bit of the destination. The clean-state implementation of this test has polynomial size. Since it starts with h=0 and j=0, the new boundary bit h equals this test at every step. It is ever one precisely on true TQBF instances. Alternatively mark h by [j=L] alone and read the destination's decision bit at the first hit. That hit then exists on all instances. Applying the bank compiler proves both hardness claims.

For membership one may simulate at most 2^s states with a binary counter and polynomial workspace. In a controller producing a full stream, the address-zero update can use the hard system and all later addresses can emit immediately. Polynomial padding of the declared output table length accommodates its polynomial-size register and gate description. A polynomial-time validator computing the exact first-output bit of total controllers of this type with unbounded delay would give P=PSPACE. The reduction concerns exact native first-hit evaluation. It supplies no lower bound on implementing the same stream by a different algorithm.

The [reversible checker](reversible_selector_banks_check.py) tested both matrix inverse identities on all basis vectors for three systems, exhaustive global injectivity on 256 and 4,096 states, and 600 sampled states in a five-bank system. The [ordinary checker](single_selector_banks_check.py) tested 2,800 microsteps of the simpler nonreversible compiler on 50 circuits over every three-bit input.
