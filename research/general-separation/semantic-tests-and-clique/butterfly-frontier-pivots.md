# A graph-only pivot bound for shared nonlinear circuits

On q=2^d wires build d butterfly layers. At layer i each wire is paired with the wire whose index differs in bit i. The two gates of each pair take both predecessor wires. A final binary tree consumes every output. A pivot is a named gate. Unroll each pivot definition and the final output backwards until reaching raw inputs or previously named gates. Suppose every such cone has at most w distinct frontier vertices, with 2≤w≤q. This is a condition on the displayed graph.

Put H=ceil(log₂(2w)). Each complete disjoint window of H butterfly layers contains at least q/2 pivots, charging its top layer but excluding its bottom layer. Fix one top vertex. Its unrolled butterfly ancestry all the way to raw inputs is a binary tree: at each reverse step a different index bit becomes free, so separate branches cannot merge. Its 2^H paths to the bottom of the window thus end at distinct vertices. If the top itself is a pivot all these paths are hit. Otherwise at most w paths avoid the pivots charged to the window. Indeed each such path extends downward to a distinct raw input or earlier pivot, by the binary-tree property of the top ancestry. These boundary vertices occur in a counted cone: take any forward path from the top to the first pivot or the final output, and include the top's uncut ancestry in its expansion.

There are q·2^H top-to-bottom paths. A pivot at height i in the window lies on exactly 2^i·2^{H−i}=2^H of them. Covering at least q(2^H−w) paths thus costs q(1−w/2^H)≥q/2 pivots. Summing yields (q/2)floor(d/H). For 2≤w≤q^{0.9} this is Ω(q log q/log(2w)) for sufficiently large q. Naming full layers at intervals floor(log₂ w) and the last layer suffices within the butterfly. Naming the binary aggregation gates costs O(q) more. This matches the lower bound throughout that range.

Choose the paired nonlinear gates to emit u AND v and u OR v. Within a butterfly pair the predecessor functions have disjoint raw supports, are nonconstant, and can take their values independently. Both gates genuinely use both inputs. Yet XOR aggregation of the final wires gives parity of the original inputs, since the XOR of the comparator outputs equals u XOR v. A single affine form therefore computes the whole target. An arbitrarily small fixed Bq log q gate budget is also possible by retaining a fixed sufficiently small fraction of the d layers. The same window bound applies with the retained depth in place of d whenever it contains complete windows.

Alternatively, assign AND to every gate including the aggregation. On independently supplied frontier variables every cone is their conjunction. This has affine support rank equal to their number: its restriction to those bits is true at exactly one point, so no nonzero translation preserves it. This observation concerns independently supplied frontier values. Substituting correlated earlier definitions or globally recircuitizing the target can erase the apparent cost. Neither version gives a decision lower bound for unrestricted CircuitSAT.
