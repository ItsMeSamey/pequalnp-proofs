# Common-seed evaluators for the October hierarchy witness

Use the exact unary H, d, and indexed 3-CNF decoder in quantitative-circuits.tex. Let V be a fixed NP language with queries encoding (1^n,s,i) and strictly increasing total lengths b(n)=O(n+l(n)), using a uniquely parseable padded format of length computable in polynomial time. Suppose every sufficiently large YES length admits one seed of length l(n)=poly(n) for which the bits V(1^n,s,i), simultaneously over every label i, give a satisfying assignment. No condition on seeds at NO lengths is necessary. Hardwire finite exceptions when deciding H.

Then H belongs to the third level of PH: existentially choose s and require no clause index with all three literals false. A violation check for a given seed lies in NP with NP oracle (guess index and obtain three membership answers). Hence P=NP would put H in P, contradicting its exclusion from NTIME(2^{n/2}). This construction, if obtained for this family, would itself prove P differs from NP. Even a fixed PH bit evaluator suffices for that implication. Separate seeds for individual clauses lose the conclusion: the all-zero and all-one assignments together satisfy each separate nonempty ordinary clause, even in unsatisfiable formulas.

Moreover the original witness-circuit contradiction gives infinitely many YES lengths at which EVERY satisfying assignment needs more than eta n sqrt(log_2 n) gates for a constant eta>0. A small circuit for V on the tuple length at such a length could be specialized by hardwiring its common successful seed and the fixed formatting bits; evaluate i directly on the remaining circuit wires. Thus size_V(b(n))=Omega(n sqrt(log n)) infinitely often. This is superlinear in b if l=o(n sqrt(log n)), and Omega(b sqrt(log b)) for l=O(n). The PH implication above allows any polynomial seed length. Neither implication produces V or the common seed. The source's E^NP selector writes an exponentially long explicit formula and uses NP queries on exponentially long prefixes; its bit membership lacks the required polynomial certificate.
