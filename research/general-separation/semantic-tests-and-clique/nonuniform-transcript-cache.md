# Caching accepting transcripts within a stream-controller budget

Split an n-bit address into halves of floor(n/2) and ceil(n/2) bits, and write N=2^n. Suppose the target has K monochromatic accepting rectangles whose union misses at most pN accepted addresses. For 1≤K≤√N a bounded-fanin circuit computes their union in O(√N+K√N/log₂(K+2)) gates. Consequently K≤cα√N suffices for the αN/n stream budget at all sufficiently large lengths, for a sufficiently small absolute c and fixed α>0. The zero-rectangle case emits zero.

Decode both half-addresses to one-hot indicators by shared prefix gates in O(√N) gates. On each side group the indicators in blocks of b=max(1,floor(log₂ K)). Build all subset ORs within each block in O(2^b) gates by adjoining its last wire to each earlier subset OR. For each rectangle, combine its designated subset OR from each block. The total is O((1+√N/b)(2^b+K)) per side. AND the two answers per rectangle and OR across rectangles. The budget consequence follows by monotonicity of K/log₂(K+2) and by evaluating the bound at the stated endpoint.

The bit is emitted immediately on each address. The circuit needs no initialized work bits and can carry n arbitrary borrowed bits by identity wires on every tick. All those bits are restored and their terminal states distinct. Construction time for the rectangle descriptions is uncharged. This applies standard shared-subfunction synthesis to the specified controller budget. [Lupanov, synthesis of networks](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=tm&option_lang=eng&paperid=1277&wshow=paper)

A deterministic two-party protocol with public turn and stopping rules supplies one rectangle per complete accepting transcript. At each message the sender's local response restricts its own side of the current rectangle. Include the final output in the transcript if necessary. Private computation may take arbitrary time and space. A machine tape split can use this model if all information transferred on each crossing, including finite control, is included in the messages. A separate uncharged shared work tape would require its own simulation. This use of the transcript rectangle property and monochromatic covers is standard in communication complexity. [Roughgarden, communication complexity, Lemma 2.1](https://www.timroughgarden.algorithmsilluminated.org/w15/l/l4.pdf)

A protocol with at most n/2−O_α(1) communicated bits meets the exact-output budget. A distributional variant permits unbounded transcript lengths. For uniform addresses let q>0 be the acceptance probability and H the base-two entropy of the transcript conditional on acceptance. After sorting the conditional probabilities, every transcript outside the first K has probability at most 1/(K+1). Its contribution to H thus bounds its mass times log₂(K+1). Retaining the K most likely accepting transcripts misses at most qHN/log₂(K+1) addresses. The conclusion follows whenever qH≤p log₂(K+1), or directly whenever the omitted mass is small enough. The assumption concerns the actual protocol and chosen split; it does not follow for all E computations.

The [checker](transcript_cache_check.py) tests projection bitmaps, subset ORs, and the entropy tail inequality on 320 protocol/retention cases.
