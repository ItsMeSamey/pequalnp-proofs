# Advice and sparse errors at the hard lengths

Fix 0<a<a'<1/2. Use arbitrary binary formulas or acyclic existential query programs with at most n ceil((loglog(n+16))^a) occurrences per atom. Use a' at the original hard-language lengths. The fixed-exponent result supplies for every s some NP language needing more than n^s summands infinitely often. The following additional condition would give a single NP language independent of s.

Suppose s_j is unbounded, L_j has the stated s_j lower bound, and one integer k works for all j. At infinitely many of those same hard lengths, there is an advice string of length at most n^k for a fixed per-j nondeterministic machine V_j using at most O(n^k) binary nondeterministic choices, whose acceptance predicate within n^k original machine steps disagrees with L_j on at most n^(s_j)/2 inputs. Then one explicit NP language escapes every polynomial sparsity at infinitely many lengths.

The universal language reads a machine description, a binary cutoff T, advice and n parity blocks of length R. It simulates at most min(T,N) original transitions on their parities with that advice, where N is the full length, by a fixed polynomial-time universal nondeterministic simulation. At a hard length hardwire V_j, T=n^k and its advice, and use R=n^(p−1) for fixed p>k+2, encoding the header with linear advice overhead. Then N=(1+o(1))n^p. No effective advice construction is needed. If the universal language had sparsity N^d at all large lengths, the [parity-depadding lemma](parity-depadding.md) yields n^{O_p(d+1)} terms at the n-bit cap with exponent a', absorbing constant slack. Correct each erroneous input by adding or subtracting one conjunction of its n literals, realizable as a formula or query chain. Choose s_j larger than the transferred exponent with fixed slack. At the infinitely many selected large lengths the corrections and original terms total at most n^(s_j), a contradiction. This argument allows arbitrarily slow original verifiers. The uniform-clock-on-advice condition and the previous hardness-to-time ratio condition concern different resources.

## A finite-sample sufficient condition

Suppose at the selected hard lengths every correctly labelled multiset of m=ceil(C(n^k+1)/γ²) examples admits an n^k-bit advice for V_j giving acceptance accuracy at least 1/2+γ. Here n^(−c)≤γ≤1/2, c is common to all j and C is a sufficiently large absolute constant. For any input distribution, m independent samples approximate the accuracies of all fewer than 2^(n^k+1) possible advices to within γ/2 simultaneously with positive probability, by Hoeffding and a union bound. Every distribution thus admits one advice with advantage γ/2. Finite minimax yields a distribution on advices giving this advantage pointwise. An odd list of O((n+1)/γ²) independent draws has the right majority answer simultaneously on all 2^n inputs with positive probability. Concatenate the list as advice. Strict majority of nondeterministic predicates is verifiable by guessing distinct indices and successful computations on a majority. Time and advice have a common polynomial exponent depending only on k and c. This standard boosting argument gives the exact advised slice needed above; it supplies no efficient constructor of the list. [Freund and Schapire](https://www.schapire.net/papers/FreundSc96b.pdf)

## A nonuniform control and a conditional uniform control

Fix proposed exponents k≥1 and s≥1. Choose D so that n^k-time nondeterministic verifiers with n^k advice and a fixed description compile to nondeterministic Boolean circuits of size at most n^D after hardwiring any input suffix, eventually for each fixed machine. A direct quadratic computation-table simulation suffices with fixed-exponent slack. Choose D also large enough that an exact SUM with at most n^s near-linear atoms as defined above compiles into a deterministic circuit of size n^D. The independent Boolean column argument and Cramer's rule bound common-denominator weights by O(n^s log n) bits for fixed s. The bottom formulas or explicit acyclic graphs can be evaluated in polynomial size, followed by weighted addition. These exponents need only depend on k and s.

Take t=ceil(B log₂ n), B>D+2. There are at most 2^{O(n^D log n)} nondeterministic circuits of size n^D on t inputs, after canonical numbering of the witness inputs actually used. In contrast there are 2^(2^t) tables. For large n pick a t-bit table T_n outside this circuit class. Extend it to n bits by ignoring the suffix. This is a P/poly family by table lookup, and it has the stated n^s SUM lower bound by hardwiring the suffix. If an n^k-clock machine and advice were wrong on at most n^s/2 inputs, fewer than the 2^(n−t) suffixes could contain an error. Fixing an undamaged suffix gives a forbidden small nondeterministic circuit for T_n. Thus polynomial-size circuits and the stated restricted hardness do not imply the approximation hypothesis.

There is a uniform polynomial-hierarchy version. Choose the lexicographically first omitted table at each sufficiently large length. Tables and candidate circuits have polynomial length in n. Checking a proposed nondeterministic circuit against a table uses a fixed number of polynomially bounded quantifiers over inputs and witnesses. Quantifying tables and circuits also expresses that every earlier table is represented, still within PH. Hence each bit of the canonical table is in PH for fixed D,B. Under P=NP the extended language would even be in P. This would falsify an all-hard-families approximation claim in uniform P for any specified k,s. The assertion needed for packing concerns the existence of a particular suitable family. The counting control does not refute it.

A superpolynomial SUM lower bound for one NP language would still leave arbitrary shared Boolean circuits and polynomial-time algorithms outside this proof.

## Status of the premise

For a single language already hard against every polynomial sparsity at profile a', the hypothesis is automatic by setting every L_j equal to that language and using its own fixed NP verifier with empty advice. For the displayed a<a' the conclusion uses the smaller profile, so this observation does not prove literal equivalence at fixed profiles. It does show why unrestricted selection of the family is no independent evidence for hardness. To make the condition an attack on the problem, one needs a separately defined hard-language family and an approximation or weak-fitting theorem for that same family and those lengths. The fixed-exponent NP result does not supply such a theorem. No verified family satisfying both requirements is known from this argument. Nor does the condition assert a hierarchy collapse, since neither all NP languages nor all large input lengths are covered.
