# Majority as an existential projection of XORs of affine flats

Let x range over the q-dimensional Boolean cube and let y have b Boolean witness bits. A flat is the set of solutions of a finite affine linear system over GF(2). Suppose acceptance means that for some y, every component of a syndrome S(x,y) is zero. The components are XORs of indicators of s distinct flats on (x,y), with constant terms allowed. A direct nonconstant affine summand is itself a codimension-one flat indicator and must be charged in s, or explicitly aggregated with other affine summands in that equation before counting. Sharing the same flat in different equations is permitted in this structural theorem. Constant or empty terms may be folded.

Let D be the maximum over x of the number of distinct nonzero syndrome vectors obtained as y varies. Let ρ be the dimension of the linear span of all attainable syndrome vectors; using an a priori upper bound on that dimension also suffices. Define

    k = min(ρ, ceil(log₂(max(1,D)))+14)
    A = Σ_{i=0}^{min(s,k)} binom(s,i).

In particular D≤2^b. If the projected function is majority, with either fixed convention for ties, then log₂(1+A)=Ω(sqrt(q)). The number of equations and the value of b enter the bound only through k. The theorem concerns approximation on the uniform law of x but holds for every exact representation. No hypothesis on the number or sizes of witness fibers is needed.

## Syndrome tests

Use k independent random GF(2)-linear tests on syndrome vectors when the logarithmic term attains the minimum. For a fixed x, each nonzero syndrome survives all tests with probability 2^{-k}. A union bound over its distinct values shows that with probability at least 1−2^{-14} the tests identify exactly the original accepting witnesses for this x, simultaneously over y. If the rank bound attains the minimum instead, use a deterministic linear map injective on the syndrome span. Acceptance of the tests is a product of k factors, each one plus a linear combination of the s indicators. Expand over GF(2), folding powers by idempotence. There are at most A terms. Each is the indicator of one joint flat, since products intersect flats. The empty product is the whole space. This construction supplies a random list depending on the tests, independent of the following random hashes.

## Removing witness multiplicities

Condition on any list of at most A joint flats giving the tested acceptance indicator P(x,y). Write Y_x={y:P(x,y)=1}. At each scale j=0,…,b+1, take a random affine map from b bits to j bits, and its zero cell. For each nonempty Y_x of cardinality w, there is a j in that range with w/2^j in (1/4,1/2], allowing equality at 1/2. Pairwise independence gives probability at least λ−λ²≥3/16 of exactly one hit at this scale, where λ=w/2^j. Indeed the hit count Z satisfies Pr[Z=1]≥E[Z]−E[Z(Z−1)]. This is the elementary isolation argument of [Valiant and Vazirani](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(86)90135-0).

Compute the parity of hits in each zero cell; an isolated hit gives one. Combine these parity bits by a random GF(2)-linear map to one bit (independent fair coefficient per scale). Whenever any parity bit is one, the combination is fair. Repeat the entire hash-and-combine step R=100 times independently and take the Boolean OR. For fixed x with nonempty Y_x the false-negative probability is at most (1−3/32)^R<1/100. Empty Y_x always gives zero before truncation.

For a single joint-flat term J of P, discard it if empty. Its nonempty y-sections have the same dimension d_J, namely the kernel dimension of the projection on x. Intersect J with the chosen hash cell on y. For a fixed hash, the parity over y of this term is zero or an affine-flat indicator on x: projection gives an affine flat, and each nonempty fiber has the same power-of-two size. For j<d_J this parity vanishes identically because at least one kernel dimension remains. For j>d_J+L and fixed x, the probability the cell meets the J-section at all is at most 2^{d_J−j} by the first moment. Summing this bound over such scales gives at most 2^{-L}. Take L=ceil(log₂(max(A,1)))+20. Over R trials and at most A terms, discarding these large scales changes the outcome on any fixed x with probability at most RA2^{-L}<1/100. Conditioning on the syndrome test does not affect this calculation.

Each trial therefore uses an XOR of at most A(L+1) projected flat indicators after the termwise truncation, regardless of b. The OR of the R trials is 1+Π(1+trial) over GF(2); expansion followed by intersection yields at most M=1+(1+A(L+1))^R flats in x alone. The bounds so far are pointwise over the random tests and hashes. The next step uses uniform x: a nonempty x-flat of codimension greater than d=ceil(log₂(max(M,1)))+10 has probability at most 2^{-d} under that law. Removing every such term causes error at most M2^{-d}≤2^{-10}, for every fixed choice of randomness. Every remaining flat indicator is a product of at most d affine forms, so their XOR has degree at most d=O(log(1+A)). Averaging fixes a polynomial with uniform error less than 1/16 for the original projected decision function. This is an existence proof, with no efficient expansion required.

For the promise that every nonempty original witness fiber has odd size, the correctly hashed syndrome indicator can simply be summed over y modulo two. This gives at most A projected flats directly; the same final uniform truncation applies. The isolation argument extends the lower bound to arbitrary multiplicities.

## The majority lower bound

Here is the Hilbert-function argument in the needed form. Write t=floor((q−1)/2). Both the zero region and the one region of majority contain a radius-t Hamming ball, centered at opposite constant strings. If a binary polynomial P of degree d≤t has fewer errors than Σ_{i≤t−d}binom(q,i), some nonzero multilinear polynomial Q of degree at most t−d vanishes on all errors, by the dimension of that polynomial space. Multilinearize products on the cube. The polynomial PQ has degree at most t and vanishes on the zero-region ball, hence is identically zero: evaluations on that ball determine every coefficient of degree at most t by triangular interpolation after translating its center to zero. On the one-region ball Q must therefore vanish as well, both at errors and at rows where P=1. The same interpolation forces Q=0, a contradiction. Thus the uniform error is at least 2^{-q}Σ_{i≤t−d}binom(q,i), which is at least 1/2−O((d+1)/sqrt(q)) by the central binomial estimate. Error below 1/16 gives d=Ω(sqrt(q)). This is the Hilbert-function method of [Smolensky, On representations by low-degree polynomials (FOCS 1993)](https://doi.org/10.1109/SFCS.1993.366874).

## Deterministic solving when an explicit compiler is available

For the separate algorithmic application, take the explicit system on n=q+b variables. Each flat with independent defining equations ℓ_i=0 is a single product Π_i(1+ℓ_i). Charge **all product occurrences** in the supplied ΣΠΣ systems, including one-factor summands when needed, and denote this possibly larger count by s_occ. Distinct-flat counting in the lower bound was valid because the output polynomials could be expressed in a common indicator alphabet. It does not give the smaller occurrence count for free in a solver's input.

Lokshtanov, Paturi, Tamaki, Williams and Yu, Theorem 1.4, gives deterministic exact counting of all pairs (x,y), in time poly(u) times 2^{n(1−1/(1100 log₂(s_occ/n)+O(1)))} with explicit encoding size u. For small ratios use padding to s_occ≥n (redundant zero equations containing canceling identical products suffice), absorbing constants. Testing for a positive count decides existence of any x,y; the integer count equals the number of accepted x only with an additional unique-extension condition. The theorem has a uniform polynomial factor in u, which must be charged even for growing descriptions. [LPTWY, Theorems 1.3–1.4](https://theory.stanford.edu/~yuhch123/files/polyEq.pdf)

For example name b gate values in an acyclic circuit and require their defining equations in topological order, plus an output equation. If each remaining cone is supplied as a Boolean function of w explicit affine forms in x and earlier named variables, its truth table gives at most 2^w flat summands and its output variable adds at most one. Inputs to different cones may overlap. This is a unique extension for each primary input. The compiler must actually obtain the cone truth tables (e.g. a supplied syntactic circuit in those affine inputs can be evaluated on all 2^w assignments). It takes poly(q+b,s_circ)·2^{O(w)} time and output bits for a polynomial number of cones, with s_occ≤(b+1)(2^w+1). More generally charge the compiler's actual time as an additive cost and retain the solver term poly(u). With polynomial s_occ,u and polynomial compilation time, b=o(q/log q) suffices for time 2^{q−Ω(q/log q)}. When s_occ=O(q) and u is polynomial, some b≤εq with sufficiently small fixed ε still permits a constant-fraction saving. For growing u the saving after compilation requires control of b+O(log u) relative to n/(1100 log₂(max(1,s_occ/n))+O(1)), as well as the compiler's running time. This gives a solver for the stated decompositions, not a decomposition of arbitrary q log q-gate circuits.
