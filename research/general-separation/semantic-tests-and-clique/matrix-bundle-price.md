# Charging a shared Boolean circuit under matrix-valued tests

Let the semantic domain be a finite set, possibly a disjoint union of restriction domains. Give each raw input wire its Boolean-valued function on this set. Let T be a unital representation of the generated Boolean algebra in matrices over the binary field. Let H be a unital additive map from the same Boolean algebra into the same matrices that agrees with T on the raw inputs. Conditional restrictions are included by feeding the circuit their tuples of restricted raw inputs; each wire value is then one whole function tuple. No componentwise bound is assumed on the rank of an error for a whole tuple.

For a particular XOR–AND circuit define the unital matrix algebra A generated by the H-values of all its wires, including inputs. At an AND gate with operands u,v put D_g=H(uv)−H(u)H(v) and K_g=span{M w : M∈A, w∈im D_g}. The dimension of K_g is the charge of this gate. With E(f)=H(f)−T(f), the sum of the column spaces of E(f) over all circuit outputs has dimension at most the dimension of the sum of K_g over distinct AND gates. In particular it is at most the sum of their charges.

Indeed, XOR adds errors and input errors vanish. At a product the exact identity is

\[
E(uv)=D_g+H(u)E(v)+E(u)T(v).
\]

The K_g spaces are invariant under left multiplication by the circuit's H-values. Right multiplication does not enlarge a column space. Induction in topological order proves the result with all upstream K_g counted once, independent of fan-out. The theorem also holds with a prescribed encompassing algebra in place of A. Each charge can be computed from the supplied matrices by taking the invariant closure of the defect's columns.

A uniform bound P on the charges of synchronized operand tuples in a class of size-s implementations would imply that an output discrepancy of rank greater than sP excludes all those implementations. This is a sufficient condition. To use it for an unrestricted target requires bounding actual saturated tuple charges across implementations. The [row selection counterexample](./matrix-certificate-ceiling.md) shows that bounding ranks of the separate restrictions does not establish such a bound.

**Dimension-only estimate.** Always dim K_g ≤ (dim A) rank D_g. There is an obstruction to using this coarser estimate with a uniform multiplication-defect bound. The commuting idempotent input-pin matrices have q nonzero joint spectral projectors P_b, for their joint bit patterns b. These projectors are products of pins and their complements, so they lie in A. They are linearly independent, implying q≤dim A. Their sum is I. Every occurring b belongs to the raw-input promise, since its joint indicator has nonzero image under T.

For each b apply the coordinate-certificate identity on the raw-input promise. Pin equality gives P_b H(ℓ)=0 for each zero-at-b certificate literal ℓ. On the b spectral subspace, T(f) equals f(b)I. Thus

\[
P_b E(f)=\sum_j P_b\bigl(H(\ell_j g_j)-H(\ell_j)H(g_j)\bigr).
\]

If all these certificate defects have rank at most R, then rank E(f)≤R∑_b C(f,b)≤R(dim A)max_b C(f,b). Bounding every gate charge simply by R dim A under a test that includes these products cannot yield more than the certificate bound by this ratio. If f has a size-s implementation, the certificate factors g_j have implementations of size s+O(C(f,b)). This argument leaves open finer invariant-subspace estimates targeted at the actual operand tuples.
