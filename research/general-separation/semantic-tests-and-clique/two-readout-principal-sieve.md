# An exact principal-minor sieve for two mixed-parity readouts

Let H be a symmetric binary matrix with diagonal c and off-diagonal adjacency; S is a feasible support when H[S]1=0. On a nonzero target for two independent affine readouts, change their basis and absorb offsets to write a·x=1 and b·x=0. A zero target or dependent readouts reduce to triviality, inconsistency, or the one-readout case. All matrices and vectors below may have zero rows or repeated entries.

In the field of four elements take ω outside the binary subfield and set K=H+ωbbᵀ. There is a feasible support with these readouts if and only if some index set S has

det K[S]=0 and det(K[S]+a_S a_Sᵀ)≠0.

For any principal submatrix write H₀=H[S], b₀=b_S. If b₀ is outside the image of H₀, the relation H₀v=ωb₀(b₀ᵀv) forces b₀ᵀv=0, since extending the field preserves independence. If b₀=H₀u for binary u, the rank-one right factor I+ωu b₀ᵀ is invertible because 1+ωb₀ᵀu≠0. It fixes ker H₀ pointwise by symmetry. Thus in both cases ker K[S] is precisely the scalar extension of ker H₀ intersected with b₀-orthogonality.

Choose an inclusion-minimal actual feasible support with a·x=1,b·x=0. On S the all-ones vector lies in this last binary kernel with a_Sᵀ1=1. If the kernel had dimension at least two, a nonzero w therein with a_Sᵀw=0 would exist. The vector 1+w has strictly smaller support, still has the two target readouts, and its restriction to that support still obeys every active row of H. This contradicts minimality. At corank one the symmetric adjugate is a nonzero scalar times vvᵀ for a kernel generator v, so the rank-one update with a_S has nonzero determinant. Conversely a distinguishing S has corank one, and a_S is nonorthogonal to its kernel. Solve the binary equations H₀v=0, b₀·v=0, a_S·v=1; they are consistent by the kernel identity. Restrict v to its actual support, extend by zeros, and read out the original center and private leaf witness. The index set certified by determinants need not equal that support.

For each S form four binary scalars: D=det H[S], A and B the determinants after bordering H[S] by respectively a_S or b_S with zero diagonal, and C the determinant after adjoining both borders with zero mutual entry. Expanding det(H[S]+s aaᵀ+t bbᵀ) gives D+sA+tB+stC, also for singular H[S]; this follows from a two-row determinant expansion or the rank-two determinant lemma as a polynomial identity. Hence the exact pointwise distinguishing predicate is the nonvanishing of

(1+D)(1+B)(A+ωC).

Equivalently the multilinear polynomial formed by attaching this scalar to the monomial indexed by S is nonzero. An identity oracle for that polynomial under zero restrictions recovers a distinguishing S by successively deleting any index that leaves a nonzero polynomial. This is an oracle reduction, not a polynomial-time construction of the identity oracle.

## Pairwise products of the four bits cannot support this exact pointwise test

All 16 binary assignments of D,A,B,C already occur on an index set of size two. In that size C equals det([a b])². If D=1 and C=1 use a=e₁,b=e₂, H with diagonal B,A and cross entry 1+AB. For D=1,C=0 use H=I, choosing each of a,b to be either zero or the same e₁ according to A,B. For D=0 and (A,B)≠(0,0) take H=diag(0,1); the first entries a₁,b₁ prescribe A,B, and a₂,b₂ can prescribe C arbitrarily. For D=A=B=0 use H=0 and choose the columns dependent or independent as required. If full readout independence in the whole instance is desired, extend a,b on unused outside coordinates; this does not change the quadruple on S.

Any multilinear polynomial of total degree at most two over a characteristic-two field that vanishes whenever D=1 or B=1 must be a constant times (1+D)(1+B), as follows by restriction to the Boolean cube. If it also vanishes when D=B=A=C=0, that constant is zero. Thus even a list of such bivariate-degree tests cannot witness precisely this local support condition without false positives. Degree three suffices in the displayed four signals. This argument permits other signals, global cancellations, or a different choice of S; it gives no algorithmic lower bound.

Binary principal determinants here can each be encoded by an alternating Pfaffian signature at fixed index parity. For a binary symmetric matrix J, take its alternating matrix G with off-diagonal entries J_ij+J_ii J_jj. On an even set the Pfaffian of G equals det J on that set. Indeed in the determinant over the binary field permutations with long cycles cancel with their reversals, leaving matchings with singleton diagonal weights. In the Pfaffian expansion the vertices represented by diagonals can be paired in an odd number of ways. On an odd set add one dummy with connections J_ii; the same pairing argument applies after including that dummy. Bordering by a or b uses zero dummy-to-border edge. The sieve involves up to three coefficientwise Pfaffian factors. An algorithm for ordinary principal-minor nonsingularity, or a contraction limited to two factors, therefore needs another argument to decide this sieve.
