# A gate-dependent bound on the directions of odd Walsh coefficients

For a Boolean function f on n bits, write W(u)=2^(-n)Σ_x(−1)^(f(x)+u·x). Let g be the smallest nonnegative integer with 2^g W integral everywhere. Define R(u)=(2^g W(u)) mod 2. The usual Fourier-granularity bound is MC(f)≥g, where MC counts binary AND gates and permits free XOR, negation and constants. The refinement below constrains R simultaneously across frequencies, using intermediate gate operands. The r=0 case is excluded. [Chistopolskaya and Podolskii](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08668)

**Theorem.** If an unrestricted XOR–AND circuit uses r≥1 multiplications, there is an integer 0≤ν≤2(r−g) and a linear frequency direction D of dimension at least r+1−ν such that R restricted to every coset of D has GF(2) degree at most 2(r−g)−ν. In particular, at equality r=g≥1, R is the indicator of a single affine subspace of dimension between r+1 and 2r. The last, stronger single-subspace assertion is proved separately below.

## Proof by circuit equations and quadratic sums

Introduce r bits y_i for the ordered AND outputs. Write both operands A_i,B_i as affine forms in the original inputs x and earlier y bits, and write the output as one affine form a(x,y). Define H to be the span of all 2r linear operand parts in the dual space of (x,y) and h=dim H. Put X equal to the n-dimensional subspace of frequencies supported on x and D=H∩X. The final bit y_r occurs in no operand, so dim D≥h−r+1.

Every x has exactly one consistent tuple y. In the original normalized Walsh sum insert its r gate equations, and express each indicator of a satisfied equation as one half the sum of its two characters. This gives W(u) as the sum over λ∈F₂^r of normalized quadratic Walsh transforms on all n+r variables, with quadratic polynomial Σ_i λ_i A_iB_i and external linear frequency determined by (u,λ) and a. The normalization is 2^(−n−r) in each summand.

For a quadratic form of polar rank 2s, its normalized Walsh transform is supported on a translate of its polar image J. There its values are ±2^(−s), with signs specified by a quadratic polynomial in support coordinates. This follows by pairing 2s variables into s hyperbolic planes and summing the radical variables. The polar image of each phase here is contained in H, hence 2s≤h and 2g≤h. Thus ν=2r−h satisfies the theorem's range. The quadratic normal form and circuit path-sum technique have prior literature. [Dehaene and De Moor](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0304125) · [Dawson et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0408129)

Set k=h−2g+1. If k exceeds dim D, the desired degree bound holds by dimension. Otherwise consider any affine k-flat parallel to a k-dimensional subspace of D. In a term of polar rank 2s its intersection with the support is empty or an ℓ-flat with ℓ≥k+2s−h: both directions lie in H. The unnormalized sum of a quadratic sign over this intersection is zero or divisible by 2^ceil(ℓ/2), by the same normal form. After multiplying by 2^g, its contribution to the flat sum has valuation at least g−s+ceil(ℓ/2)≥1. Hence R has even sum on every such k-flat. The k-fold finite differences on each coset vanish, proving degree at most h−2g=2(r−g)−ν. This proof uses the Boolean circuit equations directly and allows arbitrary fanout, cancellation and intermediate degrees.

For the single-flat assertion at r=g, reverse-eliminate the gates in the output character. Replacing the sign of an active AND a·b introduces four choices of affine signs (−1)^(ua+vb), with coefficient (−1)^(uv)/2 for u,v∈F₂. Terms skipping any gate have even scaled weight and disappear mod two. The condition that every gate is active is an affine system in the 2r choice bits: the gate's coefficient is the original output coefficient plus the later choices' contributions to that wire. The last activation is fixed, so the solution space if nonempty has dimension ≥r+1. The final frequency is an affine map of these choices. Fibers of positive dimension have even cardinality, so the nonempty odd support is exactly the injective affine image of this solution space. Its dimension is between r+1 and 2r. Granularity ensures the odd support is nonempty.

## Two-arm multiplexer

Let f select between the conjunction of n variables a and the conjunction of n disjoint variables b using one selector bit, with n≥2. The normalized Walsh transform is one half the sum of the transforms of the two arms embedded in the respective frequency blocks, with a possible sign from the selector. Each n-input AND has granularity n−1 and odd residue on every frequency. Hence the mux has g=n and its residue is R=1[u=0]+1[v=0] in GF(2), independent of the selector frequency. Its degree is n+1 as a Boolean function of its original inputs, so both degree and plain granularity only give r≥n.

For any direction D let a,b be the dimensions of its projections to the two n-bit frequency blocks and let p be the maximum coset degree of R. If a≠b, a translate meeting the zero fiber of the larger projection has degree max(a,b), since the other term has lower degree. If a=b<n, a translate can meet one zero fiber while missing the other. When a=b=n and the two row spaces inside D* differ, their degree-n terms are distinct nonzero exterior products and cannot cancel. A top exterior product determines its n-dimensional linear span. In all three cases dim D≤a+b+1≤2p+1. In the remaining case both projections have rank n and identical row space. The joint rank is then n, so dim D≤n+1. Translating the two zero fibers to different points of the common n-bit quotient yields degree n−1: in coordinates these are two point indicators whose sum becomes a codimension-(n−1) flat indicator after a linear change. Thus dim D≤2p+1 also in this case.

Apply the directional theorem. It gives r+1−ν≤2[2(r−n)−ν]+1, so MC(f)≥ceil(4n/3). A direct circuit uses 2n−1 gates, by computing both conjunctions and multiplexing with one further product. This is an unconditional lower bound in a simple P family. No optimality assertion is made. A literal projection to CLIQUE encodes each of the two guarded conjunctions on a separate k-vertex component, choosing binom(k,2)≥n+1. This particular projection transfers the numeric lower bound and does not imply superpolynomial hardness.

## The linear ceiling of this necessary condition

For every nonaffine n-bit f, the residue R always has degree at most n−g−1. Indeed the sign equals 1−2·1[f=1]. At g≥1 the constant character contributes zero modulo 2 to R; the integer expansion of the remaining characters has the coefficient of every monomial of frequency degree j divisible by 2^(g+1−n+j) when that exponent is positive. Möbius inversion gives integer coefficients of the fully scaled sum. Thus all coefficients above n−g−1 vanish modulo two. In particular r=n−1, ν=0 and D equal to the whole frequency space meet the directional inequality for every nonaffine f. The saturation clause at g=n−1 also holds automatically since R then has degree zero and is nonempty. The direction/degree criterion alone certifies at most n−1 gates, a ceiling reached by n-input AND. A general superlinear lower bound would require further information beyond this criterion.
