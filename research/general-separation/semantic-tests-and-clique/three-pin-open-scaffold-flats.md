# A physical pin scaffold cannot synchronize arbitrarily many free flags

Consider an arbitrary finite undirected physical graph. Its open sites have occupancy vector e; their induced-neighbor defects are unrestricted. There are p designated sites required selected, with prescribed degree parity there, and arbitrarily many further hidden sites with ordinary selected-site parity guards. All prescriptions are fixed constants. Say e extends if some hidden word and the specified pins satisfy their guards. Restrict the open bits, if desired, to any affine subspace E. Every extendible point of E lies in an affine subspace of E of relative codimension at most p consisting entirely of extendible points.

Fix an extension at that point minimizing its number of selected nonpin hidden sites, and call their set S. Set every other nonpin hidden bit to zero. For the sites in S impose their parity equations unconditionally and allow their input bits h to vary. Also impose the p pin parity equations, keeping all pin inputs one. The result has the form

    M h = b + C e,     M = [A[S,S]; A[P,S]].

The initially accepted point has solution h=1. The columns of M are independent: a nonzero binary kernel vector could be added to that all-ones vector, removing at least one selected site. The retained parity equations still hold and the omitted sites remain unselected. This contradicts minimality. Thus the column space has codimension p in the row ambient space. On E the requirement that b+Ce belong to that column space imposes at most p independent affine checks. The resulting flat contains the starting point. For every other point on it, solve for h. Even if some of its bits vanish, all remaining selected guards and all pin equations hold. This gives actual physical extensions of the whole flat. The statement is existential; finding the initial accepted point or globally minimal witness is not asserted to be polynomial. From a supplied witness it suffices to remove support using Gaussian kernel vectors until the columns are independent, which does take polynomial time.

As a result any nonempty exact affine relation realized on these open flags has codimension at most p. In particular a common three-pin hidden scaffold cannot impose agreement of n>3 independent pairs of exposed source flags, even when all hidden sites and pins are shared between the gadgets and every hidden site may have arbitrary degree. The same obstruction holds for affine decodings when the desired relation has codimension greater than p after pullback to E. More generally an extendible fraction strictly between zero and 2^(−p) is impossible on E. The bound is tight: connect each of p independent selected pins to its own separate open bit, with zero pin prescription, to force p independent open zeroes.

In the [two-guard flow construction](common-hole-flow-guards.md), a standalone equality network requiring each of two copies of a selection flag to agree, with the defect ports independently handled by the two test layers, must implement this agreement as an open-site relation. Such a physical synchronizer fails once more than three pair agreements remain independent on the proposed affine source slice. This addresses the necessary relation of the standalone scaffold; it does not prohibit a combined circuit in which the test layers actively help synchronize, a nonlinear change of decoding, or a construction that needs only decision equivalence on a strict nonlinear subset of the flags.
