# Original-input counting through coordinate planes

Given distinct two-dimensional subspaces U_i of the dual of a binary vector space, one can decide in polynomial time whether there is a single basis B such that every U_i is spanned by two members of B. The input may use arbitrary dense linear forms and the algorithm returns the basis. This allows a circuit counter to charge the original inputs, a measured set of cut bits and explicitly enumerated signs. A common coordinate frame is a sufficient circuit property. A positive answer to the recognition question alone says nothing about the cost of finding good cuts for arbitrary circuits.

## An exact test for a common basis

Let F be the nonzero vectors in intersections of pairs of distinct planes, and let d_i be the number of members of F in U_i. Necessity gives independent F, d_i≤2, and

dim(span of all U_i) = |F| + Σ_i(2−d_i).

Indeed two distinct coordinate planes can intersect nontrivially only along a common basis vector; the basis vectors used by just one plane occur among that plane's private slots. Conversely, include all of F and complete F∩U_i arbitrarily to a basis of U_i inside that plane. The resulting list spans every plane. Its length equals its rank by the displayed test, so it is independent. Complete it to an ambient basis. Enumerating three vectors per plane and performing binary linear algebra is polynomial. The empty family is allowed. [Bounded independent checker](coordinate_plane_check.py)

For example the three planes <e₁,e₂>, <e₁,e₃> and <e₁,e₂+e₃> in dimension three fail this test. They have one common vector and demand three further independent vectors. Each pair by itself passes. This concerns simultaneous directions. Redundant Boolean predicates or alternative circuit realizations can erase a geometric obstruction.

## An exact circuit procedure

Take a fan-in-two DAG of g gates on q raw bits, a selected raw subset U of size u and a selected ordered set of c internal wires. A trial plan is explicit data, and finding or trying multiple plans must be charged. Pin the original output to one. Under a required AND=1 or OR=0, split the requirement conjunctively on its inputs; NOT flips the requested value. Apply these elementary rules in the DAG, retaining shared (wire,value) requests only once. Other requirements remain as roots to evaluate. A selected cut wire can be treated as an atom in this peeling pass.

For each exterior raw assignment and each binary cut string β, evaluate all necessary gates topologically, replacing a selected output by its β entry for later uses and also enforcing that its defining expression takes that entry. Ignore parts made unnecessary by peeling, but evaluate a shared peeled gate if another retained root uses its value. Every raw assignment has exactly one correct β. In each retained cone propagate squarefree binary polynomials in the u raw bits. Store an affine or quadratic expression when it is obtained exactly; multiplying two stored quadratics requires only polynomial work in q, including cancellation through degree four. A trial that encounters a required higher-degree intermediate may decline. This is one polynomial-time sufficient recognition pass. Constants, repeated input wires and arbitrary fanout are handled by evaluation of the original gates. A sound proof of additional gate identities can enlarge the pass if its verification cost is included.

Each cut equality or retained output request is now an explicitly verified quadratic equation on U; exterior and cut bits are constants in this system. Additional original affine equations and an arbitrary verified quadratic sign are permitted. A polynomial's space of invariant translations K consists of the radical directions h of its polar form for which P(h)=P(0). On the radical this latter test is affine with zero at the origin after subtracting P(0), so K is computable by linear algebra. The polynomial factors through the entire direction space K^perp, of dimension u−dim K. If that dimension is at most two, evaluate its four or fewer possibilities. A feasible set of size one or two in two dimensions is affine. An empty/full table is immediate. A feasible set of size three forbids a single pair and yields a direction plane. Identical planes can be grouped before classification. Two or more distinct forbidden pairs on the same plane again produce an affine restriction or a contradiction. All table evaluations are done on representatives obtained by solving a linear system.

Apply the common-basis test to the retained direction planes. A selected collection of noncoordinate equations may instead be kept as general quadratic equations, with their displayed inputs charged. In particular one can always put all quadratic equations in this latter group if recognition fails, although the resulting bound may be poor. Within the group, take binary linear combinations of equations to eliminate dependent quadratic parts. If the span of those homogeneous quadratic coefficients has dimension b, the result is b quadratic equations and further affine equations. Apply the identity [P=0]=(1+(−1)^P)/2 to these b rows. After a single invertible change to the recovered coordinates, each of the 2^b terms is an ordinary 2-CNF, a system of affine rows, and one quadratic sign. The exact original-variable signed counter costs O*(ρ^u) per term, where ρ⁴=ρ³+1 and ρ>1. The resulting sum divided by 2^b is exact. A dense phase adds no enumerated parameter. Stronger affine restrictions may be retained in the final counter. [Signed counter](paired-violation-gauss-count.md)

The procedure can use raw enumeration for any exterior cylinder at which a β trial declines. In that case discard partial results for that entire cylinder and enumerate its 2^u raw assignments once. Let D be the set of exterior cylinders using this fallback. With polynomial bounds on all certifying work, the time is bounded up to poly(q+g+input length) by

2^{q−u+c} + |D|·2^u + Σ_{exterior outside D} Σ_β 2^{b(exterior,β)}ρ^u.

One may preprocess all β in a cylinder for successful recognition before launching the counters, or store only the yes/no prepass and recompute the necessary data. All enumeration can use polynomial space. If every trial passes with b≤b₀, the exponent saved relative to q is at least (1−log₂ρ)u−c−b₀ before polynomial costs. Raw gate evaluation supplies a separate brute-force cap. For ordinary SAT use sign one. A positive count yields an actual raw witness by querying additional raw pins as external affine rows, keeping the same plan and recognition pass. Cut and exterior bits cannot add multiplicity. The optional primary P3 application below instead counts feasible centers; leaf multiplicities must be distinguished.

## What the bound covers

A polynomial-time way to select U with no cut bits applies when the required output is a conjunction of local gate predicates on raw coordinates, possibly alongside explicit dense affine rows and a fixed number of arbitrary quadratic equations. Collect each local predicate's syntactic input support by the union of its predecessors' sets in the original DAG. Let M count the triples lying in these supports with multiplicity. In polynomial time find a raw U of size Ω(min(q,q^{3/2}/√M)) meeting each support at most twice, omitting the second bound for M=0. Select each coordinate at a dyadic probability within a factor two below min(1,√(q/(2M))). The expected number selected minus the surviving triples is at least half the expected number selected. Fix bits by conditional expectations and delete one member of each surviving triple. A local circuit with at most two unpinned raw inputs is evaluated at its at most four possibilities directly; this avoids paying for its internal degree before restriction. Each arbitrary local table becomes affine restrictions or a binary clause on the chosen raw basis. Thus M=O(q log q) gives a 2^{q−Ω(q/√log q)} exact algorithm with arbitrary affine rows and a dense verified quadratic phase, including at gate budgets O(q log q) when their actual descriptions fit. This strengthens the earlier one-private-variable requirement for Boolean local predicates. [Earlier one-bit residual rule](aggregate-gauss-budget.md)

Dense linear changes of all local input coordinates can instead be handled when the bases or underlying direction planes are recoverable; an invertible change is counted once. For a direct example, a conjunction of binary predicates on a hidden common independent collection of affine forms has c=b=0 and U equal to all raw bits whenever its local polynomials are recognized. Polynomial fanout and repetitions add only displayed work. Sparse directed implication graphs can have difficult modular counts; they are legitimate binary clauses in this counter, with no claim of a polynomial-time algorithm for all such graphs. [Modular implication hardness](drift-free-two-edge-path.md)

In the one-exception Boolean P3 model the two private rows have form (a_i+d_ij)y_ij=L_ij(a). Eliminating a leaf exactly for feasibility gives the forbidden event (1+a_i+d_ij)L_ij(a)=1. It is an intersection of two affine conditions solely on the p centers. If the recovered planes fit a basis this supplies the ρ^p bound. On every instance, even when the planes fail the test, independent affine forms A,B in a forbidden event AB=1 yield the disjoint branches A=0 and (A=1,B=0). They decrease the dimension of the current center space by one and two. Dependent pairs reduce to an affine condition, triviality or contradiction. The resulting O*(φ^p) deterministic algorithm, φ²=φ+1, counts feasible centers or finds one. No invertibility of the leaf matrix is used. Once a feasible center assignment is known, choose the determined bits in unit rows and zero on free leaves. The one-defect circuit reduction uses p linear in circuit size, which can be much larger than the original q primary inputs. It explicitly defeats an invertible-only slope test and remains within the scope of these exponential algorithms. [P3 reduction](single-unequal-p3.md)

An arbitrary depth circuit may force many cuts or fail the bounded-degree pass on every trial. Neither the common-basis characterization nor the triple bound controls these events for all g=Θ(q log q). The direct SAT hierarchy implication at that gate scale therefore remains conditional.
