# A uniform running-time implication for one gap in circuit size

Set N=2^n and s=2^floor(sqrt(n)). The input is an explicit Boolean truth table of N bits. YES tables have binary fan-in-two circuit size ≤s and NO tables have size >10ns, with arbitrary behavior between the thresholds. The ordinary threshold language at s has a fixed NP verifier: guess a circuit and evaluate it over the entire table. The near-linear running-time consequence below concerns separation of the two promised sets.

Assuming P=NP, one deterministic multitape algorithm separates them in N^(1+o(1)) time. This is a uniform adaptation of the anti-checker method of Oliveira, Pich and Santhanam, who use nonuniform approximate counting under NP⊆P/poly for their circuit magnification theorem. Here the slowly growing threshold is fixed once and for all.

Fix equal-length binary descriptions of length L=O(s log(n+s)) for all circuits of size at most s; discard malformed descriptions. Repeated descriptions of a function are harmless. Starting with no labeled examples, let V contain all descriptions matching previous examples. If V is empty output NO. Otherwise scan every address a in order. For its observed bit b test whether a substantial fraction of V outputs the other bit there. Use a test which always says low at fractions ≤1/64 and always says high at fractions ≥1/16. On a high answer append (a,b) to the examples and restart; if the full scan finishes with no high answer output YES. There can be O(L+1) appended examples, since every update removes at least a 1/64 fraction of the surviving descriptions.

At the final YES outcome every address has error fraction <1/16 under a uniform description from V. Sample h=2n+1 independent descriptions. The probability their majority errs at a fixed address is at most 2^h (1/16)^((h+1)/2), strictly below 2^(−n). A union bound proves existence of a majority agreeing everywhere. The h subcircuits use at most hs gates and counting their votes by binary addition uses O(h log h) more. Since s/log n tends to infinity, this is below 10ns at all sufficiently large n. Small n can be hardwired. On a YES table, some exact circuit survives forever, so the NO outcome is impossible. No random choices occur in the decision algorithm.

The test uses two approximate counts of strings satisfying polynomial-time predicates, comparing erroneous descriptions to all surviving descriptions. Both predicates take unary s, the prior labeled examples, and the new labeled address as input, of size poly(s,n). V emptiness is an ordinary NP question. With relative error at most 1/10 for both estimates, comparing their ratio to 1/32 distinguishes the stated endpoints. Stockmeyer approximate counting uses BPP with an NP oracle, with amplification giving simultaneous accuracy with probability greater than 2/3. Assuming P=NP the oracle calls become ordinary fixed-polynomial computations. The usual Sipser–Lautemann hitting-shifts construction gives a total PH predicate agreeing with this randomized test on both promised sides; on intermediate ratios either answer is allowed. Under the same collapse this total predicate is in P. All subroutine exponents are fixed independent of n. There is no assumption that exact counting is in FP. A sequential scan uses poly(s,n) work for each bit, with at most O(L+1) rescans; poly(s,n)=N^o(1). Hence a fixed N^(1+ε) time lower bound for every separator, for some positive constant ε, would imply standard P≠NP. The implication alone supplies no such lower bound.

There is an obstacle to a proof via two-party communication. For every partition of the addresses, two parties can perform the same algorithm unconditionally with arbitrary local computation, using exact counts by enumeration. Both retain the labeled examples; each examines its local positions, and sends a failing address and bit, or reports that none exists. The total deterministic communication is O((n+1)(L+1)) bits including control flags. For the contiguous two halves, each party can test if its restricted table has a size-s circuit, and send a single answer bit. Restrictions of any size-s global circuit pass both tests after constant folding, while two local circuits passing them glue with a multiplexer into size 2s+O(1). This protocol presumes the usual circuit basis with constants at negligible fixed cost. These communication bounds allow arbitrarily expensive candidate counting and restricted-table fitting.
