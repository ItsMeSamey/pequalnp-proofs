# A quantitative hazard condition on unrestricted CLIQUE circuits

The fixed uniform NP language is CLIQUE with the vertex count, adjacency bits, and requested order. The polynomial-size certificate lists the vertices. At each fixed (N,k), hardwiring the two parameters leaves a circuit on adjacency bits. Set r=k−1 and take N=rv. The negative test set O is the single vertex-relabeling orbit of complete r-partite graphs with v vertices in each part. Its members have no k-clique.

For B in O, replace all its present edges by * and leave absent edges at 0. Evaluate an exact Boolean AND/OR/NOT circuit C for CLIQUE on this partial assignment using Kleene gates. Its output lies in {0,*}, since every Boolean completion gives 0. Write p(C) for the fraction of O giving 0. This depends on circuit presentation.

**Finite theorem.** If C has s gates and p=p(C)>0, there is a monotone circuit of size at most (s+1) ceil((1+ln|O|)/p) separating all k-cliques on v vertices from all complete multipartite graphs with at most k−1 nonempty parts on those vertices.

At each wire record its value on the empty graph. The bit indicating whether its ternary value changes to * as more edges become unknown is computed by a monotone circuit D of size at most s. For AND with input baselines 00,01,10,11, use AND, first projection, second projection, OR, respectively. OR has the dual table, and NOT copies its input variation. These rules follow by applying the Kleene gates. At a graph containing a clique, the available completions include the empty graph and the given graph, hence D outputs 1. At a partial test graph in O the stable outcomes give D=0. This construction uses the circuit hazard derivative. [Ikenmeyer et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.01904)

Take t=ceil((1+ln|O|)/p) independently uniform vertex relabelings of D and AND their outputs. For each fixed B in O the chance all copies accept is (1−p)^t. The expected number of uncovered orbit elements is at most e^(-1). Some fixed list of relabelings rejects the entire orbit and still accepts every k-clique. Finally keep the first v vertices and set every edge touching the others to zero. Every at-most-r-partite graph on the first v vertices, padded with isolated vertices, is a subgraph of some balanced member of O. Extend each of the color classes to v vertices using the available padding vertices. Monotonicity and acceptance of cliques give the conclusion.

**Unconditional consequence.** For 5≤k≤v^(1/4), Krajíček and Oliveira's Theorem 2, part 1, applied with no oracles and d=1, lower-bounds this ordinary monotone promise separator by v^(Ω(√k)). Their size includes sources. A trimmed binary circuit with g gates has at most g+1 input nodes, preserving the exponent. Combining with the finite theorem yields
p(C) ≤ (s+1)(2+ln|O|) / v^(Ω(√k)).
The inequality for p=0 holds directly. Since |O|≤r^N, the logarithmic factor is polynomial in v. Set k=floor(v^(1/4)). The adjacency representation with binary parameters has input length Θ(v^(5/2)). Every hypothetically polynomial-size exact ordinary CLIQUE circuit must then give * except on a fraction at most 2^(−Ω(v^(1/8) log v)) of this orbit. This conclusion does permit ordinary circuits with p=0. [Krajíček and Oliveira, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.06241)

**Explicit open sufficient conjecture.** Put Q(N,s)=2^(ceil(log₂(N+s+2))³). For every v with k=floor(v^(1/4))≥5, any exact size-s CLIQUE circuit on N=(k−1)v vertices has some Boolean-equivalent circuit C' with size ≤Q and p(C')≥1/Q. Each finite claim has specified size bounds and can in principle be refuted by exhaustive finite enumeration. For polynomial s, Q²(2+N ln r) is asymptotically smaller than the promise lower bound, so this repair claim would imply CLIQUE has no polynomial-size ordinary circuit family and hence P≠NP. No such repair is established. The explicit exponent 3 is a proposed budget, not a theorem about optimal repair.

**What is required of a repair.** For any one-sided monotone filter D on graphs accepting every k-clique, C AND D is exact whenever C is. At any B in O on which D(B)=0 the monotone filter stays 0 on the entire partial input. Conversely every C' with stable fraction p yields a monotone D of size ≤|C'| accepting every k-clique and rejecting p of O, by the variation construction. Thus in the presence of C, achieving the proposed repair is equivalent up to additive circuit size to constructing such a monotone filter. The promised structural step carries the force of the missing ordinary lower bound. Neither the derivative nor the orbit argument supplies it.

**A falsified stronger statement.** No lower bound p(C)>0 can hold for every exact presentation, even if one permits an additive O(N) gates in an example. For any exact C replace the output by C OR the disjunction of (e AND NOT e) over edges in any spanning tree. This gives the same Boolean answers. Every balanced graph B with r≥2 has a cross-part tree edge. On its partial input, that term is *, and the output is * on every B. For N=4,k=3, the monotone triangle DNF is stable on all three balanced bipartite graphs. Its modified circuit is stable on none. The accompanying checker compares all 64 Boolean inputs and all three partial inputs.

The observation on monotone filters also gives a finite exact formulation. At fixed parameters and target p let T(p) be the least monotone filter size and R(p) the least size of an exact circuit attaining the target stable fraction. If ordinary CLIQUE size is s*, then T(p)≤R(p)≤T(p)+s*+1. This singles out what can be optimized in finite synthesis experiments.

## An exact finite check on repair cost

For N=4 and k=3 let t range from 1 to 3. The minimum monotone filter size T(t/3) is exactly 2t−1 with binary AND/OR and input wires free. The complement of any one balanced K₂,₂ is a perfect matching of two edges. Adding either missing edge creates a triangle. Every one-sided filter rejecting that graph must depend on both missing-edge variables. The three perfect matchings partition the six edges of K₄. Rejecting t balanced graphs needs 2t distinct essential inputs, hence ≥2t−1 binary gates. For the converse take t perfect matchings, form the OR of the two edge variables in each, then AND the t clauses. Every triangle hits each perfect matching, and its missing-edge clause rejects the corresponding balanced graph. The costs for one, two and three stable negatives are 1, 3 and 5 gates. With all three clauses this filter still accepts a triangle-free star, demonstrating the role of the ordinary exact circuit in the repair construction.

More generally, every missing edge of a complete balanced r-partite graph is essential to any one-sided monotone filter rejecting that graph. Inserting it creates an (r+1)-clique. For any prescribed list of t balanced graphs with pairwise disjoint sets of missing edges, the minimum filter size rejecting all t is exactly t r binom(v,2)−1 when v≥2. The same essential-input lower bound and the AND of the t missing-edge OR clauses meet. This concerns specified negatives. An optimizer covering an unrestricted fraction can pick overlapping missing-edge sets.
