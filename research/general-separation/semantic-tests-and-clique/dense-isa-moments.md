# Dense conditional moments and squared error for products of address lookups

Use r disjoint copies of two-level indirect storage access with m=2^t payload bits and P=Θ(m/t) pointer rows per copy. Condition on all row indices and pointer words, arbitrarily fixed, with selected addresses p_a. Payload bits are independent uniform bits on the full cube. The target F is the product of the selected payload bits over r banks. Its conditional mean and squared norm are both u=2^(−r).

Fix d≥0 with t(d+2)≤m. In each bank take t disjoint blocks of d+2 payload bits; their XORs give an address R_a. After prescribing any d+1 coordinates, each seed block has an untouched bit, so all t parities remain independent and fair. For a shift tuple s of r addresses let M=m^r. Define A_s as M times the product, over all banks, of the Boolean indicator [R_a XOR s_a=p_a] and the payload bit at R_a XOR s_a. Its values are zero and M. For any payload monomial Q involving at most d bits per bank, condition on its coordinates and every selected target bit. The seed tuple remains uniform, and on the matching seed event the lookup values equal the target bits. Hence E[A_s Q]=E[FQ] conditionally on every choice of the pointer data, for every shift. Linearity gives equality for arbitrary mixed polynomial tests of degree at most d separately per bank, including pointer-dependent coefficients. The comparisons use unrestricted dense payloads.

The address equality in a bank costs O(Pt+t(d+2)) leaves: retrieve each pointer bit by an O(P)-size multiplexer and compute each parity by d+2 XOR leaves. A complete m-entry payload multiplexer using the shifted seed as selector costs O(m(d+1)) leaves. Each A_s is therefore one weighted O(rm(d+1))-leaf formula. Alternatively split each payload into aligned intervals of size Θ(m/(d+1)) rounded to a power of two. In each term guard the shifted seed's high bits and look up within that interval. Each bank's O(d+1) terms cost O(m+t(d+2))=O(m) leaves apiece. Multiplying across banks gives at most [O(d+1)]^r terms of O(rm) leaves each. Their coefficient M uses O(r log m) bits. For fixed r and d=m^{o(1)} there are subpolynomially many linear-size atoms. For d=O(K) one atom fits O(rmK) leaves.

## The exact squared-error tradeoff

The M shift events partition the space. Distinct A_s have disjoint supports, while E[A_s²]=Mu and E[A_s F]=u. For L distinct shifts and real weights w_s summing to one, H=sum_s w_s A_s matches all the same moments and satisfies

    E[(H−F)²] = u (M sum_s w_s²−1) ≥ u (M/L−1).

Equal weights attain the lower bound. Exact zero error in this shift dictionary requires all M shifts, and their equally weighted average recovers F pointwise. The formula is an exact dictionary-specific result.

One can also trade sparsity against coefficient size. Pick an aligned block length c=2^b≤m/(d+2) and use only b parity seed bits per bank. Guard the high bits of the actual selected pointer, compare its low bits to the seed and read the payload only at the seeded address inside this block. For one bank the m/c block terms have O(m) leaves and coefficient c. Exactly one guard can match, and the low-bit seed matches with conditional probability 1/c. The same proof yields squared error (c−1)/2 while preserving the moments. For independent banks with sizes c_a replace M by their product, and multiply the block counts. At c=1 the construction reads the actual payload bit and is exact.

For d≥1 the constant test and the product of target bits are included. A [0,1]-valued H matching both must equal F pointwise. Indeed E[(1−H)F]=E[H(1−F)]=0, and the distribution has full support. The scaled test functions above fail this range condition. Linear statistics alone cannot distinguish them from F; using Boolean range or a nonlinear norm constraint changes the problem. These formulas give no unconditional superpolynomial SUM lower bound for unrestricted product ISA. Such a lower bound would place a language in P outside the restricted SUM class, since the shared circuit for the target is linear. Separating P from NP would additionally require a bound valid for arbitrary polynomial-time deciders of an NP language.
