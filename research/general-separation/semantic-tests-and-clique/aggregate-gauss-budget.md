## A measured budget

Choose U of u coordinates such that every local restriction meets U in at most one bit, and each squarefree monomial of every phase polynomial meets U in at most two. After fixing the n−u other bits, the local tests either conflict, become constant or pin individual U bits. The remaining phase is quadratic on its free bits. Each block costs polynomial time by binary quadratic elimination, even when its coefficients and polar depend on the exterior assignment. The enumeration exponent is n−u+j. Thus the gain relative to q is u−(n−q)−j, charging every named bit, flag and selector. A verified rewrite increasing this integer by d lowers this exponent by d; its preprocessing and polynomial branch costs remain chargeable. True counts after pinning raw bits recover a raw witness.

For a constructive U use a conflict hypergraph with each pair in a local predicate and each triple within any phase monomial. Call their counts m₂,m₃. Take a dyadic probability θ within a factor two below min(1,n/(4m₂),sqrt(n/(4m₃))), omitting zero denominators. The expected number of selected bits minus bad pairs and triples is at least nθ/2. Fix bits by conditional expectations, then delete one endpoint per remaining bad edge. This deterministically gives u=Ω(min(n,n²/m₂,n^{3/2}/sqrt(m₃))) with zero denominators omitted, in polynomial time in the expanded support. Compact large symbolic polynomials cannot be expanded for free.

An alternative keeps the residual as a circuit. For each selector actually synthesize or otherwise verify a circuit of g_s gates returning zero or the sign and exponent e of the Gauss value ±2^e including C, with 0≤e≤u. Generic construction tests both values of each private bit to compute its local admissible domain, substitutes pins and performs alternating elimination with pivots conditional on the outside bits. This takes polynomially many gates in the explicitly expanded data and can be expensive. For each e take two accepting-count calls using the sign and multiply their difference by 2^e. Ordinary Tseitin then needs n−u+O(g_s+log(u+2)) variables per call with fixed density. Alternatively retain private-free local tests as bounded clauses and charge their actual density. Direct exterior enumeration remains available. Partial integer sums use O(n+j+1) bits. Dovetailing a fixed finite menu after paying all preparation costs avoids comparing unknown big-O constants on a specific instance.

## Actual comparisons and controls

The round-robin XOR of edge products from injective-local-interface-count.md is a single quadratic on the original inputs. Add the output-bit requirement as one polynomial equation (no named output bit is necessary). Then n=u=q, j=1 and two Gauss sums suffice. Replacing the edges by M=O(q log q) cubic monomials also fails as a counterexample: a private U avoiding all cubic triples has size Ω(q/√log q), regardless of their order or connectivity. Fix the other bits and count the resulting quadratic for each sign. If O(q log q) bounded-size local tests are present the same packing analysis gives Ω(q/log q) private bits before interface charges. For explicit fixed-degree polynomial *systems* there is already a stronger deterministic fractional-exponent saving by Lokshtanov, Paturi, Tamaki, Williams and Yu. Their Theorem 1.4 also handles explicit sums of products of affine forms with its occurrence-dependent bound. Convert bounded local truth tests to their fixed-size ANFs and, for signed counts, split on the one added quadratic sign equation. The packing estimate is a simple checkable kernel, not a new best general polynomial-system algorithm. [Primary theorems 1.2 and 1.4](https://theory.stanford.edu/~yuhch123/files/polyEq.pdf)

For a family using both pieces let a,b be disjoint ℓ-bit vectors, V an exterior vector of Θ(ℓ) bits, and M a fixed nonsingular binary matrix. Take M upper triangular with its diagonal and the next Θ(log ℓ) diagonals all ones. Write each nonzero entry as a distinct original pair product, followed by their XOR, so the supplied DAG has Θ(ℓ log ℓ) such terms. Add L(V)ᵀb and d(V) to the phase, where L can use coordinates from V and d is a Boolean bit with an O(ℓ)-gate circuit. Require this phase to be zero together with private-free local predicates H(V), for example the Θ(ℓ log ℓ) essential fixed-width edge clauses on injectively coded labels in the companion note. All size specifications refer to supplied circuits, not circuit lower bounds.

The earlier round-robin lower bound was for original interfaces compiled as signed ANDs of bounded-support predicates. The quadratic test invalidates its use as an obstruction to the combined portfolio. A separate reversible-interface control shows that translating one bypass wire into prescribed coordinates can require Ω(q log q) gates, so a universal small-cost *bypass translation* is false. Neither control proves that all possible SAT rewrites are expensive. Arbitrary Bq log q circuits still lack a universal positive bound on the charged exponent or residual gates, and no unrestricted complexity separation follows.
