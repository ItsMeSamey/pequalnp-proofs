# A uniformity test for the common catalytic budget

Let N=2^n and fix 0≤p<1/2. Consider the proposed upper bound that every E language has, on infinitely many lengths, a uniform deterministic controller with at most Cn initialized bits and at most N total state bits. Every initial borrowed string yields a complete N-bit transcript within pN Hamming errors of the desired table, and the terminal states of these runs are distinct. The constants C, a and b below must be common to all the languages. The controller scheme may differ by language. Its description and initialization convention can be constructed in time N^a, and a tick can be simulated deterministically in time N^b including tape and circuit access. These bounds need only hold on good lengths if all construction and simulation algorithms are cut off at their declared limits everywhere. In particular the argument applies to a fixed native catalytic Turing machine per language with the indicated common space coefficient and fixed simulation overhead.

This upper bound would fail under the usual Impagliazzo–Wigderson hypothesis that some language in E requires at least 2^{εn} deterministic gates at every sufficiently large length for a fixed ε>0. We use its quantitative generator conclusion: for any fixed constant δ>0, a single uniform logarithmic-seed, polynomial-time generator fools each Boolean circuit of size m to additive error δ as a test of m random bits, with standard padding of its output or circuit as needed. A fixed polynomial in m bounds enumeration of all generator outputs. [Impagliazzo and Wigderson](https://www.math.ias.edu/~avi/PUBLICATIONS/MYPAPERS/IW97/proc.pdf)

For a uniform random borrowed string on a good length, the expected number of ticks to print the full table is at most N^{C+1}, since the sum over distinct terminal representatives is at most N·2^s and there are at least 2^{s−Cn} strings. For a small fixed τ>0, truncate at ceil(N^{C+1}/τ) ticks, using a default all-zero transcript on failure. Its expected fraction of wrong bits is at most p+τ. For each x separately, a uniform-random-initialization experiment returning the x-indexed bit of this truncated transcript has an ordinary bounded-fanin circuit of size N^u for a constant u depending only on a,b,C and a fixed simulation convention, after absorbing machine-dependent constants eventually. For example direct polynomial-time circuit simulation suffices. Its uniform simulation on each supplied random tape also takes N^u time after increasing u.

Enumerate the generator seeds for m=ceil(N^u), using at most a fixed polynomial round-up if padding is necessary. Simulate and output the mean g_n(x) of their output signs in {−1,+1}. This is an exact dyadic number in [−1,1] with O(log N) bits, computable within N^K for one *common* K. The pseudorandom mean differs from the uniform mean sign by at most 2δ at each x, regardless of whether a bit has a bounded-error gap. The true sign table therefore has correlation at least 1−2(p+τ+δ) with g_n on every good length. Choose fixed positive τ and δ so this lower bound is positive.

For every fixed K and every fixed rational 0<ε₀<1 there is an E language with absolute correlation less than ε₀ on every sufficiently large length against each uniform deterministic algorithm clocked at N^K that outputs a dyadic predictor in [−1,1]. Enumerate the first n machines of a standard coding and compute all their time-capped output tables, reading ordinary finite binary fractions, clipping values to [−1,1], and defaulting to zero on timeout or malformed output. On that length choose a parity character having absolute correlation less than ε₀ with every listed table. Parseval bounds the number of characters with correlation magnitude at least ε₀ by 1/ε₀² per table, since the mean square of each table is at most one. Exhausting all characters, addresses and clocks takes N^{O_K(1)} time, including exact arithmetic on time-capped binary fractions. Declare a fixed table on the finitely many small lengths where the selection fails. For a time bound only up to a machine-dependent constant, increase the universal clock exponent by one. Choosing ε₀ below the preceding positive correlation contradicts the infinitely-often uniform upper. Each selected table is itself an ordinary parity with O(n) nonuniform gates. Thus this test cannot refute nonuniform short controllers.

There is an unconditional version for a timing-restricted compiler. In addition to the same constructive bounds, suppose that at every good length a designated successful initial seed can be computed within the same N^a construction clock (or N^h for a further common fixed h) and is guaranteed to take at most N^j times the shortest successful run, with fixed j. The full restoration and clean-bit assumptions give a successful run within N^{C+1} ticks by averaging, so direct simulation of the designated one yields a common deterministic time bound and at most pN errors. The same parity-table hierarchy gives a contradiction without the circuit-hardness hypothesis. For an untrusted constructor on other lengths all routines are truncated deterministically. The result tests a required seed-timing guarantee. Native delays can differ substantially between catalytic starting states.

These conclusions are reasons to separate a uniform compiler conjecture with common construction and simulation clocks from the weaker nonuniform representation condition. Neither the exponential circuit-hardness hypothesis nor the nonuniform all-E simulation bound is established. [Terminal-state clock and its catalytic antecedent](terminal-diversity-clock.md)
