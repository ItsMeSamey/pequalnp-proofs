# A direct row-pair decision on dominating-pair remainders

Input is a simple undirected graph G with three distinct named vertices. Try each named vertex z adjacent to every other site; p,q are the remaining names. Work in K=G−z. Its selected vertices must all have odd induced degree, which makes their cardinality even by handshaking. Putting z back is an exact inverse.

Require p,q nonadjacent with nonempty disjoint neighborhoods A=N_K(p), B=N_K(q) and all A–B edges present. D consists of the remaining unnamed sites. Test that each of A,B,D induces a clique or an independent set, separately; all eight modes are admitted, with unrestricted A–D and B–D incidences. The partition is recovered from original adjacencies. An isolated compulsory site in K gives NO; adjacent pins immediately give YES. On inputs outside these tests a class recognizer may decline. In H=overline K−pq the endpoints p,q together dominate, so no p–q path avoids any closed neighborhood.

Let N_i be the neighbors of i in A∪B within D. If D is a clique, complement the rows for B within D; otherwise keep them. Call the transformed rows R_i. The answer is YES exactly when some a in A and b in B have incomparable rows. Take d in R_a minus R_b and e in R_b minus R_a. The original witness is {z,p,q,a,b,d,e}. All tests take O(n³) time using explicit incidence scans. For D of size zero or one both modes reject.

Write X_A,X_B,Y for the selected parts. Both X classes have odd size because of their pins. At every selected row the pinned neighbor and the odd opposite-class occupancy together contribute zero parity. Its own clique or independent set contributes even degree. Every selected row thus has odd intersection with Y. Handshaking gives |Y| even. A selected column in an independent D needs an odd number of selected row neighbors; one in a clique D needs an even number. The B-row flip precisely in the clique case makes each selected column meet an odd number of selected transformed rows while preserving all selected row parities.

Count modulo two the sum over selected a,b of |R_a∩R_b∩Y|. At each column its contribution is a product of the two selected class multiplicities. Their sum is odd, so their product is even. If every cross pair were comparable, each intersection would be the smaller restricted selected row and have odd size. This would give an odd number of odd summands. Conversely two incomparable rows and the indicated two columns have one incidence per row and column after the transformation. Reversing the flip and inserting the three pins gives the actual witness. No trace oracle or width bound is required.

The smallest counterexample to dropping A/B homogeneity has |A|=3, |B|=1 and |D|=2 with D independent. The A rows are 01,11,00; the sole B row is 01. The only edge inside A links 11 and 00. All nine original sites are a witness, but no cross pair is incomparable. In any missed witness at least one X class must contain three selected sites; with one of each, the parity double count still applies whatever internal edges exist. Both classes are odd, and |Y| is even and nonzero: summing the row guards within A rules out an empty Y even with arbitrary edges internal to A. Thus nine is minimal.

For graphs with unbounded width use A and D as the two sides of an m-by-m grid with its usual incidences, B={b}, and make A,B,D independent in K. Give b no neighbor in D for NO; add just b–d at a fixed d in D for YES, since some a misses d and has another D neighbor for m≥4. In both cases H[A∪D] is the complement of the grid. It has a clique of unbounded order and unbounded rank-width. Complementation changes each cut rank by at most one. A balanced vertex cut of the grid has Ω(m) crossing edges: either ≥m/6 rows are mixed or there are whole rows on both sides, forcing a crossing in each column. Greedily discard edges conflicting with each chosen crossing edge to find a crossing induced matching of size Ω(m) since degrees are ≤4. This gives a binary identity submatrix. Every subcubic leaf layout has a balanced cut by a centroid argument. Restricting a layout to an induced subgraph never increases cut rank.

Separately take the prior K_6-minor-free H formed from a planar square grid with corner q and apex p adjacent to every grid site except q. Add pq to obtain J. There is no even-cardinality even witness through p because p is universal in J. Removing one additional edge pr, where r is the grid neighbor of q along a side, gives YES. With s the next grid vertex along that side, {p,q,r,s} induces a cycle in J and adjoining z gives the raw witness. In both cases p,q dominate H, which contains grids of unbounded width, and deletion of p leaves a planar graph. These direct tests make no claim for general one-apex instances.

A dominating set of two specified endpoints alone imposes little. Given any two compulsory nonadjacent a,b in an all-odd instance, add ab and attach two newly compulsory leaves p,q at a,b, replacing the old pin set. The leaves force both old pins and each old pin gains two selected neighbors. Projection and extension are inverse on all witnesses. In the complementary remainder H the endpoints p,q dominate. Thus deciding this whole subclass already decides the unrestricted universal-pin branch.
