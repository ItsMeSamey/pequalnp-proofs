# First emission under affine silent updates

Fix one output address of a sequential controller. Write its mutable state as z in F₂^s. Suppose a Boolean flag f(z) of algebraic degree at most e determines emission. On each silent state, suppose the next state agrees with one affine map Φ. Both the flag and map may depend on the address. Values emitted and states after emission may come from arbitrary Boolean circuits. Count ticks from zero, so an initial emission is at tick zero.

The first emission occurs at a tick strictly smaller than D = ∑_{j=0}^{min(e,s)} binom(s,j), or it never occurs. The maps need not be invertible. To prove this, use the vector space of functions spanned by squarefree monomials of degree at most e on the Boolean cube. It has dimension D. Pullback P by Φ preserves it, since substitution by affine forms and reduction x²=x preserve the degree bound. By Cayley–Hamilton the D-th iterate of P is a linear combination of earlier iterates. Evaluation on f at the initial state gives a homogeneous linear recurrence for f(Φ^t z) valid for all t≥0. If the first D values are zero, all values are zero. Up to its first one this sequence agrees with the actual emission flags. More generally, for an arbitrary map on an invariant domain the same proof uses the dimension of the span of all pullbacks of the flag. Filtered linear registers and linear complexity have a classical literature. [Key (1976)](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1976.1055626) and [On the linear complexity of nonlinearly filtered PN-sequences](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2003.818400).

For each fixed e≥1 the order D=Θ_e(s^e) is attained up to constants. Allocate e disjoint binary one-hot registers of respective lengths m_j, one per first e prime numbers p_j. For large s let m_j be the greatest power of p_j at most s/e. These lengths have sum at most s and m_j>s/(e p_j). Rotate each register by a linear permutation and position its sole marker so that its designated coordinate is one exactly when t equals −1 modulo m_j. The degree-e product of those coordinates first lights at t=∏_j m_j−1, by pairwise coprimality.

A quadratic update with linear flag can wait exponentially longer. Its 2k+1 bits are digit bits b_i, one-hot token bits u_i (0≤i<k), and h. Set b=0, u_0=1, all other u_i=0, h=0. Define these simultaneous updates in F₂:
 
    b_i' = b_i + u_i
    u_i' = u_(i−1) b_(i−1)                       (i≥1)
    u_0' = ∑_i u_i(1+b_i) + u_(k−1)b_(k−1)
    h'   = u_(k−1)b_(k−1).

Emit when h=1. A token on a one clears that digit and moves to the next position, wrapping on overflow. A token on a zero sets it to one and returns to the least significant position. The token stays one-hot. Each run from token zero-position back to zero-position increments the represented integer by one modulo 2^k. Before and including the first complete overflow, digit i is visited 2^{k−i} times. Thus the first emission occurs at tick ∑_i 2^{k−i}=2^{k+1}−2. All coordinates together cost O(k) binary gates. An affine fixed-degree-e controller reproducing this timestamp requires Ω_e(2^{k/e}) bits for fixed e≥1. The example constrains tick-for-tick simulation. A controller reproducing only the output bits may skip silent work.

In the conditional truth-table avoidance theorem, take N=2^n, n≤s≤αN/n, at most αN/n gates and at most αN/s exceptional addresses. For fixed e, delay D is polynomial in N even at the largest permitted s. More generally the same conclusion holds whenever D≤N^c for one fixed c. For instance, s=n² and e=O(n/log n) meet this bound. Take the avoiding language for the fixed polynomial-delay cap in that theorem. Truncate simulation of *every* descriptor at this cap. This covers each controller meeting the semantic affine/degree promise without needing to verify that promise. Consequently, under SAT∈P, one E language has constant uniform error against every such total controller at every sufficiently large length, for the chosen c. This supplies a native-time condition for structured machines. An upper bound representing arbitrary E languages within the controller budget remains open.
