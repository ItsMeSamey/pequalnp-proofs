# One missing handshake state in an irreducible three-pin pair

Color six vertices by the binary integers 1 through 6 in order. Let T(s,t) indicate whether a selected set has color XOR s and inner selected degrees prescribed by color dot t. Handshaking allows exactly 36 pairs (s,t) with s·t=0. The graph with adjacency masks 42,57,8,55,10,11 realizes all of them. The graph with masks 44,48,25,21,14,3 realizes every one except (7,0).

Empty and singleton sets settle every allowed pair with s other than 7. For s=7 the only potential six-bit selection masks are 11,12,18,21,33,38,56,63. The full-table graph realizes t=0,3,5,6 with masks 11,18,12,12, respectively. On the second graph all eight candidates fail at t=0; t=3,5,6 are realized by 18,12,12. Adjacency-mask indices start at zero.

Both tables give identical answers to any decision-only query of either following type. The first asks for all-even degrees and at most two affine conditions on the selected label sum. The second allows a fixed boundary signal t₀ and at most one affine label condition. In either type one may first add U L Uᵀ to the graph over the binary field and clear the diagonal, where U is the color matrix and L is any symmetric 3-by-3 matrix. Such a query at selected sum s tests the original state T(s,t₀+Ls+diag L). Its handshake condition is s·t₀=0, since s·Ls=s·diag L. In either type the allowed s form an affine subspace of dimension at least one whenever nonempty. At least two distinct compatible states are queried in every nonempty test, so removing just one cannot change the answer. This includes adaptive choices based on these decision answers. It does not compare returned witnesses or procedures with unrestricted access to the original graph.

To obtain literal instances first add the off-diagonal label update with L having its only ones in positions (1,2),(2,1), indexing the three label coordinates starting at one. Add three selected pins with colors 1,4,6, joined to old vertices by the binary color dot product. Join only the first two pins to one another. Their colors are independent and sum to 3. Their inner-degree vector (1,1,0) forces the free color sum 7 because its dot products with 1,4,6 are 1,1,0. The prescribed free boundary is 3, canceled by L·7=3. The resulting instance therefore asks exactly for T(7,0). The adjacency lists with pins at positions 0,1,2 are

    YES 170,449,240,97,356,477,446,359,242
    NO  170,449,240,81,300,341,174,327,178.

The YES graph has exactly two witnessing masks, 95 and 175. Both graphs have vertex connectivity at least three and admit no side of size at least four allowed by the [pin-free rank-two or one-pin rank-one reduction](rank-two-cut-replacement.md). Their private span dimensions are both two. Their best-template dimensions are one for YES and zero for NO. These claims can be checked by direct enumeration of the nine sites and their 64 exterior loop or selection choices. The label queries refer to the six-site graphs before attachment; the irreducibility claims concern the resulting physical instances.
