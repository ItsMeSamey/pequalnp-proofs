# A lower bound across CNF encodings of CLIQUE

Fix v, 5≤k≤v^(1/4), r=k−1 and N=rv. Let O be the orbit of complete r-partite graphs on N vertices with all blocks of size v. An encoding is a CNF F(x,y) such that, on every Boolean graph x, a satisfying assignment y exists exactly when x contains a k-clique. Let m≥2 bound the combined number of variables (including all adjacency inputs), clauses and literal occurrences. A polynomial-size encoding exists by the NP verifier. The encoding may be nonuniform and is fixed before B is drawn from O.

For B∈O, assign zero to each edge outside B, leaving all other variables unset. Simplification here means substitution of these constants and removal of true clauses and false literals, with no unit propagation or semantic preprocessing. The simplified formula is unsatisfiable. Proofs use ordinary binary resolution without extension axioms. Let p_w(F) be the uniform fraction of B for which this simplified CNF has a resolution refutation using clauses of width at most w, including its used axioms. An empty initial clause has width zero.

**Theorem.** For an absolute constant c and w≥0,
p_w(F) ≤ m^(c(w+1)) (2+ln|O|) / v^(Ω(√k)).
At k=floor(v^(1/4)) and m≤v^d for fixed d, some c_d>0 ensures every refutation requires width at least c_d v^(1/8), except on a fraction at most 2^(−Ω(v^(1/8)log v)). The bound is uniform in the fixed encoding and allows arbitrary auxiliary variables and long initial clauses.

**Proof.** Use missing-edge variables z_e as inputs. Let L count clauses of width ≤w over all variables of F, representing a clause by a set of signed literals. Allow tautologies and include the empty clause. Then L≤(2m+1)^(w+1). For each candidate D initialize its derived bit if some initial C contains D and all literals in C minus D are positive graph edges e with z_e=1. This needs one monotone AND per eligible pair and an OR. It is sound under the partial assignment because all discarded literals are false. Every surviving width-w initial clause of the simplified formula is included: only falsified positive-edge literals were removed. A surviving clause can thus enter the table even when its original width exceeded w. Extra entries containing already true negative literals remain valid under the partial assignment.

In each of L rounds retain previous clauses and add all width-w resolvents with both parents derived. All inferences are sound even for tautological candidate parents. This process includes every width-w refutation of the simplified formula. Soundness and this one-way completeness suffice; we do not require equality of refutation models. The clause set grows until stable, which takes at most L rounds. Initialization costs O(m²L) gates; O(m L³) suffices for all pivots, pairs, stages and binary AND/OR reductions. Dualizing the empty-clause bit as 1−empty(1−x) gives a monotone filter on allowed edges of size m^O(w+1). For every graph containing a k-clique the restriction has a satisfying extension and the filter accepts. It rejects at least p_w(F) of O.

Randomly relabel and intersect copies of the filter as in the hazard note. Padding v-vertex tests into the balanced N-vertex orbit yields an ordinary monotone separator of size at most (1+m^O(w+1)) ceil((1+ln|O|)/p_w(F)) when p_w(F)>0. The lower bound v^(Ω(√k)) follows from Krajíček–Oliveira, Theorem 2 with no oracle inputs. Rearrangement proves the claim. [Krajíček and Oliveira](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.06241)

**Input-dependent encoding.** A selector given B may choose one of at most 2^b fixed encodings with size ≤m. A union bound gives a factor 2^b in the allowable fraction. For any such choice to succeed on at least half the orbit it is necessary that
b + O((w+1) log₂ m) + O(log N) ≥ Ω(√k log₂ v).
This holds even for selectors of unlimited computational power. Specifying B completely permits width zero: append to a standard encoding the redundant positive clause OR_{e outside B} x_e. Every k-clique uses an edge outside B. Under the indicated restriction this clause becomes empty. Thus encoding dependence on the test graph matters even though each encoding is globally correct.

**A structural sufficient condition with a missing premise.** Suppose a hypothetical polynomial-size deterministic CLIQUE circuit could be compiled into at most 2^polylog(v) correct polynomial-size encodings such that on at least half the balanced restrictions, some residual CNF had polylog(v) primal treewidth. The tradeoff would then contradict that circuit family. Treewidth is measured after assigning missing edges and simplifying the CNF. An unsatisfiable CNF of primal treewidth h has a resolution refutation of width ≤h+1: take a width-h elimination ordering in a chordal completion, eliminate variables by resolving opposite occurrences and deleting the old clauses, and discard tautological resolvents. At elimination all remaining neighbors fit a clique of at most h vertices. Resolution elimination preserves feasibility by existential quantification, so the final formula contains the empty clause. The condition can also be stated in terms of resolution width directly. Polynomial deterministic running time alone supplies no such decomposition. The unconditional theorem rules out a family of compilers and leaves ordinary circuit lower bounds open.

## Width zero, width one and short arbitrary proofs

Write P_B for the positive clause of all edges missing from B, and q=r binom(v,2) for its number of literals. B is an edge-maximal k-clique-free graph: any inserted edge joins two vertices in one part, and one vertex chosen from each other part completes a k-clique. Consequently, if F entails an entirely positive adjacency clause P⊆P_B, then P=P_B. Otherwise insert an omitted missing edge; the resulting graph has a k-clique, admits a witness for F, and still falsifies P.

Let F have a clauses and L literal occurrences. Its width-zero refutation on B exists exactly when P_B is an axiom. An empty restricted axiom had only missing positive graph literals originally, so the maximality argument applies. Consequently at most floor(L/q) members of O can have width zero. A globally correct encoding can cover any specified t members with tq additional literal occurrences by adding their P_B clauses.

A width-one refutation in a CNF contains either an empty axiom or two opposite unit axioms: the only new nontautological resolvent of two width-one parents is the empty clause. In the latter case lift the two axioms back to F and resolve on their common pivot. Only positive missing edges were dropped from each axiom. The original resolvent is a globally entailed positive clause contained in P_B, hence equals P_B. An unordered axiom pair has at most one pivot yielding a positive nontautological resolvent, since another complementary pair would survive the resolution. We may remove tautological axioms in advance as they never become opposite units under simplification. At most a+binom(a,2) balanced graphs can therefore have width ≤1. Conversely each original direct nontautological resolvent equal to P_B yields an empty axiom or opposite units on that restriction. Distinct P_B are distinct clauses. For these two widths the advice tradeoff sharpens to b≥log₂|O|−O(log m) for success on half the orbit. Here |O|=N!/((v!)^r r!). Encoding the orbit index and appending P_B uses ceil(log₂|O|) advice bits and polynomial individual encoding size.

A related counting bound permits unlimited width but at most t resolution inferences. A proof of the empty clause from the simplified formula lifts step by step to a proof from original axioms whose clauses differ only by additional positive missing-edge literals. All pivots in the simplified proof are unset; adding the discarded literals cannot alter those pivots. The final lifted clause is a globally entailed subset of P_B, so it equals P_B. Thus bounded proofs for different B require different derived final clauses. There are at most (t+1)(a+t+1)[2m(a+t+1)^2]^t possible final clauses obtainable in ≤t inferences, allowing either a derived clause or an axiom for output. Choose each pair of parents and signed pivot in sequence, and then choose the output. This also covers t=0. Dividing this number by |O| bounds the successful fraction. In the polynomial-size regime above, log₂|O|=Θ(N log v) by the factorial formula. If t<N then log₂(a+t+1)=O(log v), so the count implies t=Ω(N) for success on half the orbit. The same lower bound is immediate when t≥N. This inference bound allows arbitrary width and applies to an encoding fixed before B. This is an inference-count bound for projected CNF encodings on these restrictions.
