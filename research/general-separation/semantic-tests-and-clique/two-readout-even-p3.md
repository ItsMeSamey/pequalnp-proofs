# A support or a cut for two parity readouts

Color a finite simple undirected graph by vectors in the binary plane. The following algorithm constructs a vertex set of target label sum d inducing only even degrees, or certifies its absence. Target zero uses the empty set. A vertex of color d realizes a nonzero target as a singleton. Otherwise let E,F be the classes of the two remaining nonzero colors. Any witness must use an odd number of each. If some E vertex and some F vertex are nonadjacent, their pair realizes d.

Otherwise E is completely joined to F. Let Z be the neutral (zero color) class. If a component of G[Z] touches E and F, take a shortest path from E to F with at least one internal vertex, all internal vertices in that component. For this search omit E–F edges and use only E–Z, Z–Z and Z–F edges, with the endpoints in their respective classes. A shortest such path has no chord except the edge joining its endpoints; any further chord shortens an allowed path. Its vertices therefore induce an all-even cycle with label sum d.

If no neutral component touches both classes, take X equal to E together with all neutral components touching E. Only E–F edges cross X. Any set S with target d has an odd number of induced edges across this cut, whereas every induced all-even graph has even cut size. Breadth-first searches and adjacency tests run in polynomial time. Empty classes are covered.

This supplies genuine center witnesses for the symmetric P3 problem with all normalized prescriptions zero under two simultaneous affine readouts. Absorb constants into the target; undo the bit translations and fill private leaves as in [the one-readout theorem](symmetric-single-row-p3.md). A single edge with endpoint colors e,f is already a span-only failure for e+f. A triangle under two distinguished bit requirements both equal to one can force the full triangle, defeating search restricted to induced linear forests.

For arbitrary normalized prescriptions c, adding an apex adjacent to all odd-prescription vertices and requiring it to be selected reduces the problem to three readouts on an all-even graph. Assign the apex zero on the original readouts. The parity equation at the new vertex follows by handshaking on the original selected graph. This does not extend the two-readout cut dichotomy to all mixed prescriptions. The [rank-two twist](symmetric-two-readout-twist.md) gives another construction and handles more mixed cases.
