# Keeping a shared outer circuit after a symplectic quadratic frontier

Let F have order N=2^r, r≥3, and let a quadratic binary map from 2kr input bits to r output bits have polar equal, up to unknown independent binary coordinate changes, to a regular alternating F-bilinear form on F^{2k} with values in F. The complete map can have any binary-affine perturbation. We give a polynomial preprocessing test for a signed quadratic description of its output multiplicities. When the test succeeds, exact counting after an arbitrary Boolean DAG H uses one baseline H count and at most two counts with a system of affine constraints and a quadratic bit appended to the same H. The actual complexity of those counts is charged.

## Recovering the coordinates

For the binary alternating polar β, solve β(Ax,y)=β(x,Ay)=Uβ(x,y) linearly in matrices (A,U). This is the standard centroid computation. In native field coordinates its solutions are exactly the simultaneous field scalars. Indeed surjectivity of β and field bilinearity give U(ab)=aU(b) by comparing β(Ax,ay) and aβ(Ax,y). Hence U is multiplication by U(1); nondegeneracy forces A to be the same scalar. The regular form and this calculation are classical. [Brooksbank, Maglione and Wilson, centroid definition and algorithm](https://joshmaglione.com/papers/Genus2.pdf)

In unknown coordinates check the projected output algebra has dimension r, commutes, includes identity, is closed under products, and has unique companions A. Recover a field generator with the following deterministic rule. For each prime p dividing r form the linear kernel of U↦U^{2^{r/p}}+U by repeated squaring. In a field the sum of their cardinalities is strictly smaller than 2^r, by r2^{r/2}<2^r for r≥5 and directly for r=3,4. Exclude all these proper subfields successively by fixing basis bits, counting completions in each kernel by linear algebra and preserving strict inequality of their sum to all completions. Check that the resulting U has irreducible minimal polynomial of degree r; this polynomial represents F. Any nonzero output vector yields a field-coordinate basis using U powers. On the input use corresponding powers of A, repeatedly taking a vector outside the current F-span, checking that the complete set of resulting binary basis vectors is independent and has dimension 2kr. Perform alternating Gram–Schmidt over F and check every original polar coefficient in the obtained coordinates against the field symplectic form. Such final verification makes the certificate sound on arbitrary inputs. All stated steps are polynomial in the explicit polar size.

In the resulting coordinates the map is
Q(a,b)=Σ_{j=1}^k (a_j b_j+L_j(a_j)+M_j(b_j))+c
with binary-linear L_j,M_j. Subtract the displayed quadratic part to read all affine coefficients. The trace pairing gives unique binary-linear adjoints L_j*,M_j*. Each adjoint has a unique linearized polynomial with at most r terms by the root bound and dimension. Obtain its coefficients by solving the binary linear system for its values on a field basis.

## A sparse expression for every nonzero selector

For s≠0, summing (−1)^{Tr(sQ)} over b forces a_j=M_j*(s)/s, then contributes N^k (−1)^{ψ(s)} with
ψ(s)=Tr(cs+s^{−1}Σ_j L_j*(s)M_j*(s)).
The zero-selector sum is N^{2k}. Expanding the r trace conjugates and reducing exponents modulo N−1 yields a polynomial representative of ψ on F* with fewer than O((k+1)r³+1) pre-combination terms. Exponents use O(r) bits. Its degree as a univariate representative is at most N−2; uniqueness holds on F*. Set ψ(0) to its constant coefficient. This coefficient is binary because Frobenius preserves ψ on F* and fixes only exponent zero among exponents that can square to zero modulo N−1. Thus the canonical extension is Boolean on F.

Its Boolean degree is the largest binary Hamming weight of an occurring exponent, with the zero polynomial treated as degree at most zero. In fact z^e is the product of wt(e) binary-linear Frobenius powers. The univariate monomials with weights ≤d are linearly independent over F as functions, and their number equals the dimension of the F-valued Boolean functions of degree ≤d, showing equality of spaces. An alternative value at zero changes the reduced full-field representation by 1+z^{N−1}, of Boolean degree r. For d<r a low-degree extension, if it exists, is therefore the canonical one. In particular sparse coefficient aggregation decides the degree-at-most-two promise exactly for r≥3, with no enumeration of the N selectors.

## Compiling the output multiplicities

Write W_ψ(y)=Σ_s (−1)^{ψ(s)+Tr(sy)} including zero. Orthogonality gives for all y
|Q^{-1}(y)|=N^{2k−1}+N^{k−1}(W_ψ(y)−(−1)^{ψ(0)}).
For degree at most two, compute the quadratic coefficients of ψ by evaluations at zero, basis vectors and pairs. Let R be its polar rank. Alternating elimination expresses the phase plus Tr(sy) as a sum of R/2 disjoint pair products, linear terms whose coefficients are affine bits of y, and a constant. The radical-coordinate coefficients impose affine equations E(y). On E the Walsh value is 2^{r−R/2}(−1)^{b(y)} where b is the constant plus the sum of the products of the two affine coefficients at each paired coordinate. It vanishes off E. Consequently the requested count is
(N^{2k−1}−N^{k−1}(−1)^{ψ(0)}) #H + N^{k−1}2^{r−R/2}(#(H∧E∧[b=0])−#(H∧E∧[b=1])).
Each appended test uses O(r²) binary gates by straightforward synthesis of the affine bits and pairs, including the explicit output coordinate conversion if needed. The integers use O(kr+r) bits, in addition to the bit cost of the scalar counts. This is a conditional compiler on a supplied or otherwise verified quadratic frontier, with all costs of finding it separate.

When ψ is affine the correction is concentrated at one output, so one #H and one direct H evaluation suffice. The case of an ordinary regular F-quadratic form, including its standard two Arf types, has the classical one-exception fiber counts. These representation numbers are already given by Klapper. [Klapper, Propositions 2.4 and 2.5](https://www.cs.uky.edu/~klapper/pdf/even-quad-xc.pdf) The compiler also covers nonlinear signs: take k=1 and L*(s)=M*(s)=s². Then ψ(s)=Tr(s³). For odd r its quadratic polar has rank r−1, since the radical equation v^{2^{r−1}}+v²=0 reduces to v+v⁴=0 with solutions 0,1. For r≥3 it is not affine. The correction uses an affine hyperplane of output values and a quadratic sign rather than a single exceptional point.

For the multiplication frontier ab on F², the odd-fiber property under every binary-affine perturbation needs the entire private domain if it must survive arbitrary input restrictions. For any missing pair (a₀,b₀), choose t with Tr(t)=1 and put A=a+a₀, B=b+b₀. Then B²+AB+tA² is ab plus binary-affine terms. It vanishes only at A=B=0: for A≠0 the quotient B/A would solve X²+X+t=0, and at A=0 one must have B=0. Hence restricting the domain by even that single point destroys universal surjectivity under affine perturbations. On the full domain the odd-fiber property follows, for example, by summing ranks of the multiplication pencil on its full 2r-space: all N−1 nonzero selectors have nonsingular polar, so the mixed Pfaffian parity is one.

No guarantee of a suitable private cone or a low-degree selector phase is asserted for general shared circuits. The standard uniform separation remains open.
