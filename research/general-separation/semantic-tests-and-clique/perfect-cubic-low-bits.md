# Exact low bits of a cubic matching gap by connected omissions

Let F be the sum modulo two of m disjoint cubic monomials on all 3m variables, plus a quadratic Q including its affine and constant terms. Join two triple blocks in H whenever Q has a cross term between them. Write Δ for its maximum degree. For every positive k, the residue of Z(F)/2^m modulo 2^k is computable deterministically in poly(m,k,input)·max(2,Δ)^{O(k)} bit operations and polynomial working space, with k output bits. In particular fixed quadratic maximum degree permits polynomial time for k=O(log(2+m)). The matching and Q are explicit. These low 2-adic bits do not directly give the odd-modulus residue required by the counting reduction.

For an omitted set A of triples fix all variables outside A to one. Write Q_A(x_A)=Q(x_A,1_out)+Q(1_all) in F₂, and put

w(A)=(−2)^{−|A|} ∑_{x_A}(−1)^{Q_A(x_A)}.

Expanding each triple's sign as one minus twice its monomial, and selecting every triple outside A, gives Z(F)/2^m=(−1)^{m+Q(1_all)} ∑_A w(A). For a=|A| the quadratic sign sum is zero or ±2^{3a−r/2}, where r is the even rank of its polar. This follows by eliminating symplectic pairs and checking the linear character on the radical. Thus w(A) is integral and divisible by 2^{ceil(a/2)}. Terms with a≥2k−1 vanish modulo 2^k. Put D=min(m,2k−2). We need only coefficients through degree D of P(z)=∑_A w(A)z^{|A|}.

In coordinates x+1 the polynomial Q_A has constant zero and splits over components of H[A], since all outside variables are zero after centering. Hence w(empty)=1 and w is multiplicative on these components. We use the connected induced-subgraph method of Patel and Regts, proving the truncation directly for these weights. No complex zero-free region enters. [Patel and Regts, Theorem 2.1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00453-018-0511-9)

For S⊆[m] put P_S(z)=∑_{A⊆S}w(A)z^{|A|} and work over the rationals modulo z^{D+1}. Define L_S=log P_S and M_S=∑_{T⊆S}(−1)^{|S|−|T|}L_T. If S is disconnected then M_S=0: each L_T splits additively across components of S, and the alternating sum over a different component kills each contribution. Furthermore [z^j]M_S=0 for j<|S|. Expand the logarithm in powers of P_T−1. In degree j the selected nonempty sets have total size j, so alternating over T kills every term unless their union covers S. Möbius inversion gives log P as the sum of M_S over connected nonempty S of size at most D.

These S can be enumerated by their minimum vertex and a canonical depth-first tree walk of the induced connected subgraph, using vertex order for ties. Enumerating rooted walks of lengths 0,2,…,2D−2 and filtering by this canonical walk visits every required set, with at most poly(m,D)·max(2,Δ)^{2D} attempts. Equivalently use connected-set reverse search. For each retained S enumerate T⊆S and A⊆T sequentially to accumulate P_T. Each w(A) takes at most 8^{|A|} sign evaluations or one quadratic Gaussian elimination. Sum the Möbius contributions and compute coefficients from jP_j=∑_{i=1}^j i[z^i]log P · P_{j−i}, beginning at P_0=1.

For exact bit complexity one may work directly with the integer coefficients of z P_T′/P_T. For a truncated integer polynomial with constant one, polynomial division by P_T uses no nonunit scalar. The inverse coefficient at degree i can equivalently be written as a signed sum over ordered compositions of i. Since |w(A)|≤4^{|A|}, the P_T coefficients at degree j have magnitude at most (4m)^j, and every inverse, derivative or logarithmic-derivative coefficient through D has O(D log(2+m)+log(2+D)) bits up to an absolute factor. Summing over the streamed subsets and sets adds O(log(2+m)+D log(2+Δ)+D) bits. The final recurrence uses exact division by j over integers. Storing the current walk and a polynomial number of temporary integers suffices. For D=0 return the signed constant residue; its reduction modulo two is one. The sign (−1)^{m+Q(1_all)} restores the physical residue after summing coefficients.

The order cutoff from a single omission term cannot in general be replaced by a smaller uniform valuation bound. For any a take disjoint polar pairs on 3a omitted variables, with at most one unpaired coordinate, and choose centered quadratic phase equal to the sum of their products. The Gauss sum has absolute value 2^{ceil(3a/2)} and |w(A)|=2^{ceil(a/2)}. These pairs can be chosen with quadratic maximum degree one. This witnesses tightness of the termwise divisibility; cancellations among different sets may permit other algorithms.
