# Logarithmic modular neutralization for fully matched cubic phases

For every odd prime p a deterministic polynomial-time-in-p construction produces a path of O(log p) disjoint cubic triples with a distinguished logical port. Summing all other variables gives the same nonzero weight modulo p for either port value. The path uses only quadratic links between consecutive logical ports, optional quadratic terms inside triples, and linear terms. Its length is optimal up to an absolute factor even among arbitrary perfect-matching cubic gadgets with unrestricted quadratic interactions. Applying it independently to each spare coordinate in perfect-cubic-matching-count.md gives a deterministic nonadaptive reduction from exact #CircuitSAT with N input bits plus gates plus one to polynomially many exact perfect-matching cubic gap queries, each of O(N log(2+N)) variables and gates with quadratic maximum degree four.

## Two positive-word generators

Summing (−1)^{uab+εab+u} over the private a,b gives weights (4,−2) for ε=0 or (2,−4) for ε=1. A link to an existing suffix logical v contributes (−1)^{uv}, so a new front block multiplies its conditional column by T₀ or T₁, whose rows are respectively ((4,4),(−2,2)) and ((2,2),(−4,4)). Write g_i=T_i/4. Both have determinant one and entries in Z[1/2]. Start with the terminal block ε=0 giving vector (4,−2). We need a forward word over g₀,g₁ reaching the projective point [1:1] modulo p. The start equals g₀²[1:1], since g₀ first takes that point to [1:0] and then to [2:−1]. Thus the two points are in the same group orbit at every odd prime. Each T_i is invertible modulo p, so equal final entries are nonzero. Their modular value is computed with the physical matrices T_i and terminal vector.

The subgroup Γ generated by g₀,g₁ has Zariski closure SL₂ in characteristic zero. Indeed g₀g₁⁻¹=diag(2,1/2). Its powers are dense in the diagonal algebraic torus since a nonzero one-variable Laurent polynomial has finitely many roots. The Lie algebra of the closure contains H=diag(1,−1) and g₀ H g₀⁻¹ with off-diagonal entries −2 and −1/2 and zero diagonal. This latter matrix and its bracket with H span both off-diagonal lines. The three-dimensional closure inside the irreducible three-dimensional SL₂ is the whole group.

By Salehi Golsefidy–Varjú, Theorem 1, the Cayley graphs of the reduction images Γ_p, with fixed symmetric generators g₀,g₁ and their inverses, expand uniformly for all sufficiently large primes p. The theorem applies in GL₂(Z[1/2]) with perfect connected Zariski closure. It concerns the image groups, which already contain g₀⁻², so no assertion that the image is the whole finite SL₂ is needed. [Expansion in perfect groups](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00039-012-0190-7)

For any subset X of a finite group and any left multiplier g, |gX outside X|=|g⁻¹X outside X|, since intersections have equal size. Therefore the two positive generators contribute half the four labeled outgoing edge boundary. Their union of neighbors gains a constant fraction of |X| while |X| is at most half the group. The same applies to the two inverse generators. The forward ball from identity and backward ball to g₀⁻² consequently intersect after O(log |Γ_p|)=O(log p) steps in each direction. Multiplying out gives a positive word for the desired transport. Finitely many exceptional odd primes are covered with a fixed larger constant, since each generates a finite group and its positive semigroup contains its inverse elements. A uniform uncapped breadth-first search on the p+1 projective points returns a shortest transport word without knowing the expansion constants; it is polynomial in p, not in log p. Each step represents an additional front block.

## Optimal scale and exact recovery

For a gadget of m disjoint cubic triples covering every bit, write w₀,w₁ for the port-conditioned signed sums. Their difference is the gap after adding the port as a linear term. By the valuation proof in perfect-cubic-matching-count.md this nonzero integer has 2-adic valuation exactly m. Its absolute value is at most 2^{3m}. If w₀≡w₁ modulo an odd prime p, then p divides its odd quotient and p≤2^{2m}. Thus m≥(log₂ p)/2. This lower bound applies to arbitrary topology and arbitrary quadratic and affine drift within the stated gadget class.

For the #CircuitSAT reduction, prepare the sparse cubic G with k spare coordinates and known Z(G)=2^{b+e} #CircuitSAT. For each selected odd prime p attach the same neutral path to every spare. The new gap modulo p equals c_p^k Z(G) with nonzero c_p. Undo this factor and the known power of two, and reconstruct the nonnegative original count by the Chinese remainder theorem using a product exceeding 2^{n_in}, where n_in counts original primary bits. For example take the first n_in+2 odd primes. Their size is polynomial in N by the usual prime-count bounds. Sieve, search, construct all circuits and compute the CRT in deterministic polynomial bit time. Choices and instances are independent of the oracle answers. Each instance has O(N log(2+N)) variables and gates: old ports receive at most one additional quadratic chain link, internal logicals at most two, and each private helper at most one extra εab edge. The bit encoding may additionally cost O(log N) per gate identifier. The number of queries is O(N), the total size over all queries is larger, and the returned gap has O(N log(2+N)) bits.

This bound improves the *largest instance size* of the one-query signed radix construction in exchange for multiple queries. It does not turn a subexponential-in-output-size clock into a subexponential-in-N guarantee without checking the logarithmic factor. For example 2^{O(Q/√log Q)} at Q=O(N log N) only implies an exponent O(N√log N). It also concerns exact counting. It supplies no polynomial-time decision algorithm for unrestricted SAT.

The bounded checker cubic_modular_chain_check.py tests all positive words of lengths zero through three by raw phase enumeration, the directed projective search at all 53 odd primes through 251, and CRT on a signed test value. Its longest observed shortest word was length nine.
