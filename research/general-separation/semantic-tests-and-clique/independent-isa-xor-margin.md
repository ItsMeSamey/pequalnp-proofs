# Average-case prediction and normalized threshold margins for XOR of independent lookups

Use the layout of [the signed product bound](independent-isa-signed-weight.md) with r=log₂m independent banks and n=Θ(rm). Change the output to B=(1+Z)/2, where Z is the product of the r addressed payload signs. This is XOR or its complement, depending on r. It is one explicit language in P with O(n) shared gates; reject lengths outside the layout. Under uniform inputs each selected bit is unbiased independently across banks, so E[Z]=0 and E[BZ]=1/2. For any fixed b, the cited proof gives |E[AZ]|≤δ for every binary formula or unrestricted-successor acyclic query program of size W≤rm(log m)^b, with δ=m^{−(1/3−o(1))r} for formulas and m^{−(1/4−o(1))r} for programs.

Each Boolean atom A has prediction success Pr[A=B]=1/2+E[AZ]≤1/2+δ. A randomized predictor choosing an atom independently of the test input has the same bound by averaging, provided every choice respects W. This does not cover arbitrary postprocessing of many atom outputs. For a real-valued G=c₀+sum_h c_h A_h, let Λ=sum_h |c_h| exclude the free constant. If E|G−B|≤1/2−γ, then E[GZ]≥γ, hence Λ≥γ/δ. This permits any signed coefficients and any constant γ>0 for average absolute error; it is distinct from the accepting-input support lower bound for AND.

For a threshold score with Z(x)G(x)>0 everywhere, the normalized margin ρ=min_x Z(x)G(x)/Λ satisfies ρ≤δ by averaging and the same inequality. A classifier with integer bottom weights bounded in magnitude by n^b' for fixed b', with an arbitrary real threshold, can move the threshold to a half-integer without changing classifications on its integer-valued sum. This gives raw margin ≥1/2. Consequently it needs at least (2δn^b')^(−1) terms, exp(Ω((log n)^2)). In particular a majority of near-linear atom outputs requires this many terms. Arbitrarily small real weights do not create the same conclusion about count: the integer step invokes a genuine minimum margin after fixing the scale.

As a control, the classical odd-max-bit decision list has a sparse threshold representation on the raw literals, with exponentially small best normalized margin in that dictionary. For a direct degree-one illustration, assign +1 at the all-zero point and sign (−1)^i when i is the largest index set. In a correct threshold score b+sum w_i x_i of raw margin γ>0, b≥γ. Let u and l be the max and min possible partial scores on the earlier variables. At an odd step w_i≤−γ−u, and at an even step after the first w_i≥γ−l. From the first odd step onwards u+γ and γ−l are both at least 2γ. In each following pair of alternating updates their sum grows by a factor of at least two. Their sum equals the partial sum of |w_i| plus 2γ, proving exponential required weight relative to the raw margin. Conversely exponentially increasing signed weights do realize the decision list with only n literal atoms. As an ordinary formula the function has O(n) leaves by overwriting the earlier answer with a constant whenever the next input is one. This example concerns a fixed display of features, not the independently addressed target's optimal representation. [Servedio, Tan and Thaler on odd-max-bit weights and their predecessors](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v23/servedio12.html)

The proof uses a score that is linearly correlated with the target sign and an explicit quantitative margin or approximation guarantee. It does not give a lower bound for unrestricted real-threshold sparsity or arbitrary Boolean circuit computation.
