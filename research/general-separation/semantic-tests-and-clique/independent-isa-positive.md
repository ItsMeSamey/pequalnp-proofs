# Full independent-address product lower bounds for sound covers

For an integer t≥4 put m=2^t, P=2^floor(log₂(m/t)) and r=t. Each of r banks contains a log₂ P-bit primary index, P pointer words of t bits and m payload bits. All input bits are independent coordinates. Bank a outputs the payload bit at the address in its indexed pointer word; F is the AND of the r outputs. Its input length n_t=r(log₂P+Pt+m)=Θ(rm) increases strictly with t. Define one uniform language to reject every other length and use this layout at length n_t. Length identification and evaluation are polynomial time. A circuit for each bank multiplexes the P pointer words one bit-position at a time, then multiplexes the m payload bits. This gives O(rm) bounded-fan-in Boolean gates for the full product. The bounds below hold even allowing r≥1 other than t in a single slice.

A sound atom is Boolean and can accept only where F=1. A sound cover must accept every positive input with at least one atom. The atoms may be binary trees of any two-input Boolean gates with at most W leaves, or acyclic existential query programs with at most W single-bit query nodes, arbitrary successor sets, and arbitrary interleaving of banks. In the latter model arcs are free. Put D=1+W/(rP). There is an absolute C such that every sound atom under the uniform distribution accepts with probability at most

* (CrD/m)^r for a formula;
* (CrD/sqrt(m))^r for a query program.

No claim is needed when the right hand side exceeds one. Since F has acceptance probability 2^(−r), these give corresponding lower bounds (m/(2CrD))^r and (sqrt(m)/(2CrD))^r on the cover size. They apply to exact nonnegative real sums because every nonzero positively weighted summand is sound and the summands cover every positive input. They also apply to sound covers with overlaps.

## Conditional support lemma

Fix the primary indices, the nonselected pointer words, and the per-bank counts l_a of occurrences of bits of the selected word in the given atom. Relabel banks in decreasing order of l_a. The selected words are still independent uniform addresses in [m]; their m-bit payloads are independent uniform vectors. For the given payloads let B be the subset of selected address tuples on which the atom accepts.

At depth i in a prefix tree for B, consider any prefix of i−1 pointer values. The atom section on the remaining q=r−i+1 words is one of a common family of residual masks. This family permits all assignments to the variables outside those selected suffix words, including all the prefix words and payload bits. It therefore contains every section for every prefix without charging the number of prefixes. Write l=sum_(a≥i) l_a, so l≤r l_i. A fixed formula's skeleton on l surviving leaves has only 2^{O(l+1)} residuals: suppress constant-only branches and record one of four unary maps per suppressed path. A fixed query program's residuals number at most 2^{O((l+1)^2)}: on the l named live query nodes, record the first possible live nodes or terminal at the start and after either answer at each node, contracting paths of pinned nodes. This is exact for acyclic existential acceptance. Both constants are absolute, and l=0 allows both Boolean constants.

For each nonempty mask U in such a suffix dictionary, compatibility with the actual payloads requires that all addresses in every coordinate projection of U have payload bit one in that bank. This statement uses global soundness and holds for every realized section. The probability that a fixed mask is compatible is 2^(−sum_a |projection_a U|). In particular it is at most 2^(−|projection_i U|). Let V_i be the largest number of i-th coordinate values in a compatible nonempty dictionary mask, or zero if there is none. For a dictionary of at most 2^b masks, the union bound gives Pr[V_i≥z]≤min(1,2^(b−z)), and always V_i≤m. Thus its L^r norm is at most min(m,b+C₁r) for an absolute C₁: the positive part above b has exponential tail and r-th root moment O(r). All possible prefixes in the actual B have at most V_i continuations at level i.

Hence |B|≤product_i V_i. Hölder bounds its expectation by the product of their L^r norms without assuming independence of V_i. For a formula each such norm is at most C₂r(l_i+1). For a query program it is at most min(m,C₃r²(l_i+1)²)≤C₄r sqrt(m)(l_i+1). Divide the expected |B| by m^r to get the conditional acceptance probability. The bound is symmetric in the l_a after multiplying, so sorting did not change that product. Before fixing the independent primary indices, l_a is a function only of bank a's chosen index and the fixed atom description. If W_a denotes its total occurrences querying pointer words in that bank, E[l_a]=W_a/P and sum W_a≤W. Independence and arithmetic–geometric mean give E[product(l_a+1)]=product(1+W_a/P)≤D^r, proving the stated bounds.

## Leading exponent and matching constructions

For r=t=log₂m and W between C₅rm and rm(log m)^b for any fixed b≥0 (when this interval is nonempty), the formula lower bound is m^{(1−o(1))r} and the program bound m^{(1/2−o(1))r}. Every fixed near-linear density n_t(loglog(n_t+16))^a with a>0, including a fixed positive multiplicative constant, eventually fits the regime. In particular each count is exp(Ω((log n_t)^2)) on this explicit sequence. This includes ordinary nonnegative real weights of arbitrary magnitude.

For matching upper exponents, one bank has a disjoint cover by P formulas of O(m) leaves, one for each primary row and its selected-payload multiplexer. A bank also has a disjoint cover by O(max(1,P/sqrt(m))) unambiguous query programs of O(m) nodes, each covering a group of Θ(sqrt(m)) primary rows. Decode the row and first half of its pointer, choose a matching shared payload node, check that node is one, then nondeterministically choose and verify the row-specific low pointer suffix and retest the primary index. At most the actual row and payload can pass. Full details and the control omitting the final retest are in the [single-copy tradeoff](isa-sparse-range.md). Multiply the per-bank covers into r-fold conjunctions. Each formula costs O(rm) leaves and each sequentially chained unambiguous program O(rm) nodes. The sums remain disjoint and assign coefficient one to every term. Thus for W≥C₅rm, choosing C₅ sufficiently large, their counts are at most m^{(1+o(1))r} and m^{(1/2+o(1))r}. This matching is in the leading exponent.

The individual residual counts follow the classical indirect-storage subfunction method of [Beame, Grosshans, McKenzie and Segoufin](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.01932). The argument above additionally uses one-sided support, nested residual dictionaries and independent full payloads. It proves neither arbitrary signed sparsity nor P≠NP.
