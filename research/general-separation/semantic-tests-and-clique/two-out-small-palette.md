# A one-sided subexponential decision bound for sparse directed Hamiltonicity

On an N-vertex digraph with O(N) arcs, including every maximum-outdegree-two instance, there is a one-sided randomized polynomial-space Hamiltonian-cycle decision algorithm taking

    poly(N) · 2^{O(N / √log N)}

bit operations. It always rejects a no instance and accepts a yes instance with probability at least 2/3. This uses the deterministic fixed-density exact #3SAT theorem in the revised October 25, 2026 preprint. That theorem gives 2^{O(V/√log V)} times a polynomial for a fixed constant bound on the ratio of the number of clauses to V variables. Its arbitrary-density version has an extra log log V in the numerator. [Theorem statement](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/main_full_version.tex)

Let m=O(N) be the number of arcs after duplicate removal. A cycle cover is a permutation of successors using allowed arcs. Use a Boolean variable per arc and impose exactly one selected outgoing and incoming arc at every vertex. Even for unbounded indegree the constraints have a linear-size fan-in-two circuit. For each vertex and each of its two incidence lists, scan the list with flags for “seen at least one” and “seen at least two”, using constantly many gates per incident arc. Introduce one Boolean color per vertex, pin color zero at a fixed root, and require equality on each selected arc. A cover with c cycles has exactly 2^{c−1} color extensions. For fixed integer weights w_e and total s, the parity of the number of valid colored covers of weight s therefore equals the parity of the number of Hamiltonian tours of weight s. Here a tour is a successor permutation, independent of cyclic starting point. This uses the consistent-cut cancellation of Cut&Count. [Cygan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.0534)

Take K to be the largest power of two at most max(2,⌊log₂ N⌋). Give each arc the weight 2^j for j uniform independently in {0,...,K−1}. Sampling uses exactly log₂ K random bits per arc. For any nonempty family of tours, the chance of a unique minimum is at least (1−1/K)^m. Start with each vector of ranks in {1,...,K−1}^m and take a canonical minimum-weight tour H. Lower the rank by one exactly on arcs of H. For any distinct tour T the new difference between its weight and that of H is the old difference plus the sum of the positive decrements on H∖T. That set is nonempty because the two tours have equal numbers of arcs. Hence H is the unique new minimum. Adding one to the resulting ranks on its arcs recovers the initial vector. This proves the probability bound by injection even though the numerical decrements vary. The isolation lemma originates with Mulmuley, Vazirani and Vazirani. Faber and Harris state this improving bound for arbitrary increasing weights and credit the lowering argument to Ta-Shma. [Mulmuley, Vazirani and Vazirani](https://doi.org/10.1145/28395.383347) · [Faber and Harris, Propositions 2.1–2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.07035#page=4)

For one sampled assignment, evaluate the exact colored-cover count separately at each integer weight 0≤s≤N·2^{K−1}=O(N²) and accept if any count is odd. A selected arc contributes one Boolean signal in its fixed weight column j. Use a carry-save circuit. Among three signals of equal column, compute sum and carry with a constant number of binary gates, replace the three by the sum in that column and the carry one column higher. This reduces the number of signals by one. The sum of the formal column weights of all signal slots is preserved, since at column j three slots have the same total weight as one slot there and one at j+1. It starts at most m·2^{K−1}, so every occupied column is at most log₂ m+K (the m=0 case is immediate). At most m such compressors leave two rows of O(K+log N) bits. Add these rows, compare to s, and conjoin the degree, color and root tests. The entire circuit has O(N) gates, including the arithmetic, and at most m+N primary bits.

Translate this bounded-fan-in circuit to 3-CNF using one bit per gate, at most four clauses enforcing its truth table, and a unit output clause. All intermediate values are forced, so this preserves the count exactly. The formula has V=O(N) variables and M≤D V clauses for an absolute constant D. The fixed-density theorem gives 2^{O(N/√log N)} per total. The sampled ranks are hardwired into the formula and each chosen weight has a single nonzero binary digit.

A yes input with an isolated minimum has an odd count at that weight. A no input has all even counts for every weighting. Repeating independently O((1−1/K)^{−m}) times gives success at least 2/3 on any yes input. This factor is 2^{O(N/log N)}. The polynomial number of totals and the repetitions preserve the stated exponent. Queries run sequentially in polynomial space. This includes the [pure-four NP-complete family](four-switch-hardness.md). The exact counter is deterministic. Isolation makes this decision algorithm randomized.

**Why ordinary fixed-width timestamps do not suffice.** Storing an O(log N)-bit rank at each vertex with successor-increment constraints uses O(N log N) variables and gates when outdegree is bounded. The fixed-density theorem then gives only 2^{O(N√log N)}. An alternative deterministic encoding really does use only O(N) variables: store a successor selector per vertex and the orbit label every L=⌈C log₂ N⌉ steps starting at a known root, with final return at N. For each segment, enumerate the at most N·2^L walks of its prescribed length, including the shorter last segment. Whenever its starting checkpoint and all its edge selectors match the walk, force its next checkpoint and forbid any premature root return. For nonpower-of-two N forbid invalid checkpoint codes. The exact forced orbit accepts precisely when its first return is at N. It has at most N/C+O(log N) checkpoint bits and polynomially many clauses for fixed C. Each clause can have width O(log N). The October result for fixed clause width does not give the same bound on this encoding.

The [finite checker](isolation_check.py) compares direct tour counts with colored-cover parity at all weights. It passed 810 parity vectors on general two-out and pure-four instances, including zero-weight tests, and verified 7,618 power-of-two isolation images, 7,618 ordinary-weight images and 9,331 exhaustive compressor cases.
