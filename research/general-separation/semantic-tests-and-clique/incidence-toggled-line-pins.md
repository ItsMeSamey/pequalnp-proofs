# Induced-even decision on a bounded-rank perturbation of a line graph

Fix the number h of required vertices and a dimension s. Supply a simple loopless base graph B, a bijection from physical sites to its edges, binary s-vectors ψ(v) at the vertices, and an alternating binary s-by-s matrix J. For e=uv write b_e=ψ(u)+ψ(v). Require the physical adjacency to equal line-graph adjacency plus the matrix with entries b_eᵀJb_f. These data can be verified directly. The algorithm below deterministically finds a physical induced-even support or rejects, in polynomial time for fixed h,s.

Let F be the pinned edges and W their endpoints. Guess w in the binary s-space. For chosen edges D put d(v)=deg_D(v) modulo two. If w=Σ_{e∈D}b_e=Σ_v ψ(v)d(v), physical evenness at each selected uv is exactly equality of λ(v)=d(v)+ψ(v)ᵀJw at its two endpoints. This follows from the ordinary line degree deg_D(u)+deg_D(v)−2 and zero self-toggle. Guess λ on W with equality across each F edge. Partition W into monochromatic nonempty classes.

Seek vertex-disjoint trees in B−F, one per class containing exactly its named sites, plus at most s unnamed trees. Give each tree a constant color α. Write β(v)=α+ψ(v)ᵀJw and ν_α(v)=(β(v),ψ(v)β(v)) in the binary space of dimension s+1. Require that on each tree its first-coordinate sum equals the sum of forced degrees over its names modulo two. Across all trees require that the remaining s coordinates sum to w. Solve by leaf elimination for tree-edge sets with degree parities β(v)+deg_F(v); the first test permits this. Adjoin F. The resulting degree parity equals β on the trees and zero outside, and the second test gives the guessed w. All selected edges join equal λ. Return the physical names of these edges.

For completeness start with any actual solution and take the components of B−F on each λ-color. Since D−F has no edges between these components, each one meets its forest consistency test and together they give w. Retain all named components. Discard dependent zero-sum subcollections of the remaining components' s-vector contributions until at most s remain, all with nonzero contribution. Replace each retained component by a spanning tree. If it has more than s+1 unnamed leaves, delete a nonempty subset of these leaves whose ν-vectors sum to zero. Tree connectivity, all names and the total vector survive. An unnamed retained tree cannot vanish because its contribution is nonzero. Iteration leaves at most s+1 unnamed leaves per tree. Suppress unnamed degree-two vertices. The total number of named sites, retained leaves and branch vertices is O(h+hs+s²+1).

Enumerate skeletons, colors and injective skeleton placements in B−F, preserving the names. For every base edge uv form the concatenation of ν₀(u)+ν₀(v) and ν₁(u)+ν₁(v), using ordinary integer representatives of bits and unoriented addition modulo four in each coordinate. Guess each skeleton path's label sum. In the α-coordinates of a path from a to b this is ν_α(a)+ν_α(b)+2Σν_α(v) over internal vertices, modulo four. After subtracting both endpoint labels, residues zero or two recover the binary internal sum; odd residues are invalid. Adding each skeleton vertex exactly once supplies all certificate tests.

Use the [disjoint endpoint construction](line-graph-even-pins.md): handle length-one and length-two paths directly, guess distinct first and last internal sites on longer paths, delete the skeleton and length-two interiors, and subtract the known end-edge labels. Liu and Yoo's Theorem 1.3 applies to the remaining disjoint paths on this unoriented abelian group. Forbid the other three residues in every Z/4 coordinate to prescribe the desired sum. All path and coordinate counts are fixed for fixed h,s. Lift the found paths and perform the incidence reconstruction above. [Liu and Yoo, Theorem 1.3](https://people.tamu.edu/~yyoo/stoc25.pdf)

This class includes graphs with claws and unbounded overall adjacency rank. In a sufficiently long base path assign four nonincident edges b-labels (1,0),(0,1),(0,1),(0,1), leaving the other labels zero. Every such assignment comes from vertex differences by propagation along the path. With the standard two-dimensional alternating matrix the four sites induce a claw. The rest can contain an arbitrarily long path, whose binary adjacency rank grows without bound.

The parameter cannot be fixed on all graphs. Equal b-labels induce an ordinary line graph, hence a claw-free graph. In the t-th lexicographic power of the four-vertex claw, any claw-free subset projects to a claw-free set of at most three outer sites and has claw-free fibers in the previous power. Thus it contains at most 3^t of the 4^t vertices. Every valid presentation must satisfy 2^s≥(4/3)^t. This bounds the presentation dimension and does not bound decision complexity. Recognition without a supplied presentation is not established.
