# Rare successful regular charts in highly connected physical hosts

Let m be odd and sufficiently large, t=⌊m/16⌋. Start from the complement of the m-cycle with three consecutive prescribed vertices. Its unique induced-even witness is the entire cycle. Indeed an even-size selection requires selected cycle degree one at every selected vertex, impossible at the middle pin. An odd-size selection requires selected cycle degree zero or two, and the selected pin edge propagates around the cycle. [Base family](complement-cycle-rooted-cut.md)

Color the three prescribed vertices by independent binary unit vectors and every other vertex zero. Replace each marked vertex by t independent false twins of its color; leave all m−3 neutral sites single. Realize the resulting three-readout input with the [fixed 35-state reservoir](one-missing-reservoir-universality.md), including its color relabeling. Replace each of the six reservoir vertices by t independent false twins after attachment. The three named interface pins stay single. Odd-multiplicity projection preserves the reservoir table: a selected member of an odd class sees exactly the parity of the collapsed neighbors. Conversely taking one representative per selected class lifts. The same argument applies on the input side before its signed edge update. Hence every escape in the original payload chart selects all m−3 neutral sites and has odd multiplicity in each marked class. Escapes exist. There are m−3+3t free payload coordinates and m+9t physical vertices in the host.

The host has connectivity at least t. After fewer than t deletions every enlarged class survives. The surviving neutral vertices induce the complement of an induced subgraph of a path: on at least five vertices it is connected, since a disconnection would require the path to contain a complete bipartite graph on five or more vertices. Each marked copy has neutral neighbors except for at most two missing cycle neighbors, so attaches to this connected part. The enlarged inner classes remain connected through their connected six-class template; every surviving named pin has inner neighbors. At least one marked class has an inner neighbor because its virtual input column V is nonzero and the inner incidence has full rank. The link still uses the independent pin-to-port matching after payload deletion. In the linked matrix H all neutral cross incidences are the original symmetric ones, as V and R vanish on each neutral site. All payload sites therefore lie in one strongly connected component.

Call a regular chart T successful if a clamp word of weight at most one escapes. On every neutral site the original coordinates of any escaping physical word are (1,0). Its clamp bit there is therefore one precisely when that site is outside T. Thus there are at most (m−2)·2^{3t} successful masks, even counting singular masks. For a lower bound on regular masks take an independent set of size b=⌊(m−3)/2⌋ in the neutral cycle path. Every even subset induces a neutral clique in the complement. Its principal H block I+11ᵀ squares to I in characteristic two, so is regular; the empty set is regular too. The number of regular masks is at least 2^{b−1}. Uniformly sampling a regular mask has success probability at most p=(m−2)2^{3t−b+1}=2^{-Ω(m)}. For L samples with uniform marginals the chance of ever seeing a successful chart is at most Lp even without independence. With independent sampling the miss probability is at least (1−p)^L when p≤1. This concerns precisely zero/singleton-clamp tests under uniform regular sampling. The graph family itself is explicitly decidable.

## Gaussian conflict search over singular and regular orientations

For each real site the two possible equations are z_i=0 and (a+Hz)_i=0. A word is legal exactly when some choice of one equation per site admits it. Adjoin the permanent target Cz=r. Fix any center orientation and a maximum flip count k. At each node solve the displayed target together with its current site equations. A solution is an actual escape. Otherwise Gaussian elimination supplies an inconsistent row combination. Every successful descendant must flip some as-yet-unflipped site used by this combination. If none is eligible or the budget is exhausted, prune. Otherwise branch on each eligible site, committing its opposite and decreasing the budget. Following any successful final orientation within distance k is always possible by choosing a discrepancy in the conflicting rows. This completeness proof uses neither regularity nor pointwise independence of any intermediate clamps. With at most d eligible sites per encountered conflict, the time bound is poly(n,k) max(1,d)^k and a depth-first traversal uses polynomial space. A conflict may be reduced to inclusion-minimality by deletion tests before branching.

On the same physical family the initial zero-clamp center has minimum possible conflict width exactly t. The linear target alone says the parity in each of the three marked classes is one, because their three port directions are independent and their sum is r. Fewer than t retained site equations z_i=0 leave at least one free representative in each class. Taking one in each solves the target together with these equations. Keeping all t equations in any one class already contradicts its required parity. This is a linear certificate lower bound at the actual first node, independent of Gaussian pivot choices. Since t is linear in the total host size, the branching bound does not imply a uniform sublinear conflict width even in this single physical component.
