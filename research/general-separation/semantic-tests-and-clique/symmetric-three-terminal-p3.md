# Three required vertices in a subcubic induced even graph

Given a simple undirected graph of maximum degree three and three distinct required vertices, a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm finds an induced subgraph containing all three in which every vertex has even degree, or correctly rejects. This is the special case of three unit readouts set to one. An induced even subgraph of a subcubic graph consists of isolated vertices and mutually nonadjacent chordless cycles, since every positive degree is two. Specifying three vertices of such a subgraph differs from requiring all terminals on one chordless cycle. The latter distinction matters for interpreting known hardness results for two terminals on a prescribed cycle. [Lévêque et al., Detecting induced subgraphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.0971)

If the terminals induce no edges or a triangle, their three vertices suffice. If they induce a path u-v-w, any solution has u,v,w on the same chordless cycle, as both required incident edges belong to it. Delete v and all its neighbors other than u,w. A shortest remaining u-w path closes with v to give a chordless cycle. Existence of such a path is necessary by the same cycle argument.

It remains to consider one induced terminal edge uv and the third terminal w isolated from both. Delete edge uv to form G′ and remove all of N[w] in G′. If the remaining graph contains a u-v path, take a shortest such path and restore uv. This gives a chordless cycle avoiding all neighbors of w, so add w as an isolated vertex. Otherwise let Cᵤ,Cᵥ be the distinct remaining components containing u,v.

In this latter case any feasible support must have w on the particular cycle containing uv. A distinct component containing w would have no edges to that cycle, which would supply the u-v path just ruled out. Removing uv from a chordless cycle containing w gives an induced path in G′, with consecutive interior vertices a,w,b and a,b distinct neighbors of w. It has no other neighbor of w, and a,b are nonadjacent. Before a the path lies in Cᵤ, and after b it lies in Cᵥ. Its u-to-a prefix avoids every neighbor of b, and its b-to-v suffix avoids every neighbor of a.

Conversely enumerate ordered, nonadjacent neighbors a,b of w. Reject a pair if u is adjacent to b or v to a. Find a shortest u-to-a path restricted to Cᵤ∪{a} with all neighbors of b removed, and a shortest b-to-v path in Cᵥ∪{b} removing all neighbors of a. Use G′ throughout. If both exist, join them with a-w-b and restore uv. Each shortest path is induced; the two component interiors have no edge between them in G′. Avoidance excludes chords to the other distinguished neighbor, and no interior belongs to N[w]. Thus the resulting cycle is chordless. The characterization gives a witness by ordinary breadth-first searches. It does not require solving the unrestricted prescribed-cycle problem.

## A structural bound and an equivalence for arbitrary readouts

For any graph and any r-row binary readout L, an inclusion-minimal all-even witness support S at a fixed target has adjacency nullity at most r over GF(2). In fact L restricted to ker A[S] is injective: if z≠0 is in that kernel with L_S z=0, delete the vertices on which z is one. Because A[S]1=0, every retained vertex still has even degree and the target is unchanged. This contradicts minimality. Starting with any actual witness, the deletion step can be repeated by elimination until injectivity holds; the resulting support need not be inclusion-minimal. The alternating adjacency has even rank, so its nullity has the parity of |S|. This bound alone does not find an initial support.

The general all-even three-readout problem is polynomial-time witness-equivalent to two readouts with arbitrary mixed prescriptions on an undirected graph. In the mixed-to-even direction add a new vertex adjacent exactly to the prescribed-odd vertices and require it as a third unit readout. On its inclusion, evenness at the old selected vertices expresses exactly the original prescriptions. The new vertex sees an even number of selected odd vertices by the handshake identity for every original witness. Removing or adding this vertex gives the witness conversion.

In the other direction handle zero target with the empty support, linear inconsistency by rejection, and dependent row rank at most two by the [two-row algorithm](symmetric-two-readout-twist.md). Otherwise change basis on the three readouts, taking the first two requested values to 1. Call those rows a,b. Replace the adjacency by A+abᵀ+baᵀ, prescribe c=a+b, and keep the first and third readouts. Handshake now forces b·x=a·x=1. At these two values the added adjacency term acts on x as c, ensuring equivalence of the updated local equations to all-evenness in the original graph. This conversion may create high degrees and dense readouts. The subcubic terminal algorithm does not settle arbitrary three-row instances.
