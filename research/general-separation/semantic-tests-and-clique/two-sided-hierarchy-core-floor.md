# A quantitative NO-core consequence for a two-sided unary hierarchy choice

This is a deduction from the unary nondeterministic time hierarchy, the Jahanjou–Miles–Viola local reduction, and the October 25 manuscript's stated deterministic fixed-density width-three SAT algorithm. It uses a newly chosen unary language; the manuscript's original hierarchy statement does not by itself say that the complement of its H is hard. The underlying exact counting theorem has not been independently re-proved here.

## Hardness on both polarities

Fix the stated unary H in NTIME(2^m) outside NTIME(2^(m/2)). Define a unary J. On even lengths n=2m, put 1^n in J exactly when 1^m is in H. For odd n=2m+1, choose an ordinary nondeterministic multitape machine M_i using i equal to the exponent of 2 in m+1. Every index occurs at arbitrarily large odd lengths. Accept precisely when M_i accepts its actual input 1^n in at most 2^floor(n/3) simulated steps. Use a length-ordered enumeration with malformed descriptions defaulting to rejection. Reject nonunary strings.

J belongs to NTIME(2^n). In the odd case a direct universal simulation records the visited tapes and heads, guesses one encoded transition at each step and checks it. At most O(poly(n)(T+n)^2) time suffices for T simulated steps, fitting 2^n with slack at T=2^floor(n/3). Even inputs simulate the fixed machine for H. If J were decidable in NTIME(2^(n/4)), mapping 1^m to 1^(2m) would put H in its excluded time class. If the complement of J were in NTIME(2^(n/4)), choose its nondeterministic machine M_i. At large odd lengths of index i its fixed time bound fits the cutoff. There J's membership equals the *acceptance* of M_i, which is supposed to decide its complement. Thus neither polarity belongs to NTIME(2^(n/4)). This construction does not use an oracle or a common exponent for polynomial-time machines.

## Guessing a small core and checking it

Apply JMV to the fixed J machine on 1^n as in the October argument. There are q=n+O(log n) bits per clause index. Any uniform polynomial-time clause decoder with the same correctness and q=poly(n) would suffice for the NO-core conclusion. A subcollection of K clauses mentions at most 3K variables. Guess their indices, allowing repetitions; decode and consistently rename equal variable labels, then pad with unused variables to exactly V=3K. There are K clauses, within the density-five limit. The October bound at width three and density five is V^b 2^(A V/sqrt(log₂ V)) for fixed A,b. Set K=floor(ηn floor(sqrt(log₂ n))) with a sufficiently small positive rational η<1/(12A). Equivalently one may compute the inner floor with an integer square root of the binary-logarithm floor. Guessing, renaming and padding cost polynomial time in n. The deterministic solver costs 2^((3Aη+o(1))n), within 2^(n/4).

On a YES length every sampled subformula is satisfiable. On a NO length possessing a core of at most K there is a guess for which the solver reports UNSAT. Therefore, if all sufficiently large NO lengths had such a core, a nondeterministic complement decider would contradict the hierarchy. Nonunary complement inputs are accepted immediately, and bounded exceptional lengths can be hardwired. It follows that infinitely many NO lengths have minimum unsatisfiable core greater than ηn floor(sqrt(log₂ n)) up to integer rounding. This holds for the unmodified correct reduction applied to this chosen J, regardless of the geometry of its output clauses. No NP/coNP assumption is used. Ordinary brute-force SAT checking would only give the linear-in-n scale in this calculation.

The ordinary witness-circuit argument also transfers to the YES polarity of J, with its constants chosen below the 2^(n/4) bound. It yields infinitely many YES lengths at which every satisfying assignment needs Ω(n sqrt(log n)) gates. The YES and NO conclusions concern different sets of lengths.

## Simultaneously excluding another certificate type

For any fixed sound NO-verifier V whose time on (1^n,π) is O((n+|π|+2)^c), choose positive rational δ_V<1/(8c+8). A second nondeterministic branch guesses π of length at most 2^floor(δ_V n) and runs V. This branch is too fast to change the asymptotic hierarchy bound and cannot accept on a YES length. Joining it to the core branch shows that infinitely many of the *same NO lengths* have neither a small arbitrary core nor such a certificate. This applies to finitely many fixed sound verifiers simultaneously via a finite union. Enumerating all sound verifiers only as an external countable set and choosing successive increasing NO lengths against its initial segments gives a single subsequence eventually excluding each of them at its own δ_V. This is not an effective procedure for identifying sound verifiers or finding those NO lengths.

The certificate bound is a fixed root power in the length of the expanded formula. The core bound is measured in the logarithmic indexing parameter n. Neither is a superpolynomial lower bound for ordinary explicit SAT. A stronger exponential minimum-core conclusion for the original hierarchy formula requires the additional NP=coNP hypothesis, or new information about that particular decoder. No standard P versus NP separation follows.
