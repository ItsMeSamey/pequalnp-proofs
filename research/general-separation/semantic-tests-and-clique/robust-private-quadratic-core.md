# A finite core for arbitrary affine perturbations of quadratic circuit wires

Given r binary quadratic polars on a p-bit private input space V, decide whether every tuple of affine perturbations maps V onto F₂^r. There is a deterministic 2^{O(r³)}poly(p,r) algorithm. A negative answer gives a specific perturbation and unattained target. A positive answer gives one explicit subspace W of dimension O(r²) such that every target occurs on every coset of W under every affine perturbation. Failure of the universal test may come from affine terms never reached by a particular outside circuit.

## Removing high-rank selectors and factoring the radical

Use the rank closure from conditional-quadratic-image.md. For D≤F₂^r and t=dim span{B(s):s∈D}, require rank B(s)≥2(r+t) for all s outside D. Compute the least such D starting with zero and spanning in selectors below the current threshold until stable, retaining independent selectors actually added at each stage. The conditional-image theorem makes the full image the inverse image of its projection to D, for every perturbation. In brief, an attainable low fiber has at least 2^{p−d−t} points by the minimum weight of its nonzero degree-(d+t) indicator, where d=dim D. The sum of normalized high Gauss magnitudes is at most (2^r−2^d)2^{−r−t}<2^{-t}. Fourier inversion gives positive multiplicity in every completion. Every affine perturbation of the d projected outputs lifts to a perturbation of the r original outputs by a linear right inverse of the projection T.

The recorded basis polars each have rank strictly less than 4r, since the threshold never exceeded 4r. Let K be their common radical and U a linear complement in V. Its dimension a is the joint codimension and is less than 4rd for d>0 (a=0 when d=0). There are no low-polar terms within K or between K and U. After subtracting a binary-affine map, the low forms on V are their homogeneous representatives on U extended constantly along K.

Their universal robustness on V is equivalent to robustness on U. If robust on U, restricting any affine perturbation to U fills the projected codomain. If a perturbation on U misses a projected target, extend its resulting functions constantly along K. This is an available affine perturbation since the polars match. Lift the projected perturbation and choose any full extension of the omitted target. Enumerating the d(a+1) binary affine coefficients on U and all 2^a private inputs there takes 2^{O(r³)}poly(p,r) time, including the 2^r rank preprocessing. The zero-dimensional projected codomain is automatically robust.

## Constructing one simultaneously robust input subspace

Set R=2(r+t). If high selectors exist, all their polars have rank at least R. For each such B(s), take R independent linear functionals from its row space. Their span contributes fewer than 2^R nonzero functionals, giving fewer than 2^{r+R} total. Construct a subspace X of dimension at most r+R on which no listed functional vanishes identically. Choose successive vectors, each detecting at least half the undetected functionals. Uniform averaging gives existence, and fixing coordinate bits by conditional expectation finds the vector deterministically, by counting completions of one affine constraint per outstanding functional. Restriction to X is injective on each chosen R-dimensional row subspace. Hence B(s) as a pairing between X and V has rank at least R for every high s.

For each high s now select R independent functionals B(s)(x,·) with x∈X. Repeat the same construction to obtain Y of dimension at most r+R detecting all their nonzero combinations. The rectangular pairing B(s)(X,Y) then has rank ≥R for every high s. Its rank lower-bounds the restriction of B(s) to W=U+X+Y. If there are no high selectors use X=Y=0. This additional work takes 2^{O(r)}poly(p,r) and dim W≤a+2(r+R)≤4r²+10r. In the positive case the projected outputs on W remain robust because W contains U. Their restricted polar-image dimension is at most t, so threshold R guarantees the conditional-image conclusion on W. Since polars are invariant under translations, every coset of W has the property. Robustness on any one subspace is also sufficient globally. Thus W is an O(r²)-bit kernel for the universal question: in the negative case the bad projected perturbation constant along K already misses the target globally.

For a verified cut H(z,Q_z(u)) with fixed polars, a positive result allows SAT of H(z,y) on q−p+r input bits, where q was the original input count. A private witness for any accepted (z,y) can be recovered in 2^r poly(description size + p+r) time by prefix search on W: after fixing a prefix compute the desired fiber size for each next bit by summing its 2^r quadratic Gauss contributions. Positivity selects a continuing prefix. The cost of finding or verifying the original cut is separate. The recognition clock is advantageous only in appropriate parameter regimes, and arbitrary circuits need not furnish a useful cut.
