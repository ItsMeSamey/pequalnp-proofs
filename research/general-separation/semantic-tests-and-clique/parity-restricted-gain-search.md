# Parity queries on quaternary principal-support masks

For symmetric GF(4) matrices J,K on n indices, equality of their nonzero-principal-minor indicators restricted to an affine system T1_S=d on membership masks admits a randomized polynomial-space-in-the-table algorithm with time poly(input)·4^h and space poly(input)·2^h, where h=rank T over the binary field. This detects a difference in either direction. It also implements equality to the constant zero or one indicator on that fiber. A separate structural condition below detects a gain deterministically and directly.

## Evaluating the equality test on a parity fiber

Eliminate redundant equations, rejecting inconsistent right sides. Work over a characteristic-two finite extension F of GF(4) of polynomial size sufficiently large for the interpolation and error bounds below. In R=F[(Z/2)^h], let e_v e_w=e_{v+w}. Use fresh independent z_i in F and replace X_i by z_i e_{T_i}, where T_i is the column at index i. The e_d coefficient of the support polynomial now equals the weighted sum over precisely the requested masks. The even and odd block Pfaffian constructions in [the equality proof](quaternary-support-equality.md) are polynomial identities and still hold over R; only the fixed GF(4) coefficients are conjugated. For the constant-one support use the ring product Π_i(1+X_i). A genuine support difference on the fiber gives a nonzero multilinear polynomial of degree at most n in the scalar z_i. Uniform evaluation detects it with probability at least 3/4 for |F|≥4n, and independent repeats amplify. Deleting an index is a further zero restriction on its variable, with the same parity rows. Greedy extraction with amplified tests and exact determinant and parity verification gives an actual differing mask.

The ring has zero divisors, so taking a square root of a determinant or dividing by an arbitrary pivot is insufficient. Represent an element r by its 2^h coefficients. In this group algebra r²=aug(r)² times the identity basis element, with aug(r) the sum of its coefficients. Thus r is a unit exactly when aug(r)≠0, with inverse r/aug(r)². Naive multiplication costs O(4^h) scalar operations. To evaluate an alternating matrix M of order 2k over R, add tP with P the block-diagonal matrix pairing consecutive indices by unit edges. The augmented leading principal Pfaffian of size 2j is a monic degree-j scalar polynomial in t. At most k(k+1)/2 field values are ruled out by the union of their roots. Take at least k+1 other values, detected by trying consecutive-pair Pfaffian elimination with unit pivots. Indeed the pivot is the ratio of consecutive leading Pfaffians after prior eliminations. For each such t, ordinary skew elimination works over R because it uses only unit divisions. The Pfaffian of M+tP has degree at most k and is recovered at t=0 by scalar interpolation in R. A field of size greater than k(k+1)/2+k+1 and 4n suffices, with polynomial-time construction by enumerating monic polynomials of logarithmic degree. Enumeration, interpolation and error amplification retain the claimed bounds.

## When parity cells decide one-direction gains

Suppose both matrices are supplied or recognized as an invertible diagonal matrix plus a low-rank correction. For J=D+UVᵀ of correction rank r and any S, the matrix determinant lemma gives

det J[S] = det D[S] · det(I_r + Σ_{i∈S} d_i^{-1} v_i u_iᵀ),

where u_i,v_i are the column transposes of the corresponding rows of U,V. The first factor is always nonzero. The r² entries of the sum are binary-linear functions of the mask once each quaternary coordinate is written in a fixed binary basis. Form the analogous list for K, stack these lists and reduce their binary rank to h≤2(r_J²+r_K²). The diagonals and factorizations can be verified by multiplication; trying or finding good diagonals has its own cost. Every attainable syndrome has a binary mask preimage by Gaussian elimination. Enumerate the 2^h syndromes, evaluate the two small determinant tests, and recover a mask of the desired orientation whenever it exists. This requires polynomial space and poly(input)·2^h time. In the mixed two-readout application a gained mask yields a genuine binary witness via the kernel equations; a loss alone never suffices. For center feasibility a separate supplied diagonal completion of the original binary adjacency may give a better rank exponent. [Earlier diagonal completion algorithm](symmetric-two-readout-twist.md)

On the connected [coordinate-tree lower bound](gain-search-loss-families.md), view the two matrices as identity plus class-constant blocks on A,B,{u},{w}. This holds on the entire matrix, not just the face excluding the last two indices. With class-indicator matrix U each is I+U C Uᵀ for a fixed four-by-four quaternary C. Hence det J[S]=det(I+C diag(p_A,p_B,1_{u∈S},1_{w∈S})) and analogously for K. Sixteen parity cells decide all masks and can explicitly distinguish their orientations.

Diagonal rank is not uniformly small. For even n consider the n-vertex path with off-diagonal adjacency one along its edges. Its odd-row/even-column submatrix in path order is triangular with unit diagonal, irrespective of the full matrix's diagonal completion, so every such completion has rank at least n/2. A rank-one update can lower that bound by at most one. The parity-fiber test does not imply that a low-cost parity decision tree exists for arbitrary mixed readout instances, nor does the determinant formula automatically produce a low-rank diagonal.
