# A signed-ideal normal form for the two-edge cubic matching residue

Let an explicit instance consist of n binary variables, a list of directed implications s⇒t, and a quadratic phase Q over the binary field. We evaluate the sum of (−1)^Q over assignments obeying all implications, modulo three. There are linear-size polynomial-time transformations in both directions between this model and the following raw phase model, up to a computable nonzero scalar in the field of three elements. Each raw variable belongs to exactly one cubic monomial. The raw quadratic graph consists only of isolates, single edges and paths of two edges. The raw phase has no linear or constant terms. Size counts vertices and displayed arcs/monomials, hence dense quadratic phases are fully charged. On the return transformation no pair of distinct triple blocks is joined twice and every raw quadratic has endpoints in different triples.

The direction out of the cubic model expands each cubic sign as e⊗³−2δ⊗³, with e=(1,1), δ=(0,1). Each isolated edge equates its selectors at scale −1 modulo three. Each free port gives −(−1)^s. A two-edge path with middle selector s and ends t,u gives
[s⇒t][s⇒u](−1)^{s+t+u+tu}.
The eight-entry verification allows repeated selectors. Contract the equalities and, optionally, the strongly connected components of the implications. One obtains a poset-ideal sum after orienting the order appropriately. The exact signs are proved in [the signed partition note](quadratic-2cnf-affine-partition.md).

## A shared zero selector enforced by cancellation

Begin with one logical selector per original variable and two fresh selectors a,w. Realize each required implication s⇒t using a path with middle selector s and both ends t. Its factor is [s⇒t](−1)^{s+t}. Build one such implication w⇒a as well. For every quadratic phase term tu with t≠u, use another path with middle a and ends t,u. Its factor on a=0 is (−1)^{t+u+tu}. In particular the same selector a can serve every quadratic term.

A selector v taking part in p_v path ports is implemented by a chain of H_v=p_v+U_v−2 distinct triples with H_v−1 single-edge equalities. Choose U_v from the two consecutive integers starting at max(2,3−p_v). There are p_v+U_v free slots after fusing the chain, and H_v≥max(1,p_v), so its p_v path incidences can be assigned to distinct triples. Each equality contributes the scalar −1 and each of U_v free slots contributes −(−1)^{s_v}. Triple coefficients themselves are one modulo three. We can thus give every logical variable either desired linear phase coefficient by selecting the parity of U_v. The global scalar (−1)^{sum_v(H_v−1+U_v)} is known. Self-arcs can be dropped and repeated quadratic monomials canceled before construction. In every raw two-edge path its three ports are distinct; allocating incidences to distinct blocks of each logical chain prevents repeated interblock pairs.

Choose these linear corrections so that, after removing the linear terms of the ordinary implication factors and of the quadratic path factors on a=0, the phase on that slice is Q(s) without its constant, plus w. A constant of Q is recorded as an external sign. More precisely the coefficient of w, including its ordinary path factor, must be one on the entire cube; no other path uses w. At a=1 the constraint w⇒a accepts either w, so the two opposite contributions cancel for every setting of the original selectors, including settings violating another path. At a=0 only w=0 survives, and every quadratic path is unconstrained. The surviving sum gives exactly the required value times the known global sign and the recorded constant sign. There are O(n+#arcs+#terms) triples. The transformation is an identity of signed sums modulo three. It need not preserve individual raw witnesses.

## Full ternary-field two-spin systems

For any binary pair interaction with values in the field of three elements, each zero entry can be forbidden by one 2-clause. The remaining entries are ±1, and their signs can be extended arbitrarily across forbidden entries to a Boolean two-variable truth table. Every such table has binary algebraic degree at most two. Unary factors work the same way. Thus any explicit binary pair-interaction model over this field is a signed quadratic over 2-CNF support, even with input-dependent edge matrices, zero entries and cycles. The deterministic O*(1.38027757…^n) algorithm in [the paired-violation proof](paired-violation-gauss-count.md) applies with arbitrary additional binary affine equations on the n spins. Its bit time is polynomial in the displayed data times the exponential bound.

To also compile general 2-CNF plus a quadratic phase into the drift-free cubic model, without additional affine rows, introduce a selector y_i for each complemented literal needed alongside x_i. An arbitrary negated condition can instead be imposed with a locally canceled sign gadget, as follows. Introduce a fresh unconstrained selector h for each y_i and add the quadratic phase h(x_i+y_i+1). Summing it gives two on y_i=1+x_i and zero otherwise. Rewrite each clause as one implication on appropriately oriented literal selectors, introducing a complemented variable only as needed. Existing Q can remain on original x. The induced phase is still quadratic; the known extra scalar 2 per copy is invertible modulo three. For any additionally supplied affine row ℓ(x)=b, a fresh selector h' with phase h'(ℓ(x)+b) enforces it at scale two, with cost linear in the displayed row. The result then compiles by the preceding construction. These transformations give equivalence of explicit modular sums at linear displayed cost; they do not turn testing a vanishing modular count into a deterministic general SAT algorithm.
