# A small matching backdoor need not exist for the physical affine clauses

Fix the three named vertices of a simple graph to be selected. With n remaining input bits z, its physical constraints are n disjunctions, one per free site i, of z_i=0 or A_i z=c_i, plus three single parity equations B_p z=d_p. Here A is the free adjacency and B is the pin-to-free block. For clause groups S the rank deficiency is |S| minus the rank of the union of their linear normals. Its maximum is exactly three: the free groups include their distinct unit normals, and the full family of n+3 groups spans dimension n. A matched family has a choice of one independent normal from every group. Gaussian elimination then satisfies the chosen equations for any right-hand sides.

In ordinary CNF, the characterization cited as Theorem 1 in Szeider's paper gives a satisfying restriction on at most the maximum *syntactic* deficiency variables leaving a matched formula whenever the input is satisfiable. The following physical example rules out the direct affine version even if arbitrary linear measurements can be fixed and subsumption is allowed. It does not exclude other algorithms at small Rado deficiency. [Szeider, section 4–5](https://www.ac.tuwien.ac.at/files/pub/szeider-jcss-2004.pdf)

For all sufficiently large n there are graphs of vertex connectivity at least floor(n/20) with an explicit four-site witnessing support such that every affine restriction yielding a matched satisfiable residual has codimension Ω(n). In taking the residual, remove tautological clauses and allow deletion of duplicate or individually subsumed clauses; implicit combinations of multiple clauses are not a rule in this claim.

Use pins 0,1,2 with the single internal edge 0–1. Fix the incidence of one free site h to these pins to be 110 (adjacent to 0 and 1). Choose every free-to-free edge and all other free-to-pin incidences independently and uniformly. The three pins and h induce a triangle and an isolated vertex and are always a physical witness.

With probability 1−2^{-Ω(n)}, the normal spaces of every at most floor(n/16) clause groups form a direct sum, with dimension two for every free-site group and one for every pin. Indeed a nontrivial relation uses some nonempty set of groups and in a free group selects one of e_i, A_i, e_i+A_i, while a pin group contributes its row B_p. If all contributions are just unit vectors there is no relation. Otherwise check every coordinate j outside the involved free indices and h. The sums there are independent fair bits: for different such j all participating A_ij and B_pj are disjoint raw edge variables; at least one is present. For a choice on s groups the zero-sum probability is at most 2^{−n+s+1}. The union bound is at most the sum through n/16 of binom(n+3,s)3^s 2^{−n+s+1}, exponentially small by the binary entropy bound. The same test at one group excludes a zero normal or an internal coincidence of the two free normals.

Take a nonempty affine slice of codimension t with normal space U. A clause becoming tautological must contribute a nonzero member of U from its original group space: its forbidden values were jointly feasible before restriction, so some nonzero combination of their normals was fixed incompatibly by the slice. If the residual is satisfiable, each surviving non-tautological clause has a nonempty proper violation flat. A clause i individually subsumed by a surviving clause j has its violation flat contained in that of j. The normal space of the latter in the quotient by U is then a nonzero subspace of the normal space of i. There exist nonzero representatives v_i,v_j in the two original group spaces with the same nonzero quotient image. Charge the removed clause i the vector v_i+v_j in U. Equal violation flats are handled by keeping one representative. All subsumption deletions can be charged to final representatives by transitivity.

If at least t+1 clauses disappear, choose t+1 charges, one for each removed clause, using its own original nonzero vector for a tautology. At most 2t+2 old groups participate. When 2t+2≤floor(n/16), their direct-sum property makes the charges independent: each deleted group appears exactly once as a deleted group and never as a retained representative. This contradicts dim U=t. Therefore at most t clauses disappear. The residual has at least n+3−t groups, but only n−t independent normals are possible on the slice. It cannot be matched. The argument includes all choices of measurement right-hand sides with a satisfiable residual. A greedy propagation rule that fixes additional independent measurements changes the codimension being charged.

The same random graphs have the claimed connectivity with probability 1−2^{-Ω(n)}. A disconnection of the free graph after deleting w≤n/20 sites has a nonempty smaller component side of size x≤n/2 and an opposing union of at least (n−w)/2≥0.475n sites. With a fixed deleted set and smaller side the no-edge probability is at most 2^{-0.475nx}. There are at most 2^{H₂(1/20)n} choices for the deleted set and at most n^x possibilities for the smaller side. Summing over x works since H₂(1/20)<0.29. Each pin also has more than n/20 free neighbors with overwhelming probability, even after fixing its incidence at h. All surviving pins consequently attach to the surviving free graph.

There is an exact core formula for the linked anisotropic chart as well. On a consistent target slice Cz=r of rank k, consider the groups with original normals e_i and H_i. For any S, with T its complement, its deficiency modulo row C is

    k − rank of the vertical concatenation of C[:,T] and H[S,T].

Indeed lift the group normals along row C and quotient by the independently included e_i for i in S before subtracting k. The expression applies to the original displayed groups before simplification, including degenerate literals. It follows that the maximum is k, and a maximizing S is exactly one containing all port-active sites and having zero directional H cut toward its complement. The minimal maximizer is the closure of those sites along their row dependencies. Thus on a single port-active SCC a Hall–Rado maximum-deficiency core reduction need not reduce the chart at all.
