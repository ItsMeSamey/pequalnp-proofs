# A counter defeats shared binary linear systems at sampled times

Fix n≥1, N=2^n and D=N/2. Use the public odd number L=∏_{j=1}^D(2^j−1). For each address i=0,…,N−1 sample time t_i=L(N+i). Start an n-bit counter at zero, increment it modulo N each tick and read its most significant bit. Its sampled table is a uniform P function of i. Modulo N the factors with j≥n equal −1, so L mod N is computable by O(n) multiplications on n-bit words and one parity calculation on the number of remaining factors. The finitely many small cases with D<n follow by the original product. The full sampled times have O(N²) binary digits and need not be executed by the address algorithm.

**Sharp width.** Give the predictor an arbitrary nonuniform binary matrix A of dimension d and arbitrary vectors a,b. Its output at time t is bᵀA^t a, with A,a,b held fixed across all addresses. If d≤D, its agreement with the sampled table is exactly 1/2. Width D+1 achieves exact agreement, even at every unsampled time.

To see the lower bound factor the characteristic polynomial of A over F₂. Each irreducible with nonzero constant coefficient, of degree j≤d, divides X^{2^j−1}−1 by the multiplicative group order of the degree-j field. It therefore divides X^L−1. Since D is a power of two at least d, all its required multiplicities divide X^{DL}−1=(X^L−1)^D. The remaining X factor has multiplicity at most d. By Cayley–Hamilton,
     
    A^t (A^{DL}−I)=0 for all t≥d.

This holds for singular and nondiagonalizable matrices as well. For i<D the paired sample at i+D has time t_i+DL, so the two predictions agree. The counter's values differ: modulo N, adding the odd multiple LD of D flips its most significant bit. Each of the D pairs contributes exactly one correct prediction.

For the upper bound let J be the nilpotent shift on D+1 basis vectors sending each to the next and the last to zero. Start at e₀, update by I+J and read coordinate D. The output at t is binom(t,D) modulo two. In F₂, (1+X)^t is the product over set bits j of t of (1+X^{2^j}); its coefficient of X^D is the corresponding binary digit of t, since D is a power of two. This is exactly the counter output. It also shows that the example is a width lower for a fixed transition system with linear readout, while the address table itself has a small Boolean circuit.

**A native exponential-time version.** The huge sampling times above allow the sharp D+1 versus n bound. Within the first H=2^m consecutive ticks, an ordinary small counter also defeats a narrower class to vanishing advantage. Take m sufficiently large, counter period M=2^{ceil(log₂(2m))} and predictor dimension d≤floor(m−3log₂ m). Expose all times t=0,…,H−1 on m-bit addresses. The true table is a single address bit. Any fixed binary linear system of that dimension has absolute agreement bias |Pr[correct]−1/2|=O(m^{−2}).

Indeed its state is eventually periodic after at most d ticks: the stable image im(A^d) is invariant and the restriction of A there is bijective. A particular eventual orbit has period P≤2^d. By the same characteristic-polynomial proof, P divides (M/2)·∏_{j≤d}(2^j−1). Thus the power of two dividing P is at most M/2. On any full joint period of length lcm(P,M), some multiple of P is congruent to M/2 modulo M. Translating by that multiple preserves predictions and flips the output, giving exactly half agreement. Omitting the initial transient and final incomplete joint period loses at most d+M2^d positions. Since M≤4m and 2^d≤2^m/m³, dividing by H proves the estimate. This uses only O(log m) native counter bits and at most H ticks, with an exact linear upper of M/2+1=O(m) bits by the same Jordan construction.

Both statements falsify the candidate that polynomial-width linear evolution in the native state size suffices for fixed-system approximation on these observations. Neither statement treats a predictor allowed to choose a different A,a,b for each i, a nonlinear readout, or a controller that can simply read i. The [catalytic matrix-power compiler](catalytic-binary-matrix-power.md) explicitly permits address-dependent coefficients, so its general nonuniform controller model includes the target. Standard binary linear-complexity arguments for power-of-two periods also appear in the [earlier register control](debruijn-stream-control.md); the selected odd sampling multiplier handles arbitrary candidate periods.

The [checker](sampled_counter_width_check.py) verified 1,320 matrix identities through dimension eight (exhaustive through three), the matching Jordan construction and 15 distinct counter pairs.
