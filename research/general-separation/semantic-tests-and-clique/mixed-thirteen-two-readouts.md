# Two readouts for arbitrary overlaps of physical thirteen-state tables

Each edge has an explicit table of at least thirteen states realized by a private mixed graph, with disjoint interiors. Under the [port model](all-fourteen-two-readouts.md) with arbitrary symmetric affine exterior drifts, two affine occupancy readouts have a deterministic polynomial-time decision and witness algorithm. Physical lifts use supplied row representatives.

By the [physical-fiber classification](thirteen-state-isolated-double-pairs.md) use ordinary, directed and double edges under selected-site semantics. Ordinary edges have a free selected defect at singleton occupancy and a full-projection relation at 11. Directed edges have an adjustable head and a tail whose baseline can be changed when selected if its head has the release occupancy. Double edges have a fixed pair at 11 and free selected defect at either singleton. Absorb directed-tail and double-incidence baselines into constants. Permit explicitly slack vertices with a free parity correction.

Let U consist of ordinary connected components containing a slack vertex or directed head. Ordinary spanning-tree toggles together with a vacant boundary or a free bit correct any occupied region in U. In S, its complement, every directed incidence points to U. Double edges to U supply release literals when their U endpoint is vacant. Build D on S from all internal ordinary and double edges, counting distinct neighbors. Singleton D-sites have ordinary symmetric guards unless released. Each site in a nonsingleton D-component can be selected alone there for any y on U because an edge to a vacant neighbor supplies a correction.

With at most one readout involving S and arbitrary affine restrictions on U use the [one-readout algorithm](all-fourteen-two-readouts.md). A mark in a nonsingleton D-component permits a singleton solution when parity one is required. Otherwise marks lie on singleton sites and failure for the symmetric one-mark problem is affine in y and the nonsingleton helper bits. Zero and unit helper assignments suffice and always lift for all y. Only this singleton property is needed. The one-readout algorithm permits singleton sites without releases. Prescribing an S-site vacant permits deletion, giving each of its ordinary and double neighbors a free bit; its directed heads retain their free bits. U bits can be pinned by y equations. At entry to each rank-two call require that all singleton D-sites have a release. This initially follows from having no isolated vertices.

## The two-color case

At S-rank two, two independent columns suffice whenever their sites' occupancies can be chosen freely, by solving the two equations after choosing y. Signed singleton sites co-release if their release literals are compatible. A singleton and a nonsingleton from different D-components, or sites of different nonsingleton D-components, also co-release. Two sites in one component co-release if each has a neighbor outside the pair (spares may coincide; their vacancies supply distinct incidences). An ordinary edge between the pair can transmit a spare correction from one endpoint to the other. Test these rules with other S-sites vacant.

If an independent pair are singleton D-sites and no free pair applies, every nonzero S-column is on a singleton. The [signed incompatible-literal argument](signed-fourteen-two-readouts.md) branches on one true head or U bit, releasing a full color class and leaving S-rank at most one. A double edge to an already adjustable U vertex gives the same release. Otherwise all nonzero S-columns lie in one nonsingleton component (compare any site outside it to the two independent columns inside). Test empty S and every singleton there with arbitrary y. If none succeeds, exactly two nonzero colors occur, their sum is the target, and all U labels vanish: the shifted target must avoid zero and every occurring color for every y.

Every opposite-color pair must now be adjacent and at least one member must be a leaf with only the other as D-neighbor, or spare incidences solve the instance. Thus the two colors cannot both occur twice. Choose h bearing a unique color. If both colors occur once and just one of their sites has extra D-neighbors, choose that site as h. Write B for the sites of the other color. The marks say h=1 and require odd occupancy on B.

First suppose the independent pair forms a whole two-site D-component. Both sites are forced to 1. Apart from internal ordinary/double edges, all incidences are directed tails to U or doubles to U. For each external release literal at either site test the case of that release, dropping that site's requirement. On the other site the projection of the internal aggregate is free or a singleton. Test its baseline and each one nominated deviation, with the relevant literals on y; the remaining requirement is at most one affine readout. All these branches terminate. In the baseline case compute the XOR K of all internal defect sets at 11. If it has at least two points cover it by at most six affine lines, each giving one readout. If it is a singleton, its two equations give one smaller rank-two instance. Delete the pair and restore slack to surviving directed heads. A surviving double neighbor in U can use its own independent correction. No surviving S-site loses a D-neighbor.

In the remaining case h has another D-neighbor for every b in B. Each b has only double edges to h and no U release. Otherwise its U release or an ordinary edge sharing h's spare correction solves the pair. Take T to be all sites with that strict satellite property, including unmarked ones. Every incidence of T goes to h on a double edge. In particular B is contained in T. Let C be the ordinary connected component of h, lying entirely in S.

## The vacancy tests

Set h=1 and provisionally give it a free bit. This makes all of C correctable. For each j in T or C other than h (only these sets), test j=0. A vacant member of T releases h on a double edge. A vacant member of C gives every occupied ordinary region in C a vacant boundary. In either case a relaxed witness is genuine. Each test costs one one-readout call: one may temporarily designate h slack, include C in U, prescribe h=1 and j=0, and keep only the mark on B. Deleting vacant j in S is valid by the preceding rule. Likewise test every exterior incidence that could release a vertex of C (directed tail release or double neighbor vacant outside C). A release makes all of C correctable, either because it is fully occupied or because some ordinary boundary is vacant. These calls use a fixed U literal or a vacant S-site, and always terminate. If none succeeds, any actual solution requires no successful release to be considered further; for a remaining candidate use baseline contributions on all such exterior incidences. Using a baseline is sound even if that incidence happens to be adjustable.

The first set of vacancy tests implies a stronger assertion. Write z for ALL sites outside h,T, including U and C minus h. Ignore their guards temporarily. For fixed z the selected satellites have strict native guards with affine prescribed vector c_T(z) and the one mark supported on B. The existence set for z is the complement of an affine flat F (allow empty or whole), by the symmetric one-mark path theorem applied to the exterior drift graph on T. If one satellite is prescribed vacant, delete it from T before making this assertion; a vanished mark makes the target impossible.

For j in T or in C minus h, if a relaxed satellite solution with j vacant exists anywhere, some such solution lifts to an original witness. Here is the affine spanning argument. In the exterior z-space, impose z_j=0 if j is in C minus h. Consider all assignments on the original U with all other z-bits zero. In addition test each one-bit change on a site outside U,h,T other than j, at any U setting enabling that change. These probes span the stated z-space affinely. An added site in C is correctable by the vacancy. A site outside C with an ordinary or double neighbor other than h in S has an unselected such neighbor (it cannot be in T). If it has an ordinary edge to h it belongs to C. If it has no such neighbor and only double edges to h, it either belongs to T or has a U release literal, which can be enabled. A singleton D-site without adjacency to h has a U release by the rank-two entry invariant. Thus every added coordinate admits a probe: compare at the same U setting with and without its unit. All non-T occupied sites in those probes can correct their parities for ANY satellite witness with the given vacancy. Since failure of the satellite system is affine, it cannot contain this spanning set and omit another point. The actual one-readout vacancy solver already decides the requisite test in polynomial time; this spanning argument needs no probe enumeration over U.

After all such tests fail, every relaxed satellite solution at EVERY z therefore occupies all of T and all of C minus h. If the all-ones satellite word misses its required mark, reject. Otherwise its guards at all ones give an explicit affine solution set L in z. The preceding implication shows L is precisely the complement of F. If empty reject. Two disjoint nonempty affine flats that partition a binary space are hyperplanes, by cardinality. Hence L is either the whole space or one affine hyperplane. It can be reduced to at most one scalar equation by Gaussian elimination. Since every z in this nonempty L has every C minus h coordinate equal to one, that set has size at most one. If it is a singleton k, L is exactly the equation z_k=1.

## The smaller instance

Fix h and all T selected. If C consists only of h, the equation for its baseline defects gives one affine readout in z. Add it to the at most one equation for L. Eliminate the fixed sites, inserting their occupancies into all remaining drifts. Treat every surviving directed head incident to a removed tail as explicitly slack. Any surviving double incidence facing fixed h contributes its baseline on its selected end; an unoccupied end permits the fixed endpoint to take its baseline. No ordinary edge leaves C, and T has no other incidences. A surviving S-site which loses its final D-neighbor to h either already has a U-release literal or was a strict satellite in T. Thus the recursive entry invariant holds. Newly slack sites do not invalidate it: when ordinary components enter U, a surviving double neighbor gains a U release, and an ordinary neighbor joins U itself. This is one strictly smaller call with at most two fresh readouts and no y-pins.

If C consists of h,k, fix k=1 as well and discard L, which says just that. Compute the possible aggregate K of all internal 11 defect pairs, including any doubles between h,k. At least one internal ordinary edge gives two choices, so K contains at least two points. The two required aggregates are affine in the remaining occupancies at baseline exterior defects. Each line in K tests one affine equation. Remove h,k,T keeping head slack as above and run the one-readout solver on these at most six terminal branches. Some residual singleton sites may now have no release, which is allowed at rank one.

For any original solution the vacancy tests either find a witness or every selected vertex in C uses only baseline external defects, and the L and aggregate equations hold. Conversely, each solution of a smaller instance restores all fixed occupancies, the chosen internal aggregate and baseline defects, so satisfies the original parities. One can reconstruct the final original edge rows from the occupancies using the [fixed-occupancy routine](fourteen-port-uniform-boundary.md). Every nonterminal rank-two stage has at most one rank-two successor, with strictly fewer sites. All other branches use polynomially many rank-one or finite aggregate tests. Binary graph and affine operations give polynomial bit cost. The [bounded mixed-star checks](mixed_thirteen_star_check.py) exercise the forcing argument on small networks.

This proves a polynomial algorithm on arbitrary overlaps at physical table size thirteen. At size twelve a single fixed box with two readouts already contains the native three-pin induced-even problem, whose unrestricted complexity is unresolved.
