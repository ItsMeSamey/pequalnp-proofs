# Combining prefix shears and suffix selectors

Start with a normalized circuit on q primary bits where each formal product z_i=a_i b_i has affine operands in earlier coordinates. Apply the forward quadratic shear compiler through any prefix. It leaves w on q+f bits constrained by f quadratic equations P_j(w)=0. Exactly 2^q values of w are valid. The remaining products and output o are in their original triangular form on w and the remaining symbols z. [Prefix transformation](forward-quadratic-shear-count.md) · [Reverse selector screen](circuit-reverse-selectors.md)

For the output sign sum on valid inputs, expand the f constraint indicators and all remaining gate indicators as binary characters. The residual exponent is

    o + Σ_j s_j P_j(w) + Σ_i t_i (z_i+a_i b_i).

For each s,t this is quadratic in all coordinates and its sign sum takes polynomial time. The normalization is 2 raised to the number of expanded constraints. If z_i occurs in neither operand of any later gate with t=1, summing z_i kills this term unless t_i equals its coefficient in o. This test is independent of the prefix selectors s because their constraints only use w. Enumerate t backwards, forcing the bit whenever this condition holds and branching on both values otherwise. Every partial choice extends to a leaf. Let T denote the resulting number of leaves, including possible zero sums missed by this preliminary test. The output-one count is (2^q minus the normalized output sign sum)/2. The total time for this cut is poly(size)·2^f T. Dovetailing all polynomially many cuts obtains this bound for the best cut, even without predicting T. The exponential terms are summed exactly with polynomial-bit integers.

**Interior cut separating two long regions.** Begin with s≥q rotating reversible Toffoli updates on q registers as in the prefix theorem. In the final registers take the XOR of all registers as a first operand and one register as the second to start an AND chain. Continue the chain for n≥q steps, multiplying by single final registers; the output is its last gate. Prefix shears absorb all s Toffoli products since every subsequent use of original information goes through final registers. After the split the chain with affine guards on w has at most n+1 reverse choices, as support can only continue or stop on moving backwards. The algorithm therefore counts in polynomial time. Pure forward processing fails at each direct chain-symbol read, including the output pin, and the initial product operands are independent. In the unprocessed reverse screen there is a branch with all chain multipliers active. The operand reading the XOR of final registers contains each previous Toffoli product once. Every one of those s selectors then branches, giving at least 2^s leaves. These are lower bounds on the two displayed statistics, not on computing the function. Both regions can have Θ(q log q) gates.

**A circuit defeating every fixed cut of these two rules.** Split q bits into immutable bits u and at least two dynamic registers r, for example q/2 of each. Select M≥2q distinct pairs of immutable coordinates, possible for all sufficiently large q, and define D_i as their products. In alternating topological order, form D_i and perform a reversible update to the dynamic registers controlled by it. Specifically update a target r_j to r_j XOR (D_i times another dynamic register r_k), with j≠k. The final output is a single product Z=(XOR of all D_i) times final register r_1.

Each D_i has independent primary linear operands, and the immediately following update reads D_i directly. Its required forward direction would have to solve 0=1. Therefore all D_i processed before a cut contribute a failure. For every cut before Z, t_Z=1 is forced by the output; the operand XOR of all D_i still contains every unprocessed D_i as its own formal symbol. Their selectors all branch, regardless of choices elsewhere. Prefix shears preserve coefficients of later formal symbols. Even if an update product were affinely simplified, its symbol never occurs in the operand XOR of all D_i. If d of the D_i have been processed, then 2^f T≥2^d 2^{M−d}. Cuts after Z already have all M failures. In the original global rank test, the direct reads give rank B≥M, hence rank[L B]−rank L≥M−q. M can grow as Θ(q log q), with a small coefficient if a prescribed gate budget Bq log q must be met.

**Conditional permutations solve this example.** With u fixed, the dynamic program is a composition of bijections on its r registers. For any u with XOR_i D_i=1, exactly half of the initial dynamic register strings produce final r_1=1. Otherwise none do. Count the immutable assignments where that single quadratic is one by a Gauss sum, then multiply by 2^{|r|−1}. The same elimination is valid for any verifiable register stage of updates r_j←r_j XOR G(u,r_without_j), where the supplied subcircuit for G omits its target register and every later read of the prefix goes through u and the final registers. One may replace the final register block by fresh independent primary bits conditional on each u, even if different updates share large nonlinear calculations. Surviving side outputs of interior states need their own consistency checks. This rule provides a semantic simplification justified by actual dataflow. The fixed-cut counterexample supplies no lower bound on unrestricted circuits or SAT.
