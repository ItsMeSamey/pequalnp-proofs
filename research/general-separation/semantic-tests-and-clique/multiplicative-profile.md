# General circuits and high-degree algebraic normal forms

Work in the binary Boolean ring x_i²=x_i. A circuit with r AND gates and free XORs and constants can be written y_j=A_j B_j for j=1,…,r, where A_j,B_j are affine in x and earlier y's, and its output L(x,y) is affine. Any ordinary binary AND/OR/NOT circuit has r at most its gate count, by a OR b=a+b+ab. Counting the coefficients in these shared affine forms gives 2^O((n+r)(r+1)) possible functions on n bits. Consequently some functions need Ω(2^(n/2)) multiplication gates. This model escapes the linear ceiling for scalar approximate evaluations. Counting does not locate the hard functions in NP. The degree lower bound dates to Schnorr. [Schnorr](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-51083-4_47) · [Boyar, Find and Peralta](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4957665/)

## Two gate-dependent transfers

Identify the homogeneous degree-k part of the squarefree algebraic normal form (ANF) with a k-form in the coordinate basis. Its exterior rank is the fewest simple forms, each a wedge of linear forms, summing to it. Exterior multiplication agrees with Boolean multiplication only in the highest possible degree.

**Theorem.** For r multiplication gates, deg f≤r+1. For 1≤d≤r+1 put h=r+1−d. The exterior rank of the degree-d part is at most binom(2r,h)(r+2)^h. In particular a degree-(r+1) part is simple or zero.

Eliminate y_j backwards by substituting A_j B_j. Projection of a simple k-form to y_j=0 stays simple or zero. The coefficient of y_j is contraction by its coordinate covector and is likewise simple or zero: in the space of the k linear factors take a basis adapted to the kernel of that covector. Substitution adds this coefficient times A_j B_j to the projection. Multiplying a simple l-form E by an affine form a+c in the Boolean ring yields a simple degree-(l+1) part E∧a and a degree-l part D_a(E)+cE of rank at most l+1. On a basis monomial with support I the repeated-index contribution has multiplier Σ_{i∈I} a_i. This is the exterior derivation defined by e_i↦a_i e_i, and the Leibniz rule gives at most l simple summands on E.

Consequently each eliminated variable sends one simple k-form to at most one simple form at degree k+1, at most 2k+2 at k and at most k² at k−1, summing the projection and the two successive affine products. A constant undergoes only projection. Every degree encountered is ≤r+1. Relative to an ideal increase of one per elimination, the one-step counting polynomial is bounded coefficientwise by (1+(r+2)z)². Starting from the output's linear part gives the stated bound after r eliminations. The output's initial constant contributes only to degree zero.

**Affine-flat indicators.** For d≥1, at most Σ_{i=0}^{r+1−d} binom(r,i) affine-flat indicators suffice modulo functions of degree less than d. Over F₂ the identity f(x)=Σ_y L(x,y) ∏_j [1+y_j+A_j B_j] holds because exactly one y respects the gate equations. Expand by selecting 1+y_j at i positions and A_j B_j elsewhere. Before summing y each term is a product of ≤2r+1−i affine forms, hence an affine-flat indicator or zero. Its parity sum over y is again zero or an affine-flat indicator because all nonempty fibers have the same power-of-two size. The sum picks out the coefficient of the product of the r auxiliary bits and thus has degree ≤r+1−i. This decomposition follows from the shared circuit. Subleading homogeneous pieces of one flat need not be simple.

## Full degree of a uniform NP target

Use CLIQUE on N=2^a vertices with m=binom(N,2) adjacency bits. For 2≤k≤N put t=ceil(log₂k). Its full graph monomial has coefficient binom(a−1,t−1) mod 2. By Boolean Möbius inversion this coefficient is the parity of the satisfying labeled graphs. Sibling-subtree swaps in a complete binary tree of height a generate a 2-group. Its edge orbits are classified by the level of the endpoints' least common ancestor. Each graph orbit has even size except singleton orbits. A fixed graph selects a subset of levels, giving complete join at selected levels and disjoint union elsewhere. Its clique number is 2 raised to the number of selected levels. Therefore the parity is Σ_{j≥t} binom(a,j) ≡ binom(a−1,t−1) mod 2 by Pascal cancellation. This fixed-graph description specializes the known Sylow method. [Döring, Marx and Wellnitz, Lemma 7.11](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08988)

If a itself is a power of two, every entry in Pascal row a−1 is odd, by (1+z)^(a−1)=1+z+…+z^(a−1) in F₂[z]. Every fixed 2≤k≤N then attains degree m and needs at least m−1 multiplication gates. If an additional a-bit string z encodes k=1+value(z), the full graph coefficient is OR(z), whose own full coefficient is one. Thus this valid adjacency-plus-threshold slice attains full degree m+a in all its bits and needs at least m+a−1 multiplication gates. The bound is linear in the actual input length.

## A partial profile with no linear ceiling, and its failed relaxation

Let K_d(f) be the fewest multiplication gates whose *one shared list* of affine forms produces all the ANF coefficients of f of degree at least d. Lower-degree coefficients may be arbitrary, so equivalently K_d(f)=min_{deg p<d} MC(f+p). This is a concrete partial approximation model for arbitrary circuits. It is nonincreasing under affine substitutions because composition preserves Boolean polynomial degree and affine substitutions cost no multiplication. Its unrestricted-circuit upper transfer follows by taking p=0. At d=ceil(n/2) there are at least 2^(n−1) independent coefficient positions. Each r-gate list fixes one correlated profile, with at most 2^O((n+r)(r+1)) lists. Therefore almost all such partial profiles require Ω(2^(n/2)) gates. This genuine nonlinearity survives even while roughly half the truth-table algebraic coefficients are ignored. It does not identify a hard uniform NP profile.

The earlier exterior-rank inequalities, if applied separately in each degree without imposing the same affine forms, cannot certify more than n−1 gates on any n-bit function with n≥2. At r=n−1 and d=n−h, the largest possible exterior rank is at most binom(n,h) by monomial expansion, already bounded by binom(2n−2,h)(n+1)^h. Thus independently estimating each grade discards an essential part of the model. An isolated unproved route is to lower-bound K_ceil(m/2) superpolynomially *for CLIQUE itself*, by excluding the simultaneous shared gate arrays on the indicated profiles. Counting random profiles gives no such exclusion.

One can probe these profiles entirely outside the earlier theta rejection tests. Adjoin as a disjoint component a k-clique-free graph that passes every h-vertex conditioning test: for k≥11 join a core K_(k−11) to h+1 disjoint copies of the complete join of five pentagons. On the Boolean subcube allowing every edge inside an arbitrary separate input graph while keeping all other adjacencies fixed, exact CLIQUE is the original unrestricted predicate and every graph passes the theta test. Every h-vertex set leaves an intact pentagon block, and selecting the queried core vertices gives a lower estimate k−11+5√5>k from the original theta number. Profiles transfer by the affine restriction itself, independently of tests on colorable graphs. As a simpler control, blow one pentagon vertex up to t independent twins in the blocker. Turn on an arbitrary subset of their binom(t,2) possible edges. Any added edge plus a common neighbor creates a triangle in that factor, hence a k-clique in the whole graph. With none added, the clique number remains k−1. Thus this all-accepted cube is exactly OR and already has maximal Boolean degree in its free edge variables.

## Independent positive and negative controls

Parity has no multiplications and its profile vanishes above degree one. A one-bit two-way multiplexer is v+s(u+v), with one multiplication gate and degree two. More generally a 2^b-way multiplexer has a binary tree with 2^b−1 multiplications; its top data-address term is (product of the b address bits) times the sum of all data bits, a simple (b+1)-form. The polynomial-time theta separator has polynomial multiplicative complexity by the general transfer, even when monotone promise circuits are large.

For a less trivial control use f_l=∏_{i=1}^l (a_i b_i+c_i d_i) on disjoint groups of four input bits. It has degree 2l and an explicit circuit with 3l−1 multiplication gates. The exterior rank of its leading form is at least (3/2)^l. In a single block the dimensions of all iterated exterior-contraction spaces by order are 1,4,1. Across disjoint blocks multidegrees are independent and their spaces tensor, so the sum of dimensions for the product is 6^l. For one simple form of degree 2l, the same sum is at most 2^(2l), by contracting within its linear span. Subadditivity proves the rank bound. In particular K_(2l)(f_l)≥2l−1+Ω(l/log(l+1)) by the degree-deficit theorem and the given linear upper bound. The rank alone grows exponentially for a function in P with a linear circuit.

In fact the *exact function* has MC(f_l)=3l−1. At each multiplication gate branch on one affine operand after substituting all prior branch choices. This makes that gate affine on the branch. Following any true input leaves at most r linear equations from these choices and one further equation fixing the affine output to one. Hence there is an affine flat of codimension ≤r+1 within the support. The six inputs with ab+cd=1 sum to zero over F₂ (equivalently these are the invertible binary two-by-two matrices after relabeling entries). No four distinct such points sum to zero because the other two are distinct. Thus an affine space contained in that support has dimension at most one. Project a flat from the support of the product into each of its l components. The component dimensions sum to at most l, bounding the original dimension by l. A supporting flat therefore has codimension ≥3l, forcing r≥3l−1. This support argument does not persist after an arbitrary low-degree addition, so it establishes the exact-function value only.

## An exact partial-profile value after arbitrary low-degree perturbation

For two blocks, the stronger statement K_4(f_2)=5 holds. Equivalently, XOR-ing the eight-variable function with *any* cubic or lower polynomial cannot reduce its multiplicative complexity below five. The explicit exact circuit gives the upper bound. Suppose four gates suffice. The earliest nontrivial multiplication has affine operands on the original variables. A constant operand can be eliminated. Otherwise restrict a nonconstant operand to zero. The resulting circuit has at most three multiplications, so its degree-four top part must be simple or zero by the tight-degree case above. Substitution of an affine hyperplane changes the leading four-form by restriction to its underlying linear hyperplane; the arbitrary lower-degree perturbation cannot affect it.

For every hyperplane the restriction of the four-form (x₁x₂+x₃x₄)∧(y₁y₂+y₃y₄) has first-contraction image of dimension six or seven. A simple four-form has first-contraction image of dimension at most four. Here is also a direct evaluation. If the defining linear form uses just one block, symplectic changes of basis in that block put it at x₁=0. The restricted form is x₃x₄(y₁y₂+y₃y₄), whose six nonzero first contractions are independent. If both block parts are nonzero, use symplectic changes separately to identify x₁=y₁=t. The resulting exterior form is tx₂y₃y₄+x₃x₄ty₂+x₃x₄y₃y₄. Its seven first derivatives each have a monomial present in none of the other six. The coordinate choices use the elementary fact that any nonzero vector in a nondegenerate alternating four-space extends to a symplectic basis, by pairing it with a vector of pairing one and taking the two-dimensional orthogonal complement. The checker independently enumerates all 255 linear hyperplanes without invoking symmetry. The two ranks occur 30 and 225 times. This contradiction proves the lower bound.

A finite follow-up is whether K_6(f_3)=8 or whether some degree-five perturbation admits fewer multiplications. A witness consists of the affine gate list; agreement in degrees six and above is checked by a Boolean Möbius transform. This tests the shared-gate model at the next length without identifying its finite resolution with an NP lower bound.
