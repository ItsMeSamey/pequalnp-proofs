# Exact sums of deterministic branching programs with a total query-node budget

**Theorem, as a consequence of the stated October counting theorem and Murray–Williams.** Write K(N)=ceil((log log(N+16))^(1/3)). For every fixed positive integer d some language in NP has infinitely many lengths N at which it has no exact real sum of at most N^d deterministic acyclic Boolean branching programs each with at most NK(N) query nodes. Programs may have arbitrary width, arbitrary variable orders chosen by the current node, repeated queries, and arbitrary sharing of successor subprograms. Each nonterminal has one input-coordinate label and a successor for each Boolean value. Terminals can be identified with the two Boolean constants. Structural checking and forward execution on an assignment use time polynomial in the description. Acyclicity is essential to the verification below.

**Projection and exact counting.** Apply the negation (one exponent d for every NP language at all sufficiently large lengths) to the single P evaluator E(C,i,Z) with R=(n+s+2)^2 slots per logical bit; x_j is their parity. Its raw length M satisfies nR<=M<=BnR, with s<=2^(epsilon n). Guess bounded descriptions of the S<=M^d programs and common-denominator signed weights. Independent evaluation columns, an invertible minor and Cramer's rule give O(S log(S+1)) bits per integer. For each program mark a position heavy if its variable labels more than L=ceil(CK(M)) query nodes in the full raw DAG. There are at most M/C heavy positions. Selecting one slot per block uniformly gives a sum of independent indicators of mean at most Bn/C for each program. Given fixed delta>0, take C so that the probability of more than delta n heavy slots is at most exp(-delta n). Since log M=O(epsilon n+log n), choose epsilon sufficiently small for a union bound over M^d programs. Guess and check the single tuple of selected slots; classification precedes all gate-index restrictions.

For each product of up to four hardwired programs used by the evaluator checks, branch over the union of their heavy selected bits, at most 4delta n. After fixing their values, each remaining input labels at most L nodes per program. In reverse topological order, a fixed node is redirected to the representative of its chosen successor; a live node retains its own identity and references the representatives of its successors. No copying of predecessor or successor subgraphs occurs. This takes polynomial time in the original description and leaves at most nL queried nodes per atom. Each remaining node v receives a Boolean auxiliary variable a_v. For its label x and successors b,c impose the four width-three clauses for x=0 => (a_v=a_b) and x=1 => (a_v=a_c). Interpret sink references as constants. On any assignment to the original variables there is exactly one extension to all auxiliary variables, by reverse topological induction. Pin the representative of each program's start to one and enforce any required input literals. Retain unqueried original variables as declared variables. Even if a full layer contains different query labels, the charge counts nodes, and a fixed query node has exactly one successor under the restriction. Constant branches can be counted directly.

With b unbranched original inputs and q residual query nodes across the product, the instance has v=b+q variables and m<=4q+O(1) clauses. Here b>=(1-4delta)n, q<=4nL, and m/v is bounded by a constant independent of widths and parameters. The exact deterministic fixed-density 3CNF count takes 2^O(nL/sqrt(log n)) poly(nL)=2^o(n), since K(M)=O((log n)^(1/3)). This count is for one heavy assignment. Summing counts over all branches is parsimonious.

**Verification and clock.** For the integer numerator z_i for gate i and common denominator h>0, sum z_i²(z_i−h)² over all x to certify the Boolean range. Use squared differences for input-node consistency, the multilinear degree-three indicator of each invalid gate triple for local consistency, and sum z_out to certify zero output. After the range check all tested violation indicators are nonnegative and the gate checks determine the genuine evaluator by forward induction. Expanding gives at most polynomially many products in M,S,s, each of degree at most four. Scans, coefficient arithmetic and indices cost poly(M) for fixed d. Total nondeterministic time is 2^(4delta n + O_d(epsilon n) + o(n)). Choose delta first with 4delta<1/2, then C, then one sufficiently small positive epsilon for both the union and time bounds, giving O(2^((1−epsilon)n)). Finite exceptional lengths are treated by a fixed small-input exhaustive fallback. To meet the three-output gap convention use a separate SAT-witness branch and return unknown on failures. Murray–Williams Theorem 1.1 gives NP ordinary-circuit lower bounds at every fixed exponent. But bounded-weight sparse sums of the guessed type simulate within one common polynomial ordinary-circuit size: evaluate the original DAG backwards with a constant-size selector per node, then add the selected signed integer weights. This contradicts the negation at a sufficiently large fixed exponent. The same proof permits formulas and these branching programs intermixed in one sum by applying the respective projection compiler per atom.

**Width and monoids.** A deterministic layered program of length ell and width w has at most w ell query nodes. This includes different queried variables at different states of one layer. The proof allows any efficiently bounded per-node density H(N)=o(sqrt(log log N)). Thus at ell<=NK(N), widths satisfying w(N)K(N)=o(sqrt(log log N)) work. A fixed envelope H=(log log(N+16))^(5/12), up to integer rounding, absorbs every fixed width at all sufficiently large lengths and gives a common L_d for them. An instruction selecting one of two elements of a fixed finite monoid can be implemented by the right regular action, with one state per element, including the chosen terminal accepting subset. Products of transformations acting on w points also cost w states when acceptance is tested on the final state from a fixed start. If acceptance depends on the whole transformation, the regular-action upper bound applies to the generated monoid instead. Arbitrary uncharged monoids invalidate a length-only lower bound: N instructions over (Z/2)^N can accumulate the input vector in order and an arbitrary accepting subset specifies an arbitrary Boolean function. If an oblivious layer is nondeterministic with width w fixed, its two transition relations implement elements of the finite relation monoid; a fixed finite powerset automaton also suffices. No claim about unrestricted-width nondeterministic branching graphs follows from the pointer-bypass proof.

A bounded-width route to an upper for the full E evaluator would already imply P/poly=nonuniform NC1 by [Barrington](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~barring/publications/bwbp.pdf). Direct prior single-program size lower bounds use subfunction counting and do not by themselves give bounds for arbitrary signed exact linear combinations.
