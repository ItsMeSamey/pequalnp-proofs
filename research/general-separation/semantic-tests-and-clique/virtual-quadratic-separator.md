# A small circuit needs every primary in any original quadratic-wire separator

A set of wires separating every primary-to-output path permits the quadratic-frontier counter to use its rank whenever their actual values are certified quadratics. Wire order is irrelevant. After overriding the separator with symbolic values, set other primary inputs arbitrarily and evaluate the output. On any realizable separator row this gives the original output since all input-to-output paths are interrupted. Gaussian elimination modulo constants and Fourier inversion then count the primary assignments in poly(size)·2^r time where r is the rank of the separator functions. Minimum *cardinality* among the currently certified choices is found by a standard vertex-split flow network: give each eligible wire unit capacity and all other vertices and connecting edges capacity larger than the number of original vertices. All primaries are eligible, so a minimum cut uses only finite unit edges. Minimizing cardinality need not minimize polynomial rank. [Quadratic-frontier count](original-dag-affine-independence.md)

For every q≥4 let g=x₁x₂x₃, form q individual gates D_i=g x_i, then XOR their outputs. Accumulate all i≥4 first, followed by i=1,2,3. For i≤3 the actual value of D_i is the nonquadratic cubic g. For i≥4 it has degree four. Every partial XOR has a distinct term g x_j for some j≥4 and therefore also has degree four. On the direct path x_i→D_i→output all nodes after the primary are nonquadratic. This holds for every i, hence every separator consisting of actual degree-at-most-two gate outputs and primaries must include all q independent primaries and has rank at least q. It holds for arbitrary choices of gate order and semantically exact node labels. The output nevertheless equals g times the parity of the q primaries and depends on every bit. Its exact count is 2^{q−4}.

**Rewrite the reconvergence before choosing the cut.** Propagate every affine gate as an affine combination of prior nonlinear outputs and primary wires, temporarily viewing these as independent formal Boolean atoms. In the output affine combination, expand each nonlinear output occurrence through its one defining binary truth table, using the affine representations of its operands. This produces one quadratic Q in formal wire values. Even if some wires depend on others, the resulting identity holds after substitution of their actual Boolean values. At most quadratically many monomials occur in the number of original wires. For a cross term ij write Q=ij+iJ+jK+R where J,K are affine in other variables. Extract the product (i+K)(j+J) and continue with R+JK on the remaining variables. When no cross terms remain, rebuild the extracted products of affine forms and the final affine term, then prune unused gates. The rewrite takes polynomial time and preserves the full truth table. The half-polar-rank characterization of multiplicative complexity of a single quadratic Boolean form is classical; here the formal inputs may themselves be shared nonlinear circuit wires. [Mirwald and Schnorr, 1992](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(92)90235-8)

In the stated example the top quadratic on formal wires is g·Σ_i x_i and extraction yields a single product. The rebuilt DAG has a three-signal quadratic separator consisting of x₁x₂, x₃, and the complete input parity. These signals have polynomial rank three. For q=4,8,12 the implemented minimum-cardinality selector returned original ranks 4,8,12 and rewritten ranks 3,3,3. The original full-rank cut enumerated 2^q symbolic rows but required only 16 nonzero quadratic sign sums in each case; the rewritten rank-three cut required eight rows and eight sign sums. Exact counts were 1,16,256. In all 96 frozen seeded input circuits the rewrite preserved the entire original truth table and returned the correct count. This experiment does not give a uniform bound on the rank after rewriting an arbitrary circuit. [Original-gate rewrite and separator implementation](virtual_quadratic_separator.py)
