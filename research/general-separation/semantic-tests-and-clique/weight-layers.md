# Hamming-weight layers and the cost of balancing the clique tests

Every ordinary Boolean circuit admits a polynomial-size representation in which an externally selected Hamming weight suffices for all nonmonotonicity. Convert an s-gate circuit on m bits to negation-normal form with at most two copies per original gate. On weight j, NOT x_i equals the monotone predicate that at least j other bits are one. For j=0 use the constant-one threshold. Substituting monotone threshold circuits for all negative literals gives C_j agreeing with the original circuit on that layer. The list for j=0,…,m has polynomial size since the prefix dynamic program computes each threshold. An unrestricted selector reads the input weight in ceil(log₂(m+1)) bits and returns C_j for that index. This classical slice simulation, attributed to Berkowitz, also applies to hypothetical small CLIQUE circuits. [On the complexity of slice functions](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0304397585902099)

Fix N=(k−1)v, 5≤k≤v^(1/4). Let U be the relabeling orbit of a single k-clique padded with isolates, and O the orbit of balanced complete (k−1)-partite graphs of class size v. Their respective edge counts are a=binom(k,2) and b=binom(k−1,2)v². Put Δ=b−a. Suppose preprocessing retains all original adjacencies and appends M Boolean outputs h_i from a circuit of A monotone gates.

**Theorem.** If preprocessing shrinks the difference of expected output weights between O and U by D>0, some appended bit has advantage at least δ=D/M between U (positive) and O (negative). There is an exact binary monotone promise separator of size O((A+1)t+t²) for t=O((1+ln(|U|+|O|))/δ²). Thus at k=floor(v^(1/4)) with A and M polynomial in N the padded difference remains at least Δ−2^(−Ω(v^(1/8) log v)). A monotone padding retaining the data cannot even reduce the expected gap by one asymptotically.

Indeed the sum of E_U[h_i]−E_O[h_i] equals D. Independently permute the vertices t times and evaluate a bit of advantage at least δ on each permuted graph. For any fixed orbit member these are independent samples with that orbit's mean. Threshold the count at the midpoint of the two means with integer rounding. The per-input error is at most exp(−tδ²/2) by Hoeffding. Summing over the two orbits gives fixed permutations correct everywhere. A monotone threshold costs O(t²) gates. Padding the usual v-vertex tests with isolates maps each k-clique into U and each at-most-(k−1)-partite negative to a subgraph of a member of O. Restricting the monotone separator therefore gives a separator for those original tests, of required size v^(Ω(√k)). Consequently δ can be at most a polynomial factor times the negative fourth root of the lower bound in the stated polynomial regime. [Krajíček and Oliveira, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.06241)

Without monotonicity, appending every complemented input attains constant output weight. The resulting preprocessing itself then needs treatment. Using a nonmonotone selector for the unconditional slice representation supplies no monotone choice among the different C_j.
