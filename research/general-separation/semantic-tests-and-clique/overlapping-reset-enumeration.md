# Enumerating representatives under overlapping local resets

Consider q Boolean inputs and a boundary map Φ. An arbitrary downstream Boolean DAG H reads Φ. Unexposed raw coordinates can also be included in Φ. A supplied reset guard E_i, supported on B_i, certifies that clearing its distinguished raw coordinate v_i from one to zero preserves Φ. Every triggering input has that coordinate one. A globally consistent reversal of the bit meanings is harmless. Let π_i be the uniform density of E_i, λ_i=−log₂(1−π_i). Then 0≤λ_i≤1. Guards are evaluable in polynomial time. The graph and hypergraph construction below proves the reset condition syntactically. For other supplied local reset rules, correctness uses their preservation premise.

For nonnegative real weights w_i satisfying ∑_{i:x∈B_i} w_i≤1 at each raw coordinate, put Λ=∑_i w_i λ_i. One can deterministically find a raw satisfying input of H∘Φ, or certify none exists, in poly(description)·2^{q−Λ} bit operations and polynomial space. The algorithm does not require the weights or the densities. This gives the same bound with the best fractional packing for the listed scopes and densities.

Every Φ-value has a representative avoiding all the guards. Repeatedly apply any enabled reset. Hamming weight strictly decreases and each move preserves Φ, so termination takes at most q moves even if one reset enables another. Now search raw variables in any fixed order. Abandon a prefix as soon as a guard wholly inside the assigned coordinates is true. At a feasible leaf evaluate H∘Φ normally. At depth q−t, writing I for the fully assigned guards, product-space generalized Hölder bounds the fraction surviving by ∏_{i∈I}(1−π_i)^{w_i}. To see the inequality, omit zero weights, write each factor g_i=(g_i^{1/w_i})^{w_i} and apply ordinary Hölder over each independent coordinate in turn, inserting the unit factor for unused weight. Each unresolved guard touches one of the t missing coordinates, so ∑_{i∉I} w_i λ_i≤t. There are at most 2^{q−t−∑_{i∈I}w_i λ_i}≤2^{q−Λ} live prefixes at each level. Testing their at most two children and the root gives the clock. This enumeration preserves existence. Different fibers can contain different numbers of representatives. The algorithm makes no claim of polynomial-size random access to the representatives. The uniform read-d version w_i=1/d is a standard product-space inequality. [Gavinsky, Lovett, Saks and Srinivasan](https://users.math.cas.cz/~gavinsky/papers/Tail-read-k.pdf)

For positive conjunction masks from an arbitrary hypergraph on p=q raw bits, assume each vertex belongs to at most D edges. Permit isolated vertices. On each edge of size at least two fix a cyclic order. Every vertex v that belongs to no singleton edge is eligible. Include the guard x_v=1 and x_{succ(v,e)}=0 for every incident nonsingleton edge e. Each affected edge is already zero, an isolate can always be cleared, and unrelated outputs do not change. The scope size is ≤D+1. A coordinate is its own center once and is named by at most one predecessor in each incident edge, so its total load is ≤D+1. With e eligible vertices this proves

Λ ≥ [e/(D+1)] [−log₂(1−2^{−(D+1)})] = Ω(e/((D+1)2^D)).

The same proof applies to globally unate masks by reversing designated coordinates. The bound improves the dependence on D from disjoint-scope greedy packing in private-term-mask-rewrite.md. The canonical subset consisting of all endpoints of fully active edges is another representative for positive masks. Enumeration of hypergraph unions has polynomial-delay algorithms. [Mary and Strozecki](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/articles/5549)

## Matching dependence on the degree

On a simple graph the mask of active edges determines their incident vertex set. Each mask is obtained uniquely from that canonical vertex set, whose induced subgraph has no isolated vertices. The empty set is allowed. On one complete bipartite component K_{D,D}, every nonempty canonical subset meets both sides and every subset meeting both sides is canonical. Thus there are exactly 1+(2^D−1)^2 masks. For p divisible by 2D, take p/(2D) disjoint copies. The deficit p−log₂|image Φ| is exactly

(p/(2D)) · [−log₂(1−2^{1−D}+2^{1−2D})] = Θ(p/(D2^D)) for D≥2.

The constants are absolute, including for growing D, since −log₂(1−u)=Θ(u) on 0≤u≤1/2. Together the two bounds give the worst-case order of the deficit on p-vertex simple graphs of maximum degree D in this divisible parameter range. They do not identify an exact extremal graph. Neighborhood-multiset coloring results use different local predicates. [Cutler and Radcliffe](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.00998)

At D=log₂p−2log₂log₂p+O(1) the displayed family loses only Θ(log p) bits; such parameters exist, for example p=2D⌈D2^D/2⌉. It uses pD/2=Θ(p log p) distinct binary edge gates, before the downstream DAG. In particular a code representing every possible boundary value must have at least log₂|image Φ| bits at fixed codeword width. No universal superlogarithmic shortening of this entire interface is possible at that degree. A solver inspecting the downstream DAG may use much more structure. In the other direction the enumeration always saves ω(log p) when p/(D2^D)=ω(log p) and every vertex is eligible. The bound counts actual raw-input work for deterministic decision, independently of any new encoding.

The bounded checker overlapping_reset_check.py exhausts 35 seeded hypergraphs and K_{D,D} for D from 2 through 5, including all partial assignments and prefix estimates. One three-variable edge exhibits a reset enabling the next reset. These checks use the uniform read weight. The preceding argument covers fractional weights.
