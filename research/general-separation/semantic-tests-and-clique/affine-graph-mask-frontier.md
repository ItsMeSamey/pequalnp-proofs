# The exact nonlinear gate budget for graph masks under affine restrictions

Let G be a simple graph on q vertices. The exposed boundary is Φ_G(x)=(x_i x_j)_{ij∈E} over F₂. Charge one nonlinear gate for any binary Boolean gate whose truth table has degree two, allow arbitrary affine gates and constants, and permit arbitrary DAG sharing. The inputs on a t-dimensional affine cell are t independent binary coordinates. Define m_G(t) as the minimum number of edges induced by exactly t vertices.

For every affine parameterization by independent coordinates of every t-dimensional affine cell, any circuit that returns its whole edge mask uses at least m_G(t) nonlinear gates. For each t there is a single coordinate t-space all of whose parallel translates admit circuits with exactly m_G(t) nonlinear gates. An invertible affine recoding of the vector output preserves both statements when linear work is free.

**Proof.** The linear parts of the q primary restrictions span the dual of the t-dimensional direction space. Choose t original coordinates I forming an affine coordinate system on the cell. Products for the edges of G[I] are distinct squarefree degree-two monomials in these coordinates, independent modulo affine functions. At any stage of an arbitrary binary Boolean circuit the span of its wires modulo affine has dimension at most the number of nonlinear gates so far. Affine gates add no dimension and a new wire adds at most one, regardless of earlier degrees or cancellations. The required outputs belong to the final span. For the converse choose I attaining the minimum, and fix the other coordinates arbitrarily. Only the edges within I require actual multiplications, one each. Edges crossing I return a single bit or zero; edges outside return constants. The same I works on every translate. Invertible affine output recoding preserves the span modulo affine and is free in this measure.

Deciding whether m_G(t)=0 is precisely the independent-set decision problem. When an exact optimum is unnecessary, a uniformly random t-set has mean |E| t(t−1)/(q(q−1)) induced edges for q≥2. Conditional expectation finds a t-set attaining at most this mean in polynomial time. With fixed inclusions and exclusions, the inclusion probability of each surviving edge depends only on the number of its still-undecided endpoints, at most two. Compare the two conditional means using integer arithmetic, maintaining feasibility of completing to size t.

## A tight growing-degree family

Take b disjoint cliques of w vertices, q=bw. For t=bh+r, 0≤r<b, the exact minimum is m_G(t)=b·h(h−1)/2+r·h. Indeed the sum of the within-clique binomial coefficients is minimized when occupancies differ by at most one. In particular m_G(t)≥(t²/b−t)/2. Every cell of dimension at least εq for fixed ε>0 requires Ω_ε(qw) nonlinear gates on this vector for large w. Taking w=Θ(log q) uses Θ(q log q) supplied raw pair gates, with an arbitrarily small fixed leading gate constant on an appropriate sequence. Arbitrary internal nonlinear re-encoding that returns all specified outputs obeys the same bound. Earlier gate-occurrence controls allowed products to coincide after restriction; this lower bound charges every simultaneous representation of this boundary.

Let F(t,g) be a nonnegative price for a leaf of dimension t with g nonlinear gates, nondecreasing in g. Charge the sum of leaf prices and ignore branch-construction work. The minimum price over complete partitions by binary affine queries and full-output leaf circuits is exactly

2^q min_{0≤t≤q} 2^{−t} F(t,m_G(t)).

Each nonempty leaf has cube mass 2^{t−q}. Taking the weighted average of its rescaled price gives the lower bound. A parallel coordinate slicing at the best t with the preceding explicit circuits realizes equality. Queries may be adaptive. Computational work to find an optimum is a separate question.

For an explicit certification-price example take F(t,g)=2^{A(t+g)/√log₂(2+t+g)} for any fixed A>0. On the clique family with w=Θ(log q), the best exponent discount t−log₂ F(t,m_G(t)) is Θ_A(q/√log q), with constants also depending on the clique-width coefficients. For the upper bound on the discount use m_G(t)≥wt²/(2q)−t/2 and log₂(2+t+m_G(t))=O(log q). Then t−log₂F≤t−c_A(√log q)t²/q, a quadratic with maximum O_A(q/√log q). Conversely take t=⌊ηq/√log q⌋, η>0 sufficiently small and fixed. The balanced allocation gives m_G(t)≤O(wt²/q+t), and log₂(2+t+m_G(t))=Ω(log q). Hence the price exponent is O_A(η²q/√log q+ηq/log q)≤t/2 at sufficiently large q. This is a calculation for the specified charge functional. An actual Tseitin solver must additionally account for all downstream gates, clauses, their width, and polynomial overhead; its certified upper time is not a lower bound on its runtime.

The vector is a difficult interface for simultaneous affine gate elimination, while H equal to its total parity gives a single binary quadratic whose exact count is computable by Gaussian elimination. Feeding an arbitrary H requires charging the representation of H separately. Neither the rank calculation nor the pricing identity implies a circuit-size lower bound for an NP decision language or rules out direct CircuitSAT algorithms.

The bounded checker affine_graph_mask_check.py computes quadratic spans by truth-table Möbius transformation on every affine flat of all 75 graphs of orders one through four. On the even-parity plane of K₃ the three edges share one nonzero quadratic part, giving a noncoordinate minimizer.


An unrestricted nonlinear change of input coordinates can escape the bound. On K₃, take a=x₁+x₃, b=x₂+x₃, h=majority(x)=x₃+ab. These three Boolean functions form a bijective coordinate system since x₃=h+ab. In those coordinates x₁x₃=(1+a)h, x₂x₃=(1+b)h and x₁x₂=h+x₁x₃+x₂x₃, using two nonlinear gates instead of three after the coordinate change. The circuit cost of computing a nonlinear change from the raw inputs must be charged for an end-to-end implementation.
