# Recovering sparse witness insertions and deletions with parity queries

Let F be a Boolean circuit on q original inputs. Suppose an unknown Boolean polynomial G over F₂ has degree ≤d and F differs from G on at most r assignments. Write E for this exceptional set, assume r≥1, and choose u so Q=2^u≥4qr² and Q<8qr². Suppose d+u+1<q.

With a parity-CircuitSAT oracle, the entire set E is deterministically recoverable using at most Q²(q+1) nonadaptive oracle queries. Each query has the original q inputs, contains one copy of F and adds O(qu/log(u+2)+q+u) bounded-fanin gates. The queries are uniformly constructible in poly(q,Q,size(F)) time. This uses the Reed–Muller parity checks: any Boolean polynomial of degree strictly below q has even sum on the cube, since every monomial omits a variable. [Reed–Muller duality background, Abbe, Shpilka and Wigderson](https://www.ias.edu/sites/default/files/math/csdm/14-15/AbbeShWi2014.pdf)

**Syndromes.** Work over K=GF(2^u) in a fixed binary basis. For t∈K define H_t(x)=∑_{i=0}^{q−1}x_i t^i. For each t,v∈K ask the parity of F on H_t(x)=v, and its separate parities on this bucket subject to x_j=1 for each j. The bucket indicator is a product of u affine linear factors. Its product with G, even after multiplying by x_j, has degree ≤d+u+1<q. Therefore all returned parities are the corresponding parities on E.

For each odd bucket use its q coordinate parities as a candidate vector. Retain candidates as a set for each t. Whenever H_t is injective on E, every occupied bucket is a singleton and the candidate set is exactly E. A pair of distinct inputs collides for at most q−1 values of t by the polynomial root bound. Thus at most binom(r,2)(q−1)<Q/2 indices are noninjective. The assignments appearing for a strict majority of t give exactly E. Even a single hidden positive added to the zero function is recovered without an exception descriptor.

The u output bits of H_t are ordinary linear forms. Group inputs into blocks of max(1,floor(log₂(u+1))) bits, precompute every subset parity within each block, and combine one parity per block per output bit. Add an equality test, the optional literal, and the output conjunction. This gives the stated gate count. A defining irreducible of degree u is found by exhaustive trial and division in 2^{O(u)} time, and the hash coefficients are computable in poly(q,Q). When r is polynomial in q the overhead is o(q log q), preserving any fixed q log q gate budget with coefficient slack. This is a reduction to parity decision; a faster parity solver at the desired primary-input clock has not been established.

**SAT and witness recovery.** First evaluate F at every recovered exception and return any satisfying point. The list supplies an evaluator for G(x)=F(x) XOR [x∈E]. A nonzero polynomial of degree ≤d attains one at some vector of weight ≤d, since evaluations on such vectors determine all its coefficients by the subset transform. Coordinate restrictions preserve degree. Thus a prefix SAT test for G takes at most B(q,d)=∑_{j=0}^{min(d,q)} binom(q,j) evaluations on the remaining bits. Enumerate the first r+1 solutions or exhaust the list by pruned depth-first search, using O((r+1)(q+1)) such tests. Any r+1 solutions include one outside E. This covers both insertion and deletion, including F identically zero after all witnesses were removed. Work outside the oracle is poly(q,r,size(F))·B(q,d).

For fixed γ>0 and d≤q/2−γq/(log q)^{1/4}, the binomial tail gives B(q,d)≤2^{q−Ω_γ(q/√log q)}. A deterministic parity-CircuitSAT algorithm at this same saving for ≤Bq log q gates therefore gives this SAT/witness saving on the promised class for r=poly(q) and any fixed smaller gate coefficient. Polynomial query overhead is absorbed. If G is a function of ℓ unknown quadratics then d≤2ℓ, and one may instead use the [quadratic hitting test](quadratic-feature-sat.md) to search prefixes in time 2^{O(ℓ)}poly(q,r,size(F)) outside the oracle.

The promise excludes some small circuits. The AND of q−floor(q/4) designated variables has 2^{floor(q/4)} satisfying points. Its distance from zero already has that size. A nonzero degree-d polynomial has support ≥2^{q−d} by the Boolean minimum-distance bound, so its distance from this AND is at least 2^{q−d}−2^{floor(q/4)}. For d≤q/2 and large q neither permits polynomially many changes. The AND itself has a direct witness. The [unrestricted E^NP gate lower-bound consequence](october-next-gate-scale.md) requires an algorithm covering every circuit at the target size.

The [checker](sparse_error_syndrome_check.py) verified 48 reconstructions on ten variables over GF(32), using test cases with more injective hashes than the worst-case guarantee requires. It also checked a false candidate from three colliding errors and failure of coordinate-slice cancellation at the degree boundary d+u+1=q.
