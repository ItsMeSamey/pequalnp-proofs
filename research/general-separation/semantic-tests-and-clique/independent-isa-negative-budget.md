# Negative budget and the one-negative-constant obstruction

Use the [independent-address ISA product and its sound-cover bounds](independent-isa-positive.md). Consider an exact real representation F=sum_h c_h A_h on all inputs by S Boolean atoms of size at most W. Remove zero coefficients. Let t_- count negative coefficients, B_- be the sum of their absolute values, and μ>0 the smallest positive coefficient. There is a positive coefficient since F is not identically zero. Set q=1+floor(B_-/μ).

There is a sound cover with at most 2^(t_-)(S+1)^q masks, each a conjunction of at most q+t_- positively or negatively queried original atoms. To see this, at an accepting assignment record the set T of negative atoms that are on. Active positive mass exceeds B_T=sum_{h in T}|c_h| because the represented value is one. Choose active positive terms until their mass first exceeds B_T. At most q are needed, since each contributes at least μ. The cover mask tests these chosen positive predicates and asserts that every negative atom outside T is off. Its assigned points have at least the chosen positive mass, and negative mass at most B_T, so their represented value is strictly positive and hence one. Tests for the active negative atoms are unnecessary. Enumerating all qualifying choices gives the claimed count, using (S+1)^q as an upper bound on lists of at most q atoms. This argument allows unbounded coefficient magnitudes.

The cover proof for arbitrary interleaving applies with budget (q+t_-)W. Formula conjunctions and output negations have the asserted occurrence budget up to a constant for negation. For existential query programs no inexpensive general complement construction is needed. Count residual masks of each virtual conjunction directly. On a chosen set of live pointer variables, constituents with zero live nodes give constants; an entire conjunction then either vanishes or is the conjunction of the positive-node residuals with fixed polarities. If the total live-node count is l, the dictionary size is at most 1+product_h 2^{O(l_h²)}≤2^{O(l²+1)}, where each positive l_h counts a constituent's own live nodes. Repeated occurrences are charged separately and total at most (q+t_-)W. The nested residual argument depends only on this dictionary bound, soundness and these occurrence charges. The formula version likewise has log dictionary size O(l+1).

At r=log₂m, W≤rm(log m)^b and q+t_-≤(log m)^e for fixed b,e, it follows that

    q log₂(S+1)+t_- ≥ (α−o(1))r log₂m,

with α=1 for formulas and α=1/2 for programs. In particular if q=o(r) and t_-=o(r log m), polynomial sparsity is impossible. For fixed q and t_- this gives m^{(α/q−o(1))r} terms without imposing a bound on the largest absolute weight.

The number of negative terms by itself imposes essentially no restriction for formulas. Replace each −bA by b(1−A) and pool all constant corrections into a single negative term of weight −sum b. Complementing a formula just changes its output Boolean function at the root; a lone leaf can be negated with constant overhead. This changes at most S terms to S+1 and keeps the per-atom leaf scale. For deterministic programs it likewise suffices to exchange the two terminals. Thus any universal lower bound for formulas with a single negative constant of unrestricted weight would already apply to general signed formula SUM. The ratio B_-/μ above is a substantive restriction. Nothing here establishes the unrestricted signed sparsity lower bound.
