# Small physical replacements for low-rank cuts

In a simple graph with named vertices required in an induced-even support, suppose X contains no named vertex and the binary adjacency between X and its complement Y has rank at most two. One can replace X by at most three vertices in deterministic polynomial time, preserving every possible selection on Y and reconstructing the original support. Internal rank-width and size of X are unrestricted. For a cut of rank one the same size bound holds when X contains one named pin, which is retained.

Factor the cross matrix as U Vᵀ over the binary field with k=rank columns, using independent columns in both factors. Label each vertex of X by its row u_i of U. For proposed selections x on X and y on Y put s=Uᵀx and t=Vᵀy. The contribution to all Y degrees is exactly Vs. On each selected site in X, the internal degree must have parity u_i·t. Consequently every feasible pair satisfies s·t=0 by handshaking. It suffices to preserve precisely the set of feasible (s,t) and record an original witnessing x for each. A new site of color u is joined to Y according to V u.

## No named site on the removed side

For k=0 remove X entirely. For k=1, the empty set gives s=0 at both t. A singleton of nonzero color gives s=1 at t=0. Handshaking excludes the remaining state. One vertex of nonzero color realizes the table.

For k=2, empty sets and singletons realize all allowed pairs whenever all three nonzero colors occur. Three independent vertices carrying those three colors suffice. Otherwise there are exactly two independent nonzero colors a,b. Put g=a+b. The only open states have s=g and t either zero or the unique nonzero t_g orthogonal to g. At t=0 apply the [two-readout all-even algorithm](two-readout-even-p3.md) to obtain a witness for g or detect its absence. At t_g a vertex of either color a or b has odd prescribed degree, and neutral sites have even prescribed degree. To obtain g, one needs an odd number of each color. If no connected component meets both colors, handshaking in each component precludes this. Otherwise take a shortest path between the two classes in a component meeting both. Its internal vertices are neutral, it is induced, and it realizes the required odd endpoints and sum g.

If the second state is absent, an a vertex and a b vertex are nonadjacent (in different components), so the first state is present. Hence there are only three cases. With just the first state for g use independent vertices colored a,b. With just the second use adjacent vertices colored a,b. If both are present use three independent vertices colored a,b,g. All these gadgets realize the empty and singleton cases already described. The three-vertex bound is sharp for the full table among physical simple replacement graphs with literal linear interfaces: two sites of full cut rank must have independent colors, so their one edge choice provides exactly one of the two states for the missing color.

## A rank-one cut with one named site

Let p be the single required site in X. Lists below describe the three potentially possible pairs in the order (t,s), namely (0,0), (0,1), (1,0). Determine the feasible subset constructively: t=0 uses [two readouts](two-readout-even-p3.md), the pin bit and the boundary sum; at t=1 the sum must be zero and the pin is the sole extra requirement in the [mixed one-readout algorithm](symmetric-single-row-p3.md).

If p has zero color, selecting p alone realizes (0,0) and (1,0). When (0,1) is absent keep just the pin with zero color. When it is present add one unattached vertex of color one to the retained zero-color pin, giving all three allowed pairs. If p has color one, selecting it provides (0,1). When neither other state exists keep only p. When just (0,0) or just (1,0) exists add one site of color one, respectively nonadjacent or adjacent to p. When both exist add two sites of color one, one adjacent to p and one nonadjacent, with no edge between the added sites. These cases preserve the pin's original color and every feasible state. A rank-zero side containing just one pin can always be replaced by that isolated pin.

## Finding cut reductions and lifting their witnesses

For any four nonpins ordered only as a set Q, minimize the cut-rank of X subject to Q⊆X and X avoiding every pin. For rank-one cuts with one permitted pin p, also allow Q to include p, require p∈X, and forbid the other pins. Enumerating the four-element anchors within the permitted sites finds every applicable side of size at least four in the sense that if any exists, some iteration returns one. The cut-rank is submodular: in the column matroid [I A], the columns indexed by both copies of X have rank |X|+rank A[Y,X]. Hence their usual symmetric matroid connectivity equals twice the cut-rank. Fixing mandatory and excluded elements preserves submodularity, so polynomial-time submodular minimization with a Gaussian-elimination value oracle suffices. [Iwata, Fleischer and Fujishige](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0004089)

Apply reductions until no such side exists. Each shrinks the graph by at least one vertex, keeping all pins and original vertex identities on the other side. After later stages return a witness, recover its exterior y, t and replacement output s. Restore the stored old x with the same (s,t); exactly the same Y degrees result. Repeating in reverse order gives the original witness. For three pins every rank-one partition has a side with at most one pin, but its small side need not have four vertices. The procedure makes no claim that the remaining graph always has bounded size.
