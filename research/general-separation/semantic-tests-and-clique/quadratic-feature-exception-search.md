# Finding exceptional witnesses by ordered support comparison

Given a Boolean circuit F on n bits, supply an exception descriptor E and promise that B=F XOR E is an arbitrary Boolean function of at most ℓ hidden GF(2) quadratics. The descriptor is a layered linear representation over GF(2): E(x)=u M₁(x₁)⋯Mₙ(xₙ)v with two explicitly supplied matrices per layer. Let D count their input description, including the endpoints. This includes the parity of m literal subcubes of unrestricted widths, using m diagonal entries per layer. Sparse diagonal input can be expanded in polynomial time. A conjugated width-two descriptor, for example, need not contain any row explicitly naming its exceptional assignment.

There is a deterministic algorithm finding an F-witness or deciding that none exists in time

    (1+min{|B∩E|, |Bᶜ∩Eᶜ|}) · 2^{O(ℓ)} poly(n, size(F), D),

where sets denote positive supports. In particular the time is polynomial in the number of actual flips, with no list of flipped points, sign promise, or lower density requirement.

On any pinned prefix, B is evaluable using F and the supplied descriptor and still has at most ℓ quadratic features. Both B and its complement have the deterministic restriction SAT test in the [quadratic-feature lemma](quadratic-feature-sat.md), based on [Viola’s quadratic pseudorandomness theorem](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~viola/papers/d.pdf). To test E on a prefix, propagate its row vector along the pinned prefix, then propagate a basis of all reachable rows through each remaining layer. At a free bit take the span of both images of the preceding basis. A final row has nonzero pairing with v on some extension exactly when one basis row does. The complement has the same representation augmented by a constant-one summand in block diagonal form. This is the finite-field layered version of the forward-space test used for weighted automata. [Kiefer et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.2818)

Each prefix test gives a lexicographic enumerator with polynomially many tests per delay (at most O(n) on the pruned depth-first binary trie), including startup and exhaustion. Compare B and E in lockstep order. At their first distinct outputs, the lexicographically earlier one is a witness for F. Exhaustion of only one stream also gives a witness at the next output of the other. Simultaneous exhaustion proves equality. Every preceding matched output belongs to B∩E. Run the same comparison on both complements in parallel and take the first decision. Bounds on work follow by counting the matched outputs of the faster comparison. No support sizes need to be known in advance.

For an explicit one-flip test, choose a point a with E=1[x=a]. If B=0, the first comparison reports a even though the base has no witnesses. For the opposite outcome, let ℓ=ceil(n/2) and AND the pair-products of the matching literals (use a single literal for an odd last position) to obtain B=1[x=a]. The same E now gives F=0; simultaneous exhaustion certifies the negative answer. A negative flip from a dense base is also covered: if B=1 then the only forbidden point is a. Without any exception descriptor, B=0 and a black-box F promised zero or a singleton require every cube point in the worst case for deterministic exact decision, by the all-zero adversary.
