# A modal prediction proof for exact MCSP under P=NP

McKay, Murray and Williams already prove a stronger one-pass search theorem (Theorems 1.2–1.3). For time-constructible s(n)≥n their oracle queries go to the third level of PH. Under P=NP the deterministic streaming solver uses polynomial-in-s space and update time. In particular it solves exact MCSP at s=2^floor(sqrt(n)) in N^(1+o(1)) time, N=2^n. Their construction merges minimum circuits for consecutive intervals. The multipass derivation below uses approximate modal predictions. Equivalence-query learning under P=NP and approximate version-space counts for anti-checkers also have earlier proofs.

Fix the total language of truth tables f of power-of-two length N for which a circuit of at most s gates computes f, rejecting malformed lengths. Use ordinary fan-in-two Boolean circuits with constants. Large n have s≥n; handle small n directly. Count the input length to compute n,s by integer arithmetic. A fixed NP verifier guesses L=O(s log(n+s)) bits encoding a circuit of size at most s and checks equality with every successive input bit. A multitape verifier can scan the gate records to retrieve each operand, and even this direct simulation and validation fit O((N+2)^6) time. Malformed descriptions reject.

Let S be a common list of labeled addresses and V the valid L-bit descriptions matching S. Duplicated functions from different descriptions are allowed. On the compact input (1^n,1^s,S), V nonemptiness is in NP by guessing and testing one description. Under P=NP it has a fixed deterministic polynomial test. Reject if V is empty.

For nonempty V, let h(S,a) return 0 whenever at most one quarter of V outputs 1 on a, and 1 whenever at least three quarters output 1. Either value is permitted between these cases. Under P=NP there is one fixed total deterministic polynomial-time h on compact inputs. Use Stockmeyer relative approximation for the counts of all V and its descriptions predicting 1, error ≤1/10, with simultaneous success probability at least 5/6 by amplification. Comparing the ratio to 1/2 separates the stated endpoints. Zero numerator is included by the relative guarantee or an NP emptiness check. Under P=NP the oracle calls have ordinary polynomial simulations.

To obtain a deterministic separator without assuming exact counting or majority, amplify this randomized comparison to error δ so small that (r+1)δ<1/2, where r is its polynomial number of random bits. Define a total polynomial-hierarchy predicate asking if r+1 translated copies of its accepting coin set cover the r-bit cube. On a high-probability YES input random shifts cover the cube with positive probability since 2^r δ^(r+1)<1. On a high-probability NO input the union density is at most (r+1)δ<1. The quantifiers are existential over shifts and universal over an r-bit string, so this total predicate has a deterministic polynomial clock under P=NP. Its answers on intermediate ratios may be arbitrary. All exponents are fixed.

Scan the input, using h(S,a) to predict each label. On the first discrepancy append that labeled address and restart. Each discrepancy eliminates at least one quarter of V: a bit predicted by h has support exceeding one quarter. Thus O(L+1) updates suffice for all tables and S has O((n+1)(L+1)) bits. A full pass with no discrepancy identifies an evaluator for the entire input, since h(S,a)=f(a) everywhere. It does not yet determine whether f has a circuit of size s.

At that stop test on the compact input (1^n,1^s,S) whether there exists a valid size-s circuit C such that for every n-bit address a, C(a)=h(S,a). With the fixed deterministic polynomial algorithm for h this is a polynomial existential-universal test, hence is in P under the collapse. The test includes all addresses and works even if h has no size-s implementation. It uses h's uniform algorithm directly. If needed this algorithm can be compiled into a compact polynomial-size circuit and specialized by fixing the summary.

Each scan costs N·poly(n+s) on sequential tapes, rewinding in O(N). There are poly(n+s) scans. The final compact equality test costs poly(n+s); the workspace throughout is poly(n+s) apart from the read-only input. For this fixed s, the total time is N^(1+o(1)) and workspace N^o(1). The language is total. A deterministic multitape N^(1+ε) time lower bound against every decider, for some fixed ε>0, would imply P≠NP. No such lower bound is proved here.

## Finite test and a communication limitation

The independent check_exact_mcsp_halving.py checks 4,270 small codebook/target pairs and two one-hot counterexamples, allowing arbitrary permissible decisions between the modal thresholds. With the 128 one-hot words as descriptions, the all-zero table has no size-class representative but has less than 1/16 disagreement with the ensemble on every coordinate. A no-update stop alone would accept. The final equality test rejects. This example tests the finite hypothesis-class argument, not an assertion about the unrestricted circuit class at a prescribed n.

There are at most 2^L YES tables. Hence for any bipartition of the N coordinates, even exact membership admits an unrestricted-computation one-way deterministic protocol using L+O(1) communication bits. Enumerate all distinct projections of YES tables onto Alice's coordinates in canonical order. She sends the rank of her projection or a no-match symbol. Bob reconstructs the projection, and checks by enumeration of gate descriptions if some circuit matches both sides. This does not give a fast state update.
