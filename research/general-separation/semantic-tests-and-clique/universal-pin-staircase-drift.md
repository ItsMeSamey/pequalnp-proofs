# Two-sided block perturbations and exact interblock drift

In the [distance-three residual](universal-pin-distance-three-trace.md) partition the row sites into A and B, each required to have odd selected parity, and call the column sites D. All selected degrees are prescribed odd. The residual has no A–B edges. Handshaking forces the total selected column parity to zero. Toggling the clique inside A or B preserves selected guards because the selected count there is odd. Toggling the D clique together with all B–D edges also preserves guards: each selected D site gains the sum of two ones, and each selected B site gains even column parity. These normalizations preserve witnesses both ways since every feasible word in either graph has even total size.

## Recognizing a canonical order

After any such normalization, test whether every A-row neighborhood on D is comparable by inclusion with every B-row neighborhood. If so, form connected components of the incomparability graph of all rows. They are monochromatic, and their neighborhoods are ordered by non-strict inclusion across components. An external row comparable with both ends of an incomparable pair must lie above both or below both. Propagating along paths orders nonsingleton components; identical rows in distinct components are isolated and ties may be broken arbitrarily, for example by label. Assign each column to the first row block it hits, or a final empty row block if it hits none. For column group j all rows before j miss it and all rows after j hit it. Within group pair j the incidence matrix is unrestricted.

If row–row edges occur only inside row blocks, and D–D edges only inside column groups, the answer is NO, regardless of the graphs there. Let w_i,u_i be selected count parities on row and column groups i. Their two total parities are zero, but at least one w_i is one since the blocks are monochromatic and the A-parity is one. Let z_i be the parity of locally selected row-to-column edges in pair i. Summing selected degree equations on either group cancels its internal edges twice, giving

    z_i = w_i (1 + Σ_{j<i} u_j) = u_i (1 + Σ_{j>i} w_j).

Before the first nonzero w_i, the suffix sum in the column formula vanishes, so u_i=0 there. At the first nonzero w_i the row formula gives one and the suffix column formula gives zero. This does not require that each selected column hit its first global block at a selected row. With arbitrary within-group column perturbations an incomparable cross-row pair alone no longer guarantees a witness: one a, one b, two exclusive columns and the edge between them form a four-site residual path. The two required rows force both columns, whose degrees would be even.

## Exact algorithm for arbitrary interblock joins

Take any supplied ordered pairs of monochromatic row groups and column groups, partitioning the sites. Within each pair allow arbitrary graphs, including arbitrary row–column incidence. Between pairs require each of the four group-to-group blocks to be individually empty or complete. Write Q for the symmetric binary matrix of interpair joins on two labels per pair, assigning zero to joins incident with empty groups. Let κ be the maximum binary rank of a prefix/suffix crossing matrix, cutting between pairs. The original occupancy problem is solvable constructively in 2^{O(κ)} poly(n). In the canonical nested incidence setting κ is at most one plus the maximum, over cuts, of the sum of the row–row and column–column cut ranks: only the past-column to future-row rectangle can add cross incidences, and it has rank at most one.

The canonical procedure above constructs its particular ordered partition in polynomial time. For this or any supplied order, inspect each interpair block for uniformity, then compute the actual κ by binary elimination at every cut. Try the finitely many indicated clique normalizations if desired. Failure for the constructed orders does not rule out another admissible partition or one of smaller width. No algorithm for minimizing κ is assumed.

At each cut let P,F be the processed and future group labels, and B=Q[P,F]. A state records forward drift Bᵀy_P on F and backward drift B y_F on P, with y the group occupancy parities. Enumerate their two image spaces by elimination, giving at most 2^{2κ} choices. Track also the two original readout parities contributed so far. When processing a pair I, try its two parity bits e and a state at the next cut. On the later suffix, its forward drift must equal the old forward drift restricted there plus Q[F_later,I] e. On the earlier prefix, the old backward drift must equal the new backward drift restricted there plus Q[P_earlier,I] e. At I the contributions from earlier and later sites are respectively the old forward and the new backward drift restricted to I. Forward induction from the empty prefix and backward induction from the empty suffix prove that every complete chain has precisely the global drifts of its chosen y. Conversely an actual occupancy word supplies such a chain; require both original color parities one at the end.

Inside I every selected row requires one common internal degree parity and each selected column another. Write this pair as c after subtracting the two external drifts from one. Handshaking requires c·e=0. On this condition there is a symmetric binary two-by-two matrix T with Te+diag(T)=c. For e=0 choose a diagonal; otherwise set diagonal entries to match c off the support of e and pair the even number of required ones on its support by off-diagonal entries. Toggle each edge between distinct sites in the local graph by T evaluated at their two types; leave the diagonal clear. At a selected site of type a the change in degree parity is (Te+diag T)_a. Thus the local feasibility question is exactly the [all-even two-readout problem](two-readout-even-p3.md) with the same two occupancy parity bits. Precompute a genuine local witness for each feasible (e,c). Empty groups force their parities zero. Store transition predecessors, concatenate the recovered local words, undo the occupancy-preserving normalization, and restore p,q and the universal vertex as in the distance-three reduction. This returns an original induced-even support with all three names.
