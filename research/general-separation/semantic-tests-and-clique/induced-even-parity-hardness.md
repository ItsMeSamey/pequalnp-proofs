# Parity of induced all-even supports on connected bipartite graphs

Computing the parity of the number of vertex subsets inducing only even degrees and containing three named pins is ⊕P-complete, even on connected simple bipartite graphs where the three pins are leaves with a common neighbor. Every instance of the constructed family has an explicit witness consisting of the three pins alone.

Use parity of independent sets as the source problem. Theorem 3.6 of [John Faben's primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0809.1836) gives completeness. The target belongs to ⊕P by guessing one bit per vertex and checking all degrees and pins. We give an explicit parity-preserving reduction with linear output size.

For a simple input graph J keep its vertices as ports and remove its edges. For every former edge choose endpoints in an arbitrary order u,v. Install two copies of the following gadget, with fresh internal vertices a,b,d in each copy. Its five edges are ab, ad, ub, vb and va. There are no other edges. First fix the two port bits u,v and check only the three internal degree conditions in one copy. When a=0, d has no selected neighbor and either value of d is legal independently of the rest, giving an even number of choices. When a=1, the private leaf d must be zero. The degree condition at a then forces b=v. If v=0 this assignment is valid. If v=1 the condition at b says 1+u+v=0 modulo two and is equivalent to u=0. Thus the parity of allowed internal assignments is one for (u,v) in {00,01,10} and zero for 11.

The group generated by exchanging the two copies independently for each original edge acts on induced all-even sets. Its nontrivial orbits have even size. In a common fixed point the two copies agree site by site, so the contributions to every original vertex degree come in identical pairs and those vertex conditions hold automatically. At fixed port bits, therefore, the fixed points are the Cartesian product of the allowed single-copy interiors. Summing their parities over the port bits gives exactly the independent-set parity of J, including isolated vertices.

Now form the bipartite double cover of the gadget graph, with an edge between i-left and j-right whenever ij is an edge of that graph. Swapping the two copies fixes precisely the lifted diagonal all-even supports. Thus parity is unchanged. To obtain a connected host, choose one vertex in each nonempty component of the double cover, orient its bipartition so that the chosen vertex is on the same side in every component, and connect all chosen vertices to a new common vertex h. Add three named leaves adjacent only to h. They force h to be unselected. Every all-even support of the double cover extends in exactly one way by adding all three leaves, giving the stated connected bipartite host. This works for the empty source as well.

For this host and these three pins, the [bilateral polynomial](three-pin-bilateral-anchors.md) at all weights equal to one is exactly the same bit, in every characteristic-two extension field. Any uniform polynomial-time exact evaluator covering arbitrary such substitutions would therefore imply P=⊕P. This includes the special case of binary extensions of size at least eight times the graph order, since the all-one tuple is available in them. The reduction concerns values of the enumerator. The identical family has constant YES answer for the support-existence question, and the argument does not establish hardness of identity testing on that promise.
