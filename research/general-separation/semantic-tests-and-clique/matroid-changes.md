# Low-change witnesses for a function with exponential monotone circuit complexity

Let M rows of a binary matrix have rank R≥1. For an active subset A of rows, accept when a specified target vector lies in their linear span, returning the support of a subset summing to the target. Give all rows one uniformly random permutation as priority. Recompute the greedy independent basis of A in that order. Return null if the target is absent from its span and otherwise return the support of its unique expression in the basis. This is a polynomial-time memoryless selector with genuine positive certificates of at most R rows.

**Theorem.** On every fixed chain of activations the expected number of selections, counting the first nonnull output and each change, is at most R(1+H_M−H_R) for a nonzero target. This bound is sharp for the number of basis insertions over general matroids. It is O(R log(eM/R)).

Generate the order by i.i.d. continuous priorities in [0,1]. Consider any fixed insertion order and row e at time t. With priority u, it enters the greedy basis iff it is independent of earlier active rows having priority below u. If it does not enter, no basis element changes. For fixed u>0, sample each row whose priority is below u. Summing over t, the expected number of independence events times u is exactly the expected final rank of the sampled set. Each event is independent of sampling its current row, and sampling an independent row increments the rank by one. Denote that sampled set by A_u. Integrating over the new row's possible priority gives the exact formula
E[total basis insertions] = ∫₀¹ E[rank(A_u)] du/u.
In particular this expectation is independent of the fixed insertion order and depends on the rank structure of the whole row family. A prefix or a subsequence, with a specified initial activation batch, has no more witness selections than a full refinement from the empty set. When the basis is unchanged so is the unique expression of the target in the basis.

Since rank(A_u)≤min(R,|A_u|), the integral is maximized by the uniform matroid of rank R on M elements. For that matroid a new item at position t enters the basis exactly when it lies among the min(R,t) lowest priorities seen so far, with probability min(1,R/t). Summing gives the formula. The sharpness claim is about basis insertions, not changes of every target certificate. The comparison matroid need not be binary representable.

**Application.** Take binary affine 3-XOR constraints as the universe of rows and encode a selected equation a·x=b by its augmented row (a,b). The selected equations are inconsistent iff (0,…,0,1) is in their span. The returned support is a short actual contradiction witness whose rows XOR to that target. With n variables there are M=O(n³) possible rows, and R≤n+1, so expected witness changes are O(n log n) on every fixed order, with one order of priorities reused across the chain. Yet the monotone inconsistency function has monotone circuit size 2^(n^Ω(1)) while admitting small ordinary circuits. Thus efficient memoryless selection, short certificates checked on the positive inputs, and this change bound do not generically imply small monotone circuits. The exponential lower bound and span-program description are due to Göös, Kamath, Robere and Sokolov. [Theorem 1 and §1.2](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol124-itcs2019/LIPIcs.ITCS.2019.38/LIPIcs.ITCS.2019.38.pdf)

An exact uniform permutation can be represented by O(M log M) bits and supplied to a polynomial-size selector; a uniform sampler uses expected polynomial time by rejection. If a fixed budget of independent fair bits is required, give each row an independent integer priority from a power-of-two range of size at least M³ and break ties by index. The probability of a collision is at most 1/(2M), yielding an additive at most one to the expected changes.
