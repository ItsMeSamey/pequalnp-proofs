# Exact quadratic phase sums on 2-CNF solutions

For an explicit Boolean 2-CNF on n raw coordinates, one can list a disjoint affine partition of its solutions in polynomial space and at most poly(input)·2^{n/2} bit operations. Its pieces are coordinate subcubes except for disjoint pairs of coordinates which can be tied by an affine equation. This partition is independent of the integrand. Given any additional system of binary affine equations and any binary quadratic Q, the same bound computes the exact integer sum of (−1)^Q on the constrained solutions. The sign may couple variables from different clause components.

Nagao, Seto and Teruyama reported the same deterministic exponent for 2-CNF satisfiability inside an affine subspace. We use the disjoint partition explicitly for signed sums. The conference abstract states the decision result. We make no priority claim for the partition technique. [Nagao, Seto and Teruyama, COMP2026-5](https://ken.ieice.org/ken/paper/20260521McuY/eng/)

## Disjoint branching

Delete tautologies and duplicates and propagate units. An empty clause closes the branch. As long as some unpinned coordinate occurs in both signs, split on it. Either polarity falsifies one of its occurrences and forces the other variable of that clause, or closes the branch. Both surviving children have at most n−2 unpinned coordinates. The two contributions to the 2^{n/2} budget sum to at most the parent budget.

Once each coordinate uses a single polarity, flip coordinates so every clause forbids selecting both endpoints of an edge of a simple graph. A vertex v of degree d≥3 gives two disjoint branches. Omitting v removes one variable. Selecting v removes v and its d neighbors, which are forced absent. The sum of the surviving budget fractions is at most 2^(−1/2)+2^(−2)<1. Flips and prior pins are invertible or recorded explicitly, so every output piece lifts to the original coordinates.

The remaining graph consists of isolates, paths and cycles. For a path or even cycle, list assignments of the smaller bipartition class, fix the necessary neighbors to zero, and keep every other bit free. This costs at most 2^{floor(h/2)} cells for a component of h vertices. An isolate is a single free bit. On a triangle the feasible points are 000,100,010,001. Pair the first two and the last two into affine lines, giving just two cells. For a cycle of length 2k+1 with k≥2, select alternating coordinates numbered 2,4,…,2k around the cycle. On the complement just the edge (1,2k+1) remains. Its two endpoints are both unforced only when coordinates 2 and 2k both vanish, on exactly 2^{k−2} of the 2^k assignments. On those assignments split the edge's three choices into a line and a point. Consequently there are at most 2^k+2^{k−2}≤2^{(2k+1)/2} cells. Cartesian products of the components' disjoint cells are affine and retain all cross-component phase terms. Enumerate tuples by depth-first iteration. With polynomial bookkeeping per node and depth at most polynomial in the displayed input, this gives the stated time and space, including failed branches. Equivalently the elementary budget covers a closed child with the same unit charge as one leaf whenever the parent has at least two coordinates in a mixed-sign split.

Intersect each cell with the additional affine equations by Gaussian elimination. On a nonempty intersection choose a bijective affine parametrization. The pullback of Q has degree at most two. A nonzero quadratic coefficient gives a pair with terms uv+uA+vB; summing the two bits gives 2(−1)^{AB}, leaving another quadratic. The remaining affine character sums to zero or gives a signed power of two. All intermediate integers for a cell and all partial sums over disjoint cells use O(n+1) bits.

## Application to drift-free cubic matchings

Suppose every raw bit belongs to exactly one cubic monomial and the quadratic edges have components of size at most two edges. There are no linear or constant terms. Expand each cubic sign as e⊗³−2δ⊗³ with e=(1,1), δ=(0,1). Give each triple its selector spin. Modulo three the relative local coefficient −2 is one. A free raw port contributes the common factor −1 and the sign (−1)^s. A single edge enforces equality of its two selector spins with common scalar −1 and no relative sign, since its diagonal is (2,−1).

For a path of two edges with middle selector s and endpoint selectors t,u, the exact eight entries are 4,2,2,2 on the s=0 slice in the order tu=00,01,10,11; on s=1 only tu=11 contributes, with value one. Modulo three the factor is the product of the two implication indicators [s⇒t][s⇒u] and (−1)^{s+t+u+tu}. These identities also hold after selectors coincide. Fuse all equality edges and strongly connected implication components, substitute once into the phase, and combine duplicates over the binary field. This produces a known global sign times a quadratic phase summed over ideals of a finite poset (choose the order orientation so that ones form ideals). At most one remaining variable per original triple survives. The formula computes the original cubic gap modulo three. The corresponding integer cubic gap has additional magnitudes and does not follow from the same substitution.

The poset case also has a shorter half-exponent proof. If one element has both a predecessor and a successor, either assignment forces a second coordinate. Once the order has height at most two, assign the smaller side of the bipartite comparability graph and sum the remaining affine cube by Gauss elimination, leaving isolates free. Thus V surviving implication components give O*(2^{V/2}) bit time, even for dense phase couplings. In particular q raw bits give V≤q/3 and O*(2^{q/6}). For large sparse raw instances the pinned October fixed-density counter gives the stronger asymptotic 2^{O(q/√log q)} via the linear-size circuit computing the raw sign. The V-dependent exponent can apply when the number of fused spins is much smaller than the raw size. A zero residue does not determine whether a source SAT instance has a witness.
