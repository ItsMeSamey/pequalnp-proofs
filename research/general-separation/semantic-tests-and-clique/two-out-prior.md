# Two-out directed Hamiltonicity: exact-algorithm attribution

For N vertices with both indegree and outdegree at most two, Kowalik and Majewski explicitly give a deterministic polynomial-space weighted ATSP algorithm in O*(2^(N/3)) time. Their Lemma 2.1 splits each vertex into an input and an output vertex joined by one forced edge. Their Theorem 2.5 invokes Eppstein's subcubic forced-TSP bound O*(2^((v−|F|)/3)). Corollary 2.6 substitutes v=2N and |F|=N. Thus 1.260^N is a valid rounded version of the published bound. Corollary 2.7's O*(2^(N/6)) assumes total degree at most three, a stronger promise. [Kowalik and Majewski, §2.3](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol180-ipec2020/LIPIcs.IPEC.2020.23/LIPIcs.IPEC.2020.23.pdf)

For arbitrary indegree and exactly two distinct outgoing heads per row, build the undirected multigraph on heads with one edge per row between its options. A cycle cover chooses an endpoint per edge, each head once. Each connected component must have equal edge and vertex counts. Otherwise reject. In a unicyclic component, iteratively force the incident edge toward a leaf and remove both. Each surviving cycle has two cyclic orientations, including a pair of parallel edges. Delete all arc choices belonging to no cover. Every head now has at most two choices of predecessor, and every row has at most two choices of successor; the same N vertices and arc weights suffice. This preprocessing extends Corollary 2.6 to this two-out class without changing the weighted objective. Singleton rows in an at-most-two instance can first be forced, deleting competing incoming choices, until either a zero row/column appears or the residual assignment has two choices per remaining row. Rusu proves NP-completeness of directed HC on exactly two-out graphs. [Rusu](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304397522001487)

The vertex-only cubic-TSP bound O*(1.2312^v) of Xiao and Nagamochi would have v=2N under this split. Their finer measure includes both vertex weights and unforced-component terms; their §7 assigns positive weight to critical components and other nontrivial components except four-cycles. Substituting N into their vertex-only bound, or retaining only the forced-vertex weights in their measure, has no justification. [Xiao and Nagamochi, §§7–9](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.6831)

## Unweighted two- and three-channel switches

After fixing all alternating choices of length at least four in the head multigraph, a three-channel choice selects between the identity and one specified three-cycle; decompose the latter into two sequential binary transposition switches and constrain their settings to agree. A two-channel choice contributes an unconstrained binary switch. The base permutation closes these channels. Suppress degree-(1,1) stretches. A closed component without a switch must be the only component for a positive answer. For a nonempty connected switch network the resulting directed multigraph has indegree and outdegree two at each vertex. A setting produces a single tour precisely when its induced transitions follow one directed Euler circuit. The two transpositions use successive stages, so an intermediate channel common to the two switches is allowed.

Take any directed Euler tour and its binary alternating interlacement matrix over F₂. The feasible absolute switch sets form the twist by its setting vector of the delta-matroid of nonsingular principal submatrices. Section 4.3 of Geelen, Iwata and Murota describes the Eulerian delta-matroid with arbitrary reference transitions. Their linear delta-parity algorithm minimizes, over a feasible set, the number of prescribed pairs it splits. Its zero optimum imposes equality on each pair. For unconstrained single switches use two disjoint copies of the represented delta-matroid, pair corresponding free elements between copies and retain the constrained pairs separately in both copies. Any solution duplicates, and a solution in the doubled system restricts to an original solution. The matrix for the doubled system is block diagonal, with the combined twist. In characteristic two the matrix required by the even-case algorithm is symmetric with zero diagonal, as the interlacement matrix is. The augmenting-path algorithm in §5.2 takes O(v⁴) field operations for v ground elements. Over F₂ these scalar operations are constant cost; building the Euler circuit and its matrix is polynomial. [Geelen, Iwata and Murota, §§3, 4.3 and 5.2](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~jfgeelen/Publications/deltamatroidparity.pdf)

This gives a deterministic polynomial-space decision procedure using O*(2^h₄) time, where h₄ counts residual cycles of length at least four. Since these cycles are vertex-disjoint, h₄≤N/4. The parity algorithm addresses feasibility. A minimum-weight choice needs an additional weighted argument. I found no primary text explicitly stating the O*(2^(N/4)) result for arbitrary two-out directed HC and have not established a best-known ranking.
