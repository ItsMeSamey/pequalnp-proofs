# Certifying deletion at fixed proof degree

Fix D≥2. The native binary polynomial system pins three inputs to one, includes their zero-output equations, all free zero-product guards and Boolean field axioms. Formal degree-D polynomial calculus permits variable multiplication only when the parent has degree at most D−1. At fixed D one can compute refutability in polynomial time: maintain the span in monomials through degree D, repeatedly multiplying the entire degree-below-D subspace by each variable. Boolean normalization is valid at the stated formal degree. The [cubic checker](bounded_cubic_pin_pc.py) implements D=3.

Test the original graph. If refuted, return a verifiable NO proof. Otherwise scan its free vertices once, deleting v when the induced graph without it remains unrefuted. Once a vertex is retained, its deletion refutation remains valid after any later deletions: restrict their variables to zero so discarded guards vanish and other generators specialize exactly. The final core K stays unrefuted and every one-free-vertex deletion of K refutes. At most n+1 calls to the degree tester are needed before optional certificate verification. Elimination provenance can be retained for the proof identities used.

Evaluate the word selecting all of K. A legal word is a YES witness on the original graph, with zero at every removed site. Otherwise K is a genuine NO: any proper witness omitted some free vertex and would survive in its vertex-deleted graph. Thus K certifies failure of universal degree-D completeness even if the starting graph had unknown truth. The original graph may still be YES in this last case.

In the last case K has exact minimum formal degree D+1. For each free vertex i, lift every row g of the degree-D proof on z_i=0 to (1+z_i)g. For a restricted generator the lift agrees with the original generator times 1+z_i modulo Boolean field equations, in degree at most three: the vanished part of the linear or quadratic generator was z_i times a polynomial of degree at most one. Other Boolean axioms lift the same way. Sums lift linearly, and a permitted multiplication in the restricted proof now has formal product degree at most D+1. The refutation ending at one therefore derives 1+z_i in K. After deriving these literals for every free i, reduce any equation violated at the full word to its constant all-one value using the derived linear assignments with multipliers of degree at most one. The final proof has degree at most max(D+1,2)=D+1, and K was unrefuted at D.

The test extracts a sharp degree gap whenever the relaxation gives a false positive on a genuine NO. If no degree-D counterexample exists, it instead decides every original graph and constructs all requested positive witnesses. Finite trials do not establish the premise. Even universal cubic completeness would only settle this graph language; a P-versus-NP conclusion requires a separate reduction from an NP-complete language.
