# How many free components can carry a hard three-pin refutation?

Let G be a simple graph with three named pins P. Fix their selection bits to one. Every degree-at-least-three NO instance (minimum formal binary polynomial-calculus degree at least three) has a unique connected component of G−P which can send any nonzero pin-degree contribution. Its attainable contributions span the entire even-weight plane in three bits. Deleting the other components preserves the exact minimum degree. In particular any vertex-deletion-minimal NO surviving the cubic relaxation has connected G−P and full syndrome span there.

## Constructing the component spans

Put B_i∈F₂³ equal to the vector of pin neighbors of free vertex i and c_i=1·B_i. Write A for the free adjacency. The required word x satisfies the local guards x_i((Ax)_i+c_i)=0 and the pin equations Σ_i B_i x_i=d, where d records the parities inside the three pins. Its weight is even. For a connected free component K let S_K be the set of outputs Σ_{i∈K} B_i x_i on assignments obeying its guards alone. It contains zero. Handshaking on K gives Σ_{i∈K}c_i x_i=0, hence S_K lies in the even plane E.

The span of the legal selections on K is precisely the single parity space c|_K·x=0, interpreted as the whole space when c|_K=0. Each vertex with c_i=0 gives a singleton. For any two vertices with c_i=1 take a shortest path between them, split it at all other c_i=1 vertices, and retain the induced segments. Each segment is legal. Adding the segment indicators and suitable singletons gives exactly the two desired endpoints. Choosing one reference odd vertex builds a polynomial-size list of genuine selections spanning the space, including components with fewer than two odd vertices. This is the [single-readout path-span lemma](symmetric-single-row-p3.md). Its image H_K under B is the computable span of S_K; store actual representatives for the generating outputs. Any linear row ℓ annihilating H_K has ℓ·B_i=λ_K c_i for every i in K, with a fixed λ_K∈F₂. Consequently its output zero equation on K is derived by taking λ_K times the sum of the original quadratic guards there, at formal degree two.

## The plane sum and proof degree

The components are independent before imposing pin outputs. If every H_K has dimension at most one, each S_K equals H_K. Their sum contains d exactly when the instance is YES. Witnesses use the stored representatives. On a NO instance choose ℓ annihilating the sum but nonzero on d. Adding the componentwise degree-two identities to the corresponding linear combination of pin equations refutes the instance in degree two.

If some H_K has dimension two, its polynomial-size list including zero has at least three distinct elements of the four-element plane E. If another component can send a nonzero value, its list including zero has at least two elements. For each desired d, the first list and d plus the second must intersect. The two representatives therefore satisfy the pins with every other selection zero. This remains valid if the two components have arbitrary size and unrestricted internal adjacencies.

The only remaining case has one two-dimensional H_K and all other H_J=0. All three coordinates of the pin contribution from every such J vanish by the degree-two component identities. Thus the residual graph on P∪K has the same witnesses after projection, and any witness there lifts by zeros. Any residual proof of degree D lifts in degree at most max(D,2) by deriving its pin equations from the original rows and those identities. Conversely zero substitution on the removed components gives the residual system directly. In degree at least two equality of the refutation thresholds follows. If the original graph was unrefuted at a fixed D≥2 then the residual is unrefuted also. A degree-D refutation after deleting any one vertex of an ignored component would restrict to a refutation after deleting all of them. This proves the core assertion.

This analysis also excludes a direct proof-degree amplification by taking a disjoint union of free gadgets attached only at the same three pinned sites. At most one active component from such a union can carry a NO above degree two. Connections through shared free sites fall outside this argument.

## The hard component needs physical pin cut rank three

In fact H_K=L_K∩E, where L_K=span{B_i : i∈K}. The local parity condition defining the span is exactly 1·Bx=0, so every preimage of an even word in L_K has the required parity. If H_K=E but dim L_K=2 then L_K=E and every c_i vanishes. This reduces to two readouts on a pure all-even component. Here a NO also has a formal degree-two proof. For completeness, if d=0 choose the empty word. A vertex colored d, or two nonadjacent vertices of the other two nonzero colors a,b, supplies a witness. Otherwise the a,b classes are completely joined. If a connected zero-colored component touches both classes, a shortest a-to-b path using only neutral internal vertices, found with the joining a–b edges omitted, closes to an induced cycle of color d. If one color class is empty the linear pin rows already refute. In the remaining NO case take the shore consisting of the a vertices and all neutral components touching that class. Only the complete a–b join crosses. Summing guards on this shore gives the product of the two color-class selection parities. Both parities are one by the pin equations since a+b=d. Multiplying those linear pin consequences by affine forms gives the contradiction at degree two. The graph criterion is the earlier [two-readout even support/cut theorem](two-readout-even-p3.md).

Thus an unrefuted degree-two NO has one free component with physical pin-neighbor rank three; all other components send zero. After conditioning any other free vertex outside that component to be selected as a fourth pin, if the component remains connected and unchanged internally, its old three-output projection still has linear span of dimension at least two: its new local span is the kernel of at most one linear parity condition on its selections, and B had rank three. This rules out completely switching off the hard carrier by conditioning a single external articulation. It does not give a solution when that articulation splits the carrier itself.
