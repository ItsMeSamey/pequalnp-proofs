# Black-box SAT for functions of few hidden quadratics

Let a Boolean circuit of size s on q bits compute F(x)=h(Q₁(x),…,Q_ℓ(x)), where each Q_i is a degree-at-most-two polynomial over GF(2) and h is arbitrary. Given an upper bound ℓ, deterministic black-box SAT, including finding a satisfying input, takes 2^{O(ℓ)} poly(q,s) time. The algorithm queries the circuit on a universal list; it needs neither the feature polynomials nor the outer table.

For a reachable feature value a, its fiber indicator over GF(2) is the product of 1+Q_i+a_i. This nonzero Boolean polynomial has degree at most 2ℓ, so its density is at least 2^{-2ℓ}. Indeed for a nonconstant multilinear polynomial of degree d choose a variable in a degree-d monomial. Its derivative is nonzero of degree d−1. On every input where the derivative equals one, exactly one of the two extensions has polynomial value one. Induction gives density at least 2^{-d}.

Over the reals the same fiber indicator is the average, over u in GF(2)^ℓ, of the signs (−1)^{u·a+u·Q(x)}. The absolute coefficients in this displayed expansion sum to one. Every phase is quadratic. Viola's Theorem 2 gives quadratic-sign error at most 16√ε for the XOR of two independent ε-biased spaces. Choose ε=2^{−4ℓ−12}, making the error strictly smaller than the density of every nonempty fiber. Consequently the resulting support hits every nonempty fiber simultaneously, including any accepted by h. Uniform explicit small-bias generators need O(log(q+2)+log(1/ε)) seed bits and polynomial time per generated point in q and 1/ε. Enumerating all seed pairs gives the asserted time. A constant circuit on zero inputs can be evaluated directly. [Viola, Theorem 2](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~viola/papers/d.pdf) · [Alon, Goldreich, Håstad and Peralta](https://johanhastad.se/almostindep.pdf)

## Exact residual complexity versus decision

In the previous construction F=h(Mx), the number of hidden linear forms ℓ is O(log q), and restricting coordinates leaves affine forms. The algorithm above therefore decides each such residual in polynomial time even though every k-free residual has circuit complexity Ω_B(k log q), where k=ceil(q/√log q). [Residual construction and gate budget](october-next-gate-scale.md)

For sufficiently large q, replace F by F′=F XOR the product of all q input bits. The full monomial changes one point. The original degree is at most ℓ<q, so F′ has degree q. Any representation by d quadratic features and an arbitrary Boolean outer function has degree at most 2d, by substituting into its multilinear outer polynomial. Thus F′ requires at least ceil(q/2) quadratic features. The change costs O(q) gates on the full cube, which fits the original B q log q cap by starting at B/2. After pinning q−k bits the added term is either zero or one k-fold product. Reversing this change costs O(k) gates, so every k-free residual of F′ still needs Ω_B(k log q) gates. The global feature lower bound need not hold separately on every residual.

There is also a robust witness procedure in this example. Every original k-free residual is nonconstant by the gate bound and each occupied affine fiber has at least 2^{k−ℓ} members. Thus it has at least two satisfying points for large q. For any such residual G, query its one-point modification G′ on the two restricted hitting sets for each choice of one coordinate and each of its two possible values. Two distinct original satisfying points differ somewhere; the two corresponding restrictions both have original witnesses in their hitting sets. At most one queried witness was changed, so some query to G′ succeeds. This tests the modified residual itself in polynomial time. The same argument applies to the entire function when it has at least two original witnesses. It does not promise detection of a lone new satisfying point created in an originally unsatisfiable function. Exact function-compression lower bounds and the degree obstruction alone therefore give no decision lower bound.
