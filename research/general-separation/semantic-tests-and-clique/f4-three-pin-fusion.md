# Constructing full words with three prescribed symbols in a Hermitian self-dual code over GF(4)

Given a generator matrix of a GF(4)-linear Hermitian self-dual code C on n coordinates, one can decide in deterministic polynomial time whether a codeword is everywhere nonzero and takes any three prescribed nonzero values on three distinct labeled coordinates. One can construct an original codeword. The proof uses a two-site shortening followed by the [all-even two-readout algorithm](two-readout-even-p3.md). It does not require all constant words in the input code. The normalized nonsplit graph codes from [the site algebra](connected-site-endomorphism-f4.md) are a special case.

Let a,b,c be the respective required symbols; reorder the first two positions and call the third j. The line N={(s,bs/a)} in the first two coordinates equals its Hermitian perpendicular, since every nonzero element has norm one. Intersect C with the condition that the first two coordinates belong to N, and puncture those coordinates. Denote the resulting code by C'. Its generator matrix, preimage basis and kernel are obtained by elimination over GF(4).

Write n=2m. If (N,0) lies in C, orthogonality implies that every codeword projects into N. Puncturing drops one dimension and each fiber is a full copy of N. If (N,0) is not contained in C, the ratio condition has rank one on C: otherwise (N,0) would be orthogonal to C and hence contained in C. Puncturing the constrained code is then injective. In both cases C' has length 2m−2 and dimension m−1. It is Hermitian self-orthogonal since each removed pair lies in an isotropic line. Therefore it is Hermitian self-dual.

In the injective case, the first value in the unique lift of w∈C' is a computable linear functional f(w). Seek a full word of C' satisfying the one homogeneous field equation f(w)=(a/c)w_j. Full support ensures w_j≠0. After lifting, multiply by c/w_j, which restores all three prescribed values without introducing zeros. In the full-fiber case there is no field equation: select any full word, scale to satisfy the third pin and choose the desired first pair in its fiber. In particular the answer is not determined merely by linear consistency at all three pins.

## Full support with one field equation

Every Hermitian self-orthogonal GF(4)-linear codeword has even Hamming weight, since its self-pairing sums one per nonzero position in characteristic two. In binary coordinates for GF(4), the trace of its Hermitian pairing gives the sitewise symplectic form. C' has binary dimension equal to its length, so it is a binary Lagrangian. By invertible local binary maps and binary row operations put it in a standard graph chart (x,Ax) with A symmetric and zero-diagonal. For clarity, if projection onto the first bits is not bijective, choose a nonzero row-space vector with all first bits zero. Its second bits define a dependence on the first-bit columns by isotropy; any nonzero position i in this dependence is redundant among these columns. Swapping the two bits at i removes that redundant column and introduces a new independent one, since evaluation on the chosen row distinguishes it from the old span. Repeat until full rank. Isotropy gives symmetry, and local shears adding x_i to y_i clear the diagonal. Record inverses for lifting. This is the standard additive graph-code representation. [Danielsen, graph-code construction](https://www.codetables.de/larsed/nonbinary.pdf)

Local invertible maps preserve the support of each word. In the resulting graph each generator at i has weight 1+deg(i), hence every degree is odd. Now (x,Ax) has full support precisely when z=1+x induces only even degrees: for z_i=1 we need (Ax)_i=1, and Ax=1+Az. The single GF(4)-linear equation on w, after inversion of the chart and substitution x=1+z, consists of at most two binary affine readouts of z. Use the constructive two-readout induced-even algorithm, invert the local maps, and then perform the field scaling in original coordinates. Verify the constructed word against the original generator span, all n nonzero tests, and its three actual pinned coordinates. Basis changes in the middle need not commute with GF(4) scaling. Every step takes polynomial time.

In the nonsplit graph application, local site swaps put the graph word into a normalized Hermitian self-dual code containing every constant word; the formerly forbidden (1,1) site symbol is unchanged by the swaps. Adding the constant forbidden symbol on every coordinate takes admissibility to full support. Translate each pin by that constant after its site swap. Its image is nonzero, so the theorem applies. Invert the translation and the swaps to recover the original pinned even support.

## Controls and scope

For the classical [6,3,4] hexacode, every three-coordinate projection is surjective by the dual distance. Even weights leave possible nonzero weights four and six. Each coordinate vanishes on 15 nonzero codewords, so counting zero incidences gives 45 words of weight four and 18 of weight six. Each triple is an information set, and exactly 18 of its 27 nonzero triples extend to a full word. The polynomial procedure tests which ones do. The unrestricted full-weight problem includes incidence kernels of cubic graphs, where a full GF(4) flow is exactly a three-edge coloring. Those kernels do not generally satisfy this Hermitian self-duality hypothesis.
