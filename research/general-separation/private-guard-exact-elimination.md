# Recovering and eliminating the private guard triples

Take the strict phase formula of private-parity-guard-portfolio-audit.md, including an attachment containing each core and marker variable. Input here is the decoded clause list, possibly with renamed variables, independent sign changes and permuted clauses and literals. Consider the phase list before any other inference that could delete occurrences. Literal ordering plays no role. Deduplicating exact repeats from the core does not affect the gadget certificate.

Enumerate unordered triples of variables. Keep a triple when each member occurs in four distinct rows, the union of their incident rows consists of six ternaries, each such row has exactly two distinct triple members and one outside variable, and precisely two distinct outside variables occur. All genuine private triples pass. Conversely every graph variable occurs in at least nine distinct gadget rows, three contributed by each edge at that cubic vertex. Thus no candidate contains a graph variable. Every core or marker variable y has an attachment with y and two graph variables. A candidate containing y would meet that row in exactly one position, contradicting its certificate. Finally a private variable a has precisely four distinct neighbors: its two siblings and the two graph endpoints. Both endpoints must remain outside. Only two external variables are permitted, so both siblings must be inside. Consequently the recorded candidates are exactly the intended private triples. Their six-row sets are pairwise disjoint and no private variable is a boundary of another. Record all candidates before modifying any clauses. On a general CNF the same batch rule is sound whenever both disjointness conditions are verified; a recognizer may otherwise decline or choose a certified noninterfering subcollection.

For each assignment to the two boundary variables, substitute its bits into the six rows. The resulting formula on the three private variables has width at most two. Solve it and retain one extension for each feasible boundary tuple. In the original orientation, boundary 11 requires both halves; together they impose a=b=c and a≠c. The other three boundary tuples 00,10,01 extend by 000,111,000. Independent sign changes permute this table and preserve its unique forbidden tuple. Replace the six rows by the binary clause blocking this tuple. This is an existential equivalence and permits direct original witness recovery.

Each path or attachment row contains both literals of the blocking clause for one of its graph edges. Signed subsumption therefore removes every such row regardless of the renamed coordinates. What remains is the disjoint union of binary graph clauses, the marker clause and the original core (up to harmless normalization). The graph clauses are satisfiable, even without knowing the signing, by ordinary 2SAT. A lone marker clause has an immediate model. Without labels identifying the original core, simply split connected components and remove satisfiable width-two and lone-clause components, retaining their assignments. An accidentally tractable component of the core can be removed the same way. Contradictory components give a sound NO answer. Otherwise the unsolved residual has no more variables or clauses than the original core. No particular graph-variable coloring or list of marker identities is necessary. Lift any residual witness through the component assignments and then the private tables; clause verification on the decoded input checks it. The certified cycle phase maps subsequently return a model of the positive-star/negative-conflict source.

For V variables, R rows and explicit input bit length B, testing all O(V³) triples by full row scans costs O(V³ poly(B)) deterministic bit time and polynomial space. Processing a passing triple uses four constant-size 2SAT problems. Signed subsumption, component finding, 2SAT and final reconstruction have polynomial bit cost. The argument describes a preprocessing algorithm with an independently verifiable certificate; it supplies no general polynomial clock for the remaining arbitrary core.
