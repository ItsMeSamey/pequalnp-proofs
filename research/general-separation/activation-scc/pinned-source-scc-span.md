# Source component span under a varying right side

In x_i((Ax)_i+t_i)=0, let C be a source strongly connected component of the row-to-read graph and D its remainder. No row of D reads C. Every genuinely feasible downstream y extends with u=0 on C. Consequently the full feasible span is U direct-sum span(Y), where U is the span of projections of actual feasible words onto C. The projected basis is never assumed to consist of genuine zero-downstream assignments.

Suppose A_CD y is nonzero for some feasible y. For every nonzero functional β on C, append an artificial pinned root with equality βu=1, no incoming arcs. It reaches all C vertices. The shortest-path theorem in one-readout-canonical-rhs.md leaves at most one rejected local right side. Of t_C and t_C+A_CD y at least one differs from its candidate, keeping the root RHS equal to 1. Recover the corresponding genuine u by that theorem and concatenate with 0 or y respectively. Hence U is full. Recover a basis by testing functionals annihilating previous local projections. Keep full genuine lifts plus zero-source extensions of downstream basis words. A traced downstream basis finds a changing y by linearity.

If A_CD annihilates the downstream span, all feasible downstream words induce t_C. Thus solutions are the Cartesian product with the isolated C solution family. Processing the condensation from sinks to sources, apart from polynomial work, constructive full-span recovery only needs local single-readout decisions with witnesses on strongly connected components with zero downstream variation. For bounded such component size these can be solved exhaustively. Arbitrary isolated strongly connected local instances remain open.
