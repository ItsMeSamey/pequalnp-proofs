# Robust row-closure growth on genuine signed-star Latin images

Let G be a connected bounded-degree graph on N indices. Take variables A_i,B_i,C_i and triples (A_i,B_i,C_i) for each i, plus (A_i,B_j,C_j) for each oriented incidence ij of G. On each triple impose one all-positive and one all-negative clause. Interpret this 3-CNF by the exact isolated-clause single-Latin-block reduction with signed stars. There are O(N) source clauses. The source primal graph is connected, has bounded degree and contains G as a minor after contracting A_i to B_i and discarding C_i, so bounded-degree expanders can be used as G. The instances are satisfiable.

Every prescription on at most two distinct source bits extends. For prescriptions in one part, set the other two parts to opposite constants. For prescriptions in different parts, set the two parts to their specified constants and the remaining part opposite if they agree. Consequently even the strongest binary-clause background true on all source models has no nontautological clause after the signed equality stars and pins have been substituted. This observation concerns original variables only.

Order the three occurrence rails of each source clause arbitrarily before routing. Removing true-pinned Latin rows/leaves then gives exactly one surviving binary-clause requirement D_i per source clause conditioned on its first signed literal being false. Combine any identical signed first literals into one common row requirement by taking the conjunction. Positive first literals contribute only positive two-literal clauses, negative first literals only negative ones. The merged requirement of any chosen positive diagonal is satisfiable and nonconstant. Its variables are neighbors of its chosen first source variable in the primal graph.

Pick a constant fraction of the diagonal triples whose closed one-step primal neighborhoods are pairwise disjoint: each diagonal has constant size, the primal degree is bounded, and greedy packing removes O(1) candidates per step. Whatever rail is first in each positive diagonal, their merged positive row requirements lie on disjoint source coordinates. For any subset S of these rows, the conjunction of its requirements fails to imply any omitted packed row: satisfy its members and independently falsify the omitted nonconstant requirement. Therefore the collective 2-CNF implication-closure algorithm of latin-row-closure.md has at least 2^{Ω(N)} closed sets, even with all valid original binary consequences and after merging equal signed controls. Arbitrary orientation here means choice of first rail per original clause; the claim does not quantify over general nonlinear recodings or newly introduced auxiliaries. This is a bound on enumeration, with no lower bound on deciding these satisfiable instances.
