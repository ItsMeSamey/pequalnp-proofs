# Single-row projection always accepts the original circuit pin chain

In the Circuit-SAT-to-P3 construction of single-unequal-p3.md, retain the exceptional source block imposing r_1=0 and retain the other non-pin blocks unchanged. At each pin center r_i replace its two checks (r_{i+1}, E_i) by any one binary linear combination, still checked only when r_i=0. Interpret the terminal successor as identically zero. The resulting system is feasible for every choice of these projections and every source circuit, including an unsatisfiable circuit computing x AND (NOT x).

In fact, assume merely that all non-pin blocks can pass together with any individually selected E_i=0 and that they do not read the pin centers. Let j be the first index with zero effective successor coefficient, treating the terminal as such. Choose the non-pin assignment witnessing E_j=0. Starting at r_1=0, each i<j with r_i=0 uniquely determines a legal value of r_{i+1} from the projected equality, whatever E_i is. If the successor is one, set all subsequent pins to one; their guards are inactive. If j is reached with r_j=0, the jth equality holds because its successor coefficient is zero and E_j=0. Set every remaining pin to one. The exceptional selector always extends once r_1=0; free and determined private leaves can be supplied according to their coefficients.

For an AND/NOT circuit with distinct input/output wire names in each NOT gate, set both fresh AND selectors s and h to one in every gate. This disables their physical guards. Each master block permits arbitrary wire bits. Each single E_j is individually consistent: an alias fixes one master input, a NOT equation fixes an ordinary affine relation of distinct wires, and the terminal output equation sets a free master to one. Circuit acyclicity ensures these wire distinctions. This supplies the hypothesis even when all equations together are contradictory.

The argument applies to every input-dependent choice of local projections and hence to random choices as well, once fixed. It does not apply to global rewiring, affine recoding of participating centers, new nonlocal constraints, or arbitrary width changes.
