# What signed Horn charts cover on the actual SAT reduction

Consider clauses on distinct source variables with falsity bits f_i, three selectors p_i, odd syndrome p1+p2+p3=1 and conflicts f_i p_i=0. Keep source bits as coordinates and allow any invertible affine two-coordinate parametrization per clause. The three selector normals are the three nonzero private directions. Two become coordinate bits up to constants, and the remaining p_k becomes their sum up to a constant. Any one k can play that role.

For the conflict on p_k, the forbidden flat has a fixed source bit and two forbidden private tuples differing in both private coordinates. By the active-zero criterion for a signed Horn chart, the shifted forbidden source bit must be one and the two forbidden private tuples must be 01 and 10. Equivalently the common shifted origin must have f_k=0 and p_k=0. By the odd syndrome exactly one of the other selectors is active there. Each of their two conflicts on independent single coordinates cannot forbid shifted tuple 00. Therefore the source literal corresponding to the other active selector must also be true. At least two literals of every source clause hold at the common polarity.

Conversely choose any source assignment making at least two literals true per clause. Choose k at one such literal, making p_k the summed selector, and choose exactly one other true literal's selector active. With this configuration as shifted origin the two single-selector conflicts forbid nonzero tuples and the summed-selector conflict forbids precisely 101 and 110 in source-first order. These all give Horn clauses. Thus existence of this entire family of charts is exactly a 2SAT question: require every pair of literals in each input clause to contain a true one.

The four clauses forbidding all four even assignments on three variables have four satisfying odd assignments. Each is adjacent to a forbidden even assignment, whose clause has just one true literal. Therefore the input has no chart in this family despite being satisfiable. These conclusions concern private affine charts and shared original source polarity only; mixing sources with selectors or changing the encoding remains open.
