# Exact shared-star conditioning on recognized coordinate flags

For G=F2^d of size q retain the original ordered payload pair (x,y) as the address of every surviving Latin clause after pins, signed-star substitution and tautology simplification, including binary clauses. Reject a false constant clause. Give each extra background 2CNF clause one explicitly chosen fixed address; charge all its representatives there. Unused original bits may be filled arbitrarily consistently with the star/pin equations.

A cell fixes coordinates S of x and y to two specified patterns. A fresh coordinate j partitions its clauses into four child cells. The static separator T is exactly the representatives appearing in more than one child's clauses. Try every valuation of T consistent with the ancestor pins. At fixed valuation test all four children, propagating all inherited pins. Their unassigned names are disjoint across children, so the conjunction is exact and the recovered assignments merge. Return NO only after exhausting the compatible valuations. With all coordinates fixed, the cell has at most one native clause plus arbitrary background 2CNF. Trying a true literal of the native clause and using 2SAT thus gives polynomial time, even if many background clauses share the address. Always reconstruct and replay in the original stars and sites.

Precompute a static plan on every possible cell. There are 5^d since each coordinate is free or fixed to one of four ordered bit pairs. For each free j compute T from the addressed input, including all background names. Child scores are already computed. Minimize 1+2^|T|(1+sum(child scores)) over splits and optionally the brute leaf score 1+2^v for v representatives. A polynomial terminal solver has unit score with a common polynomial per-node factor. Upper bounds computed before conditioning remain valid under ancestor pins; no memoization of feasibility across different valuations is assumed. Score arithmetic has polynomial bit length since a brute leaf is always available and all intermediate products use exponents at most linear in the number of representatives. The table and all scans cost poly(q,input). The search with this plan explicitly charges all disjunctions and costs at most the root score times a common polynomial.

In particular an adaptive coordinate flag with all separators at most w yields (4·2^w)^d poly(q,input) time. Existence of such a flag can be recognized by the analogous min/max recurrence with full-address leaves. This is standard SAT cutset conditioning with a polynomial search over geometric layouts in a supplied coordinate chart. Enumerating all linear bases instead costs up to 2^{d²}. No claim that projected alternatives coalesce is necessary.

Example: in the two-axis induced-clause embedding use addresses ((i,0),(0,i)); suppose consecutive clauses share one variable each, with no other shared variables and arbitrary literal signs. Splitting index bits most significant first in the first axis creates intervals, with at most one shared variable per split. Physical clause signs can be obtained from signed stars. There may be arbitrarily many genuinely ternary clauses. This illustrates the geometric width condition; generic tractability of path-shaped clause systems is classical.
