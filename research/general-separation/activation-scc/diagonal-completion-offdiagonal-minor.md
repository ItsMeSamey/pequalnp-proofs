# Constructing a low-rank completion of a free diagonal

All matrices here are square and binary, without a symmetry requirement. Let r be the minimum rank obtained by selecting all diagonal entries; start with arbitrary given off-diagonal entries. Maintain disjoint row and column lists I,J with C=A[I,J] nonsingular. Initially C is empty. For fresh distinct indices i,j outside both lists, the Schur value A[i,j]−A[i,J]C^{-1}A[I,j] uses only given entries. If one is nonzero append i to I and j to J, keeping the minor nonsingular. Stop when none exists. With s=|I|=|J|, every completion has rank at least s.

Choose arbitrary diagonal values on I union J and form B=A[:,J]C^{-1}A[I,:] (zero if s=0). The skeleton uses no other diagonal values. On the remaining vertices set the diagonal of A to the corresponding diagonal of B. Then A−B vanishes on rows I, on columns J, and on the full square outside I union J. Its support is contained in the union of rows J and columns I. Therefore rank(A−B)≤2s and rank(A)≤3s≤3r. Gaussian elimination gives a polynomial constructive approximation without knowing r.

If an exact optimum is needed, test a budget k beginning at s. When k≥s the constructed A has rank at most 3k. For any feasible rank-at-most-k completion A', rank(A−A') is the number of differing diagonal positions and is at most 4k. Seek a set of at most 4k flips from A. At any current matrix of rank>k find a nonsingular (k+1)-by-(k+1) minor with independent row and column index sets, which may overlap. Every successful completion must disagree on some diagonal in their intersection. Branch on each such not-yet-flipped index, without flipping an index twice on a path. If the intersection offers none, reject the branch. A valid target can be followed until found. Budget-zero needs only the all-zero off-diagonal test. A fixed budget has time (k+1)^{4k}poly(n) for k≥1, polynomial space by depth-first search. The approximation alone suffices to replace a supplied rank-r diagonal in the one-readout SCC solver with a factor-three loss in the exponent. These elementary construction claims do not assert priority over matrix-completion literature.

**Prior algorithm and improved constant.** Eugene Kogan's Theorem 2 and Remark 3 give a constructive rank-≤2r completion in O(n^4) for the same arbitrary binary square matrices. Lemma 5 also gives the greedy nonsingular diagonal used in the affine support sections. Using that prior algorithm, the one-readout SCC bound is 2^{2r}poly(n) with no diagonal supplied; the exact-search branching depth above becomes 3k. Kogan's separate Theorem 1 gives O(n^{k+4}) exact fixed-threshold decision. No priority claim is made for the minor-branching improvement without a broader literature check.
