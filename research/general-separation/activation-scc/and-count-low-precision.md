# Low-precision counting and randomized witness recovery by multiplicative gate count

Input is a Boolean circuit on n original bits with m fanin-two AND gates and arbitrary affine binary gates (XOR, NOT, constants). Contract affine gates by forward evaluation of their coefficient vectors on the N=n+m joint bits (x,z), where z_i is the named output of AND i. Its inputs a_i,b_i are affine functions of x and earlier z. Require final affine output to equal one. Extra affine equations on raw or joint bits may be imposed. A satisfying original assignment extends uniquely before imposing the output pin, because the AND equations are triangular. All complexity bounds allow polynomial dependence on explicit circuit size.

For actual Boolean values,
[z_i=a_i b_i]=(1−z_i)+(2z_i−1)a_i b_i.
Expand the product of these gates by T, the sites taking the first term. Together with the final pin and l extra affine equations, the T term sums an affine sign over a binary affine solution space: impose z_i=0 for i in T, a_i=b_i=1 elsewhere, and all pins. Use the sign product of (2z_i−1) on i outside T. Gaussian elimination gives zero if inconsistent or if the sign character is nonconstant there; otherwise the sum is plus or minus 2^{N−rank}. There are at most 2m+1+l−|T| equations, including dependencies and constants. Therefore modulo 2^p one may omit every T with |T|≥m−n+1+l+p. This includes a zero answer if the cutoff is nonpositive. Enumerate the remaining subsets in polynomial workspace. For p=1 sign need not be evaluated. This computes an exact residue, without unknown cancellation assumptions.

Fix an integer p between zero and n. If the number S of accepting raw assignments exceeds 2^p, O(2^{n−p}) independent uniform raw trials find a directly verified witness with constant probability. If 1≤S≤2^p, for each r from 1 through p+2 try independent random affine hashes H:F2^n→F2^r and impose H(x)=0. Include the case S=1. For r=ceil(log2 S)+1, the expected surviving count μ=S/2^r is in (1/4,1/2]. Because random affine maps yield pairwise independent images on distinct raw inputs (no full-rank assumption), the probability of exactly one survivor is ≥μ−μ²≥3/16. For each trial use the p=1 residue algorithm to compute parity of the survivors and parity after adding x_j=1 separately for each j. In an isolated branch those n parities equal the unique solution bits. Accept only after direct original-circuit verification, and repeat a fixed number of trials for standard constant success probability. Inputs with no solution can never be falsely accepted. The algorithm does not require knowing S. A randomly chosen large-count witness and an isolated raw witness are ordinary original inputs, not internal certificates.

For l≤p+3 in the parity probes, the number of enumerated subsets is at most sum_{j≤m−n+p+4} binom(m,j), with the usual empty/full conventions. The uniform one-sided randomized witness clock is
poly(input) [ 2^{n−p} + sum_{j≤m−n+p+4} binom(m,j) ],
in polynomial workspace. In particular if m≤n use p=floor(0.227 n): the entropy bound H2(0.227)<0.774 gives 2^{0.774 n+o(n)} time. For m=c n the analogous bound has exponent max(1−alpha, c H2((c−1+alpha)/c)) when the binomial fraction lies between zero and one half. A constant saving extends to fixed c>1 whenever c H2((c−1)/c)<1. These are decision and witness bounds; exact integer counting at this exponent, deterministic isolation, and arbitrary n log n AND counts are not established. A circuit of O(n log n) total gates with at most n ANDs qualifies regardless of the number of affine operations.

The residue computation also gives deterministic decision under the explicit promise that S<2^p, by testing the residue. Do not use the unpinned cost bound unchanged for a growing sequence of self-restrictions. The independent one-bit parity probes above avoid accumulating pins. This is an algebraic counting construction with standard pairwise-independent isolation and requires comparison with prior algorithms for low-multiplicative-complexity SAT before any priority claim.
