# The pair-factor obstruction survives repeated section compilation

For every even m≥4, take the ordered circuit in the [common-source construction](standard-clock-section-tap-obstruction.md). On coordinates disjoint from u₁,…,uₘ construct the nonconstant function G=(¬t AND F(x)) OR (t AND b AND [x=0]). Then process every pair i<j once, always appending the three fresh ordered gates

    E = NAND(u_i,u_j),   J = NAND(P,E),   P' = NAND(J,J).

The incoming P is initially the output of G and subsequently the preceding P'. The final P' is the output. Its model count is (m+1)(2·#F+1). The following statement concerns the code of [compile_sections](section_syndrome_cnf.py) and the [tap factor extractor](linear_tap_separator.py).

**Claim.** After any finite sequence of successful section compilations, the arity remains h=m+r+2 and the quotient still has this very pair-chain suffix, up to renaming coordinates and wire addresses. The incoming function is still nonconstant and its preceding circuit uses none of the m designated primary coordinates. Every binary tap merge schedule on any resulting quotient has width Ω(m).

### A single compiler pass

Consider first an ordered representation having this suffix and a nonconstant disjoint initial circuit. Primary input IDs themselves may be interleaved. The compiler's profiles carry a slope on the current bank and a formal offset. A zero-slope otherwise-opaque NAND gets its own wire token as offset. An inverter toggles the constant offset bit. Before processing any bank's proposed outputs, the compiler fixes its dictionary of previously visible wires and uses this frozen dictionary to validate every token in a dependent output's residual offset.

Suppose a successful run names a chain gate in a bank, and take the first such bank. None of E,J,P' anywhere in the chain was previously visible. The two operands of E are distinct raw coordinates, so E cannot be an inverter or a four-NAND XOR. The XOR recognizer on J cannot find a shared internal NAND predecessor of its operands because E has only raw operands. On the self-NAND of J the inverter rule applies when J has a profile. Even if J has no profile, the XOR scan cannot rescue this gate: matching the two identical immediate branches would require E to be NAND(P,P) or P to be NAND(E,E). The first fails on distinct primary inputs; the second fails because P precedes E. The same tests apply at every step, including the initial P.

Consequently an E or J with a profile at this bank has zero slope and its own opaque token. A profiled P' has zero slope and the J token plus one. Otherwise the candidate lacks a profile altogether. With zero initial slope the compiler performs no pivot-row elimination: the original offset must already use only visible tokens. The required E or J token is absent in every case, even if the bank nominated that token elsewhere in the same list. Thus no chain gate can ever be an accepted nominated output.

The backward output walk therefore visits every J and E and reaches every u_i. It rejects an unmapped raw input. Each u_i must have been public or nominated in its own bank. Nomination before that bank has no profile. A later nomination of the as-yet-unmapped primary is a zero-slope token naming itself and fails the previous-visibility test. In its own bank the primary has a fresh unit slope, zero offset. All nonchain gates lie in the preceding circuit over disjoint variables, and all other nominated primaries have other unit slopes, so no other nominated row contains this unit direction. The compiler assigns a distinct quotient input directly to u_i.

In particular, mixing private bank memberships does not introduce dependencies into the disjoint initial circuit. For one bank, pivot elimination splits into the isolated u_i unit directions and the span of the other coordinates. An equation from the disjoint circuit never encounters a designated pivot or a designated offset token. Every previously visible token occurring there already has a mapped expression using only nondesignated quotient inputs, by induction over the banks. The newly emitted XORs and inverters for these residual offsets also use only nondesignated inputs. This includes intermediate or unused gates the compiler emits while constructing its output dictionary. The compiler finally copies the remaining live gates in increasing old address order, without interning or algebraically rewriting them. All newly built gates and the copied disjoint circuit precede E of the first pair. Every pair gate, join and self-inverter is copied and the designated inputs remain distinct. Hence the quotient has the claimed same ordered suffix.

Each verified section quotient has constant fiber size 2^(h−q) on q quotient bits. The odd model count forces q=h and the section map is bijective. More explicitly, after fixing the m carried u bits, the map on the other raw bits is onto the nondesignated quotient cube: surjectivity on full words and the identity on designated bits suffice. The initial output computed by the copied or mapped P agrees pointwise with the old P under this map by the cut equations and gate copying. Thus it remains nonconstant. These arguments reestablish all hypotheses at the next pass, regardless of new bank partitions, ranks, input ordering, or gate addresses.

### Consequences for the clocks

The [common-source proof](standard-clock-section-tap-obstruction.md) applies verbatim to the current suffix. Each pair's live NAND factor contains the two designated raw unit rows. A balanced cut of the pair factors in any binary merge tree has at least m/√3−O(1) shared independent unit rows. In particular the implementation enumerates exponentially many interface assignments or returns UNKNOWN under a smaller cap. Width is charged on the chosen final circuit, after all successful section passes. For the alternative full-schedule branch-CNF route, every emitted leaf retains at least h−b declared primaries after b input/gate guesses, and the schedule has 2^b cases. The October fixed-density envelope on these declared variables still does not give a polynomial clock in the original length for polynomially many guesses of branch assignments.

Certificate validation, construction of each quotient including its additional gates, and search or rejection of candidate schedules all take time. Any claim of a polynomial composition must pay their sum and control the total intermediate encoding lengths. Even granting all successful certificates for free leaves the obstruction above for these particular entry points. The conclusion permits arbitrary finite chains of the specified section compiler followed by one tap run on its literal output. It does not cover arbitrary recircuitization, exchanging factors for a different AMO encoding, or interleaving arbitrary semantic simplifications and adaptive branching with those passes. Directly factoring AMO still gives the independent count multiplier m+1.
