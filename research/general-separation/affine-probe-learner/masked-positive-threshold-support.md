# Supporting a positive threshold forecast after masking

Deciding whether a positive forecast has a genuine supporting assignment is NP-complete even at the strict crossing described below. The preceding labels are all positive, every committed forecast was zero, and one public assignment realizes every label. The source has full two-coordinate projections and full affine hull.

Given a formula φ on r bits, let m=r+3. Use source bits (t,u,v,x) in that order and set F=(¬t AND u AND v AND φ(x)) OR (t AND u AND v AND [x=1^r]). The all-ones word satisfies F. Zero and every one-hot word fail F. A model at or below the one-hot index j=2^(m−1) exists exactly when φ is satisfiable.

Compile F by the four-site mask to G on d=4m raw bits, with 3m free triple bits followed by m tail bits. On raw indices below 2^m, decoding is identity on the tail. The raw word w=2^m−1 is a public model. One source model suffices for the claimed projection and affine properties. [Four-site construction](sat-without-global-affine-or-binary-exclusions.md)

Use the query class consisting of G and ordinary comparisons T_k=[z≤k] on the raw integer. G accepts the all-ones raw input and rejects zero, so it is distinct from the comparisons. Start with all virtual pairs (z,b) except (0,1), since G(0)=0. Read G as b and comparisons as comparisons on z. Predict majority with fixed ties and condition on supplied labels. Each mistake at least halves the population of 2^(d+1)−1, so there are at most d mistakes on realizable streams in this query class. Counts require two intervals and an anchor subtraction.

The initial forecast on G is zero. Commit one. The virtual raw interval becomes [1,2^d−1]. For h=d−1,d−2,…,m the interval before the next crossing is [1,2^(h+1)−1]. Its strict threshold majority changes from zero to one between 2^h−1 and 2^h. The raw word 2^h has one triple bit set and zero tail. Its decoded source is zero or one-hot, hence G(2^h)=0. The single-history procedure commits T_(2^h−1) with label one. This is an error, w survives, and the new virtual interval is [1,2^h−1]. The procedure's bisection finds these indices because the virtual votes form a single ascending step; its artificial outer sentinels agree with that step. [Single-history procedure](positive-threshold-repair.md)

After 3m threshold errors the virtual interval is [1,2^m−1]. At j=2^(m−1), the positive forecast is strict, the preceding forecast is zero, and G(j)=0. A true survivor supports T_j=1 exactly when φ is satisfiable. Such a survivor has zero triple prefix, so it decodes to the same m-bit tail and satisfies F. Conversely any satisfying x yields the tail (0,1,1,x) with zero triple prefix. It lies below j and realizes all preceding labels. A proposed raw witness is checked against G, the history, and T_j in polynomial time. The entire transcript has polynomial encoding length. The procedure reaches this crossing on both YES and NO inputs of φ.

This verifies hardness for a particular forecast, with a public surviving model above the queried cutoff. One exact repair is to test satisfiability of the history conjoined with the proposed positive threshold and to override the forecast only upon proving emptiness. Each override is correct on all realizable continuations. The reduction identifies the cost of that support test. It gives no computational lower bound on other predictors for explicit circuits.
