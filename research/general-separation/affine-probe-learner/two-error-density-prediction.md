# Three forecasts decide SAT under a density guarantee

Let V be the set of actual h-bit assignments consistent with all feedback supplied so far. Call a forecast admissible if its label occurs on at least a 2^(−h/16) fraction of V. A deterministic uniform polynomial-time predictor that returns an admissible bit at the three realizable states below would put CircuitSAT in P. These states have zero, one and two prior mistakes. No lifetime mistake bound, exact majority computation, or behavior on inconsistent transcripts is assumed. The premise need only hold at widths h=12r+20 for integers r≥0. At these widths the fraction is below one half, so a qualifying bit always exists.

Given an r-input circuit φ, put g=b=r+2, m=1+g+r+b=3r+5 and h=4m. In most-significant-first order take the source word to be (t,q,x,p), with one selector bit t, g guard bits q, r variables x and b low bits p. Define

    F = [q=1^g] AND ((¬t AND φ(x)) OR (t AND [x=1^r] AND [p=1^b])).

Write A for the number of satisfying x. On the left the b low bits are free; on the right exactly the all-ones source word survives. Hence F has M=2^b A+1 models. Compile F by the [four-site NAND mask](odd_masked_circuit.py) to G on h raw bits. The upper 3m bits are mask triples and the lower m bits are the tail. For each fixed mask prefix, XOR by the corresponding offset bijects the tails with all source words. Thus each complete prefix block has exactly M models of G. In the zero-prefix block the offset vanishes. In particular the publicly known raw word w=0^(3m)1^m satisfies G for every φ.

1. **Ask G.** Its positive fraction on the whole cube is M/2^m. The guards alone give M/2^m ≤ 2^(−g), and g−m/4=(r+3)/4>0. The positive bit therefore falls strictly below 2^(−m/4)=2^(−h/16). The forecast must be zero. Supply the true label one from w.
2. **Ask U=[z≤2^m−1].** Among the surviving models of G, U retains exactly the complete zero-prefix block, one of 2^(3m) blocks of equal size. Its positive fraction is 2^(−3m), again strictly below the guarantee. The forecast must be zero. Supply one, also true at w.
3. **Ask T=[z≤2^(m−1)].** The surviving words are precisely the models of F in the zero-prefix block. All left models fall strictly below the cutoff. The cutoff word itself has its selector set and its guards zero. The planted right model is above it. The zero and one bins of T therefore have sizes **(1, 2^b A)**. If A=0 only bit zero is admissible. If A≥1 the zero fraction is at most 1/(1+2^b)<2^(−b)<2^(−m/4), since b=g. Only bit one is admissible. Return this final forecast as the SAT decision.

Both preceding feedback labels are genuine mistakes on the same public raw word, including when φ is unsatisfiable. The proof uses the inclusive comparison on original raw integers at both cutoffs. Neither a witness from the predictor nor a label for the third query is needed. Empty x at r=0 is handled by the usual true empty conjunction. The guard, padding, circuit conversion, mask decoder and two comparators have O(|φ|+r+1) gates and wires, with logarithmic address overhead. A common public cap can be computed directly from the three emitted gate lists. Building them and supplying the two feedback bits requires no advice or access to A.

The [one-wise feasibility converse](local-forecast-consistency.md) uses only positive support of the forecasted label, with repeated restarts and bit restrictions. Its SAT implication already requires no total mistake bound; a separate forward construction under P=NP attains the optimal mistake bound. The stronger density requirement here allows a single run with three forecasts and a planted survivor at every queried state. The [exact-majority construction](exact-tail-masked-majority.md) instead establishes hardness for a specified counting rule and uses 3m threshold mistakes to fix the mask prefix. Here one threshold fixes it because the positive fiber is too small for any admissible forecast at the second query.
