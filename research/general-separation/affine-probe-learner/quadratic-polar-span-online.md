# Learning quadratics with a low-dimensional space of restricted polar forms

Fix one evaluator on all lengths. A query supplies the squarefree coefficients of an arbitrary quadratic Boolean polynomial Q on V=F₂^h; its label is Q(s) for one fixed hidden seed s. Write B_Q(x,y)=Q(x+y)+Q(x)+Q(y)+Q(0) for its alternating polar form.

**Theorem.** Fix nonnegative integer budgets k,r. Suppose the mistaken queries on a prefix admit a common W*⊆V* of dimension at most k such that their polars restricted to H=ann(W*) span a space of dimension at most r. A deterministic learner makes at most h mistakes on every such prefix and spends 2^{O(kr+k²+k+r)}poly(h+1) bit operations per round on promised streams. Neither the common directions nor the surviving quadratic combinations are supplied. The new query may be any quadratic, regardless of the space already found from earlier mistakes. The clock is polynomial if kr+k²+r=O(log(h+2)).

**Discovering W.** Store only the mistakes with their true labels. Search a tree of subspaces W, starting at zero. At a node of dimension d let U=ann(W). Return W if the stored polar span on U has dimension at most r. Otherwise reject this node if d=k. In all other cases choose r+1 independent restricted polars and enumerate their nonzero binary combinations B. Skip individual combinations with rank 2u>2(k−d). Each remaining combination has u≥1. Choose any u+1 independent functionals in im B⊆U*. For every nonzero combination of these functionals choose one lift to V* by linear algebra, adjoin it to W, and recurse. If the whole search fails an arbitrary fixed default defines the learner.

To prove completeness, follow a node with W⊆W*. The chosen r+1 forms become dependent on H×H. Some tested nonzero B on U therefore vanishes there. The space U/rad B is nondegenerate alternating of dimension 2u. The image of H in this quotient is isotropic, hence has dimension at most u, while its codimension is at most dim(U/H)≤k−d. Thus u≤k−d. The space im B is the full dual of this quotient pulled back to U. Its intersection with ann_U(H) has dimension 2u−dim(image H)≥u. Any (u+1)-dimensional subspace of im B meets that intersection nontrivially. Every ambient lift of a good nonzero functional annihilates H, so it belongs to W* and increases d. Thus a successful branch exists.

There are at most (2^{r+1}−1)(2^{k−d+1}−1) children per node at depth d. Multiplication over d<k bounds the total number of nodes by 2^{O(kr+k²+k)}. At each node processing all candidate combinations adds at most 2^{r+1}poly(h+1) work on a promised prefix.

**Exact counts.** For each proposed label b, include the new equation Q(s)=b. Enumerate the 2^d assignments to a basis of the W returned by the search. Each cell is an affine translate of U. The restricted polars do not depend on the translate, and their span dimension after adding the new query is at most r+1. Row-reduce the polar coefficient vectors, applying all operations also to the full equations with their labels and affine offsets. Every zero polar row is an affine equation. Solve the affine system, discarding inconsistent cells. On the resulting affine solution space A, at most p≤r+1 quadratic equations f_i(s)=0 remain. Count their common solutions using

    2^(−p) ∑_{λ∈F₂^p} ∑_{s∈A} (−1)^(∑_i λ_i f_i(s)).

Parameterize A affinely and put the polar of the resulting quadratic in a symplectic basis plus its radical. In these coordinates there is a constant, a linear function on the radical, and terms xy+ax+by on disjoint pairs. Each pair contributes 2(−1)^(ab). A nonzero linear term on the radical gives zero; otherwise multiply by the radical size and the constant sign. All operations have polynomial bit cost for one quadratic. Quadratic counting is classical. [Ehrenfeucht and Karpinski, report 8543](https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/Zope/English/csreports/report_1990/report.html)

The total counts have at most h+1 bits and intermediate signed sums have O(h+r+k+1) bits. Predict the larger count, breaking ties arbitrarily. Every error at least halves the maintained version space and leaves the true seed in it. A putative (h+1)st error therefore cannot occur, and at most h stored rows enter each calculation before it. Each dense input row has O((h+1)²) bits. These bounds yield the stated clock. Correct rounds need no data retention.

**A fixed evaluator with an unrestricted stream.** Let h=3m+1 with distinct coordinates t,x₁,…,xₘ,a₁,…,aₘ,b₁,…,bₘ and put R=∑aⱼbⱼ. Allow every query t·L(x)+eR+ℓ(s), where L is an arbitrary linear form, e a bit and ℓ an arbitrary affine form. This single uniform evaluator has the h-mistake polynomial bound for every stream, as W=span(t) leaves all polars in span(B_R). Padding by at most two unused coordinates handles any positive h. The general evaluator receives the same queries in its ordinary coefficient encoding.

The polars of t x_i+R are independent as witnessed by the coefficients on t,x_i. Their full span therefore has dimension m. Each has rank 2m+2, so making even one affine on every cell of a common anchor requires codimension at least m+1. Any representation by linear measurements followed by a Boolean readout must span im B_Q: the measurements' joint kernel consists of translation symmetries of Q, all of which lie in rad B_Q. Bringing the residual measurement dimension down to two thus requires anchor dimension at least 2m for this family. Here k=r=1 suffices simultaneously.

For an affine product with independent linear factors, the restricted polar vanishes precisely when the restricted linear factors become dependent. Thus r=0 includes the earlier plane-cover theorem, with a different discovery cost. No super-h mistake bound against unrestricted polynomial learners or complexity-class separation follows.
