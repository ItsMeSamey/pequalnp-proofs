# How many adjacent forecast repairs can one genuine mistake require?

Use the blind predictor and prefix circuits from the companion proof. Take n≥2, N=2^n, z=0, and the explicit satisfiable formula F(x)=[x=N−1]. Before any feedback the blind predictor forecasts 0 on F, since virtual pairs with b=0 outnumber those with b=1 by one. Commit the true label 1 on F. The surviving pairs now have b=1 and u∈{1,…,N−1}. No tie occurs on any prefix query. The forecast at index j, from −1 through N−1, is zero for j<N/2 and one for j≥N/2. The sole true satisfying assignment is N−1.

Consider the following exact equivalence repair. For this fixed state, issue prefix forecasts through a union-find table on indices with a representative index in each class; ask the original forecast of the representative. When bisection finds a change at a false point j, use the direct test F(j)=0 to union precisely classes containing j−1,j, selecting either old representative, and rerun. This class interpretation preserves the true query labels. If a sentinel forecast changes, the outer solver may react and process new feedback; our bound concerns the work before the next such event. The companion's online predictor is otherwise unchanged.

Initially the forecast array is a single ascending step at j₀=N/2. After its first conflicting edge is united, all nonsingleton indices form one contiguous block containing that edge. Outside this block the original forecasts remain unchanged. If endpoints are untouched and there is another change, it lies at either edge adjacent to the block: every point to its left has forecast zero and every point to its right has forecast one. Union therefore expands that block by one index, regardless of which of the two old representatives is retained. The step still exists, so the only possible result before touching a sentinel or reaching N−1 is another false crossing. After t merges its eligible next crossing is at distance at most t from j₀. Reaching the true crossing N−1 costs at least N/2−1 earlier merges. Reaching the zero sentinel −1 likewise requires at least N/2 merges. Until then every additional failure costs no genuine prediction error. Thus this explicit formula alone requires at least 2^(n−1)−1 certified adjacent unions before a witness or a new feedback step, independently of representative choices.

The lower bound covers one-edge unions at the false crossing and does not charge arbitrary equivalent circuits to that procedure. Direct inspection of this formula of course supplies its satisfying assignment. For an opaque predicate, adding direct evaluations to arbitrary blind predictor transcripts costs N−1 worst-case evaluations simply to distinguish the zero predicate from an unknown singleton away from the anchor.
