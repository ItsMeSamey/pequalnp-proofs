# Exact raw range counts from an affine tail syndrome

Write a raw word in numerical order as a high prefix u followed by a w-bit tail t. Suppose every wire on a cut has the form M_i t + g_i(u) over the binary field. Select cut wires with linearly independent slopes as a basis. Require each other cut wire, after subtracting the basis combination with its slope, to have constant residual offset. The original output is then B(z) for an explicitly constructed NAND circuit on r basis coordinates z. Every prefix has exactly 2^{w−r} tails of each syndrome, since the basis slope matrix M has full row rank. The basis functions g are computed by evaluating the original circuit at (u,0).

For an inclusive boundary j=U·2^w+T, the rank in a fixed syndrome fiber z is U·2^{w−r} plus the number of tails t≤T satisfying Mt=z+g(U). To count them, for every set bit of T impose the more significant bits of T and a zero in that position. Add the equality case t=T. These disjoint affine systems are counted by binary Gaussian elimination. Rank at −1 is zero. Subtract the ranks at the interval endpoints and sum over the syndromes accepted by B. In a nonempty contributing fiber, binary search for the first raw index whose rank exceeds the rank before the interval. This yields an original satisfying word. The argument covers both zero tail width and zero syndrome rank.

The implementation tries each physical suffix. It propagates certified affine profiles through canonical XOR circuits, inverter gates and recognized constants. For prefix functions it uses formal token sums, introducing a token for an otherwise opaque NAND of two zero-slope operands. Traversing backwards from the output and stopping at each first profiled wire gives a cut. Gaussian elimination tracks both slopes and formal token sums. A formally constant offset proves the required identity even if distinct prefix tokens secretly compute identical functions. Failed formal cancellation simply rejects this particular cut. The implementation also retains the earlier permutation certificate and chooses the candidate of least rank. The preprocessing is polynomial, and exact interval counting and witness reconstruction take 2^r times a polynomial in raw width and circuit size with polynomial space. ## A strict comparison with the permutation recognizer

For h≥4 build two separate left-associated canonical XOR chains. Start the first at x₀ and the second at x₁. To each chain add x₂ through x_{h−1} in increasing order. Use fresh internal NAND gates for every XOR, and take the NAND of the two chain outputs. The circuit has 8(h−2)+1 NAND gates. At physical suffix width h the two chain outputs have independent slopes, distinguished by x₀ and x₁. The final NAND therefore admits a syndrome certificate of rank two. The independent syndrome bits are jointly uniform, so the final NAND accepts exactly three quarters of all words, whereas any rank-at-most-one certificate at any suffix has acceptance density in {0, 1/2, 1} by equal fiber sizes, proving the optimized rank is exactly two.

For each shared input x_i, i≥2, choose a route through each chain to the output. The two routes meet only at x_i and the final NAND. A single wire intercepting all routes from x_i is therefore either x_i or the final NAND. The latter is neither an inverter nor a recognized XOR gate, since its parents are distinct and their immediate operands are disjoint internal gates. Consequently the earlier recognizer can only cut x_i at the primary input. If x₁ is a tail coordinate, its eligible gates lie entirely within its own chain: the other chain never reads x₁ and the final NAND has no signed spine. In any proper suffix x₀ is a prefix input, and a route from x₀ through the first chain to the output avoids every cut just listed. Thus every proper suffix fails the outer-cone test and the permutation recognizer returns width h. The comparison is between specified syntactic recognizers on this exact gate encoding.
