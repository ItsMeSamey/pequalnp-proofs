# Finding one assignment to binary clauses and Gaussian equations

The input consists of an explicit 2-CNF Γ and explicit binary affine equations Ax=b on the same n original variables. [The solver](mixed_binary_anchor.py) computes two cut dimensions p and q in polynomial time, chooses the smaller, and returns a checked original assignment or exhausts all fibers to certify unsatisfiability. Its deterministic bit cost is 2^min(p,q) times a polynomial in the input length and n, with polynomial space. The dimensions denote the cuts constructed below. Neither is claimed minimum.

**Project onto the clause coordinates.** Let I be the coordinates occurring in the remaining nontautological clauses. Eliminate all Gaussian columns outside I first. Keep the rows whose outside part reduces to zero and echelonize them. They describe the projection exactly: given an assignment on I satisfying these rows, the rows with an outside pivot can be solved for their distinct pivots in increasing order, setting other outside bits freely. A contradiction in the original Gaussian system is checked first.

Scan the projected rows and nominate additional coordinates until each row has at most two unselected coordinates. Let p be the number selected. Fixing them reduces every parity check to width at most two. Forbid each local tuple of wrong parity with one binary, unary, or empty clause. Solve these clauses together with Γ and the chosen pins by 2-SAT. From a satisfying assignment retain only its coordinates in I and complete them by Gaussian elimination in the original system. This last operation can change outside coordinates that the 2-SAT model chose freely.

**Parameterize the Gaussian solutions.** In an affine chart x=o+Dt with independent columns, each binary-clause literal pulls back to a constant or a linear equation on t. Discard true clauses and false constant terms, merge identical terms, and discard complementary pairs. From each remaining genuine two-term clause, include the first term's normal if neither term's normal is already in the chosen span. Let q be the final rank. Enumerating values on a basis of that span fixes at least one term per binary clause. A false clause rejects the fiber; every surviving nonconstant requirement is a single affine equation. Solve these equations together with the basis pins, lift by x=o+Dt, and verify against both original inputs. Units need no nomination. An empty clause forces failure.

Both plans are prepared before enumeration. Coordinate cuts win ties; inconsistent initial inputs can return immediately. This routine solves a conjunction and produces one assignment. It does not count assignments or recognize a general circuit as a conjunction of these types.

The earlier history-cover solver detects affine packets in bounded-width CNF, tests independent literal images with 2-SAT, and handles dependent images by enumerating SCC generators up to the image codimension. That method has a bound polynomial to an exponent depending on the codimension. Here the input equations are explicit and the exponent is fixed after the 2^min(p,q) factor. Dense projected checks and clauses with many independent factor normals can make both cuts large. A portfolio can retain the archived route for inputs with small literal-image codimension.
