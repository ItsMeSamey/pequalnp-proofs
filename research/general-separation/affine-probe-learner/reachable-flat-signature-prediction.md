# Exact prediction from reachable flat-membership patterns

Fix a hidden seed in the binary cube of dimension h. Each query supplies affine flats by lists of linear equations and a Boolean selector circuit. Given the flat-membership bits, the circuit outputs the squarefree coefficients of a quadratic on the entire seed, whose value is the label. The stream may be adaptive. The learner stores only mistaken queries and their labels. It predicts the larger of the two exact feasible-seed counts, breaking ties toward zero.

## Local support and the guarantee

Use the feasible-hull promise of [the flat-selected learner](flat-selected-quadratic-prediction.md). For each old membership pattern a with a nonempty set of consistent seeds, retain their affine hull H_a. Freeze each old selector at its slice of a, producing labeled equations E_a. On the direction space of H_a, some further linear restriction of codimension at most k makes the span of the polars of E_a have dimension at most r. This restriction can depend on the pattern and prefix. The empty history has one pattern, the full cube as hull, and no equations.

For this round let D_a consist of points in H_a satisfying E_a. Let P_a be the number of distinct patterns of **all old and current flat tests** attained in D_a, and put P equal to the sum of the P_a over live old patterns. A point of D_a can have an old membership pattern different from a. Those points must be included in this parameter because they can contribute to intermediate counts.

The algorithm discovers these supports without advice. With N the total description length of the retained probes and the held-out query, its per-round bit cost is

    2^O(kr + k² + k + r) · poly(P, N, h+1).

On any prefix for which every required old hull satisfies the polar promise, the learner makes at most h mistakes. The cost of a correct round includes the current tests and selector; correct rounds retain no new geometry. The fresh quadratic has no polar-span requirement.

## Recovering support from positive intersections

For any finite nonnegative integer measure on bit patterns, identify a pattern with the set of its true positions. Suppose C(S) gives the mass of all patterns containing S. Given a list of previously extracted patterns with exact masses, subtract from C(S) the masses of listed patterns containing S. The result R(S) counts the remaining mass containing S.

While R(∅)>0, start S empty and inspect each test once. Adjoin its index if the enlarged S has positive residual mass. Each skipped extension stays impossible as S grows. At the end S has positive residual mass and no proper superset with positive residual mass, so R(S) is its exact mass. Append S and its mass to the list. This proves completeness and termination after one pass per supported pattern. Nonnegativity justifies the feasibility tests. With q tests the procedure needs O((q+1)(P_a+1)) moment evaluations on D_a; scanning the extracted list for each subtraction adds polynomial bit work. This is a positive-weight special case of sparse recovery from Boolean subset sums. [Related sparse Möbius algorithms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06246)

For D_a, compute C(S) by intersecting H_a with all flats selected by S, then counting solutions of E_a there. An empty intersection contributes zero. The old polar promise passes to the direction space of every subflat by intersecting it with the good further restriction. The [quadratic histogram algorithm](quadratic-polar-span-online.md) therefore computes the moment using a constant dummy current row. It searches for an anchor on the old equations alone. All moment values and residuals are nonnegative integers at most 2^h.

## Frozen counts and hull recovery

For each discovered target pattern b whose old slice equals a, freeze the current selector at its current slice. For a proposed label and an optional affine cut K, impose that current quadratic equation on D_a∩K. For each discovered pattern v containing b, count solutions satisfying all positive tests in v. Each call uses the same current quadratic and old labeled system, pulled back with their offsets to the intersected affine flat. The current row adds at most one polar direction.

Process v in decreasing order of cardinality. From its positive-intersection count subtract the exact masses already computed at every strict superset of v. This gives the exact mass at v because the imposed equation and the cut can only remove signatures from the support already recovered on D_a. In particular, the computation for b includes supersets whose old slice differs from a. At b itself every seed has the correct old membership bits, so all old equations are genuine. Conversely every consistent seed with pattern b belongs to H_a. Summing the resulting masses over targets and old atoms gives both exact histogram bins, with no double counting.

On a mistake recover the new hull for each target having positive true-label mass. The preceding counts with optional affine cuts give a witness by fixing coordinate bits successively while keeping the count positive. To enlarge the affine span of the witnesses, try the complement of each of its defining equations. A positive count supplies a new witness by the same coordinate search with this extra cut. Stop when all such counts vanish. Each success raises dimension, so O((h+1)²) count requests suffice per target. These cuts require no new signature discovery. At most polynomially many quadratic calls and subtractions per discovered pattern suffice for prediction and commit. Exact masses are counts of subsets of the seed cube, and temporary sums of polynomially many counts need O(h+log(P+1)+1) bits. Each mistake at least halves the consistent-seed count and retains the true seed, proving the bound.

An implementation may take a cap K_max on the total support size. After extracting K_max patterns across old domains, a positive residual on the current or any remaining domain certifies overflow; it can also stop at the first additional pattern. The cap and the quadratic search budgets then bound work even on off-promise inputs, provided the history is capped at h mistakes. One total convention is to predict zero on provisional failure, restore the old state on any correct round, and enter a permanent default state if a failed prediction is wrong. Also enter that state on a putative (h+1)st mistake. On a successful round, build successor hulls before replacing committed state. No default state is reached on streams satisfying the promises and cap on all rounds; no claim of exact majority is made for a clipped round. A support promise only on mistaken probes would omit the cost of inspecting a potentially large held-out query.

## When the improvement is strict

Take all nonzero homogeneous linear hyperplane tests on m active coordinates. Every point gives a distinct signature on those coordinates because the coordinate hyperplanes occur, hence there are precisely 2^m signatures. Every linear subspace is an intersection of listed hyperplanes. In particular the graphs of all linear maps from a fixed floor(m/2)-dimensional summand to a complementary summand give 2^floor(m²/4) distinct intersections. With h=m the listed input has polynomial size in 2^m. Even the first round therefore separates polynomial support enumeration from enumeration of the whole intersection family by a superpolynomial factor. Inactive seed coordinates can be appended freely.

For signed coordinate subcubes in a common coordinate system, let p denote the number of geometric signatures over the entire cube. Every nonempty intersection has two opposite completions of its free coordinates, keeping the fixed coordinates unchanged. A listed subcube contains both points exactly when it contains the whole intersection. Thus its full list of containing predicates is determined by an unordered pair of realized signatures, and there are at most p(p+1)/2 distinct intersections. Local pruning can still help exponentially. With an all-ones seed, present fresh coordinate quadratics s_i with no tests on the first floor(h/2) coordinates. Each round is a tie and a mistake, so the feasible hull fixes those bits to one. A new query testing just these coordinates has exponentially many ambient patterns and exactly one local pattern.

The unconditional prefix/suffix evaluator from the earlier result still qualifies: its global intersection count is polynomial for arbitrary changing prefixes and suffixes in one common order, so its global signature count is polynomial as well. Every local pattern is a global pattern, and the number of live old atoms is at most the number of global joint patterns. The common budgets k=r=1 continue to apply to its allowed quadratics. For unrestricted coordinate tests or general affine flats the held-out support can be exponential. The guarantee concerns the supplied evaluator and its realized local supports.
