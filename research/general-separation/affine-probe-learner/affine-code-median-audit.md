# A width-two obstruction for global clause repairs

Let d≥3, n=2^d−1, N=2^n and B=2^(n−d). Index the n raw coordinates by the distinct nonzero vectors a in the d-dimensional binary space, with the ordered standard basis appearing first. A message m gives the raw word y_a=a·m+δ_a, where δ_a=0 on the basis and δ_a=1 elsewhere. Interpret the word as an integer with the first coordinate most significant. Let F be its code membership predicate. This predicate has a polynomial-size circuit using the systematic message coordinates to test each displayed parity.

Every pair of distinct columns has rank two. All four assignments to the corresponding raw coordinates therefore extend to codewords, with the offsets affecting only their order. Each individual raw bit takes both values. Consequently no non-tautological clause of width at most two can be added as a global consequence of F, including clauses with repeated variables. Such clause repairs leave the original blind predictor's candidate counts unchanged when applied to the positive virtual fiber. This statement concerns clauses on raw coordinates alone.

The zero message gives the sole codeword in [0,B), namely B−1. The all-zero raw anchor is false. The blind learner after its initial false forecast on F and feedback one retains virtual integers 1 through N−1. The first leftward spine of its median search visits N/2,N/4,…,B. These d integers have one-hot message e_i and tail zero. Choose two distinct j,k other than i. The tail column e_j+e_k would give value one, contradicting the zero tail. Thus all d checks fail. After the check at B the left-first solver traverses the whole perfect median subtree on 1 through B−1. The only feasible codeword is its rightmost leaf, which is visited last. At the extreme left endpoint, an additional history for virtual zero, if retained by the solver, has n+1 mistakes and is pruned before any point evaluation. The first witness therefore occurs on evaluation B+d−1, after B+d−2 false evaluations. The initial anchor evaluation in constructing the learner is separate and adds one if charged. Construction and syntactic decoding also evaluate auxiliary circuits; the exact count here is for the DFS point-witness check on F. At d=2 both one-hot ancestors are codewords. At d=1 the zero raw anchor is a codeword.

The source itself also displays a satisfying word. The point count applies to the stated DFS with global implicate repairs. A conditional repair can already extract binary relations on the first left branch. Once its top message coordinate is zero, outputs for distinct nonzero a and a+e_1 differ by the known sum of their offsets. After d−1 zero message bits are fixed, two codewords remain, and their units and pairwise equality or complement clauses describe those two words completely. Global ternary clauses also suffice. For each nonbasis a choose i in its support and put b=a+e_i. The parity on y_a,y_b,y_(e_i) equals the sum of their offsets and has a four-clause ternary encoding. By increasing support size these n−d parities reconstruct every tail bit.
