# Three-coordinate gate probes preserve the exact mistake threshold

The same deterministic local evaluator is used for all lengths. A query has three literal slots. Each slot contains a constant or a seed coordinate with a public complement bit. The response is L₀ ⊕ (L₁ AND L₂). Indices may repeat. Each query therefore mentions at most three original coordinates of its own H-bit seed and has O(log(H+2)) description length. Taking one factor zero reads a literal or constant. The evaluator is independent of any learner, adversary, circuit family, and exponent.

**Local premise.** A uniform deterministic learner A, initialized with H, never gives H+1 consecutive incorrect predictions from initialization on a sequence realized by a single H-bit seed. This is required at each relevant length, on all query/feedback sequences of that length H+1. The learner may be improper. Its running time on these prefixes is polynomial in H and the encoded transcript length. Correct predictions on other streams may change state arbitrarily.

**Transfer.** Given n and a public gate cap s, the same A yields a uniform deterministic learner for arbitrary n-bit Boolean circuit queries with at most s gates in fan-in-two NAND form, allowing literal and constant wires. It makes at most n mistakes on any adaptive stream sharing one original seed x. The per-round bit cost is polynomial in n+s+1 and the input query length, independent of the horizon. A standard explicit fixed finite Boolean basis converts to NAND with a constant-factor gate overhead, charged to s. The cap is supplied in advance or follows from the application. No uniform exact-n theorem for an initially unknown, growing cap is asserted.

Put m=(n+1)s and H=n+m. The local learner's first n coordinates represent the original seed x. The remaining m coordinates are reserve wires, fresh in increasing index order. Store a committed local transcript, initially empty. For each actual query start A at length H and replay the committed inputs and feedback. Process all gates of the new circuit in topological order. A parent is represented by a literal equal to its intended bit: original inputs and constants are already literals, and earlier gate outputs use the public corrections below.

For a NAND with parent literals u,v and fresh coordinate z, ask the local query z ⊕ (u AND v). If its prediction is p, supply the synthetic label 1⊕p. Denote that supplied bit by d. For any given original seed and assigned preceding gates, assign z=d⊕(u AND v). The intended NAND output is exactly the corrected literal z⊕d⊕1. Thus every gate consumes one mistake by A and a single fresh coordinate, without restricting even one original input. Fanout reuses the same corrected literal. A gate may take a constant or repeated parent. Nothing in the simulation asks for the values of an original bit, gate, or reserve.

Ask A for the output literal using a zero factor, and use that prediction for the original query. If the actual label is correct, discard the whole trial. If it is incorrect, commit the trial's gate probes, forced labels, output query and actual label. Determinism reproduces all prior predictions on replay. Rollback involves no erasure of output/feedback already supplied to the *original* online adversary, only local simulations.

Suppose an original seed x yields n+1 mistakes. The committed transcript at that moment contains exactly n+1 mistaken output queries and G≤(n+1)s=m mistaken gate probes. It contains no correct local predictions. Assign each of the used G reserve coordinates in chronological order by its gate equation on x. These assignments realize the entire committed transcript, including outputs, because the original answers were all evaluations at x. Append a literal query for each unused reserve and supply the opposite of A's prediction. Choose that coordinate accordingly. The one now-complete seed realizes every label and A has made G+(n+1)+(m−G)=H+1 consecutive mistakes. This contradicts the local premise.

Before any putative (n+1)st original mistake at most n previously mistaken circuits occupy the transcript, leaving sufficient reserve for one more trial. The trial visits at most H+1 local steps including replay. State can be recomputed on each trial so the simulation does not require an efficient black-box state-copy operation. For a total simulator with arbitrary supplied A, permanently default after n+1 committed errors; this branch is unreachable under the premise. If caps and times are encoded in binary, the clock is polynomial in the numeric public cap s, not merely its logarithm. The statement includes s=0, for which no gate coordinates are needed.

**Fixed arity boundary.** Every Boolean readout of two affine measurements on an h-bit seed has a uniform polynomial h-mistake learner. Maintain an affine cell containing the seed. The image of the cell has at most four equally frequent points; predict the majority on them. After an error the opposite fiber in that image has zero, one or two points, hence is empty or affine and contains at most half of the prior mass. The true seed eliminates the empty case. Gaussian elimination updates the cell. In contrast the three-bit function f(x,y,z)=xy+z admits no representation as a readout of two affine measurements on independent input bits: the difference f(s+v)+f(s) equals v_x y+v_y x+v_x v_y+v_z and is identically zero only for v=0. Two measurement rows necessarily have a nonzero common kernel. Polar rank alone does not detect the third direction. The local family also admits polynomial-time O(H³)-mistake learning by memorizing its polynomially many literal triples. This gives no exact H bound.

**Conditional consequence.** For a fixed uniform length-preserving permutation with forward circuit cost p(n), the reverse-orbit inverter uses horizon T=8n(n+1) and public queries each representable by s=O(T(p(n)+n+1)) NAND gates. This cap is known before learning begins and has one fixed polynomial bound for that permutation. With the transfer, a polynomial local learner satisfying the premise would give a deterministic polynomial-time inverter succeeding on more than three quarters of uniform n-bit challenges. The inverter verifies each returned original input. For a cryptographically secure one-way permutation, every uniform polynomial local learner therefore has a realizable all-mistaken H(n)+1 stream at each sufficiently large corresponding padded length. This is a conditional consequence of an unproved cryptographic assumption. The transfer and the circuit simulations use the same local evaluator throughout. They do not construct an unconditional hard seed or resolve P versus NP. [Original-seed orbit inversion](fixed-evaluator-orbit-inversion.md)

Prediction-preserving reductions have precedent in Pitt and Warmuth's distribution-independent learning framework. Here the embedding may depend on predictions, all expanded labels come from one seed, and every added coordinate consumes one mistake.
