# Exact prediction of quadratics selected by affine-flat tests

A single secret s in F₂^h supplies every label in an adaptive stream. A probe lists affine flats, each specified by binary linear equations, and gives a Boolean circuit that maps their membership bits to all squarefree coefficients of one quadratic Q, including its constant and linear part. The label is Q(s). Coefficient evaluation, Gaussian elimination, and the selector circuit size are charged to the input length. In particular, the model permits arbitrary Boolean branching on the membership bits.

**The learner predicts by exact majority and makes at most h mistakes** under the following prefix promise. For the tests in the committed mistaken probes, form their family of distinct nonempty intersections, including the whole space V. For each intersection O let O° consist of the points in O outside every proper family member contained in O. Let S_O be the seeds in O° that match all committed labels. For every nonempty S_O put H_O=aff(S_O). Freeze each committed selector on the membership vector of O°. On the direction space of H_O, the polars of these frozen quadratics admit a common further restriction of codimension at most k on which their span has dimension at most r. The common restriction may differ between atoms and between mistake prefixes. Polars and codimensions are taken in coordinates on the indicated affine hull. Empty feasible sets impose no condition.

The guarantee applies prefix by prefix. The per-round clock also charges M, the number of distinct nonempty intersections of **all committed tests plus the current tests**, even on a correct round. A current quadratic need not meet the stored polar promise. With total supplied description length N for those probes, the cost is

    2^O(kr + k² + k + r) · poly(M, N, h+1)

bit operations. Including the constant in the polynomial handles zero budgets and the empty history. A budget failure may return a fixed default in a total implementation; the promised prediction and next-state computations below always succeed. Time bounds stated here concern promised streams.

## One count on a refined atom

Construct the intersection family incrementally. For each test intersect each current member with that test, discard empty intersections, and deduplicate by canonical linear equation systems. Intermediate family sizes never exceed the final M. Inclusion tests by elimination give the strict-order relation. The atoms partition V: each point belongs to the atom labelled by the intersection of exactly the tests that contain it, taking V for the empty list. On a nonempty atom I°, a test is true precisely if it contains I. Empty atoms can be recognized by cardinality subtraction from the bare flat sizes; proper intersections may have empty atoms even if their labels are nonempty flats.

For a refined nonempty atom I°, its old membership vector identifies one committed atom O°, with I°⊆O°. Discard I if the committed state marked S_O empty. Otherwise fix every old quadratic and the current quadratic using I's test vector. The old quadratics coincide with those for O°. Let b be a proposed current label, and K an optional additional affine cut, initially V. We want the number of seeds in I°∩K satisfying these equations with the observed old labels and current label b.

For every family member J⊆I, count the solutions to **this same fixed system** on J∩K∩H_O. An empty intersection contributes zero. Parameterize a nonempty intersection affinely, pulling back full equations with offsets and labels. The stored polar condition passes to the smaller direction space: intersect the good subspace with it, losing at most k dimensions, and restrict the remaining polars. The current row adds at most one to their span. Thus the [restricted-polar counting algorithm](quadratic-polar-span-online.md), whose anchor discovery uses only the old rows, computes the count within the indicated exponential factor. This applies even if J° belongs to a different old atom: the equations and the hull here are still those fixed for I. The count on the artificial domain is an intermediate arithmetic quantity.

In increasing order of inclusion, subtract from each count on J the already computed atom counts for **all** proper members below J. This gives the frozen-system count on J°∩K∩H_O. At J=I it is the desired answer, since every actually consistent seed in I° lies in S_O and hence in H_O. Summing the answers at K=V over refined atoms gives the two exact histogram bins. At most O(M²) quadratic counting calls and O(M³) elementary subtractions suffice for both bins. Counts of actual sets have at most h+1 bits; the total size of a sum of at most M such integers has O(h+log(M+1)+1) bits.

A one-bit example shows why the rows must remain frozen. With just the test {0}, use current constant zero at 0 and constant one elsewhere. For the ambient atom {1}, the frozen label-one equation has two solutions on V and one on {0}. Refreezing on {0} would subtract zero and report two instead of one. Similarly M cannot be charged only to mistaken probes: h independent coordinate tests on the first probe already have 2^h nonempty intersections, even if its selector is constant.

## Reconstructing feasible hulls after a mistake

Initially the only hull is V. Predict the larger bin, breaking ties toward zero. Keep the prior committed state unchanged on a correct round. On a mistake the chosen class of seeds has size at most half the previous feasible total, and it contains the fixed secret. For each refined atom commit its hull for the true label, or its empty flag. Only the mistaken probe and label join the history. Consequently at most h probes join on promised prefixes, and correct-round geometry does not accumulate.

For completeness the hulls themselves require no external oracle. For one refined atom, denote its true-label count with an extra affine cut K by c(K), evaluated by the preceding frozen subtraction using its **old** hull. If c(V)>0, find one point by successively fixing its h coordinate bits, at each stage testing the zero extension by a count. Given the affine span L of points obtained so far, test for another point outside L by comparing c(V) and c(L). To recover it by the same coordinate search, use c(K)−c(K∩L) for each prefix cut K. Each added point increases dimension, so at most h+1 points and O((h+1)²) cut-count evaluations per atom suffice. Each cut count needs at most M frozen quadratic calls and O(M²) subtractions. Additional cuts are intersected for that invocation only; they do not enlarge the global arrangement. This charges polynomial work for hull reconstruction and avoids using the new polar promise to establish the new hull. The promise for that hull is used only on a later round. In particular, H_O need not be the old intersection label or even the affine hull of the whole old atom.

## An unconditional fixed evaluator

Use an h-bit word in its given coordinate order. A flat test may fix any specified prefix or suffix to any specified binary string. Distinct simultaneous prefix constraints are inconsistent or equivalent to their longest member; the same holds for suffixes. Every nonempty intersection is specified by at most one listed prefix and one listed suffix, provided their overlap agrees. For n total tests this gives M≤(n+1)². This bound permits arbitrary changes of tests on every round.

For h=3m+1 use one coordinate t and three m-tuples x,a,b, and set R=Σ a_j b_j. On each test vector the selector may output arbitrary coefficients of

    t·L(x) + eR + ℓ(s),

where L is linear, e is a bit, and ℓ is affine. Enforce this syntax by having the selector emit just L,e,ℓ. For other positive h take m=floor((h−1)/3) and include unused coordinates in ℓ; at h=0 use a constant. On every feasible hull, fixing the direction t costs at most one dimension and leaves a polar span of dimension at most one, from R. Thus k=r=1 suffice for **every** stream of this single evaluator and all branches, with polynomial per-round time and at most h mistakes. The exceptional cost of a current selector is covered even for arbitrary circuits computing these coefficient bits.

## Strictness and simulation controls

The feasible-hull promise is strictly weaker than requiring the same bounds on the containing arrangement flat. Take no flat tests, h=2m, and the successive probes x_i y_i on the all-ones secret. They are all mistakes: conditional on earlier true labels, the next pair is fresh and has positive probability one quarter under uniform feasible seeds. Each label one fixes both coordinates, so every committed polar restricted to the current feasible hull is zero. The budgets k=r=0 suffice. On V, the m stored polars have disjoint pairs. If their restrictions span at most r dimensions on a codimension-k subspace, the coefficient kernel has dimension at least m−r. For r≤m it contains a vector of weight at least m−r, by setting every coordinate in an information set to one. Its alternating form has rank twice that weight. A codimension-k subspace on which this form vanishes must have k at least the weight, by the dimension bound for isotropic subspaces in the nondegenerate quotient. Hence k+r≥m on the ambient flat. There is no nonzero affine combination of the original m quadratic rows.

The separation persists with a high-rank quadratic surviving on every nonempty committed hull. Let h=4m with m≥2 and disjoint m-tuples a,b,x,y. Start with the probe R=Σ a_j b_j and a secret with R=1 and all x_i=y_i=1. The zero label has a strict majority: when a is nonzero the two values are equally frequent over b, whereas a=0 always gives zero. Thus the first observation is a mistake and retains exactly R=1. This set affinely spans the full (a,b) space. Otherwise an affine hyperplane contains it and R vanishes on the complementary hyperplane. In affine coordinates taking that complementary hyperplane to z₁=0, uniqueness of the squarefree expansion gives R=z₁ times an affine form, of polar rank at most two. In fact R has rank 2m≥4.

Now present Q_i=R+x_i y_i for i=1,…,m. The previous labels say exactly R=1 and x_j=y_j=1 for j<i. The next pair is uniform on four assignments under the feasible-seed count, so the majority predicts one on three quarters, while the actual label is zero. After each error the new pair is fixed to ones. The feasible set is the Cartesian product of the R=1 locus, those fixed pairs and the fresh cube. Its affine hull fixes only the specified pairs. Every stored polar restricts there to the same B_R, giving k=0,r=1 on every prefix. On the whole ambient flat the differences Q_i+R give the m independent disjoint products from the preceding rank argument, again forcing k+r≥m. The rank 2m of R also defeats the two-residual-measurement condition with small anchor dimension: any linear measurement map through which R factors has kernel contained in rad(B_R), hence measurement span of dimension at least 2m. Reducing this to dimension at most two modulo an anchor costs at least 2m−2 dimensions.

An ordinary Boolean circuit can evaluate any explicit flat-selected probe with the same h-bit secret and polynomial description overhead: test each listed system of affine equations, evaluate the selector, and sum its selected monomials modulo two. Conversely, without the geometry bound, any ordinary circuit on the same h input bits is a flat-selected probe: use the h coordinate tests s_i=1 and let the selector emit the circuit's answer as a constant quadratic. No seed padding is needed in either simulation. This latter construction can use an exponentially large intersection family, so the polynomial evaluator above does not establish a learner for arbitrary polynomial-size circuits.

**A full-hull boundary.** Take disjoint triples of secret bits and the fixed query family $Q_i=x_i y_i+z_i$ for $1\le i\le m$. Use the seed with every $x_i=y_i=0$ and $z_i=1$. Query each index once. Its fresh $z_i$ makes the two histogram entries equal even conditional on the earlier mistakes. Breaking ties toward zero therefore incurs $m$ mistakes. Each surviving triple satisfies $z=1+xy$. Its four points span the entire affine three-space: the graph contains the three points $(0,0,1),(1,0,1),(0,1,1)$ of the plane $z=1$ and the point $(1,1,0)$ outside it. The Cartesian product of these graphs and the fresh cubes still has full affine hull, as one can vary each factor separately. The disjoint-product polar bound above thus gives $k+r\ge m$ after all $m$ mistakes, even on the feasible hull. A learner can memorize one label per index and make at most $m$ mistakes on arbitrary repetitions of this family.
