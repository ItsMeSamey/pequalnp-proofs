# Learning Boolean readouts of affine measurements with a fixed hidden seed

A finite or infinite adaptive stream uses one unknown seed s in F₂^h throughout. A query supplies a binary measurement matrix L, an offset d, and an explicitly evaluable Boolean circuit F. The learner predicts F(Ls+d), then receives the true bit. Mistakes count over the entire stream. All algorithms below are deterministic, and the adversary may choose the next query after seeing every previous answer. Costs are bit operations. Affine forms include constants. T denotes the span of the linear coefficient rows of L in the dual space.

## Exact halving for quadratic queries

Consider queries Q=ab+c, with a,b,c affine forms of the seed. Call a query nonlinear when the linear parts of a,b are independent, and call their span its plane. Suppose all nonlinear mistaken queries admit a single subspace W* of dimension at most k meeting each of their planes nontrivially. There is a learner with at most h mistakes and per-round cost (3^k+2^{k+1})poly(h+1), given this budget k. For the promise on the learner's own mistakes, interpret this assertion on each prefix for which the promise holds. The algorithm is total with any fixed fallback if the promised search fails.

Store only the previous mistaken queries and their true labels. To find a covering W, start at zero. If some stored plane misses the current W, branch on adjoining each of its three nonzero vectors. Stop at coverage or at dimension k. Following a vector of W* maintains a successful path. The search uses O(3^k) nodes and polynomial work per node on at most h stored rows under the promise.

For the new query adjoin the linear part of a temporarily to the returned W. Enumerate all assignments to a basis of the extended space. On each resulting cell every stored query and the current query is affine. Indeed a covered plane fixes a, b, or a+b on the cell, using the offsets explicitly. In the third case ab=(1+a+b)a as a function when a+b is fixed. Dependent factors already give an affine product by Boolean idempotence. Gaussian elimination on each cell counts the seeds consistent with all stored labels and each possible new bit. The cells partition the seed cube. Counts have at most h+1 bits. Predict the more numerous bit, resolving ties arbitrarily. Each mistake at least halves the version space; it always contains s. Induction gives at most h mistakes and justifies the stored-row bound. Discard the temporary extension before the next query.

## Arbitrary readouts with at most two residual measurements

Suppose a single W* of dimension at most k satisfies dim((T+W*)/W*)≤2 for every mistaken query. There is a learner using O(14^k)poly(h+1, current input length) bit operations per round with

    M ≤ (h log 2 + k log 7) / log(4/3)

mistakes. In particular k=O(log(h+2)) allows polynomial per-round time. The supplied representation L determines the promise. A circuit may ignore some of its supplied measurements.

An expert stores a subspace W of the dual, the values assigned to a basis of W, and a nonempty affine cell A consistent with these assignments. Its mass is |A|/7^{dim W}. Start with one expert of mass 2^h. Expand separately and provisionally for each new query. If dim((T+W)/W)>2, choose three measurement forms independent modulo W. For each of their seven nonzero linear combinations u, create both children assigning u(s)=0 or 1, extend W by u, and intersect A with that equation. Omit empty cells. Iterate on the same query. Drop an unresolved branch at depth k. For each u its two children partition A, so the sum of masses across the seven choices equals the parent mass. A budget cutoff can only decrease mass.

On a resolved cell compute the actual affine image L(A)+d. It contains at most four points with equally large seed fibers. The expert predicts the majority bit on those points, breaking ties deterministically. If wrong, the surviving opposite-bit set has size zero, one, or two, and is an affine subset if nonempty. Its inverse image inside A is therefore empty or affine and has at most half the prior size. Predict by mass-weighted majority of expert predictions, with a fixed default when no experts remain. On a correct master prediction discard all provisional changes. On a master mistake commit the resolved experts, restricting the wrong experts to their true-label inverse images and leaving the correct experts unchanged. At least half the provisional mass voted incorrectly, so remaining mass is at most three quarters of the mass before the round.

For any eligible prefix, fix W* for its mistakes. Across committed rounds there is always an expert with W⊆W* and s∈A. Indeed, the three independent forms become dependent modulo W*, so some nonzero combination belongs to W* and extends W strictly. Choose its true value. This path never fails at depth k, and the subsequent label update retains s. Its mass is at least 7^{-k}. Together with the initial mass and contraction this proves the bound. It also handles empty provisional votes on correct rounds. The tree has at most 14 children per node and depth k, including old and provisional branches together when counted as descendants of the initial expert. Experts with identical underlying cells may be retained separately. The common mass denominator 7^k and numerators have O(h+k+1) bits up to constant factors, even when summed, since total mass is at most 2^h. All remaining operations use linear algebra on explicit binary matrices and at most four evaluations of the current circuit per resolved expert.

## Controls and the remaining lower-bound question

For variables x₁,…,xᵣ,t, queries xᵢxⱼ+t on every pair have residual dimension at most two after conditioning on t. Covering all their nonlinear planes, a stronger geometric requirement, needs dimension at least r−1. The pivot coordinates of any covering row space hit every pair, since the leading coordinate of each nonzero member is a pivot. A coordinate cover of size r−1 attains the bound.

More generally, minimizing the dimension in the residual-dimension condition already encodes Vertex Cover. For every graph edge ij introduce a private coordinate tₑ and use Tₑ=span(eᵢ,eⱼ,tₑ). The pivot coordinates of any feasible W hit every displayed triple. Replacing every private pivot by one endpoint yields a vertex cover of size at most dim W. Conversely, a coordinate vertex cover suffices. One fixed query per edge is nevertheless learnable by memorization in at most |E| mistakes, bounded by the number of seed bits here. Optimization hardness alone gives no mistake lower bound.

Even at k=0, deciding whether supplied labels admit a common seed is NP-complete. Given a Not-All-Equal 3-SAT instance, use its variable assignment as the seed. For each ordered triple of literals x,y,z make one query with the affine measurements x+y and y+z and the fixed two-input OR readout. Require label one. Both measurements vanish exactly when all three literals agree, so consistent seeds are precisely the satisfying assignments. Negations add one to the corresponding literal's affine constant. Each coefficient row is the XOR of two coordinate masks. The dense encoding uses O(m(h+1)) bits for m clauses on h variables and is computable in polynomial time. Unsigned triples already suffice by Schaefer's dichotomy. A seed certifies feasibility in NP. In the realizable prediction task, a learner receives the true label after voting. At k=0 it has just one expert, whose affine cell at least halves on every mistake. It therefore makes at most h mistakes even for arbitrary adaptive two-measurement readouts. Deciding feasibility of externally supplied observations remains NP-complete. [Schaefer, The complexity of satisfiability problems](https://doi.org/10.1145/800133.804350).

Without conditioning, a single three-bit majority probe has both fibers affinely spanning the whole cube. It equals (x+y)(x+z)+x over F₂. Maintaining just the affine hull of a wrong vote's surviving seeds then gains no information. For arbitrary quadratic queries, linear prediction on all squarefree monomials of degree at most two still gives O(h²) mistakes in polynomial time by Gaussian elimination. Neither observation bounds all polynomial-time predictors from below.

For any fixed polynomial-time seed/query evaluator, P=NP implies a deterministic learner with **at most h mistakes**. The per-round time is polynomial in h and the largest query description seen. Let N₀,N₁ count seeds agreeing with all stored mistakes and giving the two possible new labels. Set t=4(h+1). Using t disjoint h-bit seeds gives a counting predicate with Nᵢ^t witnesses, represented and verified in polynomial time. Use Stockmeyer's fixed-factor approximation in the polynomial hierarchy; under P=NP the approximation is deterministic polynomial time. Detect zero counts by feasibility. For positive counts use estimates between half and twice the true count. Predict the larger product estimate without extracting a root. On a mistake the surviving count satisfies N_true^t≤4N_pred^t, so at most the fraction 4^{1/t}/(1+4^{1/t})≤4^{1/t}/2 survives. After h+1 mistakes the size would be at most 2^h(4^{1/t}/2)^{h+1}=1/√2, contradicting the true seed. Prior to any putative (h+1)st mistake only h stored rows are needed, product witnesses have O((h+1)²) bits, and counts have at most th+1 bits. The coefficient one is sharp even for coordinate queries. For a deterministic learner, recursively prescribe each fresh coordinate bit opposite its prediction; the resulting fixed seed realizes all h errors.

Consequently, forcing h+1 mistakes against every uniform polynomial-per-round learner for a single explicit evaluator would separate P from NP. Proving that lower bound remains open here. The two constructive results delimit where a quadratic or small-measurement candidate could survive.

Stockmeyer supplies the approximate-counting result. Littlestone studies online mistake bounds.
