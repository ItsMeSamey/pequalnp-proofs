# Preserving every satisfying seed at a forecast disagreement

Two equivalent circuits can receive opposite forecasts in one learner state. Their true labels agree at each seed. That common bit can vary with the seed. Deciding whether one feedback bit preserves every satisfying assignment is coNP-complete even at a strict disagreement of the explicit blind predictor in the [prefix search](lexicographic-mistake-converse.md).

Given a CNF φ on n≥1 bits, use m=n+2 bits in lexicographic order (t,u,x) and form

    F(t,u,x) = (¬t AND u AND φ(x)) OR (t AND u AND [x=1^n]).

The zero word is a public false anchor and the all-ones word is a public satisfying assignment. Put N=2^m and j=N/2. The point j is also false because its u bit is zero. The explicit circuits Q(k)=F AND [v≤k] therefore satisfy Q(j−1)=Q(j). With bounded fan-in the circuits including comparators have O(|φ|+m) gates, counting input pins and wires in the formula size.

The blind predictor counts virtual pairs (v,b) with every combination allowed except (0,1), interpreting F at the secret as b. It predicts an exact virtual majority and conditions on mistakes. It has at most m mistakes on realizable streams since the starting size is 2^(m+1)−1. Here its counts require only interval lengths and one anchor subtraction. The forecasts do not depend on φ. The initial forecast on F is strictly zero, so feedback one is a genuine mistake for every satisfying seed. There are now N−1 virtual pairs, all with b=1. The positive counts for Q(j−1) and Q(j) are respectively N/2−1 and N/2. Their forecasts are strictly zero and one.

Committing feedback zero to the second probe would thus be a genuine mistake for every surviving satisfying seed exactly when φ is unsatisfiable. A satisfying assignment of φ gives a surviving seed (0,1,x) with common bit one. Conversely every such seed yields a satisfying assignment of φ. The all-ones seed always has common bit zero. Thus if φ is satisfiable neither bit on its own retains all satisfying seeds, and if φ is unsatisfiable zero retains them all. This also gives the coNP upper bound directly by checking a candidate discarded seed. The test concerns preservation by a particular feedback action. A satisfying assignment of F was supplied at the outset.

## The cost of forking explicit learner states

For a separate fixed evaluator, let the query be a dyadic prefix cylinder with an ignored bit tag on its description. Predict the exact majority of surviving n-bit seeds with ties broken by the tag. After a mistake intersect the feasible set with the indicated cylinder or its complement. The representation and predictions take polynomial time. The intersections of positive cylinders form one cylinder or the empty set; subtract the union of negative cylinders by retaining their maximal members after intersection. Dyadic cylinders are nested or disjoint, so their cardinalities can be subtracted directly. On a realizable stream at most n mistaken rows accumulate.

From a feasible cylinder with prefix p, the query asking whether the next bit is one, written as membership in the cylinder p1, is balanced. Its two tagged copies are equivalent and give opposite predictions. Choosing the copy whose prediction differs from an assigned bit yields an actual mistake and leaves precisely the child cylinder with that bit. Keeping both children preserves every seed. At depth d the 2^d distinct child histories thus exhibited cover all seeds, each with d genuine mistakes. Moreover, under this learner an arbitrary realizable history charged with d mistakes covers at most 2^(n−d) seeds by exact halving. Any cover of the whole cube by explicitly replayable states each charged at least d mistakes requires at least 2^d states, irrespective of the particular repairs chosen. A symbolic decision tree can describe or evaluate these states efficiently on a supplied seed.

For an opaque predicate with one known false anchor, a zero oracle and a singleton at an unqueried nonanchor have identical point-query transcripts until that point is read. Any additional calculation determined by the transcript, including branching and merging simulated states of the blind predictor, preserves this identity. Distinguishing the two cases deterministically requires N−1 point evaluations in the worst case, attained by scanning. This last bound concerns oracle access. These constructions leave open repairs using an explicitly given formula, other state summaries, or a procedure allowed to discard some satisfying seeds.
