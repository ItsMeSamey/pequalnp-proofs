# Counting original assignments in an interval through a certified NAND cut

Let $G$ be a single-output ordered NAND DAG on $h$ primary inputs, listed most significant bit first, with Boolean constants allowed. Write the original input as $(u,t)$, with $m$ least significant tail bits. The [range counter](tail_permutation_range.py) looks for a cut proving

$$G(u,t)=B(t\mathbin{\oplus}g(u)).$$

Here $B$ is a circuit on $m$ latent coordinates and the prefix mask $g$ can be nonlinear. All counts, bounds, and returned witnesses below use the original $h$-bit order. The running time charges $h$ explicitly, even when a compact arity field or unused inputs make the gate list much shorter.

## Certifying a cut on the original DAG

Compute a syntactic support mask and a signed-pivot mask for each wire. Input $i$ starts with both masks equal to $\{i\}$; constants start empty. Support takes unions at NANDs. A tied-input NAND propagates its operand's pivots. Recognize XOR by the four-NAND identity with shared node $\operatorname{NAND}(a,b)$ and the two arms feeding the outer NAND. At such a root keep a pivot of either operand only when the other operand's support omits it. Other gates supply no pivot. Inductively a marked pivot $i$ makes that wire equal to $x_i$ XOR a function of the other inputs; inversion is included in that function. This is a sufficient syntactic certificate.

For a live candidate wire, stop a backward search from the output at that wire. Discard a pivot if its raw input remains reachable, since that path bypasses the candidate. Try suffix widths in ascending order. For each tail bit start with its own input as cut; among eligible live wires whose support meets the suffix only at that bit, take the last in the original topological order. This furthest eligible postdominator can depend on any prefix coordinates. Check the cuts **jointly** by traversing from the output, stopping at cuts and constants. Reject the choice if another raw input is reached. Otherwise remap the remaining outer NANDs to obtain $B$. Individual postdomination alone would not exclude an uncovered prefix path.

The selected cuts in tail order have values $t_i\oplus g_i(u)$; evaluating them at $(u,0)$ recovers the whole mask. A dead tail bit may stay at its own input, with its latent coordinate unused by $B$. Width zero works when the joint traversal encounters no raw input. The full width $h$ uses the identity cut and always succeeds, including $h=0$. The returned width is the first accepted by this procedure, with no assertion of globally minimal semantic width. Recognition takes polynomial time and space in $h$ and the gate description.

## Inclusive rank and witness recovery

Fix a latent word $y$. In each original prefix block $U$, exactly one raw word maps to it, namely

$$L(U,y)=(U\ll m)\mathbin{|}(y\oplus g(U)).$$

Order these words by their original integer values. Their zero-based indices are exactly their prefixes, whatever the mask. For the inclusive upper bound $j=U2^m+T$ put

$$R_y(j)=U+\mathbf 1[y\oplus g(U)\le T],\qquad R_y(-1)=0.$$

There are $U$ complete earlier blocks and one possible endpoint contribution. For a nonempty closed raw interval $[\ell,r]$, sum $R_y(r)-R_y(\ell-1)$ over the $m$-bit words with $B(y)=1$. If a summand is positive, $R_y(\ell-1)$ is the first eligible **prefix index** for that fiber. Lifting that index with $L$ gives an original satisfying word in the interval. The implementation checks the returned word against both the original NAND circuit and the bounds. It returns zero and no witness on an empty interval. This also covers $m=0$: each prefix has one empty tail and the formula counts ordinary interval length when $B$ is true.

Evaluate the two boundary masks once; enumerate $2^m$ latent words with polynomial workspace. One interval costs $2^m\operatorname{poly}(h+s+1)$ bit operations for $s$ gates, including original-word reconstruction; empty intervals require no latent enumeration. The formula is a fiber rank, not an assertion that XOR preserves order within a block. A general per-prefix bijection gives the same counting identity with its computable inverse at the boundary. The NAND recognizer certifies XOR masks.

## Exact forecasts and the physical four-site mask

The [forecast implementation](tail_range_forecast.py) converts recognized raw threshold labels into one interval and leaves other circuit descriptions as ordinary predicates. For each prospective label, it conjoins the required signed predicates, interning equal ordered NAND pairs, and recognizes that bin independently. A current raw threshold instead further restricts the interval. If $M$ is the maximum of the two widths on nonempty intervals, a posterior request costs $2^M\operatorname{poly}(h+D+1)$ bit operations, where $D$ is the combined description length supplied to the request. If both intervals are empty only polynomial overhead is needed. A positive bin has a witness checked on every supplied row and the current query, at the claimed bit.

The standalone posterior conditions on **all** supplied labels. The online majority learner conditions only on **mistakes**, breaks ties toward zero, and leaves its committed history unchanged after a correct response. At most $h$ mistakes occur on a realizable stream because each one at least halves the retained population. Its witnesses support the retained labels; these comprise the full history on an all-wrong stream. A pure replay with $e$ supplied earlier rows and one current query can charge up to $e+1$ posterior trials, with the width and description length of each trial, or the maxima across those trials as a common bound. Replaying correct rows still costs a trial when uncached. An explicit caller limit on latent assignments can return UNKNOWN before enumeration; this is neither a zero count nor a forecast and commits no update. The online wrapper likewise enforces the declared gate cap. Without a binding caller limit, the output is exact, potentially at $M=h$.

For the [four-site compiler](odd_masked_circuit.py), $h=4m$ and the three prefix bits $(a_i,c_i,e_i)$ give each original tail coordinate the signed-pivot decoder $t_i\oplus a_i\oplus(c_i e_i)$. The source reads only completed decoder outputs. For each live tail, its completed decoder is eligible at width $m$. Every path from that decoder to the final output passes through its selected furthest eligible cut: such a path can be extended backwards to the corresponding tail input, and candidate postdominators lie on that extended path in topological order. Every live prefix path also goes through a completed decoder. A dead decoder has no path to cover. Consequently the joint check succeeds at width at most $m$. This remains true after interning equal ordered NANDs: expanding the shared terms conceptually leaves the same ordered expressions and the same paths through completed decoder terms. A source term over decoder outputs and constants cannot be an interior raw decoder fragment, since that fragment has a raw leaf outside any completed decoder subterm. A final output negation or a conjunction of rows all using those same decoders preserves coverage. Unrelated nonthreshold rows require fresh bin recognition and can increase $M$.

The [exact masked-majority control](exact-tail-masked-majority.md) uses these original counts, including the repeated raw threshold ties and a final histogram $(1,2\#\mathrm{SAT}(\varphi))$. The range bound there is $2^m=2^{h/4}$ latent assignments, with a polynomial gate and bit overhead. The [archived pivot rewrite](private-direction-branch-count.md) already establishes unrestricted count preservation under a postdominated XOR. [Moon et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-36126-X_4) treat range-preserving circuit cut reparameterization, and [Gajavelly et al.](https://theory.stanford.edu/~barrett/fmcad/papers/FMCAD2019_paper_43.pdf) treat input elimination and trace reconstruction. Lexicographic access from prefix counts is also established in [ranking and unranking restricted permutations](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0166218X24001914). The result here specifies the raw interval multiplicity and original-word lift for this structural cut.

## A strict forecast after two strict mistakes

In a separate episode on the companion's circuit, put $A=\#\mathrm{SAT}(\varphi)$, $M=2A+1$ and $K=2^{3m}$. Each of the $K$ complete raw prefix blocks contains $M$ models of $G$, with $0<M<2^{m-1}$ and $m\ge4$. Thus the initial majority on $G$ is strictly zero. Supply its true label one. Next query the original-word threshold $T_{2^m-1}$, which selects precisely the zero-prefix block. Among the surviving models its zero and one bins are $((K-1)M,M)$. Since $K>2$, supplying one makes a second strict mistake. The public original word $w=2^m-1$ realizes both labels for every source formula.

All survivors now lie in the zero-prefix block, where decoding leaves the tail unchanged. As in the companion's final probe, $T_{2^{m-1}}$ has zero and one bins $(1,2A)$. Its forecast is strictly zero when $A=0$ and strictly one when $A>0$. None of the three forecasts depends on the tie convention. The final label at the same public word is zero, so the final probe would incur a further mistake exactly on satisfiable sources. The companion's staircase is a separate episode that exhibits ties along its intermediate trims.
