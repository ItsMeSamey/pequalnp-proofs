# Finding a primitive Boolean rewrite that changes a circuit forecast

Consider a deterministic polynomial-time predictor on NAND circuits with a known polynomial mistake bound B(n,S) at every public cap S and on every stream sharing one n-bit assignment. Fix a source circuit F of size s. On any satisfiable input there is a polynomial-time search which returns either a satisfying assignment or a pair of opposite forecasts from the same replayable state, with the two descriptions obtained by compiling one shared-circuit context before and after one universally valid bounded-size Boolean rewrite. The equivalence certificate need not solve a general circuit-equivalence problem. The output has original assignment coordinates or explicit gate lists, a context and a rule instance. No bound is asserted on the number of such forecast faults as the query representation is repaired.

## Circuit presentation

Work with constant-fan-in AND, OR, NOT, NAND and Boolean constants. Compile by a fixed constant-size NAND macro for each operation. Before presenting a circuit to the predictor, delete unreachable gates, identify syntactically equal ordered subterms, and assign new addresses by deterministic output-rooted postorder traversal (the child order is fixed). These operations take polynomial time on a DAG, by bottom-up sorting or interning of ordered gate keys. The resulting serialization depends only on the ordered expression *with repeated identical subterms shared*. It is insensitive to unused gates, labels, and duplicate copies of the same ordered subexpression. No semantic equivalence algorithm occurs in this normalization. The induced prediction rule inherits the mistake bound by forwarding its compiled queries and their actual labels.

A rewrite instance may give its context as a shared DAG with a distinguished placeholder, wired in one or several places. Replacing that placeholder by either side of a fixed Boolean identity gives the two presented circuits after the deterministic compilation above. Other uses of a syntactically equal expression can remain present independently. This explicit convention handles fanout without unfolding the circuit. It does not assert that the serialized byte strings differ at one address. The finite rules below use metavariables for arbitrary circuit wires, and have bounded skeleton size.

## Point deletion

Let the integers 0,…,N−1 index the n-bit inputs, with N=2^n. Let D(j) be the left-associated OR (initial accumulator 0) of the canonical maximal dyadic intervals covering [0,j], in order. An interval of length 2^k is expressed by its high n−k literals as a left-associated AND with initial accumulator 1. Use literal 0 and 1 for the two whole boundary predicates D(−1),D(N−1). Denote by M_j the full singleton AND for j. For every j, D(j−1) OR M_j rewrites to D(j) in O(n+1) steps. Append the singleton. When the last two dyadic cells are siblings, reassociate the rightmost two OR terms and use ((A AND (NOT x)) OR (A AND x))=A. This performs one binary carry. At most n carries occur. Identity elements handle an empty initial list or the full cell. The argument is reversible and includes n=0.

Take the client queries Q(j)=F AND D(j), allowing the two endpoint presentations to be exactly zero and F by elementary constant laws. When F(j)=0 the circuits Q(j−1),Q(j) are connected by a polynomial list of the specified rewrites. Expand the prefix increment in Q(j) and distribute its outer AND to get (F AND D(j−1)) OR (F AND M_j). Only one shared copy of F is needed.

To simplify the second term commute it to M_j AND F. For every wire w use the intended expression g_w=M_j AND w. For a NAND gate w=NAND(u,v) apply
     
     M AND NAND(u,v)  =  M AND NAND(M AND u, M AND v).

Choose reverse topological order of the original gates. At every occurrence of a guarded parent use references to the same guarded copies of its children. Unused definitions may be placed in a work DAG beforehand. At the moment of rewriting g_w all the occurrences that are to change may be connected to its single placeholder, while unguarded original circuitry for F AND D(j−1) stays available. Each gate creates O(1) new nodes, including its incoming references and guarded operand nodes. This works for arbitrary reuse, repeated parents, and constant input wires.

At an input occurrence x_i, the copy M_j AND x_i simplifies to M_j or 0 according to j_i. Move x_i down the left-associated AND for M_j with the rule (A AND b) AND c = (A AND c) AND b until it meets the literal of x_i. Idempotence or complementarity removes it or yields zero, using associative regrouping at the meeting point. Propagate zero or retain the original factors. This takes O(n+1) constant-skeleton steps per input and never duplicates the old F. Constant wires simplify directly. A complemented input/output wire can either be an explicit NAND(w,w) or a NOT macro expanded first. Finally visit the guarded internal gates in forward topological order. Their children have become 0 or M_j. The four fixed identities M AND NAND(a,b)=M or 0 for (a,b) in {(0,0),(0,M),(M,0),(M,M)} give g_w as M_j times its value at j. Because F(j)=0 the guarded output is zero, and OR with zero gives Q(j−1). All displayed laws hold on every Boolean assignment independently of j and of the surrounding DAG.

There are O(s+n²+1) local rewrites and O(s+n²+1) live gates at every step, with a common absolute constant after NAND compilation. These bounds count storing all guarded copies of the original gates and making a separate O(n)-gate working conjunction for each of n input wires. The initial O(n) prefix cubes each cost O(n) gates before sharing. One can materialize the whole list in polynomial time and space; for example with K=s+n²+1, straightforward O(K²) sorting and normalization per member and O(log(K+n+2))-bit addresses give an O(K³ polylog(K+n+2)) bit budget in addition to predictor calls. A fixed sufficiently large S=cK covers all queries, including those tested before a false crossing is known.

## Searching for the fault

At that single public cap run the prefix procedure on these particular D(j) circuits. The sentinel trials are zero and F. A satisfying source makes all B+1 potential feedback mistakes impossible on one original assignment. If prefix bisection reaches a false crossing j, its two opposite forecasts are endpoints of the explicit rewrite list just constructed. Bisect that list using the same frozen state. At most O(log(s+n+2)) additional cloned forecasts isolate one adjacent rewrite with different predictions. The rest of the reducer uses O(B+n+1) logical forecast calls, each with O(B+1) previous rounds on replay. Invariance of the compiled forecasts under these finitely many rules in the exhibited polynomial-size contexts would suffice for SAT search. The bounded-mistake hypothesis by itself gives the witness-or-fault disjunction.

A different presentation gives a simple byte-level companion. Use one fixed wired circuit for F(x) AND [x<k], with n+1 hardwired constant bits for the binary-reflected Gray code k XOR (k shifted right by one), where 0≤k≤N. A chain of XORs reconstructs the binary digits and a linear-size comparator compares the padded x. Use the actual templates k=0,N as the sentinel queries, committing labels 0 and 1 respectively when required by the SAT search. The labels are justified by the same fixed satisfying assignment. Without folding constants or relabeling wires, consecutive presentations differ in one recorded constant bit in an otherwise identical gate list. At a false crossing these circuits compute the same function. This observation concerns the chosen circuit descriptions; adaptive raw gate traces of a fixed local learner can differ in many positions.
