# Incomparable continuations at every variable ordering

Consider the fixed ordered-image language and its polynomial-time [physical recognizer](read-two-ring-image.md). For every integer m≥2, compile the following signed source on m² declared variables using the selector-free encoder. For each of m rows and each of m columns of the variable matrix, require one positive clause over the entire row or column together with all pairwise negative binary clauses within it. The satisfying assignments are exactly the permutation matrices. The source has 2m positive clauses and 2m·binom(m,2) binary clauses. Its bit length is O(m³ log m). The physical word has polynomial length in m and recovers precisely that source.

Fix any ordering of the m² individual source variables. Let A consist of the first floor(m²/2) matrix entries in that ordering, and let B be the remaining entries. For every prefix assignment on A admitting a satisfying completion, record the sets S and T of rows and columns in which that assignment puts a one. Each row and column contains at most one prefix one, and |S|=|T|. A suffix assignment completes this prefix exactly when its ones are a matching in B covering all of the rows outside S and all of the columns outside T, with no other endpoints. Consequently all viable prefixes with the same pair (S,T) have identical continuation sets. Distinct viable pairs have disjoint nonempty continuation sets. This disjointness also excludes domination by a third reachable continuation, since every viable prefix itself has just one such pair.

Here is a lower bound uniform in A. Choose a uniform permutation π and let X be the number of its entries lying in A. Then E[X]=floor(m²/2)/m. For distinct rows i,j, let r_i,r_j count their entries in A and let c count columns in A in both rows. The probability that the permutation uses A in both rows is (r_i r_j−c)/(m(m−1)). Subtracting r_i r_j/m² shows their indicator covariance is at most 1/(m−1). The variance from individual indicators is at most m/4, hence Var[X]≤5m/4. Chebyshev's inequality gives P(m/3≤X≤2m/3)≥1/2 for all sufficiently large m. For a fixed pair of size j, at most j!(m−j)! permutations produce it, since the entries must map S bijectively to T and the complement bijectively to the complement. Thus the number of distinct viable pairs is at least

    (1/2) min_{ceil(m/3) ≤ j ≤ floor(2m/3)} binom(m,j) = 2^{Ω(m)}.

This proves an exponential lower bound in the side length for every static variable order on this family. In particular, a search that explicitly covers all viable prefixes at this cut with individual reachable residuals, allowing removal whenever one reachable continuation contains another, needs that many representatives. The inequality also applies if the containment test is computationally unbounded. It gives a superpolynomial bound in physical word length, which is polynomial in m. The argument concerns a frontier defined on the specified input coordinates. Branch-dependent variable ordering, inference that outputs a witness early, matching-specific algorithms, and symbolic representations that combine many residuals require their own analyses.

The exact same family has a polynomial-time decision and source-witness algorithm. Treat variables as edges of K_{m,m}, negatives as intersecting-edge conflicts, and positive clauses as the incident-edge stars of its vertices. A perfect matching covers all the positive clauses. In fact the diagonal assignment suffices on the full matrix. The continuation-set bound therefore falsifies a proposed small explicit frontier for an easy ordered-image family while leaving the uniform worst-case complexity of the full decision language open.
