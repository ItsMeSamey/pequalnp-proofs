# Learning binary affine hulls of bipartite perfect matchings

Use one bit per labelled original edge, preserving parallel edge identities if present. Given any finite bipartite graph and prescribed forced and deleted edges, one can return EMPTY or at most m+1 actual perfect matchings affinely spanning the feasible matchings in F₂^m. Thus membership in any affine equation on the entire fiber can be tested just on the returned matchings.

Reject contradictory pins or intersecting distinct forced edges. Reserve each forced edge, remove both endpoints and their incident edges, and remove deleted edges. Solve ordinary bipartite perfect matching by augmenting paths. An unbalanced or unmatched residual has no perfect matching. In the feasible case let M be one residual perfect matching (possibly empty). Restoring forced edges and all fixed zero coordinates is an affine injection to the original coordinates.

Contract each edge of M to one vertex. Each other residual edge from a left endpoint paired at u to a right endpoint paired at v becomes a distinct arc u→v; a parallel alternative to a matched edge gives a loop. A simple directed cycle toggles its arcs in and the matched edges at its visited vertices out, yielding an actual perfect matching. The symmetric difference of any two perfect matchings is a disjoint union of alternating even cycles, including the length-two parallel-edge case. In particular every difference from M is a sum of toggles of directed cycles. An arc outside the strongly connected components' internal arc sets belongs to no such cycle.

In each strongly connected component choose a root and directed simple paths P_v from the root to v and Q_v from v to the root, from an outward and inward search tree. For each internal arc e=u→v form the closed walk P_u e Q_v. For each vertex form P_v Q_v. Split these walks at repeated vertices into simple directed cycles, retaining loops and ignoring empty walks. For any directed simple cycle Z, sum the edge walks over its arcs and the vertex walks over its vertices as parity vectors. Each P_v and Q_v occurs twice, leaving Z. Thus the extracted simple cycles span all cycle parity vectors. The map back to original edge differences is linear: copy each arc coordinate to its unmatched edge and put the sum of outgoing arc bits at each vertex on its matched edge. It agrees with the matching toggle on every simple cycle. No formal sum is presented as an actual matching. Generate M and one actual toggle of M per extracted cycle, lift the fixed bits, take differences from the anchor, and retain an actual matching whenever its difference adds rank.

Equivalently, on the contracted digraph the exact direction space consists of the binary flows with zero bits on cross-component arcs and equal incoming and outgoing parity everywhere. For any such flow sum the edge walks for arcs set to one, and add P_v Q_v at each vertex with odd outgoing parity. The path terms cancel exactly as above. This also accounts for residual forced matched edges: a contracted vertex without an internal outgoing arc cannot have its matched bit toggled.

For one distinguished subset D, the avoid fiber is the preceding construction with D deleted. The hit fiber is the union over e in D of fibers forced to contain e, intersected in each case with any prior pins. Overlaps are harmless. Keep a single actual anchor across nonempty branches and rank-test every branch representative relative to it. An affine combination of branch representatives lies in the span of the retained differences about the global anchor, so they generate the hull of the union. Report EMPTY if no branch succeeds, including a hit request for empty D. These claims address one disjunction per fiber; simultaneous hits on many independent sets are a different problem.

Let N=2+|V|+m+the number of explicitly listed pin and D entries after replacing identifiers by dense indices. The O(N) closed walks of length O(N) give O(N²) total arc occurrences and at most as many extracted cycles per branch. Even naively splitting each walk by scanning for a repeated vertex is polynomial. One bit-packed row or actual matching uses m bits. Incremental elimination with at most m existing m-bit rows needs O(m²) elementary bit operations per candidate when rows are read sequentially in pivot order. A fixed array of m rows permits a full sequential scan per candidate. With explicit bounded-width indices, standard record searches on tapes, even the conservative bound O(N⁸) bit time for all O(N) branches suffices: store endpoint and tree records of O(N log N) bits, find/split walks by scanning lists, emit each toggle by scanning the edge list against the cycle, and scan the full elimination table on each candidate. Index comparisons and arithmetic cost O(log N) per elementary record operation. Arbitrary original labels can first be compressed by sorting; include their total input bit length in N for the stated bit clock. The retained certificate has at most m+1 matchings of m bits. Planarity, a face embedding and exact counting are unnecessary.
