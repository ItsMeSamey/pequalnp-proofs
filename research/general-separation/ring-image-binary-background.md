# Ordered-image counting with a binary background

The [four-field solver](ring_image_binary_background.py) checks the ordered image and decodes its signed source CNF. It returns the exact original model count, one verified source assignment when positive, and the physical residue. Off-image words have a separate status.

On normalized width at most three, [restricted-rank projection](cnf-binary-background-rank.md) keeps every mandatory unit or binary constraint, up to normalization and duplicate removal. It selects ternary rows whose misses form a basis on the entire binary model space. The two conjunctions therefore agree on each assignment. The [conditional counter](cnf-binary-background-count.md) charges the cost of the binary background by conditioning on its computed feedback vertex set and running a tree dynamic program. If t is the restricted rank, f the computed feedback set size and h the number of names in the reduced conjunction, at most min(2^h,(h+1)2^(t+f)) elementary terms suffice including witness recovery. The counter restores free-variable multiplicities. Decoded source arity a is bounded by the explicit physical input length, allowing the adapter to expand the factored count, verify a dense witness against every recovered clause and multiply the integer count by (−15)^(k−a−1) modulo 3^k for the supplied precision k.

The [previous raw and planar solver](ring-image-span-gram-count.md) remains available at all widths. At quota q the new counter gets q terms and the old solver gets q raw terms and q determinants. Quotas start at one and double until an answer is available. An optional nonnegative max_round caps the largest quota, truncating the final step. Zero gives one attempt with no paid operations. Exhausting the final attempt returns unknown. The three cumulative charges include unsuccessful calls as well as the final one. With final q≥1 each charge is less than 3q, even when max_round truncates the last doubling. The result also contains the successful backend's own last-call counters. Each attempt includes polynomial input decoding, preprocessing and validation.

With default limits, let T be the smaller of the guaranteed old raw term count and the preceding new term bound when eligible. Once q≥T, at least one of these methods must succeed. On planar-eligible inputs one can also minimize against the full Gram determinant budget: if the old wrapper imposes a stricter internal cap, its own raw fallback was guaranteed to fit q. This includes the polynomial Gram discovery cost and the polynomial factor multiplying 2^(r+rho) for affine rank r and rational nonlinear miss rank rho. Thus the composite takes O(1+T) elementary charges and O(log(2+T)) rounds with polynomial bit overhead in the physical input and polynomial space. On wider formulas the previous full bound applies. The worst case is exponential.

On the [connected star family](cnf-binary-background-rank.md), t=1 and f=0. The procedure counts 3·2^m+1 models and returns one of them in polynomial bit time. The prior raw assignment enumeration uses 2^(m+3) trials on those same inputs. A direct split on the common guard also counts this particular family efficiently.
