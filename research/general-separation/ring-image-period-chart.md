# Essential normal spaces of arbitrary forbidden relations

The [sparse source and physical solver](ring_image_period_chart.py) first computes the [allowed-hull fixed point](affine-hull-closure-referee.md). After this step each nonempty local forbidden set S is given explicitly in d reachable coordinates t=C u, where C is surjective and u ranges over the remaining affine parameters. There is no flatness assumption on S.

The full period space P consists of all translations v with S+v=S. It contains zero and is closed under addition. For any fixed s₀ in S every period occurs among the |S| candidates s+s₀. At most |S|² membership tests suffice, because inclusion of the translate in an equally large finite set implies equality. Compute its orthogonal complement N by Gaussian elimination. The indicator of S factors through a linear map L precisely if ker L is contained in P: the fibers of L are the cosets of its kernel. Taking annihilators proves that row(L) must contain N and that N itself suffices. This proves unique minimality among all linear factor spaces, whether or not their bases are coordinate subsets. Empty and full sets are constant relations with zero essential space. For the pullback to u the periods are C inverse of P, so the essential rows are exactly the rows of N times C; this also follows by surjectivity and rank-nullity.

Use the [arbitrary-rank simultaneous coordinate constructor](arbitrary-rank-common-chart-referee.md) on these lifted spaces and the entire surviving free-coordinate space. In the common chart take every axis with that group's membership bit. Express each such axis in the independent rows of C and evaluate those coefficients on every word in S. Keeping the distinct resulting forbidden tuples captures precisely S, because the common fiber direction of these essential axes is P. The number of projected words does not exceed the explicit input count. The use of an affine parameter offset in the original hull has already been accounted for in computing the reachable words.

Construct one clause per projected forbidden tuple, using every axis on that group's essential scope. If those supports admit the [beta nest elimination](ring_image_beta_chart.py), run it and reconstruct its model. Otherwise run [groupwise semantic Horn recognition and solving](ring_image_group_horn_strict.py) on the projected tuples. This permits a common polarity renaming, and merges groups with identical scopes. Failure of the Horn check applies to the chosen chart; a different chart may work. A cyclic essential hypergraph may also be easy by another method. Only successful exact decisions produce zero or nonzero.

Lift a chart model by solving the saved equations and the free-coordinate axis values; unused axes may be set to zero. Verify every source clause by membership in the sorted true original IDs. No allocation is indexed by a declared binary identifier. All closure iterations, membership tests, common-basis construction, projected CNF materialization, nested elimination and explicit-tuple Horn recognition use polynomial bit time and space in the actual input. Nonflat forbidden sets are genuine examples: the four-bit set {0,1,2,9,8,11} has period 9, has cardinality six (thus is not flat), and its allowed set has size ten, hence spans the four-dimensional space. Its essential dimension is three and the single essential scope admits nest elimination.

The [staged checker](check_ring_period_chart.py) compares all 256 subsets of a three-bit domain against the defining translation test, exercises a non-coordinate period and a cyclic Horn case through physical compilation, and checks sparse output on a thousand-digit identifier.
