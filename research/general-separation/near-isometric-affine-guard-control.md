# Iterated affine decoding, a positive branch rule, and a near-isometric control

Fix B>0 and the clock poly(n) 2^{n-Omega(n/sqrt(log n))} for circuits with Bn log2 n arbitrary fanin-two gates. An exposed conjunction of affine requirements can be solved first by elimination. On its nonempty solution flat consider the remaining circuit, with every input to its nonlinear portion supplied by known affine forms. These include any direct raw inputs to that portion. If these forms together have rank h on the flat, enumerate the 2^h attainable tuples, evaluate the remainder and lift a successful tuple by Gaussian elimination. An affine prefix (gates implementing affine maps) and conjunctive affine outputs can be read directly from supplied gate wiring. The algorithm proves the clock if h<=n-Omega(n/sqrt(log n)) and the description has polynomial size. A semantic high-rank presentation may still admit other simplifications. Savings are measured on actual primary inputs, including any padding.

A second exact rule works for conjunctions of affine 2-clauses, OR(a,b) for two Boolean affine functions. Maintain the solution flat. Simplify constant/proportional affine linear parts into tautologies, impossibility or a forced affine equation. In a genuine independent pair branch on a=1 or on a=0,b=1. These cases are disjoint and cost one and two dimensions. With polynomial bookkeeping, exact counting or search takes O*(phi^n) time by the Fibonacci recurrence, assigning terminal count 2^remaining_dimension. On arbitrary circuits an exposed family of t clauses whose 2t linear parts are jointly independent also permits enumerating only 3^t 2^(n-2t) assignments. Without such exposed constraints the recurrence is not justified.

Here is a deterministic near-isometric SAT-equivalent compiler for arbitrary q-input, B q log2 q-gate F. Put lambda=floor(log2 q), b=ceil(lambda^(2/3)), d=floor(lambda^(1/8)), k=d+ceil(log2 b)+2. Break q logical bits into blocks of length at most b, using their actual length in the last block. Store physical y bits for each block and two k-bit field seeds u,v in GF(2^k). Decode logical bit j=1,...,length as x_j=y_j+Tr(v u^j), with absolute trace to GF(2). Feed the q decoded bits to F. For any x one encoding is y=x and zero seeds. The physical circuit uses N=q+2k ceil(q/b) inputs and s+O(qk^2) gates via successive field multiplications and hardwired linear trace. Polynomial-basis multiplication and reduction use O(k^2) gates. An irreducible degree-k modulus can be found by exhaustive trial in 2^{O(k)} time, polynomial in q. Here k=o(lambda/b), and N-q=O(q lambda^(-13/24))=o(q/sqrt(lambda)), so B(N-q)lambda covers the extra gates for all sufficiently large q. Bit-level thresholds may equivalently use integer roots and rational comparisons.

Fix one logical x. Under independent uniform seeds and the corresponding y, each nonzero binary linear character on one physical block has absolute bias <=epsilon=b/2^k. A character involving a y coordinate, after substitution and writing all seed characters with the nondegenerate trace pairing, averages over v to zero unless P(u)+c=0, where P has at least one nonzero positive-degree term and degree <=b. That equation has at most b roots. A nonzero seed-only character is exactly unbiased. Independence gives the same bound for any nonzero global character. For s'<=d independent linear probes, Fourier inversion gives each of their 2^s' output tuples probability at least 2^(-s')(1-(2^s'-1)epsilon)>0. Thus every fiber realizes every attainable tuple for every rank<=d affine map on the full physical cube.

Consequently after up to d-2 independent affine pins every feasible choice of the pins can still be extended within this single fiber to every jointly consistent pair of further probe values. On YES instances no genuine rank-two clause can be inferred there as a necessary condition beyond what the pins already imply. This is invariant under arbitrary invertible affine changes of variables. It does not forbid unguarded branching, nonlinear probes, decoding, or algorithms for an arbitrary residual circuit. Unlike the companion Reed–Muller example, this construction guarantees surjectivity of arbitrary low-rank probes rather than disjoint exterior rows. Decoding and simply enumerating q inputs falls short of the target by the near-isometry; a full target-clock solver on this image class gives the target clock on arbitrary circuits by composition. No such solver has been obtained.
