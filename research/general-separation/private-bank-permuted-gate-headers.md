# Private banks with pairwise support gaps

Fix d,k≥1 and rational 0<g≤1. Use exactly the real SUM, gate, interface, direct-leaf and atom budgets of [the four-factor theorem](np-sum-formula-private-dag-banks.md), replacing the bank support condition by the following promise on a supplied representation on N bits. Every pair of atom bank footprints, including a pair of identical atoms, has union of size at most (1−g)N. The charged footprint includes every named raw bank input and raw output interface, including aliases and unused entries. Then some NP language lacks such representations at infinitely many lengths. The language may depend on d,k,g. This includes every fixed individual support fraction below one half. It also permits a common private support of size (1−g)N and permits global union N when all pairs have a gap. For instance, three disjoint one-third footprints have global union N and pair gap 1/3.

**Common evaluator.** Under the contrary eventual upper bound for every NP language, represent one fixed P evaluator. Its inputs encode a binary Boolean circuit C with s indexed nodes on n inputs, a gate index i, a permutation π of [n] in n fixed-width entries, and n payload blocks of R bits. Reject malformed fields. Simulated input j is the parity of block π(j). For R=(n+s+2)^2, including a self-delimiting header gives M=nR+O((n+s+1)log(n+s+2)), independent of i and π at fixed C. Thus M/(nR) tends uniformly to one. For s≤2^(εn), log M=O(εn+log(n+2)). One representation for this length works at all permutation and gate headers. Guess its S≤M^d atoms and integer numerators with a common positive denominator H, each of O(S log(S+1)+1) bits. Cramer's rule on an invertible minor of independent Boolean atom columns supplies such coefficients; the zero target can use no terms. Guess length and all syntax and budget checks are polynomial in M.

**Common slots.** Before specializing any header, mark each atom's bank footprint and the raw names with more than L=ceil(c K(M)) direct occurrences, where K(M)=ceil((log log(M+16))^(1/3)) and c>8/g. Every pair of marked sets has union at most (1−g+2/c)M. Take one independent uniform position per payload block. For each atom a let B_a be the block indices where its selected slot is marked. For any pair the expectation of |B_a∪B_b| is at most (1−g+2/c)M/R. For large n this is ≤(1−5g/8)n. Hoeffding gives failure probability exp(−Ω(g²n)) for the bound |B_a∪B_b|≤(1−g/2)n. Union over ≤(S+1)^2 pairs succeeds for sufficiently small fixed ε. Guess and explicitly verify one common qualifying choice. Thus each single marked set also has size ≤(1−g/2)n.

**Separate gate permutations.** For each gate i independently choose a uniform π_i. Fix the evaluator header to (C,i,π_i), and set the selected position of block b to logical x_{π_i^{-1}(b)}, all other payload bits zero. The specialized evaluator computes the actual original gate on x. Maintain this specialization for i throughout every subsequent test. Atom a at gate i has marked logical set π_i^{-1}(B_a), using the original syntactic marks regardless of subsequent simplifications. At two occurrences at one gate, the union misses ≥gn/2 bits by the common-slot property. At t≤3 distinct gates, the t sets are independent uniform subsets of their respective sizes. The uncovered count U has expectation ≥(g/2)^t n. Expose the t permutations one image at a time. Two possible next images are coupled by a transposition of the remaining completion, changing a single marked set by a swap and U by at most one. The exposure martingale has at most 3n differences of size ≤1. Azuma gives probability ≤exp(−(g/2)^6 n/24) that U<(g/2)^3 n/2. This holds even for the same atom at different gates. Union bound over O((s+1)^3(S+1)^3) choices works by decreasing ε. Guess all s permutations, validate bijectivity and check the required union sizes explicitly in poly(M) time. Use any fixed rational h with 0<h<min(g/2,(g/2)^3/2). The random choices prove existence only.

**Verification.** Let v_i be the normalized signed reading at i under its fixed gate-specific specialization. Check input and constant readings by summing squared differences from their prescribed values. At every subsequent gate, the parent readings A,B have already been certified as the actual Boolean gate values at every assignment. Let f be the multilinear interpolation of the gate's truth table; if the two parent indices agree, first take its unary diagonal. Since f²=f on these inputs, the sum over x of v_i²−2v_i f+f equals the sum of squared errors (v_i−f)². A zero total certifies the entire reading even for a malformed real guess. Finally require that the output reading sum to zero. Every product requires either two atoms of one gate or at most three distinct gate indices. An input check can include a raw literal. H³ clears all denominators; every expanded signed coefficient and summed integer has poly(M) bit length since there are polynomially many terms, counts ≤2^n and polynomial-bit numerators.

For each term set the union of selected marked logical coordinates, at most (1−h)n. With specialized headers and nonselected slots pinned, every participating bank is fully determined and can be evaluated once per specialization. Constant folding leaves at most 3nL+O(1) direct raw occurrences; a chain of unary gates collapses to one unary Boolean map. Conjoin the residual trees and encode with a gate variable and at most four truth-table clauses per binary gate plus an output requirement. Retain unbranched unused inputs among the declared variables. Unique extension yields exact counting on V=O(nL+n) variables in fixed-density 3-CNF; constant and tiny cases can be counted directly. Here L=O((log(n+2))^(1/3)), so the October bound poly(V)2^{O(V/sqrt(log V))} gives 2^{o(n)} time per branch. With polynomially many scans, terms and gate checks the complete nondeterministic bit clock is 2^{(1−h)n+O_{d,k,g}(εn)+o(n)}. Take ε small enough also to give O(2^{(1−ε)n}). Accept UNSAT on a passing guess, otherwise return unknown. A separate branch guesses a satisfying assignment, returning the opposite answer if successful. Correct true representations supply a passing UNSAT guess; topological verification excludes it on satisfiable instances. Murray–Williams Theorem 1.1 now yields an NP lower bound at each fixed ordinary circuit exponent under the contrary assumption. That assumption gives a common ordinary polynomial exponent for every NP language by evaluating the bounded descriptions and signed integer arithmetic. This is the contradiction. **Scope.** For any fixed g>0 the distinct-gate estimate provides a constant saving, which can shrink as g tends to zero; the proof gives no uniform result for vanishing gaps. With bare individual support ≥N/2, two different atoms in v_i² may cover every physical block through complementary halves. Their shared gate permutation cannot alter that coverage. The pairwise promise addresses this obstruction directly. Williams's printed four-factor Sum-Product theorem has the corresponding original-arity quarter-support consequence; its gate-evaluator proof hardcodes portions of the represented input. These slot and header arguments explicitly control the live arity after hardcoding. [Williams, Theorem 1.5 and Lemma 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.09121)
