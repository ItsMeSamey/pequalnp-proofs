# A linear-exponent prefix cover for the unary JMV hierarchy reduction

Use Jahanjou, Miles and Viola, **Local reductions, ECCC TR13-099, revision 3**, specifically its proofs of Theorems 7, 11 and 1. Use the native field order and right padding of the [quadratic cover proof](jmv-native-bucket-triples.md), the unary hierarchy language H from the [October proof](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/42907c9e999cc00d6abe7a50bfc1023250edd1b9/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/quantitative-circuits.tex), and JMV locality parameter r=2. For fixed 0<β<1/2 let b=floor(βn), B=2^b, and let K count the ordered triples of first-b-bit variable prefixes that actually occur in a decoded clause. **K=O(nB)**. The argument below uses the internal transition rules of this revision, including those of unary gates. [Pinned JMV revision 3](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2013/099/revision/3/download/)

Use constant-width outer region tags and each region's listed field order. Right-pad short variable payloads with zeros and right-pad the resulting label to Dn bits. This is an injective naming of the native variables when their region and subtype are retained, with one fixed dummy variable reserved. Unused variable names remain free. This preserves satisfiability of the reduction and hence the very same H. The October proof does not specify a padding direction. The shard result concerns the stated naming, without identifying its lexicographically first assignment with that obtained after a different arbitrary relabeling.

The b-bit prefix ends inside the first long identifier for sufficiently large n. This is i in the local verification label (i,j,ℓ), w after the sorter's fixed network and type tags, i in configuration address (i,s), and p following the type tag in the fetch address (t,p,k,d,i,c). The fetch data string has length n/2 up to rounding since r=2, which explains the strict upper bound on β. The local and sorter identifiers have length log T+O(log n)=n+O(log n). Short tags before these fields change the visible identifier length by at most a constant. Distinct full labels in one bucket can have different children.

1. **Local verification and its outgoing edges.** An ordinary internal child of a gate in the ith copy of C_j retains i,j, covering AND, NOT and Copy. In an AND clause all three buckets are thus determined by the parent's identifier prefix. For a local Input gate, revision 3 specifies that the child names the mth bit of z_i or the mth bit of the ith output of sorter j. The configuration wire is formed by concatenating i and m. The sorting output uses row i and puts its bit number after that row. Each choice yields O(B) pairs: one string of b+O(1) initial identifier bits determines both prefixes. For a local x-Oracle gate there are at most n possible fetch output wires on the fixed length-n input. This gives at most nB pairs even though low bits of the parent select the requested output. [JMV, proof of Theorem 1, pp. 17–18](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2013/099/revision/3/download/)
2. **Sorting and its external wiring.** The inner non-input label is (00,w,I,s,d); first-input and control-input labels are (01,w,s) and (10,w,I), with tag 11 reserved for a constant. Within a bit switch a child retains w. A predecessor-switch child uses the cyclic left shift of w or of w with its leading bit flipped. A first-input or control-input child copies w, unless the child is a fixed constant. Which operation is used may depend on fields outside the bucket, but there are only constantly many cases per child. For both children simultaneously, the parent prefix, their new tags and their row prefixes depend on at most b+O(1) row bits including any boundary or wrap bit. Thus there are O(B) such tuples. A first-input gate wired to an external configuration names bit s of the wth input configuration; its raw source is (w,s) in the configuration region. Its two tags change at most constantly many visible row bits. This also gives O(B) pairs. Second-input leaves have no such incoming wire. [JMV, Theorem 7 and proof, pp. 12–14; proof of Theorem 1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2013/099/revision/3/download/)
3. **Fetching.** Every gate here has fan-in at most one. The r=1 construction copies the data, shifts it cyclically by one left or right on indicated states, or ANDs it coordinatewise with the fixed input when reversing direction. A terminal return chooses one of two constant labels. The r=2 construction uses p of length n/2, selecting one of two fixed input segments and updating the segment selector as needed; the stated shift is still by one position. Counter, flags and machine state may select the operation but are after p. For each finite choice of operation, direction and segment, a prefix pair is determined by b+O(1) initial bits plus at most one wrap bit. A fixed mask needs no data bits beyond that prefix. The bound applies to all p strings, including invalid one-hot encodings: the copy, shift and bitwise mask operations are defined on arbitrary strings. For the unary input the interior source bits of both masks are ones. Thus this region contributes O(B) pairs. [JMV, Theorem 11 and proof, pp. 14–15](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2013/099/revision/3/download/)
4. **Clauses, invalid labels and sinks.** Configuration bits have no internal children. A constant, dummy or control-one required-output clause repeats one fixed address three times, so all such clauses together use at most B bucket triples. NOT, Copy, x-Oracle and wired Input clauses repeat one of two addresses, with at most a constant number of permutations. An unwired Input yields a dummy clause. Only the ordinary AND clauses have three potentially distinct labels, handled jointly in cases 1 and 2. Invalid configurations processed by the displayed regular transition rules are included in their bounds; the arguments do not assume valid (or reachable) counters or data. For unused finite region or gadget tags with unspecified behavior, choose the constant/dummy rule. Last-layer and first-column switch cases have the constant, copied-row, or preceding-row addresses listed in case 2. These conventions add no unrestricted address mapping on malformed codes. [JMV, proof of Theorem 1, outputting the clause; Theorems 7 and 11](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2013/099/revision/3/download/)

The four regions exhaust the construction. To list actual triples, use the output-sensitive 3b-bit trie in the quadratic proof. Each feasible-prefix test asks whether some index meets the specified decoded prefix bits. The alternative using the constant supports of those output bits and the October fixed-density counting theorem takes 2^o(n) per test, with O(nK) tests. No recognition of the native region predicates or enumeration of B³ candidate triples is needed by the verifier.

Reuse one guessed circuit per variable bucket using the sorted multitape request join of the companion proof. On each reachable triple, the violation circuit evaluates its shard circuits and its three literal signs on the original shared clause index. If deterministic CircuitSAT costs O(L^e) on input encodings of length L, e≥1, and all shards have at most 2^(σn) gates, the full nondeterministic clock is 2^((β+eσ)n+o(n)). Thus whenever β+eσ<1/2 with σ>0, infinitely many YES lengths of the chosen H admit no satisfying assignment with every native-prefix shard of that size. The conditional result concerns this representation of witnesses, not a claimed completeness property of H. Locality alone cannot yield its interaction bound: a projection decoder can copy three disjoint b-bit chunks of the index when 3b≤q and thereby realize B³ triples.

**Uniform parameters and source versions.** For the verification algorithm initially take fixed rational β,σ with β+eσ<1/2. Compute b by integer division; for σ=a/c in lowest terms compute floor(2^(σn)) by taking the integer cth root of 2^(an). The root computation uses polynomial time in n. The gate cap bounds guesses on every branch. The counts and cover themselves are statements about the physical b-bit prefix and make sense for any integer b in range. For arbitrary real β,σ with 0<β<1/2, σ>0 and the strict clock margin, choose rationals β′>β, σ′>σ still satisfying β′+eσ′<1/2. Use the hard YES lengths established by the rational verifier at these larger parameters. If a satisfying assignment had all its coarser β-prefix shards of size at most floor(2^(σn)), fixing the extra address bits in each circuit would give every finer β′-prefix shard at size at most floor(2^(σ′n)). Constants in the basis make this restriction gate-nonincreasing. This contradicts the rational conclusion on those lengths and never requires a machine to compute noncomputable parameters.
