# All centered comparison pairs survive on the shared cactus source

Use the exact CNF F defined in [the cactus/DFA transfer](cactus-phase-fixed-dfa-transfer.md). Its variables are the edges of a simple cactus of minimum degree two. Every vertex contributes all binary negative conflicts among its incident edges. The remaining rows are positive vertex stars and positive demand triples. Every row has at least two distinct variables, and within a row the unpadded variables are distinct. If the triangle-expanded cubic graph has n vertices, the phase construction has n+1 cycles, 36n cycle vertices, n connecting bridges, 37n edge variables, 36n stars, 40n conflicts and 3n demands. Thus F has k=37n occurring variables and C=79n rows. These dimensions count F itself, before either physical compiler.

The centered algebra chart repeats the last literal of each binary row and gives each row j its own private potential p_j. Its two shells have endpoints (p_j,A),(p_j,B) and (p_j,C_j),(p_j,rail), where A,B,C_j denote source variable IDs of the padded row. The signed requests on B and C_j use opposite signs whenever these coincide through padding. All four signed requests per row are therefore distinct, and no request is shared between rows. In the grouped signed chart the conflict components are exactly 2C copies of K2. The connected comparison graph has comparison-image dimension k+C=116n and cycle rank r=3C−k=200n. These give the frozen affine/Fourier exponent min{116 log₂(3/2),200}n before the affine sublinear overhead. For the separate orientation solver, a compatible cycle uses at most one edge per conflict pair. Its incidence is a row combination of the binary fundamental-cycle matrix. The corresponding dual functional is one on each included edge column and zero on other columns. Each compatible cycle annihilates the column span of a choice of one complementary shore in each pair. It also solves all dual shore-product equations, since at least one member per pair evaluates to zero.

**Coverage lemma.** Every comparison edge of this centered chart lies on a compatible simple cycle. Every source variable x appears at an A/B port of at least two binary conflict rows, one from each endpoint of the corresponding cactus edge. These conflict rows are distinct because a different cactus edge cannot meet x at both ends. For an A/B comparison between x and p_j, follow that edge and the rail edge of p_j. Close the cycle through another binary conflict row containing x, using its rail edge and its A/B edge to x. The same construction covers the rail edge of every row j. For the C_j comparison x–p_j, choose an A/B comparison p_j–y with y distinct from x. Close this path through two other, mutually distinct binary conflict rows, one containing x and one containing y, by using their A/B and rail edges. If j is itself a binary conflict row, x and y occur together in at most one binary conflict, so their remaining choices are distinct. Otherwise each has at least two available rows. All cycles constructed have length four or six and use edges from different shells whenever they visit a private potential. In particular they are compatible. Literal and clause permutations do not affect the argument.

**Survival.** At initialization all compatible-cycle functionals annihilate the empty free space. Suppose inductively that they annihilate the current free space. Coverage for each member of a K2 gives a functional taking one on that column and zero on its mate. In the quotient both columns are nonzero and distinct; their one-dimensional spans have zero intersection and neither contains the other. Thus no containment reduction or common-shore gain is possible. Every gain inferred by eliminating quadratic terms from the squarefree dual equations vanishes on all compatible-cycle functionals, since each satisfies every equation. This preserves the induction through termination. Consequently all 158n components remain active when the actual biclique-dual solver reaches its orientation loop. For the residual rank, choose one A/B edge and the rail edge for every row. The chosen subgraph consists of C two-edge paths from the rail to at most k variables, so its cycle space has dimension at least C−k=42n. All of these row combinations vanish on complementary shores and on the final free space, by the same induction (or by decomposing into simple cycles). The residual syndrome rank is at least 42n. Here shores mean the one-edge sides of the K2s. [Orientation code](../direct-sat-algebra/block-residue/biclique_dual.py)

The orientation loop can return on its first witness. These bounds describe the retained parameters and the number of possible orientations, without lower-bounding time to the first success. The phase assignment with no graph vertices selected gives an immediate satisfying source model, and resolving opposite guard clauses after taking phases reduces source decision to a binary formula. No claim covers semantic rewriting or a separate matching-hull backend.
