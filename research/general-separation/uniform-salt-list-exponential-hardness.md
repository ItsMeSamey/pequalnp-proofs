# Uniform salt lists imply exponential circuit hardness in E

Fix p=2 or 3, B>0 and a binary field or ternary field F_n of degree k(n) over the prime field, with k(n)=poly(n). Require the field parameters from 1^n, arithmetic, and extraction of the k(n) scalar coordinates in a fixed represented basis over the prime field to be deterministic poly(n)-time operations, as in an explicit polynomial basis. For an n-input Boolean function f its multiplicative weighted count at z in F_n^n is

    S_f(z)=Σ_x f(x) ∏_{i:x_i=1} z_i.

Suppose one deterministic algorithm on 1^n produces in poly(n) bit time a list of salts z^(1),…,z^(t) common to all n-input circuits. At every sufficiently large n assume every satisfiable Boolean circuit of at most B n log₂ n gates has nonzero weighted count at some listed salt. A scalar list testing just chosen coordinates of the field result suffices as well. This uniform hitting-list assumption alone implies the existence of a single language in deterministic E whose general Boolean circuit size is at least 2^{εm} at every sufficiently large input length m for a fixed ε>0. No counting oracle is used in constructing the hard language.

Let D=k(n)t bound the number of scalar linear equations specified by the salts. Fix an integer d large enough that M=2^ℓ with ℓ=d floor(log₂ n) is much larger than n(D+1)² for every large n; ℓ≤n there. Both M and D are polynomial in n. Consider a source table f on n inputs that ignores all but its first ℓ bits and equals g on those bits. Each weighted count factors as S_g on the prefix times ∏_{i>ℓ}(1+z_i). Thus it suffices to force S_g on the prefixes to be zero for every salt, regardless of vanishing suffix factors. Form the D-by-M scalar matrix over F_p with columns indexed by the ℓ-bit prefixes and coordinates of their monomial weights. All entries are computable in poly(n) total time. A nonzero Boolean vector in its kernel yields a satisfiable source missed by the list. Such a source must therefore have circuit size greater than B n log₂ n; because any circuit for g can ignore the remaining variables at no gate cost, g has this same lower bound.

For p=2 choose arbitrary values on the free coordinates of the homogeneous system, split as evenly as possible between zero and one, and solve for the pivots. There are at most D pivots; density is 1/2±O((D+1)/M).

For p=3 a linear nullvector need not itself be Boolean. Here is a constructive substitute. Among any L=(D+1)² columns in F₃^D, find a nonempty subset with sum zero in poly(D) time. Partition into D+1 disjoint blocks of D+1 columns. In each take a nonzero linear dependence by Gaussian elimination. If either sign class is empty, the other class already gives a nonempty zero subset. Otherwise the +1 and −1 classes are disjoint nonempty subsets with equal sum w. On that block any of the ternary coefficients 0,1,2 times w can now be realized as a Boolean subset sum: choose neither, the positive subset alone, or both. Find a nonzero dependence of the D+1 resulting vectors w and realize it blockwise. Some coefficient is nonzero and hence the zero-sum subset is nonempty. This argument does not assume any promise on the columns.

Repeat the subroutine on any L remaining columns while possible, removing the selected nonempty zero subset each time. At most M iterations partition all but fewer than L columns into disjoint zero subsets, each of size at most L. Take a union of these sets until its cardinality first reaches M/2; this is possible when M>2L. The result has density between 1/2 and 1/2+L/M and is in the kernel. These are polynomial-in-n computations. Consequently in both characteristics f is not merely satisfiable: it has density 1/2±O(1/n), and its bits are uniformly computable in poly(n) time by regenerating g. The explicit P language so obtained exceeds the proposed gate size on every sufficiently large length.

For the exponential lower bound, use the construction at n=2^j. On an input of length m=dj+e, 0≤e<d, construct the ℓ=dj bit table g for n=2^j and read the table at the first dj bits, ignoring e bits. This takes poly(2^j)=2^{O(m)} time for fixed d. Pinning the e ignored inputs of any circuit at length m gives one for g. Its size must exceed B2^j j at every sufficiently large m, hence is at least 2^{εm} for some ε>0. Ordinary Boolean gates may use constants; replacing constants, if needed, changes the bound only by O(1). This proves the usual exponential E circuit-hardness hypothesis, which entails standard derandomization consequences by Impagliazzo–Wigderson.

For fields of size at least 8(n+1), independent salts hit a fixed nonzero source with constant probability. A polynomial-size list common to all circuits of the specified size exists by a union bound over descriptions. The theorem charges the uniform computation of such a list. It does not require the field to have that size for its implication, grant a method for finding the list, apply to a salt chosen after inspecting C, or exclude algorithms using different nonlinear oracle queries. It gives a barrier to a particular route for deterministic search, not an impossibility theorem for uniform lists or a separation of P and NP.
