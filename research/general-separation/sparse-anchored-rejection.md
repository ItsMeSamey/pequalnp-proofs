# Stable rejection on explicit negative graph distributions

A polynomial-total-size family of Boolean-correct CLIQUE circuits can stably reject only a superpolynomially small fraction of an explicit distribution of partial inputs. The upper completion of each test graph comes with an easily recoverable coloring. Bipartite perfect matching gives a second example for an ordinary polynomial-time decision problem.

## Inputs and fixed-base gate evaluation

Encode an m-vertex CLIQUE input by the bit length ell of m in unary terminated by zero, then m and the threshold k in ell bits each, then one bit per unordered vertex pair. Reject malformed encodings and thresholds outside [1,m]. The input length N depends on m alone. One fixed verifier checks k distinct labels of ell bits each and verifies every pair. Even a separate sequential scan of the input per pair, with binary counters, costs polynomial bit time. For matching use an explicit m-by-m bipartite adjacency matrix with a self-delimiting dimension. All padding below uses physical edge-indicator coordinates.

At a Boolean rejecting base a, replace the coordinates in a mask y by unknown. A Boolean-correct circuit C over binary AND/OR and unary NOT gives either stable zero or unknown under gatewise three-valued evaluation. Let D(y) be its output instability bit. Given the precomputed Boolean base bits of its inputs, an AND gate at 00,11,01,10 uses, respectively, AND, OR, first projection and second projection on the two instability bits. OR has the dual rules. NOT copies instability. Thus D costs at most one binary monotone gate per gate of C. A possible accepting completion forces D(y)=1 by soundness, since the base also completes that input. Every zero of D persists throughout its downward closure. [Ikenmeyer et al., fixed-base hazard derivative](https://doi.org/10.1145/3320123)

Let O be a transitive orbit of masks under vertex relabelings. If D is zero on a fraction p>0 of O, then t=ceil((ln|O|+1)/p) independent relabelings leave a particular mask uncovered with probability at most exp(−tp). Some t relabelings cover O. The AND of these relabeled derivatives has at most t(|C|+1) gates. Every positive test persists when the family of positive tests is invariant. This nonuniform choice of relabelings is permitted in a lower bound for all circuits. For a collection of different circuits, a union bound charges the sum of |C|+1. The selector may have full knowledge of the mask.

## The CLIQUE orbit

For every sufficiently large valid N, let k=4 floor(log₂(N)/4), L=ceil(log₂ k), n=k²L² and r=2n. Inside the ambient graph choose an r-vertex block, set the threshold to k and set all outside edges to zero. The base is the empty graph with that fixed header. Erase the edges of a uniform relabeling of the complete balanced c-partite graph inside the block, c=k/2. Every completion has clique number at most c. Nonadjacency recovers the upper graph's partition. Rejection sampling and a shuffle sample a uniform permutation in polynomial expected bit time. Writing every input bit costs polynomial time. The erasure budget is at most binomial(r,2)=O((log N)⁴(log log N)⁴).

Suppose a size-s circuit yields stable rejection with probability p. The relabeled conjunction described above accepts every graph containing a k-clique. Restrict it to n vertices by setting all remaining edges to zero. Given any (k/4)-colorable graph on those vertices, split each color class into independent chunks of size at most b=r/c. The total number of chunks is at most k/4+n/b=k/2. There are exactly enough new vertices to fill these chunks and unused parts to b. The original graph is a subgraph of that balanced complete multipartite orbit member. The restricted conjunction separates k-cliques from (k/4)-colorable graphs on n vertices.

De Rezende and Vinyals, Theorem 3, equation (4), gives a size lower bound (n/(Ak² log n))^{Ω(k)}. Its base here is Ω(log k). Since |O|≤r!, the resulting inequality is

    p ≤ O((s+1) r log r) exp(−Ω(k log log k)).

With k=Θ(log N), every polynomial-total-size family has probability N^{−ω(1)} of producing even one stable zero. The same argument applies to a Boolean circuit for a predicate accepting all graphs with a k-clique and rejecting all c-colorable graphs, since the base and the specified completions have those answers. The cited paper identifies Tardos's total monotone polynomial-time extension of the clique–coloring promise. Correctness on that promise and polynomial-time semantic evaluation of the ternary input are consequently insufficient. [De Rezende and Vinyals, Theorem 3 and proof of Lemma 24](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2025.36)

The outstanding implication is precise. If total CLIQUE has an ordinary deterministic polynomial-time decider, can its algorithm be used to obtain a polynomial-total-size family of Boolean-correct circuits stably rejecting probability at least N^{−d} on infinitely many of these lengths, for some fixed d? A proof would rule out all such deciders. Their standard time-to-circuit simulation supplies only Boolean correctness. The exponent can depend on the decider throughout. The fact that CLIQUE belongs to NP follows from the fixed verifier independently of any circuit construction.

## A matching control

Take r divisible by four, h=r/4, r≤m. Outside an r-by-r active block, fix disjoint diagonal matching edges and no cross edges. The active base is empty. Choose h vertices independently and uniformly on each shore and erase every edge incident to the chosen vertices. Each orbit member's upper graph has maximum matching size exactly 2h<r. Its chosen sets are immediately recovered as its vertices of degree r. There are at most 4^r orbit members.

Coverage p>0 by a size-s circuit yields a monotone conjunction of size O(r(s+1)/p) accepting every perfect matching. Every graph of matching number less than h is a subgraph of an orbit member. Endpoints of a maximal matching meet every edge, use fewer than h vertices on each shore and can be padded independently to h. Thus the subgraphs supply the small-matching negative promise. Rao's Theorem 1, revision 5, with 2r vertices and thresholds r and r/4, gives

    p ≤ O(r(s+1)) exp(−Ω(√r)).

If at most u≤m² input bits may be erased, take the largest multiple of four r≤√u. The condition u^{1/4}/log N→infinity makes coverage smaller than every inverse polynomial for every polynomial-total-size circuit family. Matching has an ordinary polynomial algorithm. Given a separate stable encoding of the unknown mask, one can also evaluate both extremal graphs in polynomial time to recover exact ternary semantics. This computational ability coexists with the gatewise lower bound. [Rao, Theorem 1, revision 5](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/129/revision/5/download/)

For comparison, covering all negative matching masks by sound positive edge-disjunctions requires exactly binomial(2r,r−1) clauses. Hall's condition says that each maximal negative graph misses a rectangle S×T with |S|+|T|=r+1. Adding any edge from that rectangle produces a perfect matching. Match the remaining rows in S to all columns outside T and the rows outside S to the remaining columns of T. A clause that rejects this graph uses only missing edges and must contain every one of them. Conversely all these rectangle clauses suffice. Summing over |S| gives the stated count. Conjoining an individual clause with the ordinary decision circuit gives a dedicated stable rejecting circuit. Bypass the clause when any fixed coordinate differs from its slice value to retain global Boolean correctness. For arbitrary size-s branches picked by a b-bit index, coverage of all negatives only implies b+log₂(s+1)=Ω(√r) by the preceding inequality. The larger exact binomial count uses the single-clause restriction.
