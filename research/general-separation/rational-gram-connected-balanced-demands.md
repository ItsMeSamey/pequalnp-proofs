# A connected subcubic family separating rational miss rank from single-demand affinity

Fix an even integer k≥2. Use the expanded k-path theta of affine-hit-theta-matching.md: terminals U and V joined by paths U—a_j—b_j—V, with each terminal replaced by a path of alternating ports and spacers in cyclic order. The replacement is planar, simple, and bipartite. Each perfect matching selects both outer edges of exactly one path j, the middle edge of every other path, and the unique internal port-path completions. It has k matchings and 7k−4 edges. Add a four-cycle with one designated edge e. Join one cycle vertex by a single bridge to the degree-two vertex a_1, choosing the relative coloring appropriately. This preserves planarity (place the four-cycle in a face incident to a_1), bipartiteness and maximum degree three. The bridge cannot be used because it is the only cut edge of an even-order four-vertex set. Thus matchings are exactly pairs (j,z), j in [k], z in {0,1}, with z the selected bit of e. In particular there are 2k and every edge coordinate is a function of j alone, of z alone, or is constant.

Form the standard signed source with one bit per edge, all vertex stars and all negative pair conflicts at each vertex. For every balanced subset S of [k] (size k/2), add one positive demand comprising e and the U-side outer edge at each index outside S. There are d=binom(k,k/2) such sets. They are distinct and equicardinal. In each, e is disjoint from every outer edge, hence none is contained in a vertex star. The stars delete no edges, and no extra demand contains another or has a star as a subset (the demands themselves are matchings). The connected root avoids the triangle/claw ambiguity of line-graph inversion. Thus all d survive the single-demand frontend's original filter.

The miss function on base matchings is the product of the indicators of z=0 and j∈S.
Every affine GF(2) function of the complete root edge coordinates restricts to the sum f(j)+h(z) modulo two, since the bridge is fixed and the two blocks depend separately on j and z. Its difference between z=0 and z=1 must be independent of j. This fails for every g_S, because S is nonempty and proper. Complementing g_S preserves this failure. Thus every hit is nonaffine even when the classifier may inspect every physical root edge. The older single-demand classifying algorithm finds zero affine rows and d nonlinear ones and its explicit inclusion–exclusion expansion has 2^d subsets per full count.

Let A be the d by 2k matrix of miss functions evaluated on the matchings. Delete the zero z=1 columns, leaving the incidence matrix of the k/2-subsets. Its rational row span contains every e_i−e_j: subtract the two rows formed by adjoining i or j to a common (k/2−1)-subset avoiding both. These differences span the zero-sum hyperplane, and any incidence row has coordinate sum k/2≠0 over the rationals. Therefore rank_Q(A)=k. The Gram matrix AA^T has entries |S∩T| and the same rank by positivity over the reals and equality of rational and real rank. Choosing k independent actual rows suffices for the conjunction of all demands: where every basis miss evaluates to zero, every miss evaluates to zero. Conversely all-zero misses include those of the basis. No change to the original source or matching table is involved.

The Gram entries are counts of matchings simultaneously missing the corresponding two actual demands, computable by deleting the union of their named edges in the original planar root. Constructing that matrix needs O(d²) such count requests and rational elimination polynomial in the explicit source size. Inclusion–exclusion on the chosen original demands then has 2^k terms. Since d≥2^k/(k+1), this count and the O(k) self-reduction passes for witness extraction remain polynomial in explicit input length. On z=0 a balanced S containing any given j exists, so that matching fails at least one demand. On z=1 every demand is hit. The exact survivor count is k. For an original witness take j=1 and the alternating square matching containing e, then use the indicated unique theta completions.

For exact dimensions the connected root has h=7k+1 edges, 6k+2 vertices, 2k−2 vertices of degree three and 4k+4 of degree two. Consequently it yields 10k−2 conflict rows and C=16k+d total signed clauses; their literal count is L=34k−2+d(1+k/2). Order them and their edge IDs explicitly, then use the selector-free ordered ring compiler. Its pre-expansion graph has e_0=3h+L edges and v_0=3h+C vertices. Its output has 46e_0+26v_0+1 raw variables, 45e_0+27v_0+2 rows, E=13e_0+6v_0 expanded edges and precision E+h+1. The physical residue is k(−15)^E and is nonzero modulo 3^(E+h+1) since 0<k≤2^h<3^(h+1). The physical bit length is at least d and at most O((kd)²+kd log(kd)), using canonical residues and indexed rows. Enumerating the d rows is uniform polynomial time in this explicit output size. Thus the old 2^d expansion is superpolynomial in physical input length while the rational-rank procedure is polynomial on this family.
