# Sparse support affine-hull closure

Compress the binary IDs of the m used variables. Discard tautological clauses, deduplicate literals, then group by exact support S. Each normalized clause contributes a single falsifying tuple. Deduplicate these tuples and retain the empty clause if present.

Maintain H=h+B u with B injective, initially the whole space. Factor B restricted to S as D C with D injective and C surjective onto d coordinates. A reachable local word is h_S+D t where t=C u. Solve D t=f+h_S for each listed forbidden f. Discard unreachable tuples and denote the resulting forbidden t set by E, of size k.

If k=2^d the group rules out all of H. If 2k<2^d the allowed points span the entire local affine space, since any proper affine subspace contains at most half its points (handle d=0 directly). Otherwise enumerate at most 2k coordinates, compute the affine hull of their allowed subset, and describe it by independent equations L t=b. Impose L C u=b. A nonempty proper hull strictly reduces dimension because C is surjective. Reparametrize H by Gaussian elimination and restart scanning. Every original solution survives every restriction. At most m strict restrictions precede a stable pass. The update is exactly the affine hull of the allowed subset of H because every allowed local point includes its full parallel kernel fiber. These updates are monotone under inclusion, hence a stable result from the full space is the greatest simultaneous fixed point, independent of update order.

Recompute all projections at the fixed point. Skip empty E, and test other E for flatness by comparing cardinality with the size of their affine hull. Translate a flat of codimension one or two described by A t=b into the forbidden conjunction (A C)u=b. All groups use the same global u. Apply the simultaneous independent chart criterion, solve 2SAT, lift its chart model to u, and return h+B u. Conversely each original solution realizes a chart model. Unsupported groups give unknown. The procedure uses polynomial time in explicit bit length with compressed IDs and a sparse assignment (default zero). Dense output adds the declared arity cost. The example (¬x) ∧ (x∨a∨b) now reduces to binary form.

## Beyond flatness

Complete a compatible chart of flat groups to a basis by greedily inserting unit rows in free coordinates. For each remaining group consider chart axes v in row(C), solving w C=v. For every one- or two-axis pattern count words of E on which the selected w rows realize the specified bits. Since these rows are independent, the whole cylinder has 2^(d−j) points for j selected axes. Equality of count certifies containment in E. If all words of E are covered by certified cylinders, emitting the corresponding 2SAT clauses gives exact equivalence. This sufficient test is polynomial for each chosen chart. In x,y,z order, the explicit forbidden set {000,001,100} is nonflat, and its complement affinely spans all three dimensions. Its standard-chart representation is (x∨y) ∧ (y∨z), demonstrating a strict extension.

## Exact test for a prescribed chart, group by group

The restriction that each chosen axis lie in row(C) can be eliminated. Let P consist of one or two distinct chart rows and consider every pattern b on P. Project the cylinder Pu=b into t=Cu. The image J_b is a coset of C(ker P). Its codimension j is dim(row(C) intersect row(P)), by the dimension formula for the stacked matrix. Compute this coset by linear elimination; it has at most two normal equations. It is contained in E exactly when the number of listed E words in it equals 2^(d−j). For each such pattern emit the clause forbidding Pu=b. Require the union of all certified projected cosets to cover E.

For sufficiency, take any u with Cu in E and a certified J_b covering Cu. With b'=Pu, both J_b and J_b' contain Cu. Images associated with the same P are parallel cosets, hence equal. Thus b' was certified and this u violates an emitted clause. Conversely an emitted clause only excludes u with Cu in E. Necessity follows because any exact 2CNF in this chart is a union of such forbidden cylinders and each constituent projects into E. This is an exact polynomial test for groupwise 2CNF representability in a fixed chart. Global semantic equivalence attained through redundancy between different groups is a further question.

## Semantic recognition barrier

Given arbitrary CNF F(z), form G=F(z) AND (a OR b OR c) on fresh variables. If F is unsatisfiable then G has the empty model set, representable by contradictory unit clauses. If z0 satisfies F, restrict any putative affine-chart 2CNF to the three-dimensional affine slice z=z0. Each chart clause forbids an affine flat of codimension at most two, whose intersection with the slice is empty or has at least two points. The actual forbidden slice consists of the singleton 000, which cannot be a union of such intersections. This proves coNP-hardness of exact whole-formula recognition even with an arbitrary invertible affine chart on the original variables. This statement does not quantify over auxiliary variables. In a fixed identity chart the problem is also in coNP: failure of closure under bitwise majority has a three-model certificate. Conversely, closure under majority implies that an assignment whose every unary or binary restriction extends to a model extends on successively larger coordinate sets. For a set of size at least three, take three models matching it except possibly at three distinct individual coordinates, by induction, and apply majority. Thus the clauses forbidding nonextendible unary and binary patterns represent every nonempty majority-closed relation.

Guard each clause of G above with an additional literal ¬s. The all-zero assignment now always satisfies the input. When F is unsatisfiable the represented relation is exactly ¬s. Otherwise fixing s=1 and z=z0 produces the same forbidden singleton. The hardness therefore holds even on promised satisfiable inputs supplied with a witness.
