# A polynomial-time tour verifier with a hard formal degree component

For each N≥2 take N² Boolean edge variables X_ij for a labeled directed graph, including the diagonal. The single uniform predicate Tour(X) accepts when the entire edge set is one directed spanning cycle. Check that every row and column has exactly one 1. Follow the resulting successor permutation from vertex 1, accepting exactly when its first return to vertex 1 occurs at step N. Scanning the explicit matrix to find successors gives a deterministic polynomial bit-time algorithm in the ordinary input encoding (include N in a header and reject malformed matrices). As an NP verifier for Hamiltonicity one can use a proposed matrix X, check Tour(X), and also check that X is contained in the graph supplied as the instance. Fix that instance to the complete directed graph; the verifier on witness coordinates then computes Tour.

**Cube coefficients.** Over any field F, the unique multilinear polynomial representing a Boolean function f on the cube has coefficient on the squarefree monomial indexed by S equal to

    c_S = Σ_{T⊆S} (−1)^(|S|−|T|) f(1_T).

Indeed, evaluation on the indicator of T sums the coefficients indexed by subsets of T. Subset Möbius inversion works in all characteristics because the incidence matrix is triangular with diagonal one. If f rejects every support smaller than N, all coefficients below degree N vanish. At size exactly N the coefficient is f(1_S). For Tour these nonzero coefficients index the directed spanning cycles, each once, without a choice of starting vertex. Hence the degree-N homogeneous component is the formal Hamiltonian-cycle polynomial HC_N. The predicate asking whether a graph contains a directed spanning cycle gives exactly the same component, since on graphs of at most N edges the two predicates agree. The polynomial-time predicate that its entire input is a permutation matrix likewise gives Per_N as its degree-N component.

**Quantitative consequence of the cited theorem.** The pinned November 24 preprint states that there is an absolute c>0 such that for every characteristic χ, including zero, there is N₀(χ) so that for every field of that characteristic and every N≥N₀(χ), the degree-N-component border size of HC_N is at least exp(c(log N)²/(log log N)²). For Per_N the same statement requires χ≠2. Size counts binary additions, subtractions and multiplications; scalar inputs and variables are free. The closure is over the algebraic closure and concerns degree-N coefficients, allowing arbitrary components in other degrees. Applying that theorem to the coefficient identity above gives the stated lower bound even to approximate this single formal component in its border sense. In terms of the N²-bit matrix input it is still superpolynomial. No algorithm for extracting homogeneous components is needed for this inference. **The exact obstruction.** Arithmetizing a polynomial-size Boolean circuit by AND ↦ product and NOT ↦ 1−x gives a polynomial-size formal circuit agreeing on Boolean points, using a Boolean basis of AND and NOT. Cube agreement identifies formal polynomials modulo the ideal generated by X_e²−X_e. Even the degree-N homogeneous component can change on choosing a representative: multiply X_e²−X_e by a squarefree monomial of degree N−1 on other edges. The product vanishes at every Boolean point but has a nonzero degree-N term. For the particular Tour family, the preprint's bound precludes any polynomial-size arithmetic family whose formal degree-N part equals the degree-N part of the unique multilinear representative of those cube values. This rules out an unrestricted polynomial-size lift with that guarantee even for a fixed P verifier; it also rules out polynomial-size output circuits computing that whole multilinear representative. Polynomial-size circuits for other representatives continue to exist by direct arithmetization. The argument gives no standard Boolean circuit lower bound for Tour, for Hamiltonicity, or for counting tours from Boolean encodings.
