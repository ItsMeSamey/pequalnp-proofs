# Limits of dense table amplification by nondeterministic oracle verifiers

Let a source be a binary table T on n-bit addresses, with N=2^n. A fixed uniform nondeterministic verifier, on (1^n,y) and oracle T, defines one output bit f_y(T) by existence of an accepting computation. Assume all branches take polynomial time in n, the target addresses y have a fixed polynomial-time computable length ℓ(n)=poly(n), and at most b(n) source-table membership queries occur on each accepting computation for a known polynomial bound b. The verifier may adapt its queries, use either answer, and choose a different witness for every target bit. Encoded distance is the fraction of the 2^ℓ target coordinates at which two encodings differ. Distinguish the following unconditionally nonuniform controls from a uniform extraction under P=NP.

**Monotone encodings.** First assume semantic monotonicity: each f_y is nondecreasing under addition of ones to T. For T_i(x)=[int(x)<i], take successive i from N/4 to 3N/4, with n≥2. All these sources have density between 1/4 and 3/4 and circuits of size O(n). The sum of the relative distances across the N/2 adjacent pairs is at most one, because each target bit changes at most once. Hence some pair differing in exactly one input has relative encoded distance at most 2/N. The argument needs no bound on accepting path queries. The index of a minimizing pair can depend on the verifier and length.

**Mixed-polarity query encodings.** Put B={x:x₁=0}, of size M=N/2, and use the baseline T(x)=x₁. For S⊂B write T_S for the baseline with the points of S set to one. Choose a uniform t-element S, 1≤t<M, then choose a uniform j∈B outside S. Let S'=S∪{j}. For each target y the probability of changing from acceptance on T_S to rejection on T_{S'} is at most b/(M−t). Given an accepting T_S, fix one of its accepting paths. It survives unless it queried j. For the reverse event condition on S': it is a uniform (t+1)-set, and the removed j is uniform within it. An accepting path for T_{S'} survives deletion unless it queried j, giving a reverse-event bound b/(t+1). Adding the two bounds and averaging over y gives a pair with encoded distance at most

    b/(M−t) + b/(t+1).

For every fixed positive integer h choose t polynomial in n so that t≫b and this bound is O(n^{−h}); for example take t=n^{h+1}(b+1) for large n. Both sources then have density 1/2+o(1), differ at exactly one input, and are represented by O(nt) gates using a sparse list. For each n these are actual succinct sources. The advice selecting the list may depend on n.

If exact balance on both sources is desired, remove the same t predetermined points in the baseline one-half, then add a uniform t-set S in B. Swap uniform i∈S with uniform j∈B outside S. This changes precisely two raw bits and keeps both tables exactly half full. Given S and an accepting path, the chance of touching i or j is at most b/t+b/(M−t). The ordered pair of resulting sets is symmetric under reversal; hence the expected encoded distance is at most 2b/t+2b/(M−t). Some exactly balanced pair has that distance. Distinct equal-weight tables cannot differ at only one raw bit.

**Conditional uniform extraction.** For every fixed verifier and choice of h the above selected pairs can be made into two uniform P languages under P=NP. A candidate consists of i for the threshold construction, or polynomially many explicit n-bit addresses encoding the sparse pair or swap. Admissibility is polynomial-time testable; source membership on either side is P with the candidate given. Thus under P=NP the two encoded bits are decidable in P given n in unary, the candidate, and y. The number D of y where these bits disagree is then a #P function of (1^n,candidate). Stockmeyer's deterministic polynomial-hierarchy approximation to #P yields a uniform factor-two estimate D̂, with D/2≤D̂≤2D, in polynomial time under the same collapse (including the exact zero case). Let δ be the corresponding rational upper bound proved above. There exists an admissible candidate with D̂≤2δ·2^ℓ. Using the polynomial-time estimator as a predicate, lexicographic prefix search under P=NP finds such a candidate in polynomial time: query whether a completion of the bounded candidate exists. Every resulting pair has actual relative distance ≤4δ. Each selected source language recomputes the candidate on 1^n and evaluates its threshold or sparse list directly. Finitely many small lengths can be assigned separately. This gives a uniform-P monotone-encoder control with distance ≤8/N and, for each h, a mixed-encoder control with distance O(n^{−h}). For mixed queries the two P languages can depend on h as well as on the verifier; a single negligible-distance uniform pair is not asserted.

The obstruction concerns black-box encodings of source decision bits and a proposed distance guarantee for every pair of sources. It does not bound methods that inspect verifier descriptions, nor prove hardness of either source. A witness for one accepted target coordinate protects a pair whenever its queried inputs are unchanged. The construction therefore applies even with arbitrary nondeterministic witness selection; it does not assert that such a witness can be found deterministically. The averaging argument needs no unary hard slice: the controlled sources are dense ordinary binary-input languages.
