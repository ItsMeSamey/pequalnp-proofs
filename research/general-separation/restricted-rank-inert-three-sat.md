# Pure ternary SAT with no degree-two consequences in the raw failure span

A fixed strict 3-CNF gadget has exactly one satisfying assignment, the all-zero word, and at most one clause per triple of variables. On 16 bits take one clause per unordered triple, independently forbidding a uniformly selected one of its seven nonzero bit patterns. Zero always survives. Each nonzero word has a one in some position and therefore has a nonzero pattern on at least binom(15,2)=105 triples. Its survival probability is at most (6/7)^105<2^−21, since 2·6^5<7^5. The expected number of nonzero survivors is less than one, proving existence. **Twelve bits also suffice**: binom(11,2)=55 and 4·6^9=40,310,784<40,353,607=7^9, so 2^12(6/7)^55<1. The [12-bit generator and direct checker](forced_zero_ternary_fixture.py) choose signs deterministically by conditional expectations. Process the 220 triples in lexicographic order. For every still-surviving nonzero word let r count its nonzero patterns on unprocessed triples. With common denominator 7^220 its current expected survival weight has numerator 6^r·7^(220−r). At the next triple a zero-pattern word is unaffected. For a nonzero pattern j, the word is killed if j is forbidden and otherwise its remaining weight is the precomputed numerator for r−1. Sum those future numerators separately for each of the seven pattern classes and forbid a largest class, breaking ties by the smallest pattern. The minimum resulting expectation is at most the average over the seven choices, which is the current expectation. At termination the survivor count is an integer below one. This needs at most 4095·220 word/triple visits plus updates of the classified words, using integers of O(220+12) bits. The separate signed-literal checker enumerates all 4096 original assignments. In the accounting below use g=12 or 16 and designate one gadget coordinate z.

Start from a strict 3-CNF with m clauses on n declared variables (three distinct variables per clause). For the s occurring variables with t_i occurrences form disjoint cycles of h_i=max(3,t_i) fresh copies. Assign each occurrence a different copy and rewrite the original ternaries with those copies and their original polarities. On each directed cycle edge a→b add the clause (¬a ∨ b ∨ z). Include the fixed gadget with entirely separate variables, except that its z is shared by these new rows. This is the occurrence-splitting construction of the [raw cycle note](cnf-miss-independent-occurrence-cycles.md) with a shared forced-false padding literal. The ordinary implication-cycle construction is classical. [Tovey, A simplified NP-complete satisfiability problem](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-218X(84)90081-7)

Every row has exactly three distinct variables. Gadget rows have distinct all-gadget supports. Link rows have two copy variables and z; h_i≥3 makes their unordered copy pairs distinct. Rewritten source rows use only occurrence copies and have pairwise disjoint supports. Hence all ternary supports in the entire output are different. The failure indicator of each row, as a multilinear polynomial on the full binary cube over F₂, contains its unique cubic support monomial with coefficient one. Any nonempty linear combination contains exactly the selected rows' cubic monomials at top degree, so its degree is three. In particular the raw failure span contains neither a nonzero affine function nor a nonzero polynomial of degree at most two, including any individual width-at-most-two clause failure. For an engine starting from that span without preassigned bits or supplied affine equations, a rule that can make its first restriction or add a binary failure only by extracting such a nonzero element stops at this first test. This concerns linear span on the original cube. Arbitrary consequences under multiplication or semantic preprocessing need not have this property: z itself vanishes throughout the feasible set. The earlier unpadded link clauses are binary and therefore immediately available to a binary-extraction engine.

Every satisfying assignment sets the complete gadget to zero, so the link rows enforce equality around each cycle. Conversely every source assignment determines all copy bits and all gadget bits uniquely. Let H=Σh_i≤3m+3s. With a binary arity header, use H+g active variables, declare n−s additional unused output variables by extending the binary arity, and biject the unused inputs and the trailing output bits in sorted order. Thus satisfiers correspond exactly, preserving the full count including unused assignments. The output has m+H+binom(g,3) clauses, each with three literals, and polynomial encoded length and construction time in the ordinary explicit-clause source with binary arity. For explicit variable lists the unused names may instead be listed explicitly. No bound of three on z's occurrence count is claimed. The target requires no auxiliary supplied basis or branch condition.

The earlier [binary-span saturation families](binary-span-saturation-families.md) already establish distinct-scope NP-hardness with 1,024 lifts per source model. The additional result here is exact parsimony: the generated original-variable gadget has a unique zero model, giving exactly one lift per declared source assignment that satisfies the formula.

This yields NP-completeness of satisfiability on pure width-three, distinct-scope formulas satisfying the stated rank property (indeed distinct ternary scopes already imply it). Membership uses only occurring bits; the syntax can be checked in polynomial time. This is a limitation of the specified rank-based inference rule, not a computational lower bound for SAT.

## An exponential branch count for the specified production wrapper

Consider the uncapped [saturated rank-pivot wrapper](ring_image_saturated_rank_pivots.py) at SHA-256 517ad10e6eb52f511bc676e37e9c2c5424ca1b3adb40b71a08162172090fe12d with [its pivot engine](source_remote_pivot_rank.py) at f6ecce6a6179e1f60ace15ced2952c9ae0371a00f43f65ebece5ce79986cb5f6. On the displayed pure-ternary image the width test always selects saturated pivots. Canonicalization deletes nothing, saturation adds no binary clause, and the initial binary implication graph is empty. For each ternary all three partial assignments satisfying exactly one of its literals pass the binary test. Hence no row is common-reducible. Implication search from such an assignment reaches just the three input literals in that assignment. Across the three assignments both signs of each underlying variable, and no signs of any outsider, are reached. A pivot therefore covers exactly the triples containing its variable.

All failure vectors are independent on the unrestricted binary seed domain by their private cubic monomials. Expressing them relative to a fixed root is an invertible translation, so full rank survives. For the selected pivots to reach full rank they must collectively cover every row. In particular each of the m rewritten source ternaries must be hit, and these m triples are vertex-disjoint. Thus the number g' of selected pivots satisfies g'≥m, regardless of tie-breaking.

For each m≥8 use the eight clauses forbidding all patterns on three variables, then m−8 arbitrary positive ternaries on fresh disjoint triples. Apply the construction above with no unused names. Every output is UNSAT. In the uncapped pivot engine the final loop iterates over the Cartesian product of g' binary choices. Each iteration increments branch_calls and calls the binary solver. The assigned pivot bits and retained binary halves are equivalent to all owned ternaries conditional on these bits: a deleted literal is the singleton excluded by that bit. All ternaries are owned because the vectors are independent. Thus each branch fails, and the loop executes exactly 2^g' binary calls before returning UNSAT. The width-greater-than-three fallback is never invoked.

Source arity, rows and occurrences are O(m), so the recovered source has O(m log(m+2)) explicitly encoded bits. Under the selector-free [physical compiler](read_two_ring_nonzero.py) its graph has O(m) vertices and edges, O(m) physical variables and constant-width rows, and precision O(m); the explicit ring point and dense incidence names take O(m²+m log(m+2)) bits. Thus at least 2^m calls are superpolynomial even in the physical input length. This is a lower bound for this particular sequential uncapped wrapper on its own compiler image. Other algorithms can, for instance, detect the contradiction in the initial source before rewriting; there is no SAT complexity-class conclusion.
