# Separator width under bounded affine contexts

Fix a nonempty relation C={x in F₂ⁿ : Ax=b}. A compiled relation R on the same original variables preserves t parity probes if every conjunction of at most t affine equations on those variables is satisfiable on R exactly when it is satisfiable on C. Auxiliary variables, if any, have already been existentially projected. When t≥1, R⊆C: the opposite of any defining row is an allowed unsatisfiable probe. No analogous containment follows from the empty context alone.

At a terminal cut S|T suppose the projected relation is the union of w rectangles in the two terminal blocks. This holds for a genuine separating interface of w states even with arbitrary local existential choices and complemented literals. Inside any nonempty rectangle contained in C the partial syndrome A_S x_S is constant: fix a right member and vary the left one. For C the feasible left syndromes form an affine translate of im(A_S)∩im(A_T) of dimension λ=rank(A_S)+rank(A_T)−rank(A). Put h=min(t,λ). Choose h linear functionals independent on the intersection; every prescribed joint value occurs on C and can be tested with h probes supported on S. Therefore R realizes at least 2ʰ syndromes and w≥2ʰ for t≥1. For t=0 the empty context forces at least one rectangle. For exact representation the syndrome classes give a matching rectangle cover. Partial syndromes on tree edges and addition constraints at their incident nodes give the classical simultaneous fixed-tree realization. [Kashyap, On Minimal Tree Realizations of Linear Codes](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.1383).

In fact for h≥1 one has w≥2^(h−1)(λ−h+2). Prescribe h−1 independent syndrome bits. In every such fiber the realized syndrome set must affinely span the whole (λ−h+1)-dimensional fiber. Otherwise one further affine probe on S separates a feasible syndrome from every realized member of the fiber, contradicting preservation. This needs at least λ−h+2 syndromes in each of 2^(h−1) disjoint fibers. For t=1 the stronger bound is attained at a fixed cut by retaining codewords with syndromes forming an affine basis and completing to a global affine basis of C without adding new syndromes: take a base word, lifts of a syndrome basis, and translates of the base word by a basis of the syndrome kernel. Their union uses only λ+1 syndromes; to attain λ+1 rectangles take the full feasible class for each retained syndrome. This one-cut statement need not use the same basis at all cuts.

For a simultaneous upper bound write C=x₀+G F₂ʳ with G injective. When r=0 the singleton is exact. Otherwise choose the smallest power of two q>(r+1)2ᵗ. Enumerate (a,c) in F_q² and messages m_i=Tr(c a^i), 0≤i<r, using the absolute field trace and a⁰=1. For any nonzero binary coefficient vector z, its message character has expectation equal to the fraction of roots in F_q of the nonzero polynomial Σ_i z_i X^i. This follows by summing over c using nontriviality of the trace character. The bias is at most (r−1)/q. For a consistent affine message fiber of codimension s≤t the character expansion of its membership indicator gives probability at least 2^(−s)[1−(2ˢ−1)(r−1)/q]>0. Thus the enumerated witnesses x₀+Gm hit every feasible parity context and preserve rejection automatically. This is the finite-field polynomial small-bias construction. [Alon, Goldreich, Håstad and Peralta](https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.3240030308).

The list has at most min(2ʳ,q²) distinct words and can be constructed deterministically in time polynomial in n, r, the matrix input size and q by ordinary finite-field arithmetic (brute-force irreducible-polynomial search suffices within this bound). It can be represented on any terminal tree by copying a chosen witness index along each edge and locally checking each original terminal bit. Hence the number of separator states at every cut is at most min(2ʳ,O((r+1)²4ᵗ)). At most t ordinary clauses are preserved too: from a satisfying original word pin one true literal per clause, a feasible context of at most t unary equations. The same argument allows a mixed conjunction of parity probes and ordinary clauses of total count at most t. If t=0 a single word suffices.

There is also a smaller nonuniform list. Put v=min(t,r). Every consistent affine message fiber of codimension ≤v contains a codimension-v fiber. There are 2^v times the Gaussian binomial [r choose v]₂ such fibers. The product formula bounds that binomial by 4·2^(v(r−v)), since ∏_{i≥1}(1−2^(−i))>1/4 (the first two factors give 3/8 and the tail is at least 1−Σ_{i≥3}2^(−i)=3/4). N independent uniform messages miss a specified fiber with probability at most exp(−N 2^(−v)). A union bound therefore gives a list of O(2^v(v(r−v)+v+1)) messages hitting every required fiber. The same index compiler uses that many states in every order. For the equality relation on two r-bit words separated at their two sides, λ=r, so this existential upper bound is within a factor O(v+1) of the preceding lower bound. This sampling argument gives existence with no deterministic small-list construction at that sharper size.

The lower bound uses all affine parity contexts and an actual decomposition into rectangles. It does not apply as stated to clause-only contexts or encodings with uncounted communication crossing the cut. These are fixed-interface state counts and give no general running-time lower bound for SAT.
