# Searching within-block basis changes and Horn polarities together

The [essential-coordinate reduction](ring-image-period-chart.md) supplies a common linear coordinate system for the residual affine parameter space of an explicit signed CNF. It lists forbidden tuples on subsets of these coordinates. A tuple excludes a whole fiber and gives one clause. Models avoiding all tuples lift to original models. On the ordered ring image, the algorithm first recovers the signed source. Both decoding and recognition must be charged. One can also start directly with the list of scopes and forbidden tuples.

Partition axes by their incidence signature, the set of indexed scopes containing the axis. Each scope is a union of whole blocks. In each block allow any invertible affine transformation of its values. On the dual space this permits every change of normal basis within that block, and translations may be chosen independently across blocks.

**Theorem.** One can decide in polynomial bit time whether these transformations can make the clause for every listed tuple Horn. On success, one can decide their conjunction and return a sparse original witness on satisfiable inputs. Recognition is exact for this class of transformations and this tuple representation. A different representation of the same relation can have other Horn descriptions.

For a block B let D_B be the distinct restrictions to B of listed tuples whose scopes contain B. Each zero bit in a transformed forbidden tuple contributes one positive literal to its clause. Therefore, if all tuple clauses become Horn, every transformed member of D_B lies in the all-ones vector and its immediate Hamming neighbors. Every subset of this affine simplex is affinely independent. Test the requirement by taking differences from any observed point. Empty D_B imposes no constraint.

Conversely, suppose D_B is affinely independent. Choose any observed c_B as center. Extend the vectors v+c_B for v different from c_B in D_B to a basis of the whole block value space. For the matrix A_B with these basis vectors as columns use y_B=1+A_B^{-1}(x_B+c_B). The center maps to all ones, and each remaining observed point maps to a distinct neighbor. The block therefore contributes one positive literal exactly on tuples with v different from c_B. If a working chart had its all-ones preimage outside D_B, choosing any observed center and rebuilding this way clears some mismatches without creating any. It suffices to search observed centers.

Introduce one indicator X(B,v) per observed value. Add pairwise negative clauses allowing at most one true indicator per block. For each listed tuple and each unordered pair B,C of distinct blocks in its scope, require X(B,v_B) OR X(C,v_C). At most one incident center can then differ from the tuple. If a block has no true indicator in a satisfying 2SAT model, setting any one of its observed indicators to true preserves its negative clauses and the positive pair clauses. A center can thus be recovered from any satisfying indicator assignment. Failure of the binary test is a chart refusal. It does not decide whether the source itself is satisfiable.

For the selected centers transform the tuples and solve the resulting Horn clauses by forward chaining. Invert the affine block maps, lift through the saved affine equations and verify the original signed clauses against the resulting set of true identifiers. An empty forbidden tuple is an empty clause. An unobserved block can keep the identity map.

**Range of the chart search.** Discard unconstrained coordinates. If all distinct nonempty active signatures are pairwise incomparable under inclusion, the search covers every simultaneously adapted linear chart up to relabeling. For each signature T the intersection of the normal scopes indexed by T equals the sum of blocks whose signatures contain T. Incomparability reduces it to the single intrinsic block T. No other adapted chart can introduce additional signatures: the dimensions of the intersections are the sums of multiplicities over supersets, determining every exact multiplicity by descending subtraction over subsets. This last argument proves uniqueness; the algorithm groups only the observed signatures. When signatures are comparable, basis vectors in one block may have components in strict supersignatures. The polynomial test retains its stated exactness for within-block transformations.

For r coordinate bits and q tuples, words have at most r bits and every D_B has at most q elements. The binary instance has at most rq indicators and O(rq²+qr²) clauses. Rank tests, basis extensions and chart inversions use ordinary Gaussian elimination on explicitly represented matrices of dimension at most r. Constructing the transformed Horn instance and lifting its model also take polynomial bit time and space. The sparse preprocessor produces at most as many tuples as source exclusions and uses at most the number of occurring identifiers as dimension. Unused binary-encoded names require no iteration.

A four-bit block forbidding exactly 0000, 1111 and 0101 has zero translation period, because a nonzero period would partition three words into pairs. The allowed set spans all four dimensions. The forbidden points are affinely independent, whereas the first two have Hamming distance four and cannot lie in a single translated coordinate radius-one ball. The block test therefore adds a genuine basis change to fixed-coordinate Horn renaming. Other source solvers, including a single-scope elimination, also decide this example.

The ordinary fixed-coordinate renaming problem is classical. Earlier workspace algorithms handle polarity in one supplied affine basis and independent recognition of simultaneous coordinate scopes. The present test combines basis selection inside a block with the center constraints shared across rows. [Mannila and Mehlhorn](https://people.mpi-inf.mpg.de/~mehlhorn/ftp/RenamingHorn.pdf)
