# Affine overlap with two or three local choices
The input is a conjunction of explicit fan-in-two circuits F_i on m_i local inputs evaluated on supplied affine forms y_i=A_i x+b_i of n physical bits. Suppose each full-cube local support has size at most two. At most 2m_i+1 parity queries to F_i with at most two extra gates per query recover its local support; every query has exactly m_i inputs.

First query total parity and the parity after conjoining each coordinate y_j in turn. If total is odd there is exactly one word, read from the single-coordinate answers. Otherwise their vector h is zero for empty support and is the nonzero difference for a two-point support. In the latter case choose k with h_k=1 and query each product F_i y_k y_j. Exactly one word v has bit k=1, hence the answers yield v; the other is v+h. A zero-input factor only needs the total parity. For every two-point factor impose A_i x+b_i=v+t_i h with one unknown binary scalar. Impose the analogous full-vector equality for singletons and reject any empty list. Solve the combined binary linear equations and return x. There is no overlap or rank restriction. This is a promise reduction, not a recognition algorithm for the ambiguity bound.

At maximum local support three, compatibility is already NP-complete with all local factors the identical two-input OR and at most two physical bits per affine coordinate. Give each graph vertex a word in GF(2)^2. Require the word nonzero at each vertex and its bitwise difference nonzero across each edge. The three nonzero words form a three-color palette, so assignments are exactly proper named three-colorings. Membership in NP follows by evaluation. If the graph has an edge, permutations of the colors act freely, so the parity of every nonempty solution set in this family is zero. The hardness concerns direct compatibility with local lists already provided; it does not preclude reductions querying parity on global circuits.

Under the October fixed-density counting bound the positive method costs polynomial preprocessing and encoding factors times the sum of (2m_i+1) 2^{O((m_i+s_i+2)/sqrt(log(2+m_i+s_i)))} over local factors. This uses the unique-extension Tseitin encoding with at most four clauses per binary gate and one output unit. A comparison to 2^n must bound each local clock and the total number and description sizes of factors. On an arbitrary n-input s-gate circuit the direct theorem uses n+s variables, attaining 2^{o(n)} at s=o(n sqrt(log n)) and constant fractional savings for sufficiently small s/(n sqrt(log n)). Its proved E^NP circuit bound is Omega(t sqrt(log t)) infinitely often. It does not establish the all-polynomial-size Williams SAT premise or the exponential-size Murray-Williams gap-UNSAT premise for NP fixed-exponent lower bounds.
