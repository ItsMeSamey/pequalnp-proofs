# Finding shared nonlinear signals and checking locally decoded witnesses

Fix a gate order after contracting affine wiring over F2. Write the i-th nonlinear output z_i as u_i v_i+w_i with affine u_i,v_i,w_i in the raw bits and earlier z's. The final output is affine. At cut i form the matrix of all still-future operands and final output, restricted to z_1 through z_i. Make an undirected conflict graph with an edge for each pair of columns independent at some cut. Polynomial construction by Gaussian elimination suffices. For any S, deleting its columns makes every cut rank at most one exactly when S covers that graph. This uses all operand rows, including rows in the producers of S. Find a cover of size at most t by branching on endpoints of any uncovered edge in 2^t poly(size) deterministic time.

Use one formal port per selected gate, and a checkpoint for the scalar history immediately before each selected gate. On unnamed past coordinates choose a basis row h_i for the surviving future row span, using zero when empty. Its scalar value s_i is h_i applied to the true unnamed past. Every current operand is an affine form of raw inputs and available named ports plus zero or one copy of s_(i-1). Thus z_i=A_i s_(i-1)+B_i, with A_i affine and B_i a product of two affine forms plus one affine form, by Boolean idempotence. The old-coordinate part of h_i lies in the preceding span by restriction of future rows. Consequently at an unnamed gate s_i=c_i s_(i-1)+d_i z_i with constant coefficients, and at a named gate s_i=c_i s_(i-1). At the latter check the actual z_i formula against its named port. At an interval of unnamed gates the recurrence unrolls as initial state times a product of affine factors plus one B_j times a suffix product per gate (absorbing constant coefficients). Each interval writes its endpoint just once, into a checkpoint or the final affine acceptance equation. Initial states following a named gate are affine functions of its preceding checkpoint. Thus O(m+t+1) product occurrences suffice over n+2t or fewer variables for m nonlinear gates. Dense lists of affine factors have polynomial bit size. Induction gives unique ports and checkpoints for every accepting raw input. Pinning raw inputs gives witness search. Empty intervals and zero-dimensional spans give ordinary affine check equations.

Apply deterministic counting Theorem 1.4 of Lokshtanov, Paturi, Tamaki, Williams and Yu (https://sites.cs.ucsb.edu/~daniello/papers/polyEQFF17.pdf). At m=O(n log n) and t≤αn/loglog n for sufficiently small absolute α, the clock including discovery is 2^(n−Ω(n/loglog n)) poly(size). This gives a verifiable syntax condition on an actual ordered circuit. For example, compute t independently readable raw pair products and then a length Θ(n log n) AND/OR accumulator using raw inputs and those products as controls. Read each product separately after all t are produced. Their conflict graph contains a t-clique, and deleting all t leaves only the current accumulator. Arbitrary overlapping raw controls are allowed.

This graph has O(n) edges, so greedily extracting a minimum-degree independent set, or greedy coloring the subgraph of vertices with degree at most twice the average, gives a set U of size Ω(n). Fix its complement exhaustively. Each decoder bit becomes affine in U. Guess one witness circuit and gate order of the asserted size and cover, then check the syntactic promises. For each restriction, take three separate copies of the polynomial equations described above, substituting the appropriate affine variable labels. In place of final acceptance require each copy to equal its literal's falsifying value, itself affine in U. The three systems share U and have separate internal ports, at most 6t auxiliary bits total. Each joint solution is precisely a violated clause address on this restriction with unique internal bits. Count deterministically to test emptiness. With u=|U|, N≤u+6t, the unrestricted system clock is polynomial(description) times 2^(N−Ω(N/(1+log(1+P/N)))), with P=O(m+t+1) product occurrences. Multiply by 2^(q−u), choose the displayed α small enough, and obtain 2^(q−Ω(n/log n)) for polynomial m and 2^(q−Ω(n/loglog n)) at linear-log gate size.
