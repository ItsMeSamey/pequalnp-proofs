# Hidden product structure and a limit of witness-preserving filters

Use the positive additive per-bit weights w_i from sparse-witness-multiplicative-salts.md at a prime p, so E_p(x)=Σ_i w_i x_i≡I(x) modulo p, where I is the q-bit input index. That deterministic list also detects many circuits whose witness count far exceeds its sparse-support bound.

Suppose the nonempty satisfying set is a Cartesian product of nonempty sets S_1,…,S_j on disjoint raw-coordinate blocks covering all variables. The partition and local sets need not be supplied. For an arbitrary nonfree block choose any local marked pattern. Each comparison with another marked pattern, embedding the local patterns on the original indices by zeros outside the block, gives a nonzero integer difference of magnitude less than 2^q. At most q primes divide it. Therefore among the first 1+q Σ_b(|S_b|−1) odd primes some p isolates the selected pattern in every block. At that prime each local polynomial

    L_b(Y)=Σ_{x_b∈S_b}Y^{Σ_{i∈b} w_i (x_b)_i}

is nonzero: the isolated exponent has a unique contribution. The full univariate polynomial equals their product in F₂[Y] and is nonzero. Its degree remains bounded by the global integer exponent limit. A wholly free block needs no prime exclusions at all, since its polynomial factors into (1+Y^{w_i}) for every free coordinate and all the weights are strictly positive. Thus a supplied upper bound K on the sum of |S_b|−1 over the remaining blocks suffices with 1+qK primes. Some exact exponent bucket has odd occupation, and some member of the multiplicative salt list gives a nonzero field value. After this hit, self-reduction queries the same scalar and requires no product promise for later restrictions.

For polynomial K, the previous O(q) added gates, q original inputs and polynomial number of deterministic parity calls carry over. Likewise, K≤2^{q^ε} obeys the larger-support bound at the stated gate-coefficient slack. For example, requiring equality on each disjoint pair of q even bits gives 2^{q/2} witnesses but only q/2 excess local patterns. This is a sufficient structural condition for detection without recovering the decomposition, and no claim is made that arbitrary inputs have it.

## Why common filters alone do not supply sparse promises

A filter keeps witnesses in a fixed H_i⊂{0,1}^q. For any t filters and positive integer K, start with a halfcube S. While some 1≤|S∩H_i|≤K, remove that entire intersection. This i is thereafter empty. At most t iterations remove at most tK points in total. The residual has size at least 2^{q−1}−tK, and all filter intersections are empty or larger than K. Starting instead with the whole cube shows that a common family having, for every nonempty arbitrary source, at least one nonempty intersection of size at most K must have t≥ceil(2^q/K). Partition into blocks of size at most K achieves this number.

The halfcube residual is exactly that halfcube AND the complements of a selected subset of the filters. Thus if a common multi-output circuit of G gates computes all t filter bits, the adverse source has a circuit of at most G+O(t+1) gates, regardless of how the subset was selected. In particular nonuniform polynomial-size families of polynomially many filters face a P/poly adverse language across lengths. This does not give a counterexample at a smaller prescribed gate cap unless the total circuit just described fits the cap.

Under P=NP, for a P-uniform family of polynomially many polynomial-time evaluable filters, a uniform P residual exists as well. Assume K in binary is computable in poly(q) and tK=o(2^q). Maintain the current residual as the halfcube minus the union of the removed filters. For every remaining candidate the number of q-bit rows in its intersection with this residual is then a #P function of its index and the selected list. Stockmeyer's deterministic polynomial-hierarchy factor-two counting algorithm runs in P under the collapse, with exact zero detectable by an NP query. Remove any strictly positive intersection whose estimate is at most 2K; these removals cost at most 4K points apiece. Repeat until none qualifies. Every true intersection of size 1 through K would qualify, and at most t removals occur. The resulting source is a uniform P language of density 1/2−o(1) (bounded above by 1/2). This conditional construction charges the entire filter family rather than an individual member and depends on the family. It is a limitation of imposing few witnesses by common intersection filters, not of arbitrary decision reductions or instance-specific deterministic hashing.
