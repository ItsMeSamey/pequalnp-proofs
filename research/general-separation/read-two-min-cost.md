# Minimum-cost witnesses for read-two clauses

Each variable has an explicitly listed finite domain, a rational cost per value, and unary literal occurrences in at most two distinct clause IDs. Each literal specifies which domain values satisfy it. Clauses are disjunctions of arbitrary width; all clauses must hold. The algorithm returns infeasibility or an original assignment of minimum total cost. Boolean signed occurrences are obtained by using singleton truth sets. Restrict domains for pins, reject an empty resulting domain, and combine all occurrences of a given variable in the same clause by union of truth sets. Preserve distinct clause IDs even for identical clauses.

For each variable and each subset S of its at most two clause endpoints, record the cheapest original value covering **at least** S, together with its price c(S); use infinity when impossible. Start with total baseline Σc(∅), which includes unused variables. For endpoints u,v the extra singleton prices A,B and pair price P are the differences from c(∅). These extras are nonnegative. A finite P implies finite A,B. With zero or one distinct endpoint use no edge or one edge to a private slot at the finite singleton price. Infeasible singleton options create no edge.

Use one common matching vertex per clause. Edges to private slots nominate their clause for that variable. For two endpoints use these rules.

* For infinite P, use one common private slot with singleton edges at prices A,B whenever finite. At most one endpoint can be nominated.
* For finite P≥A+B, let Δ=P−A−B. Use two private slots, one accepting either endpoint at its singleton price and one accepting either at that price plus Δ. One nomination has minimum price A or B. Two nominations use both slots and cost P.
* For P<A+B use separate singleton slots at A,B and one direct edge uv at P nominating both. Selecting both singleton slots is legal; their joint price exceeds P, and replacing them with the direct edge decreases cost while freeing the slots. For reconstruction in either representation choose the value stored for the **union** of the nominations.

These gadgets implement the minimum additional price for each exact nomination pattern. Every feasible original assignment can nominate one satisfying variable at each clause. The local gadgets realize those nominations as a matching saturating the clause vertices, at cost no greater than the assignment after including the baseline. Conversely every saturating matching selects per-variable nominations jointly coverable by the recorded original value, and reconstructs a feasible assignment costing at most baseline plus the matching price. These inequalities imply equality of the two optima and exact optimality of the reconstruction. An empty clause cannot be saturated. When two variables offer parallel direct edges, retain a cheapest one and its owner: substitution uses the same endpoints, and neither owner can simultaneously match any of its other incident options on these endpoints.

Clear denominators of extra edge prices by their lcm. Take W one greater than the sum of all scaled edge prices and give an edge of price c weight W times its number of clause endpoints minus c. Total weight first maximizes the number of saturated clauses and then minimizes cost. Thus one ordinary integer maximum-weight matching suffices. Check saturation explicitly. The graph has O(number of variables + number of clauses) vertices and O(number of variables) edges. Each per-variable table has at most four entries. Rational denominators after scaling have polynomial total bit complexity because the lcm divides the product, and all scaled costs and their sum have polynomial bit length. Deterministic integer weighted blossom gives polynomial bit time; the implementation uses NetworkX's cubic-operation integer algorithm. Costs may be negative before baseline subtraction. No field arithmetic, modular cancellation, counting oracle or shared state across pinned instances enters the decision procedure. [NetworkX matching algorithm](https://networkx.org/documentation/stable/reference/algorithms/generated/networkx.algorithms.matching.max_weight_matching.html)

With a single price for choosing each edge, weighted edge cover already reduces to weighted matching. The endpoint-dependent prices above require distinguishing one nominated endpoint from two, including an independently specified joint price. The construction implements those costs with constant-size gadgets and needs no oracle for general-factor feasibility. [Schrijver, Chapter 27, as discussed by Pothen, Ferdous and Manne](https://smferdous1.github.io/files/pdf/papers/Pothen%20et%20al_2019_Approximation%20algorithms%20in%20combinatorial%20scientific%20computing.pdf) · [Solver](read_two_min_cost.py)

## Boolean predecessors and the signed case

Tovey gave a linear-time decision algorithm for Boolean SAT with at most two incident clauses per variable and arbitrary clause widths. Porschen and Speckenmeyer, §5, solve minimum- and maximum-weight SAT for monotone Boolean read-two formulas by weighted edge cover, including preprocessing for signed variable weights. Their model assigns a variable weight to setting it true; two per-value prices differ from this by an additive baseline. [Tovey (1984)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-218X(84)90081-7) · [Porschen and Speckenmeyer, §5](https://kups.ub.uni-koeln.de/54908/1/zaik2007-534.pdf)

For ordinary signed Boolean literals, first remove tautological clauses and duplicate literals. Globally flip all-negative variables, exchanging their costs. Each remaining mixed variable has one positive and one negative occurrence. Replace these by separate positive flags u,v and add the clause (u OR v). Each flag still has at most two occurrences. Normalize each variable's two costs by subtracting its minimum. Give u,v costs M plus the respective normalized costs for true and false, where M exceeds the sum of all normalized per-variable maxima. For pure variables use a constant offset and the difference of the two prices as the true-variable weight. Including their offsets, their normalized contributions are nonnegative. Every assignment selecting exactly one flag per mixed pair pays one M per pair and at most the sum of maxima in additional normalized cost. Every assignment doubling any pair pays at least one more M. Thus a feasible signed instance has an optimum selecting exactly one flag per pair; if the monotone optimum doubles a pair, the signed instance is infeasible. The discussion gives an elementary reduction for this Boolean special case; the two-endpoint proof above directly covers arbitrary finite-domain predicates.

## Encoding and support

Charge the entire input, including all repeated literal lists in a single clause and every token in every truth set. A variable in a third distinct clause is rejected even when its truth set there is empty. The bit-time claim assumes explicit binary rational data and domain tokens with polynomial-time equality and polynomial bit length in the input size. Canonical integer IDs can be assigned by sorting, or by pairwise comparison in polynomial time. The Python interface accepts hashable tokens; the theorem charges comparisons and excludes arbitrary user code hidden in token methods. Polynomial bit complexity of integer weighted matching also follows from Edmonds' polynomial matching algorithm; in the cited integer blossom implementation a polynomial number of additions, subtractions, constant doublings and exact halvings increase bit lengths by at most a polynomial number of bits. [Edmonds, Maximum Matching and a Polyhedron with 0,1-Vertices](https://doi.org/10.6028/jres.069B.013) · [Implementation](https://networkx.org/documentation/stable/_modules/networkx/algorithms/matching.html)

A common additive offset per variable changes every total cost by the sum of the offsets and leaves the minimizers unchanged. Zero is a valid numeric cost and never denotes an unavailable value. For nonnegative per-value *weights* and a support question asking for a satisfying assignment of strictly positive product, delete precisely the zero-weight values and run the feasibility algorithm with zero costs (or keep a separate additive objective). For a finite nonnegative weighted sum, positivity is equivalent to such a witness. This does not compute the numerical sum, optimize a product through an uncharged logarithm, or determine nonvanishing in a finite field, where cancellation is possible.
