# Uniform dense controls for fixed additive-bucket parity tests

Fix n, and a uniformly supplied list of primes p. For each p test all integer bucket parities with D_p(x)=sum_i (2^i mod p)x_i. Let Q=sum_p(1+n(p-1)). If (n+1)Q<2^n, there is a deterministic poly(n,Q)-time construction of a bounded-fanin circuit of O(n(Q+1)) gates whose support misses at most Q+1 words, has every tested parity zero, and fails to factor across every nonempty proper variable split. This concerns a specified list of tests chosen independently of the resulting circuit.

Represent a word by its column v(x) in GF(2)^Q containing one set bit per prime, at its bucket. At a prefix, the column has the same representation with the partial sums. Appending bit 0 acts as identity, and 1 acts by the known direct sum of shifts on the blocks (zero-fill at overflow; no realized prefix overflows). Starting at the joint zero-bucket column, carry a linear basis of the span of all reached prefix columns and a physical representative prefix for every basis vector. Apply both transitions to these representatives, then use Gaussian elimination to retain an independent subset of the actual columns, retaining their words. Linearity gives the full next span; selecting an echelon basis never requires interpreting linear combinations as physical representatives. Independently propagate the sum of all columns by the sum of the two transition maps. After n iterations obtain basis words B of size at most Q and total syndrome s.

Choose a word a outside all closed Hamming radius-one balls around B, e.g. test the first (n+1)Q+1 binary integers. Express s+v(a) as a linear combination of basis columns and let D be the corresponding subset of B. Define C to reject exactly a and D and accept everything else. Its parity syndromes are zero because s+v(a)+sum_D v=0. Every one-bit neighbor of a is accepted. For any split into two nonempty parts, flipping a bit on each of the two sides exhibits both of a's projections among allowed words, so a Cartesian product would have to accept a. This proves indecomposability, and a conjunction forbidding the listed words gives the claimed gate size. For n>=2 and the strict bound above, the support is nonempty.

All construction steps are uniform polynomial in n and Q, including the GF(2) linear algebra and witness prefixes. For any fixed polynomial list with polynomial prime values this yields an explicit polynomial-size family of near-full supports. The gates may exceed O(n log n); the theorem does not exclude instance-dependent tests, near-linear-gate isolation, or a deterministic SAT decider. The same span construction works on any common-order linear representation of all mask columns with polynomial intermediate dimension and computable transitions.
