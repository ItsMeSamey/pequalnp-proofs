# Seven canonical point signatures plus one arbitrary signature

Suppose the point-side boundary consists of the seven target neighborhood signatures and one additional arbitrary singleton signature H. Count boundary wires with multiplicity, so the eighth may duplicate another signature while behaving differently on sets of two or more points. Then the separator requires at least **20 paid point gates**. In particular, such a separator with at least 14 mixed gates needs at least 34 residual gates. The argument allows arbitrary line-side circuitry and mixed fan-out.

Write T for one canonical neighborhood and B for an off-T pair. For an actual negative row with point set n containing B and with line j active, consider a required positive on that line at p in T. It has only that line active, so its entire line input is dominated by the negative line input. Require n to meet every other canonical neighborhood containing p. Its corresponding point boundary values on the positive are then dominated at the negative, by monotonicity on points. If p is outside H or n meets H, the eighth boundary value is also dominated. If the canonical T output accepts B, it is active at n, so all boundary values on the positive are dominated at the negative, contradicting separation. The eighth function's full behavior never enters this argument.

For each T,B the verifier enumerates all such witness choices (p,n). The witnesses all fail to handle H only if H contains every singleton p in the choices and avoids the union of their negative point sets n. This gives an explicitly enumerable set of signatures. Across the 36 pairs for each target, just ten signatures per target could leave any pair unprotected, and the seven sets of ten are disjoint. Thus at least six of the seven designated outputs reject every off-neighborhood pair, even when two boundary wires have equal signatures. The checker obtains the matrix from the pinned statement through the adjacent seed-obstructions decoder. Run `python3 verify_extra.py`.

Apply the two-charge lemma to any six fully protected targets. A charge for T is a paid gate whose singleton signature is contained in T and contains at least two points. Distinct targets have disjoint charge classes because their neighborhoods meet in one point. A protected target needs two charges or more. If it has exactly two, three different exterior gates have pairwise signature intersections equal to T. To recall the proof, a first charged OR has at most two singleton points. A first charged AND of size three or more intersects two dirty parents and accepts an off-target pair. Consequently a protected output with only two charges must be an AND with a clean parent of signature T, the first charge. That parent is an AND of two strict supersets of T. The other output parent must be a third strict superset, as the first charge alone is unsafe. The portions of these three supersets outside T are pairwise disjoint, since a common outside point and an outside point of the third would be an accepted off-target pair. This also covers the case of only a single outside point, by extending it to an off-target pair.

Let q count the two-charge targets among the chosen six, and let h count the exterior gates (signatures contained in no projective line). All strict supersets of a four-point target are exterior; all supplied primitive point signatures are contained in projective lines. The six require at least 18−q charges and consume three different unordered pairs of exterior gates per two-charge target. A pair cannot serve different targets, since its intersection identifies the target. Hence 3q ≤ h(h−1)/2. The seventh target needs at least one further charge, because none of the free point signals has its four-point signature.

A budget of 19 paid point gates implies h ≤ q ≤ 6. For 1 ≤ h ≤ 6 the pair bound gives 3q < 3h, so h = q = 0. The seventh target can then have only one charge. Its first charged OR has at most two singleton points. A first charged AND yielding all four singleton points must have two strict supersets as its operands, which would be exterior gates. This contradiction proves the lower bound.
