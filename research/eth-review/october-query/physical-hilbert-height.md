# A uniform complex bound from the packet operator norm

At the original Boolean root, for every physical packing, the contraction has |F(T)|≤exp(CM) on the closed complex disk |T|≤3/2, with an absolute constant independent of L. The same holds on any fixed-radius disk with constants depending only on the radius. If F(0) is a positive integer, the number of zeros in the closed unit disk, with multiplicity, is O(M). This removes the loglog L loss from bounding absolute row sums separately.

Extend each packed vector by zero to the whole integer line using its native slots. Within a packet W is a restriction to real slots of convolution by the coefficients K_d=W_0(T)T/(T−d) for differences d in [1−L,L−1], where W_0(T)=(1+T/L)∏_{k=1}^{L−1}(1−T²/k²). For any fixed R, W_0 is uniformly bounded on |T|≤R. When |d|≤2R+2 and |d|<L the possible pole is removable; cancel the factor 1−T²/d² for nonzero d before bounding, using ∏(1+R²/k²). For larger |d|, uniformly K_d=−W_0(T)T/d+O_R(1/d²). Complete this kernel on the infinite line by the same rational expression for differences |d|≥L when L>2R+2; those differences never occur in the packet. The finitely many smaller L are bounded directly.

Convolution on the integer line by 1/d, d≠0, is bounded on square-summable sequences with norm ≤π: these are, up to sign and the imaginary unit, the Fourier coefficients of the bounded periodic function π−theta on (0,2π), found by one integration by parts. The summable error defines a bounded convolution by the triangle inequality. Parseval therefore bounds the packet operator norm by C_R; zero padding and arbitrary coordinate deletion are contractions, and the full direct sum has the same bound. No independence of the column assignment is needed. For every Boolean column assignment, ||WX||₂≤C_R sqrt(M). Each absolute row factor is at most 1+|(WX)_i|. The arithmetic-geometric mean inequality and Cauchy–Schwarz bound their product by (1+C_R)^M. There are at most 2^(2M) terms in the original Boolean-root contraction, each with 0/1 prefactors. This gives the claim. Jensen's formula on a fixed disk of radius between 1 and 3/2 (avoiding boundary zeros) bounds the number of unit-disk zeros when |F(0)|≥1.

This bound also permits analytic extrapolation from exact coefficients at the offset 1: on |T−1|≤3 the same proof gives exp(O(M)) by taking R=4. Cauchy's estimate bounds the tail at T=0 after K coefficients by exp(O(M))3^(−K), so sufficiently large K=O(M) permits exact rounding of the integer F(0). The coefficients here are ordinary powers of T−1. Supplying them exactly has its own cost. For example expanding each W_{xy}(1+z) naively by polynomial multiplication is polynomial per entry, but multiplying entries and summing over the 2^(2M) terms still costs exp(O(M)) polynomial-bit operations. Rational common denominator |C'(0)|^M has O(ML log L) bits. Exact rational Taylor arithmetic therefore provides no faster worst-case clock by itself; obtaining the needed coefficients in the published recursive precision and time bounds is a separate obligation. This proof concerns the Boolean root, not an arbitrary descendant with lifted signed or extension-ring entries.
