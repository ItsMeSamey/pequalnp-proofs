# Nonzero projected support tuples on a sparse negative cubic family

This statement counts distinct support tuples, not distinct arrays of cell sizes or nonzero shell sums. Fix the partition and the final homogeneous system on its all-zero heavy-variable branch. Assign every surviving cubic to one row group containing it, once only. In each group choose a binary linear combination of its rows hitting at least half the assigned cubics (an independent uniformly random combination hits each nonzero column with probability one half). Retain the hit cubics as candidates. Suppose the original base graph on N vertices is simple of maximum degree d, the full three-uniform construction has maximum degree O(d), there are Θ(Nd) candidates, and each group’s base endpoint set is small enough that its induced base graph and every subgraph thereof have bounded average degree. These are sufficient hypotheses for the result below.

One suitable base family is obtained from d independent uniform perfect matchings on each side of a bipartition of size N/2 (each matching goes between the sides), with d growing polylogarithmically. For any fixed vertex set of size v≤N^.95, the probability of at least 4v edges, counted with multiplicity, is at most binom(dv,4v)(v/(N/2))^(4v): specify distinct left-vertex/matching incidences, then reveal their images within each matching without replacement. A union bound over vertex sets costs at most (eN/v)^v. For polylogarithmic d the resulting sum over v≥1 is o(1). Hence all such sets induce fewer than 4v edges, implying degeneracy at most seven by iterative deletion. The expected total number of parallel-edge pairs is O(d²). With probability tending to one deleting duplicates loses o(Nd) edges, leaves maximum degree at most d, preserves small-set sparsity, and gives a simple graph. This is an existence argument, not a deterministic generator. Extend the edges to distinct negative triples using fresh third vertices, with maximum third-vertex load O(d) and no repeated endpoint/third pairs, by the greedy assignment of final-query-period-obstruction.md. The heavy-zero deletion and final-row coverage argument there and in final-union-shell-obstruction.md retain Ω(Nd) cubics when d=o(√log N) and d≫loglog N.

Join candidate triples in a conflict graph if they share any vertex. Also join two candidates assigned to the same row group if a base edge joins their two base endpoint sets. The first type gives O(Nd²) conflicts. For the second, let σ_g(v) be the number of group-g candidates with endpoint v; it is at most d. Orient the base graph induced by the group’s endpoints with bounded outdegree using its degeneracy order. Each oriented edge u→v accounts for at most σ_g(u)σ_g(v) pairs. Summing first over its tails bounds the count by O(d Σ_u σ_g(u)) per group, hence O(Nd²) overall. In particular the argument needs the small-endpoint hypothesis for each group.

Remove vertices of conflict degree above a sufficiently large constant times d. A constant fraction of the Θ(Nd) candidates remains. Select t=⌊γN⌋ independent candidates successively, for a sufficiently small constant γ>0. Each choice removes O(d) remaining candidates, so there are at least (cNd)^t/t! ≥ d^{Ω(N)} unordered independent selections. Within a group, the union of endpoints and fresh third vertices of any selection induces precisely the selected triples: a competing base edge either connects different selected triples (a conflict), or joins the endpoints of one selected triple, in which case simplicity fixes the original edge and its unique third vertex. This recovers the selections from their grouped support sets, so the tuples are distinct.

For a group selection T, restricting all coordinates outside its triples to zero leaves precisely its selected violation cubics. In the V-adic lifted Boolean algebra at p=ℓ=n+1, each coefficient of V^j is divisible by 2^j, also after Boolean Möbius inversion. Therefore summation of the retained coefficients is evaluation at V=1 modulo 2^p and respects multiplication. The local full-support Möbius coefficient is (−1)^|T| times the sum of the all-ones character on ker C_T, where the columns of C_T are the selected cubics’ row incidences. A fully selected triple contributes +1 to Möbius inversion, and its seven other assignments together contribute −1. The previously chosen row combination hits every selected column. The local coefficient is consequently (−1)^|T| 2^{|T|−rank C_T}. The supports of all groups are globally disjoint. With n_free free variables, the column factor is 2^{n_free−3t}; the combined valuation n_free−2t−Σ_g rank C_T is below p. Each listed tuple thus contributes nonzero modulo 2^p after coefficient summation. This gives neither a lower bound on contraction time nor a noncancellation assertion for coarser shells.
