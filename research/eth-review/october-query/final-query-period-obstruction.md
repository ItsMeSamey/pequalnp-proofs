# Explicit native final-query obstruction for all period layouts

The source is quantitative-reduction.tex (in particular lines 318–369), and the disjoint sparsification and greedy-hash lemmas in main.tex. This bound concerns the grouped simultaneous-polynomial-period packing, not scalar complexity.

Let G be an explicit simple d-regular Ramanujan graph on N vertices, with d=Theta((log log N)^4). For an infinite uniform family use the Lubotzky–Phillips–Sarnak graphs X^(p,q), q running through primes congruent to one modulo four, and p the largest such prime ≤(log log q)^4. The prime number theorem in the fixed progression ensures p is of this order; q≫p ensures simplicity, d=p+1, and N is of order q^3. Either bipartite or nonbipartite graphs work: the upper Rayleigh bound on the constants' orthogonal complement is 2 sqrt(d−1), and the exceptional eigenvalue −d can only decrease it. Source: Lubotzky, Phillips and Sarnak, Ramanujan graphs, https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02126799 .

Add N fresh third vertices. Assign each original edge uv a third vertex z, avoiding reuse of any pair (u,z) or (v,z), with each z used at most 2d times. Greedy suffices: at most 2d candidates already meet an endpoint and at most N/4 candidates have reached capacity before any step, as |E|=Nd/2. Form one all-negative 3-clause on (u,v,z) per edge. The 3-hypergraph is linear, has n=2N variables, m=nd/4 clauses, maximum degree ≤2d, and no duplicate, tautological, or subsumed clauses. It goes to the sparse-query branch (m>n ceil(log2 ceil(log2 n))) and never branches during the manuscript's sparsification. Indeed its maximum literal degree is o(sqrt(log n)), below the source heart threshold M (the heart thresholds M^s for proper hearts are ≥M), while empty hearts are excluded. Width-three subcases with two-literal hearts have codegree at most one. The one remaining leaf is the original CNF.

Each generated hash polynomial is an F2 linear combination of distinct squarefree violation cubics x_u x_v x_z, after combining duplicates. In a final selected system, every hyperedge occurs in at least one nonzero row. Setting exactly the three coordinates of an absent hyperedge to one violates only its clause and would satisfy every hash, contradicting final correctness (each true model always satisfies all hashes, and final counts agree). This holds for all permitted hash-row tie choices. Moreover, any common translation period of a group is zero on the union S of all coordinates used in it: for a variable w in a cubic of some row, the coefficient of the opposite quadratic pair in the translate difference equals its shift bit. No other cubic shares the opposite pair by linearity. Conversely all shifts outside S are periods. Thus for ANY coordinate partition of variables the test-group degree equals |S| exactly. The conclusion also survives invertible F2 row operations preserving the final polynomial row space: each edge remains in some basis row and no new cubic outside the hypergraph appears.

For X⊆V(G) the number of base edges inside X is at most (d|X|²/N+2 sqrt(d−1)|X|)/2 by the indicator Rayleigh bound. A row group using S coordinates can cover no more than this many triples with X=S intersect V(G). Whenever |S|≤n^0.95 and n is large, d|S|/N≤1. Thus a group covers at most 2 sqrt(d)|S| triples. Summing and using mandatory coverage gives M=sum_g |S_g| ≥ m/(2 sqrt(d)) = n sqrt(d)/8. Balanced source row groups satisfy the small-scope condition because each uses O(n^0.75 polylog(n)) incidences. More generally, on any layout either all groups satisfy it or one group already has degree >n^0.95: the native max-degree≤ceil(s^0.9) hypothesis then forces s=Omega(n^(0.95/0.9)), much larger than n log log n. In the small-scope case the wire hypothesis M≤Cs forces s=Omega(n(log log n)^2), also exceeding n log log n. This applies to arbitrary row and coordinate groupings subject to the native implementation assumptions. It excludes a uniform loglog improvement by this period-layout upper bound alone. The published incidence-index algorithm remains available.

The construction does not prove a lower bound on arbitrary SAT execution, algebraic factor regrouping beyond linear row operations, or a different choice of sparsification thresholds. In particular the chosen formula is all-negative, and its decision problem is trivial; the source task here is exact counting. It has one leaf, so the width lower bound is on an actual final query rather than an artificial intermediate one, even though final-query scalar calculation may have a faster representation.
