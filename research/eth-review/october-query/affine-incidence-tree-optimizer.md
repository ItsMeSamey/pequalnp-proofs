# Optimizing residual clause scopes by a tree

Take sound affine equations implied by a signed fixed-width CNF. Gaussian elimination either detects inconsistency or parametrizes their solutions. On this space discard a clause when the equations making all its literals false are inconsistent. A constant-false clause ends the decision. Take independent coordinates for the span of the remaining literal normals of rank r, extending back to the full affine space by Gaussian elimination. Every residual point has the same number of preimages, a power of two determined by the difference of dimensions. The choice and order of the r basis forms are part of the input to the optimizer.

For each clause C of arity at most k, form the binary vector s_C(i) consisting of the coefficient of coordinate i in each of its literals. Introduce vertex 0 with zero vector in every clause. On vertices 0 through r put the complete graph with integer weight on uv equal to the number of clauses C for which s_C(u) differs from s_C(v). For any spanning tree T give each edge e an independent bit z_e and replace each old coordinate i other than the dummy by the XOR of all incident edge bits. The reduced incidence matrix is invertible over the binary field by stripping nondummy leaves. Retain each literal's original affine constant. In clause C, flipping z_uv toggles the literal vector by the XOR of its two endpoint vectors. If that vector is nonzero, flip at a falsifying assignment of C to get a satisfying one. Thus precisely the edges with unequal endpoint vectors are essential in the clause. Summing over clauses gives precisely the tree weight. A minimum spanning tree minimizes this sum among trees in the chosen basis. Different bases can change the optimum. A minimum sum alone gives no stipulated maximum.

For a general bounded-arity predicate first restrict its table to the reachable affine coset of its literal vector. Quotient its direction space by the translations preserving this restricted truth table, computed by enumeration of at most 2^k translations against at most 2^k values. Endpoint equality in this quotient is exactly invariance under toggling the edge bit. Use quotient differences against the zero vector for the dummy. The test for a clause above is already exact since a surviving clause's falsifying syndrome is a single reachable point.

With m clauses, weights cost O(m k (r+1)^2) elementary coefficient inspections plus polynomial-bit counter operations. Dense Prim uses O((r+1)^2) weight comparisons. Compute each resulting union and its maximum w before expansion. A per-clause budget or width cap may reject a certificate or trigger a separate fallback. Enumerating a truth table and forbidding each falsifying word emits at most m 2^w clauses of width at most w, with polynomial overhead per word. A decision witness lifts by the inverse tree map and affine solve. For fixed w, use of the pinned fixed-width SAT bound gives input-polynomial overhead times 2^{O_w(r loglog r / sqrt(log r))} for large r, conditional on that bound. No search over arbitrary affine bases is included.

## Connected signed control

Take r≥5 independent seeds y_1,...,y_r and a further independent bit a. Use x_ii as another name for y_i. For every i<j define x_ij=x_{i,j-1} XOR y_j through the four signed ternary clauses excluding the four forbidden triples. These disjoint-variable-within-scope XOR equations are syntactically extractable from their four clauses. For every nonempty interval I=[i,j] add one signed binary clause on a and x_ij, with independently chosen literal polarities. There are no unit clauses. The source primal graph is connected via the binary clauses. The raw primal cover is Ω(r²): for each fixed i the distinct nonsingleton x_ij form a path of length in vertices r−i in the primal graph; consecutive vertices share a defining ternary clause. The row paths give a matching of size sum_i floor((r−i)/2). The variable–clause incidence graph also has an Ω(r²) matching, pairing each interval variable with its own binary clause. The control asserts a representation gap with no SAT hardness inference.

After substitution into the seed basis each binary clause retains its two independent normals, one for a and one for the interval parity. The singletons supply every seed normal. This basis has r+1 coordinates and contains interval forms of support r. Put the dummy and the r seeds in cyclic order 0,1,...,r. Each interval corresponds bijectively to an unordered pair of the r+1 gaps, selecting the arc excluding 0. For two different old vertices separated by d steps, a separating interval uses one gap from each of their two intervening arcs. The graph weight is d(r+1−d). Neighbors have minimum weight r, while nonneighbors cost at least 2(r−1). The coordinate for a has a distinct first signature bit in every clause and hence has weight m=r(r+1)/2 to every old vertex. Kruskal therefore connects the old vertices using only cycle edges before any edge to a; every minimum tree consists of that cycle minus one edge and one edge to a. Equivalently the minimum needs exactly r old cycle edges of weight r and one edge to a of weight m; each other choice increases cost. Each interval crosses at most two retained cycle edges. The one auxiliary leaf edge contributes at most one more essential variable per clause. Thus every optimum has maximum union scope at most three, regardless of ties and polarities. This bound is for the stated seed basis.
