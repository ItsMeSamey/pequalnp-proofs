# Positive sparse-profile handoff on an actual greedy trajectory

Use the October primary source at commit 42907c9e999cc00d6abe7a50bfc1023250edd1b9, quantitative-reduction.tex (the density dispatcher and the arbitrary-density proof), and main_full_version.tex (the branching rule, leaf bound, Tanner checks, and greedy hashes).

Fix sufficiently large even v. Take any connected simple cubic graph on v base vertices, with e=3v/2 edges. Introduce one bit x_i per base vertex, one z_e per edge, and one guard g. Thus n=v+e+1. Write ν=ceil(log₂ ceil(log₂ n)) and D=12ν. For each base edge ij include (x_i ∨ x_j ∨ z_ij). On the same v vertices form the simple D-regular circulant joining cyclic distances 1 through D/2, possible since D<v−1. For every circulant edge ij include (g ∨ x_i ∨ x_j). The formula is connected, all declared bits occur, and the clauses are distinct triples. The number of clauses is 3v/2+Dv/2>nν, so the source selects the parity-query branch on the original input.

The sparsifier tests proper nonempty hearts of width-three clauses against thresholds M and M² for pairs and singletons respectively. Its choice has M≥Γ/σ and M=O(σ^{-1}log(2/σ)), with fixed positive Γ and σ^{-1}=ceil(sqrt(ceil(log₂ n))). Consequently D<M and Dv/2≥M². A guard-base pair has degree D, a base-base pair degree at most two, and all other pairs degree at most one. Every base singleton has degree D+3<M², and each gate singleton has degree one. The unique eligible heart is {g}. In its A-child all filler clauses are replaced by the unit (g), alongside all real edge clauses. There is no eligible heart left, and the source retains the unit without propagation. Both children use the same n declared variables. The leaf has q=e+1 clauses and uses the source bound B=2(1+M+M²). The dispatcher selected the original input once; the source runs its sparse-check and greedy procedures on this nontrivial leaf.

Every word w∈GF(2)^q is a physical violation word on this leaf: set all base spins zero, g=1−w_guard and each z_e=1−w_e. Put μ=floor(log₂ q)+1 and P=20000 B²μ². If r₀ is the rank of the initial Tanner matrix, then r₀=o(q). Indeed its row count is less than q/B if the source graph parameter q_G>1 and at most q/(4B)+2 otherwise. At a selected prefix let K be the kernel of its checks. Every word in K is attainable with positive multiplicity. If K is nontrivial, some coordinate is nonzero on K. Its singleton parity is an available trial (the list has P slots with zero coefficients permitted) and rejects at least one accepted assignment. Every row in the existing span leaves the score unchanged. Hence every minimizing choice adds an independent row until the rank reaches q, for any tie-breaking and any permitted initial Tanner construction.

After j selected rows, for 0≤j≤q−r₀, there are exactly 2^{q−r₀−j}−1 distinct accepted nonzero violation words. Choose an integer 1≤s≤floor((P−1)/2), and put h=floor(log₂(s+1)). For sufficiently large v, h≤O(log log n)<q−r₀. The first prefix having positive nonzero support at most s occurs after exactly j*=q−r₀−h selected rows, and has 2^h−1 nonzero patterns. The score stream describing that prefix begins in the following round. At the preceding prefix the support exceeds s. This is a statement about the actual sequence for the specified leaf without assuming a supplied intermediate prefix.

When h≥4, some nonzero pattern at this first prefix has multiplicity at least 2^{v/16}. For a uniform w∈K, each base vertex has its three incident edge coordinates simultaneously zero with probability at least 1/8, since their projection has rank at most three. The expected number of such vertices is at least v/8. Removing the zero word's contribution costs v/2^h≤v/16 in this unconditioned expectation. Some nonzero word therefore has at least v/16 such vertices. Fix its guard and gate bits as above, set the endpoints of its violated edges to zero and vary all remaining base spins independently. Its violation word stays fixed.

The original native run charges initial Tanner setup, all candidate score evaluations in the j* earlier rounds, then any score evaluations used for recovery at the handoff. The source enumerates (2q)^P ordered lists per round, including repeated representations, and its general sparse-query evaluator has bit time 2^{O(n log log n/sqrt(log n))}poly(n) and polynomial space here. Its full greedy procedure runs 3n rounds. A modified solver may stop this leaf upon certified recovery of its zero-pattern multiplicity, with any repeated passes or extra queries charged separately. Prior scores describe their own prefix. The other sparsification child and its descendants must still be processed for the original count. For a binary header declaring n, resource bounds in n charge the declared count, not just its header bit length. This family explicitly uses every declared bit. No runtime lower bound for alternative solvers follows from the number of selected rows.
