# A recognizable padded grid with a tractable final scalar and a large cubic character

Take the open L-by-L square grid, L≥2, with one Boolean spin x_u per vertex. Each edge e=uv receives two distinct private gate bits z_e^+, z_e^- and the clauses
[
 (x_u\lor x_v\lor z_e^+),\qquad
 (\neg x_u\lor\neg x_v\lor z_e^-).
]
For each gate bit z give it b private padding bits. Include all negative clauses on three distinct variables of its (b+1)-set consisting of z and those bits. All these sets are otherwise disjoint. Use b=floor((log_2 L)^(1/8)) and sufficiently large L that b≥2. The formula is connected even for b=2. It has no units, tautologies or duplicate clauses. Padding variables are pure negative; this is a construction for the specified exact counting procedure. No variable is forced: all gate bits may be one with all padding zero and arbitrary grid assignment; either gate may instead be zero when its grid clause is already satisfied, and one padding bit may be set to one with the associated gate one.

## Recognition from the signed clauses

No order of clauses, auxiliary naming scheme or embedding is needed for the following check. Grid variables have at least two positive occurrences (their grid degree), gate variables have exactly one, and padding variables have none. Identify these classes, then require that each clause with a positive literal is one of the two specified grid-edge clauses, with a positive gate and the required same-sign endpoints. Match the two clauses for each unordered endpoint pair; check uniqueness of all gate roles. The negative clauses containing each gate identify its padding neighbors, since b≥2. Check disjointness and cardinalities, and reconstruct the entire expected clause multiset, comparing it to the input, including all negative clauses without a gate. Verify that every declared variable is accounted for, or explicitly factor out independently declared unused bits. This uses polynomial time in the explicit formula length.

One direct square-grid test tries two degree-two vertices as proposed corners (0,0) and (L−1,0), with L the integer square root of the number of proposed grid vertices. Require their distance to be L−1. For each vertex with respective distances d_0,d_1, assign i=(d_0−d_1+L−1)/2 and j=d_0−i. Check integral coordinates, a bijection onto the square and exactly the Manhattan-neighbor edge set. Genuine adjacent corners succeed, including when L=2. Finally check b against the chosen schedule when recognizing this specific family.

## Source path and matching restriction

Write n for the declared variable count and m for the clause count. There are L² grid variables, 2L(L−1) grid edges and two private padding sets per edge, so n=Θ(L²b), m=Θ(L²b³). The maximum literal occurrence is O(b²)=o(sqrt(log n)). The source switches to its sparse-parity-query procedure at m>n ceil(log_2 ceil(log_2 n)), which holds here. Its sparsification requires a nonempty heart with threshold at least of order sqrt(log n) on this schedule; any nonempty heart occurs no more often than one of its literals. Thus there is a single original leaf. See the [October arbitrary-density reduction](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/quantitative-reduction.tex) and the [source sparsification and greedy-hash lemmas](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/build/main_full_version.tex).

Let C be the matrix of the final selected tests on clause-violation bits over GF(2). Its zero system contains every CNF model and has exactly the model count, so agreement is pointwise. Take a grid matching of size t=Θ(L²), for example adjacent horizontal pairs in each row. Set all padding bits to zero, all minus gates to one, and all plus gates outside the matching to one. Fix grid endpoints outside the matching arbitrarily. No padding clause can fail under any remaining assignment. The only possible violations are the matched plus clauses, one disjoint triple of free coordinates per matching edge. Their violation functions are (1+x_u)(1+x_v)(1+z_e^+) over GF(2), so every subset is realizable as the exact violation set. This deals with the constant and lower terms in the signed-clause expansion: after translating the three bits, each function is exactly a product of three bits. A nonempty dependence among these t columns of C would give an invalid accepted assignment. Hence they are independent, and some character λ of the final row space hits every one of them. The restricted λ phase is a sum of t disjoint cubic monomials in the translated coordinates.

In particular, a common codimension-h direction space on all of whose cosets this phase is quadratic must have h≥t/2. Intersect it with the 3t-dimensional restriction and then with the first-coordinate shore of the t triples. The latter intersection has dimension at least t−h; for h<t, setting all independent pivot coordinates to one gives a vector v of weight at least t−h. Contracting the third derivative with v gives an alternating bilinear form of rank 2 wt(v) on the restricted space. The intersected direction space is isotropic for this form, so its rank is at most 2h. This is a character/backend obstruction, not a scalar lower bound.

The matching statement can also be retained on one favorable heavy-variable branch of the final query. At most n/sqrt(log n)=o(L²) coordinates are fixed in the source heavy-variable step. Discard matching edges meeting a heavy grid spin or a heavy plus gate. Assign heavy gate bits one, heavy padding bits zero and heavy grid bits arbitrarily, and then apply the preceding restriction to the surviving matching. This leaves Θ(L²) triples. No claim is made about every such branch.

## Integrating the unspecialized count

Within a private padding set at most two bits can be one. Its conditional counts are A=1+b+binom(b,2) at z=0 and B=1+b at z=1. If the grid endpoints agree, exactly one of the paired gate clauses forces its gate bit to one; the other gate is free. If the endpoints differ, both gates are free. The effective edge weights for agreement and disagreement are respectively C_e=B(A+B) and D_e=(A+B)². These formulas also work with edge-dependent b_e≥2 provided both gates on the same edge use the same b_e. Weights may vary by edge. The original asymptotic schedule above uses a common b.

For completeness, the polynomial-time computation below uses only rational dimer weights. For a spin assignment let F be the dual edges crossing its disagreement edges. The plane dual includes the outer face. Face-boundary parity makes F even at every dual vertex. Conversely, the face boundaries span the cycle space of a connected plane grid, so every even dual F assigns consistent spin differences along all primal paths, producing exactly two complementary spin assignments. Therefore the count equals twice the sum over even dual edge sets of the product of D_e on selected edges and C_e on the others.

Split each dual vertex of degree greater than three into a trivalent tree with the old ports in their cyclic order. Use internal edges of unit weight for both states. Given old edge bits, the evenness constraints at the tree vertices have a unique extension precisely when the old incident bits have even total parity (peel leaves or solve the tree boundary system). The split graph is planar; parallel dual edges use distinct ports.

Replace each resulting degree-k vertex by a clique on its k port vertices, for k≤3. Retain each edge as an external dimer edge between corresponding ports. A present external dimer means the old edge was unselected. The remaining ports at a clique must be paired internally, in exactly one way if their number is even and in zero ways otherwise. Draw each clique in a disk respecting the cyclic port order. Give all internal dimer edges and split-tree external edges weight one. Give each original dual external edge weight C_e/D_e. Multiplying the resulting perfect-matching partition by 2 times the product of all D_e gives exactly the desired count.

Use a planar Pfaffian orientation to compute the weighted matching partition, the absolute Pfaffian, or equivalently the nonnegative square root of the determinant of the weighted skew adjacency matrix. Weights are positive. For example, multiply the entire matrix by Q=product_e D_e to clear denominators. If the dimer graph has 2v vertices, divide the integer square root by Q^v before applying the prefactor, using rational arithmetic. The graph has O(L²) vertices and edges; Q has O(L² log(b+2)) bits for equal b. The determinant and all required powers have polynomial bit length by the determinant bound, and exact determinant, integer square root and rational normalization take polynomial bit time. The combined answer is an integer. The planar dimer step uses the [Fisher construction for planar Ising models](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.1704825) and the planar Pfaffian method; the explicit gadgets above specify the convention and normalization used here.

This computes the final selected query's scalar by first recognizing the original source CNF. It does not give the same algorithm for discarded trial systems, arbitrary interior spin pins, the generic character contractions, or an input consisting solely of an unrecognized recoded parity matrix.
