## Actual intermediate and discarded sparse-hash scores on the padded grid

Use the square grid and its corner-coordinate recognizer from padded-planar-joint-scalar.md. Let L be its side, b its padding size, n the original declared variables, and m the clauses. For any parity matrix C on these clauses' violation indicators V, Gaussian elimination gives r independent basis rows. The exact query is N(C)=2^{-r} Σ_h Σ_x (−1)^{h·V(x)}, streaming h over their span. Repeated literals in a check list here mean repeated clause indices, which cancel in pairs. This equality holds before any greedy selection, even for discarded trials.

For each h and each of four assignments on an edge's base endpoints, enumerate each gate and its private pad bits, at most 2^{b+1} choices per private block. Sum the signs of exactly the selected violation indicators on the block, including its gate clause, and multiply the two block sums for each edge. All resulting four-entry edge tables are signed integers. Across edges the private bits are disjoint, so only a sum over base spins of the product of their edge tables remains. Any declared unused bits factor once. To evaluate it using polynomial space, cut each rectangle on a median line perpendicular to its longer side and condition on all separator spins. Charge edges within the line once; edges to each subrectangle give unary factors there. Multiply the sequential recursive evaluations of the two subrectangles and sum over line spins. Unit and empty subrectangles are direct. After two successive splits the longest side drops by at least a factor two up to rounding, so the sum of line lengths on any recursion path is O(L). Space is polynomial. Time per query is 2^{r+O(L+b)}poly(n+m+bits(C)). Integer bit lengths are polynomial: each original edge is charged once, all private sums have absolute value at most their number of raw assignments, and the full Walsh accumulator has at most n+r+2 bits. Divide by 2^r exactly. In the binary-header convention the complexity charges declared n, including the cost to print factors for unused bits.

Take b=floor((log_2 L)^{1/8}) for sufficiently large L, with no extra unused variables. The source makes one sparsification leaf and m=Θ(nb²). Its exact chosen leaf parameter B=2Σ_{i=0}^2 M^i is Ω(log n), since M≥Γ/σ with 1/σ=ceil(sqrt(ceil(log_2 n))). The sparse-check construction uses 2q_G c_G rows, fewer than m/B for q_G>1 and at most m/(4B)+2 otherwise. In round j with a trial the rank is therefore ≤O(n/(log n)^{3/4})+j+1 irrespective of greedy choices. Counting all trial scores and making all greedy choices through j=floor(n/(log n)^{3/5}) takes total 2^{O(n/(log n)^{3/5})}poly(n) bit time and polynomial space. The Tanner setup, trial generation and number of candidates contribute factors 2^{(log n)^{O(1)}} and L=O(sqrt n). This uses the source's sparsification-bound B, not a smaller empirical degree. For later rounds only the rank-dependent clock is established.

On the original matching restriction from padded-planar-joint-scalar.md every subset of t=Θ(n/b) matched clauses is realized as an exact violation set. Restricting C to these columns gives nullity ≥t−r. One realization of each nonzero kernel word supplies at least 2^{t−r}−1 distinct nonmodels accepted in addition to every model, throughout the stated prefix. Thus final-query equivalence cannot give the intermediate scores. The separate polynomial final-scalar solver does not reconstruct all late high-rank greedy choices. No unrestricted SAT exponent gain follows.
