# All samples of the COPY overlap graph

For the loopless graph family in overlap-child.md the same vertex decomposition gives all t, including overlapping packet pairs with many shared gates. Guess the two endpoint populations a,b on each edge consistently at vertices. At an endpoint let Q_a(u)=(1-t)u+t a/L, u=0,1. Each edge uv, connecting vertices with distinct names i,j, has a two-variable marker polynomial
$$
E_{ij} = \sum_{u,v=0}^1 z_i^u z_j^v Q_a(u)Q_b(v)
 [Q_a(u)Q_b(v)+(1-Q_a(u))(1-Q_b(v))].
$$
The first two factors are the primary positive units. The final factor is the COPY2 joining the twin ports. The complete scalar sums, over vertex populations, the coefficient of the marker monomial with those populations as its exponents in the product over edges. Every configuration of the original subdivision bits contributes once: one bit at each edge endpoint, shared with its free-packet twin, with the population forced by coefficient selection. Signed units replace just the corresponding first factor by its complement.

Use a supplied nice tree decomposition with each edge introduced once in a bag containing its endpoints. A bag state gives final populations and partial tallies of ones for all vertices in the bag. Introduce vertices at tally zero, try the four bit pairs on edge introduction and increase the endpoint tallies. Forget a vertex only at tally equal to final population, summing states. At a join, match final populations and add child tallies, discarding overshoots. Each edge has one owner so no correction for repeated unary factors is needed. The empty root yields the stated scalar. At width w the direct join uses at most (L+1)^(3(w+1)) combinations times polynomial overhead. For rational t=u/v each edge denominator divides (vL)^4. Intermediate summand counts and heights cost polynomially many bits in input and sample size. Since each edge represents one original variable and supplies four real ports, M=4N. On bounded-width graphs this acquires all samples in polynomial original-variable bit time, without restricting a linear order to small width. Arbitrary degree-L overlap graphs remain unrestricted in width.

For two vertices with L parallel edges, the coefficient is a sum over four pair-type counts summing to L. For multiplicities c_uv set a=c_10+c_11, b=c_01+c_11. Weight the edge product by L! divided by the four factorials. This costs O(L^3) terms per sample instead of enumerating 4^L bit pairs. It admits mixed signs by grouping equal sign-pair types separately. These conclusions concern the stochastic deformation only.
