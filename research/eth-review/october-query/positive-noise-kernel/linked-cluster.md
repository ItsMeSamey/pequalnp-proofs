# Low-order endpoint coefficients by connected gate sets

Let n be the number of tied occurrence bits, M the number of real ports, G the number of positive-arity gates, d their maximum arity, and Δ the maximum degree of the graph joining two gates that use the same occurrence bit. Repetition within one gate creates no additional vertex. Each occurrence bit supplies two slots in the native construction, so Δ≤d there. Remove empty clauses and handle isolated original variables separately. Write S=∏(l_p+1) over packets of real load l_p≤L. Take 0≤J≤M. The target coefficients are those of F(1−h) through degree J.

Fix final populations s and use a marker z_p for each packet. At a gate g form its conditional acceptance B_g(X,h) at the port means s_p/L+h(X_e−s_p/L). This uses the complete signed OR or COPY probability, including all terms. The value b_g=B_g(X,0) is independent of X. Give bit e marker Z_e=∏z_p^{m_ep}, where m_ep counts its source slots in packet p. For an auxiliary variable u define

    T(s,z,u,h) = Σ_X ∏_e Z_e^{X_e} ∏_g (u+B_g(X,h)).

Summing [z^s] T(s,z,0,h) over s gives the actual scalar. For each s, interpolate each marker on the positive integer grid 1,…,l_p+1 and u on 1,…,G+1. These grids suffice because the separate degrees are at most l_p and G. The grid and the s loop cost S²(G+1) evaluations. Positive grid values make every normalizing factor below nonzero, including for b_g=0.

At a grid point let independent bits have Pr(X_e=1)=Z_e/(1+Z_e). Set A_g=(B_g−b_g)/(u+b_g). Divide T by ∏_e(1+Z_e)∏_g(u+b_g). The result is H(V)=E∏_{g∈V}(1+A_g), with V the full gate set. Each A_g is divisible by h. Work over rational series truncated modulo h^{J+1}. For a gate set U compute

    D(U) = Σ_{W⊆U} (−1)^{|U|−|W|} log H(W).

Möbius inversion gives log H(V)=Σ_U D(U). Disjoint components use disjoint independent bits, so H(W) factors across components of U. The alternating sum of the logarithms vanishes for disconnected U. To see that D(U) starts in degree |U|, insert a formal variable y_g multiplying each A_g. Inclusion-exclusion discards precisely the monomials missing a gate of U. Every surviving monomial uses at least |U| factors divisible by h. Thus only connected U of size at most J are required. The empty set contributes zero. Exponentiate the accumulated log and multiply back the two normalization products before interpolation.

A connected set of k gates can be enumerated from a root using a rooted ordered spanning tree shape (fewer than 4^k choices) and at most max(1,Δ) choices per child in its parent's neighbor list. Reject repeats and deduplicate vertex sets by their sorted lists. For each U, enumerate at most 2^k subsets W and evaluate H(W) by summing at most 2^{dk} assignments to the distinct bits in those gates, counting repeated bits only once. Polynomial multiplication, formal logarithm and exponential through order J use polynomially many rational operations. The total bit time is S² poly(M,L) times (C max(1,Δ) 2^d)^{O(J)} for an absolute C. The case J=0 just uses the baseline product.

Here are explicit common denominators at a fixed grid point. Put D_g=L^{arity(g)}(u+b_g), a positive integer, and D=∏_e(1+Z_e)∏_g D_g. Every coefficient of H(W) has denominator dividing D by clearing the bit normalizations and factoring each local gate over its D_g. The bit length of D is O((M+n)log(2L)+G log(G+3)), since Σ_ep m_ep=M and 1≤z_p≤L+1. The sum of absolute coefficients of a port mean or its complement as a polynomial in h is at most 2. The gate probability has coefficient norm at most 2^{arity(g)+1}+1. At a positive grid point dividing by u+b_g≥1 gives a bound 2^{O(M+G)} for the coefficient norm of every H(W). Hence log H(W) coefficients up to degree J have denominator dividing D^J J! and absolute size at most (2+2^{O(M+G)})^{O(J+1)}. Accumulating all connected sets and their subset terms adds O(log(M+1)+J log(2+Δ)) bits of magnitude. For the exponential, if Q=D^J J!, the common denominator Q^J J! suffices through degree J. Coefficient magnitudes acquire at most another O(J+1) factor in their bit lengths. These are polynomial bit lengths since J≤M. Normalize rational arithmetic after operations. Restoring the prefactors costs polynomial bits.

For interpolation, first restore each grid value of T exactly. Its coefficients have a common denominator L^M independent of the grid. Each univariate Lagrange basis on 1,…,r+1 has denominator dividing r! and numerator coefficient norm at most (r+2)^r. Thus all interpolation weights together have common denominator dividing G!∏_p l_p! and polynomial bit length, as do the intermediate interpolation sums. Taking [z^s], evaluating u at zero, and summing over s preserve the claimed bit bound.

For the balanced native packing with P≤3M/L, log₂ S²≤6M log₂(L+1)/L. For a bounded-occurrence fixed-width CNF on N used original variables, M=O(N), n=M/2, and d and Δ are bounded. The time is therefore 2^{O(N log(L+1)/L+J)} poly(N,L) given the layout. This gives subexponential original-variable acquisition of these coefficients when L grows and J=o(N). It does not supply the full set of coefficients in that time.

Finally L^M F(1−h) has integer coefficients. At rational h of 2-adic valuation b>0, truncate after degree min(M,ceil(k/b)−1) to obtain its residue modulo 2^k, using the inverse of the odd denominator of h. To obtain F itself to additive 2-adic precision k use k+M v₂(L) bits on the scaled value before division. This loss matters for power-of-two L; the cost claim for scaled residues does not yield general exact counts.
