# Cost of acquiring the primitive sample by connected clusters

Combine physical-cluster-certificate.md with primitive-circle-coordinate-recovery.md (656d1804). Write A=C0^M, Df=q−1, K0 for the power of two in the LLL lattice, delta=1/(A K0), and V for the absolute upper bound on the sample in the cluster certificate. Graph maximum degree is bounded by Dg≥1, residual block scope by s, and original tabulation scope by b. The prime for count recovery exceeds M/2+3. For the literal lattice parameters log2 K0 exceeds Df log2 S, with S>2^(Df+2), hence P=log2(1/delta)=Omega(M²). This concerns that sufficient precision choice, not a necessary worst-case precision.

If V≤delta/2 the zero approximation is already certified. Otherwise set J=max(1,ceil(log2(16(m+1)max(1,V/delta)))); a log tail of at most m 2^(−J)/(J+1) changes the sample by at most V(exp(m 2^(−J)/(J+1))−1)<delta/2. Reserve delta/2 for arithmetic. With the same block tests, a literal upper bound is polynomial in source size, q,J,P, baseline heights, d and M,L times m[2^b + (8Dg·2^s)^J]. A more informative implementation uses subsets of size at most min(m,J) with dense arithmetic truncated to order J. Disconnected support gives zero even when arbitrarily many repeated powers of a smaller support occur. Thus the combinatorial exponent in the bound becomes min(m,J), while the arithmetic factor stays polynomial in J and P. All recognition and local table costs remain. If m=0, just evaluate the scalar prefactor.

On V≥1 and m≤M, J exceeds m asymptotically since P=Omega(M²). The certificate's generic support cutoff then omits no connected subset by size. It is invalid to plug the constant-error O(log(mV)) cutoff into this clock. This is not an exponential lower bound: graphs can have few connected subsets. For example paths have O(m²), though the literal enumeration of all W⊆A and of their s|A| scope bits still has an exponential upper bound. If m is small enough that the generic support bound improves, an exact one-node calculation is already available for the same supplied blocks by tabulating and enumerating their union of at most min(h,ms) shared Boolean bits: poly(input,q,P)·[m 2^b+m 2^min(h,ms)] with polynomial-bit cyclotomic arithmetic (charge all tabulated coefficient heights). It recovers exact coordinates directly. This simpler exact bound is at most the corresponding full-support generic bound up to polynomial factors. Each connected component can likewise be contracted on its own raw bits before multiplying. If V is exponentially tiny, use the actual V/delta in J; no such small-V guarantee follows from bounded scope or degree. All zeros within delta are distinguishable by LLL only in the certified bounded-height representation, with count-recovering prime.

By comparison, constant absolute tolerance at every point on the rounded circle costs O(M) separately certified samples and uses O(log(m+1)+log⁺V) orders per sample. Existence of a cluster certificate at one primitive embedding supplies none of the other node certificates. The two routes yield different conditional resource bounds. Neither yields a new worst-case SAT clock.

There is a sharper dichotomy even for tiny V. Let H≥2 be the uniform bound on the distinguished integral value beta=A F and every conjugate from the LLL note (its U). If A V H^(Df−1)<1, the integer norm of beta has magnitude <1, so the exact primitive value is zero and its origin count is zero. Otherwise V/delta≥K0/H^(Df−1); the chosen K0>100(Df+2)S[S(Df+H)]^Df makes this ratio >S^Df. Hence J=Omega(Df²) for EVERY case not immediately certified zero by this norm test. This uses the conservative height and precision already required by the LLL reduction. The comparison does not require positive origin count, a lower bound on V, or an additional oracle. It still allows better acquisition methods with different precision analyses or structure-specific contraction of connected subsets.
