# Extreme-center ordinary jets for the original signed Boolean root

Fix a native Boolean OR/COPY root, original physical port ordering, and source-legal packets of width L=2^a. M denotes the number of real rows. Each actual column COPY2 ties two physical ports to one Boolean bit X_j. The argument uses the Appendix-D envelope of at most 3M/L full padded packets (or substitute the actual packet count), and does not require finding a favorable layout. Work at T=t+2LZ modulo Z^(J+1), centered at Z=0. For an active source center i=v₂(t)<u, use P_t=p+8 ceil(3M/u)(L/2^i)+8M(a+1) and through-order J_native=ceil(3M/u)(u−i)−1. Source coefficients and any old truncated V live in their own integral extension, independently of Z. The result computes the actual requested ordinary coefficients through any J at the full supplied precision.

At the center the native kernel is zero except at displacement t, where c=C′(t)/C′(0) is a rational 2-adic unit. Write the row form as z_i=cX_{s_i}+Σ_j Δ_ij(Z) X_j, with missing base successor treated as zero. Every Δ has zero constant, including at a missing slot. This exact subtraction at Z=0 is the distinction from subtracting the mod-two permutation at an arbitrary translated evaluation point. Merge repeated read coefficients for a physical bit without treating its occurrences as independent.

Use one binary state h per original row vertex, including ordinary signed OR clauses of any arity. An OR predicate is the unrestricted product of ones with coefficient +1 minus the product fixing every local y_i to its unique signed falsifying bit. Thus h selects one of two product terms, with unary vertex weights +1 or −1. A nonempty COPY/equality vertex has two unit-weight terms fixing every local y_i to h. Handle constant zero-arity predicates separately by their definitions. Optional external signs on row bits multiply their individual factors. Let w_i(h,y) be the resulting binary local factor, including any row sign. Then summing the row bit against the physical Bernoulli kernel gives A_i(h)+B_i(h)z_i, where A=w_i(h,0) and B=w_i(h,1)−w_i(h,0). These are integral product decompositions. The signed-CNF root requires no division by 2 or increased precision for its gate states. For parity-vertex Fourier decompositions instead, use the exact precision lift and final division from center-jet-pairwise-fourier.md.

Select k rows for defect factors, choosing a real source bit j once per selected row. Their contribution includes Π Δ_ij(Z), unary factors B_i(h), and pins X_j=1 (duplicate pins count once). Unselected rows without a base successor contribute unary factors A_i(h). For each source bit j let I_j be its set of unselected base-successor rows. Its remaining factor is the sum, over x∈{0,1} compatible with its pin, of (−1)^(ρ_j x)Π_{i∈I_j}(A_i(h)+B_i(h)c x). Here ρ_j is an optional original column sign; an empty product is one. The slot shift is injective and each logical bit has two physical ports, hence |I_j|≤2. These are scalar, unary, or pair factors on the vertex states. Multiplying vertex weights and summing all h therefore gives one ordinary two-spin partition sum. Terms with k>J vanish from this jet regardless of precision. This extends the parity/COPY-only pairwise calculation of center-jet-pairwise-fourier.md to the actual signed Boolean SAT root, including all original occurrences and gate labels.

Each packet has at most d=L−|t| possible real successor rows. Across the entire layout at most 3Md/L rows have a base successor. A nonloop pair edge requires two such rows assigned to the same j; each row is counted at most once. Thus there are at most floor(3Md/(2L)) genuine pair edges before any deletions. Build one cover by taking one endpoint of each distinct pair edge; loops are unary. This same cover works for every defect term. Enumerate its assignments, then multiply the independent unary partition sums over the remaining states. Store only the current assignment, factors and truncated polynomial, in polynomial space. Building a cover uses a polynomial scan of the supplied slots; optionally use any smaller validated cover. If c_t is the lesser of the number of old vertices and the above cover bound, the total time is at most

    poly(input,P_t,J,L,ring dimension) · 2^c_t · Σ_{k=0}^{min(M,J)} binom(M,k) L^k.

All coefficient, preprocessing, row-selection, input/output, and pair-factor work is charged. Since the spin factor and c are independent of Z, one can sum their scalars and then multiply by the selected polynomial product. For fixed J both odd extreme centers ±(L−1) have time 2^{O(M/L)}poly(input,P_t,L) with polynomial space even on an original graph of linear width. The bound is subexponential in M when L grows. This is a source-uniform center-specific route, not a statement that an individual graph has large width after the slot shift. At t=−L the graph is empty, but that grid center is inactive in the original shell schedule. Center t=0 is also inactive.

The published active odd extreme-center request has J_native=ceil(3M/u)u−1, generally at least M. Charging the literal row enumeration then admits up to (1+L)^M terms. No reduction in the full native clock follows from the extreme-center saving for low-order coefficients alone. Recognized lower orders may be evaluated separately, only if the remaining center coefficients and other field samples are acquired by a separately charged method. The elementary binary product decompositions and vertex-cover conditioning are standard; the native claim uses the shift geometry and Appendix-D packet bound.
