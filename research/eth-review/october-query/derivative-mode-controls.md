For any original fixed-density exact 3-CNF with three distinct variables in every clause and m=Θ(n), each signed OR3 falsity indicator is a product of literals in {y,1−y}; its complement has at least two odd multilinear coefficients for every sign pattern (all-positive OR has seven and all-negative has two). Every variable equality vertex has some odd coefficient because its table takes value one somewhere. Thus the original-vertex row shore of the native subdivision has Q≥2^m at every positive precision. The other shore contains one binary subdivision equality per existing edge, with polynomial 1−x−y+2xy and three odd coefficients per vertex, hence its Q grows exponentially as well. Example: cyclic clauses (¬x_i∨x_(i+1)∨x_(i+2)) on n≥4 variables, modulo n. They are connected, have both signs per variable and no units. The bound holds for every packing and leaves the algorithm for other representations or prior semantic preprocessing open. It shows that the measured one-shore Q alone cannot lower the original all-input exponent.
