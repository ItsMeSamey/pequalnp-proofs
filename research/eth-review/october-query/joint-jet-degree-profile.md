# Coefficient-dependent precision profile

Use the globally consistent scalar polynomial constructed in joint-jet-adversary.md (7d68189a). Fix a node t with active shell j and d=a+1−j−b≥0. Before extracting the coefficient of z^k, replace z by z/2^d. The effective shift is then 2^(j+b), exactly the smallest value used in the valuation proof for this shell. For each own-shell root of valuation j the reduced z coefficient has b bits of valuation, so every lower-bit congruence in that proof still applies. For lower roots the constant coefficient stays a unit and the new z coefficient integral. For higher roots in shell j+l, both coefficients have valuation at least −l. Hence F(t+2^(j+b)z) has coefficientwise valuation at least ceil(3E/2^(j+1)). Restoring the original shift proves that the coefficient of order k is divisible by 2 raised to ceil(3E/2^(j+1)) + k(a+1−j−b). A decoder allowing a different number of precision bits per coefficient must exceed at least one of these thresholds for parity recovery. The same two globally consistent polynomials witness failure if every requested coefficient stays below or at its threshold, even under adaptive zero-branch queries. No claim about queries at other points or non-jet access.
