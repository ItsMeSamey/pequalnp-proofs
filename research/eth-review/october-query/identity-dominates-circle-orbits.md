# Identity-only orbit recognition dominates all-sample recognition for counts

Consider the specific certified-transposition CNF algorithm of circle-scalar-orbits.md (0e363fbd). For each pair of physical COPY2 coordinates use the SAME marked old vertices from that proof, but evaluate their product only at T=0. At the identity W=I, so this product is the ordinary Boolean conjunction of the marked old predicates on actual subdivision word bits. Check truth equivalence under the proposed swap on their combined support, including the two swapped arguments. There are ≤4L marked vertices, each with ≤k'=max(3,k) ports. Thus exhaustive recognition needs at most 2^(4k'L+2) assignments per pair, with polynomial time per assignment and polynomial workspace. The unmarked product remains unchanged: all old predicates incident at the identity to either swapped COPY2 bit are among the marked vertices. No ideal circuit order or semantic equivalence oracle is needed.

Every swap accepted by the all-T polynomial test passes this identity-only test, by literal specialization of its same marked-product identity. The reverse need not be assumed. Consequently every partition block for the all-T method lies inside a block of the new partition. Merging sizes a,b can only decrease the number of weight profiles because a+b+1≤(a+1)(b+1). A successful orbit representative itself is an actual word; inconsistent COPY words correctly evaluate to zero. The whole clock is poly(input,M,L)(2^O(k'L)+Q_id) for counting and recovery. All arithmetic has polynomial bit length.

Hence any CNF SAT/count advantage of this transposition test on any family can be obtained at least as well in the asymptotic profile and recognition bounds by the same source grouping at the identity.
