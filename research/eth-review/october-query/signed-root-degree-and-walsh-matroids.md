# Exact root degree and exact polynomial Walsh support for every clause sign

At an original signed CNF root, let M be its number of real row ports and o the number of odd-arity old row COPY gates, including the internal trivalent COPY gates of the compiled source and its unary COPYs. Use any physical packet placement with real incidences correctly paired by column subdivision COPY2. Omit unused variables or account for them by their constant factor. Remove tautologies as in the compiler and handle identically constant formulas or empty false clauses separately. For nonempty Boolean OR/COPY row gates the exact characteristic-zero degrees are

    deg F=(M−o)(2L−1),        deg G=(M−o)(2L−2),

where G is the reflection-normalized alternative. This remains true for an unsatisfiable input. A general recursive child whose packet entries are arbitrary previously born tensors need not have this degree.

Write D=C′(0) and clear one denominator D per real port. At slot i of packet P the cleared read N_i=sum_y [C(T)/(T−(y−i))] X_y has degree 2L−1 with top coefficient B_P=sum_(real y in P) X_y, including any physical repeated occurrence of the same logical bit. For G, division by T+L cancels in each kernel and the leading coefficient is L B_P at degree 2L−2. All B_P have nonnegative integer coefficients; at the all-ones assignment they are positive whenever a real row occurs in P.

Summing each actual old row bit computes the gate multilinear extension. For an OR with s ports its cleared value is D^s minus the product of the s false-literal factors. Thus its top sign is (−1)^(number of positive literals+1), times the product of the B_P for all its ports. For an even COPY the top is twice that full product. For odd s the degree-s product cancels identically in product_i N_i+product_i(D−N_i). The next row-degree part is exactly D times the sum, over all choices of a single omitted port, of the products on the other s−1 ports. For s=1 this is the constant D. The coefficient gives the top T-degree specified above because every remaining N_i shares the same positive leading sign and degree. Multiplying all gates and summing the true column bits gives only one sign in the coefficient of the claimed degree. The all-ones column assignment contributes nonzero, proving equality. Dividing by D^M leaves the degree unchanged. Thus the scalar-sampling bound at this root can use the exact degree plus one distinct sample per retained center; a degree-based taper can be retuned. No uniform improved exponent follows, and coefficient degrees can drop further modulo any finite precision.

**Local support oracle.** In a packet module owning each of its old gates, give a gate capacity c=s for OR or even COPY and c=s−1 for odd COPY. An external logical bit may be matched to a real row port when an occurrence of that bit and that row are in the same packet. For a selected set S, use at most one row per bit, at most one bit per row, and at most c rows per gate. This finite capacitated matching test is exactly equivalent to the local unnormalized Walsh coefficient H(S) being nonzero over Z[T].

In any monomial of the local gate product, each row read supplies at most one occurrence of a bit, and each gate uses at most c distinct row reads. A Walsh sum on S kills every Boolean monomial missing a bit of S. This gives necessity, including after integrating internal bits. Conversely match S subject to the capacities. At each OR and even COPY include all rows in its top product. At an odd COPY omit one row outside the matching, which exists by the capacity. Choose the designated matched bit on each matched row, and choose any real column bit on every other used row. The corresponding monomial contains S. At the full module top degree all such monomials have one sign by the preceding proof; after Boolean reduction they still have that sign. For any monomial with distinct external support U containing S its Walsh sum carries sign (−1)^|S| and positive multiplicity 2^(number of external bits−|U|). Summing internal bits also preserves its coefficient sign. Hence H(S) has nonzero leading coefficient and its degree is exactly the module's sum of row capacities times the kernel degree. This test holds for full rational polynomials and does not assert that the value at a chosen modular sample survives.

**Matroid type.** The local supports form the Rado matroid of bit-to-row adjacency with a partition matroid on rows (one capacity per gate). Equivalently use vertex-disjoint directed paths from selected bits through distinct row vertices to distinct copies of gate slots. For a bipartite module graph, direct sum of the local matroids on each shore gives two represented matroids on the same external bits. Allowed global supports are their common independent sets. A single-packet module has just a uniform support cap because all its independent external bits are accessible at every real row. General multipacket modules need not give ordinary transversal matroids.

For a physical counterexample, in a fully diagonal class take three full first packets and their paired second packets, with at least two real bits per packet. Place one port of the same old trivalent variable COPY in each first packet, in place of three distinct degree-one COPY variables. All other row gates in those first packets remain unary COPY (zero capacity). Their second-packet partners are ordinary unit clauses on the appropriate variable; for distinct nonduplicate original clauses the three incidences of this genuine variable may instead use three distinct binary clauses with different fresh variables, in the same second packets, using extra edges from the same diagonal cell. Concretely take in each second packet one additional unit on a fresh variable and merge it with the true variable's unit into one OR2. This leaves all port slots and mates unchanged. Fill remaining first/twin positions with private units and COPYs in all ordered classes, using the same legal balanced layout and scale as in the private-unit construction. Choose the initial dyadic scale in the interior of an a plateau if needed after the O(1) raw-size change. In the module of the three first packets a bit can use only the single useful row in its own packet, and the one gate admits only two rows. Restrict to two bit IDs from each packet. The resulting matroid has rank two and three distinct parallel classes of size two. It is not transversal: the two individually nonloop bits in any one class must have the same sole right neighbor in a transversal presentation, or they could be matched simultaneously. Different classes require different sole neighbors since representatives from two classes are independent. Three distinct such neighbors would match three representatives, a contradiction.

The flow certificate removes the need to tabulate which full polynomial Walsh coefficients vanish. At each global prefix a chosen set passing all incident gate constraints can be extended by choosing all remaining bits zero. Binary search over inclusion of edges therefore enumerates potentially contributing supports with polynomial delay and space using ordinary integral flows; actual coefficient evaluation and its arithmetic costs are additional. A global support contributes a nonzero polynomial product, and its leading sign is the same for all feasible supports because each externally selected bit contributes its Walsh sign twice. These statements concern exact polynomials. Their values at the origin must still be summed with the actual weights.

For example on four Boolean variables require exactly two ones on each of the four three-variable subsets. Use the five clauses per subset forbidding 000,100,010,001,111. Every clause has zero, one or three negative literals, all clauses are distinct, and every scope has width three. Subtracting the four exact-two equations forces all variables equal, which would require 3x=2. The raw origin count is zero. The empty global support still exists. Separately, an inconsistent parity triangle and a consistent parity triangle on the same three edges, each represented by the two distinct binary clauses per edge that forbid the wrong pair assignments, have identical supports, no duplicate clauses, and origin counts zero and two. Thus optimizing a common independent support, including by weighted matroid intersection, does not compute the coefficient sum.
