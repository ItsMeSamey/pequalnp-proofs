# All-coset quadratic restrictions for sparse ANF of unbounded degree

Given explicitly listed squarefree Boolean ANFs on N bits, one can deterministically find a linear subspace of codimension at most t on every translate of which all the input functions have degree at most two, or report that none exists. The time is 2^{O(t²+t)} poly(N,input length) and the space is 2^{O(t)} poly(N,input length) bits. The polynomial exponents are independent of the input degrees and of t. The representation lists each monomial's coordinate support. Arbitrary circuit inputs are outside this statement.

Use the hyperplane search of [All-coset quadratic restriction](all-coset-degree-fpt.md), frozen SHA-256 1ae7bc425ba1425e2de57aa21d33c0793497f00acce880bf4ad64d82075e2ebe. The following replaces its bounded-degree computation at a node. If t≥N the zero subspace is an immediate solution. Otherwise a node's ambient subspace W is given in original coordinates by b≤t independent homogeneous equations. Row reduction chooses b pivot coordinates; let z denote the remaining n=N−b coordinates. Enumerate the 2^b right-hand sides, which are precisely the cosets. In each coset the b pivot coordinates are affine functions of z. The same free-coordinate basis identifies directions in W for all cosets.

**A coefficient oracle.** Fix one original polynomial with m monomial terms and one coset. During a left-to-right scan of the free bits, a binary state vector of length b records the partial pivot-coordinate parities, initialized by their affine constants. Reading a free bit updates that vector by adding the bit times a known column. For each monomial, disallow a transition when a required free bit is read as zero. Its terminal state test accepts exactly when all required pivot coordinates are one. This recognizes the restricted monomial, including the empty monomial. The zero polynomial may be handled separately.

Form the direct sum of these m finite-state programs over the binary field, with one initial state per term and the indicated accepting terminal vector. Write W_0≤m 2^b for the width, u for the initial row, v for the terminal column, and A_i(0), A_i(1) for the layer-i transition matrices. The value on z is u times the ordered product of A_i(z_i) times v. All these objects have explicit binary representations of size polynomial in N,m,2^b. Direct summation computes XOR of the restricted monomials even when several restrictions coincide.

Set B_i=A_i(0) and C_i=A_i(0)+A_i(1). The product of (B_i+z_i C_i) is already multilinear because each layer reads a distinct coordinate. Consequently the coefficient for a support S is obtained by one ordered matrix product, using C_i for i in S and B_i otherwise, between u and v. This calculation is valid over GF(2) without lifting to a larger field.

**Finding an obstruction without listing the ANF.** At layer j and degree k maintain a row-space basis consisting of actual coefficient-prefix rows. Initialize the degree-zero space by u. The next degree-k space is the sum of the preceding degree-k space multiplied by B_j and the preceding degree-(k−1) space multiplied by C_j. Eliminate dependent candidates while retaining independent input rows and their actual support words. This is exact by linearity. At the terminal layer, degree k has a nonzero coefficient precisely if some retained row has nonzero product with v. Scanning degrees returns either degree at most two or a maximum-degree nonzero coefficient and its support S. There are O(N²) layer/degree states, each with dimension at most W_0. Storing support words explicitly costs at most N bits per retained row. Dense binary matrix arithmetic and elimination already give 2^{O(b)} poly(N,input length) time and space for this computation.

If |S|=d≥3, select D⊆S of size d−3. Coordinate finite differences in all directions of D delete those variables from monomials containing D and kill all other monomials. The resulting polynomial g is cubic. Its coefficient on S minus D equals one. Every cubic coefficient on a triple J disjoint from D is exactly the original restriction's coefficient on D union J; other triples have coefficient zero. Thus O(N³) oracle calls suffice to obtain the alternating trilinear form of g directly. No subset expansion on D is required. A successful subspace H within W must annihilate this trilinear form on H³ because all translates of the original restrictions are quadratic along H, including the translates used for these differences.

**Search costs.** At each descendant regenerate the systematic chart and the relevant programs from the original sparse ANFs. Stream cosets and polynomials sequentially. Each of at most 2^b cosets costs 2^{O(b)} poly(N,input length) including the coefficient and maximum-degree computations. With h=t−b remaining cuts, the cited radical/image lemma supplies at most 2^{3h+1} hyperplanes and can be implemented by enumerating its vectors in 2^{O(h)} poly(N) time. Every valid subspace is covered by some branch; only an all-coset check may certify a returned space. Multiplying over depths gives the stated time. Depth-first traversal retains polynomial-size chart data and enumeration counters, and can discard the program and prefix spaces once the cubic form has been extracted. The stated space bound includes the largest such program and its linear algebra; streaming the cosets alone does not remove this exponential dependence on t.
