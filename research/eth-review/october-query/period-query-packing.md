# Sparse-query packing by binary translation periods

Assume quantitative-reduction.tex lines 108–120: a≤cn multilinear binary equations of fixed degree k, each with at most ceil(log2 n)^c terms. Fix k,c. Use O(n^(1/4)) test groups of O(n^(3/4)) rows each, and variable groups of at most ceil(n^(5/8)) coordinates. Any such computable grouping suffices.

For each test group g compute W_g={v:P_i(x+v)=P_i(x) for all x,i in g}. In Boolean multilinear normal form Δ_v P_i has degree≤k−1. On a linear subspace where every difference has degree≤j, taking its degree-j coefficient vectors is a linear function of v by Δ_(u+v)P=Δ_u P+T_u Δ_v P and invariance of the top homogeneous coefficients under translation. Evaluate this map on a basis, take its kernel, and descend j through zero. The resulting common periods are exact. Translation expands a monomial into ≤2^k terms; all dense coefficient arrays, bit arithmetic and rank calculations cost n^O(k) for fixed k,c.

From variable coordinate space U_h send the quotient by U_h∩W_g to g using r_gh=dim U_h−dim(U_h∩W_g) bits. At g lift each incoming label with a computed linear section, sum, and test the original equations. Any actual input differs from the chosen lift by a sum of periods. At h solve all outgoing quotient maps jointly, returning zero if inconsistent or 2^(dim U_h−rank(stack)) otherwise. The contracted integer counts exactly all satisfying assignments. Use Z/2^(n+1) at truncation length one. With M=sum r_gh, set s=n+M. Test degrees are at most their scope sums O(n^(3/4)k log^c n). Variable degrees are O(n^(1/4)n^(5/8))=O(n^(7/8)). Hence both meet ceil(s^(9/10)) eventually without high-incidence branching. Graph sizes and entry bit complexity are polynomial. The pinned contraction theorem yields poly(n)2^O(s/sqrt(log s)) bit operations and polynomial space.

For a leaf fix row-slot groups and variable blocks throughout the greedy hash process, or price a polynomial-size layout portfolio. Denote by M_final the wire count of its finally chosen rows. Each tested alternate deletes final rows then appends one polynomial in its slot. Deletion enlarges the periods. Adding a test supported on t coordinates increases r_gh by at most its number of supported coordinates in U_h, because the new polynomial is invariant on vectors zero at its support. Thus M_trial≤M_final+t with t=polylog n for the actual hash rows. Prospective final rows need not be known, since all candidate counts are exact and ties deterministic. Minimizing over priced layouts retains the comparison with the best final layout. If each leaf has M_final=O(n), the source's 2^O(n/sqrt(log n)) tree and 2^polylog(n) trials per leaf give the same poly(n+m)2^O_k(n/sqrt(log n)) total clock, a loglog factor improvement in the exponent on that class. In general charge each leaf separately and cap using the published generic incidence route. This uses the cited contraction theorem as a dependency.

Example at the sparse-query interface: partition data into blocks of polylog length L and use for each of O(n) edges uv a quadratic equation (sum block u)(sum block v)=0, with L² listed monomials. Keeping blocks intact costs at most one bit per appearance of a block in a row group, hence M≤2a. Adding arbitrary bounded-occurrence crossing small-scope tests costs at most their total support O(n). Pair tests are parity sums of negative binary clause violations. This does not claim the final rows of every original CNF exhibit these periods or that simpler preprocessing cannot solve specific examples.
