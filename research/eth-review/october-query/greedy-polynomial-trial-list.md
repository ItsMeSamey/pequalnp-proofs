# Polynomial deterministic trial list for the actual sparse-check greedy step

In the native sparse-check greedy reduction let m≥1 be the number of clauses, P≥1 the integer trial-weight cap, and n≥1 the number of original Boolean variables. The initial distance checks guarantee each surviving nonsolution has physical violation vector v of weight w≥m/P. Every genuine model has v=0. The original trials enumerate all short parity rows and compare counts of original assignments. A polynomial list of such rows suffices to retain the same 3n rounds.

Put h=min(P,m), and let Q be the least power of two ≥100m. Choose h distinct field points t_j, 0≤j<h, in GF(Q). Enumerate all pairs a,b in that field. At occurrence j use clause i_j obtained from the binary integer encoding of a*t_j+b reduced modulo m. For uniform a,b, any two distinct occurrences give independent values. Each clause has marginal probability at least floor(Q/m)/Q≥.99/m. Thus the number X of sampled occurrences where v has a 1 has mean μ≥.99: if h=P use w≥m/P, otherwise use w≥1. Pairwise independence gives E[X²]≤μ²+μ, so Cauchy–Schwarz gives Pr[X>0]≥μ/(1+μ)≥99/199.

For each such sample, take Q' the least power of two ≥64h. Enumerate independent x,y over GF(Q'), assigning j the inclusion bit L(y x^j), L any fixed nonzero binary linear functional. For each nonempty hit pattern its parity bias is at most (h−1)/Q'≤1/64: the sum of its distinct monomials X^j has at most h−1 roots and a nonroot gives a balanced bit over y. On a hit the resulting row thus rejects v at probability ≥63/128, even if sampled clauses repeat. XOR repeated clause indices to obtain an ordinary physical subset of size at most P. Consequently the rejection probability is at least 6237/25472>24/100. All choices are enumerated deterministically; zero rows may be skipped.

Apply linearity of expectation with the actual number of surviving original assignments as multiplicity. Minimizing the trial's full count preserves all models and leaves at most 19/25 of the current false assignments. After 3n rounds their integer count is bounded by 2^n(19/25)^{3n}<1 because 2·19³<25³. No feasible input is removed. Exhaustively evaluate Q²(Q')²=O(m²h²) trials per round, and stream a winning row plus the current best integer count. Finite-field construction by exhaustive monic irreducible search costs poly(m); per trial, row construction is poly(m), and the tested system is still an actual original sparse-check query. This replaces the previous combinatorial number of trial oracle calls by a polynomial. Each call, the initial distance checks, and the outside sparsification retain their original costs. This improvement alone gives neither a better leading counting exponent nor a polynomial SAT algorithm. No independent novelty claim about the elementary sampling tools.
