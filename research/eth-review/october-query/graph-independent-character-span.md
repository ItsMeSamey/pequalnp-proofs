# Graph-only character radius and exact score coefficients

Let G be a simple graph on m coordinates and allow every word v whose support is independent. For S⊆[m], write χ_S(v)=Π_{i∈S}(1−2v_i). Its least radius P for a real linear combination of characters of support size ≤P on this domain equals α(G[S]). Allowing short characters to use coordinates outside S does not reduce this radius.

Expand the product in squarefree monomials, deleting all nonindependent monomials because they vanish. For each remaining independent I substitute v_I=2^{−|I|}Π_{i∈I}(1−χ_i). A second expansion gives integral coefficients

    χ_S = Σ_{R⊆S} a_R χ_R,
    a_R = (−1)^|R| Σ_{independent I : R⊆I⊆S} (−1)^|I|.

Only independent R contribute, and every maximal independent R has a_R=1. The independent monomials on S have an invertible evaluation matrix on independent words (the subset-inclusion matrix is triangular with diagonal one); their characters also form a basis by triangular change of coordinates. To see necessity even allowing external features, take a maximum independent T⊆S and set every coordinate outside T to zero. All Boolean words on T remain, and χ_S restricts to χ_T. When |T|>P every allowed short character restricts to one orthogonal to χ_T under the uniform measure. The empty S has radius zero. For |T|>0 uniform even- and odd-parity measures on this subcube agree on all lower-weight character sums and disagree on χ_S. This is the standard squarefree nonface-ideal/independence-complex basis and Boolean Fourier orthogonality applied to score reuse.

For true violation words, opposite literals of a common variable certify an edge. Every physical assignment satisfies these exclusions but may satisfy additional restrictions. Summing the identity over all assignments accepted by a fixed genuine prefix A gives H_A(S)=Σ_R a_R H_A(R); multiplicities are retained. For each old short row H_A(R)=2N(A;R)−N(A). Apply the quarter-sum formula in prior-score-conflicts.md with S=h XOR c for the next-row score, or use a paid equivalent representative modulo earlier rows. The lower bound holds over all graph-independent words, not necessarily over the smaller support realized by a specific CNF and prefix.

Write s=|S|. Building the induced graph from literal occurrences costs polynomial bit time in the given formula, S and output adjacency. Initialize a table b_I=(−1)^|I| on independent subsets of S and zero otherwise. The superset zeta transform computes all inner sums using O((1+s)2^s) integer operations after testing independence; each entry including partial sums has O(s+1) bits. Apply the outer signs. A direct induced-edge scan per subset gives total 2^s poly(input,s) bit time; tracking the largest independent mask also decides the radius. Working storage for the transform is O((s+1)2^s) bits plus input and counters. If t nonzero coefficients are written out, charge O(t(s+1)) bits for dense local masks and coefficients, or O(t(s+1)(1+log(m+1))) with original coordinate lists. Charge retained output separately.

For polynomial working space, enumerate R and stream its supersets I one at a time, testing independence: at most 3^s pairs and 3^s poly(input,s) bit time plus output and score accesses. A preliminary streamed independence scan decides the radius before accessing old scores. A streamed exact linear combination requires O(n+2s+1) accumulator bits for n declared variables: |H_A(R)|≤2^n, |a_R|≤2^s, at most 2^s terms. Acquiring or retrieving old scores, encoding physical lists, choosing representatives and traversing requested candidates all incur their own costs. Exponential retained lookup storage does not become polynomial merely by streaming coefficients.

For a biclique with nonempty disjoint sides L,R in S let Q=S minus (L∪R). Every independent word has no active bit on at least one side, so (χ_L−1)(χ_R−1)=0. Hence χ_S=χ_{Q∪L}+χ_{Q∪R}−χ_Q: only three prior sums when both enlarged sides have size ≤P. No internal independence of L or R is required. Verification costs O(|L||R|) adjacency checks for a supplied biclique, or a literal scan for the opposite polarities of one common variable in non-tautological clauses. On a balanced complete biclique this avoids expanding the disjoint-matching recurrence into up to exponentially many terms. It does not assert a lower bound for optimized matching or require that the abstract measures above are physical.
