# Upper coefficient band from old COPY vertices

Consider an actual Boolean OR/COPY root subdivided once per incidence, with old vertices on the row shore and subdivision COPY2 on the column shore, using the integral endpoint variable T=−L+Lz. There are e original literal incidences, M=2e real row ports, and c nonempty old COPY vertices. Use the original canonical zero/one lifts, before any child birth or precision-changing recombination. Each row port contributes the field-linear form u_i=(W(T)X)_i in column bits. Its constant term in z vanishes, and the degree-h coefficient has 2-valuation at least h−1 for h≥1 by the real-shift cardinal product in inactive-endpoint-single-jet.md. These assertions hold coefficientwise in the integral unramified ring and any V truncation.

Sum the row bits locally before multiplying row tensors. An old COPY on d≥1 ports becomes ∏_i u_i+∏_i(1−u_i). Its full multilinear monomial coefficient is 1+(−1)^d, hence even (possibly zero). This also holds for COPY with complemented ports: exchanging u_i and 1−u_i on any subset changes the sign of the full coefficient. More generally the two complementary allowed words may carry integer weights w_0,w_1 equal modulo two. Their full-degree coefficient is then even, including signs arising from Walsh characters. Arbitrary weights can break the statement, for example weights (1,0). Zero-arity vertices should be accounted as separate scalar factors rather than counted in c. Original signed ORs have integral multilinear coefficients for every sign pattern; no divisibility is assumed of them.

The product over old vertices is multilinear in the M distinct port variables before substituting the shared column variables. A monomial selecting k ports must saturate at least max(0,k−M+c) old COPY vertices, since each unsaturated COPY omits at least one distinct port. Thus its coefficient carries this many powers of two. Its k selected forms can contribute order j only for j≥k, and their coefficient carries at least j−k powers of two by the single-port bound. Combined these exponents give at least max(0,j−M+c). All integral column weights and finite sums preserve this upper-tail estimate. The lower-tail estimate max(0,e−j) additionally uses the ordinary unweighted or character-weighted subdivision COPY2 summation over e independent bits; arbitrary column weights need not preserve that lower tail.

Consequently at modulus 2^p the original unweighted scalar can use only degrees max(0,e−p+1) through min(D,M−c+p−1,J−1), inclusive, with J the earlier β cutoff and D=M(2L−1). At p=1 this is contained in [e,M−c]. On an ordinary formula with n_used occurring variables there is one old COPY per occurring variable (or use c≥n_used if the certified construction provides additional such vertices). The used-variable count is ≤2^n_used and is determined by p=n_used+1. Hence j≤M at that precision. Multiply the recovered ordinary integer by 2^(n_declared−n_used) for the unused variables, excluding this multiplier from the used-variable scalar during the calculation. This precision argument requires the original unweighted model-count bound; a signed weighted scalar can obey the valuation bounds without obeying that count bound. Handle a constant formula directly. Nothing here restricts the runtime of symbolic evaluation or claims the old COPY structure for arbitrary recursive children.
