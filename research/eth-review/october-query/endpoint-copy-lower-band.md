# Low-order divisibility of original COPY2 endpoint jets

Use the ordinary Boolean signed-OR/COPY root after subdividing each of its e literal-incidence edges once by a binary equality tensor on the column shore. There are e independent column bits b_c and M=2e real ports; summing over the two port bits of each column with its COPY2 indicator is exactly summing over b_c with both incident ports set to b_c. Keep the raw unnormalized sum. A vertex of degree zero or a separate scalar can be handled by its original integral multiplier. This result does not assert identical divisibility after replacing these columns by arbitrary child tensors.

Use z=(T+L)/L and the integral real-port kernel from inactive-endpoint-single-jet.md. It vanishes at z=0 for every physical shift. Each row interpolation factor has the form (1−Y_x)+(2Y_x−1) Σ_c w_xc(z)b_c, where w_xc aggregates matching column-port weights belonging to c and has zero constant. Fix the accepted row tuple Y. In a degree-j coefficient of the product, every monomial in the b variables comes from selecting at most j nonconstant row factors; every such selection introduces one b. Boolean reduction b_c²=b_c therefore gives an integral multilinear polynomial of degree at most j, independent of packing. Summing a squarefree monomial supported on s≤min(e,j) indices over all 2^e column tuples gives exactly 2^(e−s). Integral row weights and scalar sums preserve its common divisibility. Hence v₂([z^j]F(−L+Lz))≥max(0,e−j). The same proof allows an inserted column Walsh character: summation vanishes if any character coordinate lies outside the monomial support and otherwise yields signed 2^(e−s). At this root canonical Boolean lifts are literal zero and one; integer signs and integral truncated-ring row coefficients preserve the same ideal-membership assertion.

Combine this bound with β(j) from inactive-endpoint-single-jet.md. At precision 2^p only degrees max(0,e−p+1)≤j<J can matter, where J is the first β(j)≥p or D+1 if none. At p=1 this interval is contained in [e,M]. Consequently every odd-model-count root on declared, used variables has some odd coefficient at degree at least e in every physical layout. This is a bound on literal coefficient order, with no lower bound on symbolic recovery or runtime.
