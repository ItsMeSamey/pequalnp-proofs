# Simultaneous precision obstruction for tapered Hermite recovery

Consider the root schedule of the pinned October quantitative contraction manuscript. Write L=2^a with a=2u, integer node set S, alpha=2^(a+1), M real edges, and active shells j=v2(t)<u. Unlike a test with independent noise in individual Taylor coefficients, the following pair of inputs consists of two globally consistent polynomials. It concerns the scalar recovery interface; physical realizability as an original CNF contraction is not established.

For sufficiently large native root sizes let b=ceil(log2(4u)) and H=floor(ML/(4u·2^(u+b))). Then u+b≤a+1 and H is asymptotic to the unrounded expression. Put E=H2^(u+b), m_j=E(u−j)/2^j for 0≤j<u. At each shell j use the 2^(b−1) roots s=2^j v for all odd v in [1,2^b). Give each root multiplicity m_j/2^(b−1), an integer. Define F by multiplying ((T−s)/(−s)) to these multiplicities. Thus F(0)=1, while the comparison polynomial is zero. The degree is at most 2Eu≤ML/2. The common denominator has 2-adic valuation at most ∑ j m_j≤2Eu≤ML/2; odd factors are units in the localized coefficient field. If integer coefficients after a common power-of-two denominator are required, multiply F by the odd part of the product of all denominators, leaving its value at zero odd and its degree and the estimates unchanged. The bit description including that multiplier is polynomial in M,L.

For an active node t of shell j, take coefficientwise 2-adic valuation of F(t+alpha z), bounded below multiplicatively. All factors from shells below j are integral units at constant order with integral remaining coefficients. A root in shell j+k, k>0, costs at most k valuation bits per occurrence. Within shell j, each factor yields at least one bit; for each l from 2 through b, precisely a fraction 2^(1−l) of the roots yield at least l bits. This counts congruence to the odd part of t modulo 2^l, and j+b≤a+1 ensures the z coefficient also meets the claimed valuation. Therefore the gain is at least m_j(2−2^(1−b)). On dividing the corresponding loss by E/2^j we obtain ∑_{k≥1} k2^(−k)(u−j−k)_+, bounded by 2(u−j)−2 since ∑ k2^(−k)=2 and min(k,u−j)≥1. The gain divided by E/2^j is at least 2(u−j)−1/2. Every coefficient is thus divisible by 2^ceil(3E/(2·2^j)). At inactive nodes all denominators are canceled coefficientwise because all root valuations are lower; at t=0 this also holds. Enlarging alpha by any nonnegative power of two leaves all bounds valid.

Consequently a common precision for each complete jet of o(ML/(u2^j)), uniformly in active nodes, cannot recover even origin parity. In fact at least one node must receive precision exceeding its exhibited threshold if all answers consist of those residue jets. The degree bound, integral samples, and their common polynomial consistency are all available to the decoder. Even exact degree and exact minimum two-power denominator can be disclosed by comparing T·F with (T+1)·F: both have degree deg(F)+1 within the slack and the same denominator valuation by primitivity of the monic multipliers modulo two. Both have the same vanishing residue samples and their origin parities differ. The argument also applies if the decoder reads every Taylor coefficient at the requested modulus. It does not require coefficient independence or a particular Hermite formula. It does not bound variable precision by coefficient, support-sensitive encodings, or decoding restricted to polynomials with additional physical tensor properties. Physical nonzero terms, actual SAT time, and lower bounds for alternative node sets are outside its scope.
