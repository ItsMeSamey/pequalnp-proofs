# An ordinary-precision circle sampling interface for exact Boolean counts

Using physical-hilbert-height.md (9fe7c6ba), put H=exp(C_5 M) as a known effective bound for |F(T)| on |T−1|≤4. The operator bound there is constructive: bound the finite near-kernel terms using the displayed products, the far errors with the sum of 1/d², and the signed Hilbert multiplier by π. No tensor coefficient query is concealed in H.

Let N be the least multiple of four at least 2+ceil(log₂(32H)). Take ζ_j the N-th roots of unity and samples f_j=F(1+2ζ_j). For c_k=[z^k]F(1+z), recover the approximate origin by summing, for k=0,...,N−1, the discrete Fourier estimate for c_k multiplied by (−1)^k. Equivalently the weight of f_j is N^{-1} sum_{k<N}(−1/(2ζ_j))^k. The weights' absolute sum is ≤2. The estimate with exact samples differs from F(0) by less than 1/8: Cauchy's estimate gives |c_l|≤H4^{−l}; the neglected true terms contribute ≤(4/3)H4^{−N}; the aliased terms contribute at most sum_{k<N} H4^{−k} sum_{d≥1}2^{−dN} ≤(4/3)H/(2^N−1). This proves that ordinary absolute error ≤1/16 in each f_j suffices for exact integer recovery by rounding the real part. N=O(M+1); no full-denominator rational reconstruction is required.

All original Boolean terms must still be priced. The ordinary Cauchy row kernel can be formed to absolute error η=2^{−O(M)} / poly(M,L) per entry at each point: on a disk of radius 6 the kernel and derivative are bounded by a constant via removable-factor formula and the Cauchy estimate. Use that formula for small differences and truncate each product with extra O(log L) guard bits. Each product ∏(1−T²/k²) has absolute partial products bounded by exp(36 sum 1/k²), so interval errors accumulate at most polynomially in L. Finite precision roots of unity and weights with this accuracy cost polynomial time in M,L (e.g. power series with certified remainders). Perturbing all real entries by η changes WX by at most Mη per row. A telescoping expansion over the M factors, together with ∥WX∥₂≤C sqrt(M), bounds each term's absolute perturbation by M²η·exp(O(M)); this also holds with one or more rounded row factors because the coordinate errors are ≤Mη. Summing over ≤2^(2M) terms still costs only O(M+log L) mantissa bits plus guard bits for basic operations and summation. A direct implementation is exponential time and polynomial workspace. One may group by X and sum Y at each local Boolean row vertex independently, leaving only 2^(M/2) free subdivision COPY2 words in the original orientation; the expanded-sum bound is still a safe error bound after charging polynomially many rounded products per column assignment. This gives an alternative charged real-oracle interface. The October 2-adic crossing-valuation pruning does not supply these complex samples: its inversions and truncations have different error semantics. No smaller general SAT exponent follows without an independently justified way to acquire the circle samples.
