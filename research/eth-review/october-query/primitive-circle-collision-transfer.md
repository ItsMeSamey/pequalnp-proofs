# One primitive circle sample preserves origin count distinctions

For exact Cauchy-circle identities choose an odd prime q>max(3,2L), a primitive q-th root zeta, and T*=1+2 zeta. Include it in the circle algorithm by taking a grid divisible by 4q; q=Theta(M) with a sufficiently large accuracy constant retains O(M) nodes. This choice need not coincide with the earlier least-multiple-of-four grid.

Let C0=|C'(0)|=L!(L−1)!. If P has integer coefficients after clearing a power of C0 and P(0) is integral, then P(T*)=0 implies B divides P(0), B=(2^q+1)/3. The monic integer polynomial R(T)=sum_{j=0}^{q−1}2^(q−1−j)(T−1)^j is the minimal polynomial of T* by prime cyclotomic irreducibility (shift and Eisenstein). Its constant is B. Hence R divides the cleared P over the integers by monic division. B is odd and B mod 3 equals q mod 3. For any prime p dividing B, the nontrivial residue −2 has exact order q, so p≥q+1>L. Thus gcd(B,C0)=1.

Every partial marginal sum of actual physical COPY words has this denominator property and its origin value is an integer between 0 and 2^e, e=M/2. At this single exact sample equality of per-word scalars implies equality of their Boolean origin bits. For q>e+3 equality of any two partial marginal values implies equality of their origin counts. A full algebraic scalar in the polynomial residue representation at T* can likewise be evaluated at T=0 modulo B, inverting C0, to recover the exact count when B>2^e. This requires acquiring q−1 coordinates with all coefficient precision and bit work charged.

If a flattening of a table of these partial marginals has rank <s at T*, every s-square minor satisfies this divisibility test. Its integer origin magnitude is ≤s^(s/2)2^(es) by Hadamard. Choosing q>es+(s/2)log2(s)+3 forces all those minors to vanish at the origin. Thus exact scalar collisions or low-rank conditions demanded at all sampled nodes must survive this concrete original-input test. For fixed s and L≤M one can choose q in a factor-two range retaining O(M) sample nodes; growing s requires its larger grid to be charged. This proves neither algorithmic dominance for arbitrary adaptive schedules nor a similar restriction on approximate low rank at constant absolute error. It identifies a genuinely distinct remaining question: can approximate, rather than exact, marginal relations be exploited while maintaining the absolute precision needed for the final integer?
