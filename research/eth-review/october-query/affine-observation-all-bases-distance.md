# Linear scope in every affine chart of a signed CNF

For infinitely many dimensions r there are polynomial-time constructible signed width-three CNFs with m≤8r residual ternaries after parity extraction and the joint Gaussian falsity test. These clauses observe m code coordinates of total rank r. Under every invertible affine change of the r independent bits, some clause has at least r/16 essential bits and at least m/31 clauses have at least r/32 essential bits. This includes every tree incidence substitution and every supplied observable basis. The bounds apply to direct truth-table enumeration of individual residual clauses.

**A short binary code.** Take even h≥4, Q=2^h, and k=Q/2. Evaluate all polynomials over GF(Q) of degree less than k at all Q field elements. Use the r=hk binary coefficient bits as seeds. Evaluation at any three distinct field elements is surjective onto GF(Q)^3 by quadratic interpolation. Every nonzero polynomial has at most k−1 roots.

Use the same binary linear inner encoder on each field symbol, with dimension h, length at most 4h, and minimum nonzero weight at least h/2. To build it, start in length 4h with the zero-dimensional subspace. At each of h steps adjoin the first word of distance at least h/2 from the current span. At most 2^h times the binary ball volume of radius h/2−1 is excluded. This is bounded by 2^(h+4h H₂(1/8))<2^(4h), where H₂ is binary entropy. All new nonzero span words retain the distance bound. Scan at most Q⁴ candidates against at most Q existing words per step. Drop identically zero output positions and write ℓ for the number retained. Each inner coordinate is a nonzero functional on the field symbol. Build the field in time polynomial in Q by testing monic degree-h candidates for factors through degree h/2. The concatenated binary code has length m=ℓQ≤4hQ=8r, dimension r, and minimum nonzero weight at least (Q/2+1)h/2≥m/16. The concatenation construction is classical. [Guruswami's coding theory notes](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~venkatg/teaching/codingtheory/notes/notes7.pdf)

**An ordinary signed input.** Write c(i,t) for inner position t at evaluation point i, numbering the Q points in cyclic order. Compute each nonzero output linear form on the r seeds. Introduce a fresh raw bit for that output, using a chain of binary XORs on the distinct seeds in its support with private internal bits. Encode every XOR equation by its four wrong-parity forbidden words as signed ternaries. For singleton support use the two binary clauses for equality. No output c is an input of another definition. Every auxiliary value is uniquely determined by the seeds.

Add one signed ternary per (i,t) on c(i,t), c(i+1,t), c(i+2,t), with i modulo Q. Their three binary functionals are jointly surjective by interpolation at distinct field points and nonzero inner coordinate t. Signs may be arbitrary. A choice with at least one negative literal per ternary admits the zero seed word as a model. Each residual scope occurs once and contains only output variables. Every definition scope contains a seed or private chain variable; the private outputs distinguish definition scopes. The signed parity-bundle prepass therefore recovers the definitions and preserves these m residual rows. Each ternary has a simultaneous falsifying assignment, so full Gaussian normalization keeps it. All code coordinates occur and their rank is r. The raw formula has O(mr) variables and clauses and polynomial bit length.

**The basis-independent width bound.** Take the generator matrix with r rows and m observation columns. The linear part of every invertible affine chart gives another row basis of the same binary code. Each of its r rows has weight at least m/16. The total column weight is at least rm/16, so some column has weight at least r/16. At least m/31 columns have weight at least r/32, as no weight exceeds r. Affine offsets and signs preserve supports.

For each such column take the distinct ternary in which its output is the first specified coordinate. Every chart bit that touches any of the three literal normals is essential to this clause. At a simultaneous falsifying word, flipping the bit changes the all-false literal triple to a nonzero triple. This gives the claimed scopes. If a clause has w essential bits, its three normals restricted to those bits still have rank three. Exactly 2^(w−3) of its 2^w local words falsify it. At least 2^(ceil(r/16)−3) distinct forbidden-word rows of one clause survive plain duplicate removal when the tables are emitted. A solver with a smaller budget may reject the chart before emitting these rows. The bound leaves algorithms using clause rewriting, auxiliary coordinates, branching, or other ways of computing the scalar open.
