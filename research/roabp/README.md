# The occurrence-weight polynomial can require exponential ROABP width in every order

Let G be a simple constant-degree edge expander on n vertices, fix j, and attach a fresh leaf w only to j. For the adjacency matrix A of this graph take one clause per vertex i with alternatives xᵢ=0 and (Ax)ᵢ=0. Add the terminal sum of all xᵢ equal to one. The n+1 default coordinate normals are independent. Every proper subfamily containing the terminal omits some vertex clause, whose coordinate makes the terminal normal independent of all retained defaults. Thus the entire formula is the unique positively deficient clause subfamily and its deficiency is one. All guards have two nonconstant literals on a connected expander with n>1. The graph matrix is symmetric and zero-diagonal.

Fix the terminal weight to one, pick j's default and w's alternative by unit weights, and set the other default weights to one and the respective alternative weights to independent variables λᵢ. The coefficient for a subset S of the remaining vertices in the witness polynomial is the parity of common Boolean solutions. The equations fix xⱼ=0 twice, set every remaining coordinate outside S other than w to zero, and impose A[S]x_S=0. The terminal determines w uniquely. Hence the coefficient is det A[S] over GF(2). Expanding multilinearly in the rows gives the specialized polynomial det(I+diag(λ)A[G−j]).

Fix any order of the remaining variables and cut it into halves. The midpoint cut has Ω(n) crossing edges because removing j deletes at most a constant number from a balanced expander cut. Greedily choose an induced matching M among crossing edges. Each chosen edge discards at most O(Δ²) crossing edges sharing an endpoint with, or adjacent to, an endpoint of the chosen edge. Thus |M|=Ω(n). Set all variables except endpoints of M to zero. The specialized polynomial becomes the product over uv in M of (1+λᵤλᵥ). Its coefficient matrix between the two sides contains an identity matrix of size 2^|M|, indexed by subsets of matched edges. Any width-D read-once oblivious algebraic branching program (ROABP) across a cut factors as a sum of D prefix/suffix products, whose coefficient matrix has rank at most D. Setting constants cannot increase width or change the relative order of surviving variables. Consequently even the original polynomial needs exponential ROABP width for every occurrence-variable order.

This obstructs identity tests that assume polynomial ROABP width. The polynomials can still have small determinant circuits, and these SAT instances have trivial singleton witnesses. It does not lower-bound general circuit size, SAT runtime, hitting sets for other representations, or all kinds of branching programs.
