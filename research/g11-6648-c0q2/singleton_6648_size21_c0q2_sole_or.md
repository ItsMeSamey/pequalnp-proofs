# Size 21, c=0, q=2 and the sole OR reader of vertex 3

Under the official 6648 hypotheses, assume least size 21, no centreless clean tops, exactly two charged ORs (outside BelowF and outside Lambda), and raw vertex x=3 with sole direct reader M=x OR w. Use the existing size-21 private-donor exclusion in 6648-size21-private-x3-donor.md to know that w has another reader besides M. The following argument applies to all junk counts j; no four-port assumption and no finite search is used.

**Donor safety.** The wire w is independent of x because the only reader of x is M and w precedes it. It has no positive singleton: if w were true at b then adding x to any assignment containing b would leave all values after M unchanged. Thus b could not neighbor x, and b would have to neighbor both 6 and 7 (test {b,z} versus {b,z,x} for z in N(x)); C4 forbids this. More generally, for any independent positive assignment of w, strip x. The remainder I is still independent and positive and cannot contain 6 or 7, since adding x would change the graph output. Each of 6 and 7 must have a neighbor in I: otherwise add that undominated neighbor z to I to obtain an independent positive set, and then add x to create an edge without changing the output. The concrete neighborhoods N(6)={3,7,10} and N(7)={3,6,9} imply 9,10 both belong to every such I. In particular w is clean relative to graph inputs restricted by 9=false and also separately by 10=false.

**Identifying both charged ORs.** A least circuit has no dead wires. M is dirty on the singleton x; some downstream path must meet an AND, since an OR-only path to the output would preserve this dirty witness. TopForm excludes a clean AND anywhere upstream of M, whether directly or behind ORs. If w were clean, it could not be a primary input or an AND (singleton negativity and TopForm). If w is an OR, singleton negativity means it has empty OR-reachable raw support. There must be a last AND below it on some raw-to-w path; all such ANDs are dirty by the preceding TopForm argument. Hence w is dirty (one such dirty signal propagates along ORs) and the OR w has raw-empty children, so is charged. M is also charged because its pin w has empty raw support and M is dirty. Take the other live reader of w. An OR reader would be dirty on w's independent positive input, and charged on account of w's raw-empty pin; neither w itself nor M qualifies as this distinct reader. An AND reader is centreless if clean. Otherwise follow a live path downstream: up to the first OR or clean AND, all intermediate gates are dirty ANDs. A clean AND reached from a dirty AND is centreless; the first OR is charged since it reads a dirty AND. Neither w nor M can be this OR: w is earlier and M's only inputs are w and x. A path consisting exclusively of dirty ANDs cannot end at the clean output. The same holds if the initial reader is already the output, since a dirty output cannot compute the graph. This contradiction excludes the OR donor. Thus w=D is a dirty AND, and M is one of the two charged ORs, with raw-empty pin D.

Take an external reader of D. An OR reader different from M is immediately the second charged OR Q. From an AND reader, the preceding live-path argument shows a first OR must occur before any clean AND (c=0). It cannot be M: the path has at least one AND after D, while the only inputs of M are D and raw x. That first OR is the second charged OR Q. In either case Q directly reads a dirty AND Z reached from D along a path of ANDs only, allowing Z=D. Thus Z implies D on the entire cube. Q is certainly dirty: Z has an independent positive input, and OR is true whenever Z is. In particular Q cannot be the clean graph output, even if its two pins are both dirty ANDs with disjoint independent witnesses.

Q cannot have empty raw support. A further OR reading raw-empty Q would be dirty on the same witness and outside Lambda because one pin has no raw support; it cannot be M, whose two fixed pins are x,D and whose AND pin D is upstream of Q. A clean AND reader of Q would be centreless. A dirty AND reader would propagate along AND gates to one of these same impossibilities (a clean AND or another charged OR), or produce a dirty circuit output. This also rules out Q with a second dirty AND pin. The second pin of Q has nonempty raw support. If it has an OR-only path from M, it dominates D, so Q=Z OR second equals its second pin, contradicting leastness. Otherwise its value is a pure raw disjunction R: any path from an AND to its OR-only region has a last AND, necessarily dirty by TopForm because dirty Q must eventually meet an AND; the first OR after that AND would be charged. Only M or Q are charged, Q is later than its own input and M was excluded. Thus Q=Z OR R. Both charged ORs are downstream of D, and Z implies D.

**Confining residuals to D.** On a pair with D=false, M equals x and Q equals its raw disjunction R. An OR boundary of any top then evaluates to its raw-support disjunction: every OR-only path from a dirty AND into that boundary passes through M or Q and contributes no true nonraw AND signal. No clean AND can lie below another AND by TopForm. There is no raw-empty operand at a top since c=0. Any positive output on such a pair activates a top (the clean OR aggregation cannot have a raw input below it, by singleton rejection), and the top's two raw supports hit the pair. The supports are disjoint by singleton rejection, so this is a star edge in the original circuit. Therefore the entire Ustar lies among the actual graph edges where D is true.

**Pure conjunction bound on each restriction.** Both charged ORs lie downstream of D. Its AND-only expansion therefore has pure raw primitive factors: no clean AND ancestor is possible, and the first OR on any path out of a dirty AND would itself be charged. For d distinct dirty AND ancestors of D including D, binary connectivity bounds the number of distinct primitive inputs by d+1. A nontrivial pure OR below D is true at a singleton, hence is outside BelowF; since it is not either of the two charged ORs, it is in Lambda. Its raw support has an ordinary-star anchor joined by star edges to every support vertex. Singleton primitive supports need no anchor.

Fix deletion 9 or 10, so D is clean on the restricted graph. Its accepted residuals form an induced matching there. If two share endpoint a, a primitive missing a (existence by singleton rejection) must contain both mates; the primitive anchor shares those two neighbors with a and must equal a, star-covering both. For disjoint accepted residuals ab and cd with cross ac, no primitive can contain b and c because its anchor and a would share b,c, forcing ab to be starred. Likewise none can contain a and d. Each primitive hits both edges and thus its trace on the four endpoints is exactly ac or bd. The pairs ad and bc then both satisfy D and must be graph edges by restricted cleanliness. They and the original matching give a four-cycle. Hence there is no cross. Every endpoint orientation selecting one of r accepted residuals' endpoints per edge is independent in the restricted graph and falsifies D. Every clause hits each accepted residual and so can reject at most one orientation. For r>0, this proves 2^r<=d+1. The empty case satisfies the logarithmic bound too.

Since 9 and 10 are nonadjacent, each graph edge survives at least one deletion. Therefore U<=2 floor(log2(d+1))<=d+2<=j+2. The middle inequality follows from 2k<=2^k for integers k>=1. Issued balance |X|+j=U+|J| gives |X|<=|J|+2, the required conclusion at c=0,q=2. The reliance on the upstream private-donor exclusion is explicit; this note is an analytical argument and has not been compiled in Lean.
