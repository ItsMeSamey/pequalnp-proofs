# Pure neighbor disjunction at a clean sole AND, c=0, q=2

Let x=3, y=6, z=7. In a least size-21 TopForm G11 circuit let x have sole reader A=x∧S where S is the physical pure OR y∨z. Assume c=0 and q=2. The exact 19-gate x=false card deletes A and its unique OR reader T; it preserves every other wire. By the common-reader classification in 6648-size21-sole-and.md, S on this card has just one reader, an OR B=S∨W. This B also exists in the original: T cannot supply its other pin, since T has a clean AND ancestor A while B, positive on singletons, has a later AND. S is thus used by A and by B in the original.

B and every OR-only descendant of B have both y,z in raw support, so cannot OR-reach any ordinary top operand: its opposite raw side would have to contain a vertex adjacent to both, necessarily x, which has only the top A, and B is downstream of A's operand S. Centreless tops are absent. Consequently B is a paid OR, and every path from B must traverse a dirty AND. That AND cannot reach a clean AND through ANDs alone (the first clean AND would have an empty raw side), so must reach another paid OR Q reading a dirty AND. No OR-only nonempty path from B to Q is possible: every intermediate OR is already paid by the same y,z reasoning, and if Q itself has an OR-only B path its raw support contains y,z, forcing yet another dirty AND followed by another paid OR. Thus every path from B traverses dirty ANDs then the same Q, and every direct reader of B is a dirty AND.

Write Q=Z∨R, where Z is reached from B by ANDs alone and implies B. A raw-empty Q could only exit through another paid dirty OR or ultimately a centreless top, so its other pin R has nonempty raw support. R is pure: an OR-only path from B was excluded, and there is no other earlier OR that can read a dirty AND. The same reasoning shows every affected top has one Q-containing side and a pure raw opposite side. Both sides containing Q would accept its independent witness (or any singleton in R), and affected paths cannot cross a dirty AND after Q because no further paid OR remains. OR-only enlargements of the affected side are allowed.

On the 19-card replace B by z∨W, deleting its now unused input S. This 18-gate decreased circuit must lose an edge. The only possible loss is y–10: every other card edge lacking y has the same B value, and yz still sets B. The symmetric replacement by y∨W must lose z–9. In particular both original pairs must activate Q via Z and must fail if that B signal is removed. R contains none of y,z,9,10. For each pair take a top whose acceptance is lost. Its opposite pure raw guard contains an endpoint of the corresponding pair, adjacent to every member of R. The unique common neighbors for the four guard-endpoint choices (y,z), (y,9), (10,z), (10,9) are respectively x,z,y,0. Vertex x has no possible raw path to R and y,z are excluded by indispensability. Hence R is raw 0, and the two opposite guards contain 10 and 9 respectively.

At the nonedge y–9, the guard containing 9 fires. Consequently Z rejects this pair. Expand Z through its AND ancestors into its OR/raw primitive factors. B is true on y–9. Every primitive other than B is raw or an anchored pure OR: an OR-only B descendant before Z would have been another paid OR, and the only possible earlier dirty-AND exit is B. A factor rejecting y–9 must hit each of y–10 and z–9 since Z accepts both. It therefore contains 10 and z. Its unique common neighbor y anchors the primitive, giving a physical ordinary star on y–10. Both of this star's necessary raw paths avoid B, which cannot OR-reach any top. Its anchor cannot be the deleted top A. Thus y–10 remains true after the card replacement by z∨W, a contradiction.
