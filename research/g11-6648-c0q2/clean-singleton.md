# Clean sole-AND reader with one raw vertex on its opposite side

Fix the issued G11 and its size-21 least top-form AO2 hypothesis. Assume c=0, q=2, j≥3. Let x=3 have sole reader A=x∧S, clean. Assume the OR-reachable raw vertices of S consist of just y, one of 6,7, and write z for the other. Then |Ustar|≤5≤j+2. The issued balance of the selected edge and junk-gate counts now gives its required conclusion. No disjointness of any specific junk fiber is required.

**Interface.** S is independent of x and accepts singleton y and singleton z: toggling x makes each singleton positive, and all influence of x passes through A. The sole-AND deletion fact gives one OR reader of A; deleting x collapses these two gates and gives a card requiring at least 19 gates. S must have another live use, since otherwise its deletion would remove a third gate. Every ordinary star involving raw x must be A's, since no other gate reads raw x and OR reach stops at AND. Thus xy is starred, and xz is residual.

Any top whose operand has S as an OR disjunct and whose opposite raw side is nonempty has all vertices on that side adjacent to both y and z by cleanliness tested on singletons/pairs. The unique such common neighbor is x. Thus the only possible such top is A; on a path departing S by another gate one cannot reach A this way since the two pins of A are x,S. Every gate is live. A pure OR continuation to the output would wrongly accept a singleton. These observations also apply after ORs which keep S as a disjunct.

**Two charged ORs.** There is a charged OR within the OR cone of S: follow a singleton-z true path through ORs in S. It cannot reach raw z, hence an OR directly reads a true AND boundary. That AND is dirty by TopForm, so the OR is dirty and has an empty-raw pin, excluding it from Lambda. On an external path from S the first AND is dirty by the preceding opposite-side argument and c=0. After a dirty AND follow ANDs up to the first OR: no clean AND can intervene because it would be centreless, and a dirty gate cannot end at the graph output. The first OR Q2 is charged, reading a dirty AND with empty raw reach. An OR preceding the first external AND would also be dirty and could not be Lambda: its witness would give an ordinary top on the forbidden OR-only continuation from S. That would be a third charge. Hence the external path starts with an AND, and Q2 directly reads an AND Z≤S reached by AND steps only.

There is now one charge available within the S cone, call it M. It must have nonempty raw reach, since an empty-raw M would require another dirty, empty-pin OR to merge with the raw path from y. Every OR-only path bringing an AND boundary into S passes through M. The nonempty pure operand at M can only compute raw y. The empty operand must be a dirty AND D, since a prior OR with empty raw reach would already require a distinct charged OR. Any further OR above M within S would only combine y or a copy of M with M, hence would be redundant in a least circuit. Thus S=D∨y, and D accepts singleton z. All primitive AND-cone factors of D are pure raw disjunctions: an OR reading a dirty AND before D would have consumed an earlier charge. Every nonsingleton such clause has an ordinary-star anchor by Lambda. Any raw singleton factor would have to be z; this would make D=z by monotonicity and render its gate redundant.

Q2 cannot have empty raw reach: its first OR reader after only ANDs, or its immediate OR reader, would be charged again, while c=0 excludes an intervening clean AND on an empty-raw path. On any edge pair where D and raw y are both false, S=Z=false. Every OR-only raw-reaching expression upstream of Q2 has only raw leaves or S as an extra source, because all other direct AND-to-OR edges would cost another charge. Its other pin therefore evaluates as its raw projection on that pair. After Q2 there is no charged OR left to introduce another AND-to-OR boundary below a top. This argument only concerns below-top cones, where TopForm excludes clean ANDs. Thus every top operand evaluates on this pair as its raw disjunction; an accepting top gives a star edge. We have proved Ustar ⊆ gammaE(D) ∪ {edges incident to y}. This formulation does not require Q2's nonempty operand to be globally pure.

**G11-specific count.** Every pure factor of D contains z. Its anchor lies in N(z), and cannot be x, as that would star-cover xz. No factor of D can contain raw x, whose sole reader is A. For (y,z)=(6,7), the possible anchors are y and 9. A y-anchored factor is exactly {z,10}; a 9-anchored one is contained in {z,0,5,8}. The latter stars z9, the former stars yz. If both types occur, any edge avoiding z on which D holds must meet both {10} and {0,5,8}; only (0,10) does. If only y anchors occur D repeats a single clause, impossible in a least circuit. With only 9 anchors D depends just on {z,0,5,8}. Cleanliness of x∧S makes every singleton in {0,5,8} reject D, since none neighbors x. Thus an accepted edge avoiding z must lie fully in {0,5,8}, whose only edge is (5,8). In both cases at most three residual edges belong to gammaE(D), including at most xz and yz among edges incident to z.

For (y,z)=(7,6) the anchors are y and 10. A y-anchored factor is {z,9}; a 10-anchored factor is contained in {z,0,2,4}. The latter three vertices are independent and do not neighbor x. If all anchors were 10, adjoining x to the set {0,2,4} would force D to reject it, making D=z and its gate redundant. All y anchors are again redundant. With both types, z–10 and zy are starred; the only accepted edge avoiding z is (0,9). Thus in this orientation at most two residuals belong to gammaE(D).

Finally deg(y)=3 and xy is starred; at most two residuals are incident to y. Even counting overlaps twice therefore gives |Ustar|≤5 in the first orientation and ≤4 in the second.

**Scope.** This source-level argument excludes this slice of L-meta-6648, not the pure two-neighbor S case or other values of c,q. It uses the checked 19-gate deletion lower bound and the sharp sole-reader interface, not a gate enumeration or a Lean verification of this note.
