# Flat star covers of four-cycle-free graphs

Let a nonempty simple graph have edge function equal to an OR of `r` binary AND terms. Assume each operand of each term is an OR of raw vertices, realized by a shared binary OR circuit. Allow any binary OR circuit to collect the terms. Prune unused gates, contract ORs that only reproduce a singleton, and identify redundant output paths. Count a gate once regardless of its number of uses.

Each term has disjoint operand supports because the function vanishes on singletons. Every pair in the cross product of its operand supports is an edge, by testing the two-vertex assignment. Four-cycle freeness forces a singleton side. Choose its vertex as the center, choosing either endpoint for a singleton-by-singleton term. Every graph edge is exposed by at least one term because that edge's two-vertex assignment makes the output true. The distinct centers therefore form a vertex cover.

For a center `c`, let `r_c` count its terms and `d_c` count distinct neighbors in their leaf operands. All OR gates feeding these operands have support in the neighborhood of `c`. Any surviving gate has support of size at least two, so it cannot also feed leaf operands of a different center. Two such centers and two common neighbors would form a four-cycle.

The union of the leaf cones for `c` connects `d_c` raw sources to at most `r_c` output ports. Every weak component contains an output. With `k_c` OR gates, there are at most `2 k_c` undirected edges on `d_c + k_c` vertices. Thus `k_c ≥ d_c − r_c`, including when some ports directly tap raw inputs. The gate sets for different centers are disjoint. The output collector likewise needs at least `r−1` ORs to connect `r` distinct active term occurrences to one output. Duplicate occurrences can first be removed. A collector gate cannot also be a live raw-OR preprocessing gate because every term is false on singleton assignments, whereas every nonempty raw OR is true on a singleton.

Adding preprocessing, term and collection costs gives at least `Σ d_c + r − 1 ≥ |E| + τ − 1` gates. The last inequality uses edge coverage and the vertex-cover number `τ`. A minimum vertex cover attains equality by assigning every edge to one incident cover vertex, making one star per center, and ORing those stars. Consequently a circuit strictly below this threshold requires an AND output to feed a later AND through a directed path. The result applies to the flat family and leaves nested AND circuits open.
