# Read-once obstruction for a monotone graph-cut encoder

Suppose a part L of size d induces exactly one edge xy, and let B=L minus y. Each w in B has a right neighbor private among B. Write N(w) for the neighbors of w on the right. Assume N(y) is nonempty, N(w) is not contained in N(y) for any w in B, and x and y have a common right neighbor. Then a read-once monotone AND/OR decoder cannot compute the graph edge function from d monotone left ports, even with arbitrary right ports.

With y absent, private singleton probes distinguish all subsets of B. Their communication rows increase strictly along each maximal chain. Adding y to the maximum produces the constant-one row, a further strict increase. A monotone d-bit left code must increase strictly on these d steps. Every set on the y-absent face thus receives a code of its own weight; the singleton codes are distinct units. Containment and weight force the code on this face to be the literal indicators of the vertices in B.

The code for singleton y is nonzero. It cannot contain a unit of w in B: a probe in N(w) outside N(y) would then contradict monotonicity. Thus it is precisely the spare unit. Probe a common neighbor of x and y. With all other left ports zero, the empty, x-only and y-only codes force outputs zero, one and one. Next use the empty right input. Both the maximal y-absent and maximal x-absent codes give zero; the full code gives one. The x-absent code contains every singleton unit except possibly x, and cannot include x, since full L accepts the empty probe. Thus these three codes fix all other ports to one and force AND on the same two ports. The missing corner of each square is forced by monotonicity. The fixed lowest common ancestor of x and y in a read-once tree cannot compute both connectives. Fewer than d ports are already excluded by the strict chain.

On the issued eleven-vertex graph take L={0,1,2,6,7}, x=6, y=7. Private probes for vertices 0,1,2,6 in B are 9,4,5,3. Probes outside N(7) are 10,4,5,10 respectively. Vertex 3 is a common neighbor of 6 and 7. The full cut matrix has 19 distinct rows and 17 distinct columns. Nine mixed gates would force exactly five ports per side and a read-once decoder by incidence counting. The graph therefore requires at least ten mixed gates across this cut, including circuits outside leastness or TopForm assumptions.
