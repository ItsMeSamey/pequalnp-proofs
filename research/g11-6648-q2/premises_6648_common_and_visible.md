# G11 common AND h=yz with a visible raw neighbor at the clean sole x-AND

Assume the genuine least size-21 TopForm hypotheses, q=2, x=3, y=6, z=7, sole clean A=x∧S, and the common AND h=y∧z in the tight-card reader classification. The only original direct readers of y are h and Ry=y∨V; those of z are h and Rz=z∨V'. This transports from the 19-card because neither deleted A,T nor the bypassed K is a raw y/z wire or introduces a raw y/z pin, as detailed in premises_6648_common_or_all_c.md. No assertion is made about all direct OR readers of h itself in the original; in particular one can be the deleted clean aggregation T. The edge yz is starred by h independently of that fanout.

The true y–10 and false z–10 inputs have h=false, with Ry=1 on the first and Rz=1 on the second. Therefore Ry must be false on the second: otherwise all three channel values h,Ry,Rz on the second dominate those on the first. Cutting all three channel outputs, the remaining output is monotone in these signals and in raw inputs other than y,z (which agree); this cut argument is valid also when one channel has a path to another. Contradiction. Similarly Rz is false on y–9. Thus Ry(z singleton)=false and Rz(y singleton)=false. Leastness rules out V(y singleton)=true or V'(z singleton)=true, since that would make one raw reader redundant.

If y belongs to raw(S), its raw path must enter through Ry because h is an AND. Consequently V's raw leaves lie in raw(S)⊆{y,z}, by cleanliness of A and x's neighborhood. But V rejects both singletons. Therefore its raw support is empty, Ry is paid, and Ry lies in S's OR closure. The symmetric assertion holds for z.

If both neighbors are raw, both individual readers are the only paid ORs. Each empty sibling must be a direct dirty AND: any earlier raw-empty OR here would itself be a third paid OR; no clean AND can be upstream of A. Denote the donors by Dy,Dz; they may coincide. Both lie under S via OR-only paths and hence are dominated by S, so they are clean on the graph slice y=z=false. Simultaneously project all dirty-AND-to-OR pins to zero. All surviving primitive conjunction factors are singleton or lambda-anchored raw disjunctions; each projected donor remains clean on that slice. Every originally donor-positive edge is caught by the earliest positive donor, whose value survives the projection since earlier donor pins were zero on that input. An original residual not caught by a donor must be accepted by a projected centreless top; ordinary projection acceptance would already be a star edge. Each such centreless top accounts for at most two residuals by the anchored-conjunction induced-matching argument on G11 (three would all lie in a single ordinary star neighborhood of size at least six, exceeding the maximum degree). This argument uses the original U exactly. Among edges meeting y or z, xy,xz,yz are already starred, leaving at most the two spokes y–10,z–9. On the exterior slice each projected donor accounts for at most two original residual edges, by the same anchored-clause argument. Thus U≤2c+6, which suffices at j≥4. At j=3, if the donors coincide they account jointly for at most two exterior residuals. If distinct, the earlier donor has at most two dirty AND gates in its backward AND-only projected cone, including itself, since the later donor is third or earlier. It has at most three primitive clauses, and two accepted disjoint residuals would require four by the independent-transversal test. Zero projected factors or a false projected donor do not enlarge this count. The combined exterior bound is at most three, yielding U≤2c+5.

If only y is raw, Ry is paid and Ry(z singleton)=false. Evaluating S on singleton z gives true. Traverse a backward positive OR-only path. Start at S, whose raw support is nonempty. Since no raw z is reached, there is a transition at a raw-positive OR P whose positive input has empty raw support. This P is paid and differs from Ry (which is false on this input). The two paid gates are now Ry,P; both have nonempty raw support, so their empty operands cannot themselves be raw-empty ORs (such an OR would be a third paid gate). Both are direct dirty ANDs, each under S via an OR-only path, hence both clean on y=z=false. The positive donor at P retains its singleton z after simultaneous pin deletion: the only possible prior donor exit is Ry, already false on that singleton, and P cannot be its own ancestor. Thus the two projected donors obey the sufficient hypotheses of 6648-layered-clean-source.md, with one z-positive and at most one singleton-negative. Its conclusion gives U≤2c+j+2. Exchange y,z for the other single-visible case.

These deductions do not cover the common AND when S has no raw-reachable inputs. S itself may then be an AND; singleton truth at S does not by itself force a paid OR directly dominated by S.
