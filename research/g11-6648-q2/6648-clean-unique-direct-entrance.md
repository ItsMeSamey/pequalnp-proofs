# Clean sole AND and one direct dirty-AND-to-OR entrance

In the issued G11 write x=3, y=6, z=7, H=G11−{x,y,z}, and suppose x has sole clean reader A=x∧S in a least size-21 TopForm, q=2, j≥3. Assume exactly one OR P directly reads a dirty AND; write F for the other paid OR. We prove U≤2c+j+2. The already established common-y∨z exclusion for a clean sole AND at q=2 is used when S is pure. The established c=0 result may be used in one j=4 subcase below.

S accepts singletons y,z, has raw support inside {y,z}, and is clean on the H slice: S positive on an independent H input would make adjoining x accept a nonedge input at A. If S is an OR of raw inputs alone it equals y∨z, necessarily a physical common raw gate by leastness. Otherwise distinguish both visible (raw(S)={y,z}) and omitted. Under omission no anchored clause can contain both y,z, whose only common graph neighbor is x. In particular x cannot anchor a nonsingleton support containing y or z.

**Projection.** Erase every dirty AND pin of P simultaneously, retaining its other raw disjunction R, or false if both pins were ANDs. Before P, a path out of a dirty AND into an OR would already be a direct entrance, so such an R is pure. For residual pairs unaccepted by every erased donor the projection is exact at all gates. Projected ordinary tops only accept star edges. Each projected centreless top expands into a pure conjunction whose primitive clauses are singleton/anchored, except possibly the single support F°. Its two projected children are singleton/anchored if nonzero, because the sole other exception P has been bypassed. Delete false roots, redundant conjuncts and zero disjuncts. Original raw support and anchors of uncharged ORs are unchanged (even if the OR involved a P signal). Every such clean conjunction accepts ≤2 residuals, a fact valid also on H when the conjunction is only known clean there.

For that fact, if two accepted residuals share h, a clause missing h must contain both other endpoints, impossible for an anchored clause by C4. Thus h misses the exceptional clause; every other clause contains h and h neighbors its entire exceptional support. Each child contributing a residual neighbor is singleton (a nonsingleton child then has anchor h), so the two children are exactly the two neighbors. Every accepted residual hits one of them. Neither can be a degree-two pivot since pivots must miss the exceptional clause. If the residuals form a matching, a cross between two would require two exceptional clauses: for edges ab,cd and cross ac, anchored clauses hit both and cannot contain a,d or b,c by C4 and the residual marks. Their traces are ac or bd. At least one of ad,bc hits the exceptional clause and therefore is a graph edge, forcing incompatible traces for each anchored clause (one exists or the conjunction would be singleton-positive). The matching is induced. Three such edges would require all eight independent endpoint orientations to miss distinct clauses. At least seven are anchored, and the cube of orientations minus at most one vertex is connected. Adjacent anchored orientations force a shared anchor by two common neighbors, giving one anchor adjacent to six distinct vertices against maximum degree four. On H, restrict each support by deleting y,z and x; any anchor still stars its original support, and no deleted vertex can serve as an additional vertex of a marked edge. These arguments require only ambient C4-freeness and degree four.

**Classifying the other paid OR.** F can be physically pure, or it can be impure without directly reading a dirty AND. In the latter case it contains P through an OR-only path: the last AND in an impure OR expansion is dirty by TopForm, and its first OR must be P. If P is raw-empty such a path must start with F itself (a raw-empty dirty OR input forces a paid reader). Then F=P∨R' with R' pure and nonempty: an earlier impure OR feeding F would need to be P or a further paid OR immediately after P; a raw-empty pure input is impossible. This cover of shapes does not call every nondirect F physically pure.

**Raw-positive P=R∨D.** If both neighbors are visible, impure S is an OR and necessarily has P in its OR-only cone. Hence D≤S. Both x edges are starred. At most three remaining edges incident to y,z and two H residuals from D are possible, since D's upstream pure conjunction has at most one exceptional clause with two anchored/raw children. Projected roots add ≤2c.

Under omission, expand S over ANDs. Each primitive accepts both singleton neighbors. An impure primitive contains P through an OR-only path. Every pure primitive must equal the physically pure exception F, since no anchored clause contains both. If every primitive is impure, D≤S; this covers all nondirect impure F cases. If every primitive is pure, S duplicates F or physically equals it with both raw neighbors, impossible. If some primitives are pure and some impure, F∧D≤S. A D with F as an AND-expansion primitive already satisfies D≤S.

Whenever D≤S under omission, its H projection accepts at most two residuals by the one-exception fact. If D rejects both y,z singletons it misses both x edges. Otherwise D cannot accept both singletons, since every factor would have to be F and D would duplicate F. If it accepts v, at least one nonsingleton anchored v-clause occurs: a singleton v clause duplicates v, and a conjunction with only the unanchored F duplicates F. Its anchor is the triangle mate or private neighbor, starring yz or v's private edge. Together with missing x–other, at most three near edges remain. Thus D adds ≤5.

In the remaining mixed-primitive case F is physically pure, D uses solely anchored clauses, F∧D≤S, and A is centreless. D must accept at least one neighbor singleton: otherwise each P-bearing primitive accepts both via raw paths, so none is Lambda and the only possible P-bearing primitive is P itself. Its pure input R then contains both and must equal F, making F∧P=F and S duplicate F. D cannot accept both because no anchored clause contains both. Write v for its positive singleton. Drop inclusion-redundant clauses. At least two incomparable nonsingleton v-clauses remain by leastness. Each anchor is v's mate or private neighbor. If a mate anchor occurs, its clause is {v,other-private-neighbor}, since x lies on no input path to D; a private anchor must also occur. Their supports intersect only at v by C4. Hence any H edge accepted by D has both endpoints in the union of all clause supports; otherwise a positive H singleton would exist. For v=y the H support lies in {9,0,2,4} whose only edge is 09. For v=z it lies in {10,0,5,8} with possible edges 0–10 and 58, the latter rejected by the mate clause. If all clauses use the private anchor, their union lies in its four-neighbor set; incomparability bounds their common intersection to v plus at most one other vertex t. Every accepted H edge must be incident to t or internal to that neighbor set. For v=y this set is {0,2,4,6}, with no internal edges and at most two incident residual H edges at any t once t–10 is starred. For v=z it is {0,5,7,8} with just the internal 58; t=0 contributes at most 0–10 additionally, and t=5 or 8 counts 58 among at most three incident residuals. The mate-plus-private case stars yz and the v-private edge; the private-only case stars v-private and rejects the other private edge because both endpoints are outside the support. Thus the donor plus the possible two x edges accepted at A contribute at most six residuals overall. Count only the other c−1 projected centreless tops to obtain U≤2c+4.

**Raw-empty P=D1∨D2, F physically pure.** P has no OR reader and no dirty AND reached from P can have an OR reader (impossible extra direct entrance). P does not feed a clean top via a path through a clean AND, by TopForm. P and its dirty AND descendants have at most one direct AND reader each: propagate symbolic true from that node through all its AND descendants to the affected clean tops, factor it once around their output union, retaining the unaffected tops in the output OR aggregation. A projected top cannot be symbolic true (it would equal the dirty factored node); this transformation costs one AND, saves every original direct AND reader, and retains at most the original number of clean aggregation ORs. Leastness excludes two readers. Hence P affects a single centreless top. Expand that top into the two clean pure conjunctions selecting D1 or D2. Each has at most two residuals; other centreless tops have at most two and ordinary tops are pure stars. Thus U≤2c+2.

**Raw-empty P, F=P∨R' impure.** Suppression makes F pure R' and every remaining primitive is anchored/raw. If S omits a neighbor every primitive must be impure (a pure primitive would need a third paid gate), hence P≤S. At least one donor accepts a neighbor singleton: otherwise each primitive has both raw neighbors and must be F itself; equality or duplication with F contradicts omission. A donor positive on v has anchored clauses containing v. By the mate/private argument, a nontrivial conjunction stars the v-private edge, and its clean H slice can accept only 09 for v=y, or 0–10 or 58 for v=z. An occurrence of 0–10 in the latter case requires the mate clause, starring yz. Thus off-triangle edges of positive sources can be charged injectively to near stars (09 to y10, 58 to z9, 0–10 to yz). All such positive sources together use at most five near-plus-far residuals, at most four if one x edge is starred. An additional singleton-negative donor adds at most two H residuals. If A is centreless it vanishes in projection, giving 2(c−1)+5+2. Otherwise for j≥4 the bound 2c+4+2 suffices. At j=3 the two direct donors are incomparable (else P absorbs); the negative donor's pure AND expansion uses at most two dirty gates, hence ≤3 primitive clauses, insufficient for two accepted H residuals (these require four distinct orientations). This gives 2c+5. The same bound handles two positive donors directly.

With both neighbors visible, impure S is an OR containing P and both x edges are starred. Each donor is H-clean and pure anchored, hence accepts at most two H residuals; near adds ≤3. For j≥5 this suffices. At j=3 incomparability gives ≤3 primitive clauses per donor and hence ≤1 H residual each. At j=4 we need only consider four distinct H residuals, two accepted by each. For two accepted induced-matching edges ab,cd the four possible choices of one vertex in each pair must all occur as clause traces, so each pair of chosen vertices has a common anchor outside the four endpoints and both corresponding anchor edges are starred. No x,y,z has two H neighbors, so all anchors are in H. If all four choices have the same anchor it is adjacent to four H endpoints: the only possible anchor is 8 with H-neighbors 1,4,5,9, whose only induced-matching pair is 14,59. Since there are two disjointly allocated doubles, at least one double uses nonidentical anchors. Such a double forces at least seven distinct H stars: in the 2-by-2 array of choices a repeated diagonal anchor sees all four endpoints, forcing the homogeneous case by C4; repetitions along both whole rows or both columns at two different anchors create a C4; intersecting row/column repetitions likewise coincide and force homogeneity. Thus at most one adjacent repeat is possible, saving at most one of eight incidences. These seven stars plus xy,xz give U≤7, sufficient for j=4,c≥1; c=0 is already covered. This closes all unique-direct-entrance shapes.

The argument uses no gate search or signature enumeration. The corner count above is a hand bound; a prior small local Python check is incidental and is not a proof dependency.
