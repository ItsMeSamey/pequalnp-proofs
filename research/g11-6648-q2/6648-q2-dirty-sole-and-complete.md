# Preliminary theorem for the dirty sole AND branch at q=2, all c

Let x=3,y=6,z=7, g=x∧S be the dirty sole reader of x, j≥3, q=2 and the least TopForm circuit size 21. Write U for the number of original residual edges. This argument proves U≤2c+j+2 and hence the balanced conclusion. It relies on the graph-specific 19-card, 15-core, singleton/dominator, pure-clause and ordinary-guard lemmas cited by filename below.

1. The unique reader of g is M=g∨D by the 19 lower bound at x=false. D is independent of x, singleton-negative and every independent positive contains both 9 and 10 (otherwise toggle x at an independent positive extended by an undominated neighbor). See singleton_6648_size21_c0q2_sole_and_dirty.md. M has empty raw side, is dirty and is charged. No original star edge contains x: raw x goes solely into dirty AND g, so it has no OR-only route to any clean top's input. The g-positive graph edges are just xy,xz. Let Q be the unique other charged OR. All primitive nontrivial pure ORs contributing to a top or junk gate are original Lambda-anchored unless they are Q. Throughout we count original residuals, even after a projection.

2. If Q is pure, every top reached from M requires M as a conjunct and is centreless: any OR after M or a dirty AND before first clean top would be another nonpure charge. There are k≥1 such tops. All other ordinary tops give stars, other centreless tops each accept at most two residuals by the one-exceptional-pure-clause lemma (singleton_6648_q2_sole_or_all_c_cases.md). On each of two restrictions deleting 9 and 10 the same lemma bounds D-positive residuals by two, as D is a pure conjunction with at most the single exceptional primitive Q (D cannot itself be pure OR by singleton negativity). Thus U≤2(c−k)+2+4≤2c+j+2.

3. Suppose Q genuinely mixed and downstream of M. Set M=false, preserving every M-negative graph pair and decreasing the circuit elsewhere. On a path from M to the first dirty OR Q, its direct pin Z implies M, being M or a product containing M. No clean AND can have an AND descendant, and an OR-only path from a clean AND through Q to the clean output could not carry a dirty OR without a further AND. Thus at least one pin vanishes in the projection. If Q has raw-positive other pin it restores the raw disjunction; if that pin is raw-empty, it either vanishes or reduces to an AND product of pure anchored clauses. In this latter case Q originally has no raw reach and cannot feed an uncharged dirty OR, so every Q use before a first clean top goes through ANDs and the affected top is centreless. Every projected centreless top is accordingly zero or a pure anchored CNF, with ≤2 residuals, and ordinary tops contribute only original stars. D upstream of M is itself a pure anchored-clause product. Its two clean restrictions accept at most three original residuals jointly: ≤2 after deleting 9, and at most one remaining 9-edge in the matching after deleting 10. Thus U≤2c+2+3≤2c+j+2. This covers shared ancestors and both raw-empty arms of Q.

4. It remains that Q is genuinely mixed and not downstream of M. Then every top reached from M is centreless and implies M, since before first clean AND any OR reached from raw-empty M through ANDs would have to be charged and hence Q. Let k≥1 be their number. Allocate at most two singleton batches for xy,xz at g. If Q is outside D's ancestry (including incomparable to M or entering only S), D is pure and has two protected batches, one for each of the 9,10 deletions. Otherwise expand D at Q into at most two protected terms, each contributing two deletion batches (four altogether). For raw-positive Q=H∨R with pure R, these are D[Q←R] and H∧D[Q←true]; monotonicity gives pointwise equality of their union to D even under reconvergence. The true substitution drops corresponding clauses; H is a pure anchored CNF. All nonsingleton raw projections are subsets of original anchored supports or an anchored primitive of R. For raw-empty Q=H1∨H2, both arms are pure anchored products, Q is a multiplicative factor when it reaches D (no later charged OR ahead of D), and the two terms keep one arm each with unchanged guards. Also includes D=Q itself. All protected terms imply D, so are clean in each deletion. Allocate each D-positive residual to only one term and one deletion. When Q does not enter D the two singleton batches for g are still budgeted in full.

5. On edges not allocated to M, set M=false. M-affected tops need no batch. At most 2(c−k) batches are needed for remaining centreless tops, formed by distributing the single surviving exceptional OR Q into its pure raw side and dirty side, or its two dirty arms for raw-empty Q. Every such product is clean, being a subterm of a clean top. For raw-positive Q there is additionally at most one ordinary correction batch: aggregate its dirty donor H times the union of opposite ordinary raw guards. Each opposite guard is pure, since putting Q on both sides would propagate a dirty witness; any shared Q raw literal anchors the union by original stars. When Q is raw-empty there is no ordinary correction. For further details see singleton_6648_q2_upstream_complete.md. Neither the existence nor the number of external Q exits was restricted. When M=false leaves Q unchanged, no new inputs appear upstream of Q.

6. Each anchored clean batch accepts at most two original residuals. If it receives two distinct allocated edges ab,cd they are an induced matching and four complement orientations supply original star incidences at each corner ac,ad,bc,bd with anchors outside the marks. If all four anchors coincide the only possible double is 14,59 (the only such G11 neighborhood is N(8)). Thus all allocations allow at most one homogeneous double. If they are not homogeneous, at least seven different stars result; see singleton_6648_q2_upstream_complete.md. Here, in fact, U≤7 in the latter case. No original star touches x. Enumerating the four sets of possible common neighbors for each pair of disjoint G11 edges, excluding the four marks and x as anchors, and excluding marked edges containing x, yields precisely three footprints of size seven or eight: doubled (0,10),(5,8); (2,10),(8,9); (4,10),(5,9). Each gives seven stars internal to G−{3,6,7}. The reproduction is 6648-dirty-and-corner-footprints.py. In addition there are at least two stars touching y or z. By 6648-size21-sole-and.md each y,z has two raw readers; either four distinct ORs, or one common gate and one individual OR apiece. A common raw OR y∨z would not be Lambda (its only possible anchor is x) and is neither of the two genuinely dirty charged ORs. A common AND(y,z) is a clean top starring yz, and at least one other direct OR reader is Lambda as M is raw-empty and only Q remains. In the four-OR case each vertex has a Lambda reader. If only one incident star could account for both vertices it would be yz; similarly in the common-AND case assume only yz. A Lambda OR reader of raw v∈{y,z} would have to be anchored at the other vertex t. Its other pin has a raw leaf s distinct from v (otherwise raw v∨that pin is redundant), forcing another star ts incident t. This establishes nine stars and U≤7. Since k≥1 this alone proves the desired bound whenever a nonhomogeneous double occurs.

7. Otherwise each batch receives at most one, except for one homogeneous double. If Q not in D's cone, total ≤2+2+2(c−k)+1+1≤2c+4, covering j≥3 (no small-j exception). If Q enters D, bound ≤2+4+2(c−k)+1+1≤2c+6, sufficient j≥4. For raw-empty Q at j=3, omit the ordinary correction, yielding ≤2c+5. For raw-positive Q at j=3 with Q entering D, the junk gates are g,H,D. If H is a multiplicative factor of D outside Q, D equals its pure anchored expansion obtained by setting Q=true while retaining H, hence only two protected batches. Otherwise fully raw D0=D[Q←R] is a product of at most two anchored pure clauses: D is the only remaining conjunctive AND (g cannot feed D, and any escape of H's AND cone toward D through an OR must encounter Q). D0 accepts at most one original residual in total. In each 9/10 deletion two clauses cannot accept two residuals (four orientations). If two overall, they are 9b and 10a. A globally clean two-clause term cannot accept two residuals, so assume dirty; all independent positives include 9,10 and minimal positives have size ≤2. Hence it accepts {9,10}. Its clause supports are disjoint by singleton rejection and 9,10 on opposite sides, forcing a,9 in one support and b,10 in the other with a≠b. Then ab also activates D0 and must be an edge by its independent-positive constraint. Vertex b neighbors 9 and a, so by C4-freeness is the unique possible original anchor for the support containing 9,a; it stars 9b. Contradiction. Merge D0's two batches into one singleton slot, giving U≤2c+5 exactly. This completes the enumeration for all placements of Q and all j≥3.

This is a mathematical derivation with a small exhaustive graph lookup, not a compiled Lean proof.
