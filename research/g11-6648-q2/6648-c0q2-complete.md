# L-meta-6648 when there are no centreless tops and two exceptional OR gates

Use the issued G11, a least AO circuit in TopForm of size at most 21, and at least three junk ANDs. Let U be the cardinality of Ustar, j the cardinality of junk, c the cardinality of centreless and q the cardinality of orN outside lam. Under c=0 and q=2 the claim is U≤j+2. The issued balance |X|+j=U+|J| then gives |X|≤|J|+2 for the actual selected sets. The argument bounds all residuals and therefore also applies to selections satisfying all the other issued hypotheses.

The separately reviewed G11 lower bound of 21 gives size exactly 21. Put x=3 and {y,z}={6,7}. Restricting x=false gives a graph requiring at least 19 exact AO gates (L18373). Thus at most two gates disappear. There is at least one reader of x. A repeated-pin reader is already redundant. Distinct direct readers cannot read one another without absorption. Three readers would erase three gates. If two readers include an AND, its false output must have a further live reader outside those two, or be the output, which is impossible since the restriction is nonconstant. Two OR readers are excluded by the separate double-OR proof. This leaves one reader.

For the clean cases the reader is A=x∧S. Its nonraw sibling is independent of x and accepts singleton y and singleton z. Testing singleton and pair inputs for clean A gives orIn(S)⊆{y,z}. This set is nonempty since c=0. If S is pure it equals y∨z. In a least circuit the physical gate then has direct raw pins y,z: an OR of singleton leaf sets containing only y and z can have no nonredundant intermediate gate with singleton support, and no earlier gate computes the same two-leaf function. A is followed by a unique OR reader (the false restriction can lose only two gates). S must have a live path avoiding A, or setting x=false would also delete S, a third gate. An ordinary top with S in an OR-only operand cone and a nonempty opposite side would require each opposite raw vertex to neighbor both y,z. Their only common neighbor is x, and A is its sole reader. Such a top cannot lie on an escape path from S avoiding A.

## Both raw vertices and an impure sibling

There is an AND in the cone of S. The final AND on a path from such an AND to S is dirty by TopForm at A; the first OR after it is a charged OR (dirty, with one empty OR-reachable raw side). There must also be a charge strictly downstream of S. Follow a live escape avoiding A. Its first AND is dirty, since no ordinary clean top can terminate the OR-only prefix. After an AND-only chain a clean AND would be centreless, and termination in a dirty output is impossible. Thus the chain has a first OR reader, also charged. Write P and Q for the upstream and downstream charges; they exhaust q.

No OR descendant of S on the escape prefix can be in Lambda: any witnessing ordinary top would have an opposite raw vertex adjacent to both y and z, necessarily x, but that top would be A, upstream of that descendant along its only possible S input. Such a descendant is dirty (positive on y). Therefore no OR lies on this prefix, since it would be a third charge. The chain ending at Q gives a direct dirty input Z of Q with Z≤S pointwise. Q is dirty even if both its inputs are dirty. If Q had empty raw support, its escape to the output would require another charged OR, either as a direct OR reader of the raw-empty Q or as the first OR after dirty ANDs; a clean AND before such an OR would be centreless. Therefore the other pin of Q is raw-positive.

Expanding the OR-only boundary of S, all dirty-AND contributions enter through P. The second pin of P cannot also be raw-empty: then P has no raw reach and cannot equal S, and its first OR reader on the way to S would consume a third charge. Every earlier OR entering its raw-positive side is pure, since any earlier AND-to-OR injection would be yet another charge. Thus P is a dirty AND D OR a pure raw disjunction. Expanding S through its OR-only paths gives the semantic identity S=y∨z∨D. No separate physical gate y∨z is assumed. Every AND primitive of the AND-only predecessor region of D is dirty, and all its boundary wires are raw or pure ORs, since both charges lie downstream. Let d≤j count the distinct AND gates of this region including D. Connectivity of this binary DAG bounds its distinct boundary wires by d+1. Each nonsingleton boundary support has a Lambda anchor: its pure OR is singleton-positive and cannot be P or Q. Every edge from that anchor to a support vertex is an original star edge.

Set x=y=z=false, leaving the eight-vertex graph H8. D is clean on H8. If an independent H8 set activated D, it would activate S, and adjoining x would activate A on another independent set, since x has no H8 neighbor. On any H8 pair with D false, S and Z are false and P evaluates to its raw disjunction. Q also evaluates to its raw disjunction: its raw-positive side may pass through P on OR paths, but that contribution now projects to raw; a dirty AND on any other OR path would require a charge distinct from P,Q. On this pair every true top operand obtained along an OR-only path evaluates exactly as its raw support, since all dirty-AND injections pass through P or Q. Clean ANDs cannot occur upstream of a clean top. Any accepted pair is therefore in a raw cross of an ordinary top and is starred. For the last implication descend from the output through true ORs: a raw input would contradict singleton rejection, and a dirty AND on this OR-only route would contradict correctness. A true clean top is reached. Consequently every original residual inside H8 activates D.

The accepted original residuals in H8 form a matching induced in H8. For two meeting at a, singleton rejection supplies a primitive omitting a and containing both mates. Its anchor and a would have two common neighbors, so must coincide and star-cover the edges. For two disjoint residuals ab,cd with a graph cross ac, no primitive can contain b,c (its anchor would be a), or a,d (its anchor would be c). Each primitive meets both residuals, hence the other mixed pairs ad,bc hit every primitive. D is clean on H8 so these are graph edges and form a four-cycle with ab,cd. This proves inducedness, using original G11 for the anchor argument even if the anchor is outside H8. Every orientation taking one endpoint of each residual matching edge is independent and rejects D. A primitive hitting every one of r residual edges can reject at most one of the 2^r orientations. Hence r≤floor(log2(d+1)), including r=0. Only five edges lie outside H8. Two, xy and xz, are starred by A because both are raw-visible. At most three other edges remain. Thus U≤3+floor(log2(d+1))≤j+2 for j≥3.

This is a mathematical case proof. It and its supporting gate surgery are not a combined Lean certificate for the official theorem. Other values of c,q are separate obligations.
