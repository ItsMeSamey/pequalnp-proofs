# Second nonempty charged OR outside the protected donor cone

Assume size 21, sole OR port M=3 OR D, q=2; the second charged OR Q directly reads dirty B with raw-positive opposite pin, and Q is not an ancestor of D. The case B<=D is already closed, so let B not imply D. Both D,B are dirty ANDs. Use the existing private-donor exclusion for D.

Shrink M to 3, giving Q=B' OR R, where primes denote projections and R is a raw disjunction (no third dirty-reading OR). On D-negative inputs the entire output is unchanged. All projections below are clean when starting from a clean top. A Q-dependent centreless top expands through its AND-only region to a product of pure raw clauses and mixed boundary wires B' OR S. All such S include R. Its two contributions are the fully raw term and the dirty-side term B' times the pure clauses. Both are anchored conjunctions: B' has no Q ancestor, and the original nonsingleton raw supports are Lambda-anchored (at Q use its other pin's projected raw support, which is originally Lambda-anchored when nonsingleton). Degree-four homogeneity gives at most two residuals per term. At a top with direct B input, substituting Q=true gives its entire projected contribution, since the top already needs B'; this yields only one anchored conjunction. Tops requiring D on a direct AND path contribute no D-negative residuals. Every ordinary top's fully raw term is an original star. Their dirty-side terms jointly equal B' times H, the union of all opposite raw guards at Q-affected ordinary tops; if there are no such tops there is no charge. A clean ordinary top cannot receive Q by OR paths on both sides, which would propagate Q's dirty independent witness. Every vertex of R belongs to the mixed-side raw support of each such original top, so selecting any one gives an original star-edge anchor of H. Therefore the aggregate clean anchored term gives at most two residuals.

Because neither charged OR precedes D in its cone, the two clean anchored restrictions at 9 and 10 bound D-positive residuals by three: at most two in the 9-deletion graph and at most one remaining 9-incident edge in the 10-deletion induced matching. D has a second reader by the private-donor exclusion. Trace this external path along ANDs up to its first OR or clean AND. The first OR could only be Q with B<=D, contrary to hypothesis. The terminal clean AND is centreless, requires D on an AND-only path, and does not need an allocation for D-negative edges.

Let m be the number of centreless tops genuinely requiring both raw and dirty-side contributions on the remaining edges; an upper bound may count every top except those forced single-sided. Each of these m tops directly reads a dirty AND since both charged ORs have nonempty raw support. Two tops cannot share a direct dirty input (distributivity saves one gate when rebuilding the final clean aggregation). That input cannot be B or D by the first paragraph. Thus m<=j-2 and c>=m+1. The total is U<=3+2+2(c-1)+2m, enough when 2m<=j-1. For j=3 this always holds. For j>=6 and 2m>=j, the target 2c+j+2>=2j+4>=16, the graph's entire edge count. For j=5 the remaining case m=3 forces c>=4 with target >=15. If any ordinary top exists, it supplies a star edge, so U<=15. Otherwise omit the two-unit ordinary charge, giving U<=3+2(c-1)+6=2c+7.

For j=4 only m=2 is left. The two dual centres directly read distinct G1,G2, which together with B,D exhaust all four dirty gates. Neither dual centre has a second direct dirty pin: B or D would make it single-sided or absent, and the other centre's Gi cannot be directly reused. Take the earlier Gi. Its all-raw projected AND cone has no proper multiplicative dirty ancestor: the only earlier candidates B or D would force its top to imply B or D. Paths through M or Q project to raw ORs. Its two primitive factors and the one opposite raw disjunction at the clean top total at most three raw clauses; the latter disjunction is the raw projection of the non-dirty opposite inlet. Hence its raw term cannot own two disjoint residuals, which require four rejected orientations, nor two adjacent residuals by the anchored argument. This saves one extra unit, giving U<=3+2+2(c-1)+3=2c+6.

This is an analytic case proof with no disjointness assumption on dirty ancestor cones.
