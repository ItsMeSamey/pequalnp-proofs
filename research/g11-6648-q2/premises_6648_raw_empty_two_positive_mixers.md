# Common raw AND, empty S, two genuinely mixed raw-positive paid ORs

Assume the previous size-21 card hypotheses, h=yz the common raw reader, A=x∧S clean with raw(S)=empty, j≥3, q=2, c≥1. This note treats the case each paid OR Mi has a raw-positive pin and one direct dirty AND donor Di. Use original residuals. All other ORs below ANDs are lambda-anchored and have both pins raw-positive. Any primitive raw support of an individual mixed pin is guarded: for a paid OR use its raw-positive other pin (itself raw, anchored, or an earlier mixed OR's guarded support); otherwise use the lambda witness. A raw-empty wire below a top is necessarily an AND. Consequently each centreless top directly reads a dirty AND. Each given dirty AND can directly feed at most one clean top by fusing a pair of clean outputs with that common factor and regrouping the clean aggregation. Assign distinct dirty wires to the c centreless tops, taking S for A; c≤j.

There are at least two different original stars incident to y or z. One is yz at h. If there were no other, Ry,Rz must both be paid: a lambda witness would have to use the opposite mate as singleton centre, and the other raw-positive pin would need a distinct raw member (otherwise absorption), forcing a second near star. In that case Ry,Rz exhaust q. The individual separation Ry(z)=Rz(y)=false follows from the comparison y10 against z10 and z9 against y9 as in premises_6648_common_and_visible.md. No pre-top OR directly reads Ry: it would be lambda and, since its raw leaves include y, would have to be witnessed by centre z, forcing all raw leaves equal to y under the only-near-star assumption. Its other pin is then raw-positive with sole leaf y, and every OR-only path for raw y to that pin uses Ry (the only other raw reader is the AND h). The OR reader is absorbable. Symmetrically no OR directly reads Rz. All other primitive OR/input wires can therefore not accept both singleton y and singleton z: on each such singleton a raw/OR leaf or the corresponding Ry or Rz would be needed, and every dirty-to-OR exit is at one of these two individual wires. Unfolding the AND-only S to its primitive OR/input frontier contradicts its bivalence. This proves at least two near stars and U≤14.

Write the donor functions D1,D2 in topological order. Ordinary raw pin functions are the union of their original raw supports and a subset of these donors reached through OR-only paths. Expanding all ordinary tops together thus gives original stars plus D1 H1, D2 H2, and optionally D1D2. Here Hi is the disjunction of all opposite raw supports of ordinary tops where Di is an OR-only alternative. Any raw leaf of Mi is a common star anchor for Hi since it occurs on all those ordinary sides via that same OR path. A donor appearing in both pins of one ordinary top is impossible by dirtiness. Empty terms are omitted.

The first donor D1 is a conjunction of guarded pure clauses. If D2 has earlier mixed primitive pins, write D2=D20∨D1K, where D20 is the product of the raw primitive supports and K consists of the pure guards opposite the mixed alternatives (possibly the constant true); D20 implies K. If no earlier mixed factor is present use D2=D20 without the additional term. This decomposition works on DAGs by unfolding/reusing the function. An ordinary joint D1D2 in the nested case equals D1K and absorbs D1KH2; without the joint use D1H1,D20H2,D1KH2. Thus at most three ordinary clean guarded conjunctions always suffice.

Every other centreless root similarly has at most four clean guarded expansions. Cut the two donor inputs to OR exits, treating their values temporarily as monotone flags. At flag settings 00,10,01,11 the product of unsatisfied raw guards is respectively P00,P10,P01,P11 (symbolic true/false permitted). The union of P00, D1 P10, D2 P01 and D1D2 P11 gives the original root. More precisely this cut retains primitive donor multiplicative appearances if they are direct AND factors; they can equally be treated as flags at those multiplicative frontier pins. Nested expansion gives the four terms P00,D1P10,D20P01,D1K P11, since P01 implies P11. Each term implies the original clean root. A root having a donor as an explicit multiplicative factor needs at most two terms: only settings where its flag is true survive. In the independent case keep the joint flag product directly. If the two donors are identical there is just one flag and each root needs at most two terms.

For a batch of two residuals the graph footprint lemma applies to its anchored clean conjunction. All batch marks exclude x because the corrections to other tops are x-independent and clean; in a clean slice below they are taken outside y,z and do not involve x. Nonhomogeneous doubles require seven internal H8 stars (no star touches x since A is centreless), hence together with the near stars U≤7. Homogeneous double is unique, with marks 14,59 and four internal star edges incident to 8; then U≤10. Thus a disjoint allocation costs at most one more edge than the number of batches when U>7, and no extra when U>10. Count the two x-edge residuals of A separately.

If S differs from both donors, at least d=max(0,c+2−j) other centreless roots have an explicit donor factor by the injection of centreless roots into distinct dirty direct predecessors. The number of residuals is bounded by 2 + 3 + 4(c−1)−2d plus at most one double, or U≤7 immediately. The resulting U≤4c+2−2d suffices for c=1,2 (when c=2,j=3, d≥1). For c=3 one may assume U>10 or the target holds, hence U≤13−2d. For j=3,4,5 and larger this meets j+8. For c≥4 the bound U≤14 and j≥c suffice. If the physical donor is identical at both mixers, retain two separate ordinary terms D H1 and D H2, one per physical mixer. Each Hi has its own original anchor from that mixer. No top can put the same dirty signal on both sides. There is only one donor flag at each other centreless root and hence at most two clean terms there. Counting the two possible x edges and at most one exclusively assigned double yields 2+2+2(c−1)+1=2c+3; the U≤7 alternative also suffices since A is centreless and j≥3.

A pure anchored donor cannot equal S: each pure clause would need both y,z; its unique possible common anchor x is unavailable because A has no raw star. Thus the only remaining case is S=D2 with a genuine dependence on D1. On setting y=z=false S is clean, since any independent input positive there remains independent after adjoining x and would wrongly activate A. On that slice globally collect S-positive residuals in two guarded clean batches D20 and D1K. For the remaining residuals set S=false at all its uses: A drops, only D1 survives as a genuine mixer, so at most one ordinary guarded correction and two guarded clean products per other centreless top suffice. Count separately at most four near residuals since yz is starred. This gives U≤2c+6 (or U≤7); enough for j≥4. If j=3,c≥3 any double implies U≤10 and the target already holds. For j=3,c≤2 a nonhomogeneous double is harmless. A homogeneous double in a term containing D1 is impossible: the pure product D20 cannot accept both y,z as no anchored raw clause contains both. Hence D1 must accept at least one of them by S's bivalence. But each primitive D1 clause accepting both marked edges 14,59 would contain one endpoint from each, with unique common anchor 8; its full support lies in N(8) which excludes y,z. In the term D20 there are at most two dirty ANDs after cutting the earlier mixed alternative: S cannot have D1 as a direct/AND-only factor, as D1 cannot accept both y,z either. Thus at most three primitive factors and no double. In a remaining centreless base term with D1=S=false at most the single third junk AND and its root survive in its AND cone: at most three primitive factors again. These terms cannot own two edges since four independent orientations require four clauses. With no double U≤2c+5, as required.

The argument is mathematical and relies on the finite G11 footprint table and the exact card-reader assumptions. It does not address a paid OR with two raw-empty inputs or a paid pure OR.

## Extension to four individual OR readers (no common gate)

At least two of the four direct raw OR readers of y,z are lambda. Hence at least one original near edge is starred, and at least two different graph edges are starred (an individual lambda OR's raw other pin needs a distinct raw leaf, since repeating its own raw input would be absorbable). Thus U≤14. Of the five graph edges incident to y or z at most four are residual. No star touches x. For U≥8 any nonhomogeneous double has one of the three exact seven-star footprints. The calculation ranges over every disjoint pair of original edges, including those touching y or z, and every admissible assignment of its four corner anchors. It excludes x-star footprints; it does not initially restrict marks to H. The seven stars in each surviving row are internal to H. See the enumeration in 6648-dirty-and-corner-footprints.py. Each row's stars include both other rows' marked edges. The homogeneous pair 14,59 is also excluded from separate allocation since either 59 is in the seven stars, or 59 is a mark of the very same double. This gives at most one allocated double for U≥8 even without two near stars. In the nonhomogeneous case U≤8 by the one near star; in the homogeneous case U≤11 because its four star edges incident to 8 are internal.

The S-distinct calculation therefore remains sufficient: c=1 and 2 use its one-double bound and c≥3,j≥c can assume U>11 whenever absence of doubles is needed. The S=D2 argument has unchanged four near residuals and one-double bound. For j≥4 these suffice; at j=3,c≥3 the target ≥11 suffices for any double. For j=3,c≤2 the earlier no-homogeneous-double argument still applies. A nonhomogeneous double gives U≤8, already sufficient unless c=1 and U=8. Treat precisely that last case.

At c=1,j=3 only the exterior terms D20, D1K and the ordinary correction D1H need be considered, besides at most four near residuals. D20's at most three primitive clauses cannot double. If D1K or D1H owns a nonhomogeneous double, the seven internal stars of its footprint leave exactly one near star. D1 accepts singleton y or z as proved above. Every primitive of D1 consequently includes this vertex and a mark of each of the two doubled edges, hence has an anchor. Because exactly one near star exists, all these anchors must be the other endpoint of that edge. If that edge is yz, either candidate anchor has at most one H8 neighbor, so cannot hit both disjoint marks. If it is y–10 the support must lie in N(10), which misses the second marked edge of each of the three rows. If it is z–9 the support is in N(9); only the row (0,10),(5,8) has two edges meeting N(9). Every primitive D1 clause must include 0, the only available vertex of the first edge. Thus D1 is positive both at singleton 0 and at singleton z. Every ordinary correction guard vertex must be adjacent to both, so its only possible value is 9. All edges incident to 9 are starred in this footprint: the three internal 09,59,89 and the unique near z–9. The ordinary D1H term has no residual, removing a slot. Now even a double yields at most four near residuals plus two global exterior batches plus one extra, i.e. seven, as required.

The separate already excluded common-OR reader case, together with this extension and the common-AND case, covers all raw-reader classifications under the two raw-positive mixed-gates and empty-S hypotheses.
