# Clean sole AND with two independent mixers OR-reaching S

Assume q=2, x=3,y=6,z=7, A=x AND S clean. Each charge Qi=Di OR Ri has nonempty raw partner, distinct incomparable dirty AND donors with pure anchored conjunctive cones, and OR-only paths from both Qi to S. H deletes x,y,z. A clean implies each independent positive input for Di hits y or z; otherwise adjoining x contradicts cleanliness. Thus Di is clean on H, covering at most two residual H edges by the anchored batch lemma. At j=3 incomparability leaves each cone at most two dirty ANDs, hence at most three primitive clauses: two residual H edges need four by the orientation test. Shrinking both mixers to raw partners preserves donor-negative edges; ordinary tops then supply only original stars and centreless tops at most two residuals each.

If raw(S) has one visible neighbor, the hidden singleton activates some Di (all nonraw inputs to the OR cone enter at a charged dirty pin). This Di covers at most one H residual: two require four orientation clauses all containing the hidden singleton, so the anchors of adjacent corners agree by two common neighbors. One anchor must see five vertices, impossible. There is an additional outside star besides the visible x-neighbor. In the four-reader classification at least two individual OR readers of y,z are uncharged. Their second pin has nonempty raw support omitting the direct vertex by irredundancy. An x anchor would force both distinct raw leaves into raw(S). Another anchor stars an additional outside edge. In the three-reader classification a common AND stars yz, while a common OR can neither be a charged direct dirty-AND reader nor have its required x anchor. Thus among the five edges outside H at most three are residual. Adding at most two donor H edges when j=3 and three for j≥4 gives U≤2c+j+2.

If both are visible, there are again at most three outside residuals. The bound follows for j=3 and j≥5. For j=4 only two exclusively assigned double donor batches can exceed it. At least one is nonhomogeneous since only 14,59 can constitute a homogeneous double. It forces seven distinct original H stars by the corner lemma: no vertex x,y,z has two H neighbors. Along with xy,xz this gives U≤7, sufficient if c≥1. At c=0 an extra outside star gives U≤2+4=6. Otherwise no mixer OR-reaches any ordinary top except A, since its raw side contains y or z and the opposite raw leaf cannot be x. Such a top would give an additional outside star. Any mixer path through a dirty AND must reach a last dirty-AND-to-OR charge before going to an ordinary top, since an AND-only continuation gives a centreless top. There are none. Consequently the other ordinary tops supply only stars, and A accepts no H edges.
