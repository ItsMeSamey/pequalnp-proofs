# First-stop star decomposition for arbitrary AO2 circuits

Assume a simple bipartite C4-free graph of minimum degree two, with 2n input vertices and a perfect matching. Trim the scalar AO2 circuit to the output. Every raw is essential. A fork counts distinct outgoing physical operand pins, including two pins of one reader. If g binary gates remain, total outlet excess is g−2n+1: count 2g operand pins and subtract one for each nonoutput wire among 2n+g wires. Therefore the number f of distinct forks is at most g−2n+1.

Order fork wires topologically. Starting at each raw, follow its unique outgoing pin until encountering a fork wire or the designated output. Assign the raw to that first fork, including the raw itself if a fork, or to the terminal group. Every raw assigned to a computed group has all output paths through its group's fork. No raw assigned to a later group can influence an earlier fork: on a path to that fork its first fork would occur no later than that earlier wire. Empty groups do not cause a problem.

Each group is independent. Otherwise take adjacent u,v in it. Neither raw is itself a fork because a raw fork's group is its singleton. Their paths to their first fork or output merge at a fixed binary gate. Each premerge arm has a unique occurrence of its respective raw and excludes the opposite one. With other raws fixed these positive unary arms can transmit or become constant; any bivariate section essential in both must use their unchanged values, and any unary decoder above their merger must transmit the result. Thus their essential binary operation is fixed. With all other raws low it is AND. Setting just one other neighbor of u and one other neighbor of v high gives OR: these two neighbors are distinct and nonadjacent by bipartiteness and absence of a four-cycle. Contradiction.

For a nonterminal group S, pin all earlier groups' raws low. Its fork now carries a bit of the inputs in S alone (later groups do not influence it), and all S-origin paths pass through this single bit. There are at most two distinct residual rows on the unpinned later inputs. The empty assignment on S has an all-zero row on the singleton later inputs. A singleton i in S has ones on exactly its later neighbors in these columns. Thus every nonempty later neighborhood is the same set. The crossing edges from S to later groups form a single complete bipartite graph, possibly empty. It is a star by C4-freeness. This is an actual edge partition: charge every edge to its earlier endpoint group; the last terminal group is independent. In particular a cover of size ≤f exists, and the matching forces f≥n, so g≥3n−1.

If every vertex has degree two, the graph has 2n edges, and every nonempty charged star has at most two edges. Then 2n≤2f, giving the full inequality 2n+n≤g+1 for arbitrary computed forks and arbitrary numbers of connected components. No TopForm or least-circuit assumption beyond trimming is needed in this case.
