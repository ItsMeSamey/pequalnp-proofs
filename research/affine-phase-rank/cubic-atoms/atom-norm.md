# Minimum coefficient norm versus exact list length for the binary cubic

Let G_t(x,y,z)=(−1)^{Σ x_i y_i z_i}. Atoms are unnormalized functions 1_U(−1)^q on the 3t bits, with U affine and q binary quadratic. For a decomposition into real coefficients the minimum coefficient ℓ1 norm is (4/3)^t. After consolidating identical signed functions and orienting them to have positive correlation, its unique minimizer uses all 8^t distinguished atoms with coefficient 6^{-t}. Smaller lists may exist with larger norm. The value of the analogous normalized stabilizer extent for the CCZ state, its tensor multiplicativity, and an optimal decomposition are known from Bravyi et al., https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2019-09-02-181 . No priority claim for the norm value.

For a full-support atom write its exponent as Q(z)+z·(L(x,y)+c)+R(x,y), with polar rank r for Q. The Walsh transform of (−1)^Q has absolute value 2^{-r/2} on an affine flat of codimension t−r and zero elsewhere, under normalized expectation. Hence the absolute correlation with G_t is at most 2^{-r/2} times the probability that x⊙y+L(x,y)+c lies on this flat. In the flat's character expansion the character at u has expected magnitude at most 2^{-wt(u)}: the bilinear x_i y_i terms on supp u are independent rank-two contributions and all other variables have at most linear terms. For any dim(t−r) linear annihilator choose systematic pivot coordinates; averaging 2^{-wt(u)} over it gives at most (3/4)^{t−r}. Thus the correlation is at most 2^{-r/2}(3/4)^{t−r}, strictly below (3/4)^t if r>0.

At equality repeat this bound with each of the three variable groups as final group. There are no same-group quadratic edges between distinct indices. With r=0 every character contributes in the upper bound, in particular u=e_i: the term has zero expectation if L_i contains any x_j or y_j with j≠i. Applying this for all choices of final group excludes all cross-index quadratic edges, hence the atom factors over individual triples. On one triple its absolute correlation is 3/4 exactly when the quadratic disagrees with xyz at one point b, up to constant sign. Indeed disagreement has odd cardinality (nonzero top ANF coefficient), and distances 1 and 7 are the unique extremal values. The quadratic part of xyz+δ_b determines b, since the coefficients of xy,xz,yz are 1+b_z,1+b_y,1+b_x. Thus for a given full quadratic sign at most one linear shift can achieve the bound, ignoring a constant global sign. Expand any proper affine indicator in its nontrivial annihilator's characters: the triangle bound is strict because at least one shifted phase is suboptimal.

Taking the inner product of a decomposition with G_t proves coefficient norm ≥(4/3)^t. All optimal atoms must be maximizers with aligned coefficient signs. Their oriented functions are exactly the tensor products of G_1(1−2δ_b). The eight single-block functions form the columns of diag(G_1)(J−2I), an invertible matrix, and sum to 6G_1. Their tensor powers are again a basis with unique coefficients 6^{-t}, attaining the lower bound. Bit lengths can grow linearly while the norm grows exponentially, so this does not exclude a polynomial-time exact atom constructor with polynomial-bit signed coefficients.
