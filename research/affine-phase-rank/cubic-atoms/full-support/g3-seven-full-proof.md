# Seven full-support real quadratic signs cannot sum to the three-block cubic

Write p as the XOR of three disjoint cubic monomials on nine inputs. A full-support chirp is a sign of a binary polynomial of degree at most two. The theorem concerns arbitrary real coefficients and arbitrary such phases.

1. **Denominator.** The degree-nine coefficient of the unique *integer* multilinear expansion of the sign of p equals −8. For any full-support chirp it is divisible by 32: expand its sign as the product of 1−2M over its nonconstant binary monomials M. A term involving all nine input bits selects at least five factors. Consequently every rational full-support representation has common denominator divisible by four.
2. **Coefficient enumeration.** Remove dependent chirps, preserving a representation on at most seven columns. On a nonsingular submatrix of evaluation rows, Cramer's rule gives rational coefficients. The determinants of all r-by-r sign matrices are divisible by 2^(r−1) by subtracting the first row from every other row. Hadamard bounds their normalized magnitudes by floor(r^(r/2)/2^(r−1)). This gives at most 3 for r≤5, 6 for r=6 and 14 for r=7. The numerator and denominator of the list can be bounded simultaneously by this number by division by their joint gcd. Absorb coefficient signs into the phases, sort the positive integers a_i, and write D for the denominator. The parity relation obtained by reducing the identity modulo four on all inputs is that the XOR of the exponents of odd a_i is constant. Their count must be even. Two such indices would give proportional columns. Thus four or six a_i are odd.
3. **Projective row test.** Normalize each evaluation row by multiplying by its first sign. Its coordinates s satisfy s_1=1 and the sum of a_i s_i equals D or −D. On this candidate row set the normalized output y is the sign of that sum. The row set must have rational rank r. The finite test seeks a pair of indices i,j, one sign of s_i s_j, and a product of arbitrary relative signs (with either global sign) agreeing with y on all the candidate rows having that specified pair sign. Exact enumeration finds such a witness for every rank-r candidate. Pair signs are nonconstant on the actual input domain since independence excludes proportional columns; both fibers are therefore nonempty. The feature product is another quadratic sign.
4. **Annihilator.** Undoing the normalization, such a witness says that p agrees with a quadratic polynomial q on a fiber of a nonconstant quadratic bit. Its indicator b is a nonzero binary polynomial of degree at most two, with (p+q)b=0. This is impossible. Take a degree-d monomial m of the highest nonzero part of b, where d≤2. At least one of the three entire cubic blocks avoids m. The union of that block and m has d+3 variables and exactly one whole cubic block. It is contributed to pb only by that block and m, whereas qb has degree at most d+2.

The proof excludes only full-support lists. An eight-term supported decomposition exists, and this theorem does not establish that there is an eight-term full-support decomposition. It does not exclude seven terms when some supports are proper affine subspaces.
