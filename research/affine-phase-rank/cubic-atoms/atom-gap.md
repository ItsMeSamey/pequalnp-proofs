# Relative gap away from extremal real atoms

Use the conventions of atom-norm.md, with c=(3/4)^t. Every atom that fails to maximize absolute correlation with G_t has correlation at most (8/9)c in magnitude.

If the within-group polar rank in any of x,y,z is positive, it is even and at least two, so the ratio from the quadratic Walsh bound is at most (4/(3√2))²=8/9. Otherwise all same-group ranks vanish. If a cross-index term occurs, rename groups so one such term is z_i x_j with i≠j. Compare the r=0 Fourier sum over u with its upper bound Σ_u 2^{-wt(u)}/2^t=c. The normalized weights are independent Bernoulli(1/3). A summand vanishes whenever u_j=0 and the off-diagonal parity of coefficients of x_j coming from z_i is one, by summing the then unpaired variable x_j. The off-diagonal coefficient set is nonempty, its Bernoulli parity is odd with probability at least 1/3, and it is independent of u_j. At least 2/9 of the majorant mass vanishes. With no cross-index term the atom factors into triples; a nonmaximal triple has absolute correlation 1/4 and therefore gives ratio ≤1/3.

For proper affine support, average its at least two distinct character shifts of the full quadratic sign. If none maximizes the previous bound applies. If one maximizes, the fixed quadratic coefficients are block separated and all other shifts are suboptimal product phases. The average ratio is at most (1+1/3)/2=2/3. In particular any exact list with norm at most (1+ε)/c has total coefficient norm on nonmaximizing atoms at most 9ε/c, by pairing with G_t and subtracting the maximality gap. These stability estimates concern coefficient norm, and allow exponential cancellation in a short exact list.
