# Exact orbit size and certificates for the explicit universal cubic

Let V=GF(2)^(3t), p(x)=sum_i x_(3i-2)x_(3i-1)x_(3i), and G=(-1)^p. Define twisted translations on real functions by U_a h(x)=G(x)G(x+a)h(x+a). They form an abelian group isomorphic to V, fix G, and map each affine-supported quadratic atom to another atom (up to sign): p(x)+p(x+a) has degree ≤2. A ray identifies scalar multiples of a nonzero atom. The following concerns dictionaries closed under these translations.

**Exact symmetry bound.** Every atom ray has orbit size ≥2^t, and G has an invariant dictionary of exactly 2^t rays giving an exact decomposition. To prove the lower bound let K be the stabilizer of a ray A=1_U(-1)^q. It is a linear subspace, and K-translations preserve its affine support. Choose x₀ in U. Restricted to x₀+K the function H=GA is nonzero. For a in K its translate is a scalar multiple of itself, necessarily ±1; the eigenvalues form a character of K. Thus H on this coset is a sign times a character, so p restricts to q plus an affine form and has degree ≤2 there. Its third derivative T vanishes on K³.

Here T=sum_i det(a_i,b_i,c_i) on the t triples of coordinates (over GF(2), signs may be omitted). Define P(v₁,...,v_(3t)) by summing over all *unordered partitions of the labelled positions* into t unordered triples, multiplying T on each triple. This is multilinear, symmetric and alternating. Indeed if two arguments coincide, a partition placing them in one triple gives zero. Otherwise transposing the two labelled positions pairs the partitions with equal values; no such partition is fixed since each of their two triples contains two further distinct labels. Their sum is zero in GF(2). On the standard basis exactly the partition into original blocks survives, giving P=1. Hence P is a nonzero top form and evaluates nonzero on every basis. If dim K>2t, extend a basis of K to V. Every partition of the resulting basis must put three K vectors in one triple, forcing P=0, a contradiction. Thus |orbit(A)|=2^(3t-dim K)≥2^t. Equality is attained: partition x=(u,v,w) by fixing u=a, and take as atom the restriction of G to each coset. There p=sum_i a_i v_i w_i is quadratic. Their sum is G and U_b permutes these distinct atoms exactly.

**Sharp boundary of the symmetry argument.** A decomposition need not use an invariant dictionary. Averaging it over the group yields an invariant one but can enlarge the dictionary exponentially. The same proof shows every affine subspace on which p drops to degree ≤2 has codimension at least t; the coset construction attains that threshold. In particular any disjoint partition into quadratic-phase pieces representing G needs ≥2^t pieces, regardless of nonuniform weights, because each piece agrees with G up to a scalar throughout its support. Overlapping supports and cancellations are unrestricted in ordinary stabilizer rank. No improvement to the unrestricted Ω(t²/log t) bound follows here. The full translation group agrees with the Clifford symmetries obtained by conjugating X-translations by the diagonal phase G. Compare the original work on magic-state fidelity and extent [Bravyi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.00128).

**Uniformly checkable certificates for G sums.** Given a purported signed rational expansion S, it is possible to check S=G deterministically in time polynomial in t and its full encoding length, without an exponential truth table. Require S(0)=1; for every coordinate j require S(x+e_j)=(-1)^(p(x)+p(x+e_j)) S(x) for every x. The extra phase is quadratic. Translation and multiplication convert both sides into sums of the same atom class. Their squared difference has a polynomial-time exactly computable sum over the cube. Hypercube connectivity proves sufficiency; following any path from 0 gives the prescribed G at its endpoint. Thus under polynomial rank there is a fixed polynomial bound on short, polynomially checkable certificates specifying universal advice for each length. Ordinary deterministic search for one is not supplied by verification. An NP oracle can find one by bitwise prefix extension under the polynomial bound (existential completion and deterministic verification). A deterministic polynomial-time constructor would still imply FP=#P. This also makes the nonuniform-to-NP implication direct: guess and check one universal cubic dictionary sized to the canonical simulated circuit, contract it, and compute the exact count.

**Counting-hierarchy consequence.** Polynomial G rank implies PP=NP=coNP and NP⊆P/poly, the latter since a valid length-dependent dictionary with bounded rational coefficients can be hardwired in the contraction algorithm for #SAT. It also implies CH=NP. Here is a proof accounting for adaptive oracle queries. For any language in PP^NP take SAT as a complete oracle and a polynomial bound q(N) on query length on N-bit instances. Guess polynomial-size circuits for SAT for all lengths ≤q(N); they exist by NP⊆P/poly. Require correctness of this whole table of circuits via bounded SAT self-reducibility, using a polynomially parseable padded encoding with a partial assignment and a remaining-variable index so that branches stay within the supported lengths. Equivalently choose an NP-complete bounded-computation language with equal-length configurations and a decreasing unary clock. Each oracle circuit's violation of a base case or a recurrence has a polynomial-size witness, consisting of the offending input; therefore consistency is in coNP=NP. With consistent circuits, hardwire them into the oracle machine's polynomial-time branching computation, obtaining a polynomial-size ordinary majority circuit. Its majority condition has an NP certificate because PP=NP. Combining both certificates yields PP^NP⊆NP. For precision, the SAT encoding can keep the original formula string, a padded vector of fixed bits and a pointer; for a whole bounded table, guess circuits for that language itself (also in NP), and translate oracle queries into it with polynomial length overhead. Induct on the fixed number of PP oracle levels: if level j equals NP then level j+1 is PP^NP=NP. Thus CH=NP; the arguments establish neither a uniform deterministic algorithm nor an unconditional separation. Contrapositively CH≠NP is sufficient for superpolynomial rank of the fixed cubic.

**Arbitrary coefficients with pairwise overlap allowed.** Suppose a list of r affine-supported quadratic atoms represents G exactly, and every address belongs to at most two of their supports. Then the *same list* admits coefficients of magnitude at most 2r+1. Each address imposes a linear equation in one or two distinct coefficient variables, with all nonzero entries ±1 and right side ±1. Regard binary equations as edges of a signed multigraph. In each connected component choose a root and a spanning tree. At vertex v the tree equations express c_v as σ_v c_root+d_v, σ_v=±1, |d_v|≤r-1. A unary equation fixes c_root to magnitude at most r. Any nontree binary equation either puts no condition on the root (the given system is consistent) or fixes twice its value to an integer of magnitude ≤2r-1. If there are no anchors choose root zero. Thus every coefficient has magnitude at most 2r+1, independently of its original precision. The CCZ extent bound from universal-cubic.md then gives r(2r+1)≥(4/3)^t. This is an exponential *coefficient-free* lower bound for representations with overlap at most two and permits signed cancellations. It holds for arbitrary supports as long as each nonzero atom entry is ±1 and each atom is stabilizer-normalizable as above. For overlap three, the purely linear-system lemma has a counterexample to polynomial normalizability: fix c₁=c₂=1 with unary rows and impose c_i-c_(i-1)-c_(i-2)=1. Unique coefficients grow exponentially. This is an obstruction for the abstract coefficient lemma; it does not construct these constraints from actual phase atoms on the cube.
