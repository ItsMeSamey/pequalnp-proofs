# Exact Fourier support of a cubic against affine quadratic atoms: sharp minimum by codimension

Let G_t be the sign of the sum of t disjoint triple products on V=GF(2)^(3t). Define the unnormalized Walsh transform by Fourier(H)(ω)=sum_x H(x)(-1)^(ω·x); normalization does not affect its support. For every nonzero atom A=1_U(-1)^q with affine support of codimension k and q quadratic,

|supp Fourier(G_t A)| ≥ 2^[k+3 max(t-k,0)].

For every integer 0≤k≤3t there is equality for some U,q. The result is independent of coefficient precision and addresses a single atom before cancellations. It strengthens the fidelity-only bound in this particular setting.

**Lemma 1 (top cubic parity).** For an arbitrary GF(2) polynomial f of degree at most three in 3r variables, let P be the parity of the number of unordered partitions of all 3r variables into triples whose cubic monomials have coefficient one. If P=1, *every* Walsh coefficient of (-1)^f has 2-adic valuation exactly r. Adding a linear character simply adjusts f's linear terms. Remove its global constant, affecting only sign. Expand its sign as the integer product over nonconstant ANF monomials M of (1−2x^M). A selection J of j monomials with union of size v contributes (-2)^j 2^(3r−v) when summed over all Boolean inputs. For j<r its valuation is at least 3r−2j>r, and for j>r it exceeds r as well. At j=r it equals r exactly for disjoint cubic monomials covering all variables. The sum of their unit contributions is odd by hypothesis. The r=0 convention is the unit transform in zero dimensions.

**Lemma 2 (restriction minor).** For V of dimension 3t let T be the alternating trilinear third derivative of the G_t cubic. The parity-of-triple-partitions top form evaluated on any basis equals 1 (see cubic-orbits.md). The cube-free cubic coefficient in coordinates i,j,k equals T(e_i,e_j,e_k); this remains true after any affine coordinate substitution and addition of a quadratic. Let D be any linear subspace of codimension k<t and extend its basis by k outside vectors. In the partition expansion of the top form, group summands by the exact set of triples touching an outside index. For each such group the contribution factors into the product on those specified triples times the top partition sum on the unoccupied interior indices. Since the total is nonzero, at least one such interior partition sum is 1. There are at least t−k remaining triples because at most k triples touch an outside index. If r>t−k triples remain in this nonzero interior minor, expand according to the partners of a fixed index: some triple has value 1 and its deleted complement still has nonzero partition sum. Iterate. We obtain a basis subset of 3(t−k) vectors in D whose cubic top partition sum is 1.

**Proof of the profile.** Parameterize U affinely by D. For k<t take the subset from Lemma 2, restricting the function on U to the span of those vectors through a fixed basepoint in U. Lemma 1 gives 2^(3(t−k)) distinct Walsh frequencies on this smaller domain. Restricting to an affine subspace folds the old Fourier expansion, so it cannot increase the number of nonzero characters. Thus the intrinsic transform on U has at least this many entries. Viewing a function on U as zero off U repeats each intrinsic frequency across a fiber of the dual restriction map of cardinality 2^k, differing only by signs. This proves the bound; for k≥t use the same repetition and one nonzero intrinsic coefficient. For k≤t, fix one first coordinate to zero in each of k disjoint cubic blocks and let q=0. The other t−k blocks retain their full Walsh support, and the 2k unused free coordinates contribute just one frequency. For k≥t take U inside the codimension-t coset on which all first coordinates are zero, and again q=0. This attains the profile exactly.

**Invariant span.** If a real or complex space S of functions is invariant under all twisted translations U_a and contains a nonzero atom, multiplying by G_t turns it into a translation-invariant space containing G_t A. Every Walsh character on the support of that function belongs to the space: take its signed average of all translates to project onto that character. The dimension is at least the number of such characters, hence at least 2^t by the profile. The coset-atom construction of cubic-orbits.md yields an invariant space of precisely 2^t dimensions and contains G_t. Consequently any decomposition whose *span* is invariant requires ≥2^t terms, without requiring that its dictionary be permuted by translations. The positivity of stabilizer extent is unnecessary for this proof. Ordinary stabilizer rank has no such invariance condition.
