# Recognizing and counting affine guards with sparse image generators

Input consists of an affine map y=b+Mx from N binary seed variables into m ordered pairs of bits and a forbidden pair per guard. Let d=rank M over F₂. Fix a constant h. Enumerate all nonzero words supported on at most h output pairs by choosing their blocks and one of three nonzero pairs at each. Test membership in im M by Gaussian elimination and retain a maximal independent set. At most sum_{j<=h} binom(m,j)3^j candidates require testing. The test succeeds exactly when im M is generated by such words. Each accepted column has a preimage recoverable by solving Mx=w. The criterion is invariant under invertible changes of seed coordinates. In particular, a hidden presentation generated from a four-occurrence NAE formula satisfies it for h=4.

On success, use d independent generator coordinates as variables. Their total block weight is at most hd, so their total bit support is at most 2hd. Check constant guards and discard them when satisfied. There are at most hd surviving guards. Express every nonconstant output bit as a parity of its generator coordinates with an offset, compiling a parity chain using a fresh variable per binary XOR and four clauses of width three per gate. Complements are literal signs. Require each output pair to differ from its forbidden pair by one width-two clause, simplifying constant literals. All introduced variables are uniquely determined. The compiled formula has d to O_h(d) declared variables and O_h(d) clauses. Its model count times 2^(N−d) equals the original seed model count; a model lifts through the generator preimages. Bit complexity of multiplying by this power of two and output is polynomial in the explicit input. d=0 is checked directly.

The October fixed-width #SAT theorem for constant clause/variable ratio gives deterministic poly(input)·2^{O_h(d/sqrt(log(d+2)))} bit time and polynomial space for fixed h, including recognition. Witnesses can be extracted by at most O(d) repeated counting calls with additional unit clauses; their ratio stays bounded.
