# Constructing guarded witnesses on free linear right variables

Let x consist of p independent binary pairs, each allowed three of four patterns. The uniformly random guarded x satisfies any consistent affine linear test Lx=d of binary rank R with probability at most (2/3)^R. To see this, expose a pair. If deleting its columns drops rank by v, fixing the two bits must obey v independent projected constraints before the remaining variables can complete the test. The fractions of the three pair values permitting completion are at most 1,2/3,1/3 for v=0,1,2. Apply inductively to the remaining columns, whose consistency can be tested by elimination.

Suppose the other variables z are unrestricted and the t constraints have the form f_j(x,z)=xᵀM_jz+b_jᵀz+g_j(x), where each g_j can be evaluated in polynomial time. For λ≠0 the left-kernel event for the affine z-coefficient matrix is precisely M_λᵀx=b_λ. Thus if Σ_{λ≠0}(2/3)^{rank M_λ}<1, some guarded x yields full row rank and all target syndromes are attainable. A deterministic construction chooses each guarded pair by conditional pessimistic potentials. For the current prefix, charge each λ zero if its remaining affine test is inconsistent; otherwise charge (2/3)^rank(remaining M_λ columns). The initial sum is below one. The mean child potential is at most its parent by the rank-drop argument. Choose the minimum child. At termination the potential is integral and hence zero. Solve for z by elimination for the specified target. Computing the charges by successive eliminations takes O(p 2^t poly(input)) deterministic bit operations with polynomial space; denominators are powers of three of exponent at most 2p. In particular t logarithmic suffices for a polynomial-time witness algorithm with arbitrarily many guarded pairs. Positive clauses x_i∨x_j and negative clauses ¬x_i∨¬x_j both fit. Coupled guards or nonlinear z terms fall outside the proof. The concentration bound was exhaustively checked on all 4368 ordered two-row maps of one to three guard pairs. This theorem does not assume or construct a polynomial general-SAT solver.
