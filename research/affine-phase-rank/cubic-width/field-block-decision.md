# Intrinsic field-block decision
Each canonical fan-in-two gate has polar rank at most two, unchanged or lowered on affine restrictions. For R>2 the bad multiplier space must contain every coordinate direction and is full. Invertible output mixing preserves such a basis.
First solve all affine equation combinations found as the kernel of s↦Σs_jP_j, parameterize and retain a complementary equation basis. Then compute the common polar radical D. Centering at zero gives q(x+d)=q(x)+L(d) with linear L on D. Restrict inputs to a complement and quotient outputs by L(D), including the target. Each reduced solution lifts 2^{dim D−rank L} ways. Repeat strict reductions to closure. These polynomial steps extract linear checks on affine-consumed AND outputs without supplied wire groupings in the explicit quadratic system. Obtaining that presentation from CNF is encoding-dependent.
On the remaining bit space take a nonzero singular generator P if present. Compute K=ker P and L its common orthogonal complement for every input form. If K⊕L is the entire space split and recurse, otherwise abstain. On any hidden orthogonal direct sum of nonsingular field pencils K is exactly the sum of components where this generator vanishes, so the common complement is the remaining sum. This handles instances with no globally nonsingular binary combination.
At a block with nonsingular nonzero generators pick one anchor P0. Compute the unital algebra generated by T_j=P0^{-1}P_j. Test commutation and injectivity of Frobenius a↦a²; abstain if either fails. Split on nontrivial idempotents found by binary elimination of a²+a=0, recursively using eA and (1−e)A. A finite reduced commutative component having only 0 and 1 as idempotents is a field (every nonzero element has a nonzero idempotent power). Commuting P0-selfadjoint matrices remain selfadjoint under products. The idempotent images are therefore orthogonal for all P_j and P0 restricts nonsingularly. On a promised direct sum of field pencils the ratio algebra is a subalgebra of a product of fields; identical representations may merge without affecting nonsingularity of nonzero component forms. Treat zero pencils as affine maps.
On each final component V_i of dimension n_i compute W_i, the span of the vector-valued polar evaluations on basis pairs, of dimension r_i. The centered map mod W_i is linear; compute its rank e_i. Each functional nonzero on W_i gives a nonsingular local polar, since its ratio to P0 is a nonzero field scalar. On each nonempty linear quotient fiber the polar rank drops by at most 2e_i. If n_i−2e_i≥2r_i every possible W_i output on each quotient fiber occurs, because the sum of absolute normalized nontrivial quadratic correlations is ≤(2^{r_i}−1)2^{−(n_i−2e_i)/2}<1. The local image is exactly the span of W_i and lifts of the linear quotient image. If e_i=0 the condition is automatic: the Pfaffian on the r_i-dimensional nonsingular pencil has degree ≤n_i/2 and is the nonzero indicator on binary r_i-vectors, a Boolean function of degree r_i. Thus 2r_i≤n_i. Restore constants once.
If all tests succeed, there are no cross-component polar terms, so the global image is an explicit affine sum. Gaussian elimination decides membership of the desired target. Procedures operate in algebras of dimension ≤n² with ≤n bit components; all have polynomial bit time and space, without supplied coordinates, pivots or a field certificate. This need not compute fiber sizes. Multiplication XY in a hidden binary field followed by independent linear output checks is a positive example after eliminating private AND wires; direct sums can share output coordinates. Rectangular disjoint products with multipliers s1,s2,s1+s2 illustrate why a nonsingular global combination is unnecessary. The algorithm may abstain away from its recognized class.
