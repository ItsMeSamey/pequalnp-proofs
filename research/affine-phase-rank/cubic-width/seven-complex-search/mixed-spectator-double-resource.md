# A GHZ and two Bell resources with one common link

Take BC as the Bell link and A as spectator. We exclude a seven-ray list with five ordinary three-party stabilizer products and two exceptions of either of these types:

* A simple GHZ together with a BC double Bell.
* A simple BC Bell together with a GHZ times a BC Bell.

Local Clifford encoders are independent. Call the exception expanding into four normal-form product terms *high* and the other, expanding into two, *low*. Each pair of distinct high expansion terms has linearly independent local factors on B and on C. All four of their B vectors, and separately their C vectors, are independent in proper dimension-four stabilizer codes. The A factor of each high term occurs also in a different high term: it is constant for a double Bell and repeats twice for GHZ times Bell. The low pair also differs on both B and C.

**The spectator-party span has dimension three.** Expand both exceptions. The target plus eleven product vectors have a linear relation with nonzero coefficients on all entries. Let d_A,d_B,d_C denote the local spans including g. If the collection is connected, its rank is at most eleven, so the [connected product splitting bound](single-bell-ghz-exclusions.md) gives d_A+d_B+d_C≤13. Both d_B,d_C are at least five, since g lies outside each proper high code. The GHZ code and g give d_A≥3.

Otherwise the target component contains at least eight products by the [eight-product minimum](seven-product-exclusion.md). Every other component carries a projected nonzero-coefficient relation, so there is exactly one other component and its size s is two or three. It contains at most one high term: the span of two high terms with independent B and C directions contains no third distinct product direction (flatten across B); adding their scalar multiple instead cannot make the three vectors a connected dependent component. If no high term were outside, local spans d'_B,d'_C in the target component would still each have dimension at least five. That component's rank is at most 11−s, so splitting forces d'_A≤3−s≤1, impossible since g and any stabilizer A factor are independent. Thus exactly one high term lies outside. The other three high terms with g make d'_B,d'_C≥4, whence d'_A≤5−s.

For s=2 the outside vectors are proportional, and the high term's A ray still occurs in the target component. Hence d_A=d'_A≤3. For s=3 one has d'_A≤2. If the high resource includes GHZ, its three surviving terms still contain both logical GHZ directions on A, giving d'_A≥3, a contradiction. Otherwise the low resource is GHZ and at least one of its products lies outside, again because its two logical directions and g would give d'_A≥3. At most one low term can be outside by the same independent B,C factor argument. Consequently the three outside terms are one high, one low and one ordinary. Their projected relation puts the ordinary A factor in the span of the high and low A factors. The high A ray still occurs inside the target component, so d_A≤d'_A+1≤3. This establishes d_A=3 in every case.

**Erasing the spectator.** Write K for the two-dimensional GHZ-side Schmidt code on A (the Bell-link spectator of the other exception has local ray h). Then the entire A span is K+span(g). Use two ordinary covectors: f₀ annihilates K and retains g; f₁ annihilates all exterior stabilizer types in this three-space and retains g. Existence of f₁ follows from [the reviewed local three-space certificate](single-bell-ghz-exclusions.md).

If h is exterior, the f₀ contraction expresses two CCZ copies using at most one stabilizer for the spectator Bell plus the exterior ordinary terms; the two-copy lower bound four requires at least three ordinary terms exterior. Thus f₁ kills the spectator Bell and leaves at most two ordinary terms. For simple GHZ the other exception contributes at most two stabilizer products. All four must survive, forming a minimum four-product circuit. That pair spans both GHZ codes locally, so d_B,d_C≥3 despite the circuit bound d_B+d_C≤5. For GHZ times Bell the nonzero contracted exception has ordinary matrix rank at least two, since it includes an intact Bell and a nonzero diagonal GHZ slice. Its row and column supports lie in proper codes excluding g. Subtracting it from the two-copy product target increases its ordinary matrix rank by one; at most two ordinary products cannot give this difference. The zero contraction also leaves too few terms.

If h lies in K, f₀ kills both exceptions and requires at least four exterior ordinary terms. Hence f₁ leaves at most one ordinary term. A surviving double Bell has rank four; the target, the one ordinary term and the two products of the contracted simple GHZ together give at most four rank-one matrices. Equality of the rank would require g in the double Bell's proper B support, since the column span of the four rank-one summands would equal that support. If the double Bell is zero at most three stabilizers remain. In the other profile the contracted GHZ times Bell has rank four when both GHZ logical coefficients survive, since its two Bell blocks have independent local supports. The same rank-four argument applies with the target, one ordinary term and two products from the simple spectator Bell. If at most one GHZ coefficient survives, this exception is zero or a scalar times one stabilizer Bell with fixed logical labels. The simple Bell and one ordinary product then give at most three stabilizers in all, again impossible.

This proof uses no new finite census. The two-copy unrestricted lower bound four and the local three-space certificate are shared with the [simple GHZ and Bell proof](two-simple-ghz-bell-exclusion.md).
