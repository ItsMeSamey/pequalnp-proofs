# Independently encoded GHZ-plus-Bell and two-link-path exceptions

Start from a hypothetical seven-ray CCZ cube comprising five ordinary products and these two exceptions. The path center is the party shared by its two Bell links. The mixed stabilizer has one GHZ shared among all three parties and one Bell at two of them. We use complex product expansions for dimension bounds only; no claim that all branches in those expansions are stabilizers is needed.

First split the mixed stabilizer on its canonical GHZ bit, retaining the Bell unexpanded in each sector. The resulting coarse list consists of the target, five ordinary products, one intact path, and two sector stabilizers. No proper nonempty subcollection can have weighted sum zero: the target side would require at least eight vectors including the target (three-copy stabilizer rank at least seven), leaving at most one on the other side, whose term is nonzero.

Expand the Bell pair in each mixed sector independently in a variable orthonormal Schmidt basis. Expand the two Bell links of the path in independently variable bases. This gives a total of thirteen weighted products and the target. It is possible to make the resulting product collection connected. Indeed any disconnected component would have a vanishing proper subsum with the inherited weights. A strict nonempty part of the two branches of a mixed sector varies nontrivially with its independent basis parameter. A strict nonempty subset of the four path branches also varies: under vectorization of the two-qubit identity, its sum is a product-unitary conjugate of the diagonal indicator of the subset. A nonconstant diagonal indicator fails to commute with some product unitary (already a one-coordinate bit flip suffices). Fixed local encoders preserve nonconstancy. Therefore a proper zero subsum which is identically zero in all parameters would have to be a coarse zero subsum, already excluded. There are finitely many proper subsums, and the nonidentically zero real analytic conditions cannot vanish on any open subset of the connected product of unitary groups. Take parameters outside all their zero sets.

Use this connected expansion in the generic rectangle bound of simple-ghz-path-rectangle.md. Across its center cut the path supplies a full 2×2 remote rectangle. Sylvester across the center plus connected splitting on the remote products then give d_A+d_B+d_C+1≤13+2, hence local dimension sum at most fourteen. Local spans are the spans of the actual marginal Schmidt codes of both exceptions, the ordinary factors and g; they do not depend on expansion bases. A party with a four-dimensional marginal code has local dimension at least five since g belongs to no proper stabilizer code. If the path is centered at the mixed GHZ spectator, every party has such a code, contradicting the sum bound. This excludes profile (0011,1100).

For the other orientation let A be the path center and a mixed Bell endpoint, B be the other mixed Bell endpoint, and C the mixed spectator. Suppose d_C=3. The two local qubit codes there coincide by the local three-space certificate in single-bell-ghz-exclusions.md. Killing this code and retaining g shows at least four ordinary factors are exterior. The same certificate gives a covector killing all exterior factors while retaining g, so at most one ordinary term survives contraction at C.

The resulting path is zero or rank two across A|B, with full two-dimensional Bell support on B and A support inside its fixed four-dimensional code. The contracted mixed state is zero, rank two in one surviving GHZ sector, or rank four across both disjoint sectors. If mixed rank is four, its difference with the rank-one target has rank five because g is outside both Schmidt codes. Path plus one product has rank at most three. If mixed vanishes, the same independent-support argument for a surviving path gives rank three for target minus path, impossible with one ordinary product; zero path as well is also impossible since g⊗g is not an ordinary stabilizer product. Therefore exactly one mixed sector survives, of rank two. Its difference with the target has rank three, so the path and ordinary product must both survive. The B support of their sum is span(g,B_mixedSector) and contains the full two-dimensional path Bell code (rank three implies its row space is the sum of the two and one-dimensional row spaces). The two qubit codes in this three-space through g must coincide by the local census. Kill the common B code retaining g; only one ordinary product is left to give the nonstabilizer g on A. This contradiction excludes d_C=3.

The only remaining orientation (0011,0110) thus has d_C≥4 and d_A,d_B≥5. The sum bound gives exactly (5,5,4). In particular the two four-dimensional A Schmidt codes are identical: they intersect in dimension at least three inside the five-space, and the nonempty intersection of stabilizer codes is a power-of-two-dimensional stabilizer code. This is a necessary encoder condition, not a full exclusion of the remaining orientation.
