# Four external weights fixed by one unchanged plane

Use the control-Walsh chart and a common scale making every native coefficient one. Suppose after subtracting at most three native rays, the residual is a sum of four external rays. Pick an affine plane of four unchanged frequencies among the at least five unchanged ones. Normalize each external column to entries zero or fourth roots of unity by absorbing its scalar. Pin the discarded data pair to 00 as in four-ray-plane-tests.md. The restricted four-column representation gives a W2 vector with four unit coefficients and hence rank four. Select any four independent evaluation rows of the restriction. Entries of this square system are zero or fourth roots, and its target entries are real signs. Cramer's rule writes the external weights as N_i/D with a common nonzero Gaussian integer D. Every numerator and D has modulus at most 16 by Hadamard. The unknown residual coefficient at each remaining control is obtained at data zero and hence has numerator of modulus at most 64 over D. Its value may be zero or nonzero.

For a supplied four-column candidate and marked unchanged set, the remaining checks work over F_(131²) with i²=−1, since 131 is prime and 3 mod 4. Four-column minors of the pinned unchanged plane have modulus at most 16, so its complex rank equals four exactly when its field rank equals four. Use nonsingular rows to recover the field weights. Require the original native signs on all unchanged controls, and require the chirp-adjusted values to be constant over data on every unmarked control. After clearing D the marked discrepancy has magnitude at most 80, and an unmarked difference at most 128. A Gaussian integer that small maps to zero precisely when it is zero integrally. Exteriority, if required, is checked on the supplied rays. This is a finite certification test on specified columns, not an enumeration of columns.
