# Exact complex stabilizer rank from paired Pauli outcomes

Apply one fixed Clifford, including its phase convention, sending a chosen nonidentity Pauli measurement to the first computational bit. Write the resulting state as |0>u+|1>v. In each branch use canonical raw stabilizer columns (fourth roots or zero, first nonzero entry one). An occurrence in a branch decomposition has a key consisting of the unsigned stabilizer group of its ray and the class of its nonzero complex coefficient modulo multiplication by a fourth root of unity. For arbitrary finite decompositions of u,v let m_+(k),m_-(k) count occurrences of each key; repeated ray directions are allowed. The exact unrestricted complex stabilizer rank is

[
 \\min_{\\text{two branch decompositions}}\\ \\sum_k\\max(m_+(k),m_-(k)).
]

A parent ray can give just one branch, or both. If both survive they have the same unsigned stabilizer group and equal coefficient keys: a stabilizer generator flipping the measured bit interchanges the branches by a Pauli with fourth-root phase. Using raw columns gives identical gauge magnitudes because the supports have equal size. Thus any parent list yields branch decompositions whose displayed cost is at most its size.

Conversely two occurrences of the same key, one on each side, glue to a single stabilizer. A Pauli sends the first canonical ray to the second up to a fourth root: the two joint eigenvalue characters on the same maximal commuting unsigned group differ by a character induced by a physical Pauli. The phase needed for the two coefficients belongs to the fourth roots. Apply a controlled Pauli and a one-qubit Clifford phase to |+> times the first branch ray, absorbing the scalar normalization. Pair as many occurrences within each key as possible. The leftover rays have their first qubit pinned. The resulting parent list realizes exactly the stated cost and the actual two branch vectors. Minimization over all finite lists gives equality. An optimal parent supplies finite minimizers. Neither branch need use a shortest decomposition by itself.

For a seven-ray candidate whose two rays share the positive signed Pauli, branch lists of lengths at most seven and five, respectively, suffice, since two parent rays vanish in the negative outcome. In a rank-seven target their occurrence counts must have displayed cost exactly seven, and the positive branch has at least two unmatched occurrences under maximal pairing for lists induced by that parent: any further pairing beyond the parent's own pairs would reduce its length. Conversely such exact branch lists construct the original state and a parent with the stated sharing. This is a characterization; enumerating complex branch decompositions remains a separate problem. For prescribed branch ray slots and a proposed matching, fix the relative fourth-root for each pair and test the two simultaneous systems over the Gaussian rationals when the data are Gaussian rational. Dependent columns and unequal coefficients in repeated directions must remain allowed. A full candidate can be checked in all original coordinates after undoing the measurement Clifford.
