# Every fully active plane tests a four-ray candidate

Let c be any complex coefficient table on the 3-control cube, and suppose c_s times the native chirp with exponent sum_i s_i x_i y_i is a sum of four stabilizer rays in the control-Walsh chart. On every affine control plane P with four nonzero coefficient entries, pick any j participating in its defining normal. Pin controls to P and contract the two data qubits of block j with any stabilizer bra having nonzero overlaps with both local chirps 1 and (-1)^(x_j y_j) (only occupied s_j values matter). The other two controls freely parametrize P. Erase the redundant control by an affine Clifford.

This gives a W2 vector with four nonzero weights and thus rank four. All four contracted atoms must be nonzero and linearly independent for EVERY such P, j, bra. Thus a coincidence up to scalar between two surviving rays already rejects the candidate. A four-by-four Gram matrix suffices for finished projections. Entanglement and the phases on other indices are unrestricted.

A further support consequence uses an affine substitution rather than a fixed bra. Each atom's data-fiber support direction must contain the whole two-dimensional j-block. If a direction had a normal with a nonzero component on one j bit, extend the associated fiber parity equation to n·d=l(s) on the atom using an affine extension l of its RHS from its control projection. On P set the other j data bit to zero, solve for the first as an affine function of all remaining free controls and data so n·d=l(s)+1, then delete both. This graph substitution preserves stabilizer atoms, kills the offending column, and zeroes the discarded quadratic product. The resulting W2 target still has four nonzero weights, a contradiction. Intersect these requirements over all eligible planes and blocks. When all eight entries are nonzero every atom has full data direction, and also full control occurrence, because a proper control projection misses its complementary plane. Thus every atom in this case has full support on all nine variables.

After at most three native terms have been subtracted from the uniform eight-term target, at least five entries retain the same nonzero coefficient. Five cube indices always contain a plane: ten unordered pairs have only seven potential nonzero differences, and two distinct pairs with the same difference are disjoint and form a plane. Thus the tests always apply, including when some or all modified entries are nonzero. When exactly five entries survive this plane is unique since two distinct planes have union size at least six.
