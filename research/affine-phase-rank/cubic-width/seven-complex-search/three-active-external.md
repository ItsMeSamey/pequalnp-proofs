# Three occupied Fourier channels on two blocks

In W₂ any vector with three nonzero Fourier coefficients has stabilizer rank three. Every three-ray representation contains a native ray. Thus there is no example with three genuinely external rays on these two blocks, for any choice of nonzero complex coefficients. Mixed examples exist as described below. The argument uses the two-full-chirp rigidity and two-external secant theorem in fourier-external-secants.md. It does not assert the analogous exteriority statement after reducing a larger ambient problem to two blocks.

Work in the control-Walsh chart. By translating controls and stripping the resulting common real quadratic assume active support T={00,10,01}, with inactive index z=11. At active s the data slice is c_s times a full real chirp of polar B_s=s₁E₁+s₂E₂, where E_i is the standard alternating form of block i, image U_i in the four-dimensional data dual. A global atom's control domain is affine. Its nonempty data fibers have fixed direction with offsets affine in control. The stripped real data polar and the true symmetric Z₄ data polar are fixed across controls. In what follows “full type” concerns its data fibers, not its control domain. On a translate where one or two full terms give a full real target, each participating term has the target's stripped real polar there (two-secant lemma). The same statement applies on a common affine restriction. Suppose all three rays external. We eliminate the cases by fiber type.

**All three proper.** Connect two types if independent data normals can be chosen, one annihilating each type's fiber direction. A pair fails to connect exactly for two hyperplane types with the same normal. If all three pairs connect, the third type must occur on every active index: on a translate avoiding the other two, it must supply the target. Thus all three control domains are full and all three occur on z. Since they sum to zero there, each inactive fiber lies in the union of the others. If no fiber is contained in another, each is partitioned by its intersections with the other two into complementary halfspaces. (Two proper subflats can cover an affine space only as complementary hyperplanes.) Their dimensions are equal; covering active data forces each to have dimension three. The normals are distinct and sum to zero: independent normals would give a triple intersection, impossible for these half partitions. At any control their coverage has either odd multiplicity everywhere, or even multiplicity everywhere and a missing quarter, according to an affine parity of the three offsets. Odd coverage necessarily has singleton points and cannot occur at z; even coverage cannot occur on an active full target. An affine bit cannot separate three of four controls from the fourth. If a containment occurs, the inclusion and union condition put all inactive fibers in a single largest one. A repeated largest one would have to be a hyperplane by active coverage, violating all-pair connectivity. A unique largest is covered by its two complementary halves; active coverage forces sizes 8,4,4. Every active slice must have disjoint supports; each small fiber is opposite the large hyperplane on every active control but inside it on z, impossible for affine offsets.

If exactly two pairs connect, the nonconnecting pair are parallel hyperplanes both of full control domain by the connected-pair argument. At inactive z they must coincide: their complementary union could not be canceled by a third proper fiber. On every active control they must be complementary: otherwise the third proper fiber would need to cover the opposite half and would itself be a parallel hyperplane, inconsistent with two connecting pairs. This again demands a 3-of-4 affine bit. Exactly one connecting pair is impossible by the characterization of nonconnection. If no pairs connect, all are parallel hyperplanes. Every active index uses at least two atoms, and each half has at most two present there. The two-term lemma puts every participating polar on H, their common direction, in the same class B_s|H. Just one class can have at least two atoms. Its preimage has size at most two since rank B_w=2wt(w) and only weights ≤1 can vanish on H×H.

**One proper and two full types.** Choose a hyperplane direction H containing the proper directions, and restrict on each active slice to the half avoiding the proper P. The two full types must have two distinct restricted polar classes, with preimages cosets of one common dimension-at-most-one kernel. Otherwise three active indices would be impossible. Thus one full type A alone among the full types can serve the adjacent pair s,s′ in one class; B alone among full types serves remaining r. The difference s+s′=e_i and H's normal belongs to U_i. At inactive z neither A nor B can occur: cancellation on a half off P requires both with equal restricted polars, or neither. Then P also cannot occur. B is supported only on r and must be corrected by P there, else B native. P cannot attend both s,s′ (its affine domain would include z), but must attend one, since A cannot exactly match two different polars. A at the uncorrected slice s′ is fully real up to scalar and has polar B_s′. At s outside P it agrees with target. The defect is supported precisely on P, and its Boolean sign difference after scalar normalization has polar E_i. Thus the affine support indicator has degree exactly two, so codimension two; its two normals span U_i by their top wedge. The proper directions kill both block-i coordinates and leave the other block free. But on P at both s and r the proper chirp restriction must have the same real polar as the target (the other participating term is full), impossible since those controls differ on block j≠i.

**Two proper and one full type.** Independent normals for the two proper types would force full A to occur on every active control and hence inactive z, which cannot be canceled on a translate avoiding both proper types. Thus both proper types P,Q are parallel hyperplanes of direction H. A cannot occur at all three active controls: one half meets at most one proper there, so B_s|H would be fixed. Nor can A be absent on all active controls: complementary proper halves would enforce a single restricted class. On A-absent active slices P,Q cover complementary halves alone, so both restricted stripped polars are R₀ and both have zero data imaginary parity along H. On A-present active slices A's fixed restricted polar is R₁=B_s|H (use a half with at most one proper). R₁≠R₀ follows since one class cannot contain the three points. A cannot occur at z: its cancellation would require both complementary proper halves with restricted polar equal to A's. Thus A covers either one or two active indices, and there are no other controls in its domain. One of the two restricted classes contains an adjacent pair differing along e_i. The normal of H therefore lies in U_i. Pick local coordinates (X_i,Y_i) with X_i this normal; the native bit's mixed term is still X_iY_i up to affine terms. Write j for the other block. R₀+R₁ on H is the rank-two block form E_j restricted to H, with image U_j.

At each A-present active index P,Q must be both absent or coincident on a single half: any half with exactly one proper forces its polar R₀ to be R₁. On a half without proper terms A matches target alone. On the other half A also has zero imaginary parity along H and its frequency-domain support after stripping R₀ is, like the target, a four-point plane of direction U_j. If there is a nonzero correction from the coincident P,Q, the two Fourier planes must coincide since the correction is at most two characters and distinct parallel planes are disjoint. The correction in fact occupies exactly two points: on the common Fourier plane the two quadratic chirps have the same polar and differ by scalar times a real linear character (translation of a real quadratic Walsh spectrum). Thus either all four values survive, none survive, or exactly two survive. Consequently the frequency difference d(s) of P,Q is a nonzero vector in U_j, and the *data linear phase difference* between A and the target on that half also lies in U_j by coincidence of the Fourier planes.

The difference d(s) is affine in controls on the common domain, obtained by parametrizing each proper half by the same free Y_i and block-j variables, then stripping R₀. This follows from restricting the global quadratic phases with their affine half offsets; their data parity on H is zero. On an A-absent active r, complementary halves alone match the target, giving d(r)=r_i Y_i*; common affine parts of the native bit cancel. At z, if both proper terms occur, they are on the same half and cancel, giving d(z)=0.

If A covers just s, the two proper atoms must supply a nonzero correction there or A would be native. Both then occur on all controls, and the remaining active pair r,r′ differs along i. Affinity gives d(s)=d(r)+d(r′)+d(z)=Y_i*, contradicting d(s)∈U_j. If A covers s,s′, differing along i, P,Q cannot occur on both: they would then be global and their half-offset difference would be one only on the single A-absent active control, and zero elsewhere. At least one correction is necessary since A cannot match both exactly. At the uncorrected s′ the atom A fixes its data polar to B_s′ and has zero data imaginary parity. At corrected s its relative polar to the target is E_i. On the half where A matches alone the relative data linear phase is constant; passing to the other half changes its linear coefficient by Y_i*, contradicting membership in U_j established above.

**All full-data types, inactive empty.** Each atom occurs on ≤2 active controls. If none has triple coverage then each atom can occur at only one active control by the two-term rigidity and distinct B_s; each must then be a native ray. Two triply covered indices are impossible since each domain would be just their two-point line and could not reach the third. With a single triply covered index u, the other indices v,w have disjoint nonempty sets of present terms by rigidity. If both singletons, those atoms are real-data up to scalar with fixed polars B_v,B_w. At u divide by the target. The two ratios are real signs up to scalar and jointly take all four sign values: each of the four Boolean joint-level indicators has nonzero homogeneous degree-four coefficient since the two polar combinations B_v+B_u and B_w+B_u have independent coefficient vectors on E₁,E₂. The remaining full chirp would require |1−aX−bY| constant for all four signs X,Y, which forces Re a=Re b=Re(a conjugate b)=0, impossible for nonzero a,b. If two atoms attend v and only one attends w, strip B_v then Fourier transform data on u. The v-pair contributes at most four frequencies from its two edges/singletons. The target and the real w-atom have respective directions given by the images of B_u+B_v and B_w+B_v. Either their support sizes are 16,4, or both are independent four-point affine planes meeting in at most one point. Their difference has ≥6 nonzero entries in either case, impossible.

**All full-data types, inactive nonempty.** At z cancellation requires at least two atoms with equal true symmetric data polar M. For two present this is immediate. For three, divide by one and use the two-circle root lemma: both ratios are constant or const·i^{kℓ} for the same affine bit ℓ, so the three symmetric polars lie in a two-element set. Strip a global extension of the data chirp of one common-polar atom. The pair A,B now consists of ordinary characters on each occurring slice (a Z₄ chirp of zero polar is a real character up to scalar). In the data Fourier chart the remaining C has support a flat of size 2^r and fixed direction, r its relative symmetric polar rank. The target has support a flat of size 2^rank(D_s), direction im D_s, D_s=M+B_s at each active s. These standard quadratic Fourier facts follow by translating along the kernel of the symmetric polar and Plancherel, or squaring the Fourier sum by shifts. The two frequency masks (target and C) differ on at most two occupied points when C is present.

For r≥2, C cannot occur at z. It occurs on at most two active indices. At any active t absent from C, D_t has rank≤1; as its diagonal is ℓ=diag M, this means D_t=ℓℓᵀ (including zero), uniquely fixing B_t. C therefore occurs on precisely two active indices. If r≥3, their C and target frequency flats must have the same size and direction, since two affine flats with one size≥8 and symmetric difference at most two must coincide. If t is a leaf, the other indices' differences from t have weights one and two, yielding ranks ≤3 and exactly four for D_s=B_{s+t}+ℓℓᵀ. A rank-one symmetric update to a nonsingular alternating matrix leaves it nonsingular since ℓᵀJ⁻¹ℓ=0. If t is the central 00, on the two leaves the D_s images, if of rank≥3, are U₁+⟨ℓ⟩ and U₂+⟨ℓ⟩, which cannot be the same 3-space. For r=2 the target support≤6 forces rank D_s≤2 and excludes t a leaf. Then t=00 and ℓ must belong to both U_i to keep both leaf ranks ≤2, hence ℓ=0. The target has independent four-point frequency directions on the leaves while C has a fixed four-point direction; unequal four-flats cannot differ by only two points.

For r≤1, all D_s have rank at most two. The two leaf matrices sum to B_11 of rank four; hence both have rank two with disjoint images. If r=0, three singleton frequencies cannot cover four. If r=1, C's two-point edge and two singletons can cover the four target frequencies only if its direction belongs to both disjoint leaf images. This finishes the proof.

**Sharp mixed construction.** With any adjacent occupied pair having coefficient ratio ω∈{1,−1,i,−i}, the canonical two-ray split of χ_s+ωχ_{s+e_i} along X_i=0,1 gives two exterior atoms. Keep the third occupied native ray. This is an exact three-ray construction with two exterior rays. Conversely, once a native ray occurs in a three-ray list, subtract it. The two-external theorem forces two remaining exterior rays to correct ≤2 adjacent coordinates with fourth-root ratio. The native must therefore match one occupied coefficient exactly. Two native and one genuinely external cannot sum into W. Thus this describes precisely the possible three-ray *native/exterior counts and coefficient conditions*, without claiming uniqueness of exterior pairs.
