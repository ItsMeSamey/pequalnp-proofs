# Any five-ray saving determines its projective coefficients

Let five arbitrary stabilizer rays span a W_t vector with m≥6 active native frequencies. The rays are independent (otherwise four suffice) and their span meets W_t in exactly one projective direction.

If not, take a two-dimensional coefficient plane through the representation consisting of W_t vectors. Every atom coordinate is a nonzero linear functional there since all five participate. Group the five functionals by their projective zero points with sizes r_1,...,r_p. At least two points occur since otherwise a nonzero vector on this plane has every coordinate zero. At point i at most 5−r_i≤4 rays remain, so at most 5−r_i Fourier coefficients survive by the one-through-four-ray results. Each of the original m marked frequencies is a nonzero linear functional on this plane and vanishes on at most one of its p points. Consequently (p−1)m ≤ sum_i(5−r_i)=5(p−1), impossible.

In the control-Walsh chart normalize each column to zero or fourth roots of unity. To belong to W its values divided by the native sign must agree inside each control fiber. Every equation row on the weights is in (D−D)^5, D={0,1,−1,i,−i}. The kernel has dimension one by the result above. Choose four independent rows and take their signed maximal minors. This yields Gaussian integer weights each of modulus at most 4^4=256 by Hadamard. Each Fourier coefficient then has modulus at most 1280. At most 13^20 ordered choices for the four equation rows suffice regardless of t. These are necessary conditions.

For six rays and m≥7, if a two-dimensional coefficient plane through a full-use representation exists, at least one column-killing point must leave five rays with at least six frequencies (or such a saving is already present by dependence). Otherwise every zero point contributes at most 6−r_i, using the four-ray theorem whenever r_i≥2. The same counting gives m≤6. A one-dimensional six-ray coefficient kernel has no such implication.
