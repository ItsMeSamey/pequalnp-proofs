# Support restrictions on a rank-one exceptional quotient in a one-erasure chart

Use a fixed chart in one-erasure-exact-secants.md with remote space R. Its exceptional quotient columns are all nonzero. Suppose their span is one-dimensional and that a physical completion exists. Write S_i for exceptional local X support codes. Any stabilizer in X⊗R is a block product whose remote ray is one of the four chart products, as proved in the lifting note.

**Unequal X codes.** If S_j is not contained in S_i, there is a signed X check of S_i whose opposite projector acts nontrivially on E_j. That projector kills E_i. It commutes with the remote quotient, so the projected E_j lies in X⊗R and is a nonzero product stabilizer. The other outcome of that check also occurs: otherwise E_j itself would be that product. The two outcomes on a stabilizer state are related by an original signed stabilizer anticommuting with the measured Pauli, hence by a Pauli product over the three original blocks. The other branch is therefore also a block product. Its quotient is nonzero, and its X factor x is the common projective X factor of the three exceptional quotient columns. In particular x is a stabilizer.

For any exceptional ray whose X support is not the line x, choose a check of x with a nonzero opposite branch. Its opposite branch must again lie in X⊗R, and its complementary branch is a Pauli translate. The complementary branch carries the quotient, so its X ray is x itself. The opposite branch has an orthogonal X ray in the same stabilizer basis (it is a Pauli translate of x) and its remote ray is a single chart product. Thus every exception either factors at X with spectator x, or consists of two block products, one with spectator x and one on a single remote chart ray. All three X codes together are spanned by x and at most three more vectors of its one stabilizer basis.

Consequently the weighted exceptional sum, after cancellation in the quotient, has form x⊗v plus at most three individual chart terms with other X factors. Here v lies in R by the direct quotient identity. At least one of the four chart coordinates gets only a multiple of x from this sum. The reconstructed ordinary X factor there is a nonzero multiple of g_X plus a multiple of x; neither a zero correction nor a factor proportional to x could give that stabilizer ray. Hence x is on an actual two-stabilizer secant through g_X. There are only thirty possible common spectator rays, and on each untouched chart coordinate the ordinary X ray must be the unique partner of x. This normal form retains arbitrary weights and imposes a necessary condition, leaving the original exact test as the criterion for existence.

If all S_i coincide, the erasure assumption and three-exceptions-common-local-qubit.md force their common dimension to be four: dimension eight would contain g_X, and a common code of dimension at most two is excluded. No common projected X factor is asserted in this alternative.

**No full exception on the two-type party.** For every pair there is a combination with both weights nonzero lying in X⊗R, so its Y cut rank is at most two. If one pair member has Y rank eight and the other at most four, the rank difference contradicts this bound. For two rank-eight members, a kernel of dimension at least six puts their eight-dimensional remote codes in overlap of dimension at least six; their intersection is a power of two and the codes coincide. The rank-two Clifford difference lemma of equal-remote-one-two-bells.md makes the pair combination zero or a single stabilizer ray. Zero contradicts independence. The ray would lie in X⊗R and hence be an ordinary block product. Substituting this linear identity for one of the original exceptions would give at most seven terms with at most two nonproduct exceptions, excluded by the two-exception closure and the unrestricted rank-seven lower bound. Thus at rank one all exceptions have Y Schmidt rank at most four. When the two Z secant lines coincide the same proof applies on Z.
