# Four frequencies need at most three cubic blocks

There exist t and three stabilizer rays whose span meets W_t in a vector with four or more nonzero native Fourier coefficients if and only if such an example exists on at most three blocks. The coefficients are arbitrary complex numbers. Consequently an exhaustive W_2 and W_3 test would decide the all-t existence question. Equivalently W_3 alone suffices: append inactive control indices fixed at zero and data pairs in uniform product states in the transformed chart.

In the control-Walsh chart a W-vector has amplitude c_s times the sign of sum_i s_i x_i y_i. Pick four distinct nonzero-coefficient indices. Their binary affine hull P has dimension d=2 or 3. Project the controls onto P, which is stabilizer postselection. Choose d original coordinates I restricting to an affine bijection P to the d-bit cube via their actual coordinate values; this exists by row rank. Set every data pair outside I to zero, retaining the coordinates in I as free data. Each remaining control is a known affine function of s_I, so Clifford CNOT gates and affine flips turn the redundant controls into constants and permit their deletion. The resulting amplitude is exactly c_{s(s_I)} times the sign of sum_{i in I} s_i x_i y_i, up to one global scalar. It has at least four active Fourier coefficients. Each of the original atoms maps to zero or one stabilizer atom. If at most two external atoms survived, their sum would itself belong to W_d after subtracting internal terms; the verified two-external theorem bounds its support by two, and the entire vector would then have support at most three. Thus all three images must be external. Padding conversely preserves Fourier coefficients and term count.

The same argument maps k distinct coefficients in a sum of r atoms to between ceil(log2 k) and k−1 blocks; it does not promise the restricted vector has only k nonzero coefficients. No finite catalogue exhaustion or conclusion about whether four coefficients are attainable is implicit.
