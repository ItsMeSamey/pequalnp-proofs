# Exact rank-two classification for a product of two nonstabilizer pure states

Given nonzero pure states u,v, both nonstabilizer, the product u⊗v has exact complex stabilizer rank two if and only if u and v are, up to independent local Clifford isometric encodings and nonzero scalars, a one-qubit nonstabilizer eigenstate φ of a nonscalar Clifford and its complex conjugate. In particular both have Pauli nullity exactly one. Combining this with the elementary case of a stabilizer factor yields a classification of two-factor products of rank at most two: tensoring a stabilizer multiplies the exact rank by one, by tensoring an upper-bound list and contracting the factor with its own bra for the reverse inequality.

Use nullity-two-product-rank.md, with the target flattened as u vᵀ. Its input ray is the conjugate of v. A single product stabilizer requires both local states stabilizer. For two weighted stabilizer matrices A,B summing to the target, their Schmidt ranks differ by at most one. The previous argument excludes equal ranks r≥4 when both local factors are nonstabilizer (r=4 actually yields two stabilizer factors). Equal ranks one give a sum of two products equal to a product; the two columns or two rows must be proportional, yielding a local stabilizer factor.

For unequal ranks the pair is (2,1). The column and input codes of the larger matrix are exactly the spans of the corresponding rays of the smaller product and the target. Decode the larger Clifford isometry and absorb its scale to make it I on one logical qubit. The target becomes I+c|a⟩⟨b|, where the smaller product has one-qubit stabilizer factors a,b after decoding. If this matrix has rank one, every right nullvector lies along a and every left nullvector along b. The logical target rays are their respective orthogonal rays, which are stabilizers. Hence this case is excluded.

For ranks (2,2) suppose first the output codes differ. The target column u lies outside the output code of A, since otherwise the equal-dimensional output code of B would be identical. Its input ray must lie inside the input code of A (otherwise the rank-one update has rank three). Thus the input code of B equals that of A. On this common two-dimensional input the target has a one-dimensional kernel. The injective A maps this kernel into the intersection of the output codes, which must be a stabilizer line since the codes are distinct and the intersection nonzero. The kernel is therefore its one-dimensional Clifford preimage, a stabilizer ray. The target's logical input ray is orthogonal to this stabilizer in its encoded qubit and is itself a stabilizer. This contradicts the hypotheses. Taking adjoints gives the same contradiction for different input codes.

It follows that both codes agree on both sides. After decoding the input and output and absorbing scalars the difference becomes I−cU, with U a one-qubit Clifford. Since it is nonzero and rank one, U is nonscalar, and the difference is a scalar multiple of the projector onto one of its two eigenrays. This proves the necessary form, with conjugation from the input flattening absorbed into the appropriate conjugate Clifford encoder. Conversely the projector onto either eigenray of a nonscalar two-dimensional unitary is a linear combination of that unitary and the identity. For U Clifford both vectorizations are stabilizer rays, and Clifford encoding preserves this upper bound. Rank one is excluded since the factors are nonstabilizer.
