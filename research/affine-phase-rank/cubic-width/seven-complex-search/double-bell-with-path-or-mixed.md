# A double Bell cannot accompany a two-link path or a GHZ with an additional Bell

Let a⊗F be the double Bell, with spectator a on A and F of Schmidt rank four on BC. Let E be the path or mixed GHZ-Bell exception. Use U_X for E's local code and F_X for the B,C codes of F. Write H for the weighted sum of the exceptions. Both exceptions have proper local supports on all parties. On each cut X, the remote product target is outside the sum of the two remote codes: on one remote party take a covector killing E's proper local code while retaining g, and on the other do the same for the other exception. Consequently the ordinary X span contains g_X and the entire X support of H by remote dual extraction. In particular rank H_X≤5.

When the local supports of both exceptions at X are contained in one proper stabilizer code, erasing it leaves G on the other two parties, using k=4 or 5 ordinary products. The dimensions of all five ordinary factor spans on those parties sum to at most k+1+2(5−k)≤7 by Sylvester. Two signed local markers shared on distinct parties are forbidden by two-local-shared-markers.md. In particular one cannot have both U_B⊂F_B and U_C⊂F_C. For two dimension-four stabilizer codes, if distinct their intersection has dimension at most two by the power-of-two intersection property.

We use a middle-cut observation in two cases. Suppose E has rank four on A and its remote code M is disjoint from the line of F. The three remote spaces M, span(F), and the target line are then linearly independent as a sum. Thus the five ordinary A factors span U_A and g_A, which are jointly of dimension five. Taking duals to the five independent ordinary A factors puts each associated BC product in V=M+span(F,g_B⊗g_C). Some such product must have nonzero F coefficient, since the original spectator a is nonzero. If M is supported in a two-dimensional code U_C on C, quotienting C by U_C expresses the image of F as a sum of at most two products: the image of the chosen product and a multiple of the target image. Its rank is 4−dim(F_C∩U_C), hence U_C⊂F_C. The argument also applies on B if M has such a B support.

**Path centered at A.** Its remote code M is U_B⊗U_C with both dimensions two. The rank-four F is outside, so the observation gives containment on both B and C, yielding the forbidden pair of local markers.

**Path centered at B.** The path links are AB and BC. On C its remote code consists of an intact AB Bell times an arbitrary center qubit vector, up to local Clifford encodings. Every nonzero element has rank two across AB, whereas all double Bell remote vectors on this cut are products with A fixed at a. Therefore H's C support is U_C+F_C. On A the analogous remote path vectors have rank two across BC and cannot equal the rank-four F. Thus H's A support is U_A+span(a).

If a is outside U_A then on B its path remote code U_A⊗U_C and the double Bell remote code span(a)⊗F_C are disjoint. Hence H's B support equals the sum of the two rank-four codes. Rank at most five forces them to coincide. This shared B marker prevents U_C⊂F_C, so rank H_C≥5. Meanwhile rank H_A=3. Erasing the shared B code contradicts their sum of at least eight.

If a is inside U_A then A has a shared marker, prohibiting both equality of the two B codes and containment U_C⊂F_C. Hence rank H_C≥5. On B the local rank-four codes intersect in dimension at most two, and the remote rectangle intersects span(a)⊗F_C in dimension at most two. The rank of the B flattening of H is thus at least 8−2−2=4. Here the general inequality is rank H≥r+s−l−j for term ranks r,s and local and remote intersection dimensions l,j, by factoring through the direct sum of Schmidt coordinates and applying Sylvester. Erasing U_A now contradicts the sum bound for B and C.

**GHZ with Bell on BC.** On A denote the two copy basis rays by a₀,a₁. On B the remote code is the sum of the two spaces span(a_s)⊗C_s, where the C_s are disjoint two-dimensional Bell branches. A product in this code with A factor a can occur only when a is proportional to a_s for a single s: if both coordinates were nonzero its other factor would need to lie in both C_s. The same argument holds with B and C reversed. Unless a is such a coordinate, the remote codes of the exceptions on B are disjoint, and so are those on C. On each party rank H≤5 therefore forces the local rank-four codes to coincide, giving two forbidden markers.

If a is a copy coordinate, A supplies a shared local marker, so the local rank-four codes are distinct on each of B and C. Their intersections have dimension at most two. Remote intersections have dimension at most two as well by the single-branch property. Thus H has B and C ranks at least four each, contrary to the sum bound after erasing the shared A qubit code.

**GHZ with Bell on AB.** On A the mixed remote code M is the sum of B_s⊗span(c_s), where c_s are the two copy coordinates on C. Every element has BC rank at most two, so M is disjoint from the rank-four F. The middle-cut observation on C gives U_C⊂F_C. A second marker on B is impossible, so its local rank-four codes are distinct. If a is outside U_A then the B-cut remote spaces are disjoint since their A supports are separated (the fixed line span(a) meets U_A trivially). This would make H's B rank at least six. Thus a lies in U_A, which supplies a second shared local marker on A, also impossible.

These four cases cover (0020,1100), (0020,0110), (0011,0020), and (0011,0200), respectively up to simultaneous party permutations. The argument uses the Schmidt spaces of each actual encoded exception and permits arbitrary nonzero complex weights.
