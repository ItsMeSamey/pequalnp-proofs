# A Gaussian-rational threefold eigenspace of a three-qubit Clifford contains a stabilizer ray

Let M be an invertible 8×8 matrix over Q(i) proportional to a three-qubit Clifford, and λ∈Q(i) a nonzero eigenvalue of geometric multiplicity at least three. Its λ-eigenspace contains a stabilizer ray. The rational hypothesis is on the actual matrix and its eigenvalue in a common gauge. No enumeration is used.

Write U=M/λ. This matrix is unitary: being a multiple of a unitary with eigenvalue one forces its scale to have magnitude one. It has finite order because the projective Clifford group is finite and a scalar power with an eigenvector fixed must equal the identity. Denote the fixed eigenspace by K and the symplectic action on binary Pauli labels by S.

Suppose p is an odd prime dividing the order of U. Let V be a power of U of exact order p; it has at least three fixed dimensions. Over Q(i), every primitive pth root has degree p−1 and the primitive roots are conjugate (the odd-prime cyclotomic field is linearly disjoint from Q(i), e.g. by the cyclotomic degree formula for conductor 4p). Thus x+(p−1)y=8, with x≥3 fixed dimensions and y≥1 copies of each primitive root. In particular p≤5. For this V its trace is x−y. On the other hand |Tr V|²=2^f where f is the binary dimension of fixed labels of its symplectic action. Indeed the trace of conjugation on matrices is |Tr V|²; in the Hermitian Pauli basis it is a signed permutation, and any fixed label has positive sign because V has odd order. If p=5, necessarily x=4,y=1, giving squared trace nine, impossible. If p=3, the possible x≥3 with y≥1 are 4 and 6, and only x=4 gives squared trace a power of two. Then y=2, f=2 and the order-three action has binary rank four for T−I. Also 9 cannot divide the order of U, since a power of exact order nine has an eigenvalue primitive of order nine, of degree six over Q(i) (use conductor 36), besides the at least three fixed dimensions. For order nine this use of a primitive eigenvalue follows because every nonprimitive ninth root has order dividing three.

It follows that the odd part of the order is 1 or 3. In the first case a binary symplectic transformation of two-power order preserves a Lagrangian: it has a fixed nonzero vector in positive dimension, and induction on the symplectic quotient of its orthogonal complement by this line lifts an invariant Lagrangian. In the other case decompose S into commuting odd and two-power parts T and D, both powers of S, with T the symplectic action of a power of U of order three. The two-dimensional fixed space of T and im(T−I) are complementary orthogonal nondegenerate spaces (semisimplicity and preservation of the alternating form ω). D fixes a line in the former. On the four-dimensional latter space T²+T+I=0. Its nonzero vectors partition into triples that, with zero, form binary two-dimensional T-invariant subspaces. Such a subspace is isotropic exactly when q(v)=ω(v,Tv)=0. The polar of q is ω since T+T^{-1}=I. Thus q is a nondegenerate quadratic on four binary coordinates, and it has six or ten zeros including zero (its sign sum has magnitude four by character orthogonality). Its nonzero zeros occur in triples, so there are nine, forming exactly three isotropic invariant planes. The two-power cyclic group generated by D permutes these three planes and fixes one. That plane plus the fixed line in the first summand is an S-invariant Lagrangian.

Choose a Clifford basis taking this Lagrangian to the Z labels. Conjugation by that Clifford preserves Gaussian rationality: a Clifford matrix is a scalar times a Gaussian-rational matrix, as follows from its stabilizer vectorization. In this basis U permutes computational rays and is monomial. Its nonzero entries lie in γ·{1,−1,i,−i} for a common γ∈Q(i), by the fourth-root stabilizer amplitude form for its vectorization. K is nonzero so there is a contributing permutation cycle, on which the product of the corresponding phases equals one. This makes γ a root of unity and therefore a fourth root in Q(i). Each contributing cycle gives exactly one dimension to K. At least three such cycles on eight labels force a contributing cycle of length one or two. A singleton is a basis stabilizer, and a two-cycle has fixed eigenvector supported on the two basis labels with equal magnitudes and fourth-root relative phase, also a stabilizer. Undoing the Clifford basis change gives the assertion.

For a pair of full-rank equal-output encoders given in Gaussian-rational gauges, use their relative matrix on the original logical input and the matching Gaussian-rational coefficient ratio as the eigenpair of M. The statement guarantees one stabilizer eigenray; it does not assert that a two-dimensional stabilizer subcode lies in every such eigenspace.
