# Gaussian divisibility for full columns in the Walsh chart

Suppose unnormalized full-support stabilizer chirps A_j with Gaussian integer coefficients a_j sum to c_s (-1)^{sum s_i x_i y_i} on the t-block chart. Let π=1−i. Then every c_s is divisible by π^(t+min_j vπ(a_j)), ignoring zero coefficients.

The alternating sum over the full 3t-bit cube of a full chirp is divisible by π^(3t). Expand the chirp as a product of one-variable factors 1+(i^h−1)b and binary quadratic factors 1−2bb'. In the multilinear coefficient on all variables, each contribution contains a factor of π for each incident occurrence, covering all 3t variables. By contrast the alternating sum over data at a fixed control vanishes unless every control is 1, when it equals (−2)^t times c_1 up to sign. Alternating over controls proves the claim for c_1. An arbitrary control translation, corrected by the quadratic sign with exponent sum a'_i x_i y_i for the translation vector a', preserves full chirps and moves any specified c_s to this corner.

On W_2 rescale a hypothetical full-support three-column representation to Gaussian coefficients primitive at π, using the finite-weight lemma. All four target amplitudes vanish mod π², and exactly two coefficients a,b are π-units: mod π every chirp equals 1, their weighted sum vanishes and at least one coefficient is odd. For these two columns A,B the mod-four phase parities agree up to a global constant. Indeed mod π² an atom with parity bit p has residue 1−πp. Replace the even coefficient's atom by 1 modulo π² and subtract the evaluation at the origin. Reducing the varying terms gives p_A+p_B constant over F₂. Hence A/B has a constant fourth-root phase times a real quadratic sign. The data parity span has dimension at most two for three full columns. Proper-support columns need separate analysis.
