# Minimum remote-code overlap on a full Schmidt cut

Orient the two exceptional stabilizer matrices A,B in a seven-list with five block products from an original three-qubit block to the other six qubits. Suppose A has Schmidt rank eight, and im A and im B intersect in dimension exactly two. For the required nonzero t, rank H≤6 for H=A+tB. Every kernel vector satisfies Ax∈im A∩im B. Hence ker H equals the entire two-dimensional inverse image K of this intersection under A, and rank H=6. A nonzero intersection of stabilizer codes is a stabilizer code: inconsistent or anticommuting signed constraints cannot have common vectors. Decoding by the normalized Clifford isometry A gives a local two-dimensional stabilizer code K.

The target minus a nonzero scalar times H has rank at most five. Its local row, proportional to the real vector g^T, therefore annihilates K. Only 42 possible local codes satisfy this. Every two-dimensional code on three qubits has two independent commuting signed Pauli generators. Its outcome probability equals one quarter of one plus the three signed nonidentity Pauli expectations. Real shifted Paulis have expectation ±1/2 on g; all other nonidentity labels have zero expectation. Vanishing requires precisely two shifted real contributions, both −1/2. The product of these commuting real operators is real. Since the third contribution is zero, it must be nonidentity pure Z. The shifted labels have the same shift a≠0 and differ by (0,b) for b nonzero with a·b=0. Their phase labels z obey a·z=0. There are seven choices for a, three for b, and two choices of z modulo b in a-perp. This gives 42 distinct unsigned planes. Each has precisely one joint sign assignment giving zero.

Thus the pulled-back code for every supplied pair with minimum remote overlap must be one of 42 in the original coordinates; larger intersections are outside this particular test. The second Schmidt rank may differ from eight.
