# Complete local Pauli-pair separation test

Label each three-qubit Pauli up to scalar by (a,z) in F₂^3 times F₂^3. For independent labels R,S call the pair separated when there exists a stabilizer bra with nonzero overlap with g and zero overlap with Rg,Sg,(RS)g. Enumerating all 1080 local stabilizer rays gives 1827 separated out of 1953 unordered independent pairs. The 126 exceptions are exactly the planes whose X projection has rank one and whose three nonidentity elements all have a dot z=0. In particular every exceptional pair is locally commuting. This is characterization only for this single-bra test, not an assertion other tests cannot separate those outcomes.

The checker uses all nonempty three-bit affine flats, all fourth-root linear coefficients on independent affine coordinates of each flat, and all doubled cross phases. It evaluates the real and imaginary components of all overlaps as small integers, and checks the stated exceptional-set equality over all pairs. The generator is stabs() in ../g3-complex-conic-search/search.py. For globally commuting P,Q whose local labels are separated on any one block, contraction with the certified local bra proves that each joint outcome on G3 has stabilizer rank at least four.

In any six-term candidate suppose two atoms share a signed Pauli P. For every third atom C and Q in its stabilizer commuting with P, each block i with P_i nonidentity must have either dependent local labels or an exceptional pair, or else opposite outcomes kill three terms while retaining rank four. Thus if P_i is imaginary (a dot z=1), the local projection of Stab(C) intersect P-perp is contained in span(P_i). This is impossible, since the full stabilizer projection on a three-bit block has dimension 3+e_i, with 2^{e_i} its local Schmidt rank, and the commuting slice deletes at most one dimension. If P_i is real, that local slice lies among real Pauli labels; being a linear space of zeroes of a dot z, it is totally isotropic and has dimension at most three. It follows e_i<=1. Hence every shared Pauli in a six-term candidate is real separately in each block, and each of the other four atoms has Schmidt rank at most two on every block where the shared Pauli is active. This holds across arbitrary computational support dimensions.
