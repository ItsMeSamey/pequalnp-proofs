# Several commuting measurements can exclude a seven-term candidate

Let H be a commuting group of Hermitian nine-qubit Paulis with −I absent. Suppose some original three-qubit block has a stabilizer bra b with nonzero overlap with g and zero overlap with h_i g for every nonidentity h in H, where h_i is its local factor on that block. For every character s of H the simultaneous s-eigenspace projection of G3 has complex stabilizer rank at least four. Expand the projector as the signed group average: contracting b leaves exactly a nonzero multiple of G2. Joint Pauli projection and contraction with b map each stabilizer to at most one stabilizer.

Hence in every seven-atom list at most three atoms can be killed in total for any such (H,s). A completed atom is killed iff its signed stabilizers disagree with s on some label of H: its outcome probability is a character sum over H intersect its stabilizer labels, which is zero for a disagreeing character and positive otherwise. Partial columns can be rejected as soon as four different columns have certified conflicts, potentially on different labels. For rank two take globally commuting P,Q whose local labels are one of the 1827 separated pairs in local-pair-catalog.md. Test all four joint outcomes. Disagreements can occur on P, Q or PQ.

For a control take P=X on bit one and Q=X on bit two of the first block. Their local X projection has rank two, hence the pair separates. In the positive-positive sector two atoms fixed at negative P and two others fixed at negative Q exclude a candidate. These four can have trivial common signed stabilizer: take two product rays with X− on bit one and respectively Z and X eigenbases on every other bit. Their common group is generated by −P. Take both remaining rays with Z eigenstates on bit one and X− on bit two. This identifies a four-column conflict even when those four columns have no common nonidentity stabilizer.
