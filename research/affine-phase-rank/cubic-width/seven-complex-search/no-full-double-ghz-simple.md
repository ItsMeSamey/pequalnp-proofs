# A double GHZ exception cannot be paired with one Bell or one GHZ

Suppose the weighted target cube has five ordinary block-product stabilizers, a double GHZ exception E and a simple Bell or simple GHZ exception F. Expand E into four product stabilizers in its local Clifford coordinates, and F into two in its own coordinates. This gives a dependence of product vectors, including the target, with all coefficients nonzero. Split the list into its vector-matroid connected components; projecting along the direct sum retains the displayed full-coefficient dependence in each component.

In the target component let t,e,f be the retained numbers of ordinary, E and F entries. The local spans have dimensions at least e+1 on each party, because all e local factors from E are independent and belong to a single proper stabilizer code, which excludes the local target g. The component has span dimension at most t+e+f by its dependence. Connectedness and the Lovitz–Petrov splitting bound give the sum of local dimensions at most t+e+f+2. Consequently 2e+1≤t+f≤7. If e=3 this forces all five ordinary and both F terms into that component, leaving exactly one nonzero E term outside, which cannot carry a dependence. Thus e≤2.

When two E entries survive, their sum with the original coefficients is a simple GHZ stabilizer: in the common two-bit computational copy basis its support is an affine line, with equal amplitudes before restoring the local Cliffords. One entry is already a product. Two surviving F entries restore F itself, and one surviving entry is a product. The target component therefore has a representation with at most five ordinary entries and at most two more atoms, whose possible nonproduct resources are simple Bell and simple GHZ. With fewer than seven atoms it violates the unrestricted lower bound; with seven and at most one nonproduct it violates the sole-simple or all-product exclusions; and with two nonproducts it violates the two-simple-resource exclusions. These conclusions are independent of the relative local encoders.
