# Opposite-sector survivor counts and logical paired bases

In a full local mixed Pauli sector of mixed-survivor-pauli-compatibility.md, write m for the number of the three ordinary rays with sector probability 1/2 and f for the indicator that the one surviving exception has probability 1/2 there. These are precisely the occurrences with physical squared coefficients 2γ_i t and t, respectively, in that note. The three exceptions killed by the selected projection are entirely in the opposite outcome. The opposite therefore has 3+m+f nonzero projected terms before any consolidation. By pauli-outcome-four.md the opposite target requires at least four terms for a pure Z and at least five for a rare real-shifted selected outcome. Hence m+f≥1 or ≥2, respectively. These inequalities are necessary even if some opposite survivors become proportional.

The paired-bra condition from the same note can be counted using existing local certificates. There are 1260 two-dimensional stabilizer codes on three qubits (315 unsigned commuting two-planes with four sign choices). Each three-qubit stabilizer ray lies in seven of them, one per two-dimensional subgroup of its full stabilizer. By local-triple-erasure.md, 154 of the 1080 stabilizer rays are orthogonal to g. By two-exceptions-minimum-code-overlap.md exactly 42 qubit codes have zero projection of g. Count pairs consisting of such an orthogonal ray and a containing code. A zero code contributes all six logical stabilizer rays; a nonzero stabilizer projection contributes one (its orthogonal logical ray); a nonstabilizer projection contributes none. Thus exactly 154·7−42·6=826 codes give a nonzero stabilizer projection, leaving 392 with nonstabilizer projection. A nonzero logical stabilizer has two unbiased Pauli bases among the three logical bases. Hence the equal-magnitude nonzero paired-bra test allows 1652 of the 3780 logical paired bases; this counts necessary candidates, not actual mixed exposures.
