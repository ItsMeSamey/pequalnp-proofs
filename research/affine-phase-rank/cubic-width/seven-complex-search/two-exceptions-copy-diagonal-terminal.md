# Five product terms versus a common Bell and diagonal copy encoding

Assume the two exceptions admit the following joint form under independent local Clifford coordinates. They have identical Bell factors on the three party pairs, and the remaining common encoding sends each logical string s to |s,s,s〉. Their logical amplitudes are full-support quadratic stabilizer chirps. Assume both exceptional states have local Schmidt rank eight at each party. This note excludes the stated joint form; deriving it from equality of the three remote stabilizer codes is a separate premise.

At each party the sum of the incident Bell counts plus the copy width z is three. The three Bell counts therefore coincide at m and z=3−2m. The sum H of the exceptions has local Schmidt rank at most five across each cut, by the [remote support bound](two-copy-basis-support.md). For m=1,z=1, its rank is four times the number of active copy strings, hence exactly one string survives. H is itself a stabilizer ray, contradicting the seven-term lower bound. Thus only m=0,z=3 remains, with H an order-three diagonal on r≤5 nonzero entries. All coefficient gauges below absorb the common amplitude and the first logical chirp, whose inverse is a diagonal Clifford on one party.

**Five active entries are impossible.** Write the target local vectors in these three bases as a,b,c. Each has at least four nonzero entries: fewer than four binary strings in dimension three lie in a proper affine hyperplane, giving g a nontrivial Pauli eigenlabel. The flattened remote product factor is outside the span of the r diagonal pairs (it has support size at least sixteen). If a local factor were also outside its coordinate r-space, adding the target would raise that cut rank from five to six. Thus all three factors are supported on the same five labels S. Work on these five coordinates and write the remainder, up to sign, as R=diag₃(h)−a⊗b⊗c.

Choose a covector v₀ annihilating c with all entries nonzero, possible over ℂ since c has at least two nonzero entries. The slice R(v₀)=diag(h_iv₀ᵢ) is invertible. If R has rank at most five, the five-term product factor matrices on the first and second parties are invertible: their product through a diagonal slice matrix gives R(v₀). Consequently all R(v)R(v₀)⁻¹ are simultaneously similar to diagonal matrices, hence commute. For v annihilating c this matrix is diag(vᵢ/v₀ᵢ). Choose distinct i in supp(a), j in supp(b). There exists such v with vᵢ/v₀ᵢ≠vⱼ/v₀ⱼ, because c is not supported on {i,j}. For w not annihilating c the (i,j)-entry of R(w)R(v₀)⁻¹ is nonzero, coming solely from the rank-one correction. The (i,j) commutator entry contradicts commutativity.

**Four or fewer active entries.** At least one input cancels. After equal amplitude gauges and stripping the common Clifford chirp, the entries are proportional to 1−i^{L(s)}(−1)^{q(s)}, for affine binary parity L and binary quadratic q. If L is constant, cancellation forces it to be zero. All active entries are equal, and the at most four active labels partition into at most two pairs or singletons. Every pair encodes an equal-amplitude superposition |s,s,s〉+|t,t,t〉, locally Clifford-equivalent to a simple GHZ with fixed local ancillas. A singleton is a product stabilizer. Two simple GHZ exceptions have already been [excluded](two-simple-ghz-exclusion.md); cases with fewer exceptional rays are excluded by the previously established seven-product and sole-exception arguments or the lower bound of seven.

If L is nonconstant, all four points of the L=1 hyperplane are active, so all four points of L=0 cancel. Thus q vanishes on the latter plane and is affine on the parallel active plane, since parallel restrictions of a quadratic have the same polar. On that active plane the two possible amplitudes have equal modulus and fourth-root ratio prescribed by this affine bit. The encoded state is a stabilizer, again impossible. All common Clifford phases can be restored locally without changing the type of any resulting atom.
