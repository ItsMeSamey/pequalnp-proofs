# Opposite native bins among four pure-Z link survivors

Suppose two of three exceptional rays share a signed computational parity supported nontrivially on Y and Z, and the third has a signed local check on X whose opposite outcome on g is nonstabilizer. Postselect both opposite outcomes. The surviving target is u⊗w with u of Pauli nullity two and w a matched or mismatched parity projection of g⊗g. Only the four ordinary terms can survive and each is still a product across X versus YZ with stabilizer factors.

Take the two computational parities as local control bits s₁,s₂ by affine changes of basis. The cubic sign on each party is sᵢxᵢyᵢ up to a binary quadratic, removable by a local Clifford. Thus w is a W₂ vector supported on two nonadjacent channels in the control-Walsh chart. Its unique two-stabilizer decomposition consists of their two native plane products: an external pair can supply only adjacent channels by [the two-external theorem](fourier-external-secants.md), and one external plus one internal cannot give a W₂ element. Thus w has rank two. Its Pauli nullity is five: decode the common parity relation by a CNOT and flip to obtain a fixed bit times s(x₁y₁+x₂y₂) in the phase of five free bits, up to quadratic Clifford correction. A shift changing s gives a derivative containing the nonzero pure data quadratic. A nonzero shift preserving s gives a nonzero quadratic coupling s to a linear data form. No nonzero shift yields an affine derivative, and no nonidentity pure Z stabilizes the full support.

The factor-span dimensions on X and YZ are both at least two and sum to at most five by Sylvester. In remote dimension two the line must be spanned by the unique native pair. It has no further stabilizer direction: three normalized stabilizers in a two-dimensional span would have a singular Gram matrix, requiring a nonzero overlap at least one half. Such a pair fixes a common code of nullity at most two, unlike w. In remote dimension three there are exactly four survivors and the local factor span has dimension two. The [three-ray line bound](four-product-marker-covers.md) for u forces a repeated local type. It occurs exactly twice because killing that type while retaining u must leave at least two remote terms. The two remaining terms then provide the unique native pair. In both cases at least one ordinary product projects exactly onto each bin.

Before remote postselection, the two retained joint local-parity components of each ordinary product are themselves products. To have exactly one vanish at least one local factor must be definite in the matching parity. On the surviving bin both local restrictions agree projectively with their specified plane restrictions of g. Opposite bins therefore require two different ordinary products meeting these conditions. This is a necessary condition, not an exclusion of all pure-Z links.
