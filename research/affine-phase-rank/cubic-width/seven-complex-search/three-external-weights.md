# A finite weight list for three external stabilizers

Let W_t and its internal Fourier rays χ_s be as in fourier-external-secants.md. Use the conclusion of that lemma that a vector in the span of two distinct external stabilizer rays has at most two nonzero Fourier coefficients if it belongs to W_t. Consider a vector with at least four such coefficients represented by three external stabilizer columns. These columns are independent: dependence would place the vector in a span of at most two external columns. All three weights are therefore nonzero. Write L for the inverse image of W_t in their three-dimensional coefficient space. The column map is injective.

The dimension of L cannot be three, since then every column would belong to W_t. If its dimension is two, each coordinate hyperplane cuts L in a line: containment would force its entire image to be supported on a single pair of columns, contradicting the hypothesis on the given vector. These three lines are distinct, since any shared nonzero vector on two coordinate hyperplanes would give one external column in W_t. The images of the first two lines span the image of L. Each has Fourier support of size at most two by the secant lemma, so their union must have size at least four to contain the given vector's support. Thus both supports have size two and are disjoint. Every linear combination using both directions then has support four, including the image of the third distinct line, contradicting the secant lemma. Consequently L has dimension one.

Apply the unitary Walsh transform to the z coordinates and leave the data coordinates u=(x_1,y_1,...,x_t,y_t) fixed. This Clifford maps the external columns to stabilizer columns external to the transformed space. Absorb each transformed column's nonzero normalization and overall phase independently into its weight, so its entries lie in D={0,1,−1,i,−i}. This gauge is possible for every support dimension. On each control s multiply every entry in the row (s,u) by the known target sign (−1)^(Σ s_j x_j y_j). This row operation is invertible and preserves D; it need not be Clifford. The transformed, sign-adjusted W_t consists of all vectors constant on every s-fiber. Their fiber constants are the original Fourier coefficients times the common factor 2^(t/2), since the χ_s originally had unit-magnitude entries. No coordinate-dependent coefficient normalization is used.

Subtract the row at (s,0) from each row at (s,u) for the three adjusted columns, stacking the differences into a constraint matrix M. Membership is equivalent to Mα=0 with no extra conditions, including on fibers where the target coefficient is zero. Its kernel dimension is one by the preceding argument and the invertible gauges, so it has rank two. Select two independent rows. Their ordinary bilinear three-coordinate cross product (without complex conjugation) is a nonzero spanning kernel vector. Every row entry belongs to D−D={0,±1,±i,±2,±2i,±1±i}, of cardinality thirteen and maximum modulus two. Thus the entire ordered pair of rows, irrespective of t, comes from at most 13^6 possibilities. Each cross-product entry is a Gaussian integer of modulus at most eight. In that common weight gauge each s-fiber constant is a sum of three products of one cross-product entry with one entry from D, hence a Gaussian integer of modulus at most twenty-four. Equivalently the Fourier coefficients admit one common projective scaling with this same bound.

This is a necessary filter. Membership of a triple or its coefficient pattern in the finite list does not establish existence of the three external stabilizer columns. The count concerns weight triples in the independently unit-gauged Walsh columns, not weights in an arbitrary preassigned normalization of the original atoms.
