# Exact coefficient constraints from joint Pauli outcomes

Suppose seven unit-gauged stabilizer rays represent G3 and two carry the same signed Pauli P with eigenvalue +. Choose a commuting independent Q such that S=〈P,Q〉 has a separating stabilizer bra β on one original block: β overlaps g, and annihilates the local factor of h applied to g for every nonidentity h∈S. The reviewed local-pair catalog provides such a bra for its separated local pairs. Write Π_s for the four joint projectors and use the same β at every outcome.

Append two zero qubits and coherently extract the syndrome via Clifford gates (diagonalize the commuting checks, copy the two measured bits, and undo the diagonalization). Then postselect β on the original block. This map sends an atom φ_j to one possibly zero stabilizer column
    A_j=Σ_s |s>⊗(〈β|Π_s φ_j).
It sends G3 to τ Σ_s |s>⊗G2, where τ=〈β|g〉/4 is nonzero, including for signed generators. This is an exact linear equality, not an independent choice of four G2 decompositions.

Every nonzero A_j has a single column-wide unit gauge: its entries are one fixed nonzero scalar times elements of D={0,1,−1,i,−i}. The set of occurring outcomes is affine. Its nonempty data slices have translates of the same support and differ by data Paulis up to a scalar phase, by writing the stabilizer phase on affine support in syndrome and data coordinates. For each nonempty slice let R_js be its ray normalized so its first nonzero computational entry is 1. Absorb the column's original weight and its common scalar and divide by τ. Then the four equations for the SAME target are
    G2=Σ_{j present at s} b_js R_js,    b_js=w_j u_js,   u_js∈{1,−1,i,−i}.
In particular b_js^4 is independent of s wherever the column occurs. This uses the same bra, the coherent syndrome record and unit-entry ray gauges. Comparing coefficients of independently unit-normed rays would require correcting for their support sizes (which agree across nonzero slices of the same column). For completely specified original rays the exact ratios u_js/u_jt are computed from first nonzero entries of A_j, so the fourth-power condition can be strengthened to exact linear equations. Separate row equations plus fourth powers alone are necessary tests when cross-slice ray or phase assignments remain unspecified.

Both P-negative rows need ≥4 surviving atoms by χ(G2)=4. Their combined index set has size ≤5 since two original columns are P-positive. Thus they share at least three survivors even if further columns are lost under β. On any row with exactly four, the four R_js are independent and all coefficients are unique and nonzero. Solve on any four pivot data rows and verify all 64 rows; this gives exact Gaussian-rational b_js for the usual unnormalized ±1 target. Two tight rows require exact agreement of the fourth powers of all their shared coefficients.

If one negative row is tight and the other has five nonzero rays, all four tight indices occur in the latter. Its matrix rank is four or five by the two-copy theorem. For rank five there is at most one solution. For rank four, solve the 64 equalities exactly to obtain an affine line d+t k if consistent. At least two entries of the kernel vector k are nonzero (no zero column), so at least one is among the four anchored entries. At such an index j the four possible values of d_j+t k_j are the four unit multiples of the known b_j. Therefore there are at most four possible coefficient vectors for the entire five-ray negative row. Test all other fourth powers, nonzero weights, and exact phase ratios if available. If both rows have five, both have the same five indices; for each fourth-root relative diagonal one can stack their two systems. The stacked system has nullity at most one. A continuous parameter can remain when rank is four; no uniqueness is asserted then.

This reasoning also works without the final local bra, using the true projected targets and a single common syndrome/data Clifford chart; four-survivor independence or a bound on nullity there requires the rank of the corresponding projected target. The G2 version uses the actual amplitudes of G3 through its joint-projector expansion. It is a rejection and coefficient-recovery test, not a new upper or lower bound on χ(G3).
