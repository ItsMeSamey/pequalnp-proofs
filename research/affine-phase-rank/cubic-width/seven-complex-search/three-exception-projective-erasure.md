# A five-point test for four products and three exceptions

Assign the three exceptions to different blocks. Quotient each block by the assigned local support. Assume its target image is nonzero and outside the dictionary of nonzero projected stabilizer rays. Then a necessary condition for a seven-term decomposition is that all three quotient dictionaries contain a line through the target and four other points, with the three ordered five-point configurations equivalent by projectivity. The ordering starts at the target and uses the same four product labels at all blocks.

Indeed all exceptions vanish. Three ordinary products cannot give the target: use one covector per block, each killing an assigned product yet retaining the target. So all four products survive. Any pair of their factors must span the target on each block, since otherwise one covector kills both, and the two remaining blocks kill the other two products. In particular the factors are pairwise distinct. Fixing one factor now puts all four and the target on a common line.

In the dependence of the target and four products all five coefficients are nonzero. Flattening along one block shows the five opposite pair tensors span dimension at most three, since the first factor matrix has rank two. Some nonzero bilinear form in two binary factors thus vanishes on all five pairs. Its matrix must be nonsingular: a rank-one form's left and right kernels cover at most two of the five pairs by distinctness. Thus the five right points are projective images of the left points. Another flattening does the same for the third block.

Conversely, these three matched configurations suffice for a decomposition of the quotient target by the four corresponding products with freely chosen coefficients. Apply the projectivities and rescale to identify five points on the binary cubic Veronese curve. Any four are independent by the homogeneous Vandermonde determinant. The remaining point has a unique four-term expansion, all coefficients nonzero. Lifting this identity to the original nine-qubit space is a separate condition. This exact test handles arbitrary coefficients and arbitrary relative encoders.

One can test exact quotient feasibility by ordering four candidate points on every eligible line. Normalize the first three of each ordered quintuple to zero, one and infinity; the last two normalized values give a complete matching signature. The condition only applies when the three chosen erasures each leave the target outside the projected dictionary.
