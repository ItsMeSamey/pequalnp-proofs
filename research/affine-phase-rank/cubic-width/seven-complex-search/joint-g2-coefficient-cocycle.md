# Canonically gauged G₂ coefficients obey a quadratic syndrome phase law

Use joint-g2-phase-anchors.md, with the same coherent syndrome extraction and the same physical separating bra in all sectors. For each surviving column let its affine syndrome occurrence flat be A, and H its fixed linear space of directions within data fibers. Choose a row echelon basis for H whose leading pivot is the earliest coordinate used by each row, and use ordinary binary lexicographic ordering. In each data coset there is exactly one point having all these pivots zero. It is lexicographically least: the first nonzero bit of any nonzero added H vector is a pivot where that point had zero. Reducing all pivots to zero is a linear map on the data. Choose any affine section y(s) of the total support over s∈A (linear splitting of a surjection after picking origins). Applying pivot reduction gives an affine map y_min(s), independent of the chosen initial section.

Therefore the first nonzero slice amplitude is the global stabilizer phase evaluated on an affine section. In free binary coordinates t on A its coefficient is
    b(t)=w i^(Σ l_i t_i) (−1)^(Σ_{i<j} q_ij t_i t_j)
with constant w and l_i mod 4, q_ij mod 2. Standard affine substitution preserves this form: converting a binary XOR to an integer expression mod 4 contributes linear and doubled quadratic terms only, and substitution into a doubled quadratic needs only the Boolean quadratic mod 2.

In particular for an atom seen at all four outcomes of two independent checks one has b00 b11/(b01 b10)∈{1,−1}, irrespective of its unknown global scalar. In higher-dimensional occurrence flats, all second multiplicative derivatives are constant signs and all third multiplicative derivatives are one. The phase law is stronger than equality of individual fourth powers. It tests recovered joint G₂ coefficients with the stipulated gauges, not coefficients obtained from unrelated postselections or arbitrary phases on the rays.
