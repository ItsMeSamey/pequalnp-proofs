# Three equal full remote codes are impossible

Suppose both exceptions have Schmidt rank eight across each original block and agree on their remote code on each cut. By five-product-remote-erasure.md all three codes are computational. Each remote code is an eight-position affine flat projecting surjectively onto each of its two alphabets, since the individual block supports are full. Thus it is a matching between eight positions on either side. Two pair graphs already determine at most one triple per first coordinate, and all eight must occur, so both exceptions have exactly the same eight-point coordinate support Γ. Their nonzero entries on Γ are full stabilizer phases in three affine coordinates up to scalar.

Let m be the number of nonzero entries in their weighted sum H. Its flattening has rank m. If m=8 the rank of G3−H is ≥7 by the rank-one update inequality. Otherwise g is outside its m-position local space, and G2 outside its m-position remote space, so rank(G3−H)=m+1. Five product terms force m≤4. Conversely expanding H as m point rays gives a block-product expansion with ≤5+m terms, so m≥3 by the eight-product theorem.

At m=4 the explicit decomposition of G3−H uses the target and four signed point products. Its five local factors at each block are independent because g has full support and the four points differ. Across A|BC its remote span is the five-dimensional matching space spanned by the five paired B,C factors. In these local bases a nonzero combination is product precisely when its diagonal coefficient matrix has rank one. Thus only the five specified product rays occur. Any decomposition into five ordinary stabilizer products must supply all five remote directions, including g⊗g, impossible.

At m=3 the ratio of the two phases equals a fixed fourth root at five of eight points. Its affine imaginary-parity bit must be constant (a nonconstant fiber has size four). Up to scalar its remaining phase is a binary quadratic sign, whose discrepancy with a fixed sign has even weight on three bits, since every monomial of degree at most two has even weight. Hence m cannot be three. Other full-cut patterns are not addressed.
