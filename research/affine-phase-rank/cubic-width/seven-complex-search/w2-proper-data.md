# Four weighted two-block slices exclude a proper data fiber in a three-ray representation

Let the amplitude on controls (a,b) and data (x₁,y₁,x₂,y₂) be c_ab times the sign of a x₁y₁+b x₂y₂, with all four c_ab nonzero and otherwise arbitrary complex numbers. No representation by three stabilizer rays in this chart has an atom with proper data direction. Data direction means the fixed linear space parallel to the nonempty data fibers of the affine support. A full-data ray may still have restricted control occurrence. We use the established rank-four lower bound for the equal-weight two-block state and the two-ray restriction lemma in [fourier-external-secants.md](fourier-external-secants.md): when at most two full-relative-support chirps sum to a nonzero scalar times a real chirp on an affine flat, each participating ray has the target's stripped real polar there. All ray coefficients below absorb the respective normalization.

Write B_s for the data polar of the target sign at control s. If B_s−B_r vanishes on a data hyperplane H=ker n, it has rank at most two, so s−r is zero or a single coordinate vector. In the latter case n must belong to the dual of that data block: the projection of H to that block is isotropic and has dimension at most one. Conversely a normal in that block kills its polar on H. Thus the four B_s restrict to four distinct values for a mixed n, or two distinct values on parallel pairs of controls for a block-pure n.

**Independent normals on two different proper rays.** Select one nonzero data normal from each ray. The corresponding support equations have affine right sides in the controls (extend them affinely off the occurrence flats). If the normals are independent, solve the two reversed equations for an affine data section d(s) meeting neither ray. The third ray alone on this section has a full stabilizer chirp on the two controls. The target sign along the section is a Boolean sign on two bits, hence a quadratic sign. Dividing the third ray by this sign makes the weight function itself a stabilizer chirp, up to an overall scalar. Its inverse phase is a diagonal Clifford. Applying it produces the equal-weight two-block state, impossible with three rays.

**Exactly one proper ray C.** For every nonzero normal n of C and every s, take a translate of H avoiding C. Each occurring full ray has restricted polar B_s on H. Two rays must cover the four controls, so the restriction has at most two values and n is block-pure. All normals of C lie in the same block, since their span could otherwise produce a mixed normal. Call it block one. The two restricted polar classes are indexed by b, and thus one full ray covers each entire b-line, exclusively. C must occur on each line at least once, since the full ray cannot supply both distinct full data polars on that line alone. The direction of C contains all of block two. On any occurrence restrict to its fiber and apply the two-ray lemma to C and the sole full ray. C would have block-two polar b B_(0,1) on occurrences from both lines, a contradiction.

The independent-normal case leaves, whenever two or more rays are proper, only parallel hyperplanes with the same normal n: otherwise choose independent normals from two different rays.

**Three parallel proper rays.** At each control both halves must be covered. Each half has one or two rays. Every occurring ray has restricted polar B_s on H, by the two-ray lemma applied on its own half. Each distinct polar needs at least two rays to cover the halves at a control having that polar. There are at least two polars and only three rays.

**Two parallel proper rays C,D and one full ray F.** Wherever F occurs at least one half contains at most one proper ray, so F has restricted polar B_s on H. Wherever F is absent, the proper rays cover complementary halves and each has restricted polar B_s on H. Thus at most two restricted polar values occur. The normal is block-pure, say in block one, and the two classes are the two b-lines. F must occur (two proper rays alone at all controls would require both to have both restricted polars). Label its line b=0, twisting by the block-two quadratic and flipping b if necessary. On b=1 both proper atoms occur alone on their respective halves. Both have the block-two polar of the quadratic u v, where (u,v)=z are the second-block data. On b=0 F must always occur, since otherwise each proper atom would have the wrong polar. At a given b=0 control, any half containing just one proper ray plus F is impossible by the two-ray lemma applied on that half. Therefore both proper rays coincide on one half or both are absent. F cannot cover both complete a-slices alone. Each proper ray consequently occurs at some b=0 control as well as both b=1 controls. Its affine control occurrence contains three points, hence all four. There is a common occupied half and an empty half for the proper rays at each b=0 control.

Choose linear block-one coordinates x=n(data), y complementary. The target mixed coefficient on x,y remains a because every invertible two-bit linear change preserves its alternating form. At fixed a on b=0 the two halves have fixed x. On the empty half F agrees with the target, so its imaginary parity is constant in y,z, its z-polar is zero and its y,z mixed polar is zero. These data-direction properties hold also on the occupied half. At b=1 each proper ray agrees by itself with the target on an entire half. Its imaginary parity is constant in y,z, its z-polar is that of u v and its y,z mixed polar is zero. These properties likewise persist on b=0.

Fix a and the occupied x. For each y the target and F have z dependence A(y) and B(y)χ_l(z), respectively, with nonzero scalars and χ_l(z)=(-1)^(l·z). The frequency l is independent of y by the preceding mixed-polar fact. Write g(z)=(-1)^(u v). After multiplication by g, each proper term is one z-character times a scalar depending on y. Its frequency is independent of y, again by the mixed-polar fact. The Fourier transform of g(A(y)−B(y)χ_l) therefore vanishes outside at most two fixed frequencies. Pick any missing k. The two-bit Walsh transform of g is nonzero at all four frequencies, so B(y)/A(y)=ĝ(k)/ĝ(k+l) is independent of y. This also covers zero residual. Hence the ratio F/target is independent of y on the occupied half, and equals one on the empty half. At fixed z it has trivial x,y mixed derivative. The imaginary parity of F is independent of y, so this derivative forces its fixed stripped x,y data polar to equal a. Applying this at a=0 and a=1 gives the contradiction.

The argument concerns proper fibers in the stated control chart. It does not exclude three rays all of whose occurring data fibers are full.
