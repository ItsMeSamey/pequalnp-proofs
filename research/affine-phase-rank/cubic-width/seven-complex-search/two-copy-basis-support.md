# Two CCZ blocks occupy at least sixteen positions in every stabilizer basis

Let L be the dimension-six unsigned Pauli space of a stabilizer basis on two three-qubit parties. There is an integer e in {0,1,2,3} such that L has 3−e independent purely local labels on each party. Measuring these local labels first restricts each side to an e-logical-qubit code. The remaining labels describe a maximally entangled stabilizer basis on the two e-qubit logical parties (bipartite stabilizer normal form, or the graph of the induced symplectic isomorphism between the quotient local label spaces). This decomposes the full measurement by local syndrome pairs.

For the product input g⊗g, each party has at least (3−e)+1 occupied local syndromes. Otherwise their affine span would be proper, forcing a nonidentity product of the measured local Paulis to have g as an eigenvector. All pairs of occupied local syndromes occur independently, and the residual normalized input on each such pair is still a product state. Against a maximally entangled basis on dimension 2^e, no squared product overlap exceeds 2^(−e), hence each such pair gives at least 2^e nonzero outcomes. For e=0,1,2 this yields respectively 16,18,16 outcomes.

For e=3 the full basis has L equal to a graph of a symplectic isomorphism between local Pauli labels. The collision probability equals (1/64) times the sum over L of the squared Pauli expectations of g⊗g, by Walsh Parseval. Of the 63 nonidentity local labels, only 28 have nonzero expectation on g, all of absolute value 1/2. At most 28 graph labels therefore give a nonzero nonidentity squared expectation, each 1/16. The support size is at least the reciprocal collision probability, hence at least 24. This proves the uniform bound sixteen without assuming the measuring basis factors.

Now suppose A,B are the two exceptional matrices on an 8-by-64 cut, one (A) of full local Schmidt rank. In any seven-term list with five block-product terms the coefficient-weighted sum H=A+tB has rank at most six. Then im A and im B overlap in dimension at least two via the kernel of H and injectivity of A. Their stabilizer constraints commute and are consistent because the codes have a common vector. Both are spans of subsets of one common remote stabilizer basis. Their union covers at most 8+8−2=14 such basis positions. Consequently G2 is outside im A+im B by the bound above. Rank H cannot be six, since a rank-one update dropping the rank to at most five would require its remote target factor G2 in im H. Therefore rank H≤5, nullity at least three, and the code intersection dimension, a power of two, is at least four. In particular the minimum-overlap-two case is impossible. If the companion has Schmidt rank four its remote code must be contained in im A. The conclusion concerns actual encoders, not a new exclusion of an entire profile with unconstrained encoders.
