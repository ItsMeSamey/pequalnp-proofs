# Three external terms against four untouched Fourier channels

Assume a seven-ray decomposition of three disjoint cubic blocks has four internal rays in the Fourier span of fourier-external-secants.md. Subtract them and undo the control Hadamards. On four untouched control slices s the remaining three atoms must equal the same nonzero scalar times the full real chirp q_s=Σs_i x_i y_i. Call the set of all nonzero slices active, and B_s the polar of q_s. A plane of four untouched controls is impossible: two coordinate functionals restrict independently to the plane; restrict controls to it, pin all data pairs except those two to zero, and decode the plane affinely using those coordinates. The resulting six-bit state is the two-block cubic sign and needs four complex stabilizer rays. Hence the four untouched indices must be affinely independent (a tetrahedron).

**All three exterior atoms must have full data support whenever their slices occur.** If two proper types have independent data normals (one vanishing on each support direction), use their joint kernel H of codimension two. On each active slice there is a translate of H disjoint from both atoms, so the fixed stripped real polar of the third equals B_s on H. The kernel of s↦B_s|H has dimension at most two: each w in the kernel has polar rank 2wt(w)≤4, since the form vanishes on a codimension-two subspace. In any binary linear code dimension is no larger than maximum weight, by assigning ones on an information set. Thus four active independent points are impossible. If all proper types have only a common possible normal, their fibers are parallel hyperplanes with direction H. If two are proper and one is a full-data type, at each active slice where that full type occurs some H-translate meets at most one proper atom, so two-term rigidity makes the full atom's stripped polar equal B_s on H. Where the full type is absent, both proper atoms must cover complementary halfspaces and each one's fixed restricted polar equals B_s on H. At most two fixed restricted polar values therefore occur; their preimages are cosets of the same dimension-at-most-one kernel and cannot contain a tetrahedron. If three are proper, both halves must be covered with ≤2 atoms on each half; every participating atom then has the same stripped polar on H as the target. Each such polar class needs two atoms and three atoms can supply only one class; at most two controls occur. Finally with exactly one proper fiber, take an H-translate avoiding it. The other two full types account for at most two restricted polar values, each preimage a coset of the same dimension-at-most-one kernel. Their union has affine dimension at most two. This proves the claim.

**The three full-data types cannot have a common stripped real data polar R.** Remove this common real quadratic. On data Fourier transformation every atom now gives a singleton or a two-point edge of fixed direction ℓ_j (its data imaginary parity), because i^ℓ is a sum of two characters. For an active s, transform of the target has 2^rank(B_s+R) points; thus rank≤2 and any two active indices have Hamming distance at most two. A tetrahedron of diameter two in the 3-cube is a radius-one star: the sixteen ways of choosing one vertex of each antipodal pair consist of eight affine planes and eight distinct stars (coordinate faces and parity planes give the eight planes). Write the star as {b,b+e₁,b+e₂,b+e₃}. If D=R+B_b is nonzero it has rank two; its 2D image must meet each of the three coordinate data planes because rank(D+B_ei)≤2 (two rank-two alternating bivectors on disjoint image planes add to rank four). This is impossible for three independent coordinate planes. Thus R=B_b; choose the common stripped twist exactly q_b. At leaf b+e_i Fourier support is the four-element coordinate plane U_i. Three singletons/edges cannot reach four points unless some edge lies inside U_i. Consequently all three data parities are nonzero, one direction in each U_i. At leaf i the other two edges must each meet the plane to cover it and have distinct outside endpoints, in different nonzero cosets modulo U_i. Cancellation there is impossible.

**A necessary imaginary-parity filter.** For a full-data type let M_j=R_j+ℓ_jℓ_j^T be its Z₄ polar (the symmetric matrix for its Pauli translation derivatives). Let K be the common kernel of all ℓ_j on the six data bits. For v in K apply I−P, where P is the real signed Pauli translation X_v Z_(M₁v), with the control-dependent sign chosen to stabilize atom 1 on its occurring slices. Such a sign is affine in the controls by the chirp derivative formula. Atom 1 is removed. On each active slice the image of the target is either full, zero, or supported on an entire hyperplane; its normal is n_s=(B_s+M₁)v in the hyperplane case, and zero otherwise. The corresponding two surviving atom types have fixed normals n_j=(M_j+M₁)v, j=2,3. All derivative phase ratios are real signs since v belongs to K. If n₂ or n₃ is nonzero, necessarily n_s belongs to their span. Indeed when n_s≠0, a single nonzero input to the sum determines the target support; full plus proper gives an entire half outside the proper support; identical or complementary halfspaces are immediate. For distinct intersecting halfspaces either exclusive coset of ker n₂∩ker n₃ remains nonzero and forces n_s into the span. Two simultaneously present full supports cannot occur if at least one n_j is nonzero.

Since the tetrahedron spans all control differences, all B_ei v then belong to this at-most-two-dimensional span. These three vectors are independent whenever all three two-bit components of v are nonzero. For each such v in K we conclude M₁v=M₂v=M₃v. Let H span all of them. On data H the original three chirps have the same real polar and zero data imaginary parity. After removing that quadratic, an active slice is a sum of ≤3 characters. A real quadratic chirp has Walsh support either one point or at least four, so B_s|H is constant on active s. Thus B_ei|H=0 for all i, equivalently each block projection of H has dimension ≤1. This leaves at most one vector of H with all block components nonzero. In particular any surviving K contains at most one such vector.

The result is a necessary-condition proof. It does not rule out all full-data templates with distinct stripped polars.
