# A six-list would share a forbidden single-block signed Pauli

Use the exact two-copy rank four, the unrestricted five-list exclusion, the six-list shared-Pauli theorem, the local pair catalog, the resulting sector rigidity, and the single-block-sharing exclusion in this directory. Suppose six original pure stabilizer atoms represent G3. Some pair shares a signed P, acting nontrivially on k original blocks. Single-block sharing is impossible, so k is two or three. Let C' denote the normalized opposite nonzero P projection of any one of the four remaining original atoms C. By sector rigidity all four survive and C' factors across every active block.

If k=3 there is precisely one survivor in each of the four allowed opposite-parity local-sign patterns. Since the target in each pattern is a nonzero product, that one surviving atom agrees projectively with the target in every local factor. Two distinct three-bit patterns of the same parity differ in exactly two positions. Hence some pair of distinct surviving atoms C',D' has exactly the same local stabilizer ray h on a common-sign block i.

If k=2, each of two patterns has exactly two survivors, with independent stabilizer rays on the untouched block: contracting with the nonzero active local target leaves a representation of g, whose rank is two, so proportional remaining rays cannot suffice. Expand the full sector identity in these two independent untouched rays. Since the target is a product between active and untouched blocks, both active coefficient vectors must be nonzero multiples of the same target vector. Thus the two survivors have exactly the same local stabilizer ray h on each active block. In particular choose one such block i.

In both cases let H be the signed stabilizer group of h supported on i with all other blocks identity. This is an elementary abelian group of dimension three, contained in each of the signed stabilizer groups of C' and D'. For any original stabilizer atom C with nonzero Pauli projection, its signed stabilizers commuting with P still stabilize C'; they have dimension at least eight since commutation with P imposes at most one binary linear equation on nine generators. They therefore form a codimension-at-most-one subgroup L_C of the nine-dimensional signed stabilizer group of C'. Consequently H intersect L_C has dimension at least two. Its elements stabilize the ORIGINAL atom C with the same signs. The analogous subgroup for D also has dimension at least two in the very same three-dimensional signed group H. Their intersection has dimension at least one. Hence the original list contains a pair sharing a signed nonidentity Pauli supported on a single original block. This contradicts no-single-block-sharing.md irrespective of which pair supplied the initial P.

This excludes six arbitrary complex atoms. Together with the previous lower bound and standard eight-term representation, 7 <= chi_C(G3) <= 8. All phases, affine support dimensions, and changes of coefficients are allowed. This does not decide whether a seven-term decomposition exists.
