# Four parallel crossing lifts cannot leave a three-atom correction

Use the three-active pure-Z shared-sector setup in three-active-even-reduction.md, now with three shared correction atoms and four crossing atoms. Assume the four crossings give a bijection from the opposite sector to the shared sector by flipping the same one control block. After decoding that block's control t by CNOT, write the two independent remaining controls as E=(a,b), so t=a+b+c for a fixed bit c. Write z for the four free coordinates in the remaining blocks, and x for the two free coordinates in the flipped block. Define Q(x)=x1 x2, and G2=(-1)^(a z1 z2+b z3 z4). Up to a common Clifford diagonal the residual at each E is G2 times
    (-1)^(tQ(x)) − i^(h_E) (-1)^((1+t)Q(x)+l_E·x+v_E·z).
All h_E, l_E and v_E may vary freely.

Let delta_t be the difference of the two l_E in the t-class. If their two differences span at most one dimension, choose d nonzero orthogonal to both. Set D(x)=Q(x+d)+Q(x), an affine functional with nonzero linear part. Choose x_t with D(x_t)=1+d·l_E in each t-class. Parameterize x=x_t+s d on the selected line, and sum over s with the character matching the first term's slope t D(x_t). This is a stabilizer map: the embedding is affine in controls and s, and the correcting phase is at most quadratic. The second term cancels and the first survives. The remaining constant control sign is Clifford, so the output is equivalent to G2.

If the differences are independent choose d nonzero perpendicular to delta_0+delta_1. Then d·l_E is affine in E, varying within each t-class. Choose x_E affine in E satisfying D(x_E)=1+d·l_E. The slopes of the first and second term along x=x_E+s d differ at every E. The first slope tD(x_E) is a nonaffine Boolean function on two bits, since D(x_E) varies within each t-class. For any selected point E0 the sum of this slope and the point indicator delta(E0) is affine. Use that sum as the slope of the summation character. Exactly the first term survives except at E0, where exactly the second survives. The equal nonzero magnitudes leave the output Clifford-equivalent to
    G2 · i^(h_E0 ab) · (-1)^(ab v_E0·z).
Indeed the other real signs on E are quadratic, delta(E0)−ab as an integer polynomial has an even ab coefficient and affine remainder, and the lower degree terms in delta(E0) times v_E0·z are quadratic. Absorb the last cubic sign into G2 by replacing z1,z2 with z1+u b,z2+v b and z3,z4 with z3+w a,z4+r a. The resulting ab times linear form in z can be chosen arbitrarily and the additional terms are quadratic. Even h_E0 therefore gives G2; odd h_E0 gives G2 i^(ab) up to Clifford.

The latter six-bit target T also has complex stabilizer rank four. Its four control fibers give the upper bound. For the lower bound follow ../g3-complex-two-copy-pending/proof.md. A full complex chirp twist has sum nonzero: the untwisted-target G2 term has (1+i)-valuation 4, and the added (i−1) times the sum restricted to a=b=1 has valuation at least 5 or vanishes. Indeed the remaining four-variable sum on that fiber is a quadratic Gauss sum (the standard bound on its nonzero valuation is at least its number of variables). Simultaneous annihilation of three full atoms is therefore impossible. Every hyperplane contains an affine three-dimensional slice carrying a Clifford-equivalent G1: if a free coordinate z occurs, erase that block, zero its control and other free bit, and solve for the selected z; otherwise solve for one control as an affine function of the other, zeroing both erased free bits, and i^(ab) is Clifford there. Thus deleting one proper atom cannot leave two full atoms on this slice. With at least two proper atoms use the two-deletion argument of the cited proof verbatim: the target has fourth-root entries, and each deletion leaves two surviving terms covering the hyperplane, at least one not full there. These force both surviving scalars onto octagonal rays, and two deletions cover all coefficients. Finally Re((1−i)T)=G2 entrywise. Multiplying an octagonal scalar by 1−i preserves the octagonal rays, so taking real parts leaves at most one real atom per term, contradicting the reviewed real two-copy rank four.

Both resulting outputs therefore require at least four terms. Stabilizer maps cannot increase term count. This excludes all parallel bijective crossings under the stated shared-sector hypothesis; no conclusion about other matchings is asserted.
