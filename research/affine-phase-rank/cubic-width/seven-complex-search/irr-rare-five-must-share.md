# Five atoms at the rare IRR outcome must share a second signed Pauli

Use real-shift representatives X on the first control of each of the two active triples, oriented to positive expectation on normalized CCZ g. Hadamarding each measured bit transforms its three-bit g to the uniform graph t=xy. In the rare outcome of X⊗X, t_B+t_C=1. Decode one redundant bit. At t_B=0 the B pair runs over the three points with xy=0 and C is fixed to (1,1); at t_B=1 the roles reverse. Each triple spans its own two-dimensional plane, the directions of the planes are independent, and the selector is independent of both. The six points are affinely independent in five bits. Thus, on its eight logical bits, the normalized output is g⊗w, where w is the equal positive superposition of six affinely independent points in F₂⁵. Affine Clifford equivalence covers each Pauli in the same signed orbit. Its two-block factor w has stabilizer rank three: group its six points into pairs for the upper bound, and use five-product-remote-erasure.md for the nonpure-Z two-block outcome lower bound. The rare whole outcome has rank ≥5 by pauli-outcome-four.md.

Suppose five stabilizers span the normalized g⊗w and share no signed nonidentity logical Pauli pair. The stabilizer trace formula bounds their off-diagonal Gram magnitudes by 1/16, hence the smallest eigenvalue is ≥3/4. Their squared target overlaps must sum to at least this.

For one atom let R=2^r be its Schmidt rank on g|w, and K its W-side Schmidt support code. Its computational support is an affine d-flat D. Write h for the X-shift rank of the code's 5−r independent checks. The purely diagonal checks cut out D, so d=h+r. The remaining checks impose phases along each coset of the h-dimensional shift space inside D. Thus each such coset carries one normalized code basis vector, a unit phase on its 2^h positions divided by sqrt(2^h). Let t be the number of simplex points in D, and n_i their numbers in the occupied cosets. The mass of w in K is bounded by Σ n_i²/(6·2^h). Since the t points are affinely independent, all within-coset differences to one representative per coset are linearly independent and belong to the h-space. Thus Σ(n_i−1)≤h and t≤h+r+1. Concentrating extras on one coset gives Σ n_i²≤h(h+1)+h+r+1=(h+1)²+r (use Σ n_i(n_i−1)≤(h+1)Σ(n_i−1)). Maximizing over 0≤h≤5−r gives mass bounds 3/8, 5/12, 1/2 and 2/3 for r=0,1,2,3. Using the flat Schmidt spectrum, each squared overlap with g⊗w is ≤P_r(g) times the W mass divided by R, where P_r(g)≤9/16,5/8,3/4,1 respectively.

Assign cost k=6−t if t=d+1, and k=0 otherwise. In the former case D is exactly the affine hull of its included vertices. If two such flats both missed any one of the six vertices, the affine hyperplane spanned by the other five would contain both. Its common signed Z parity would fix both full atoms, contrary to hypothesis. Hence Σ k≤6 over all five atoms.

Claim that each atom has squared overlap ≤(7+k)/64, plus a bonus of 4/64 or 2/64 only for a product with g-side factor respectively of type H or M as in six-must-share.md. For R≥4 the earlier bounds already give at most 3/32. For R=2, t=d+1, the bound is (5/16)((h+1)²+1)/(6·2^h) with h=t−2 and 2≤t≤6, which is ≤(7+6−t)/64 by the five substitutions. For R=2, t≤d=h+1, if two code cosets are populated the W mass is ≤(h²+1)/(6·2^h)≤5/24 for 1≤h≤4. If just one is populated the W-code projection is proportional to that basis stabilizer. Contracting it into the normalized Schmidt-rank-two atom on W gives a stabilizer on the g side of norm 1/sqrt(2), so g contributes at most 9/16 in place of 5/8. The W mass is at most (h+1)²/(6·2^h)≤3/8; the fidelity is thus ≤27/256<7/64. Zero mass is immediate.

For R=1 the W-side overlap is ≤t²/(6·2^d). At t≤d this is at most 3/16, giving overall ≤27/256. At t=d+1 substitute t=1,...,6 and multiply by the appropriate local bound 9/16 (H), 1/2 (M) or 5/16 (other); the claim follows. In the absence of shared signs at most one product has H spectator and at most two have M (two such M planes must be complementary). A single H cannot coexist with two M, since its exceptional point would need to belong to both complementary planes. Total bonus is ≤6/64. Consequently total fidelity ≤(35+6+6)/64=47/64<3/4, a contradiction.

Therefore a five-ray rare-IRR representation necessarily contains a signed logical Pauli shared by a pair. In physical variables this is an additional commuting check independent of the original measurement P on the five postselected rays. By the local-pair catalog, on each active block any such Q either projects to a label dependent on its P_i or belongs to a real exceptional plane with P_i; otherwise projecting to the opposite joint outcome would leave ≤3 atoms although a separating bra retains G2. In the X-shift representative this forces the X part of Q_i to lie in the one-bit measured shift direction, and its Z part to vanish in that direction. After the local Hadamards and affine decoding its W-side factor is therefore computational Z (possibly identity). The untouched g block may carry a Pauli. This restricts a five-ray sector and does not exclude its existence.
