# A Bell triangle must share a two-dimensional local subspace with its companion at each party

Suppose a seven-list has five block products, an encoded Bell triangle E (profile 1110), and a second exception F. For each original block X write C_X,D_X for the two local Schmidt supports. The four-dimensional C_X is a proper stabilizer code and excludes g. Quotient X by D_X, killing F. On a different party Y use a linear map identity on C_Y and annihilating g, which exists since g lies outside C_Y. This kills the target while leaving E's Y support unchanged. Therefore the triangle after quotienting its X leg by C_X∩D_X has ordinary complex tensor rank at most five.

The unencoded triangle is the 2×2 matrix multiplication tensor M. With zero kernel its rank seven gives a contradiction. With a one-dimensional kernel span(u) its truncated rank is at least six, as follows. Choose a rank-one matrix covector L with L(u)=1 (a matrix coordinate nonzero on u, scaled). Lift the quotient onto ker L. Restoring the full tensor adds exactly u⊗M(L). The latter opposite matrix has rank two: in the triangle coordinates a rank-one contraction across the two incident edge bits fixes product directions at the partners and leaves one Bell on their common edge. If the truncation had rank at most five, these restored two terms would form part of an optimal seven-term ordinary decomposition, with proportional X factors u. Every optimal decomposition is equivalent to Strassen's under local invertible transformations, term scalings/permutations and the tensor symmetries, by de Groote's theorem. No two Strassen terms have proportional local directions at any one party. For example the first-input forms in variables a,b,c,d are a+d, c+d, a, d, a+b, c−a, b−d; the other parties have the same distinct-direction property from the standard seven formulas. This rules out a one-dimensional kernel. [de Groote, TCS 7 (1978), 127–148](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0304397578900452)

Consequently dim(C_X∩D_X)≥2 on all three parties. A local rank-one companion is impossible; a local rank-two D_X must be contained in C_X and inherits its signed marker. For rank four, the signed local marker of E must commute and be sign-compatible with the companion's local code. No common local encoder is assumed.
