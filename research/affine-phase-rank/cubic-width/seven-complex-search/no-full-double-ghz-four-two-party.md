# A double GHZ cannot accompany a double Bell or a GHZ with one Bell

Put the two rank-four parties of the companion F on B,C. Expand F into four product stabilizers whose B factors and C factors are separately orthogonal and avoid all four coordinate rays of the double GHZ E on their respective party.

For two Bells, a two-logical-qubit maximally entangled state pairs any stabilizer basis on B with its conjugate encoded basis on C. There are fifteen disjoint choices of stabilizer bases, from the fifteen unsigned maximal Pauli spaces (fifteen nonidentity labels, six commuting independent partners each, six ordered bases per maximal space). Each specified ray on either party forbids at most one choice. The eight E rays therefore do not exhaust them. For a GHZ and a Bell, condition on either of the two original copy labels. The Bell on the residual two-dimensional codes pairs any of three logical Pauli bases. Of the E rays lying in the B branch code there are at most two, and if two occur they are orthogonal and belong to the same logical axis. The same holds on C. Thus at most two axes are forbidden in that branch. Choose separately on each branch, preserving the four-product sum. This choice need not arise from one change of basis common to both branches.

As in no-full-double-ghz-simple.md let t,e,f count the ordinary, E, F entries in the connected target component. Its local dimension sum is at most t+e+f+2. The E ranks imply 2e+1≤t+f. Because A contains e independent E factors from a code excluding g and B,C each contain f independent F factors from codes excluding g, one also has f+1≤t. If e=4 then all terms belong to this component. If f=4 then t=5 and no omitted E entries could cancel by themselves, so again every term belongs. In this case all three dimensions are exactly five because E forces five everywhere and the upper bound is fifteen. Both local four-dimensional codes on B, and likewise on C, coincide by their intersection dimension at least three. This is excluded in no-full-double-ghz-overlap.md.

Otherwise e,f≤3. If t=5 the omitted E and F entries alone cancel. The B ranks of the two omitted subtensors equal their respective numbers of entries because both the B and C families are independent on each side. The counts are equal and nonzero. The E subtensor also has that A rank, forcing the omitted F factors on A to be independent. Their remote BC spans thus agree. The only product directions in the span of matched products of two independent coordinate families are the coordinate products themselves, by the rank of a diagonal matrix. This contradicts the chosen avoidance on B and C, also for a single omitted term.

For t≤4 and e=3, the inequality 7≤t+f forces t=4,f=3. One detached E term and one detached F term sum to a nonzero multiple of the single detached ordinary product. This requires proportional E,F factors on at least two parties, impossible by avoidance on B and C. Hence e≤2 and the retained E entries combine into at most one simple GHZ or product. If f≤2 keep the F entries as products. If f=3 in the GHZ-Bell case two entries in a common copy branch recombine into a simple Bell, leaving one product. For the double Bell case two chosen entries of the common Clifford basis are a simple Bell upon restriction to their affine coordinate line, also leaving one product. Thus the target has at most seven stabilizer terms and only simple resources as possible exceptions. The established simple-resource and sole-exception exclusions finish the proof.
