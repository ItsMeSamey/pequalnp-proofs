# Five-term hard shifted branches cannot have three factors on the shifted block

In the one-active-block Pauli representatives use R=X and J=Y on the control bit of g(z,x,y)=(-1)^{zxy}. After discarding the measured stabilizer qubit, the common real outcome has h_R=(1,1,1,0) on the other two qubits, and the imaginary outcomes have h_J=(1,1,1,±i), all up to scalar. The rare real outcome is the stabilizer δ_{11}. Our conclusions for h_J transfer by complex conjugation; on held-sign conventions the opposite branch of +R is rare and of −R common. The prior Pauli outcome theorem gives rank ≥5 for h_R⊗G₂ and h_J⊗G₂. Both h targets have nullity two: a weight-three support has no nonzero period or constant nonidentity Z character, and for the controlled phase at a single point any shifted eigencondition would force the phase ratios to be a signed character times a fixed scalar, whereas its values at the interchanged exceptional point and its translate are opposite imaginary phases and its values at the other pair are both one.

Suppose one of these hard targets is a sum of five arbitrary stabilizer rays. Nonminimal lists of length ≤5 reduce to this case, by the rank lower bound. Across the moved block versus the other six qubits, its nonzero local Schmidt supports are stabilizer codes of dimension 1,2 or 4. No stabilizer bra nonorthogonal to h can annihilate two of the codes, since the nonzero residual G₂ would then have rank ≤3. In particular no proper local code can repeat: its stabilizer-basis complement contains such a bra since h has nullity two.

The elementary checker shifted-branch-local-cover.py constructs the sixty two-qubit rays (four points, six pairs with four phases each, and 32 full chirps), and thirty signed Pauli hyperplanes. For each proper code it masks the retained bras annihilating the code and enumerates compatible code subsets of size at most five. All arithmetic uses small Gaussian integers, and no higher-qubit search occurs. For both targets, with n₁ rays and n₂ hyperplanes, the complete mask possibilities are n₁=0 and n₂≤5, n₁=1 and n₂≤2, n₁=2 and n₂≤1, or (n₁,n₂)=(3,0). At n₁=3 there is exactly one triple for h_R and four for h_J. Each consists of one computational point and two full chirps, and lies with h on the same complex line L. The checker verifies this using proportionality off the point. The listed possibilities are only necessary.

We exclude the final case. All three factorized terms and the target have local support in L. Hence the weighted sum of the two remaining full-rank encoded isometries annihilates the conjugate of L^⊥ as input, with the ordinary transpose convention for flattening. Below denote this input line-space by K and its two-dimensional orthogonal complement by L' (the conjugate of L). The two four-dimensional output codes intersect in dimension at least two, by injectivity of either encoder. If they differ their intersection is a two-dimensional stabilizer code (a nonempty common eigenspace). Its inverse image under a full encoder equals K and is also a stabilizer code. This is impossible: the complement L' of a two-dimensional Pauli code in the four-dimensional input would also be a stabilizer code, but contains the conjugate of the nullity-two h. Thus the output codes coincide. Relative to one encoder the pair sums, up to a nonzero scalar, to I−U where U is a Clifford unitary acting identically on K. The two coefficients must have the same modulus since the map vanishes on nonzero vectors of K.

For clarity, any line through two two-qubit stabilizer rays that is not itself a two-dimensional stabilizer code contains at most three stabilizer rays. Take one endpoint by a Clifford to a computational point. If the other endpoint's affine support misses it, the support size of a nontrivial combination is 2^d+1, impossible except d=0, which gives a code. If its support contains the point and has dimension ≤1, we also get a code or just one ray. Otherwise it is full on the four coordinates. A third ray must then be full (removing one point leaves support three) and after dividing by the full Clifford phase is constant away from the one point. Its relative phase parity is affine and hence constant; only the sign-flipped chirp can be a third ray. (The two plane-support endpoint case was already reduced by the initial Clifford.)

In our case a diagonal Clifford and translation carry L' to the span of |11⟩ and |++⟩. Its three stabilizer rays are these and CZ|++⟩, with |++⟩ normalized. Their pairwise absolute overlaps are 1/2. Each reflection I−2|v⟩⟨v| about one of the three rays is Clifford, fixing K pointwise, and transposes the other two rays: this follows by evaluating the reflection at |11⟩ and at |++⟩, or by noting it preserves the three-ray set and neither fixes nor annihilates either nonorthogonal companion. These three transpositions realize every permutation with at most two reflections. U permutes the same three rays. After undoing its permutation with at most two reflections, the resulting unitary fixes |11⟩ and |++⟩ projectively and fixes K pointwise. Fixing the computational point forces preservation of the diagonal Pauli label space, hence a monomial unitary, and fixing |++⟩ forces its phases constant. The differences of each pair among the other three computational points belong to K; preserving their supports fixes all three labels and then fixes the common phase. Thus this remaining unitary is identity.

Consequently I−U is a sum of at most two rank-one stabilizer maps: for two reflections expand I−R₁R₂=(I−R₁)+R₁(I−R₂), and each difference from the identity is a stabilizer projection times two. Composing with the physical encoder and undoing the logical Clifford yields at most two stabilizer rays factoring across the shifted-block cut. This replaces the original entire five-list by a list of at most five all factoring on this cut, impossible already by the local mask bound (the rank lower bound forces length five). This proves n₁≤2, and if n₁>0 then n₁+n₂≤3, so at least two survivors have full local rank four.

In a signed seven-ray proposal whose short hard outcome has exactly two killed rays, all but at most two originally ordinary block products must be among the killed terms. In particular with three ordinary products the killed pair cannot consist entirely of exceptions. This excludes branch patterns, not the entire real-common or imaginary orbit. The real-rare branch remains of rank four.
