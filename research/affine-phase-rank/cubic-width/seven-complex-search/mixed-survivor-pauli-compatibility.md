# Compatibility of mixed four-survivor sector weights

Use the 9,408-list classification of three-product-survivor-census.md. Normalize the two-copy target and each ray to unit Hilbert norm when reporting coefficient magnitudes. Every listed decomposition has ordinary squared coefficient magnitudes 1/4,1/2,1/2 in some order and entangled squared coefficient magnitude 1/2.

Indeed the unnormalized g has squared norm eight. In the local gauges g=a+p, the possible (‖a‖²,‖p‖²) for plane, point and full inside types are (4,4),(4,8),(8,4). In each case the extra ray e normalized to p on the quotient has ‖e‖=‖p‖. One direct verification of the last statement uses the representatives g=1−2δ, where δ marks 111. For plane H₀={z=0}, take a=1_{H₀}, p=g1_{H₁} and e=−2δ. Here e−p=−1_{H₁} and span(a,e−p) is an encoded-qubit code. Translating within a half and correcting by the diagonal quadratic that preserves g gives four different point extras of the same norm. For point inside a=−2δ, p=1, let e=1−2δ−2δ' for any other point δ'. Then e−p=−2δ−2δ' lies with a in the computational two-point code, while e is a quadratic sign since flipping at two points cancels the degree-three coefficient. For full inside a=1, p=−2δ, let e=1_H−2δ for any of the seven affine planes through 111. It is a stabilizer as a plane restriction of g, and e−p=1_H lies with a in the encoded qubit spanned by the two complementary plane indicators. These constructions realize the 4,7,7 local choices of the reviewed catalogue and thus exhaust the cases; the respective extra squared norms are 4,8,4.

The three normalized ordinary squared weights are ‖a‖²‖f‖²/64, ‖e‖²‖b‖²/64, and ‖p‖²‖q‖²/64 in the earlier notation. The compatible classes are plane/point, point/plane and full/full. The two equal logical Bell amplitudes give squared weight 2‖a‖²‖b‖² r/64 for the entangled fourth ray, where r is the catalogue's relative squared perpendicular norm, namely 1,1,1/4 respectively. This gives the claimed four numbers. With fixed remote rays the complete residual tensor and all three scalars, including their relative phases once gauges are set, are unique.

**Full local Pauli sector.** In a seven-ray representation with three ordinary block products and four other stabilizers, take a signed Pauli projector Π on one party X. Assume Πg≠0, exactly one of the four other rays survives, and a further stabilizer bra within that sector exposes an entangled fourth remote survivor. The remote product rays b₁,b₂,b₃ are fixed and independent; each of their local triples is independent by the catalogue. Set B=span(b_i), W=B+span(G₂). The only stabilizer ray in W outside B is the catalogued residual E₀. Indeed every such ray supplies a four-term completion; all three coefficients on the products are nonzero by two-copy rank four. A product completion cannot occur: in the independent row and column triples its matrix is, up to scale, the difference of a rank-one target and an invertible three-by-three diagonal, hence of rank at least two. The catalogue gives uniqueness among entangled completions.

Across X versus the remote blocks the projected exceptional tensor has its remote Schmidt code in W, and this code is not in B. A stabilizer code of dimension at least two crossing a hyperplane has at least two distinct stabilizer rays outside it. To see this, choose an encoded stabilizer basis. If just one basis vector is outside, its normalized plus and minus superpositions with any other basis vector are additional encoded stabilizers outside. Consequently the projected exception has remote rank one, with remote ray E₀. Comparing coefficients in the independent remote basis b₁,b₂,b₃,E₀ forces Πg and all three projected ordinary local factors to be proportional to the projected exceptional local stabilizer. In particular Πg is stabilizer: for CCZ only either pure-Z outcome (probability t=1/2), or the rare real-shifted outcome (t=1/4), is possible, by the local Pauli outcome classification. The original exceptional Schmidt rank on X is at most two: the other projection either vanishes or is a Pauli translate of the first, since an anticommuting stabilizer of the original exchanges the outcomes whenever both are present.

For each unit-gauged physical ordinary coefficient λ_i and the surviving exceptional coefficient λ_F the resulting necessary equalities are
|λ_i|² = γ_i t/q_i,    |λ_F|² = (1/2)t/q_F,
where q is that state's probability of Π and the catalogued γ_i are 1/4,1/2,1/2. Each nonzero stabilizer Pauli probability is 1 or 1/2. These equalities use the same physical coefficients across different projections; the quarter-weight index is determined by the supplied ordinary remote factors. They are conditions for a sector of the stated kind, not a claim that both complementary full projectors can each kill three of four nonzero exceptions.

**Paired local bras.** Suppose two distinct orthogonal stabilizer bras on X are the two remaining logical basis outcomes in a fixed rank-two stabilizer code Q, and both retain g, leave the same three ordinary products, and expose an entangled fourth (possibly from different original rays). In the same fixed ordinary remote gauges, uniqueness forces the three normalized ordinary coefficients to agree between the two outcomes. Thus for each i, P_Q a_i is a fixed nonzero scalar multiple of P_Q g for the local ordinary ray a_i. The projected g is therefore a stabilizer. Both its logical amplitudes are nonzero and must have equal magnitude with fourth-root relative phase. This last conclusion requires a common stabilizer measurement basis with its conventional Clifford phase gauge.
