# Local code quotient recognition for CCZ

Let g(x)=(−1)^{x₁x₂x₃}. For a proper nonzero three-qubit stabilizer code Q, the target in the quotient by Q is nonzero, since g has no Pauli eigenlabel. Its stabilizer dictionary consists of projected stabilizer rays. The following are equivalent.

1. The projected target is on a dictionary ray.
2. Every nonidentity signed Pauli check fixing Q has a stabilizer as its opposite outcome on g.
3. Q contains a member of one of the following fifteen secant pairs: the seven pairs of restrictions of g to complementary binary affine planes, and the eight pairs formed by a point mass δ_p and the full chirp h_p=g(1−2δ_p).

Each listed vector is a stabilizer: restricting the cubic to two affine parameters leaves degree at most two, and flipping the cubic at one point cancels its top Boolean coefficient. Every listed pair spans g. Thus (3) implies (1). If (1) holds, subtracting a multiple of one stabilizer from g gives a vector in Q, killed by the opposite projector of any signed check. Its action on the stabilizer must be nonzero since g has no Pauli eigenlabel, so (2) follows.

For (2) implies (3), use the local outcome classification in [the Pauli-outcome note](pauli-outcome-four.md). Every check with a nonzero shift a must be a real Pauli whose selected sign is frequent on g. The signed amplitude relation for that check fails at exactly two inputs; these form an a-directed edge since the disagreement bit is translation invariant along a. Call it the bad edge. A nontrivial pure-Z check has an agreeing affine plane H. Multiplication of a shifted check P with this Z check replaces its bad set E by E symmetric-difference H-complement; its cardinality must still be two. Thus E lies inside H-complement. Two independent pure-Z checks cannot coexist with any shifted check, since no input has opposite outcomes for all three nonidentity checks of a binary two-generator subgroup. In the presence of just one pure-Z check, every shifted check agrees on H and its shift preserves H by commutation. Hence the plane atom 1_H g lies in Q. When every check is pure Z a point mass lies in Q.

When there is no pure Z check, choose a basis of signed checks with independent shifts a_i. The real signed translation derivatives of g commute. In binary notation for the bad-edge indicators this gives
1_{E_i}(x)+1_{E_i}(x+a_j)=1_{E_j}(x)+1_{E_j}(x+a_i).
Thus the affine planes E_i+span(a_j) and E_j+span(a_i) coincide. In that plane the two distinct edge directions intersect at a point. For three independent shifts, pairwise intersecting edges have a common point: three different intersections would make a triangle whose edge directions sum to zero. With one or two shifts a common point also exists. Flipping the amplitude at this point p corrects the bad edge in each generating direction, so the full secant chirp h_p lies in Q. This proves the criterion without a census. Applying it to one-dimensional Q shows the list of thirty spectator rays is complete.

For a putative five-product and two-exception list, choose different parties A,B and kill one exception by its A Schmidt-code quotient, the other by its B Schmidt-code quotient. The untouched party retains g. If both quotient targets miss their dictionaries, their remote product needs at least three projected product atoms: a rank-one tensor expressed as a sum of two products must have a repeated local direction equal to the target on that side. The reviewed [five-product remote argument](five-product-remote-erasure.md) uses only the dictionary span condition on the remote side and the three-qubit stabilizer catalogue on the untouched side. It excludes the resulting five-term representation. Thus at least one of the two selected codes must contain one of the thirty local rays in every valid ordered erasure. This is an encoder-sensitive obstruction; it does not exclude a normal-form profile independently of the encoders.
