# Two factors of Pauli nullity at least two require at least three stabilizer summands

Let u and v be nonzero pure states on arbitrary finite qubit registers. A state's Pauli nullity is the number of its qubits minus the binary rank of its signed Pauli stabilizer group. Equivalently the smallest stabilizer code containing that state has dimension two raised to its nullity. If both factors have nullity at least two, then u⊗v has exact complex stabilizer rank at least three, even allowing entangled summands and arbitrary coefficients.

Flatten the target to a rank-one matrix with column u and row proportional to the transpose of v. In matrix notation the associated input ray is the conjugate of v, which has the same nullity. Suppose the target is A+B where A and B are nonzero scalar multiples of bipartite stabilizer matrices. Each has Schmidt rank a power of two, its respective column and input supports are stabilizer codes of that dimension, and it is proportional to an encoded Clifford isometry between them. These statements follow by the bipartite stabilizer Bell normal form. A one-summand product stabilizer has stabilizer local factors.

Write a=rank A and b=rank B; |a−b|≤1. If they differ, the ranks must be one and two. The larger matrix is the difference of the rank-one target and the smaller matrix. Its column space is the two-dimensional span of their column rays and contains u; likewise its input code contains the conjugate of v. Both nullities then are at most one. If a=b≤2, at least one of u and the conjugate of v belongs respectively to the column or input space of A. Otherwise adjoining the target's independent column and row makes rank(target−A)=a+1, contradicting b=a. This confines at least one factor to a stabilizer code of dimension at most two.

It remains that both ranks equal r≥4. Their column spaces intersect in dimension at least r−1 since their difference after sign change has rank one; likewise the input spaces. A nonempty intersection of stabilizer codes is itself a stabilizer code (incompatible signs or anticommuting checks preclude a common nonzero vector), so these intersection dimensions are powers of two and hence equal r. The two matrices therefore have identical input and output codes. After Clifford encoding/decoding, the rank-one target is proportional to I−cU for a logical Clifford unitary U on log₂r qubits. Since its nullity as a matrix equals r−1, c has magnitude one; absorb it as a unitary global phase, giving eigenvalues 1 with multiplicity r−1 and z≠1 once. The eigenvalue ratio z is a root of unity by projective finiteness.

For any logical Clifford U the squared trace is zero or a power of two at most r², by the overlap formula between its normalized vectorization and the maximally entangled stabilizer. A nonscalar therefore has |Tr U|≤r/√2 (the case r² forces scalar by equality in the trace bound). For r≥8, |Tr U|≥r−2>r/√2, a contradiction.

For r=4 the squared trace is 10+6 Re z. It is at least four and below sixteen, leaving four or eight. The latter implies z+z^{-1}=−2/3, impossible for a rational algebraic integer. Thus z=−1 and U is exactly the reflection I−2|φ⟩⟨φ| for its exceptional eigenray φ. A Clifford's normalized Pauli coefficients have constant nonzero magnitude by measuring its stabilizer vectorization in the Bell basis. Here the identity coefficient is 1/2 and the squared magnitudes sum to one, so exactly three other coefficients of magnitude 1/2 occur. In the reflection formula these three Paulis have expectation of magnitude one on φ; in particular there are two independent commuting signed stabilizers of the two-qubit φ, making it a stabilizer. Consequently the rank-one difference is a multiple of its stabilizer projector and both physical factors are encoded stabilizers (conjugating the input side where appropriate). This contradicts the hypothesized nullities in the final case.

For the CCZ state g, opposite postselection of an imaginary Pauli or of a signed real shifted Pauli held at expectation −1/2 yields a state of nullity two. The projector supplies one Pauli stabilizer. A further independent stabilizer must commute with it; a vanishing joint outcome would require 1+s〈P〉+t〈Q〉+st〈PQ〉=0 for a choice of signs. For s〈P〉=+1/2, the remaining two terms, each of magnitude at most 1/2, cannot achieve this. For imaginary P the first expectation is zero, and both Q and PQ would need nonzero (real) expectations, impossible since commuting with a real Q leaves PQ imaginary. These facts use the usual CCZ expectation formula: each shifted Pauli has expectation 0 or ±1/2, pure nonidentity Z has zero, and imaginary Paulis have zero on the real target.

In a putative seven-ray G₃ decomposition with five ordinary products and two exceptional rays, the two exceptions cannot respectively have such bad held local signs on distinct parties. Projecting to their opposite signs kills both and leaves a two-party remote target of rank at least three by this theorem. This contradicts the five-product remote erasure theorem in five-product-remote-erasure.md. The product-state rank bound itself is independent of the seven-ray problem.
