# Exact classification of rank-four Pauli outcomes on three cubic blocks

Let P be a nonidentity Hermitian nine-qubit Pauli, and let s be an outcome sign. The unnormalized vector (I+sP)G3 has complex stabilizer rank four exactly when either all its active original blocks are pure Z, or it acts on just one block as a real Pauli with nonzero X part and s is the less probable outcome. In every other case its rank is at least five.

The one-active-block contraction by a separating two-point bra in rank-six-lower.md works for any global P and sign, giving the general lower bound four. Suppose a decomposition of length four exists. Apply the same P projector to its atoms and absorb scalars, so that all four atoms have the target P eigenvalue and survive. For every signed stabilizer Q of any one of them, Q commutes globally with P. Project to the opposite Q outcome. This leaves at most three atoms. If on some active block P_i and Q_i were a separated local pair, the corresponding joint outcome would retain rank at least four by local-pair-catalog.md. Consequently every local projection of the entire stabilizer space on an active block consists of labels dependent on P_i or in an exceptional plane with P_i. Imaginary P_i would force dimension at most one, impossible since the dimension is at least three. For real P_i the whole projected subspace consists of real labels and is isotropic by polarization, hence its dimension equals three. Thus every projected atom is a product across each active block and has a definite local Pauli sign there.

Apply the sign-pattern argument of shared-pauli-sectors.md directly to these four atoms. At three active blocks each of four patterns receives one atom. At two active blocks each of two patterns requires two because the untouched block is g. Their untouched rays are independent, forcing both products on the active side to be proportional to the target there. With one active block the untouched two-block factor has rank four, so its four untouched rays are independent and all four active rays coincide with the projected local target. It follows that every required local outcome must be a stabilizer. Pure Z has two stabilizer outcomes. Real shifted P_i has stabilizer support size two for its rare sign and nonstabilizer support size six for the other. At two or more active blocks both local signs occur. This proves necessity. Conversely each allowed sector is a product of local stabilizers and untouched copies of g. Summing the sign sectors costs at most 2^(k-1) 2^(3-k)=4 terms for k active blocks, proving equality.

In any list of seven for G3, four atoms cannot share a signed nonidentity Pauli. If three share it, the opposite outcome must satisfy the classification above. At most two atoms can share a sign whose opposite outcome has rank at least five. This filter is valid for arbitrary complex coefficients and entangled atoms. It applies once enough signed Pauli memberships are certified.
