# Two GHZ factors or a GHZ and a Bell factor cannot be the sole exception

Suppose g⊗g⊗g is represented by six block-product stabilizers P_i and one entangled stabilizer E locally Clifford-equivalent to two GHZ triples or a GHZ triple times a Bell pair, with local stabilizer ancillas understood. Expand E in the standard product basis for these resources. Its four independent block-product rays D_i all have nonzero coefficients. Their local span dimensions are respectively (4,4,4) or (4,4,2), up to permutation. Removing any one of the four leaves dimensions (3,3,3) or (3,3,2), since in the second case the third party has each GHZ label twice.

The list consisting of the target, the six P_i, and the four D_i has a full-support dependence, so its span dimension is at most ten. Each party span includes both g and the corresponding proper stabilizer code supporting E, which excludes g. Thus the sum of the three local dimensions minus one per party is at least 12 or 10. Lovitz–Petrov Theorem 4 splits the list into direct-sum components. Restriction of the dependence to each component is again a full-support dependence. The component containing the target needs at least nine entries by its block-product rank eight. All others need at least two entries. Thus the two components have sizes nine and two. The latter is a proportional pair. The six P_i are independent by minimality of the original seven-list, and the D_i are independent. Hence the size-two component contains one P and one D.

The nine-element target component is a circuit of span dimension eight, again by minimal block-product rank eight. Its local spans contain g and the three remaining D_i factors. The sum of local dimensions minus one per party is at least nine or eight by the surviving local dimensions above. The splitting theorem forces this circuit to split, a contradiction. The remaining genuinely entangled single-exception profiles are one GHZ or two Bell pairs on a path before additional exclusions.
