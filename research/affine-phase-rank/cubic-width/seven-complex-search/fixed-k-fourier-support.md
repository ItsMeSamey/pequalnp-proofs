# Dimension-free Fourier support for a fixed number of stabilizer atoms

On t triples write T_i=z_i+x_i y_i and χ_s=(-1)^{s·T}. If a vector in W=span_C{χ_s} is a sum of k arbitrary complex-weighted stabilizer rays (entanglement allowed), then it has at most C(k) nonzero coefficients in this Fourier basis, independently of t. For k=3 one may take C(3)=7·2^30. This supplies a cardinality bound; it does not classify the allowed supports.

## Uniform frame gap

A pure stabilizer ray on N qubits has a signed maximal abelian Pauli stabilizer; its binary Pauli label space L is Lagrangian. For two unit rays with label spaces L,L', their squared overlap is zero if the signs conflict on the intersection and otherwise equals 2^{-d}, where d=N−dim(L∩L'). Indeed the density matrix is the average of its signed Pauli group, and Pauli trace orthogonality gives the formula. In particular distinct rays have overlap at most 1/√2. Distance d obeys the triangle inequality by dimension counting.

For each k, independent families of at most k normalized stabilizer rays in arbitrary dimension have Gram minimum eigenvalue at least δ_k>0. Set δ_1=1. Given δ_{k−1}, connect two vertices of a k-ray family when their absolute overlap exceeds ε=δ_{k−1}/(2k). In the disconnected case the component Gram blocks have minimum eigenvalue at least δ_{k−1}. The off-block matrix has operator norm at most kε by its row sums. In the connected case take a spanning tree. Along each edge the two signed stabilizers agree on a subgroup of rank at least N−b with b=ceil(−2log₂ ε). Intersect the stabilizers of successive vertices in tree order: adding a child cuts the current common signed subgroup, already contained in its parent's group, by codimension at most b. Consequently the family is contained in a stabilizer code on at most (k−1)b logical qubits. A Clifford carries the common independent generators to fixed signed Z generators, so discarding pinned qubits leaves pure stabilizer rays and preserves the Gram eigenvalues up to diagonal unitary conjugacy. In that bounded dimension there are finitely many tuples of stabilizer rays; their independent tuples have a positive minimum eigenvalue. Taking the smaller of this minimum and δ_{k−1}/2 proves the claim and makes δ_k computable.

Any unit vector in such a span has squared overlap at least δ_k/k with some member: the frame operator on its range has the same nonzero eigenvalues as the Gram matrix. For dependent lists select an independent sublist.

For a *stabilizer* target in a span of at most three stabilizers, the maximum squared overlap is at least 1/64. Use the same graph with threshold 1/4. In a connected independent list every edge has d≤3, so the common signed subgroup has codimension at most six. The target, lying in this common +1 eigenspace, has these signed stabilizers too. Some overlap is nonzero and the overlap formula then gives at least 2^−6. In a disconnected list, the Gram off-diagonal row sum is at most 1/√2+1/4, so the frame bound gives a squared overlap ≥(1−1/√2−1/4)/3>1/128. By the dyadic overlap formula this is at least 1/64. The one-vertex case is immediate.

## Fiber centers

Apply a Walsh transform only on z. The fiber at s of the W-vector is its coefficient c_s times a common scalar times the full stabilizer chirp (-1)^{Σ_i s_i x_i y_i}. The transformed original rays remain stabilizer rays, and on each nonempty control fiber their normalized data restrictions are stabilizer rays. Each fixed atom has the same unsigned data stabilizer label space L across nonempty fibers. To see this, its support fibers are translates of a fixed data direction H; use a common affine parametrization by local H coordinates and the controls in their support projection. In those coordinates its Clifford phase has a fixed quadratic part, with inter-coordinate terms even in its mod-four exponent. Under a change of controls only the global scalar and a binary linear sign on H change. Translating the fiber and extending the sign to the ambient data space amounts to a Pauli transformation, which preserves unsigned labels.

The target data chirp has label space L_s equal to the graph of its alternating polar B_s; this follows by taking its ratio under each translation. Hence d(L_s,L_r)=rank(B_s−B_r)=2 dist_Ham(s,r). On every active fiber choose a present seed attaining squared overlap ≥δ_k/k (or 1/64 for k=3). Its fixed unsigned label space has distance ≤R=floor(log₂(k/δ_k)) from the target, or R=6 for k=3. Any two indices assigned the same seed have Hamming distance at most R by the triangle inequality. Thus the active indices are covered by at most k radius-R Hamming balls, one centered at any assigned index per nonempty cluster.

## Turning the cover into an absolute support bound

Evaluate all transformed atoms at the zero data input. The target value there is a fixed nonzero scalar times c_s. This gives a sum of k control atoms, with a possibly empty affine support A_i and a fourth-root Clifford phase on it. For a pattern J of exactly those atoms present at some active s₀, let L=∩_{i∈J} A_i have dimension D. J is nonempty. For each negative support intersecting L, impose one affine equation through s₀ disjoint from that negative support inside L. This is possible because its intersection is affine and omits s₀. On L the phase of each positive atom can be expressed globally as a constant times i^p(-1)^q, p an affine bit and q a quadratic bit. Indeed the binary carries in a mod-four sum of bits are quadratic. Fix p and q to their values at s₀. The Boolean product of all constraint indicators on L is a polynomial of degree ≤k−|J|+3|J|≤3k, nonzero at s₀. Every point it accepts has the same active value. The minimum-distance bound for Boolean polynomials (by induction splitting off a variable) gives at least 2^{D−3k} active points on L, also valid if D<3k.

Some D actual index coordinates give a bijective projection from L to the D-cube. Each Hamming ball therefore contains at most Σ_{j=0}^{min(D,R)} binom(D,j) points of L. It follows that
2^{D−3k} ≤ k Σ_{j=0}^{min(D,R)} binom(D,j).
For fixed k,R this bounds D by a constant D_k since the right hand side grows polynomially. At most 2^k−1 such nonempty patterns cover the active set, so C(k)=(2^k−1)2^{D_k} suffices. For k=3 and R=6, D=31 already violates the inequality (4,194,304>2,827,947); increasing D doubles the left hand side and grows the truncated binomial sum by strictly less than two. Thus D≤30, as claimed.
