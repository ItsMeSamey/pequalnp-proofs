# Four ordinary products from one local erasure: at most eight lifts per chart

Fix three nonproduct nine-qubit stabilizer rays E₁,E₂,E₃ with arbitrary independent encoders. Use unnormalized Gaussian-integer representatives with entries zero or fourth roots. Assume g_X is outside the sum S_X of their three local Schmidt supports on X. Dependent exceptional vectors cannot occur in a seven-term minimum by the unrestricted lower bound. We give an exact completion test for these supplied rays.

Erasing S_X while retaining g_X leaves G_YZ in four ordinary stabilizer products. All four are necessary and independent by the two-copy rank theorem. Their local factor spans contain the two local targets and have total dimension at most five by Sylvester. Thus one span is a two-space; call its party Y, also trying the reverse choice. Its two stabilizers are a secant pair y₀,y₁ through g_Y. Both appear twice since each target coordinate needs two Z rays to span g_Z. For each k the two Z rays z_k0,z_k1 likewise form a secant pair through g_Z. The local secant classification gives fifteen unordered physical choices per pair. Fix a deterministic ordering of each selected pair. At most 2·15³=6750 charts are needed, counting possible duplicates. In each chart the four vectors r_kj=y_k⊗z_kj are independent; let R be their span. The unique expansion g_Y⊗g_Z=Σ_i a_i r_i has every coefficient nonzero, with i indexing the four labels.

Let P project the remote space to its quotient by R, and set K=ker[(I_X⊗P)E₁,(I_X⊗P)E₂,(I_X⊗P)E₃]. Any exceptional coefficient vector must lie in K. For α∈K expand the actual unprojected difference
[
 G₃-\sum_h \alpha_h E_h=\sum_i x_i(\alpha)\otimes r_i,
 \qquad x_i(\alpha)=a_i g_X+L_i\alpha .
]
Here each L_i is linear on K and takes values in S_X, by dual extraction from the original exceptional terms. Thus x_i is never zero and its scale is fixed by [x_i]=a_i[g_X] modulo S_X. In particular, a projective stabilizer ray can match at most one actual vector for a given coordinate i. The four L_i are jointly injective: if all vanish, the weighted combination of exceptional vectors vanishes, and the exceptional vectors are independent.

No nonproduct stabilizer lies in X⊗R. Indeed its Y support would be in span(y₀,y₁); a two-dimensional Schmidt support for a stabilizer is a stabilizer code, while this two-space contains g_Y and cannot be such a code. Thus Y rank is one, with factor y_k by the two-ray line bound. Its Z support must now lie in span(z_k0,z_k1), since the y coordinates of R separate. The same argument forces Z rank one. Hence every projected exceptional column is nonzero, so projected rank zero is impossible. Projected rank three gives K=0 and no solution.

At projected rank two, dim K=1. Any zero map L_i forces x_i=a_i g_X, not a stabilizer, discarding the chart. Otherwise one nonzero map is injective, and its candidate rays belong to a projective line through g_X containing at most two stabilizer rays. There are at most two exact coefficient vectors α to test, including arbitrary complex coefficients. A nonzero L_i value cannot be collinear with g_X since it belongs to S_X.

At projected rank one, dim K=2. Again discard zero coordinate maps. If any L_i has rank two it is injective; its candidates are contained in the three-space span(g_X,im L_i), which has at most eight stabilizer rays by the reviewed local census in seven-product-exclusion.md. Otherwise all maps have rank one: choose two with distinct kernel lines, possible by joint injectivity. Each has at most two candidate rays on its line through g_X, giving at most four simultaneous coefficient vectors. The uniform bound is eight per chart.

For the injective map or selected pair enumerate the physical local stabilizer rays in the indicated spaces (there are only 1080 on the block), solve x_i(α)=t_i v_i for the normalization t_i and the common α, and discard inconsistent choices. Uniqueness follows from the quotient normalization and injectivity just proved. Test the other reconstructed vectors against the stabilizer catalogue, require all three exceptional coefficients nonzero, and verify the recovered sum on all 512 amplitudes. Every linear solve uses exact Gaussian-rational data; uniqueness precludes missing a solution with nonrational coefficients. Conversely every successful check gives the ordinary X rays with their actual scales, their four remote product partners, and the three physical exceptional amplitudes. The bound is per supplied triple satisfying the X erasure premise; no triple enumeration or search result is asserted.
