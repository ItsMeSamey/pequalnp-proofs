# Sector rigidity for six terms

Suppose two atoms share a signed nonidentity P in a six-list. Project onto its opposite outcome. Every other atom C survives or fewer than four remain. Its projected stabilizer C' has stabilizers generated (up to signs) by Stab(C) intersect P-perp and P. The local projection V_i of that intersection, on any active block i of P, is totally isotropic and commutes with P_i by the finite local pair catalog: every label is dependent on P_i or belongs with it to a real commuting exceptional plane. The whole projection V_i+span(P_i) of Stab(C') is isotropic. Its dimension is 3+e_i for local Schmidt rank 2^{e_i}; thus C' factors across every active block. Since C' is a P eigenstate, each active factor is a P_i eigenstate.

With k active blocks all 2^{k−1} opposite-sector local sign patterns occur in the target (g has no Pauli stabilizer). Every surviving atom contributes to just one. For k=3 each pattern therefore gets exactly one atom, forcing all active local outcomes to be stabilizers. For k=2 each pattern needs at least two atoms from its untouched g. Each indeed gets exactly two. If any projected active local state were nonstabilizer, the sector would be a simple tensor across that block and the nonstabilizer untouched remainder, represented by two elementary stabilizer tensors. Two elementary tensors with rank-one sum have proportional vectors on at least one side, which contradicts both sides being nonstabilizer. For k=1 the four survivors factor as s_j tensor t_j, where t_j are stabilizers of the untouched two blocks. Contraction onto the local projected target implies the four t_j are independent and all used, since chi(G2)=4. Uniqueness in that basis forces each s_j proportional to the projected local target, a stabilizer.

Pure nonidentity Z on one block has two stabilizer outcomes, since on a plane a three-bit cubic becomes quadratic. A real local nonidentity Pauli with nonzero X shift has expectation ±1/2; summing the full sign vector with its Pauli translate leaves exactly two nonzero coordinates in the less likely outcome (a stabilizer), and six in the more likely (not a stabilizer). Imaginary local factors were already excluded by the catalog. Thus if P acts on two or three blocks, it must be pure Z on all active blocks. If P acts on one block, it must be pure Z or its shared sign must equal the more probable outcome of a real Pauli with nonzero X shift. This leaves six-term existence open.
