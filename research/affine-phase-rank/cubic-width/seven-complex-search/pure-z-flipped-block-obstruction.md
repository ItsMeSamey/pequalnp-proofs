# A common flipped computational block obstructs paired pure-Z sectors

Consider a pure-Z Pauli on two or three original blocks. Suppose three parent atoms are fixed in one sign (called positive), and let m be the number of atoms present in both outcomes. The negative decomposition has exactly four survivors by pauli-outcome-four.md. Its classification forces each survivor to factor across every active block, where its definite local parity component is the native plane restriction of g up to scale. Both sign patterns on any chosen active block occur twice among these four. This includes the two-block link, for which each of the two global patterns receives two atoms. The other branch of each mixed parent is a Pauli translate of that negative survivor. The pattern of sign flips on active blocks has odd weight. On any flipped block the positive component is supported on its new parity plane and its data chirp is a translate of the opposite native chirp, multiplied by a character.

Use target-preserving coordinates with local parity s and data p∈F₂², so g(s,p) has phase (−1)^(s p₁p₂). Fix an active block flipped by every mixed parent. Suppose m≤3. Among the projected positive mixed components on each individual sign s there are at most two, since their negative predecessors have the opposite sign. The phases of these wrong components on that sign are the opposite quadratic (1−s)p₁p₂ plus affine functions; restore arbitrary scalar gauges separately. Choose a common nonzero direction d in the two data bits on which the relative characters of any pair on a single side coincide. With m≤3 at most one side imposes a nontrivial homogeneous condition, so such a d exists.

On each side s, the difference of the phase of a wrong component and the native phase has quadratic part p₁p₂. Its derivative along d is affine with nonzero linear part, constant on each translate of span(d) with opposite values on the two translates. Choose the translate where this derivative is one. Any second wrong component has the same derivative there. If a side has no wrong component choose either translate. In the whole three-bit block, the selected two-point lines, one per control sign with common direction, form a single affine plane. Choose a stabilizer bra on that plane matching the native relative phases within each line. This is possible with a binary quadratic phase on the two free bits, and even all four native amplitudes on the plane can be matched exactly. The bra kills all wrong components on both sides by the two-point cancellation and has equal nonzero overlaps with the two correct native sectors.

After this contraction the positive target is proportional to G₂: the two local projections have the same overlap, so their complementary two-party parity outcomes sum to the identity. All m mixed positive components vanish; only the three positive-definite parents remain, violating rank four. For m=0 the same direct contradiction follows from the positive branch rank. If all four negative survivors are mixed, there are two wrong components per sign at a common flipped block; the same proof works unless the two character differences on the two sides are independent. Zero differences create no obstruction to finding d.

In particular one mixed parent is impossible, and with two mixed parents their flip sets must be disjoint. This concerns the three-definite-parent case and does not assume a minimal positive branch.
