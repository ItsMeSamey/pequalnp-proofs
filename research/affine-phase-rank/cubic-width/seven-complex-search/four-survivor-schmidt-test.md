# Entanglement needed among four survivors of a block erasure

Suppose four pure stabilizers with arbitrary complex coefficients represent normalized g⊗g. Then either at least two have Schmidt rank ≤2 across the original two blocks, or there is precisely one rank-one product and both of its local factors are from the H,M high-fidelity classes of six-must-share.md. In particular if all four are entangled then at least two have Schmidt rank exactly two.

If two share a signed nonidentity Pauli P, project to the opposite outcome. The two-copy outcome bound gives rank ≥2, so both survivors are nonzero and form a two-term decomposition. As in five-product-remote-erasure.md and pauli-outcome-four.md, the local-pair separation test forces these two projected rays to factor on every active block: a separated local pair would enable a further projector killing one atom while retaining a contraction to the rank-two single copy. With two original blocks, factorization on even one suffices. Each survivor before projection either was already in that eigenvalue or is the sum of the projected ray and a Pauli translate up to scalar, by using an anticommuting signed stabilizer of the survivor. Its original Schmidt rank is therefore at most two.

Without such a shared pair the stabilizer trace formula bounds every off-diagonal Gram entry by 1/8 in modulus. The smallest Gram eigenvalue is at least 5/8, so span membership of the target requires the sum of its four squared overlaps to be at least 5/8. For local Schmidt ranks R=2,4,8, the flat Schmidt spectrum and code-mass estimates in rank-six-lower.md give upper bounds P_R(g)^2/R equal respectively to 25/128, 9/64, 1/8 (here P_R denotes maximum projected mass in a rank-R local stabilizer code). If all four are entangled with at most one rank-two ray the sum is at most 79/128, below 5/8. If just one ray is a product and the other three have ranks ≥4, the product must contribute at least 13/64. A local stabilizer outside H (full maximizer sign) and M (native plane restriction) has squared overlap ≤5/16, whereas all local overlaps are ≤9/16, so a product having either endpoint outside H,M gives at most 45/256 <13/64. These observations prove the test.

In particular consider a seven-term proposal with three block products and four exceptional atoms. Any single-block stabilizer bra killing the three product factors while retaining g must leave four nonzero, independent remote stabilizers for G2, subject to the test above. In every nonzero exceptional image, each Bell already shared on the two unmeasured parties in its local normal form doubles the residual Schmidt rank: those remote Bell tensors are untouched by the bra, and remote local encoders preserve the Schmidt spectrum. A separating bra always exists if the three product factors on the measured block lie in one stabilizer basis; the support of g in such a basis has at least four elements (its three independent measured Pauli signs must have full affine span, or g would be a Pauli eigenstate). Thus if the three local product factors share a basis it is impossible that all four exceptions have at least one untouched Bell, with three of them having at least two. The criterion is not a general exclusion of four exceptional rays.
