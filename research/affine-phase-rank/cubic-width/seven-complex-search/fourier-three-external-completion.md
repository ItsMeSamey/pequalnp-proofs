# Three stabilizer atoms cannot synthesize four independent Fourier coefficients

Use the definitions of fourier-external-secants.md and the proper-fiber and Pauli derivative lemmas in fourier-three-external.md (SHA256 272257eefdd0bdefe24db86138df32081ce9749f11931da269e70105fb7ba256). The claims there for an untouched tetrahedron only require its four amplitudes to be nonzero: every individual slice can be rescaled separately for those claims. The preliminary projection from a *plane* to G₂ does require uniform amplitudes; it is not used to exclude tetrahedra.

**Theorem.** For all t, if a vector in W is a linear combination of at most three arbitrary complex supported stabilizer atoms, its internal Fourier support has affine dimension at most two. A decomposition strictly improving the native 2^t expansion of the indicator T=0 needs at least four external rays.

It suffices to rule out four affinely independent active controls in dimension three. For arbitrary t restrict the controls to their affine three-hull and take three original coordinates whose restrictions give an affine chart. Fix every other data pair at zero and decode the controls to these coordinates; these stabilizer operations preserve the claimed upper bound of three. All four specified amplitudes remain nonzero. We may work with exactly three nonzero summands, since the two-term case is covered by the secant theorem. The proper-fiber result requires all three atoms to have full data support on their occurring control slices. Denote this occurrence by σ_j(s)∈{0,1}.

For v∈K with precisely two nonzero block components, let m_j=M_jv. Suppose they are not all equal. The derivative lemma forces their affine span to contain the differences B_ei v for both nonzero blocks, so the three m_j are affinely independent. At every active control the exact normal identity in F₂ is

    B_s v = m₁ + σ₂(s)(m₂+m₁) + σ₃(s)(m₃+m₁).

Indeed apply I−P₁ from the derivative lemma. If neither remaining atom occurs its image vanishes, forcing zero target normal. If one occurs its derivative has the single nonzero normal. If both occur, their two independent normals define intersecting nonparallel halfspaces, each of whose exclusive cosets survives. The target derivative cannot be zero and cannot be full because the union omits a quarter of the cube. Its hyperplane contains both exclusive cosets, so its normal is the sum of their normals. This proves the identity also when the original pivot atom is absent on that slice. Repeat with pivot 2 and subtract the two identities. As m₁+m₂≠0, one obtains σ₁+σ₂+σ₃=1 at every active slice. Across the four independent controls, B_s v takes at least three distinct values, since its affine image has dimension two. Thus at least three distinct odd occurrence signatures appear, including at least two different singleton signatures. Their two atoms have zero data imaginary parity, since each appears alone on a full real target up to scalar. This would leave rank of the parity forms at most one, which the derivative lemma forbids: in the rank-one case a parity form nonzero on h blocks has (27+(−1)^h 3^(3−h))/2 fully nonzero vectors in its kernel (character sum over the three possible nonzero components of each block). In rank zero there are 27. In either case there is more than one, contrary to that lemma.

It follows that the M_j agree on every v∈K of block weight two or three. Let H span these vectors. On data H all three full terms have the same real polar and zero data imaginary parity. Dividing by the common real quadratic gives at most three ordinary characters on each active control slice, so the target quadratic there has Fourier support ≤3 and hence polar rank zero. Thus B_s|H is independent of the active index, which on a tetrahedron implies B_ei|H=0 for each i. Every block projection π_i(H) has dimension at most one.

The same projection bound holds for K. If π_i(K) has dimension two, choose v∈K projecting outside π_i(H). This v must be supported just on i by the definition of H. Some w∈K has a nonzero component outside i since dim K≥3. Its i component belongs to π_i(H), since either w has weight at least two or this component is zero. Then v+w has weight at least two and projects outside π_i(H), a contradiction. Thus K is exactly the direct sum of a line in each two-bit block. Its weight-two and weight-three vectors span K, hence H=K.

Choose local data coordinates (X_i,Y_i) with K the Y directions. Any invertible two-bit change preserves the mixed coefficient of xᵢyᵢ, so the mixed part of q_s becomes Σs_iX_iY_i, up to affine terms. Twist every slice by the same stripped real quadratic with polar R₁. On K the differences R_j+R₁ vanish with one argument in K, since the M matrices agree there and ℓ_j vanish there. Thus on any given control each occurring atom has the form f_j(X)(−1)^(h_j(s)·Y), with f_j nowhere zero. The target's Y² polar vanishes after this twist, because B_s|K=R₁|K. Its unique Y-frequency at X is h(s)+(diag(s)+A)X for a fixed 3×3 matrix A. Fourier transform only in Y. If this frequency takes at least two values, each value gives a component nonzero for some X and zero for other X. It needs at least two summands with that Y-frequency because a single contributing atom has f_j nowhere zero. Two distinct frequencies thus require at least four summands. Consequently diag(s)=A on every active slice, impossible on four independent controls. This excludes all tetrahedra.

Finally any saving with three external rays leaves at least four native coefficients untouched. For more than four untouched indices the Fourier support cannot fit in a plane. For exactly four in a plane, project controls to it, keep two coordinate-independent data pairs, pin the rest at zero and decode. All four untouched amplitudes are equal and nonzero, yielding G₂, which has complex stabilizer rank four. Smaller external counts are covered by the two-ray theorem. This proves the stated lower bound on the required number of external rays; it does not determine the unrestricted rank of G₃.
