# Two simple Bell exceptions on different original links cannot suffice

Suppose five three-block product stabilizers together with E_AB⊗c and a⊗F_BC represent g⊗g⊗g, where E,F have Schmidt rank two on their respective links and c,a are stabilizers. Write C_A,C_B^E,C_B^F,C_C for the corresponding local rank-two stabilizer Schmidt codes. They exclude g, which has no nonidentity stabilizing Pauli. All coefficients in a seven-term candidate are nonzero by the unrestricted lower bound.

Expand each Bell in two product stabilizers by its encoded Schmidt basis. In the resulting ten-entry list (including the target) there is a dependence with all coefficients nonzero. It cannot split as a direct sum: the target component requires at least nine entries by the reviewed eight-product lower bound, and any other component supporting the nonzero dependence requires at least two. The connected-list splitting theorem of Lovitz and Petrov therefore makes the list span dimension, which is at most nine, strictly greater than the sum of d_i−1 for the three local factor-span dimensions d_i. Thus their sum is at most eleven. Each local span contains g and a Bell code, so every d_i is at least three and at least one is exactly three.

In a three-dimensional local span U containing g and a rank-two stabilizer code C0, U=C0+span(g). By the local census of seven-product-exclusion.md there are at most eight stabilizer rays in U; six lie in C0. The at most two exterior stabilizer rays do not span g. Otherwise two distinct exterior rays r,t spanning g would give the forbidden seven-multiset r,r,t,t plus three distinct stabilizer rays from C0. Every four entries either contain r,t or contain at least two distinct code rays and an exterior ray, so span g. Choose f0 annihilating C0 but retaining g and f1 annihilating all exterior types while retaining g. Each stabilizer ray in U survives at most one covector; a Bell on this party survives only f1. All contractions below permit arbitrary linear functionals. A surviving vector contracted out of a Bell lies in its opposite proper Schmidt code, so cannot be proportional to g. With ≤2 product tensors whose factors on both sides are all nonparallel to g, the sum cannot equal a nonzero multiple of g⊗g: a two-product rank-one sum has proportional factors on one side, which would have to be g.

**Small endpoint.** If d_A=3 (interchange endpoints if necessary), apply the two covectors for C_A. The f0 contraction leaves G2 expressed using at least four stabilizers: the AB Bell is killed, and any surviving BC Bell was contracted on its spectator. Consequently f1 leaves at most three terms. If it kills a, these are products whose C factors are stabilizers and whose B factors are stabilizers or a vector from C_B^E, none proportional to g. With three terms Sylvester's rank inequality on their matrix sum gives d_B'+d_C'≤4. Both local spans have dimension at least two since neither side contains g as a factor, so both equal two. The C-span through g contains at most two stabilizer rays by the same local census. Each of the two stabilizer directions must have a group of terms whose B sum equals a nonzero multiple of g, requiring at least two terms each. This is impossible. If f1(a)≠0, a must be inside C_A. Then f0 kills both exceptions and requires at least four exterior ordinary products, leaving f1 at most one ordinary product. Its nonzero multiple of F_BC must complete G2 using at most two other product terms. Subtracting this Bell from G2 gives ordinary matrix rank three because g is outside both of its local Schmidt supports. This again is impossible.

**Small middle.** Suppose d_B=3. The two distinct-code possibility would put at least eleven stabilizer rays in this three-space through g (two sets of six, intersecting in at most one). Hence the two middle codes agree. Apply f0 and f1 there. At least four ordinary products survive f0 and hence at most one survives f1. The f1 branch must therefore have exactly three nonzero product terms, since no local output factor is proportional to g. Absorb coefficients into factors and write its normalized equation on A,C as
    g⊗g = t⊗w + u⊗c + a⊗v,
with t,w,a,c stabilizer directions and u∈C_A, v∈C_C. Quotient A by span(u,a). If [g]≠0, equality forces w proportional to g, impossible. Thus g∈span(u,a). Similarly g∈span(v,c). These are two-dimensional spaces, with both coordinates of g nonzero in each indicated basis. In particular a and c are exterior to their corresponding codes. The same quotient equations imply t∈span(u,a), w∈span(v,c). Only t⊗w can supply the nonzero u⊗v coordinate of the target, so t,w have nonzero u,v coordinates. If t were exterior to C_A, it and a would be distinct exterior stabilizer rays spanning g inside the three-space C_A+span(g): indeed a∈span(g,u) because the coordinates of g are nonzero. This contradicts the local census consequence above. Hence t is inside C_A and proportional to u (the intersection of span(u,a) with the code is the line of u). Similarly w is proportional to v. The right hand side then has zero a⊗c coefficient, contrary to the left hand side.

Both possible locations of a minimal local dimension have been excluded. This concerns exactly two simple Bell exceptions on different links; it does not decide the general seven-term question.
