# No shared Pauli on one original block in a six-list

In the one-block case of shared-pauli-sectors.md, each of four opposite-sector atoms equals h tensor t_j, where h is the same stabilizer (the opposite projected g). Contracting with h shows the t_j independent and all used, since chi(G2)=4. Uniqueness in that basis forces each local factor proportional to h.

Every surviving original atom crossing both sectors has a stabilizer Pauli W anticommuting with P; W maps its opposite projection to its shared projection. Thus the shared piece is a product of a Pauli translate of h and a Pauli translate of t_j. An atom lying entirely in the opposite sector contributes nothing else. Pauli translates of h form one orthonormal stabilizer basis up to phases. In that basis each crossing contributes at most one local location in the shared sector. A,B contribute at most two stabilizer terms to each location.

The shared-sector target has at least three nonzero locations in that basis. For real mixed P with nonzero X shift a, h has exactly two nonzero coordinates constituting one a-line. Its Pauli orbit is two character states (up to fixed relative phase) on each a-line. The six-point shared outcome occupies the other three lines. For pure Z with normal l and D=ker(l), the two conditional g states are quadratic signs on complementary D-planes. Translate one onto the other by v with l(v)=1. Their quadratic phase difference has polar (u,w)↦det(v,u,w), nondegenerate on D. Thus all four Walsh coefficients are nonzero: the shared outcome meets all four Pauli translates of h there.

At each of at least three locations the unmeasured target is proportional to G2 and requires four atoms. In addition to A,B at least two crossing contributions are needed per location, but only four crossings exist. Thus no shared Pauli may act on a single block. Combined with the preceding sector lemma, every shared Pauli of a six-list must be pure Z on two or three original blocks.
