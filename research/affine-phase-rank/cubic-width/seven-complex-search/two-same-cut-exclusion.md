# Two exceptional rays cannot both factor across the same original cut

Suppose a minimal seven-term representation has five block-product stabilizers and two others of the form a⊗E1 and b⊗E2 across X|YZ, where E1,E2 entangle Y and Z. Absorb the nonzero coefficients. All seven factor across X. Every four X rays span g: otherwise an arbitrary covector killing those four and retaining g gives a representation of G2 using at most three stabilizers. By the reviewed local multiset certificate in seven-product-exclusion.md their span dimension is at least four. The target has matrix rank one, so Sylvester's rank inequality makes the joint span S of the seven YZ rays at most four-dimensional. It contains G2, and every basis selected from those stabilizers must have at least four entries by the two-copy theorem. Thus dim S=4.

Let V be the span of the five YZ product rays. If G2 is in V then dim V=4 and V=S. Expanding G2 in four independent product basis vectors uses all four coefficients by the two-copy theorem. The Y and Z factor span dimensions sum to at most five by Sylvester, so one is at most two. It contains g and the corresponding local Schmidt support of E1. The support is a stabilizer code of dimension at least two, and would be a proper code equal to this span and containing g, impossible. Thus G2 is outside V.

The local rays a and b are distinct: if proportional, one can kill both while retaining g and thereby put G2 in V. Quotient the seven-term equation by V on the YZ side, obtaining g⊗[G2]=a⊗[E1]+b⊗[E2], with weights absorbed. Thus g is in span(a,b), with both coordinates nonzero because it is not a stabilizer. Coefficient comparison gives [Ei]=γ_i[G2] with γ_i nonzero for each i. These images span S/V, so dim V=3.

For each i the matrix Di=Ei−γ_i G2 belongs to V and has matrix rank at most three. A bipartite stabilizer has Schmidt rank a power of two; rank eight for Ei would give rank(Di) at least seven. Otherwise both local Schmidt supports are proper stabilizer codes and exclude g. Adding the target therefore increases the rank by one, giving rank(Di)=rank(Ei)+1. Entanglement forces rank(Ei)=2 and rank(Di)=3.

Choose three product rays spanning V. Their Y and Z local span dimensions must both be three since they support Di of rank three. These spaces U_Y,U_Z equal span(g,C_{i,Y}) and span(g,C_{i,Z}) respectively, by the independent-row/column support of the rank-three difference above. All five products in V have their factors in these same spaces. In U_Y both Schmidt codes C_{1,Y},C_{2,Y} have dimension two and contain six encoded one-qubit stabilizer rays each. If distinct, their intersection has dimension at most one, so their union supplies at least eleven different rays. The local-plane certificate in seven-product-exclusion.md bounds the number of stabilizer rays in U_Y, a three-dimensional space through g, by eight. Thus the two codes coincide, as C_Y. The same holds on Z, giving C_Z.

At least four of the five products have Y factor outside C_Y: annihilate C_Y by a covector nonzero on g and both exceptions disappear, while the residual G2 on XZ has rank four. In U_Y there are at most two exterior stabilizer rays, and their span excludes g. Indeed if two distinct exterior rays u,v spanned g, the candidate local multiset u,u,v,v and three different encoded rays of C_Y would contradict the reviewed small-span certificate: every four entries either contain both u,v or contain two distinct code rays and an exterior ray, hence span g. Thus a covector on Y kills all exterior rays yet retains g. Apply it together with a covector on Z that kills C_Z and retains g. Both exceptions disappear by the latter projection, and at most one of five block-products survives the former. The resulting nonzero multiple of g on X cannot be a single stabilizer. This rules out both exceptions sharing an original factorization cut; other two-exception patterns are not addressed.
