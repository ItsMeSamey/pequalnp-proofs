# Four nonzero two-control coefficients exclude three full-data stabilizer types

Assume the partial Walsh target at s=(a,b) is c_s q_s, where q_s=(-1)^(a x1 y1+b x2 y2) and all four c_s are nonzero. Suppose three stabilizer columns have full data fibers at every control where they occur. We use the two-full-fiber polar rigidity of fourier-external-secants.md and the full-twist facts of ../g3-complex-two-copy-pending/proof.md.

At a slice with one or two columns, every occurring column has stripped real data polar equal to B_s, the sign polar of q_s, and thus cannot occur on two such slices. Four nontriple slices cannot be covered. If there is exactly one triple p, the other three are private to three different columns. There each column has zero data imaginary parity; on p it is a real chirp times a constant with the corresponding polar. Divide the slice identity by q_p and Walsh transform on the four data bits: the three terms have support sizes 4,4,16 (relative polar ranks 2,2,4), and their sum has support one, impossible. If exactly two triple slices occur, a column covering either other slice must contain a third control point, so its affine control support is full and it occurs at both nontriple slices. Three triple slices also force all columns full. Therefore every column is globally full.

Relative to unit gauges choose primitive Gaussian integer weights α_j. Such a choice up to overall scale exists by the one-dimensional rational kernel argument in three-external-weights.md; no globally full column can be an internal ray, since its transformed support would occupy one control slice. Rescale c_s accordingly. Put π=1+i. All c_s are Gaussian integers congruent to Σα modulo π, since all column values are units. If this residue is nonzero, write the target as c_00 G2 plus corrections on the individual control fibers. After any full chirp twist the first sum has π-valuation four; each correction sum has valuation at least five or vanishes, since the correction coefficient is divisible by π and each fiber sum is a four-bit quadratic Gauss sum. This violates simultaneous annihilation of three full six-bit columns. Primitivity therefore gives exactly two π-unit weights, say α1,α2, with α3 and all c_s divisible by π.

Write φ_j for the unit-gauged phases. Fix controls and divide by φ1. Subtract the equation at data u=0 from that at u, reducing first modulo π². The terms with coefficients c_s and α3 drop out since their phase differences are divisible by π. Hence φ2/φ1 has constant phase parity in the data. The ratio is a constant unit times (-1)^F(u). Reduce the same subtracted equation modulo π³ and divide by π². For each term with coefficient divisible by π, the reduced difference is an affine binary function: a fourth-root phase relative to its origin is 1+πℓ(u) mod π² for its parity form ℓ. The term with α2 therefore implies F(u)+F(0) is affine, since −2/π² is a unit. Thus φ2/φ1 is a character times a constant on each control slice.

In each slice restrict to the kernel H_s of that character (all data if the character is constant). The first two weighted terms now collapse to one full chirp or zero. The third full column is present. By rigidity of one or two full chirps expressing a nonzero real target chirp up to scalar, the third stripped real polar R agrees with B_s on H_s. Here R is the data polar of that column, fixed across slices: representing its phase as i raised to its affine parity bit times a real quadratic, restricting controls changes the latter only affinely in data. Restriction to the linear subspace H_s commutes with this representation. Hence R+B_s, an alternating form vanishing on a subspace of codimension at most one, has rank at most two.

This is impossible for all four s. In coordinates (x1,y1,x2,y2), the Pfaffian of R+B_s is (R12+a)(R34+b)+R13 R24+R14 R23 over F2. Its ab coefficient is one, so its four evaluations cannot all vanish. A nonzero Pfaffian has rank four. The contradiction finishes the full-data case for arbitrary complex nonzero coefficients. Proper data fibers are outside the scope of this lemma.
