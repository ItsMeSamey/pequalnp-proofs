# Two independently encoded Bell triangles are impossible

Suppose two Bell triangles E,F and five ordinary product stabilizers represent G3. If the two rank-four local codes coincide at two parties, two-local-shared-markers.md excludes the list. Choose two parties B,C with distinct codes. By triangle-local-kernel.md their local intersections have dimension two. The physical E marker restricts on each two-logical-qubit F code to a signed nonidentity Pauli J. As detailed in triangle-ghz-bell-companion.md, postselecting an observable anticommuting with J followed by an inverse isometry into the intersection gives a retraction extending to fix the E support pointwise.

At B label F's logical qubits by their incident Bell edges AB,BC, and at C by AC,BC. On every incident edge where J has a nonidentity component we may measure a noncommuting Pauli axis confined to that edge. If B,C can choose distinct such edges, the two projections collapse two different Bell pairs, leaving at most one Bell and fixed local factors. Before the inverse isometries, the result is therefore product across at least one original cut and has ordinary product rank at most two. Arbitrary local postcomposition preserves both properties or kills the vector.

The only failure to choose distinct edges occurs if both J labels are supported just on their common BC edge. Select a correlated BC axis K whose local Pauli differs from J at both ends (at most two of the three choices are forbidden). At C measure K_C. At B measure K_B times any nonidentity AB-axis Pauli. Both observables anticommute with the respective J. The C outcome first fixes the Bell-partner K_B value, so the B projection then measures the AB edge. Thus both BC and AB collapse, leaving only AC, with the same conclusion after the inverse isometries.

Finally on A use any linear map fixing the E support and killing g, possible because g lies outside this proper code. No condition on its action on F is needed. This also preserves any common spectator direction or kills the associated tensor. The matrix multiplication tensor E would now be a sum of five product tensors and a biseparable tensor of ordinary rank at most two. Fewer than seven products contradict ordinary rank seven. At exactly seven, expand the bipartite matrix in the biseparable term into two rank-one terms with the same spectator ray, impossible by de Groote's optimal decomposition theorem as in triangle-local-kernel.md.
