# Bell exceptions on different links, with a double Bell on one link

Suppose the five ordinary block-product stabilizers are accompanied by E_AB⊗c and a⊗F_BC, with Schmidt ranks r=2 or 4 for E and s=4 for F. The local spectator rays a on A and c on C are stabilizers, with no alignment assumed. Write E_A,E_B,F_B,F_C for the respective local Schmidt support codes. Absorb all nonzero weights into the terms.

Across B the two remote image spaces are M₁=E_A⊗span(c) and M₂=span(a)⊗F_C. Their intersection is zero unless a lies in E_A and c lies in F_C, since its only possible direction is a⊗c. In that exceptional case both original rays share a signed local check on A and another on C (the proper codes E_A,F_C have at least one check each), contradicting two-local-shared-markers.md. Thus M=M₁⊕M₂. The remote target g_A⊗g_C is outside M: quotienting A by span(a) and C by span(c) kills M and preserves the nonzero target image.

The target and two exceptional terms now have linearly disjoint remote ranges, so the B support of their weighted difference is the span of g_B,E_B,F_B. Indeed remote duals can extract the target coefficient, or either Schmidt expansion separately. This difference is the sum of five ordinary products. The four-dimensional proper stabilizer code F_B excludes g_B, so those five ordinary B factors are independent. Taking dual coordinates to them puts each of their AC product directions in V=M+span(g_A⊗g_C).

For an ordinary direction outside M, the same quotient by the two spectator lines gives a nonzero multiple of [g_A]⊗[g_C]. Its A factor therefore lies in span(a,g_A) and differs projectively from a; likewise its C factor lies in span(c,g_C) and differs from c. A line through g on one block contains at most two stabilizer rays by the local exact certificate in seven-product-exclusion.md. With the spectator already on each line there is thus at most one exterior product direction. The target equation in the quotient needs at least two such ordinary terms, since their B factors are stabilizers and g_B is not. For any ordinary product inside M, its image under the two spectator quotients is zero, so its A factor is a or its C factor is c.

Finally quotient A by span(a,g_A). This kills the target, the whole F term, every exterior ordinary product, and every interior product with A factor a. The remaining E term retains Schmidt rank at least r−1 on its AB link: its A support E_A intersects the two-dimensional quotient kernel in dimension at most one since g_A is outside E_A. At least r−1 interior ordinary products with C factor c and A factor outside that kernel are required to match this matrix rank. Quotienting C by span(c,g_C) analogously requires at least s−1 interior ordinary products with A factor a and C factor outside that kernel. These two sets are disjoint. Together with the two exterior terms, the five ordinary products would have to number at least 2+(r−1)+(s−1)=r+s≥6. This excludes both multiplicity pairs for independently encoded Bell links and arbitrary complex coefficients.
