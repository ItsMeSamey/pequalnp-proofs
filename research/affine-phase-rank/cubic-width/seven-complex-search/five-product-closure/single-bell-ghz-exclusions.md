# Excluding biseparable and pure GHZ profiles for a sole entangled ray

Write g for the single CCZ ray. We use the reviewed [eight-product lower bound and local finite certificate](seven-product-exclusion.md), the rank-four lower bound for two copies, the rank-seven unrestricted lower bound for three, and the tripartite stabilizer normal form in [the profile reduction](single-entangled-profile.md). All tensor ranks in applications of the splitting theorem are ordinary complex span dimensions. The inequalities below use [Lovitz and Petrov, Theorem 4](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/forum-of-mathematics-sigma/article/generalization-of-kruskals-theorem-on-tensor-decomposition/515357D904DD4AD1042F37BBCBC95B35): a connected collection of product vectors of span dimension r has r≥1+Σ_i(d_i−1), where d_i is the local factor-span dimension.

**All biseparable profiles.** Suppose the sole entangled stabilizer factors across C versus AB. All seven rays then factor across this cut with stabilizer factors, including the bipartite stabilizer on AB. Any four C factors must span g: otherwise apply a covector annihilating the four but nonzero on g, leaving at most three stabilizers for two copies. The reviewed local certificate excludes this four-wise condition in dimension at most three, hence d_C≥4. Adding the target gives a circuit of rank seven by minimality. Splitting on this bipartition gives d_C+d_AB≤8. The six ordinary AB products span the two-copy target; otherwise a covector annihilating these products but nonzero on the target would make g proportional to the remaining C stabilizer. Consequently d_AB=4, and four independent ordinary AB products span the entire local AB space. Their expression for the two-copy target uses all four. Its circuit has rank four, forcing d_A+d_B≤5 among those five products. Each dimension is at least two because g is outside every one-dimensional stabilizer support. One local span thus has dimension two, contains g, and contains the marginal support of the entangled AB stabilizer because that stabilizer lies in the AB span. Its marginal support has dimension at least two, so equals this space. This contradicts exclusion of g from every proper local stabilizer code.

**One GHZ.** Expand the sole entangled term into two products of stabilizer rays; their two local vectors span a dimension-two stabilizer code C_i on each party. The eight products now constitute a minimum product decomposition. Adding the target makes a circuit of rank eight, giving Σ_i d_i≤10. Every d_i≥3 because g lies outside C_i. Select a party, say A, with d_A=3: its space U is C_A+span(g).

The code C_A contains six logical stabilizer rays. The reviewed local finite certificate bounds the total stabilizer rays of U by eight: U contains g and at least six stabilizers. Thus at most two local stabilizer types in U lie outside C_A. Their span excludes g. For two distinct such rays u,v, were g on their line, choose three distinct rays of C_A and append u,u,v,v. Any four entries would span g: the code line contains only the three code entries; the line through u,v contains g; every other projective line contains at most one of u,v and meets the code line at most once, for at most three entries counting multiplicity. This contradicts the reviewed nonexistence of such a seven-multiset in a local three-space. Zero or one exterior type cannot span g either.

At least four of the six ordinary products have exterior A factors: a covector killing C_A and nonzero on g must leave at least four products to express two copies. A different covector kills every exterior type and remains nonzero on g. It leaves at most two ordinary products plus the two GHZ products. All four must appear and form a circuit with the two-copy target. Both local spaces on B and C contain their full two-dimensional GHZ code and g outside it. Their dimensions thus sum to at least six, contradicting the rank-four circuit bound d_B+d_C≤5.

**Two GHZ factors.** Expand the sole entangled term into four independent products. In the resulting eleven-element collection including the target, partition the vector matroid into c connected components. Project the supplied nonzero-coefficient relation onto each direct-sum component: each contains a relation and at least one of the four GHZ products, since the target plus six ordinary products are independent. The target component must contain at least two GHZ products because its relation represents the target, whose product minimum is eight. Thus c≤3.

Let d_ij be the local dimension of party i in component j. Summing the connected splitting bounds gives total span rank at least Σ_ij d_ij−2c≥15−2c: each marginal includes the four-dimensional GHZ code and g outside it. Each component contains a relation, so total rank is at most 11−c. These inequalities require c≥4, a contradiction.

The mixed GHZ-plus-Bell and two-Bell profiles on two different party pairs remain outside these exclusions. No new finite enumeration is used.
