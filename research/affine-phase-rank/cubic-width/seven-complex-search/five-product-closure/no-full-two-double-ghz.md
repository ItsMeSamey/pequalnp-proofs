# Two double GHZ exceptions are impossible

In the setting of no-full-double-ghz-simple.md expand each exception into four terms in its own two-bit copy basis. In the connected component of the target retain t ordinary entries and e,f entries of the respective exceptions. The same connected splitting argument gives 2e+1≤t+f and 2f+1≤t+e. If e=4 then t=5 and f=4, and conversely. In this case the three local dimensions are each at least five and sum to at most t+e+f+2=15. Thus each is five. Both four-dimensional local codes lie in this space and intersect in dimension at least three, hence coincide since a nonzero code intersection has power-of-two dimension. This contradicts the two-equal-codes exclusion in no-full-double-ghz-overlap.md.

Otherwise e,f≤3. If both are at most two, each retained group gives at most one simple GHZ or product, reducing to the simple-resource exclusions as in no-full-double-ghz-simple.md. Up to exchanging E,F, next take e=3 and f≤2. The three E entries are a simple GHZ plus a product; if t≤4 this again gives at most seven terms with only simple resources. If t=5 then f=2 by 2e+1≤t+f. Outside the target component the only terms are one E entry and two F entries. Their weighted sum cannot vanish since the two F entries have independent local factors on every party and their sum is not product.

Finally e=f=3 implies t=4 or 5. The detached E and F entries, together with at most one detached ordinary product, have a full-coefficient vanishing sum. If there is no ordinary entry, the detached E and F products are proportional. If there is one, the sum of the two detached E,F entries is product, so their local factors are proportional on at least two parties. The target component has local dimensions at least four everywhere and sum at most t+8. For t=4 all dimensions are four; for t=5 at least two are four and the detached factors agree projectively everywhere. Thus on two parties the local component dimension is four and the detached E and F factors coincide projectively. At each such party the two three-dimensional retained local spans have intersection of dimension at least two. Their common detached ray is orthogonal to each retained span, because each original local four-tuple is orthogonal. Adding it gives intersection dimension at least three between the original four-dimensional codes. Those codes coincide, again contradicting the two-equal-codes exclusion. No common choice of the original local encoders is assumed.
