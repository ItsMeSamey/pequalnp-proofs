# A GHZ plus Bell facing a two-link Bell path

Let U be a two-link Bell path centered on A and let V be a GHZ times one Bell. Local Clifford encoders are arbitrary and independent. Subscripts on U and V refer to the local Schmidt codes. Suppose the target, which has local factor g at every party, equals the weighted sum of these exceptions and five ordinary stabilizer products. Normalizations and nonzero coefficients are absorbed into the tensors.

We use the [four-by-four marker lemma](two-ghz-bell-resources.md) and the [path erasure bounds](two-paths-exclusion.md). On every cut the target remote product lies outside the sum of the remote exception codes: quotient one partner by U's local code, the other by V's local code. If the two remote codes are also disjoint their independent dual extractions put g and both exception local codes into the span of the ordinary local factors on the cut party. If a local signed marker is shared, erasing its sector gives a nonzero multiple of g⊗g using k=4 or 5 ordinary products. The sum of their two retained local factor-span dimensions is at most k+1 by Sylvester's matrix rank inequality. Adding the other factors gives full ordinary span dimension sum at most k+1+2(5−k)≤7 on these parties. Two parties cannot both have shared signed local markers.

**Bell opposite the path center (BC).** Local ranks are (4,2,2) and (2,4,4). On A the path remote space is U_B⊗U_C, while the mixed remote code is a span of two Bell matrices with disjoint two-dimensional local supports on B and C (the two GHZ values). Any vector with both coefficients nonzero has matrix rank four, too large for the rectangle. For a single branch to belong to the rectangle requires U_B⊂V_B and U_C⊂V_C, both giving shared markers. On B every nonzero path remote vector has rank two, its C support equal to U_C and its A support lying inside U_A. A common vector must also be inside V_A⊗V_C; this requires U_C⊂V_C and its two-dimensional A support equals V_A, hence V_A⊂U_A. This too forces two shared markers. Exchange B and C for the remaining cut. Thus the remote spaces are disjoint on every cut.

All three ordinary local spans have dimension five, since each contains a dimension-four code and g outside. Shared markers are now excluded even on a single party by the sum-seven erasure bound. In particular dim(U_B∩V_B)≤1, since a two-dimensional intersection would be containment and share V_B's signed check. The five ordinary A factors are independent. Dual extraction on these factors puts each ordinary BC product in the direct sum of the target line, the path remote rectangle, and the mixed remote code. Their components in the last code span both Bell matrices: the original mixed flattening has rank two, so its image after quotienting away the other two summands is still two-dimensional. At least one Bell branch has B support meeting U_B trivially, since the two branch supports are disjoint inside V_B and dim(U_B∩V_B)≤1. Select an ordinary product with nonzero coefficient on this branch. Quotient B by U_B and C by span(g_C). This kills the path rectangle and target, and acts injectively on V_C. If just the selected Bell branch occurs, the result has rank two. If both occur, their original rank four can lose at most one through the B quotient. Each conclusion contradicts the product rank of the selected direction.

**Bell incident to the path center (AB).** Local ranks are (4,2,2) and (4,4,2). On the A cut the four-by-four marker lemma requires a unique shared-marker party. If it is not A it shares at least two checks, hence must be C. In this latter case the A local codes have intersection dimension two. A remote shared local marker forces the ordinary remainder to have cut rank at most four, and its rank is at least that of the exception sum since the remote target lies outside both codes' sum. By Sylvester that sum has rank at least 8−2−v, where v is the remote code intersection dimension. Thus v≥2. But the path rectangle on BC is U_B⊗U_C; the mixed remote space is the direct sum of V_B0⊗c₀ and V_B1⊗c₁, where the cᵢ are independent and the two B codes are disjoint subspaces of V_B. Intersecting just with U_B⊗the entire C space leaves dimension Σᵢ dim(U_B∩V_Bi)≤dim(U_B∩V_B)≤1; the last inequality uses absence of a B marker. This excludes a shared-marker party outside A.

Hence A alone shares a marker, and its two rank-four codes coincide. At C the two dimension-two codes have zero intersection, since a common nonzero vector would force their dimension-two check spaces to commute with consistent signs and share at least one label on three qubits. At B the rank-two and rank-four codes intersect in at most one dimension. Remote spaces on the B cut are disjoint by the C-code disjointness; ordinary B factors thus span at least V_B and g, giving dimension five. On the C cut every nonzero path remote vector has its full two-dimensional B support U_B, whereas any mixed remote vector has B support inside V_B. Again remote spaces are disjoint. Ordinary C factors thus span both disjoint local qubit codes, giving dimension at least four. The shared A marker would require these two ordinary dimensions to sum to at most seven, in contradiction to their sum of at least nine.

These arguments cover 0011+1100 and 0011+0110 respectively, up to simultaneous party permutations. They use neither a new enumeration nor connectedness assumptions on an expanded collection.
