# Two two-link Bell paths cannot be the two exceptions

Suppose two independently encoded two-link Bell paths E,F and five ordinary block-product stabilizers represent the target cube. We absorb their weights into the tensors and denote the weighted sum of the two paths by H. Each path has local rank four at its center and rank two at either endpoint. Its remote code on the center cut is the tensor product of the two endpoint codes. On an endpoint cut every nonzero remote vector retains an intact Bell on the other link, tensored with a nonzero center logical qubit vector in the path's own coordinates. In particular its Schmidt rank is two, its support on the opposite endpoint is that endpoint's entire two-dimensional code, and its center support lies in the four-dimensional center code.

Write U_X,V_X for the E,F local codes on party X. The ordinary X span contains g_X and the X support of H: the target's remote product is outside the sum of the two remote codes, by killing E on one remote party and F on the other with covectors retaining the local target. If the remote codes are disjoint, dual extraction then puts both U_X and V_X in that same ordinary span. When a signed local Pauli is shared on X, erasing its fixed proper code leaves G on the other two parties in k=4 or 5 ordinary products. Sylvester on this rank-one matrix identity yields a bound k+1 on the sum of retained local span dimensions. The full ordinary spans on those parties thus have dimension sum at most k+1+2(5−k)≤7.

Two two-dimensional stabilizer codes on a three-qubit party with nonzero intersection share a signed local Pauli: their two rank-two check groups generate at most three independent consistent commuting checks. A two-dimensional code contained in a four-dimensional stabilizer code shares its signed check. By two-local-shared-markers.md the exceptions cannot share signed local Paulis on two distinct parties.

**Common center A.** At least one of the endpoint code intersections must be zero by the preceding marker obstruction. Thus the two remote rectangles on the center cut are disjoint. Both four-dimensional center codes lie in the span of five ordinary A factors. Their intersection has dimension at least three and must have power-of-two dimension, hence the codes coincide. Their common A marker now forces both endpoint intersections to be zero. On B the remote codes are disjoint because their C supports are disjoint. Both endpoint qubit codes on B therefore lie in the ordinary B span; their sum has dimension four. The same holds on C. Erasing the common center code contradicts the bound seven on the sum of these ordinary endpoint dimensions.

**Different centers A for E and B for F.** On the A cut E has remote rectangle U_B⊗U_C. Any nonzero vector also in F's remote code would have its entire C support V_C in U_C, hence equal C qubit codes. Its B support of dimension two would be contained in U_B∩V_B, forcing U_B⊂V_B. These give shared signed markers on C and B, impossible. The remote codes on A are therefore disjoint, and by symmetry the same holds on B. The ordinary spans on A and B each contain their respective four-dimensional center code. A shared signed marker on C would violate their sum bound of seven after erasure. Consequently U_C∩V_C=0.

The five ordinary A factors span U_A together with g_A, outside that code, so are independent. Their BC product directions all lie in the sum of the target line, U_B⊗U_C, and F's remote code, by taking duals to the five ordinary A factors. At least one such direction has a nonzero component in F's remote code, since the flattening contains F and the three remote summands are independent as a sum. Quotient C by U_C. This kills the E rectangle and preserves rank two of the selected F component, since its C support V_C is disjoint from U_C. Its B support is contained in V_B and excludes g_B. Adding a multiple of the projected target cannot lower this rank: projecting B along g_B acts injectively on V_B and removes the target. The image of the selected ordinary product would therefore have rank at least two, although local linear maps preserve product rank at most one. This excludes the different-center pair.

The excluded unordered profile orbits are (0110,0110) and (0110,1010). No alignment between the paths' Clifford encoders is needed.
