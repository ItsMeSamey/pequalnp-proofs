# A simple Bell with a two-link path or a GHZ and a different Bell link

Expand the simple Bell into two product stabilizers and the other exception into four in their respective Clifford encoders. All coefficients of the resulting dependence, including the target and five ordinary products, are nonzero. As in simple-ghz-mixed-bell.md each connected vector-matroid component of n terms has sum of local dimensions at most n+1. If the expanded list is disconnected there are two or three terms outside the target component by the eight-product lower bound. This outside set is itself one connected dependent component with all coefficients nonzero. It includes a high (four-term) entry, since otherwise recombining the intact high resource leaves at most five other products and hence at most six stabilizers. It includes at most one high entry: any two distinct high entries differ independently on at least two parties, so their full-coefficient sum is not a single product, and all four high entries are independent.

For a path with center A and endpoints B,C there is an endpoint of total local dimension three. In the connected case the remote span across A contains the full two-by-two path rectangle. The connected rectangle bound in simple-ghz-path-rectangle.md and Sylvester give d_A+d_B+d_C≤12, while d_A≥5 and both endpoint dimensions are at least three. In the disconnected case the three retained path terms still contain both coordinate rays of each endpoint, and three independent center rays; the target is outside each code. For two outside terms the dimension-sum bound is eleven, giving a three-dimensional endpoint; the outside pair is proportional and the outside path entry has its endpoint coordinates already represented. For three outside terms the bound is ten, so both endpoints have dimension three within the target component. The three outside product terms in a connected dependence agree projectively on at least two parties, since a sum of two products is a nonzero product only when they agree on all but at most one party. On at least one endpoint they therefore add nothing to the retained span.

For a GHZ and Bell on BC, the other simple Bell is on AB or AC. Here the total dimension on A is three. The connected lower dimensions (3,5,5) attain the upper sum thirteen. With one high entry outside, the lower dimensions from the three retained high entries are (3,4,4), as both copy labels remain present. Thus only two terms can lie outside and the retained A dimension is three. Their proportional pair adds nothing on A, since the high A label repeats.

On any such small party its high qubit code U and g span the three-space. Use the local certificate in two-different-simple-bells.md: a three-space through g contains at most eight stabilizer rays, cannot contain two distinct stabilizer qubit codes, and admits f₁ annihilating all its exterior stabilizer rays with f₁(g) nonzero. Also choose f₀ killing U while retaining g. If the simple Bell is active on this party its qubit code equals U. Erasing U requires at least four exterior ordinary terms by the two-copy lower bound. The f₁ contraction leaves at most one ordinary product and the contracted simple Bell is zero or one product. The high contraction is zero or has rank at least two: the path retains its other link, and the mixed GHZ retains an entire Bell on each active copy sector with disjoint row and column supports. When nonzero, subtracting it from the target gives matrix rank at least three since g lies outside both high support codes. If zero, the remaining at most two products also cannot produce g⊗g: neither local factor of any survivor can equal g, as the arbitrary contracted simple-Bell factor lies in its proper opposite code. A rank-one sum of at most two products must have a target factor on at least one party. This covers the GHZ-Bell pair and both path orientations whenever the small endpoint is active on the simple Bell.

It remains to consider a path with its small endpoint B the simple Bell's spectator a. If a is exterior to U, f₀ leaves the simple Bell as at most one stabilizer, requiring at least three exterior ordinary terms. Thus f₁ kills the spectator Bell and leaves at most two ordinary products. The same rank bound of three rules out a nonzero path contraction; a zero contraction leaves too few stabilizers.

If a lies inside U then the two exceptions share a signed local marker on B. By two-local-shared-markers.md they cannot share one on C. In particular the two local C qubit codes U_C,L_C are disjoint: a nonzero code intersection implies commuting consistent checks and at most three independent joint checks, so the two rank-two check groups share a signed check. Erasing B's code expresses G_AC with k=4 or 5 ordinary products. Sylvester's inequality on the retained rank-one identity, adding the other ordinary factors back, gives d_A+d_C≤11−k≤7 for the full ordinary spans; they contain g_A,g_C and the local supports of the weighted exceptional sum H. Across A the path remote code is U⊗U_C and the simple Bell's remote code is span(a)⊗L_C. These are disjoint, so H has rank at least four on A by separate remote dual extraction. Across C every nonzero remote path vector retains the intact AB Bell and has Schmidt rank two on AB. Every vector in the simple Bell's remote code is product across AB with fixed B ray a, so the two remote spaces are again disjoint. The C support of H is U_C+L_C, of dimension four. This contradicts d_A+d_C≤7.

The three excluded profile pairs are (0010,0101), (0010,0110), and (0010,1100), up to simultaneous permutations of the parties.
