# Eight atoms when every ray factors across the three original blocks

The complex stabilizer rank of g⊗g⊗g under the requirement that each atom factor across the three original blocks is exactly eight. Local stabilizers and complex weights are arbitrary. The native decomposition gives eight.

Suppose seven suffice. The unrestricted lower bound seven forces them to be independent and all used. Adding the target product gives a circuit of span dimension seven. Let d_i be the dimension spanned by its factors in block i, including g. The splitting theorem of Lovitz and Petrov gives 7>sum_i(d_i−1) since a circuit is connected. Thus some d_i≤3. In this block every four local factors must span g: otherwise a linear functional annihilating these four but nonzero on g leaves at most three stabilizer products representing g⊗g, of rank four. The functional need not be a stabilizer bra since the candidate atoms factor across the cut. [Lovitz and Petrov, Theorem 4](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/forum-of-mathematics-sigma/article/generalization-of-kruskals-theorem-on-tensor-decomposition/515357D904DD4AD1042F37BBCBC95B35)

The finite local certificate rules out such a multiset. It generates all 1080 three-bit stabilizer rays using affine supports and fourth-root quadratic chirps. Entries are zero or fourth roots. Reduce Gaussian integers modulo 65537 by i→256. For minors up to size four, Hadamard bounds the modulus by 16. Any nonzero Gaussian integer mapping to zero has modulus at least sqrt(65537), since its norm is divisible by 65537. All necessary ranks are therefore exact.

Quotient by g. The largest projective class has two rays, so every projective line through g has at most two stabilizer rays. When d_i≤2, four of seven entries must repeat one ray, missing g. When d_i=3, multiplicity three is impossible: those three copies with every other entry must span g, forcing all entries into the same line. No three distinct rays can each repeat, since any two doubled rays must span g and the line through g has at most two rays. Thus at least five distinct rays occur, with multiplicities one or two.

To cover every three-dimensional vector space through g with at least five stabilizers, the verifier quotients by g and a first stabilizer ray, then groups the other rays by projective direction. It suffices to take representatives of the first ray under the implemented target-preserving affine permutations. Joining each group to the line of the first member gives the full local atom set in that space. Enumerate every seven-multiset with 5–7 distinct entries of multiplicities at most two. For each line through two distinct local atoms that misses g, discard any multiset with four entries on it. The run found 49 five-member planes, 82 six-member planes, eight seven-member planes and 21 eight-member planes, possibly redundant under symmetry, and zero surviving multisets. The enumeration source is product-plane-control.py and the receipt is product-plane-control.log. Therefore every possible seven-term stabilizer representation has at least one interblock-entangled atom.
