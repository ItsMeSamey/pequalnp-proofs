# Four-ray Fourier support is at most four for every number of controls

Use the W₃ four-ray theorem (four-rays-five-frequencies.md) and the theorem that three rays cannot produce four affinely independent active indices (fourier-three-external-completion.md). Suppose a W_t vector in the span of four complex stabilizer rays has five distinct active indices. Their affine hull has dimension 3 or 4. Restrict controls to this hull, retain original control coordinates giving an affine chart, and set the data pairs of the other coordinates to zero. This stabilizer restriction gives W₃ or W₄ and preserves the five active values. The W₃ case was excluded.

In W₄ these five points are an affine simplex. Each of its five facets, the affine hull of four of the points, is a 3D hyperplane with four affinely independent active indices. For a block j occurring in its normal, deleting that control coordinate gives an affine chart on the facet. The Pauli postselection and stabilizer bra construction of four-rays-five-frequencies.md still forces each seed to have a fixed local basis L₀ or L₁ on j: the putative erasure of that seed now leaves a W₃ vector with at least four affinely independent active indices, impossible with the remaining three rays. The facet need not be active everywhere. Only the nonzero amplitudes of the four marked points are needed; the bra can be chosen for both possible values of the dropped bit. If the bit is constant on the facet, choose the bra for that one value.

The facet normals span the dual space (for the standard simplex, the four facets through zero are its coordinate hyperplanes; invertible affine maps preserve spanning). Consequently each of the four data blocks occurs in at least one normal, and every seed has a fixed product basis type in {0,1}⁴. At each of the five controls, the unused-basis-bra argument of four-rays-five-frequencies.md applies with four typed blocks: if no ray matches the entire control, all four rays must be present and have a common type differing in exactly one coordinate. Since there are five active controls and only four types, such a control exists. All four types are therefore identical, say b. Applying the same argument to every other active control shows that the entire active set is contained in the five-point star of b and hence equals that star.

At the leaf b+e_i, all four rays are present, with four distinct local outcome labels in basis L_{b_i} on block i. Projecting onto each outcome there isolates the corresponding ray, and the target still has nonzero amplitude; its local basis outcomes on every other block are thus exactly those of the fixed local target q_{b_h}. For each seed the control occurrence set is an affine flat containing all four leaves. The labels of its local basis outcomes on this flat are affine functions of the controls (eigenvalue signs of two fixed local Pauli generators, by the affine sign lemma in four-rays-five-frequencies.md).

Take three distinct leaves indexed i,j,k and add their controls over the binary field. The result lies in every occurrence flat, with distance three from b. Each seed's outcome label on block i there is its label at leaf i plus its labels at the j and k leaves. The latter are the same prescribed local label for all seeds. Thus the four block-i outcomes at the triple sum are distinct. The four nonzero terms there have orthogonal support in this basis and cannot cancel, contradicting that the control is outside the active star. This rules out five active indices for all t. Four native rays attain equality for t≥2.
