# A simple GHZ with two Bell links: endpoint and component restrictions

In a potentially minimal seven-list let the exceptional rays be a simple GHZ and a path of two Bell links Y–X–Z with independent arbitrary encoders. The other five are ordinary products. Expand the GHZ into two products and the path into four products (i,j), with independent X factors and endpoint factors indexed by i on Y and by j on Z. Together with the target there are twelve product vectors carrying one full-coefficient relation. Use the product lower bound, local census and component splitting inequalities as in simple-ghz-mixed-bell.md.

If the global local span at Y has dimension three, it contains g and both encoded local qubits. These codes coincide by the local three-space certificate. A covector killing this code and retaining g shows at least four ordinary factors lie outside it. An exterior-annihilating covector retaining g therefore leaves at most one ordinary term. The conditional path E on XZ is zero or has matrix rank two: the Y contraction affects one Bell link only and the other Bell link survives. In the nonzero case its Z support is the two-dimensional Bell code and its X support lies in the original four-dimensional stabilizer code; both exclude their local targets g. Zero would leave at most three products representing G₂. Otherwise target minus E has matrix rank three, so both simple GHZ products and an ordinary term must survive and give its full row and column spaces. The Z space is span(g, Z Bell code) and also contains the two simple GHZ factors on Z. Their two-dimensional code must equal the Bell code by the same census. Killing this code on Z while retaining g removes both exceptions, leaving a single ordinary product, impossible. The same argument applies at endpoint Z with the parties interchanged.

The full collection therefore must be connected. To see this, the target component needs at least nine vectors, leaving exactly one other component of size p=2 or 3 in the disconnected case. This component has at most one path term, since combinations of two distinct nonzero path terms are not products (their X factors and joint YZ factors are independent), and any three path terms are independent. If it has no path term, all four path terms remain and the target component has local dimension sum at least 5+3+3=11. This is impossible at p=3 since its splitting allowance is 13−p. For p=2 the two outside terms are proportional. The ordinary products are independent by minimality, and the two GHZ products are independent; thus one is ordinary and one belongs to the GHZ expansion. Their proportional contributions cancel by the component relation. The remaining list is four ordinary products, one GHZ expansion product, and a full path stabilizer: at most six stabilizers for G₃, impossible.

If one path term is outside, the three remaining use both sectors at each endpoint and three independent X factors. The target component thus has center dimension at least four and endpoint dimensions at least three each. Its splitting allowance is 13−p. At p=2 at least one endpoint has dimension exactly three there, and the omitted proportional pair's factor there is already present through a remaining path term in its sector. Hence the global endpoint dimension is three and the first argument excludes it. At p=3 both endpoint dimensions are three. A connected three-product dependence has only one party on which the local rays vary (a linear combination using two nonproportional product vectors cannot be product if they differ at two or more parties). At one of the two endpoints all omitted factors are consequently proportional to the omitted path factor, which is already present on that endpoint in the target component. Again its global span has dimension three.

In a connected list the local dimensions sum to at most thirteen. The center dimension is at least five and both endpoints must be at least four by the first argument. Thus the only remaining case has the exact dimensions (5,4,4), center first. This is an encoder restriction, not an exclusion of the entire profile.
