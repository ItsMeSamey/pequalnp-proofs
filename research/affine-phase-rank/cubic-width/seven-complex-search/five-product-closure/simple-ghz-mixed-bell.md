# A simple GHZ and a GHZ with an additional Bell link cannot be the two exceptions

Consider five ordinary block-product stabilizers, a single encoded GHZ, and one independently encoded stabilizer consisting of a GHZ shared by X,Y,Z and a Bell link between Y and Z. Let g be the CCZ state on each party. Use the local three-space certificate and connected product-splitting inequality in single-bell-ghz-exclusions.md. For a local three-space span(g,C) containing an encoded stabilizer qubit C, the certificate gives at most eight stabilizer rays there and a covector killing all exterior stabilizer rays but retaining g. Two distinct stabilizer qubits cannot lie in this space, because each contains six stabilizer rays and they intersect in at most one. Also g is outside every proper local stabilizer code.

Expand the two exceptional rays into respectively two and four product terms. The mixed ray has indices (l,j) on Y and Z, specifying four independent vectors on each side; its two X factors depend only on l, the GHZ label. Its local Schmidt codes have dimensions 2,4,4. The target and eleven products are related with nonzero coefficients throughout, using the seven-ray lower bound. For a connected component of n of these vectors the nonzero restricted relation bounds its rank by n−1, so the splitting inequality gives sum of its three local dimensions at most n+1.

For a connected collection the lower dimensions 3,5,5 (include g) attain the upper sum thirteen, so the total X span has dimension three. If disconnected, the target component has at least nine vectors by the eight-product lower bound. Every other component has at least two, so exactly one other component has size p=2 or 3. It contains at most one mixed product: no two of the four are proportional, and a linear combination using two of them cannot be a product because their Y and Z factors are both independent. Three mixed products are independent as well. At least three mixed products remain in the target component; these use both X sectors and three independent Y and Z factors. Thus its local dimensions sum to at least 3+4+4=11. Its upper bound is 13−p, forcing p=2. One mixed product must be among the outside pair, since otherwise all four remain and give lower sum thirteen. The other member is proportional to it; its X factor is therefore already represented by the remaining mixed product with the same GHZ sector. The global X span is the target component's X span, exactly dimension three.

Both exceptional X codes must now coincide in this three-space as a single code C_X. A covector killing C_X and retaining g leaves at least four ordinary terms by the two-copy rank four theorem. The local certificate supplies a second covector killing every exterior X type and retaining g, so contracting it leaves at most one ordinary product, up to two simple GHZ products, and the mixed residual E on YZ. For each surviving mixed GHZ sector E has an encoded rank-two Bell matrix. Distinct sectors have disjoint row and column spaces, so rank E is 0, 2 or 4. Zero is excluded by the two-copy theorem. The column and row spans of E lie in proper stabilizer codes of Y and Z, neither containing g. The target minus E therefore has rank 1+rank E. Since it is a sum of at most three products, E has rank exactly two and the residual rank three. All three remaining terms must survive, with independent row factors and independent column factors.

Exactly one mixed sector remains, whose support on Z is a two-dimensional stabilizer code. Together with g it spans the rank-three residual's Z space. That space also contains the two simple GHZ factors, spanning their two-dimensional stabilizer code. By the local three-space certificate the codes coincide. A covector on Z killing this code and retaining g erases both exceptions, leaving at most one ordinary term, insufficient to give the nonstabilizer Y target g. This excludes the pair (0001,0011) for arbitrary local encoders and all simultaneous party permutations.
