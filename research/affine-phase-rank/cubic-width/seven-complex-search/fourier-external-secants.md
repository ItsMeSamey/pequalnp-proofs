# Two external stabilizer rays in the syndrome Fourier span

On t≥1 disjoint blocks let Tᵢ=zᵢ+xᵢyᵢ over F₂, χ_s=(−1)^(s·T), and W=span_C{χ_s}. A stabilizer ray is an affine indicator times a fourth-root quadratic chirp, up to a complex scalar. The following facts hold for every t.

1. W's stabilizer rays are exactly χ_s. The nonzero W-directions in a secant of two distinct external stabilizer rays are exactly the single χ_s and χ_s+ωχ_{s+e_i}, ω∈{1,−1,i,−i}. An individual secant contributes at most one such direction. Thus quotient-proportional external pairs can replace at most two adjacent Fourier terms, at unit cost per term.
2. In particular any stabilizer representation of the graph indicator 1_{T=0} with fewer than 2^t rays needs at least three external rays; cancellation of at most two cannot save a term in its 2^t-term Walsh expansion.

For (1), every syndrome fiber affinely spans the whole space: substituting z=t₀+xy into an affine equation forces every z coefficient to vanish from its xᵢyᵢ coefficient, then all remaining linear coefficients vanish. Thus a nonzero W atom has full support. Write its phase on x=y=0 as a Z₄ quadratic in z. Substitution z↦z+xy rules out mixed binary sign terms zᵢzⱼ from the cubic zᵢxⱼyⱼ coefficient. Odd linear coefficients are ruled out by the odd xᵢyᵢ coefficient. Only a constant and even linear coefficients survive, as asserted.

Apply the normalized Walsh transform on the z bits only. It preserves stabilizer rays. A W-vector with Fourier coefficients c_s has s-slice proportional to c_s times the real full chirp with quadratic bit q_s=Σsᵢxᵢyᵢ. The proportionality is independent of s. Let B_s denote its binary polar on the 2t data bits. An arbitrary transformed atom has a single global scalar; on every nonempty slice its data support is a translate of a fixed linear space. Its phase on that support extends to a Z₄ quadratic on the full ambient space. It can be written i^p (−1)^q where p is an ordinary affine bit and q is a binary quadratic bit: binary carries when replacing a sum of bits by their XOR are quadratic modulo four. The polar of q on the data directions is fixed across slices. Multiplication by const·i^{kℓ} for an affine bit ℓ preserves the real polar *relative to a real full chirp*: such a factor by itself has zero real polar after stripping its own parity bit. Fixing controls, translating a fiber, or choosing constant phase gauge modifies the stripped local q only by affine terms. In particular, if an atom equals a scalar times a real target on a translate of H, the real polars restricted to H agree.

Suppose a linear combination of two external rays A,B with nonzero coefficients is in W, and call s active when c_s≠0. If their nonempty fibers both have full data support, any atom present alone on an active s has real polar B_s. If both are present, divide their values by the target chirp and absorb common constants. We have aA'+bB'=1 for fourth-root-valued functions. The two circles with centers 0 and 1 intersect in at most two points, hence each function takes at most two values, with identical level partitions. If constant, both are constant. Otherwise both root pairs cannot be antipodal, since their sums over the two distinct levels would give zero on the left and two on the right. An adjacent root pair has opposite phase parity on its two levels; the parity of that ratio is an affine bit ℓ. Hence *both* phases are const·i^{kℓ} for some k. Both original atom restrictions have real polar B_s. Since the polars B_s are all different, each present atom can occur on at most one active slice. If there were two active slices, each atom occurs alone on one of them and their real polars differ; on the zero slices neither can occur alone, nor can two different real polars cancel. Thus each atom has exactly one nonempty slice and agrees with that internal Fourier ray, contrary to externality.

Otherwise, let one atom have proper fiber directions contained in a data hyperplane H. On every active s one can select a translate of H disjoint from that atom's slice, so the other atom is present and equals the target there. Its fixed real polar restricted to H equals B_s restricted to H. Therefore for any two active indices the polar B_s−B_r vanishes on H×H and has rank at most two. Its rank is twice the Hamming distance of s and r. There are at most two active indices, and if there are two they are adjacent. The same other atom occurs alone at some point on each active slice, so the two nonzero coefficients differ by a fourth root, including their relative phase, as its global scalar is fixed.

Conversely split χ_s by the two xᵢ values to realize any single direction. For two adjacent indices split χ_s[1+ω(−1)^{Tᵢ}] the same way. On each half Tᵢ is affine. For ω=±1 the bracket is twice an affine indicator. For ω=±i it is (1+ω) times a fourth root raised to that affine bit. Both resulting nonempty proper atoms are external. A secant of distinct external rays cannot have two-dimensional intersection with W, since that would put both rays inside. For (2), k≤2 external rays in the representation must themselves sum to a W element with at most k internal Fourier coefficients. The target has every coefficient nonzero.

The result classifies W intersection directions rather than identifying different external pairs with each other. Given a particular proposed pair the classification is testable in polynomial bit time. If both fibers are full, invert the map s↦B_s on their two real polars to obtain a list of at most two possible indices. Otherwise solve the linear system B_s|H=R|H on the other atom's polar R, obtaining at most two candidates. Test the intersection of the two input columns with the listed internal span by exact Gaussian-integer Gram elimination (unnormalized gauges). Standard stabilizer overlap evaluation is polynomial in t, or use direct enumeration for small t.
