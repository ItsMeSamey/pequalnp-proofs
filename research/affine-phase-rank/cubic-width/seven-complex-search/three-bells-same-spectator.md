# Three biseparable exceptions on one Bell link are impossible

Suppose all three exceptions factor across A versus BC as a_i⊗E_i, where the E_i are entangled bipartite stabilizers and the original set also includes four block-product stabilizers. Write W for the span of their four ordinary BC product factors and R for the span of all seven BC factors. Throughout, retain arbitrary nonzero complex weights. Every four of the seven A factors span g_A, since otherwise an A covector killing those four and retaining g_A would represent G₂ with at most three stabilizer rays. The local seven-multiset certificate in seven-product-exclusion.md then gives A span dimension at least four. Sylvester on the rank-one original A versus BC identity gives dim R≤4. The remote target G₂ belongs to R and has stabilizer rank four, so dim R=4.

If dim W=4, use the four independent ordinary BC products to express G₂; all four must occur. Sylvester gives total local factor-span dimension at most five on B and C. Each contains g, hence has dimension at least two. On one side the dimension is at most two and the span must contain both g and the entangled Schmidt support of any E_i since R=W. That support would be a two-dimensional stabilizer code equal to the small span and containing g, which is impossible.

Suppose dim W=3. G₂ lies outside W by its stabilizer rank, so R=W+span(G₂). Pick an E_i outside W, writing E_i=cG₂+w with c≠0. Every w in W has matrix rank at most three, hence E_i has rank at most four. Its local Schmidt support codes on B,C are proper and exclude the two local targets. Consequently rank w=rank E_i+1. The entangled E_i must have rank two and w rank three. The local spans of the three product generators of W therefore both have dimension three and equal the row and column spaces of w. Each equals the span of the corresponding qubit Schmidt code of E_i and the local target. All E_j lie in this same three-by-three tensor space. Each has Schmidt rank two since it is entangled and has power-of-two rank at most three. On B all three qubit codes must coincide: a three-space through g contains at most eight stabilizer rays, while distinct encoded qubit codes would contribute at least eleven by the reviewed local census. Erasing their common B code with a covector retaining g_B requires all four ordinary products to survive by the two-copy lower bound. All four B rays must therefore be exterior to that code. At most two exterior stabilizer rays occur in the three-space (six of the eight possible rays belong to the code), too few to span the three-dimensional B space of W.

Finally if dim W≤2, quotient BC by W, where the target remains nonzero. If the three spectators a_i are independent, comparison in their basis forces all three images [E_i] to be multiples of [G₂], contradicting dim(R/W)≥2. Otherwise their span contains g_A by the quotient identity and is a two-space, since a single stabilizer direction cannot contain g_A. A line through g_A contains at most two stabilizer rays, so exactly two spectator types occur and one occurs just once. Taking its coordinate in the quotient gives E_i=cG₂+w with c≠0 and w∈W. Now rank w≤2, implying rank E_i≤3. Thus E_i has Schmidt rank two, and its proper local codes exclude g on both sides, forcing rank w=3 as above. This contradiction exhausts the possible ordinary remote spans.
