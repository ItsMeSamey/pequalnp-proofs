# A Bell triangle with one GHZ cannot pair with any all-three-full exception

Assume five block-product rays and two exceptional rays, both of full local Schmidt rank on all three blocks. Suppose one has profile (1111), a Bell pair on each edge and one common GHZ. If any cut has equal remote codes, one-equal-remote-matching.md applies. Otherwise on each cut the two remote rank-eight codes intersect in exactly dimension four by two-copy-basis-support.md: the intersection is at least four and is a power of two. The two signed check groups commute and agree on their common labels. Their joint group has rank four on six remote qubits, so their common subgroup has rank two. There are no checks supported on just one remote block because all individual marginals are full. The nonlocal shared-check theorem of five-product-remote-erasure.md makes these common checks computational Z labels.

Consider these shared checks in the local Clifford normal form of the (1111) atom. On each edge its remote group is generated by the two Bell-axis checks XX and ZZ on that edge and the ZZ check of the GHZ on the same two parties. Its three-dimensional label space has local symplectic polar rank two and radical the GHZ check. The two-dimensional common computational subgroup is locally isotropic, so contains that radical and one nonzero Bell-axis direction modulo the radical. It therefore fixes both the GHZ parity and one Bell-axis parity separately. At each party the two adjacent chosen Bell axes and the local GHZ Z are three independent commuting local computational labels (their encoded Pauli labels were supported on different qubits). An invertible affine computational basis change per party and choices of offsets thus put the common checks into equality form. Both supports lie in the following sixteen-point set, with four independent bits t,i,j,k:
    A=(t,i,j), B=(t,i,k), C=(t,j,k).
The (1111) atom has full support on this set: measuring the GHZ qubit in Z gives both consistent results, and measuring either Bell pair in its correlated Pauli eigenbasis gives both consistent results independently of the other factors. The second support is affine. Its cardinality is eight or sixteen, since its individual marginals have full support. The target local g and its affine changes are nonstabilizers of full computational support; the local coordinate changes do not affect the product nature or weights.

Let H be the weighted sum of exceptions and let n_X be the number of X coordinates occurring in its nonzero support, separately on each party X. Any two party values in the displayed set determine the third; hence different rows in each flattening have disjoint remote support, and rank H=n_X. If n_X=8, rank(G3−H)≥7. Otherwise both local and remote target factors lie outside their corresponding spaces (the remote pair has at most sixteen occupied entries among 64). Thus rank(G3−H)=n_X+1 and five products force n_X≤4 on every party. At equality four on any X, the five remote product factors would have to span the full five-dimensional remote matrix range. That range lies in the span of the target remote product and the remote H rows, and equals that span by dimension. On one remote block choose a covector killing all occupied H coordinates (at most four) but retaining its target. The contraction of the remote space then has nonzero image in the line of the other nonstabilizer local target. Every product stabilizer vector in the range has zero image since its surviving local factor would be a stabilizer. This contradicts spanning. Therefore all three n_X≤3.

Write m for the number of surviving support triples. On each t slice the three local projections are the three pair projections of a set of binary triples (i,j,k). The discrete Loomis–Whitney bound gives m_t≤sqrt(A_t B_t C_t) where A_t,B_t,C_t are the respective projection sizes. For completeness, bound the triple indicator by the product of the three pair indicators, use Cauchy–Schwarz on k inside each (i,j) term, then on (i,j) with the first pair indicator, to get this estimate. Summing over t and using Cauchy–Schwarz followed by sum B_t C_t≤(sum B_t)(sum C_t) gives m≤sqrt(n_A n_B n_C)<6. Consequently the other exception cannot occupy only eight points (at least eight uncancelled points of the first would remain).

Both atoms thus occupy the same full four-dimensional affine support. Their phases there are full stabilizer chirps in t,i,j,k. If their scalar ratio is not a fourth root they cancel nowhere. Otherwise the phase ratio after recentering equals one at at least eleven points, so its affine imaginary parity is constant. The support of H is the support of a binary quadratic discrepancy. It is nonempty since otherwise five products would represent the target. A nonzero binary quadratic of weight ≤5 on four bits has weight four and affine-plane support: the alternating polar has rank zero, two or four; a quadratic unbalanced sign has Walsh value of modulus 2^(4−rank/2); rank two with the lower weight gives four and is, up to affine change and constant, the product of two affine bits. Accordingly each projected support size n_X is a power of two, and n_X≤3 now implies n_X≤2. The same projection bound gives m≤sqrt(8)<3, contradicting four. Thus no such pair of exceptions exists.
