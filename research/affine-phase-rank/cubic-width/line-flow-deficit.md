# Triangle closure on a hard degree-four coloring family

Let H be a finite simple cubic graph with N vertices and q connected components. Its line graph has 3N/2 vertices and 3N edges and is simple and 4-regular. The native private-XOR presentation of proper vertex coloring therefore has transversal deficit 3N/2 by the simple-graph deficit theorem. Holyer's cubic edge-coloring problem reduces directly to vertex coloring these line graphs. See https://doi.org/10.1137/0210055 . The transformation takes a supplied H, as in this reduction. A line graph input alone can instead use classical polynomial line-graph root recognition and verification.

Assign each edge a nonzero binary pair. At each cubic star, the three incident colors are pairwise distinct exactly when their sum is zero, given nonzeroness. Thus one may replace all three pair guards with two scalar linear equations at each star, retaining every edge-color nonzero guard. Every labeled assignment is preserved, including its multiplicity. This local rewrite is the nowhere-zero four-flow characterization of cubic edge colorings. Its deficit calculation concerns the two guard presentations of the same instance.

The new check matrix consists of two copies of the binary vertex-edge incidence matrix. Delete one dependent row per connected component in each copy. Its rank is 2(N−q). For a subset J of edge blocks, the joint column-plane dimension is twice the graphic incidence rank, namely twice the sum of s−1 over connected components of the edge subgraph, where s counts vertices. Each component has at most 2(s−1) edges: for s>=4 use 3s/2<=2(s−1), and for s<=3 use simplicity. Hence Rado's independent transversal criterion holds for every subset. Exactly 3N/2 independent representatives can be chosen, one per edge block. The new deficit is N/2−2q. Constructing the replacement is linear in the graph representation; an explicit transversal if desired takes polynomial time by linear matroid intersection. The reduction applies to both colorable and uncolorable inputs. It leaves linear deficit on connected growing inputs and supplies no polynomial solver for cubic edge coloring.

For H=K4 the line graph is the octahedral graph and has six labeled colorings; the new deficit is zero. For H the Petersen graph the parameters decrease from 15 to 3 and the graph remains non-edge-colorable. This K4 root differs from the separate ordinary K4 vertex-coloring control, which rejects.
