# Direct positivity certificate for a commuting Pauli postselection

Let t blocks each carry the normalized three-bit sign state G1, and postselect the specified outcomes of m independent commuting Hermitian Pauli generators. Assume their group excludes minus identity after their signs are chosen. The probability is 2^{-m} times the sum of their 2^m signed product expectations. For a local X^a Z^b, a=0 gives zero expectation unless b=0. For a nonzero a the two locations 111 and 111+a are exactly the negatively phased derivatives, so the unsigned expectation has magnitude 1/2 or zero (including b=0). More explicitly for b nonzero it is proportional to 1+(-1)^{a dot b}. Tensor factorization therefore bounds a global summand by 2^{-w(a)} where w counts blocks with nonzero X part. The identity summand is one. If the sum of absolute bounds on nonidentity summands is strictly below one then the requested result has positive probability regardless of generator signs. This is a sufficient decision-only certificate; no count is inferred.

A polynomially checkable version avoids enumerating the 2^m words. For each of the t blocks its X part is a three-bit linear map on generator coefficients u. Suppose independent linear functionals l_1,...,l_m each occur in the row span of B_i separate block maps, with all allocated blocks disjoint. For each u the number of active blocks is at least sum_i B_i l_i(u). Since these independent bits run through the whole m-cube, the total absolute bound on nonidentity terms is at most product_i(1+2^{-B_i})−1. If this product is strictly below two before subtracting one, all 2^m outcome syndromes have positive probability. The test uses polynomial-size integer arithmetic. For a supplied independent family, assigning disjoint blocks to meet stated multiplicities is bipartite matching. One safe constructor computes the seven possible nonzero row sums from each block and greedily selects independent functionals, then verifies its assigned multiplicities; this heuristic is incomplete. Private repetitions B_i>=ceil(log2(2m)) suffice since (1+1/(2m))^m<=exp(1/2)<2. The Z parts may couple different blocks arbitrarily subject to global commutation. Pure-Z directions prevent a spanning set in this sufficient test.

The criterion does not yield polynomial-time circuit SAT. For example with each triple locally Hadamard-transformed on one leg, the distribution is uniform on z=xy. A product of such local resources plus affine parity postselection enforces arbitrarily many AND gates with wiring equalities. Extra blocks which make some checks expose private X directions change the projected probability and can turn impossible syndromes into possible ones.
