# Direct SAT control for the triangle deficit

For the direct incidence encoding of ordinary CNF, let M be the number of nonempty clauses, n the number of active variables and p the number of literal occurrences. The p triangle blocks choose a fault-bit and witness-bit per incidence. In the affine syndrome matrix their first columns are the consistency-check vectors of the variable copies, with rank deg(v)−1 and unique dependence the sum of all deg(v) vectors. The other columns are equal unit vectors within each clause, one independent syndrome dimension per clause. Check rank is therefore p−n+M.

Let ν be the maximum variable-clause matching size. Maximum rainbow rank t=p−n+ν. One may choose a rainbow set only from the two displayed basis columns at each incidence without reducing its maximum rank (Rado's criterion depends on their span). Any variable whose every incidence is selected needs at least one clause-unit column, and those units must be distinct clauses across such variables. There are at most ν fully selected variables, while other variables contribute at most deg(v)−1. Conversely choose every variable's fault columns except one incidence; for each variable in a maximum matching choose that missing incidence to be its matched clause, and select its witness unit instead. These columns are independent by the direct-sum check structure.

Thus k=M−ν is exactly the classical maximum deficiency of the original clause-variable incidence graph. In particular k≥M−n and need not be small. On this direct image Szeider already gives deterministic O(2^k n^3) satisfiability with a certificate (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcss.2004.04.009), stronger than randomized XP. The triangle algorithm concerns arbitrary binary affine checks with disjoint matching guards, which may not have this direct incidence structure.
