# Excluding exactly two proper affine terms in a seven-term real representation (pending review)

Write G for the nine-bit sum-of-three-disjoint-cubics sign. Assume five full quadratic signs and two nonempty proper supported quadratic signs represent G. A hyperplane disjoint from all proper terms is impossible: it contains an odd-top six-dimensional cubic minor, and such a minor needs at least six full signs by the four-twist and denominator argument in one-proper-support.md. Consequently U_1 and U_2 have full joint affine hull. Their dimensions d_1≤d_2 satisfy d_1+d_2≥8, because the affine hull of two nonempty affine flats has dimension at most d_1+d_2+1. If d_1=0 this forces d_2=8; in the halfspace opposite U_2, fix a six-dimensional odd-top minor direction. Of its four affine cosets in the halfspace choose one missing the singleton. Only five full terms then remain there, again impossible. Hence d_1≥1 and d_2≥3.

Unless U_1 and U_2 are complementary parallel hyperplanes, there is an affine flat K of codimension at most two avoiding both. Independent nonzero normals picked one from each support give K by choosing both opposite halfspaces. If no such pair exists then both normal spaces coincide with the same line, so the supports are parallel hyperplanes; identical ones have an opposite hyperplane avoiding both. The complementary parallel case is treated by six-minor-profiles.md.

The restriction of G to K has a genuinely cubic term. In a basis with at most two directions outside the tangent of K, each partition term of the nonzero nine-dimensional cubic partition parity has at least one wholly interior triple. If the cubic vanished on the tangent every such partition term would vanish. Translations preserve the cubic form. Thus the five global full signs restrict to a representation of a nonquadratic sign g on K.

**Four-full-sign rigidity.** Any rational representation of a nonquadratic binary sign by full quadratic signs needs an even common denominator. Indeed multiply through by a positive odd denominator, expand every sign as 1−2b for its Boolean exponent b, subtract the constant and reduce after division by two modulo two. This would express the target bit as an affine combination of degree-at-most-two bits. Independent full signs give rational representatives by Cramer's rule even if the initial representation is real. The normalized sign-matrix determinant bound caps coefficients' common primitive denominator by one for at most three independent full signs, and by two for four. Consequently g requires at least four full signs. If four form a basis of its span representing g, every coefficient is nonzero and, after common denominator two, all four positive magnitudes are at most two in the numerator. The number of odd numerators is even. Exactly two would imply that their two phases agree up to a global sign: reducing the cleared equation modulo four makes the parity of the two exponent bits constant. This contradicts column independence. Therefore all four basis coefficients have absolute value 1/2.

Suppose the five restrictions have rank five. A nonsingular sign submatrix determines all five original full coefficients on K, with normalized Hadamard bound three. Their 2-adic valuations are therefore at least minus one. Suppose instead their rank is four (smaller is impossible). Their nullspace has dimension one. Let I be the support of a nonzero null vector v. Dropping any i in I leaves a basis of the same span of four signs. The coefficient vector b_i for g in that basis has value zero at i and values of magnitude one half at each other index. For distinct i,j in I, b_i−b_j is a nonzero multiple of v. Its entries at i,j each have magnitude one half. Varying i,j shows all |v_i| on I coincide. If there is a third k in I, the difference at k must also have magnitude one half, whereas two entries drawn from {−1/2,+1/2} differ by zero or by one in magnitude. This contradiction gives |I|=2, since a single full-sign column is nonzero. Every coefficient vector on the five columns agrees with a basis representation at the three indices outside I; hence those three global full coefficients already have magnitude one half.

In either case at most two full terms lack the lower coefficient valuation minus one. In rank four their exceptional coefficients may even remain arbitrary reals in this argument. A common quadratic and linear twist kills these exceptional terms and both proper terms simultaneously. This is the independent-direction lemma of twisted-gauss-filter.md: sort the two proper dimensions first, then up to two full dimensions. They meet the greedy thresholds 1,3,5,7. Every remaining full coefficient has valuation at least minus one, and every nine-bit full quadratic Gauss sum is divisible by 32, so the sum after twisting has valuation at least four (or vanishes). The twisted cubic Gauss sum has exact valuation three, giving the contradiction. No denominator or arithmetic assumption is needed on the terms explicitly annihilated.

For the complementary-parallel case, on each halfspace exactly one of the two proper signs acts as a full sign. Each resulting list of six full signs restricts to an odd-top six-dimensional minor. The separate exact finite profile check in six-minor-profiles.md forces all coefficients there to have valuation at least minus one. In particular the five genuine full terms do. Kill the two proper atoms by the independent-direction lemma and use the same valuation contradiction. This last case uses the finite six-minor profile result; the other cases above use no finite enumeration.
