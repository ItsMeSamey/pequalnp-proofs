# A seven-term real representation with exactly one proper affine support is impossible (pending review)

Let G be the sum-of-three-disjoint-cubics sign on nine bits. Suppose it is a real combination of six full-support quadratic signs and one genuinely proper supported quadratic sign. Choose a hyperplane disjoint from that support, then take the six-dimensional odd-partition-parity cubic minor in that hyperplane. To see the minor, extend any basis of the hyperplane directions by one exterior vector. Group the nonzero top partition-parity expansion according to the two partners of the exterior vector. Some remaining six directions have odd partition parity. Translations and adding quadratics do not affect it.

**Six independent full phases are necessary on the minor.** Every Walsh coefficient of the minor sign times any quadratic has valuation exactly two: in the integer product expansion only a partition into two cubic monomials can attain this valuation, and the parity of such partitions is odd. Quadratic Gauss sums are divisible by eight on six bits. Any four full quadratic phases can be simultaneously made to have zero Gauss sum by a single quadratic/linear twist. For four real polars B_i on a six-space, arrange the fourth last. If at least two earlier polars differ from it, choose these as the first two and choose independent u,v with (B_1+B_2)(u,v)=0, (B_1+B_4)(u,v)=1. The bilinear comparison lemma in twisted-gauss-filter.md gives this, and alternation ensures independence. Choose a third direction greedily (two constraints, six dimensions). For the final direction the allowed space has dimension at least three, but excludes v because of the equation against u, so differs from the preceding three-span. If at most one earlier polar differs from B_4, use the greedy choices up through the third step; the fourth then has at most one constraint. Complete the alternating bilinear form and choose the linear sign as in the twist filter.

A representation on at most five independent full signs would thus require at least five coefficients of negative 2-adic valuation, since every other term vanishes modulo eight. The Cramer/Hadamard normalized determinant for r≤5 is at most three. This forces five odd numerators and common denominator two, impossible by parity at any input. Therefore the six phases of the proposed list are independent on this minor.

**Denominator and support.** Normalizing the size-six Cramer determinants by 32 bounds the positive numerators and common denominator D by six. If the proper support has positive dimension, twist by its extended phase and choose a character for which its flat indicator has zero transform. Since the twisted cubic Gauss sum has valuation three and full quadratic Gauss sums on nine bits are divisible by 32, v2(D)≥2. Consequently D=4. For a singleton support, the already written nearfull certificate proof.md excludes the list; it is independently testable. Alternatively the argument below need only be stated for nonsingleton support.

If at most four full numerators are odd, twist to annihilate these terms and the proper atom at once. The sort-by-dimension conditions hold since its direction has dimension at least one and the other four directions have dimension nine. All remaining terms are zero modulo sixteen despite the target valuation three. On the disjoint minor parity makes the number of odd numerators even, so all six are odd. Absorb signs into phases and sort. Possible individual numerators are 1, 3, 5.

**Exact cell test.** Check the 28 sorted numerator multisets in check_one_proper_six.py, using only Python's standard library. For each projective row s, its first sign is +1 after multiplying all phases and the output by that first phase. The allowed rows satisfy |sum a_i s_i|=4. Independence on the minor requires rank six. Only two multisets pass, (1,1,1,1,1,1) and (1,1,1,1,3,3). Both give exactly six admissible rows. Each occurs on the minor, since otherwise its evaluation rows would have rank below six. For every one of the six row types, its singleton indicator on those six rows is the normalized output bit plus an affine combination of relative sign bits: the checker exhausts the at most 128 affine characters. Consequently each cell on the minor is the support of its original cubic bit plus a quadratic.

Each such cell has at least 14 points. Indeed its weight is 2 modulo 4 by the exact valuation two, and a nonzero cubic on six bits has weight at least eight. A size-ten subset has only 45 unordered pairs, so some nonzero displacement out of the 63 is absent among its differences. Its indicator derivative then has weight 20. A quadratic bit on six variables cannot have this weight, since its real Gauss sum would be 24, neither zero nor a signed power of two. This excludes weight ten. Six disjoint cells of size at least 14 cannot fit in 64 points.

This argument excludes one proper real term. Two or more proper terms remain outside its scope.
