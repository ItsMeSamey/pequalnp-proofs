# Descent for two complex stabilizer terms

A scaled atom on its support is u(x)i^l(x)(a+ib), where u is a real quadratic sign and l is an affine binary bit, with quadratic carry included in u. Call it exceptional when its imaginary part has two different positive absolute values according to l. This means l is nonconstant, ab nonzero and |a| differs from |b|.

For a nonexceptional term its real part is zero or a single scaled real atom. Constant l gives this immediately. If a or b vanishes, retain one affine half. If |a|=|b|, its real part has constant magnitude on the original support and its sign differs from u by at most an affine sign.

If two terms sum to a real target their imaginary parts agree up to sign. If one is exceptional then both must be, since a nonexceptional imaginary part has at most one positive absolute value. Their supports coincide because neither imaginary part vanishes on its support. The two magnitude classes coincide up to relabeling; each class is an affine flat. On each class the real-to-imaginary ratio of each term is constant. The real sum there is a constant multiple of the first imaginary part, itself a scaled real quadratic sign on that class. This gives at most two real atoms. If neither term is exceptional, take their individual real parts instead. A single complex term similarly descends when its target is real.

This holds without a conjugation-invariance premise, and supports may change. Replacing three terms of a real eight-term product by two complex terms only helps if the residual already has a two-real-term decomposition.
