# Descent of conjugation-invariant complex atom spans

Let atom columns have the form s=1_U i^L (-1)^q with integer linear L on Boolean affine coordinates of U and binary quadratic q. Absorb global phase into coefficients. Write L=2K+l modulo four, l its parity bit. K is binary quadratic: the carry from summing low bits is the sum of their pairwise products, with a constant handled as one more summand. Thus p=Re s+Im s and n=Re s−Im s are real quadratic signs supported on exactly U, with phases q+K and q+K+l. The original s is a Gaussian linear combination of p,n.

Suppose independent complex atoms s_1,...,s_r span a complex space W invariant under entrywise conjugation. Every p_i,n_i lies in W. For any subset of original indices the associated pairs of real columns span a space of dimension at least the number of indices, since that space contains the independent s_i. Rado's independent-transversal theorem yields one of p_i,n_i per original index, all independent. These r real atoms form a basis of W with exactly the original support multiset. Every real target in W has real coordinates in this basis by uniqueness; rational target data permit rational coefficients by a nonsingular minor. Testing the premise is a single rank computation on [S,conjugate(S)] over Gaussian rationals. For seven atoms one can search 128 binary choices directly.

Consequently, every excluded seven-term real support pattern for G3, including seven full atoms and four full plus three hyperplanes, is excluded in the complex model whenever the independent list spans a conjugation-invariant space. A genuinely complex improvement on one of those patterns must have rank[S,conjugate(S)]>7. This does not exclude non-invariant spans containing a real target.
