# Exact complex stabilizer rank of two CCZ signs (pending independent review)

Take G_m(x)=(-1)^(sum of m disjoint triples). A complex atom on an affine support is i^l times a real quadratic sign, with l an affine binary bit in affine support coordinates; absorb any constant phase into its scalar. This includes arbitrary complex stabilizer amplitudes up to scale. We prove rank_C(G_2)=4 conditional on the independently established rank_R(G_2)=4 for supported real quadratic signs. The four-term upper bound is the product of two expansions 1−2·1_{111}.

For m=1,2 every overlap of G_m with a full complex quadratic phase is nonzero. Each Walsh coefficient of G_m times a real quadratic sign equals 2^m times an odd integer. Indeed expanding the integer product of 1−2M over nonconstant ANF monomials M, a selection of j monomials covering v variables contributes valuation j+3m−v. The only selections reaching m are the partition into the m disjoint original cubic triples (all other monomials added by twisting are at most quadratic). A complex overlap uses i^l, hence is 2^m((a+b)/2+i(a−b)/2) for odd a,b, by separating the two real Walsh sums. Its two integer coordinates inside the parentheses have different parities. The overlap has valuation 2m at the Gaussian prime 1+i.

Conversely k full complex phases on n binary variables can be made to have zero sums simultaneously if n>=2k−1. Write their Z/4 exponents with symmetric binary polar B_j, including diagonal entries. Choose independent t_j satisfying B_i(t_i,t_j)=B_j(t_i,t_j) for every i<j. At step j there are at most j−1 homogeneous equations, and the allowed dimension exceeds the span of earlier directions. Extend their compatible polar rows to one symmetric matrix B with B(t_j,-)=B_j(t_j,-). Every symmetric binary matrix is the polar of a Z/4 stabilizer phase (diagonal bits give odd linear terms). After subtracting this phase, the derivative of the jth phase along t_j is constant and even, because the difference polar vanishes on t_j including its diagonal. A single ordinary linear sign can make all these constants two modulo four. Translating by t_j then cancels each sum.

Suppose a list of at most three atoms gives G_2. A list of one or two descends to at most two supported real atoms by the two-term descent lemma, contradicting the known real rank. One may alternatively allow zero terms when inspecting the following deletion cases. Three full terms are impossible by simultaneous cancellation with n=6,k=3 and the nonzero twisted G_2 overlap. If exactly one term has proper support, take an affine hyperplane avoiding it. Every such hyperplane contains a split G_1 affine restriction: choose a coordinate with nonzero normal, set the other two bits of its cubic block to zero, and solve for the chosen coordinate, leaving the other original block free. The two remaining global full phases restrict to full complex phases. Simultaneous cancellation with n=3,k=2 contradicts the nonzero twisted G_1 overlap.

With at least two proper terms, delete one proper term on an opposite hyperplane. The two remaining restricted terms must both be nonzero and cover that hyperplane. One alone could only be a real quadratic sign if it equaled the full real target, impossible on the G_1 restriction. Both remaining supports cannot be full on the hyperplane by the same G_1 cancellation argument. Thus at least one of the two terms is alone at some point. There its scalar must be a fourth root of unity since the target there is ±1. For the second scalar, choose a point in its support. If it occurs alone its scalar is another fourth root; if both occur, its scalar is a nonzero difference of two fourth roots times a fourth root. All such complex numbers lie on an axis or a diagonal. Deleting a different proper term on a different hyperplane forces the same conclusion for the initially deleted coefficient. A restriction's phase normalization can at most rotate a coefficient by a fourth root, preserving the property.

For an axis or diagonal scalar c, taking the real part of c i^l (−1)^q yields zero or one scaled real atom. On an axis one parity of l is retained as an affine support. On a diagonal the real magnitude is constant on the support and its sign differs from (−1)^q by an affine sign. Because G_2 is real this gives at most three supported real atoms, contrary to rank_R(G_2)=4.

This settles only the two-copy complex rank, assuming the cited real result and the displayed lemmas. It rules out improving the three-copy product by tensoring a three-term two-copy witness with the two-term one-copy identity. It does not rule out a seven-term genuinely three-copy construction.
