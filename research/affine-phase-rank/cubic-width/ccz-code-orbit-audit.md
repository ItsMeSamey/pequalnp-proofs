# Binary translation-code spans for CCZ products

Let f(x)=Σ_i x_{i1}x_{i2}x_{i3} modulo two on n=3t bits; G(x)=(-1)^f(x). The Clifford translations K_u=D_f X_u D_f commute and form an honest binary group fixing G. A normalized-amplitude stabilizer atom φ has affine support A of codimension d and entries of unit modulus there, in {1,-1,i,-i} up to one global phase. Fix this phase. Its phase on A extends to a Clifford phase on the whole space: choose affine coordinates for A, extend its coordinate forms, and use the usual description i to an integer-linear exponent times a binary quadratic sign. Substituting binary affine forms preserves this phase class (the mod-four expansion of a parity contains only linear terms and even quadratic terms).

## One possibly proper code

Suppose G belongs to the complex span of {K_u φ : u∈C}, for a binary linear code C. Averaging a representation over C shows that the sum of the orbit vectors is nonzero and proportional to G. On the projective stabilizer the possible scalar is a sign (K_u²=1); a negative sign would annihilate the orbit sum. Thus the stabilizer acts trivially. Choose a complementary code C' in C. Its 2^c elements represent all distinct projective orbit points; write Σ_{u∈C'}K_u φ=aG. Evaluating at any point shows a is a nonzero Gaussian integer.

Write π=1−i. The integer Boolean multilinear coefficient of any atom on a set S has Gaussian valuation ≥|S|−d. Indeed each unary Clifford factor is 1+(i^h−1)x, each pair factor 1−2xy; the positive-degree l coefficient of one binary affine-equation indicator is zero or ±2^{l−1}. Thus each unary/pair phase has valuation at least the degree and each indicator can lose at most one. There are d indicators. Multiplication followed by Boolean multilinear reduction can only reduce monomial degree, and valuations are preserved under the triangle inequality. The same bound applies to each translate since K_u is Clifford and support codimension is unchanged. The degree-3t coefficient of G is (−2)^t, of valuation 2t. Hence vπ(a)≥t−d and |a|≥2^{(t−d)/2} (this inequality also holds if t<d).

Take the inner product with the unit vector proportional to G. Since the norms of φ and G are 2^{(n−d)/2} and 2^{n/2}, respectively,
|a|=2^{c−d/2}|〈G_norm, φ_norm〉| ≤ 2^{c−d/2}(3/4)^t.
The fidelity input is F(CCZ)=9/16 and stabilizer-fidelity multiplicativity for tensor products of at-most-three-qubit states in [Bravyi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.00128). Combining inequalities gives c≥(1/2+log₂(4/3))t. This concerns the size of the entire code orbit whose span contains G; it does not lower-bound a sparse list of points inside a larger orbit.

## Full translation group

The projective stabilizer S of φ inside the full translation group preserves A, so S lies in its direction space. On any S-coset within A the function D_f φ is a single character times a scalar, because it is a simultaneous translation eigenvector. Thus the third multiplicative derivatives of G on S vanish in the exponent: every third derivative of a Clifford phase is trivial, including for complex phases (a first derivative is a constant times a binary character). Equivalently T=Σ_i vol_i, the alternating trilinear polarization of f, restricts to zero on S³.

Discard the blocks where S projects to zero and let r be the number left. Extend scalars to the algebraic closure of F₂ and choose v in the extended S with every surviving block nonzero, possible by avoiding finitely many proper linear subspaces. The polar form T(v,·,·) has rank 2r on the ambient 3r-space, by the blockwise volume formula. Its isotropic subspace S has dimension at most 3r−r=2r. Thus any full orbit has size ≥2^t, whether or not it spans G.

If dim S=2t all blocks survive, and a maximal isotropic subspace for T(v,·,·) contains its radical. This radical is the direct sum of the individual block lines generated by v_i. Consequently for a dense open of v∈S every coordinate projection π_i v lies in S. Linearity extends this inclusion to every v, and scalar descent gives the same inclusion over F₂. Hence S splits blockwise; each individual summand has dimension at most two by isotropy of the volume, so all have dimension two. Pinning one coordinate per triple to zero with unit amplitude on the resulting subspace yields the sharp orbit. Each coset carries G's restricted Clifford phase, and the cosets partition the cube.

## Scope

Coefficients in the initial span can be arbitrary complex numbers. The integrality argument uses the equal-weight orbital sum obtained by averaging. The lower bound for a proper code does not apply separately to each orbit if several averages together span the target. Full-group orbit size is independent of this spanning assumption.
