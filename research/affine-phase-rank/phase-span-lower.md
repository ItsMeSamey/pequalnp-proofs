# Unconditional lower bounds for exact affine-supported phase sums

Let A_n consist of atoms 1_U(x)(-1)^Q(x) with U affine over GF(2), Q quadratic over GF(2), including the zero atom. Allow arbitrary real coefficients but require the final output to equal the Boolean target *exactly*. Rank means the fewest atoms. There is no final threshold or nonzero test.

**Unconditional instantiation of a published theorem.** For every fixed k there is a language L_k in NP whose characteristic function has rank larger than n^k infinitely often. For every unbounded α(n) for which n^α(n) is time constructible, some L in NTIME[n^α(n)] has no polynomial rank bound. These follow directly from Williams' [CCC 2018 Theorem 6](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol102-ccc2018/LIPIcs.CCC.2018.6/LIPIcs.CCC.2018.6.pdf) (full-version Theorem 1.5). All hypotheses hold: (i) row reduction gives O(n²)-bit atom descriptions plus the O(n²)-bit phase; (ii) evaluation is in P and x_i is the atom 1[x_i=1]; (iii) a product of up to four atoms is another atom (intersect supports, add phases), whose sum over the cube is a polynomial-time quadratic Gauss sum. The exact polynomial algorithm is stronger than the required fixed-exponential speedup. These are corollaries of the existing meta-theorem, without a novelty claim and without the October assumption.

**Audit of coefficients and gates.** If a real span of t atoms represents a Boolean target, first take an independent column basis of their N×t value matrix, then choose as many independent rows as columns. Cramer's rule for this {-1,0,1} matrix and Boolean right-hand side supplies rational coefficients having O(t log(t+1)) bits. This loses no sparsity; it makes guessed representations checkable. If F,G are Boolean exact sums of sizes s,t, multiplication gives AND in st terms, and 1-F gives NOT in s+1. Any binary Boolean gate has a multilinear real polynomial in its two inputs, so it has O((s+1)(t+1)) terms. For an arbitrary supplied gate list, nondeterministically guess exact sums for the gate signals only under the assumption that they have small descriptions. Verify each gate identity over the entire cube by the squared L² norm of its residual; the expansion uses products of at most four atoms. Verify input wires and zero output in the same way. Induction on the gate list yields soundness, and small gate-signal descriptions yield completeness. Under the hypothetical polynomial-sparsity representation of Circuit Evaluation, one may hardwire the circuit description and the gate index to provide these representations for every gate (the class is closed under projections and constant substitutions). The theorem derives its lower bound by combining this under-the-hypothesis verifier with a nondeterministic time hierarchy/easy-witness implication; there is no unconditional witness-compression assumption here.

**An extra fixed-language dichotomy.** If Circuit Evaluation has polynomial rank, the argument above puts ordinary Circuit-UNSAT in NP, since this special atom model computes all four-fold products in *polynomial* time. Consequently either the fixed P language Circuit Evaluation has superpolynomial rank, or NP=coNP. Equivalently, NP≠coNP implies a fixed P language with superpolynomial rank. This is a conditional restricted-model separation. It does not establish NP≠coNP. A polynomial-time uniform compiler from general circuits to exact polynomial-sparse sums would even put SAT in P by summing the Boolean indicator.

**Which closure the stronger theorem needs.** Williams' full-version Theorem 3.3 requires the *base atom* class to absorb an arbitrary specified bounded-fan-in NC⁰ layer at its inputs. This is distinct from applying a binary Boolean gate to two represented outputs. Already the affine atom 1[y=0] precomposed with y=x₁ AND x₂ has a three-element support, which cannot be the support of one atom. Thus the base-class input-closure hypothesis fails for fan-in two; the logical basis for directly invoking that stronger theorem is absent. This shows a failed proof step, not an impossibility result about an exponential E^NP bound. [Full-version Theorem 3.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.09121)

**Complex stabilizer comparison.** The standard computational-basis form of a qubit stabilizer amplitude is, up to one global scalar, 1_U(x)i^ell(x)(-1)^Q(x) in affine coordinates, with ell linear modulo four and Q quadratic over GF(2). The parity bit of ell is affine, while the second bit is quadratic (linear terms for the high bits of coefficients plus pairwise carries of low bits). Splitting support by the low bit gives two real signed quadratic atoms times scalar 1 or i. Affine coordinates on U extend to affine forms on the ambient space. For a real Boolean target, take the real part of the complex linear combination, obtaining at most two real atoms per stabilizer term. Conversely each real atom, normalized on its affine support, is a stabilizer amplitude. Thus real atom rank and exact complex stabilizer rank of the vector of Boolean values agree within a factor two. The restricted-model language lower bounds above transfer with adjusted constants/exponents. No claim about a prescribed magic-state tensor power, approximate rank, or AND without affine supports follows. AND itself is one singleton-support atom. [Peleg, Shpilka and Volk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03214)

**Realization-aware deterministic score recovery.** For any real integer-valued score S in the preceding moment model, set μ_j=sum_x S(x)^j. The Hankel matrix H_k=[μ_(i+j)]_(0≤i,j≤k) is a positive weighted Gram matrix of the vectors (1,b,...,b^k) for attained values b. Its rank is min(k+1,h), where h is the actual number of distinct scores, by Vandermonde independence. Increase k until the matrix first becomes singular, which occurs at k=h. A kernel vector with monic top coefficient gives ∏_(b attained)(z-b) since its squared evaluation sum vanishes. The integer roots are recoverable in polynomial bit time by deterministic univariate rational factorization or Sturm isolation and binary searches over the known score-bit range. Then solve the Vandermonde system from μ_0,...,μ_(h-1) for the exact multiplicities. All moment costs up to 2h are poly(L,n,h) binom(m+2h,2h), using the earlier expansion and caching. Running up to a cutoff K either supplies the exact score histogram or proves h>K. This method can decide arbitrary scalar outputs whenever the *realized* support fits K, without guessing rejecting levels or claiming general complement tractability. Binary weights on n independent bits realize 2^n different values.
