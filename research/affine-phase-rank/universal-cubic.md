# A fixed cubic family under affine substitution and exact summation

Write G_t(u,v,w)=(-1)^(sum_{i≤t} u_i v_i w_i) on 3t bits. Use exact real atom rank with 1_U(-1)^Q as defined in [the affine phase rank model](phase-span-lower.md).

**Universal simulation.** For any ordinary circuit C with input x, s≥1 ordered binary gates, output the last gate, use extra Boolean gate variables y and multipliers z. Let a_i,b_i be the wires feeding gate i, interpreted as input or gate variables, and g_i its Boolean polynomial of degree ≤2. Over GF(2) form
p(x,y,z)=y_s+sum_i z_i(y_i+g_i(a_i,b_i)).
It has at most 5s+1 monomials of degree at most three. Every such monomial is one triple of G, using coordinate projections, repetitions or pinned ones. Summing signs over z enforces each equation, yielding 2^s for the valid gate vector and zero otherwise. For each x there is exactly one valid y. Consequently
(-1)^C(x)=2^(-s) sum_{y,z} G_(5s+1)(affine(x,y,z)),
padding unused triples with zeros. The bit-valued characteristic function costs at most one additional atom and a scalar rescaling. Wires, constants and empty circuits are covered by adding a dummy output gate.

**Each atom survives projection as at most one atom.** Affine substitutions preserve affine support and degree ≤2. To sum over some coordinates, project an affine support onto the retained coordinates. Its fibers above the resulting affine support are cosets of a common kernel; write eliminated coordinates as an affine section of the retained coordinates plus a basis times a fiber variable v. In the resulting quadratic phase the purely-v quadratic part is fixed and the v-linear part has coefficients affine in the retained coordinates. A fixed change of basis puts the v-polar form into paired and radical coordinates. Summing a radical coordinate enforces that its linear coefficient vanishes, adding an affine constraint. Summing a paired block yields 2(-1)^(ab), where a,b are its two linear coefficients; ab is quadratic in the retained coordinates. Radical summation in the feasible case contributes powers of two. Thus the marginal is zero or a scalar times one atom with explicit affine support and quadratic phase. Everything here is computable by binary linear algebra in polynomial time. The coefficient 2^(-s) is harmless. Therefore rank((-1)^C)≤rank(G_(5s+1)), with no dependence on the circuit's time exponent beyond s itself.

**Fixed-family consequences.** If G_t has polynomial rank as a function of t, every signal of every size-s circuit has polynomial rank; there is one common exponent. Independent-atom bases and Cramer's rule bound coefficients by polynomial bit length. On input C, guess all gate-signal expansions as bit indicators. For each gate check its claimed identity as a real polynomial in predecessor indicators by summing the square of its residual; expansion uses at most four atoms per term and exact Gaussian sums. Wrong identities have positive norm. An accepting list therefore computes every gate correctly by induction, and a valid list exists by the universal simulation. Compute the exact #SAT count from the output expansion. For any PP machine on input x, build its polynomial-size circuit on a fixed-length choice string and test whether the certified count exceeds its specified majority threshold. Both majority and its complement have NP certificates. As NP⊆PP and PP is closed under complement, PP=NP=coNP follows. This is an implication from an unproved nonuniform representation upper bound, not an unconditional class separation. Contrapositively, PP≠NP implies superpolynomial affine-phase rank for the single linear-time cubic family. Since G=1-2·G_bit, this also applies to its Boolean cubic. Polynomial-time construction of G expansions, with polynomial coefficient bit lengths, would deterministically produce circuit output sums and exact #SAT and thus put #P in FP. Nonuniform compact G representations give #P computations with polynomial advice by hardwiring representations for all O(s) relevant lengths. If the signs are instead represented by complex stabilizer amplitudes, splitting each into ≤2 real atoms transfers all polynomial-rank conclusions. Earlier work already connected polynomial stabilizer rank to simulation of universal quantum computations; this is a direct Boolean path-sum and local-certificate derivation for this G family. [Bravyi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.00128)

**Quantified NP time bound.** For each k≥1 some L_k in NTIME[n^O((k+1)^5)] lacks O(n^k)-sparse exact sums. If the polynomial-time Circuit Evaluation language already lacks them, take it. Otherwise its O(n^k)-term representations, after pinning the circuit description and gate index, yield O((n+s)^{O(k+1)})-size certificates for a fan-in-two circuit on n inputs and s gates. The polynomial cost of four-atom checks has an absolute degree, so s≤2^(εn) gives nondeterministic UNSAT time ≤2^((1-ε)n) for ε=c/(k+1) with sufficiently small universal c. This handles gap-UNSAT as well. An O(n^k)-term exact sum has an ordinary circuit of size O(n^{d}) with d=O(k+1), using bounded-bit rational coefficients and ordinary arithmetic. [Murray–Williams Theorem 1.1](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/easy-witness-nqp.pdf) gives some language of nondeterministic time n^O(d^4/ε) outside this ordinary size bound, hence outside the O(n^k)-term model. All implicit exponents in the elementary steps are absolute. The argument supplies different languages for different k. Padding a time n^p verifier to length n^(p/c) to get fixed degree c reduces its transferred hardness exponent k to ck/p; the displayed fifth-power guarantee does not justify an unbounded transferred exponent.

**Bounds for this explicit cubic.** Under the normalization 2^(-3t/2), the sign vector G_t is t copies of the CCZ state. Bravyi et al., Proposition 1 and Equation (31), give stabilizer extent (16/9)^t. An unnormalized atom on codimension h contributes a normalized stabilizer ket with coefficient 2^(-h/2) times its original weight c. Thus every decomposition into r terms with |c|≤B satisfies rB≥(4/3)^t, including integer or rational coefficients with a stated magnitude bound. Fractional precision alone is not a magnitude bound. For unrestricted coefficients the October [Sinha–Lovitz preprint, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06819) gives extent ≤2^O(sqrt(r log(r+1))) for a normalized state of stabilizer rank ≤r. Combining the two yields r=Ω(t²/log(t+1)), equally for arbitrary real exact atoms. The Boolean cubic represented by indicators instead of signs has at least this many terms up to an additive one. These are applications of cited quantum results; no independent verification of the recent rank–extent theorem is asserted. They prove an explicit near-quadratic restricted-model rank lower bound, far short of superpolynomial.

**Elementary cross-check without stabilizer extent.** Three applications of Cauchy–Schwarz bound correlation of any sign with a quadratic phase by its Gowers U³ norm (the third multiplicative derivative of the quadratic phase is 1). For one triple, the additive third derivative of uvw is the determinant of the three random direction vectors in GF(2)^3. Their 3×3 direction matrix is invertible in 7·6·4 of 8³ cases, so the cube average is 1-2·168/512=11/32. Independent triples factor, yielding U³(G_t)=(11/32)^(t/8). An affine indicator is the average of signed linear characters from its annihilator (or zero for empty support), so this also bounds correlation with every atom. Pairing an exact decomposition with G_t in normalized counting measure gives sum |c|≥(32/11)^(t/8), weaker than the quantum constant but independently exponential. Pairing with the Boolean cubic gives half the same bound up to the vanishing bias correction, or simply convert the Boolean expansion to the sign expansion.
