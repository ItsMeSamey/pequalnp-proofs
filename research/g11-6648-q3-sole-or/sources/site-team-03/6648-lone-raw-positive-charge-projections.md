# One raw-positive exceptional OR among raw-empty exceptions

Assume q≥3, only one raw-positive paid OR P, and vertex x=3 has sole AND reader g, either dirty or clean centreless. All other paid ORs have empty raw support. The no-x-star footprint gives at most one exclusive double whenever U≥8. The following projections give partial coverage; no numerical parameter scan is included.

A raw-positive exceptional P with two raw-positive pins is physically pure: an empty-raw signal could not enter either pure-raw path without an earlier paid OR with nonempty raw output. To split P choose one of its two pure inlet disjunctions everywhere. All raw-positive pins in the resulting branches are pure. For Lambda gates above P the resulting raw support is contained in the original anchored raw support. At P itself the chosen pure pin has its own Lambda anchor or is singleton, as there is only one raw-positive exception. Each projected centreless root admits at most B(n,r) anchored products in each branch, where n is the surviving dirty index count, r the surviving empty paid OR count, and B(n,r)=min(2^r,binom(n,floor(n/2))) with the antichain cap sharpened to 5 at n=4. This uses the 6648-all-raw-empty-charges.md flag and four-atom lemmas. Raw-positive (ordinary) top pairs are original stars on the original circuit, even if an ordinary side contains P. Thus no ordinary corrections are needed.

Otherwise P=H∨R with H raw-empty and R pure raw-positive. There are exactly two functional branches at each centreless root T: T[P←R] and H∧T[P←true]. The replacement may be performed on all occurrences simultaneously, with any amount of fanout. Equality of their union to T follows pointwise by considering the value of H; each term is dominated by the original clean root. In the raw branch every raw-positive pin becomes its original raw support, and at P use R's anchor. In the true branch any positive OR pin reached from P through ORs is identically true; the other positive OR pins retain original pure supports. This remains true when both arms of a Lambda gate reach P. For each dirty index reassemble one formal atom from its surviving pure direct pins in this branch. The H cone is earlier than P so the same atom definitions work inside it. Thus H times the true-branch root can be counted as ONE monotone function on n atoms, rather than the Cartesian product of two families. Its product-batch bound is B*(n,r)=min(2^r,binom(n,floor(n/2))); the four-atom refinement for a physical binary top is not asserted for this virtual product. Whenever H is a dirty AND with at least one pure raw-positive pin in the true branch, every nonzero minimal support of this virtual product includes H's own atom, and the antichain part of B* improves to binom(n−1,floor((n−1)/2)). The pure or raw branch retains the original B bound.

All ordinary corrections combine into H∧G. Here G is the raw disjunction of all opposite ordinary guards at tops having H as a disjunct. H cannot occur in both pins of one ordinary top, since its value can be true on an independent input. A raw literal r∈R anchors G by original stars, since every affected mixed side includes r. All other ordinary contributions are original stars. The same B* bound applies to H∧G, or omit it if G is empty. If H itself is a pure product only one batch is needed.

First pay at most the two graph edges containing x. In the clean case g's only positive minterms are among these; use a=c−1,n=j,r=q−1. In the dirty case set g=false on every wire, which is exact on all other pairs and decreasing globally, and eliminate its sole raw-empty charged reader M=g∨D. Then a=c,n=j−1,r=q−2, using original raw support and flags. If this projection kills P's dirty donor, omit both the correction and the second branch. Otherwise the preceding discussion applies to the surviving functions. If the original pure pin of P changes after deleting g, that would require a second raw-positive exception on its OR-only path, impossible.

Thus the pure-P and mixed-P sufficient bounds for U≥8 are 3+2aB(n,r) and 3+a(B(n,r)+B*(n,r))+B*(n,r), respectively; replace individual terms by the smaller caps above if applicable. Tops independent of P need no second batch family; roots already requiring H need only their true-side family. These are sufficient inequalities, not universal conclusions on the whole feasible region.

For clean g and c=1 all cases are settled without sparsity assumptions. The two incident x edges are followed only by ordinary corrections. Put G0=G11−3. When G is nonempty it is contained in the neighbors of r in G0, with rG starred. Any residual edge covered by H∧G is incident to G and is not one of these spokes. The count of such candidate edges is monotone in G within N(r), since a newly removed spoke was not previously incident to G; compute on all neighbors. For r=0,1,2,4,5,6,7,8,9,10 these counts are at most 6,4,5,6,6,5,5,6,6,6 respectively, by summing degrees minus one on the neighbor set and subtracting its internal edges. Thus there are at most six such x-free corrections and U≤8≤j+2c+q. This incidence bound also permits capping the total ordinary correction on x-free edges by six in the other cases.

The open cases concern simultaneous losses in the raw and dirty antichains across several centreless roots and the ordinary term. This is a mathematical reduction, without an arithmetic census or a Lean certificate.
