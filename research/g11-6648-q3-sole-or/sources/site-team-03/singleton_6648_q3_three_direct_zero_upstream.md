# q=3, three direct dirty-AND-reading ORs, zero secondary ancestors of D

Assume M=x OR D is x=3's sole reader, with D dirty AND, and that the two other paid ORs Q1,Q2 also read dirty ANDs directly. Neither Q is an ancestor of D. The Qs may be earlier than, incomparable with, or descended from M, and either comparable to each other or not. Index them in their own topological order. Then U≤j+3+2c.

D has a pure anchored conjunctive cone: any dirty AND entering an OR there would require another direct receiver, and a clean AND ancestor violates TopForm. The 9/10 sole-reader safety and induced-matching argument give at most three D-positive residuals in two protected batches. Globally set D=false and forward M to x. This preserves all D-negative inputs and decreases all output values. A secondary cannot reach M, since the only possible end of that path would be D, which has no secondary ancestor. This is the only placement constraint needed when using the two downstream notes below; no secondary must receive M.

By the private-donor exclusion D has an external reader. Follow an AND-only continuation until first clean AND or first OR. In the former case a centreless top requires D and may be omitted on D-negative edges. In the latter the reached input implies D and the OR is a secondary receiver: it cannot re-enter M through its designated D input without a cycle. A live path of dirty ANDs cannot end in a clean graph output.

**Escape to a receiving OR.** That secondary forwards its other pin after the global D=false cut. For a positive secondary this other wire has the entire original raw support and can carry the other selector. For an empty secondary its other wire is a dirty AND, an earlier raw-empty selector, or zero after projection. It retains empty reach. In particular no uncharged OR can directly consume that empty wire: a dirty OR with an empty inlet cannot belong to lambda and a clean aggregation OR cannot read a dirty signal. If an earlier surviving raw-empty selector is forwarded, retain that selector once. If a surviving positive selector is upstream of the forwarded AND, expand its same bit throughout the AND cone. This introduces no new raw primitive. There is at most one real selector after forwarding and the other charges are gone. If it is positive, its supplier is now a pure anchored product or zero, each centreless top splits into ≤2 products, and ordinary dirty corrections form one product with the union of opposite raw guards (anchored by a fixed raw partner vertex). If empty, choosing either physical arm globally gives ≤2 products at a centreless top and no ordinary correction: empty signals cannot enter positive OR reach except through a genuine further charge, and the collapsed receiver preserves the raw reach of its remaining operand. Include D to get ≤2+1+2c anchored batches. With only homogeneous doubles this gives U≤2c+4. With a nonhomogeneous double U≤9, sufficient unless c=0 or c=1,j=3. At c=0 donor ≤3 plus ordinary ≤2. At c=1,j=3 failure requires U=9 on ≤5 batches; exactly seven stars permit only one nonhomogeneous and one homogeneous exclusive double by the seven-spoke rigidity in 6648-q3-sole-or-downstream-positive.md, giving ≤7 there.

**Escape to centreless.** Omit the D-required top on D-negative inputs. For two positive remaining receivers use 6648-q3-sole-or-downstream-positive.md from “Otherwise there are k≥1”. Its calculations use D's purity, the common global projection and the physical distinctness counts; they never use M as an ancestor of a supplier. When a supplier equals D its contribution vanishes. With at least one empty secondary use 6648-q3-sole-or-downstream-empty.md. If the last secondary is empty, its AND-only route to the top cannot re-enter M because M is inaccessible, and no later secondary exists. Its unique-terminal physical factoring therefore applies before projection even in this broader order. If empty precedes positive, an OR escape from the empty gate or a later dirty AND must be that positive receiver, not M. Its positive raw operand can initially carry M, but after M→x is pure and retains original raw reach. The same four-mode analysis and shared direct-input counts follow. This includes two empty gates, empty arms which themselves forward an earlier empty selector, repeated dirty suppliers, and collapsed arms. It proves the stated bound on all zero-upstream branches.

One upstream secondary gives two protected modes instead of the single original pure D (choice of actual arms if empty, raw vs guarded donor if positive). Two upstream secondaries can require four protected modes. These need their own counts and are not asserted by this zero-upstream argument.
