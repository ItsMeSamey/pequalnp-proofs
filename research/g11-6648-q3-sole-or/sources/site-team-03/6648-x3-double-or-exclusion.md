# G11 vertex 3 cannot have two OR readers, conditional proof for review

Let x=3,y=6,z=7. N(x)={y,z}, N(y)={x,z,10}, N(z)={x,y,9}. Assume the eight-vertex graph function H obtained by deleting x,y,z requires at least 15 exact-two earlier-wire AO gates. Assume also the unrestricted lower bound 19 for G minus x (L_meta_18373). Suppose a globally least TopForm G circuit of size at most 21 has exactly two direct readers of x, both ORs. Every restriction below eliminates or bypasses the named gates and keeps the other gates in order, renumbering wires, including the output. Nonconstant graph cofactors need no physical constant at output. A restriction meeting its lower bound has no dead gates, absorbable gates or duplicate pins, since these can be bypassed or removed.

**Cofactor floors.** The x=true cofactor is y OR z OR H and needs ≥17: each successively fixed false free variable is essential and loses at least one reader. The cofactor obtained from G minus x by deleting y still has essential z and z=false leaves H, hence needs ≥16. In a size-17 circuit for y OR z OR H, each y,z has a unique direct OR reader: deleting that raw variable false may cost only one gate, whereas an AND reader becomes zero and has a further reader since that nonconstant cofactor cannot have the AND as output. These variable-deletion arguments also exclude a raw output (there are edges avoiding each variable).

**Original y,z cannot have sole OR readers.** A sole reader y OR W makes W independent of y. If W is true on a singleton t, then toggling y at t or at t together with each neighbor of y implies either acceptance of a singleton or two common neighbors of y and t. Thus W rejects every singleton and is nonraw. If W were clean, it would be a clean AND or have a clean AND ancestor (expand clean ORs; no input is clean). The live y OR W has a downstream AND because otherwise singleton y reaches the output. This contradicts TopForm. Thus W has an independent witness T excluding y. T excludes N(y) or adding y would create an edge while leaving W true. Every neighbor of y must touch T or, adjoining that neighbor to T, toggling y again changes the graph but not the only y-port. This is impossible for neighbor x, whose only raw neighbors y,z are both absent from T. Similarly for z.

**Two live masks.** Let L=x OR A,M=x OR B. Neither can consume the other directly (duplicate) and neither can be output (singleton x). At true both disappear and every distinct direct reader of them also disappears. At most two such readers exist by the ≥17 bound. If there is only one it consumes both L,M and distributivity or associativity factors x to save one gate. If two readers include a shared reader joining L,M, that shared reader becomes true and cannot output the nonconstant cofactor. Its next reader must be the second direct reader to avoid a fifth loss; the latter would combine L or M with their product or sum, an absorbable gate in the original. Hence two distinct unique unshared readers exist. Neither reader can be OR: it too becomes true, and must feed the other direct reader to avoid a fifth loss. Both pins at that second reader are then true, forcing a further deletion before the nonconstant output. Thus R=L AND C and S=M AND D. At true delete exactly L,M,R,S, forwarding C,D (recursively if one is the other AND's output). An output alias to R or S can in general be forwarded to the corresponding surviving wire; before relying on a surviving reader below we exclude the output either by its being bounded by y/z or by its evaluating to a constant in a nonconstant cofactor. At false delete L,M and forward A,B. These retained cards are optimal of sizes 17 and 19, and the original has size 21.

Each y,z occurs at some raw pin of these four deleted gates: otherwise all its original direct readers survive at true, forcing a sole original OR reader by the size-17 property. If a deleted AND forwards a raw variable on to a surviving reader, no additional original direct reader of that variable can survive. Distinct surviving gates contradict unique readership, and duplicate pins on the same surviving gate contradict optimality.

**At least one donor A,B is y or z.** Otherwise C=y,D=z up to swaps. When R,S each has a surviving reader, the original has no other direct reader of y,z, respectively. With x=0, yz on, H inputs zero, gates remain zero: the first nonzero gate would have to be R or S and would require another nonzero inlet, impossible. If R has no surviving reader it cannot be output (R≤y) and its only possible downstream deleted consumer is M as B=R: both AND guards are raw and L precedes R. Now S has a surviving reader by acyclicity (and S≤z cannot be output). Hence z enters only via S. With x=y=0, B=R=0 and S=0, so z9 is indistinguishable from singleton 9. Symmetrically if S has no surviving reader.

**The other donor.** Relabel and exchange arms so A=y. At singleton y, B must be false: if already true both x ORs would stay true upon adjoining x, even with cross-arm dependence by forward induction, but xy is an edge. Suppose B is not z; it is not y either (duplicate). Then z must occur as C or D. If C=z, R=(x OR y) AND z is a clean top. It cannot be output (R≤z) and must have a surviving reader: it cannot feed S directly or through M, by TopForm, or feed its predecessor L. The true card forwards R as raw z. If no other original raw z-reader exists, R cannot realize edge z9; if another exists it cannot survive, so D=z. Now both x paths are bounded by z, missing xy. In the other case D=z and C differs from z. If S has a surviving reader, z originally enters only via S and hence B, an ancestor of S, is z-independent. On yz with x=0, B retains its false singleton-y value and z cannot produce the graph edge. If S has no surviving reader it is not output (S≤z); only possible deleted consumer is R as C=S since L has raw x,y. Then all x-influence is bounded by z and xy is missed. Consequently B=z.

**Nonraw guards.** C is true on singleton z: comparing z with xz keeps M true and changes only L at the entry ports. C cannot depend on L except through its own consumer R, so C is unchanged; to distinguish these inputs R must change. Similarly D is true on singleton y. Hence only possible raw choices are C=z or D=y. If C=z, R is clean and has a surviving reader as above. Original z readers must be M and R (D=z is incompatible with D being positive on singleton y). Compare x9 and z9. M is true at both and R false at both; L only feeds R, x only feeds L,M, and z only feeds M,R. Thus all other outputs agree but only z9 is an edge. Similarly D=y contradicts the differing inputs x10 and y10. Both guards are nonraw. If either is the other AND gate, recursive true forwarding still ends in a nonraw old guard. Neither can be either raw OR L,M (cyclic or a shared direct reader). Thus no forwarded raw y,z can manufacture the unique surviving OR readers in the true card. Call these original ORs E_y,E_z; they may coincide if they are y OR z.

**Final 19→16→15.** At x=false the two ORs disappear and the card still contains R=y AND C and S=z AND D, with the raw y,z direct ORs E_y,E_z. Fix y=false. R and E_y disappear (R becomes zero, E_y forwards its other input). R cannot be output because there are edges avoiding y. E_y cannot be a consumer of R: y OR R equals y in this 19-gate least card since R≤y. Thus R's zero signal consumes at least one *distinct* additional gate T. Exactly these three may be removed by the ≥16 floor. T must be OR. If AND, T≤R≤y, T becomes zero, and cannot be output because the restriction is nonconstant. A reader beyond T would be a fourth deletion, unless it were already E_y (R precedes T). But E_y reading T would already compute y OR T=y, contradicting leastness. This covers both possible chronological relationships of E_y with R,T: an upstream E_y cannot be a downstream reader, and a downstream one relying on their forced signal would be redundant. Bypasses to or from the output are permitted but cannot make a zero wire the output of the nonconstant remaining function.

Therefore only R,E_y,T with T an OR need be deleted. In the 16-gate retained circuit S, a distinct AND, survives and its direct raw z pin remains. If S had consumed R directly it would itself be the forbidden AND T. Any further simplification at y=false (forced pin, dead or aliased S, duplicated gate) would separately violate the ≥16 floor. Now put z=false. S is forced zero and cannot be the output because H is nonconstant. S is live by size-16 optimality, so its reader too can be bypassed or eliminated even if that reader itself is the output. This gives at most 14 gates for H, contradicting the assumed 15 lower bound. No classification of optimum H wiring and no preservation of TopForm under restriction was assumed.
