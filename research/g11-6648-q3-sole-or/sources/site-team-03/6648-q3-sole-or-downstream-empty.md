# A raw-empty secondary charge on a centreless escape

Continue the hypotheses of 6648-q3-sole-or-downstream-positive.md. The two further exceptional ORs descend from M, at least one has empty raw reach, and here an external AND-only path from D reaches a centreless top requiring D. Set D=false globally and M=x, preserving D-negative edges. Omit that centreless top in the projection. D itself is an upstream anchored conjunction with at most three protected positive residuals over two deletion batches. In the purely anchored-batch cases use the earlier seven-star rigidity: U>9 precludes any nonhomogeneous double; U=9 permits at most one such exclusive double. The only nonhomogeneous small threshold here is c=1,j=3; the omitted centreless root leaves at most two ordinary batches, hence U≤3+4≤7.

If the last charge E is raw-empty, it has just one centreless destination. Every post-E path to the first clean top consists of AND gates. For E and each downstream dirty AND, symbolic true substitution on AND readers, reaggregation of affected tops and one final restoration show at most one direct reader by leastness. This is exactly the last-empty factoring lemma in singleton_6648_q2_sole_or_all_c_cases.md; earlier M and the first secondary charge cannot be downstream of E. After D=false, one earlier effective selector supplies at most two anchored projections at each ordinary/centreless term (if positive); a raw-empty earlier selector has two conjunctive alternatives and no ordinary correction. The E destination has ≤4 anchored batches, two expansions for each of the two E arms. If an E arm is the earlier raw-empty OR itself, expand its two alternatives directly; the common guard still uses that same selector, so fixing a true arm suffices. Other centreless roots use ≤2 and ordinary corrections ≤1. If the E destination is the D-required root it is omitted; otherwise c≥2. Thus there are at most 2+1+4+2(c−2)=2c+3 batches in the latter case, including D's two. This proves U≤2c+j+3 by the preceding homogeneous/nonhomogeneous cases. If instead the earlier charge has two raw-positive pins and only indirectly inherits dirt, its D=false value is a single exceptional pure OR with individually anchored/singleton children. There is no earlier secondary nonraw source. Each non-E root is a pure conjunction with at most that exceptional primitive, each E alternative likewise. The one-exception lemma gives ≤2 residuals per term without any homogeneous classification, so U≤3+2c+2.

Now let the first secondary E be raw-empty and the last Q raw-positive. Before E no raw-empty OR can exist below tops (it would be dirty and another charge). Hence E's pins are two distinct dirty ANDs H1,H2. After D=false each is an anchored conjunction or zero. If an arm is zero, E has just one conjunctive alternative and the remaining positive Q gives at most one ordinary and two per centreless top if Q reads a dirty or raw-empty inlet, treated just as the one-remaining-selector argument. If Q has two raw-positive pins see the exceptional-pure case below.

When Q directly reads Z with empty raw reach, Z is either E itself or a dirty AND. Its other, raw-positive pin projects to a pure clause: E cannot enter an intervening OR before Q. At any AND-product region, its expression is a raw conjunction times E if E occurs on an AND-only input path, since E is the sole possible disjunction of nonraw products before Q. In particular Z=K times an optional factor E, including Z=E. Ordinary corrections aggregate across all tops into Z times one virtual raw guard, expanding into ≤2 batches. Each centreless expression is K_T times an optional E times a product of mixed pins Z∨R_i (with ordinary multiplicative Z factors retained as needed). Distribution gives at most four anchored conjunctions: up to two alternatives for the all-raw contribution and two for the Z contribution.

A direct H1 or H2 input forces E=true and costs at most two terms. A direct Z input forces Q=true and also costs at most two, since it may still read E. Distinct centreless tops cannot share a direct operand in a least circuit: combine their products by factoring the common wire and reaggregate all tops, saving one gate. All raw-empty operands of such tops come from dirty ANDs or E (other exceptional ORs and all Lambda ORs are raw-positive). If D,H1,H2,Z are distinct dirty gates, at most m≤min(j−4+1,c−1) nonomitted tops can require four terms: each selects either a remaining distinct dirty AND or E. Including D protection and ordinary terms gives at most 2+2+2(c−1)+2m batches. With the homogeneous bonus this is ≤2c+3+2m; 2m≤j suffices. Otherwise j≥7 and c≥floor(j/2)+2, making the target already ≥16. If Z=H1 or H2, the Z term itself implies E so costs one and the raw term costs ≤2. At most j−2 tops need the extra term (unused dirty slots plus E); only one ordinary aggregate is needed. If Z=E, a top that requires E kills the Q distinction; otherwise its raw term costs one, dirty term ≤2. Direct H1,H2 or E therefore cost ≤2, leaving ≤j−3 extras. These counts also suffice. Equality with D or dominance by D that makes a pin false reduces to the fewer-selector case.

Finally Q can inherit dirt indirectly with two raw-positive pins. E cannot reach either pin: the first way from E into raw reach would itself be an earlier exception. Accordingly Q projects to one exceptional pure OR, whose immediate children are anchored or raw singletons (M reduces to raw x). No charged gate can consume the all-AND output path out of E, so it again has one centreless destination by factoring. Ordinary tops are pure star products; ordinary OR-only raw supports remain the original ones. Non-E roots each have one conjunction with at most one exceptional primitive, and E's destination has at most two. Use the one-pure-exception lemma, not the homogeneous-double argument: U≤3+2c+2 throughout.

The counting addresses arbitrary j≥3 and c in this centreless-escape regime with at least one empty secondary charge. No new computation or Lean verification.
