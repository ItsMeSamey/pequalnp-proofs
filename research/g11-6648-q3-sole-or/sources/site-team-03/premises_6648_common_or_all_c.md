# G11: exclusion of the common OR at a clean sole AND, for every c

Assume a size-21 least TopForm AO2 circuit computing the issued graph, x=3, y=6, z=7. Suppose x's only raw reader is the clean AND A=x∧S and the direct-reader classification has the physical common OR h=y∨z. Make no assumption that S is pure or that h lies in its OR cone. The lower bounds used are 19 for G11−x and 15 for H8=G11−{x,y,z}. Write q=|orN minus lambda|. We prove q≥3.

## The raw-reader interface, before and after restriction

S is independent of x, positive on singleton y and on singleton z, so is neither raw y nor raw z. Setting x=false deletes A and its unique OR reader T=A∨K: this is the tight 19-card reduction in 6648-size21-sole-and.md. In particular there can be no second direct A reader, and a sole AND reader would force an extra deletion. The exact 19-card is least; keep each other original gate. K is false on each singleton y,z: if K were already true on such a singleton then T would remain true on adjoining x, and every path from x passes A,T, so the graph could not distinguish the singleton from its x-edge. K therefore is neither raw y nor raw z. Deleting A,T and forwarding K introduces no new direct y or z reader, removes none, and preserves the op of every such reader. The 19-card's raw-reader classification with common h says y has precisely h and an individual Ry=y∨V, and z precisely h and a distinct individual Rz=z∨V'. Thus this is also an exhaustive enumeration of ORIGINAL readers, not only of card readers. Here that classification uses the H8 lower bound, the impossibility of original sole OR readers for y,z, and the sole-AND obstruction at those variables when x has only A; see 6648-size21-sole-and.md.

The classification also gives exactly one card reader B=h∨W with the other pin not forced false on deleting y,z. B exists unchanged in the original. In particular its other pin cannot originally have been T forwarding W: T has clean AND ancestor A, whereas B is positive on a singleton, is not output, and has a downstream AND (an all-OR path to the clean output would accept that singleton). Such a path violates TopForm. The new wires from forwarding T cannot point to h since K is false on the two singletons. B,Ry,Rz are distinct. At Ry or Rz a direct h pin would make that OR equal to h; B with another pin y or z would likewise be redundant.

## Two losses on the card and their transfer

On the 19-card replace B=h∨W by z∨W, then delete the now unused h. This yields an 18-gate decreasing circuit. It cannot compute the whole graph card. A positive graph-edge minterm must be lost since graph minterms suffice to check a decreasing monotone replacement. The only possible changed edge is y–10: absent y, B is unchanged, and yz still sets B. Hence y–10 is lost and W is false on its pair input. Exchanging y,z shows z–9 is lost in the opposite trial and W is false on that pair as well. Consequently W rejects both singletons y,z.

Both spokes are genuine residuals in the original circuit. If a spoke were an original raw-star cross pair at an ordinary top, its two raw endpoint paths suffice to activate that top. This top cannot be A, whose cross pairs all contain x. Neither path can pass B: raw reachability through B brings in y and z, and a vertex in the opposite nonempty raw side must be a common neighbor of both, namely x. Only A can have x in a raw side, since x has only an AND reader. Nor can such paths depend on deleted h except through B on the 19-card: originally an extra h→A is possible when S=h, but A cannot occur on any raw path to another top. T cannot be on such a path because the clean A would be below another AND. Thus the two endpoint paths survive the card and the loss trial. Once the top activates, its path to the output is OR-only by TopForm and liveness, and cannot use dirty B or h (either above this clean top would put a clean AND below a later AND). The card bypass of T forwards its input K since A is zero; it could not destroy propagation of this other true top. This would preserve the spoke, contradicting the loss.

## Three paid physical gates

B is dirty since positive on singleton y. If B lay in lambda, its raw side brings both y,z, so its opposite singleton would be their unique common neighbour x. Only A has raw centre x; thus B lies in the OR cone of S. Cleanliness of A forces every raw leaf of S to be a neighbor of x, i.e. y or z (x in S would accept singleton x). It follows that the raw support of W lies in {y,z}. But W is false at both singletons by the two losses, giving empty raw support, contrary to lambda requiring two raw-positive operands. Thus B is paid even when it lies in the OR cone of S.

Ry is dirty since positive on singleton y. If in lambda, its opposite singleton centre must neighbor y, so is 10,z or x. Centre 10 would star the residual y–10. Centre z forces every raw leaf in V to be starred with z. No raw y can occur in V: then V≥y and the original Ry equals V, contrary to leastness. Of z's remaining neighbors x and 9, x cannot feed V by a raw OR path, and 9 would star the residual z–9. Thus this centre cannot supply nonempty raw support for V. Finally, centre x implies Ry lies in S's OR cone, hence raw(V)⊆{y,z}. Raw y was just excluded. If V were true at singleton z, both entire outgoing channels of y (h and Ry) would already be true on that input. On adjoining y their values could not change; all later gate values would therefore be unchanged, contradicting that yz is a graph edge and singleton z is not. This formal last step follows by induction on gate index because only h and Ry read raw y; it does not presume that V is independent of y. Thus V has no raw z either. Centre x cannot witness lambda for Ry. Exchanging y,z and their private neighbors 10,9 proves Rz paid. The three distinct gates B,Ry,Rz establish q≥3 for any number of centreless tops.

The result uses the original circuit's star edges, paid gates and physical readers. It requires no projected-donor hypothesis and does not cover a common AND or the four-private-reader regime.
