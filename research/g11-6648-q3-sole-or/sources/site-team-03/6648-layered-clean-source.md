# A projected donor bound with an omitted neighbor

Use x=3,y=6,z=7 and clean sole reader A=x∧S. Assume S omits at least one raw neighbor. A donor means a dirty AND with a direct OR reader. Suppose every paid dirty OR directly reads a dirty AND, so there are no additional paid pure ORs or paid ORs arising only from a raw-empty OR child. Erase every donor-to-OR pin at once by giving that pin zero. Denote a projected donor by D°. Different donors and their AND subgraphs may share wires or occur in layers. Suppose each nonzero D° is clean on y=z=0 and accepts singleton y or z, except optionally one donor rejecting both. Cleanliness on that slice follows, for example, from D°≤S, since a positive independent assignment there could otherwise be extended by x.

On any edge activated by any original donor, its first positive donor in topological order has that same positive value after projection. Every ordinary top whose projected inputs accept an edge stars it: its two operands have become raw ORs. Remaining projected centreless tops each cover at most two residuals. Indeed each projected AND expansion has raw singleton or Lambda-anchored disjunction clauses (false clauses kill it). A charged OR receiving a direct dirty-AND pin either collapses to its remaining input or to zero. Every nontrivial OR resulting from an uncharged nonclean OR uses its original raw support; every clean OR is above the ANDs by TopForm. The anchored-clause induced-matching argument of team08's one-charge proof applies verbatim. The same bound applies to D° on the clean slice. For r induced-matching edges accepted by one conjunction on that slice at least 2^r distinct primitive clauses are needed, by testing independent endpoint orientations.

A projected donor positive on v=y or z has every clause containing v. It cannot be positive on both, because no Lambda support contains y,z when a neighbor is omitted at A. If any clause is singleton v, D°=v; this possibility is allowed even if the original donor was nonredundant. For a nontrivial D°, its nonsingleton clauses are anchored at the mate or private neighbor of v. Anchor x is excluded since A omits a raw neighbor, and there is no x in any such support. If all clauses have only the mate as anchor, they coincide: with x unavailable, that anchor's only two allowed support vertices are v and the mate's private neighbor. The resulting D° equals that pure disjunction, a case that CAN occur after projection; it would accept the private-neighbor singleton outside y,z, contradicting cleanliness on the slice. Hence some private-neighbor anchor occurs, starring the private edge of v.

On the clean slice, the set of possible support vertices outside y,z is contained in {9,0,2,4} for y and {10,0,5,8} for z. Cleanliness implies every accepted edge has both endpoints in this support; otherwise an independent singleton suffices. The only candidate off-triangle edges are therefore 09 for y, and 0–10 or 58 for z. For 0–10, a z-type conjunction must use the mate-anchored clause to contain 10; this stars yz. Thus among edges positive at any of these donors, all off-triangle contributions can be credited to distinct already-starred near edges: 09 to y–10; 58 to z–9; 0–10 to yz. Counting all five edges incident to y or z as a starting budget yields at most five residuals for ALL these sources combined. If raw(S) has size one, its corresponding x edge is a further starred near edge disjoint from these credits, reducing this bound to four including all remaining near edges.

With no exceptional donor this proves U≤2c+5 at any q satisfying the structural assumptions. For the optional singleton-negative donor D0°, its off-triangle residual accepts are at most two. If raw(S) is empty, the projected A is false and centreless, so the total is at most 2(c−1)+5+2. If raw(S) has size one the total is at most 2c+4+2. This suffices for q=2,j≥4. For q=2,j=3 with at least one positive-type nonzero donor, select such a Dv°. If Dv is an AND-only ancestor of D0 then D0°≤Dv°, adding no new residual. Otherwise D0's AND-only expansion uses at most two dirty AND gates (the third is Dv). It has at most three distinct primitive inputs even under sharing, so cannot accept two off-triangle residuals under the 2^r orientation bound. This gives 2c+5. If D0° is the only nonzero donor, it covers at most two off-triangle residuals and at most three near residuals since it rejects singleton y and z and does not depend on x. The same 2c+5 bound follows.

This is conditional on the projected source hypotheses. In particular the projection may destroy all neighbor singleton accepts of some donors, and even an original donor with an arbitrary external reader need not be clean on the indicated slice. These hypotheses do not follow just from the count q=2.
