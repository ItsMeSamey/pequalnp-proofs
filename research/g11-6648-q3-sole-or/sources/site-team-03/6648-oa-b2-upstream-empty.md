# Sole direct OR at 3, a second raw-empty direct entrance upstream of its donor

Assume a least size-21 TopForm G11 circuit with j≥3, q=3, exactly two physical dirty-AND-reading ORs, M=x∨D the sole reader of x=3, and Q strictly ancestral to D with empty raw support. W denotes the remaining paid OR and has no dirty AND pin. We prove U≤2c+j+3. The protected-product lemma is used only with originally anchored supports.

Q=A∨B has two distinct dirty AND pins. Indeed an earlier empty OR would be W and must have two recursively earlier raw-empty operands, neither dirty AND by nondirectness nor clean AND by TopForm, impossible without an earlier direct entrance. M is later. The private-donor exclusion forces another reader of D besides M; it must be an AND. The first OR after a sequence of dirty ANDs would directly read one, but cannot be Q upstream or M at its prescribed pins. Thus a centreless clean AND T requires D and c≥1. D implies the graph on both deletions of 9 or 10 by sole-port protection. To treat D-negative pairs forward M to x globally; this preserves these pairs, decreases all outputs, and retains M's original raw support. A and B may share ancestors. For accounting use the original D cone for D-positive edges, and the forwarded circuit for D-negative ones; omit T in the second family.

**W directly reads Q.** Its other pin R is a raw input or positive-raw OR. Any differently indexed empty OR before W must be Q: M is positive and an empty OR is paid. A repeated Q is redundant. At R, after forwarding M if present, any dirty-to-OR route through Q would have to begin at another OR consuming the empty Q or re-enter a direct entrance after an AND; the former can only be W itself, the latter can only be M (already forwarded). Therefore R projects to its original raw support, anchored or singleton: an OR at its root is Lambda or the forwarded M. In the D cone M is absent and the same purity statement holds when W occurs. A and B's own clauses precede Q and W, so are pure and anchored.

At D and each surviving centreless top take three assignment-independent products: replace Q by false; or replace Q by true and conjoin A or B. Original Q is A∨B, so these three terms recover the whole monotone expression. A false OR-only path through W has its original raw support, using R itself at W. Any original Lambda clause keeps its anchor in that mode; M forwards a singleton. True Q paths drop their OR clauses in the true modes. Any AND between a Q occurrence and an OR would require the other direct inlet M, which was forwarded in the D-negative analysis and is absent from the D cone. Every nonzero term is consequently a conjunction of anchored clauses, each implying the respective product root. Every ordinary dirty disjunct must come from W along OR paths; the opposite sides have only raw guards after M forwards. Q cannot reach both sides this way since Q is dirty. All ordinary corrections combine into A·G and B·G for the union G of the opposite guards. Any fixed raw r of R anchors G by the original star spokes; empty correction families are omitted. The raw-only ordinary terms contribute solely original stars. There are at most 3+3(c−1)+2=3c+2 anchored products. Each is clean on both protected deletions, globally so for the D-negative terms. If the target fails, U≥2c+j+4≥9; by singleton_6648_protected_pair_corners.md exclusive allocation gives U≤3c+3. This suffices for c≤j. If c>j, the only case with target below 16 is c=4,j=3. Failure would leave at most one star. An original nonsingleton anchored clause requires at least two distinct starred spokes. Every surviving primitive is thus singleton; a protected product of singletons accepting two distinct graph edges would have only their common vertex as a required literal (or none), contradicting singleton rejection on a deletion containing it. Here U≤3c+2=14.

**W does not directly read Q.** Between Q and any analyzed AND, Q can propagate only multiplicatively: its first direct OR reader would be charged because Q is dirty and empty; it cannot be W by hypothesis. M's pins are D,x and M has been forwarded wherever relevant. Any later dirty-AND-to-OR step is equally excluded. W may inherit M through any number of positive OR gates; after forwarding M it has just its original raw support, and before D it is physically pure if present. All other nonsingleton primitives are originally anchored except for W's own common support S. Each pin of W supplies an anchored raw clause or singleton: dirty AND pins are excluded, direct Q is excluded, and the root OR of a positive pin is Lambda or M. The same holds of every primitive in A or B, possibly with S if W precedes Q. These statements allow W to be pure, inherited from M, or on both sides of a later AND. S is one common binary union of guarded children. All anchored witnesses are original.

Replace multiplicative Q by A or B. For a conjunction requiring Q these two terms distribute the factor Q; if Q is absent keep just the original term. At most two protected conjunctions at D and two globally clean conjunctions per remaining centreless top result. No ordinary correction survives, since Q cannot reach an ordinary OR-only inlet after M forwards. Each conjunction has at most the one common binary exception S, so the all-anchored protected-product surplus theorem is inapplicable. For c=1 the two protected conjunctions each accept at most three residuals over the two deletions by singleton_6648_two_slice_arm_supplement.md. For c≥2 a failure has U≥11 and at most five original stars. Use two separate safe-deletion batches per protected term, giving at most 4+2(c−1)=2c+2 batches. Apply the one-common-exception sparse-star argument in upstream-positive-q3-v1.md: at most one exclusively assigned disjoint double globally and at most two incident doubles from the common full/deleted traces of S. The proof depends only on original starred anchors, one fixed binary S, the same two deletions, and global exclusivity. Hence U≤2c+5, contradicting failure. This completes both placements of W, including W downstream of M or independent of the Q-to-D route.
