# Four products with two shared exceptional supports cannot cover nine residual H8 edges

Let H have G11 vertices 0,1,2,4,5,8,9,10 and the eleven induced edges. Fix a set U of at least nine of those edges. A guarded support is empty, singleton, or a subset of an original anchor's H-neighborhood with every anchor-to-support edge outside U. A near anchor (3,6,7) sees at most one H vertex. Every nonsingleton guarded support, if one exists, must therefore be exactly the same pair B: it uses both unmarked H edges, which must share their anchor. Write B={b1,b2}. Its anchor a is the unique common neighbor of b1,b2 by C4-freeness, and both spokes to a are unmarked.

Take the two common exceptional supports E,F of the shared-library relaxation. Each is a union of at most two guarded terminals, except that F may instead be E union one guarded terminal. Products are conjunctions of positive disjunctions on these supports or on guarded supports. Each product is clean on H and owns a subset of the marked pairs it accepts. Empty clauses kill a product, and an empty conjunction cannot be clean. Repetitions and supersets of another conjunct can be discarded without changing the function. An empty or singleton exceptional support is therefore an ordinary constant or singleton when used; no disjointness or private use of any physical wire is needed. Ownership may be made disjoint by assigning every covered edge once.

**Incomparable E,F.** Neither can be constructed as the union of the other with a terminal. Both have at most two guarded terminals. Consider an owned degree-two-or-higher pivot h at any product. Some clause must miss h by singleton rejection. A guarded clause missing h would contain at least two owned mates of h. It would then be B, and h would equal its anchor, contradicting the marked status of those spokes. Thus all missing clauses are exceptional. The intersection C of their supports is E, F, or E∩F, according to which of these actually occur and miss h. Every owned mate is in C, and every vertex in C is adjacent to h: together with h it hits every clause.

If C is E or F, a B terminal inside that support contributes no marked mate, since h then sees both b1,b2 and hence is a. The one other terminal can contribute at most one further vertex. Thus two owned mates require that the support consist of exactly those two mates, from singleton terminals. If C=E∩F and at least one support uses only singleton terminals, C has size at most two. If both use B, incomparability implies their only common part is B (each is B plus at most one vertex), and h has no marked mates in it. Consequently every pivot has exactly two owned mates. Each of the three possible types E,F,E∩F fixes both mates and their unique common neighbor. Each type can occur at most once across the disjoint ownership allocation: its occurrence uses the same two marked spokes. This also bounds multiple pivots in a single product.

A product with any effective singleton clause has matching number at most one, since every accepted pair hits that singleton. A nonzero clean product without singleton clauses must have all three distinct nonsingleton supports B,E,F as conjuncts. One clause would accept a singleton. Two clauses would either intersect and accept a singleton or be disjoint and create a K(2,2) in H, contradicting C4-freeness. There is thus at most one distinct singleton-free product function, W=B∧E∧F (here each support denotes its disjunction). If two or more groups used W, pool all of their owned edges into one W group; all remain accepted by that same function, and there are still at most four groups. W has matching number at most two because B meets every accepted pair. If B does not exist or a candidate support is singleton or redundant, only the singleton-product case can occur.

In each resulting group delete one incident edge at each remaining degree-two pivot until a matching remains. This costs at most one per original pivot, hence at most three in all. At most three singleton groups plus W leave at most five matching edges; four singleton groups leave at most four. The total is at most eight.

**Comparable E,F.** A product can retain at most one effective exceptional clause, the smaller if both are present. A singleton-free clean nonzero product is again impossible: at most B and one other nonsingleton clause remain. Every group thus has a singleton clause, and its owned graph is a star. If two or more mates occur at its center h, the only effective exceptional support C must miss h, all guarded clauses contain h, and h sees all of C. Each nonsingleton B terminal of C contributes no marked mate; a singleton terminal contributes at most one. A given effective support C can service only one global pivot h (two members fix their common neighbor). Even if several groups have that same pivot, their disjoint mate allocations together use at most the number of singleton terminals of C. If both global supports are guarded binary joins each has at most two terminal slots. If F=E union a guarded support it has at most three and E at most two. Thus globally the groups with at least two owned edges can contribute at most (2−1)+(3−1)=3 edges beyond one edge per group. This includes equal or unused supports by dropping unused types. Four groups cover at most seven.

This proves F(2,4)≤8 for the shared H8 relaxation uniformly over the original mark set and both permissible library shapes. The argument uses no numerical enumeration or leastness hypothesis.
