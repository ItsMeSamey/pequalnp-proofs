# q=3 with S and the sole dirty-reading gate M both raw-empty

Same hypotheses and original H/near accounting as premises_6648_shared_projection_scope.md, but S and M are raw-empty, c≥1. Write M=J1∨J2. No earlier raw-empty OR can exist because a first such OR would directly read an AND. The two distinct, incomparable dirty ANDs J1,J2 are therefore products of pure clauses. Nor can a later raw-empty OR appear: a first one would need two distinct raw-empty pins which are not ANDs, but only M is available. Thus the raw-empty wires below a clean top are the dirty ANDs and M. Let k∈{0,1,2} count the distinct ORs directly reading M below a first clean top. Each has nonempty raw other arm and is paid. If two exist their raw arms are pure (a raw-positive mixed arm already dominating M would make the OR absorbable). A subsequent OR not directly reading M may inherit mixed M through those positive regions.

Each centreless top reads a raw-empty pin directly, and any fixed pin has at most one direct clean AND reader by factoring two top outputs with the same input. Therefore c≤j+1. For k≥1, if S≠M then S is a dirty AND distinct from J1,J2. Any pre-M donor equal to S would be positive on both singletons y,z. All primitive clauses of that donor would contain both. No lambda support can contain them (their only common neighbor x has no ordinary star), and only one pure paid OR at most remains besides M and its k direct exits. Raw singleton pins cannot meet both. Hence such a donor would equal the single remaining exceptional wire, contrary to leastness. In particular when j=3 and S≠M the only possible dirty wires are J1,J2,S.

On H the root A never contributes. In the k=0 case M cannot be used in any OR before a clean top. Cofactor M=true at all its direct AND pins and restore M once around the affected top union. No OR interrupts the AND-only paths, so two direct AND readers would save a gate; each subsequent dirty AND similarly has unique reader. Thus at most one non-A root requires M and produces two batches rather than one. There is no ordinary correction; at most c H batches with the same two possible projected exceptions. Combining the frozen H-batch bounds (1,2,3,4,5 groups give maxima ≤3,5,6,8,9 respectively) with ≤4 near residuals proves the target. At least one near star follows from common h=yz or one of four individual ORs being lambda; the common OR case contradicts q≤3 since h adds a fourth paid gate when A is centreless.

For k≥1 erase M, or set M=true, as appropriate. Every other centreless root T has at most three clean product terms: its pure base, J1 times its positive-mode guards, and J2 times those same guards. A top directly reading M has only the last two, and a top directly reading Ji has only one (Ji itself entails M). Each ordinary affected top has only one mixed side, for otherwise it would pass the dirty M on all its positives. Aggregate ordinary corrections into Ji times an opposite raw guard. For each exit separately the union of its opposite guard supports is anchored by any single raw leaf of that exit's partner, since these incidences are original cross-pair stars. Thus ordinary corrections require at most 2k fully separated terms. Alternatively use two terms total, with the joint guard the union of the (at most k) anchored guards. Extra projected paid roots beyond the k exits number at most 2−k. The projected supports of direct exits collapse to existing operands. Consequently when k=1 or 2 the two ordinary terms and centreless terms jointly need at most one globally shared binary exceptional clause, including a possibly unanchored joint guard at k=2. The baseline is n=3(c−1)+2 H batches in this version. If S=M one can globally assign its two donors, each clean on H (since S clean there), then every remaining root needs only one projected batch and ordinary tops contribute only original stars. This gives c+1 H batches, with ≤1 exception, and is sufficient (c=1,2,3,4 admit ≤4,5,7,9 H edges by the existing numerical bounds; add ≤4 near).

Only one near star has special consequences. If it is yz, no individual lambda reader is possible: it would need its other raw-positive pin to provide a leaf distinct from its own raw label, producing another near star. With common AND h the two individual ORs must then both be among the two paid raw-positive ORs. With four individual ORs at least two would be lambda, impossible. If instead the only near star is a private spoke, say y–10, the common AND case is impossible and both z readers must be paid. The symmetric statement applies for z–9. At k=1 the paid gate not used as a direct M exit is therefore an individual raw OR; it collapses on H to its other existing input. Thus there is no effective H exception in the one-near-star case. At k=2, two direct paid z readers could only be duplicate copies of z∨M, impossible. In the common AND case both paid readers would instead be y∨M,z∨M. The y10 versus z10 and z9 versus y9 separation forces M false on both singleton y,z. On those two inputs S would agree with its M=false projection. This projected pure conjunction cannot be positive at both singletons: before taking H restrictions every projected primitive is raw or lambda-anchored, with no anchor seeing both y,z (and a zero projected term cannot accept either). Consequently k=2 always gives at least two near stars.

Any H double of a fully anchored clean batch costs at least four internal H stars; a nonhomogeneous double costs at least seven. Under exclusive allocation at most one batch can double at U≥8 (the three seven-star footprint rows exclude each other and a separately assigned homogeneous double). At k=2 use 3(c−1)+4 fully anchored groups and ≤3 near. At c=1 this gives ≤8. At c=2 or c=3 with j≥4 a putative failure has U≥11, so no double (four internal plus two near stars already give U≤10). The bounds are then ≤7+3 for c=2 and ≤10+3 for c=3. The same no-double bound already suffices for c=2,j=3. For c=3,j=3,S≠M, each non-A top must directly read J1,J2 or M since the remaining dirty S already directly feeds A; these can be assigned distinctly, so at most three non-A batches (one Ji, one M or two Ji). Even the four ordinary terms suffice. For c≥4 use U≤14, which meets 2c+j+3.

At k=1 with only one near star, use the n fully anchored terms proved above. For c=1,2 at most one double plus four near edges suffices. For c=3, a failure at j≥3 requires U≥13, so no double is possible, yielding 8+4. For c=4,j=3,S≠M all three non-A roots must be assigned distinct J1,J2,M. They need four terms total and ordinary tops two; the budget suffices. For c≥4,j≥4 use U≤15. The remaining c≥5 case with j=3 is impossible by c≤j+1.

At k=1 with two or more near stars, use ≤3 near residuals and n H batches sharing one exception. c=1 costs ≤4+3. For j=3,S≠M the non-A owners use distinct J1,J2 or M, with at most c total non-A batches. Then c=2 costs ≤7+3 and c=3 costs ≤9+3 by the frozen one-exception H bounds (at 4 and 5 groups respectively). c≥4 needs only U≤14. At c=2,j≥4 or c=3,j≥4, any violation implies U≥12 and hence at most two internal H stars, since at least two near stars exist. Every guarded projected H support is then empty, singleton, or the very same two-element B. Indeed a near vertex has at most one H neighbor and two distinct H neighbors at a common anchor already exhaust both internal star edges. The possible exception E is the union of two guarded H supports. Each clean product with no singleton clause can only use B and E; two nonsingleton supports cannot have clean product, since overlap activates a singleton and disjoint supports complete to one another would produce a four-cycle. A product with two or more distinct singleton clauses can own at most one edge. With a singleton clause b, any double must be two spokes ba,ba' and all other clauses except E must contain b: omitting b in a guarded clause would star both mates. E omits b, so cleanliness makes b adjacent to every vertex of E. Each child carrying a residual mate must therefore be a singleton, or its anchor coincides with b. Thus E is precisely {a,a'}, fixing the center uniquely by four-cycle-freeness. Across any exclusive allocation there can be at most one double. Consequently H contributes ≤n+1, i.e. ≤6 at c=2 and ≤9 at c=3, which suffices. Larger c uses the same U≤14 bound. This finishes both-empty S,M conditional on the already requested H-batch verification, without any additional numerical run.
