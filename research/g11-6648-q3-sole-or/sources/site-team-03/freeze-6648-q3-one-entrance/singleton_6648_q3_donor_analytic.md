# q=3, sole OR M=3∨D as unique dirty-AND-to-OR entrance

Under the hypotheses of singleton_6648_one_mixer_shared_smt.md, D has an external AND reader and c≥1. On the original residuals, U is contained in the donor-positive edges plus at most c−1 projected clean centreless products T_i, by shrinking M to raw x=3. The only exceptional raw OR supports are the two paid gates other than M. All restrictions below are on original stars.

**Donor bound.** D is a pure product. If any primitive is a singleton, all its positive pairs meet one vertex, giving ≤4 by the original graph degree. If a primitive is originally anchored, write its raw support as C⊆N(a); all a–v for v∈C are original stars. Every D-positive residual meets C, excludes a (its only possible edges meeting C are already starred), and excludes x by x-independence and neighbor-singleton negativity. For each a the upper bound counts all edges meeting N(a) with endpoints outside {a,x}. In vertex order 0 through 10 these counts are bounded above respectively by 6,4,5,3,6,6,4,4,6,6,5, directly from the posted edge list (vertices 6 and 7 may be overcounted). Thus one free primitive suffices for ≤6.

Otherwise all D primitives are among the two paid raw supports. Singleton negativity forces two distinct disjoint supports, since a common variable or a single surviving support would activate a singleton. Hence its positive pairs form a complete bipartite subgraph inside G plus 9–10: any independent positive pair must contain both 9,10. This graph has maximum degree five and any two vertices share at most two neighbors, since G is C4-free and a single added edge increases a pair's common-neighbor count by at most one. A star contributes ≤5 and a biclique with both sides of size≥2 has at most four edges. In particular |U∩Gamma(D)|≤6.

**Projected top bound.** Each T_i is a pure product of primitive clauses, aside from the two raw support exceptions considered as binary ORs. Replace the earlier exceptional OR by either of its children throughout T_i. These two projected functions have union equal to T_i: on any given input one of the children attains its disjunction. They are both clean by shrinking. Each remaining uncharged raw support is a subset of its original anchored support. The only remaining exceptional gate's two children are anchored or singleton too, including a child formerly equal to the first exception. The one-exception clean-product lemma in singleton_6648_q2_sole_or_all_c_cases.md bounds each branch by two original residuals, so a single T_i accepts at most four.

Therefore |U|≤min(16,6+4(c−1)). This yields the official target 2c+j+3 for all c=1,2, all j≥5, and all c≥5. The only still-open (j,c) in this analytic reduction are (3,3),(4,3),(3,4),(4,4). No solver or scan was used.

**Closing the four small-star signatures.** Write S for the ORIGINAL ordinary star edge set, s=|S| and t for the number of ordinary tops. Each raw-positive dirty OR wire other than M either reads only raw/OR inputs or would constitute another dirty-AND-to-OR entrance. All raw x paths go through M. Consequently each raw OR wire with support R has the unique function OR of sigma(v) for v in R, where sigma(x)=M and sigma(v)=v otherwise. Here raw x itself occurs solely at M and is never an ordinary inlet. This is a topological induction starting at raw v≠x and M. Clean AND inlets below dirty ORs cannot occur by TopForm and liveness, as a dirty OR must ultimately precede an AND.

Each ordinary top therefore computes the union (over its actual raw cross edges uv) of sigma(u) AND sigma(v). If its cross edges were all supplied by other ordinary tops, the top could be removed from the output aggregation. Leastness therefore forces a private cross edge per top and t≤s. Distinct lambda gates have distinct raw supports, since equal functions permit deletion by redirecting all uses of the later gate to the earlier gate. A singleton lambda is redundant relative to the corresponding raw variable (or M for the support {x}). Thus lambda gates inject into distinct subsets of size at least two in original star neighborhoods N_S(a). For s≤1 this gives |lambda|=0; for s≤2 at most one; for s≤3 at most four (a three-star gives three pairs and the triple; otherwise at most two adjacent-edge pairs).

All clean ORs aggregate the t+c clean tops, hence replace them by a binary tree with t+c−1 gates. Leastness and liveness give actual gate count at most j+q+|lambda|+2(t+c)−1. It is at least 20, because x=false deletes M and yields a circuit for the exact 19-card. For (j,c)=(3,4),(4,4), strict failure forces s≤1 and the upper bound is at most 16. For (4,3) it forces s≤2 and the bound is at most 17. For (3,3) it forces s≤3, and with t≤2 the bound is at most 19. If t=3 then each top has exactly one distinct private edge, and each has no other edge since there are just three stars. Two adjacent such edges ab,ac would let us replace their two gate outputs by one grouped term sigma(a) AND (sigma(b) OR sigma(c)), regrouping the final aggregation using one fewer OR. The three available signals exist: every sigma(v) is either a raw vertex or M. This saves a gate even if both tops had multiple clean OR consumers, as the entire aggregation is rebuilt. Therefore the edges are a matching, lambda is empty, and the gate count is at most 17. All possibilities contradict the floor of 20.

This closes q=3 for all j,c in the stated sole physical M=x OR D entrance case, without a computation.
