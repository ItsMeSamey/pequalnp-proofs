# Relative placement of the second direct entrance

Assume q=3, j≥3, x=3 has sole reader M=x∨E, E is a raw-empty OR, and E and Q are the two direct dirty-AND-reading ORs. Write U for the number of residual edges. The serial case Q directly entering E is treated separately by w06. Otherwise E=A∨B has two dirty AND pins.

If Q is not ancestral to E, the arms A and B are pure anchored conjunctions. By the two safe deletions each gives two batches and at most three residuals (matching after deleting 9, then at most one further edge incident to 9 after deleting 10). Project E to false and M to x for the remaining pairs. The raw support of every retained pre-top OR is unchanged. Sole-port private exclusion gives E another reader; trace an escape through ANDs to a first OR or clean AND.

If it reaches Q before a clean AND, its pin there implies E, including direct E→Q. This pin is raw-empty. Forward Q to its other pin on projection. This preserves Q's original raw support. A raw-empty surviving dirty AND at this forwarded pin cannot inject into a subsequent pre-top OR: such a reader of the original empty Q would be charged, while E precedes Q and M's two pins are fixed. A raw-positive surviving pin is a pure raw clause after M forwards to x. Its support is singleton or originally anchored: its root cannot be Q or E, and M forwards a singleton. Consequently surviving centreless tops supply at most two residuals each and ordinary tops only original stars. U≤6+2c.

Otherwise the escape reaches a clean AND T, centreless and implying E. In particular c≥1. In the E projection a single positive Q gives at most two pure batches for each surviving centreless top, and one aggregated ordinary correction (single positive mixer lemma). An empty Q splits multiplicatively into its two conjunctions and gives no ordinary correction: a dirty OR-only escape from the raw-empty Q would require a further charge. Thus the number of batches is at most 4+2(c−1)+1. A failure implies U≥2c+j+4≥9; the original seven-star double-footprint bound permits at most two exclusive doubles. Hence U≤2c+5, contradiction. When Q and E are incomparable this clean-escape argument applies unchanged.

If Q strictly feeds E through an AND arm, acyclicity gives the clean escape T. A positive Q is one Boolean donor plus raw clause. Expanding each E arm with that donor false/true gives at most two anchored products per deletion and arm, retaining the donor product in its true term. Raw clauses originally containing Q's raw support keep their anchors on the false branch, including Q's own raw partner (pure and originally anchored or singleton). On the true branch clauses reached through Q are true and drop. For empty Q either it does not occur in an arm, or it occurs multiplicatively: an intervening OR reading empty Q or a dirty AND would be an additional charge. Split that factor into its two anchored conjunctive donors. Thus there are ≤8 E-positive batches. After projection the previous one-Q centreless and ordinary estimates still apply. At failure U≥9 this gives U≤2c+9 for positive Q or 2c+8 for empty Q. At U≥10 each improves by one because only the homogeneous double can occur. These bounds close positive Q for j≥5 and empty Q for j≥4 (at these thresholds failure implies U≥10). They leave the earlier nonspecial positive-Q j=3,4 and empty-Q j=3 regimes unresolved by this count.

The remaining upstream empty-Q j=3 case also closes. Q=D1∨D2 uses two distinct dirty ANDs. One E pin is strictly downstream of Q. The other must coincide with Di, because only three dirty wires exist and the pins are distinct by leastness. The downstream arm has form QK by AND-only propagation. Thus E=Di∨Dother K gives just two anchored products, ≤4 positive batches and ≤2(c−1) negative batches. At failure U≤2c+4. This exhausts empty Q. For positive Q with its dirty donor D itself an E pin the same expansion absorbs the D-containing term of the other arm, leaving D and its D=false expansion (where only Q's injection is cut and independent uses of D may be retained).

## Closure for upstream positive Q by joint protected-product counting

Use singleton's strengthened footprint lemma: at U≥9 an exclusively allocated family of original anchored products, each clean on both G−9 and G−10, covers at most the number of products plus one. Globally clean products are included.

Write Q=D∨R with pure anchored donor conjunction D and pure raw partner R (singleton or anchored). For either arm A, define A0 by replacing Q with R throughout that arm; define A1 by replacing Q with true. Pointwise A=A0∨(D·A1), since A is monotone in the shared Q wire, and D and R are independent of this wire. Here independence means topological precedence; the signals themselves can be correlated. Both summands have fixed original-graph supports. In A0 all Q-containing raw clauses keep their original raw supports; in A1 all OR-only clauses containing Q become true and drop. Repeated or shared factors do not alter the identities. Direct separate uses of D are retained in both cofactors. Both products imply E and are therefore clean on each of the two deletions. Applying this to A and B supplies at most FOUR products before any deletion. There is no per-deletion choice or matching between different cofactor terms.

On E-negative edges project E to false and M to x. The clean escape top T is inactive. The remaining single positive Q gives at most two globally clean anchored products per surviving centreless top and one globally clean aggregated ordinary correction. These too satisfy both deletion conditions. Allocate edges exclusively across the entire family. Failure would require U≥2c+j+4≥9, since c≥1 and j≥3. The protected-product lemma gives U≤4+2(c−1)+1+1=2c+4, excluding failure. In particular the j=3,c=1,U=9 corner is impossible. This supersedes all open upstream-positive thresholds above and completes the relative-placement analysis conditional on the joint protected-product lemma.
