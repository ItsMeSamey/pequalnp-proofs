# Sole OR M=x∨D, two entrances, secondary outside D

Assume the issued least size-21 TopForm hypotheses, j≥3, q=3, and sole x=3 reader M=x∨D with dirty AND D. The only other distinct physical dirty-AND-reading OR is Q, outside D's strict cone. Write W for the third paid OR. All residuals and all anchors are original. Then U≤2c+j+3.

**Donor frontier and positive allocation.** Before D there is no direct entrance on any path into D. A first OR coming from an AND would directly read an AND; a clean AND cannot precede D by TopForm, and a dirty AND would give a forbidden entrance. Hence D is an AND of pure disjunctions. The only possible primitive that is nonsingleton and lacks an original Lambda anchor is the output of W itself. If present, W is physically pure with both its operands singleton or anchored. A W buried inside a Lambda primitive creates no extra exception. An inherited W cannot precede D.

The sole-port protection lemma makes D clean on deletion of either 9 or 10. The one-exception lemma of singleton_6648_q2_sole_or_all_c_cases.md allows at most two residuals on each such deletion; if two share an endpoint, the exceptional primitive's entire surviving support is exactly their two mates. Thus D accepts at most three original residuals in total. Indeed four would require exactly two 9-edges and two 10-edges since 9–10 is absent. On both deletions the two mates are vertices outside {9,10}. The same W must have both pairs as its entire trace outside {9,10}. This gives two common neighbors of 9 and 10, impossible. Without an exceptional primitive the restrictions are induced matchings, already excluding four. Pay D-positive residuals first, then set D=false everywhere and M=x. All remaining evaluations on graph edges are exact and all projected physical tops remain clean by monotonicity.

**Secondary with positive raw support.** Write Q=B∨R with B a dirty AND. There is no pretop raw-empty OR: an earliest one would have dirty AND inlets, while both direct entrances are raw-positive. After projection M=x, any OR primitive not inheriting Q is pure. If W does not OR-reach Q backwards (equivalently Q is not in W's OR-only gate closure), W is pure after projection; other pure primitive supports are anchored or singleton, allowing the alias M=x. If W does inherit Q, every mixed OR-only descendant computes B'∨its raw part even if both inputs inherit Q. This statement describes OR-only paths starting at Q; any route crossing an AND and then an OR would need another direct entrance, impossible downstream of Q (M has fixed pins x,D). Here B' is the projected donor, which itself has no Q ancestor.

In either case fixing Q to its projected opposite pin R' gives pure raw supports for all positive primitives. Their masks are their original raw supports. At Q itself use the pin R' (possibly the exceptional W if earlier); M aliases to singleton x. If W is exceptional in this projection its immediate children have singleton/anchored supports: at an immediate Q use R', which cannot itself descend from W, and at M use x. In the dirty-side mode fix Q=true and multiply by B'. OR-only descendants become true, and other primitives remain pure with at most the same one exception. A projected centreless top thus contributes at most two pure conjunctions, each clean and with at most one exceptional binary raw clause. If its AND region requires B', only the dirty-side term is needed. Roots killed by D are omitted. Every ordinary all-raw term is starred. All remaining ordinary corrections pool as B' times the union G of opposite original raw guards at affected ordinary tops: Q cannot OR-reach both sides of an original clean top by its independent dirty witness. These opposite sides have no Q in their OR-only closure and are pure after D=false. Every r in the original nonempty raw support of R anchors the entire union by original stars. Thus the pooled correction also costs at most two residuals by the one-exception lemma. If B implies D the dirty side vanishes everywhere: there are ≤2c+3 edges.

Otherwise follow the external escape of D, supplied by the sole-port private-donor lemma. A first OR on an external AND-only route could only be Q at its B pin, implying B≤D. Therefore there is a centreless top requiring D multiplicatively; omit it. Every surviving centreless top has a literal dirty AND pin since there are no raw-empty ORs. Distinct tops cannot share such a pin: factor their common operand in the clean output union and save one gate. Roots directly requiring B have only one mode; none can directly require D. Consequently at most m≤min(c−1,j−2) tops need two modes. Including the ordinary correction yields U≤3+2+2(c−1)+2m. This is enough for 2m≤j. Otherwise j≥5 and c≥floor(j/2)+2 give 2c+j+3≥16.

**Secondary with empty raw support.** Its pins H1,H2 are dirty ANDs: an earlier empty OR into Q would require an earlier direct empty entrance or W inheriting one. M is positive. If Q never feeds W along OR gates, Q has only an AND continuation to its first clean destinations. Indeed any first later OR reading a dirty AND would be another direct entrance; an OR directly reading Q would be dirty, with an empty pin, hence paid W. The original last-empty factoring lemma in singleton_6648_q2_sole_or_all_c_cases.md forces one centreless destination. W is pure after D=false,M=x as it has no Q inheritance. At the one destination split Q into its two arms, elsewhere use one term, all with at most the one pure exception W. Ordinary tops yield stars, so U≤3+2(c+1).

Otherwise the first OR inheriting Q must be W and it reads Q directly. Its other operand R has positive raw support: the only earlier empty OR is Q and Q∨Q is an identity. After D=false and M=x, the arms H1',H2' and all nonmixed AND-region factors are anchored products, and R' is anchored/singleton. Every OR-only mixed boundary now computes Q'∨raw; every AND-only Q factor is just Q'. A centreless root therefore contributes ≤3 anchored products, one fully raw and at most two dirty-side terms (one for each H_i'). A root requiring Q' or either arm multiplicatively needs only the two dirty-side terms. Ordinary dirty-side terms pool into H1' times G and H2' times G, with G anchored by any r∈R as above; ordinary raw terms are stars. If one arm vanishes, all centreless roots cost ≤2 terms and the ordinary correction ≤1. Otherwise D,H1,H2 are distinct (repeated arm would make Q an identity) and each three-term centreless top has a distinct literal dirty AND pin outside these three, since a raw-empty OR pin can only be Q, forcing its dirty-side mode. Thus m≤j−3 tops can require three terms.

Here W is downstream of Q; D is a wholly anchored batch protected on both deletions. For U≥9 the global protected-pair lemma singleton_6648_protected_pair_corners.md permits at most one exclusive double over D and all globally clean anchored products. Thus U≤1+2c+m+2+1≤2c+j+3. With one arm vanishing the analogous bound is 1+2c+1+1. For U≤8 all c≥1 cases already follow, as do c=0,j≥5. With c=0 the external escape of D must reach Q at a dirty AND pin by an AND-only path, so one arm vanishes. Then the only possible nonstar payment is ≤3 from D and ≤2 from the remaining ordinary correction, which suffices.

This is a source proof using the existing protected-pair, private-donor, last-empty and one-exception lemmas. The secondary inside D is a separate branch.
