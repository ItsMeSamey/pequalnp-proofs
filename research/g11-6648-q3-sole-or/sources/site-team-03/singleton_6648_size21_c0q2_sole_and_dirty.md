# Size 21, c=0, q=2: dirty sole AND reader of vertex 3

Let g=x AND S, x=3, be dirty and the sole raw reader of x. The 19-gate x=false lower bound for a 21-gate circuit gives a unique reader M=g OR D (6648-size21-sole-and.md): setting x=false collapses g and each reader; an AND reader would propagate false and consume a third gate or give an output divisible by x. Earlier D is independent of x since all downstream x paths pass through g and M, and duplicate pins violate leastness.

When D is true on an x-free input, toggling x cannot affect the output, because M remains true. Any independent positive input of D, after stripping x, therefore avoids 6,7 and dominates both 6 and 7: otherwise adjoin an undominated neighbor z of x to keep an independent positive input, then adjoin x and create an edge without changing the output. From N(6)={3,7,10} and N(7)={3,6,9}, every such input contains 9 and 10. Thus D is singleton-negative and clean on each of the two restrictions setting 9 or 10 false.

D has empty raw support by singleton negativity, and g is an AND. Hence M has empty raw support and is charged; it is dirty on any independent positive for g. Follow a live path from M along ANDs to the first OR. A clean AND would be centreless, since its path input is M or another AND; a dirty AND cannot end at the graph output. M itself also cannot be the output. Consequently a first OR exists, with dirty raw-empty input Z and Z<=M by the multiplicative path, including Z=M. This OR is dirty, cannot be Lambda, and must be the unique other charged OR Q. If Q also has empty raw support, the same path argument forces a third charged OR downstream. In particular a second dirty AND pin at Q is impossible even if proposed as an output gate: Q remains dirty on a witness for its first input regardless of its other input.

The other pin of Q is a positive raw disjunction R. Its necessarily nonempty OR-only region cannot receive M directly: such an OR would be dirty and charged on account of M's raw-empty pin, distinct from M and the later Q. A dirty AND cannot enter that region by any other OR, for the same charge reason. A clean AND ancestry here would violate TopForm since dirty Q has an AND descendant (a path of ORs alone cannot reach the clean output). Thus Q=Z OR R. This proof takes one live path to find Q and requires no uniqueness assumption on the readers of M, Z, or the intermediate nodes.

On any graph pair with g=D=false, M contributes false and Q contributes exactly R. Thus both charged ORs evaluate at their raw projections. All top operands evaluate at their raw OR-supports on this pair: clean AND ancestry is forbidden and every OR directly fed by a dirty AND is charged. All clean tops are ordinary; the two raw supports are disjoint by singleton rejection. An active top therefore witnesses a star edge. The output has at least one active top on every edge by the clean OR aggregation argument. Hence every residual edge is in Gamma(g) or Gamma(D).

D is itself a dirty AND. A primary input contradicts singleton negativity. An AND in its ancestry cannot be clean by TopForm, because M has an AND descendant. If D were an OR, its empty raw support forces its OR-only input paths to pass through dirty ANDs, so it inherits a dirty witness, and both pins have empty raw support: D would be a third charged OR before M,Q. Every primitive factor of D is therefore pure as well, since all charged ORs lie downstream, and every OR directly reading a dirty AND would be charged. Every nonsingleton pure factor has an ordinary-star anchor by Lambda. For d distinct dirty AND ancestors including D, binary connectivity bounds distinct primitive slots by d+1. The restricted induced-matching proof in singleton_6648_size21_c0q2_sole_or.md now applies to both 9=false and 10=false, with original residual status and original star anchors. Each accounts for at most floor(log2(d+1)) residuals of D, and every edge survives one deletion since 9,10 are nonadjacent. Thus D accounts for at most d+1 residuals, using 2k<=2^k for k>=1. Gate g can accept only the two graph pairs 36 and 37, and g is outside D's ancestor cone, so d+1<=j. This proves U<=2+d+1<=j+2, exactly sufficient at c=0,q=2 after applying the issued balance. No private-donor lemma or numerical search is needed in this branch.
