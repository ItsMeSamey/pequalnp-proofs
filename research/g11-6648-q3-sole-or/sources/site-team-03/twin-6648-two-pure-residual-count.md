# Two pure exceptional ORs: a residual-only bound

Suppose a least exact-binary TopForm AO realization of a nonempty simple C4-free edge function has at most two exceptional pure OR gates (orN outside Lambda), and no dirty AND feeds an OR. The latter also follows from purity of the exceptional gates, since an OR with a dirty AND pin is itself dirty and lacks raw reach on that pin. Then U=|Ustar|≤j+2c. In particular the required bound U≤j+2c+2 holds on G11. This argument charges edge counts only and does not require globally private use of primitive OR clauses.

Every ordinary top has pure sides and accepts only star edges. All residuals must activate a centreless top. At each such top the intervening AND cone is a conjunction of raw OR supports; TopForm rules out clean AND ancestors. If it has d dirty ancestors then d≥1 (otherwise both sides have nonempty raw support) and its connected binary DAG has at most d+2 primitive leaf slots. Delete repeated and inclusion-redundant clauses, writing m≤d+2 for the effective count. A clause is either a singleton, anchored by star edges from a common ordinary-top center to every support vertex, or one of the exceptional gates. The dirty AND regions of different roots are disjoint by the original least-circuit factor extraction in 4186-zero-charge-all-j.txt. Pure OR gates may be shared arbitrarily between roots.

Fix one top, and let R consist of the distinct residual edges activating it. Write k for its maximum matching size. Empty R costs zero. Every effective clause hits every edge of R. For a pair of disjoint matching edges ab and cd with an ambient cross edge ac, a nonsingleton anchored clause cannot contain a,d nor b,c: its anchor would equal respectively c or a by two common neighbors, making cd or ab a star edge. Hence every anchored clause meets the four endpoints exactly in ac or bd. A singleton cannot hit both matching edges. There is at least one anchored clause in this situation: one pure exceptional clause alone accepts a singleton, and a clean conjunction consisting of exactly two unanchored exceptions would have disjoint supports, both of size at least two, giving a four-cycle. Pure exceptional ORs with singleton support would duplicate a raw input and violate leastness.

The opposite cross bd would close a four-cycle; another cross ad or bc would impose an incompatible two-set restriction on the anchored clause. Thus ad and bc are nonedges. Both hit each anchored clause, so two exceptional clauses are necessary, one rejecting each assignment. Restricted to the four endpoints their supports must then be ad and bc in some order. With at most one effective unanchored exception there are no conflicts between disjoint residual matching edges. With both present, mark the endpoints on each conflicted edge by the unique exceptional clause containing each. Every anchored clause picks opposite labels along a conflict. A path of length two in the conflict graph of matching edges would force the same anchored label on the outer edges. Selecting opposite-labelled endpoints of the two outer edges would hit every anchored clause and both exceptional clauses, in either orientation. These two resulting graph edges form a four-cycle with the outer edges. Hence conflicts are pairwise disjoint. The independent endpoint transversals number 3^p 2^(k−2p) for p conflicts. Each must miss an effective clause and each clause misses at most one. Thus m≥2^k with at most one exception and m≥2k−1 with two (minimize at p=floor(k/2), check k=1,2,3,4 and multiply by three when adding two).

If a residual vertex h has degree at least two in R, singleton rejection supplies a clause omitting h, which must include all its residual neighbors. This cannot be anchored: its anchor would be h and the spokes would be starred. With no exception R is a matching and |R|≤m.

With one effective exception W every high-degree pivot h is missing from W and belongs to all other clauses. Therefore h is adjacent to every vertex in W. There can be at most one such pivot, since W contains at least two vertices. Truncate W's OR cone at singleton or anchored supports. At most two exceptional binary ORs are available even when one is a proper ancestor of W; consequently there are at most three distinct support terminals. Each terminal contains at most one residual neighbor of h, by the anchor argument. Thus degree(h)≤3. The graph R is a matching or one star plus independent edges, so |R|≤k+2. At k≥2 this is ≤2^k≤m; at k=1 it is ≤3≤d+2.

With two effective unanchored exceptions E,F their supports are incomparable, so neither is an ancestor of the other and each has only singleton or anchored children. An exception omitting h therefore carries at most two residual neighbors of h: degree(h)≤2. If h belongs to F but is missing from E, all other clauses contain h, so h neighbors all vertices of E. A nonsingleton anchored child of E would then have h as its anchor and contribute no residual neighbor. The two residual neighbors require both children singleton, hence E={u,v}. Every residual meets E, and each of u,v has degree at most two. Four residuals would force u,v both to have degree two; since they belong to E they must be missing from F. Applying the same argument gives |F|=2, disjoint from E. Four edges between these two pairs make a four-cycle. Thus |R|≤3≤d+2. The reversed case is symmetric.

Otherwise every degree-two pivot lies outside E∪F. Its neighbors belong to E∩F, and it neighbors all vertices of this intersection since all anchored clauses contain the pivot. Two such pivots would have two common neighbors. Thus at most one exists and |R|≤k+1. This is ≤2k−1≤m at k≥2 and ≤2 at k=1. These cases prove |R|≤d+2 at every centreless root. Sum over roots, counting overlapping graph pairs only in the upper estimate and charging no physical dirty AND twice, to obtain U≤j+2c.
