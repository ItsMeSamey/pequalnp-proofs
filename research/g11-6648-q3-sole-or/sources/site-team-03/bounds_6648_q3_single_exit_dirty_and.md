# q=3, one OR gate receiving dirty AND pins, sole dirty x-AND

Use x=3, y=6, z=7. Suppose the only receiving OR is M, permitting two dirty AND pins at M. The size-21 and x=false card-19 results give g=x∧S as x's sole dirty reader, with unique reader M=g∨D. The donor D rejects every singleton and every independent positive contains 9 and 10 (singleton_6648_size21_c0q2_sole_and_dirty.md). Thus D is clean on each of the two deletions of 9 or 10. Its pre-M ancestry is pure ORs and conjunctive ANDs. In particular D is itself an AND: raw input or pure OR would accept a singleton. No original star contains x. S is a pure-clause conjunction, a pure OR being the one-clause case. It is true on both singleton y and singleton z, so each primitive contains both. At least one such pure OR W exists and is charged: only x could anchor y and z, but x never stars an edge. M is raw-empty and charged.

If an additional genuinely mixed OR with some raw reach occurs, its first ancestor restoring positive raw reach from M must be a charged Q. It reads M directly: going through any AND and then back into any pretop OR would give another dirty-AND-receiving gate. Going through an intermediate raw-empty OR would require an extra charge (and would also need distinct incoming operands). Its other pin is pure with nonempty raw support. Thus M,W,Q exhaust charges. Every M-positive residual edge occurs at g or D. There are at most two from g and at most four from D, by applying the one-exception lemma in singleton_6648_q2_sole_or_all_c_cases.md to D on each deletion of 9 and 10. Each deletion retains the original anchored raw supports restricted to surviving vertices; exceptions consist only of W and both its children are anchored or singleton. Every graph edge survives one of these two deletions. For the remaining M-negative pairs set M=false. Q collapses to its pure other pin. Each projected nonzero centreless top is a clean pure conjunction, with all its nonsingleton primitive raw supports originally anchored except possibly W. An originally Lambda-anchored mixed wire has exactly its raw support after projection. An ordinary top in this projection accepts only its original stars. The same one-exception bound yields ≤2 further residuals per centreless top, and U≤2c+6≤j+2c+3.

Otherwise all pretop OR gates apart from M are pure. Indeed an OR-only continuation of raw-empty M with an additional raw-empty pin would require a different upstream raw-empty OR or dirty AND pin, or duplicate M itself; a raw-positive continuation is the preceding case. Set x=false, eliminating exactly g and M, aliasing M to D. This produces the **least size-19 x=false cofactor** without any further gate removal. The original top-OR aggregation persists; all pretop OR gates are now pure, all pretop AND outputs are used only through ANDs until the original top boundary. The original non-x graph edges and their original-star classification are retained for accounting. The only two remaining charges are pure W and another pure V. Each cofactor centreless top's primitive factors are anchored, singleton, W or V; ordinary tops give just original star edges.

If a cofactor top has one effective exceptional primitive E, it accepts at most two residuals. With anchored/singleton children this is the cited one-exception lemma. The only other possibility is E containing both y,z: if E does not contain both, it is V and cannot depend on W, so its children are anchored/singleton. In the alternative case, two assigned spokes at vertex p would force every anchored/singleton clause to contain p, hence the exception misses p and its entire support lies in N(p) by pair activation. Both y,z survive x=false and have no common neighbor on this cofactor, contradiction. Thus its residuals form a matching; the one-exception matching-of-three exclusion in that same lemma is independent of child anchoring. These anchor arguments use original residual status; the original anchor of any nonsingleton support cannot be x since x has no original stars. If two exceptions are effective they are incomparable by support (otherwise the larger is redundant). Neither is an ancestor of the other, so each has anchored/singleton children. Split W into its two children in the conjunction: each resulting clean pure term has at most the one exception V and accepts at most two residuals, hence this top accepts at most four.

There is at most one cofactor top where W is a physical primitive. For an AND with multiple direct readers before the top boundary, factor that gate from the aggregation of all roots reached along AND-only paths, cofactoring its direct readers true and restoring it at a single AND; this saves a gate. The same holds for multiple direct AND readers of a pure OR primitive, leaving all of its OR uses and their descendants untouched except for the cofactored direct AND pins. Reconstructing the changed root functions is exact when the primitive is true, and they all originally implied it when false. This argument only needs the actual OR aggregation over top roots and the lack of an AND-to-OR path before this boundary. It applies to the **least cofactor** despite any original sharing before g,M were removed. Private AND descendant paths and at most one direct AND reader per pure OR give the stated root uniqueness. Thus at most one top uses both exceptions effectively. Including the two x-edges gives U≤2+2c+2≤j+2c+3, also covering c=0 by the looser bound. No new enumeration or compiler run is used.

## Physical-wire and dependency audit

The sole entrance is counted by receiving OR gates, not by input pins or outgoing paths. M may have multiple readers. For a fixed global M=false projection a physical OR wire has exactly one projected value at every fanout: induction through its OR inputs gives the disjunction of its original raw-support vertices. A pure input to Q cannot already contain another unaccounted copy of M: such a positive-raw copy would require an earlier first raw-positive join, and a raw-empty other copy must either equal M or involve a new dirty-AND entrance. A duplicate pin or an input containing the other makes the OR redundant. Once Q exists, Lambda descendants can merge two copies of its mixed contribution or add pure disjunctions; the same induction removes all copies and keeps each descendant's own original anchored raw support. If a path from M passes through an AND before reaching an OR, that OR is a second entrance unless the AND is already a clean top and the OR is in the output aggregation. The latter path is outside the pretop statement. In the x=false case the global projection at M is D everywhere, and with no later pretop OR reading M the original pure wires keep their physical values. The 9=false and 10=false projections concern D only; their separate numerical bounds cover a union of edges and make no disjoint physical gate charge.
