# Sole OR at 3 and two further downstream positive-raw mixers

Scope: size-21 least TopForm circuit, q=3, j≥3, sole reader M=x∨D of x=3, and both other exceptional ORs Q1=H1∨R1, Q2=H2∨R2 downstream of M. Each Q_i has a genuine dirty AND input and a raw-positive opposite operand. No pin-disjointness or private-cone assumption. The issued bound U≤2c+j+3 holds.

D is a dirty AND upstream of both further charges, with pure anchored clauses. Singleton negativity of D follows from the standard sole-OR interface; a raw-empty OR D would itself be a charged gate before M. Each independent D-positive assignment contains 9 and 10; D is clean separately with 9 and with 10 deleted. Assign D-positive residuals first to these two clean anchored-conjunction batches. Each is an induced matching of size at most two; their union contains at most three residuals since all leftovers from the first deletion are incident to its deleted vertex. Globally shrink D to false on every reader, including M which now equals x. This preserves every D-negative pair and can only decrease the remaining output. It introduces no additional nonraw source.

By the size-21 private-donor exclusion, D has a reader other than M. Follow such a use along ANDs up to the first clean AND or first OR. The clean AND is centreless and requires D. The first OR must be charged because its input on this path is raw-empty and dirty. It cannot be M. These exhaust the cases; an output reached solely through dirty ANDs cannot compute the graph.

If an external multiplicative escape terminates at a charged gate, its dirty input implies D. That Q collapses to its other pin on projection. One positive-raw selector remains (if none remains the bounds improve). If the collapsed Q is later, its raw-positive other pin may carry the surviving selector, which is still only one Boolean signal. A conjunction expanded at that selector gives at most two clean anchored batches at each centreless top. Ordinary dirty corrections form at most one anchored conjunction, as the nonempty raw partner supplies an ordinary-star anchor for the union of opposite raw guards. Nonsingleton raw factors of ORs before, after or at the collapsed Q keep their original star anchors: the collapsed AND pin had no raw reach. The D-positive counts and the direct estimates 3+2=5 settle c=0; for c>0 use the global double accounting below.

Otherwise there are k≥1 centreless tops requiring D, with no remaining D-negative residuals. For distinct surviving donors H1,H2 (wires in the original circuit), apply the first donor projection to get a single anchored pure-clause product for H1. H2 can depend on the first selector. On the branch replacing Q1 by its raw support, H2 becomes an anchored conjunction; on the branch where H1 is supplied and Q1 is replaced by true, H2 becomes an anchored conjunction after discarding true factors. Every centreless top therefore gives at most four clean anchored batches by expanding the two positive selectors in order. This is the monotone expansion f(b)=f(0)∨b f(1) at each donor injection, preserving any separate uses of the donor by unfolding their AND cones. No negated literal or cleanliness of an unguarded donor is assumed.

For ordinary tops, after dirty-input cuts each side is a raw disjunction plus the donor disjuncts supplied by reachable mixers. The global raw-only term is the union of original star products. Each donor's linear correction aggregates its opposite raw guards, anchored by the corresponding mixer's nonempty original raw support. If R2 OR-reaches Q1, carry H1 through that side too; its raw support contains the first raw partner. In that event a joint product H1H2 can still arise if another top has opposite disjuncts, but an individual top cannot have H1 on both sides. Let H2° be H2 after projecting Q1 to raw. If H2 uses Q1 in its AND region, H2=H2°∨H1K, where K is its cofactor with Q1 true; if not, keep only the linear H2° term. If H2 includes H1 as a multiplicative factor, take only H1K. The projected H2-only aggregate, H1-only aggregate, and H1K times the H2 opposite raw guard aggregate are three clean anchored conjunctions. If an ordinary top gives a joint H1H2 term, replace the last nested aggregate by the joint H1K, which absorbs it; with independent donors the joint term is simply H1H2. Thus there are at most three ordinary batches in total. Empty or redundant terms can be discarded.

Every remaining centreless top has at least one direct dirty AND pin: all charged ORs have positive raw reach and an uncharged nonclean OR must have positive reach to belong to Lambda. No two tops share a direct dirty input, by factoring this input once across their clean OR aggregation and saving a gate. A top directly reading H1 or H2 needs at most two batches: fix the corresponding selector true while retaining that dirty conjunct; then expand only the other selector. Distinct from D,H1,H2, at most j−3 other dirty wires can provide direct inputs to tops needing four batches. Consequently m, the number of four-batch tops, is at most min(c−1,j−3). If a selector disappears on the D projection, or the two original donors coincide, use one donor bit instead (ordinary correction at most two aggregated guard terms for the repeated bit, each with its own anchor; centreless tops ≤2). If an original donor equals D, the corresponding gate collapses.

Finally all exclusive double batches have two induced edges. In G11 the only homogeneous double is 14,59: among the three degree-four vertex neighborhoods only N(8) contains an induced two-edge matching. A nonhomogeneous double forces at least seven original stars by the four corner anchor argument, even when x can star. If exactly seven stars exist, each nonhomogeneous double has the same entire star-edge set: relative to its four marked vertices their four corners yield eight incidences, with precisely one repetition under a common anchor on adjacent corners. Opposite repeated anchors would force homogeneity; two separate adjacent repetitions would force two common neighbors for distinct anchors. Thus its corner graph is a connected bipartite six-cycle with one pendant edge, bipartition classes of sizes three (anchors) and four (marks). The unique larger class recovers the same four marks from the seven stars. Those marks induce exactly the two matched edges. Hence at U=9 at most one nonhomogeneous double and at most one homogeneous double can be assigned exclusively.

With no nonhomogeneous double, at most one total surplus over the first edge per batch is needed. The escape-to-charge branch uses at most 2+1+2c batches, hence U≤2c+4. The centreless escape uses at most 2+3+2(c−1)+2m batches, so U≤2c+4+2m. If 2m≤j−1 this suffices. Otherwise j≥6 and c≥ceil(j/2)+1, whence the target is ≥17, above the whole graph. Repeated-donor cases clearly fit the same target without four-batch costs. With a nonhomogeneous double U≤9. For c≥1 the only issue is c=1,j=3, where either branch has at most five batches; at U=9 the two-double limit actually gives U≤7 on those five batches, contradiction. The c=0 escape-to-charge case was handled directly. This finishes the stated positive-mixer range.

Full-cube mathematical argument, no new sweep or kernel check. The first escape-to-charge counting also works if the one surviving selector is an OR of two raw-empty conjunctive arms (only centreless corrections); the broader centreless-escape proof above asserts only the positive-raw scope.
