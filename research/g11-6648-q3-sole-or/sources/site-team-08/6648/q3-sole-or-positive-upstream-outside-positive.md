# Positive mixer upstream of D and a second positive mixer outside D

Assume the original least size-21 G11 circuit, j≥3, q=3, and sole port M=3∨D with dirty AND D. The three direct dirty-AND-to-OR receivers are M, Q=B∨R in the ancestry of D, and T=V∨P outside that ancestry. R,P are raw-positive. The aim is U≤2c+j+3. We use the prior protected-donor, ordinary aggregation, unique direct dirty input per centreless top, and anchored matching lemmas. All assignments of residual edges are exclusive and keep original stars.

**Double counts.** If U≥9, at most one assigned batch of anchored clean products (including products clean on an induced vertex deletion) can own two residuals, as proved in q3-sole-or-positive-upstream-last-empty.md. If U≥8 there are at most TWO such batches. Indeed s≤8. A homogeneous double always uses the same marked pair 14,59. A nonhomogeneous double with four distinct anchors uses an eight-cycle exhausting the stars. Its marked set must be one of two bipartition classes, each fixing its unique induced pair; maximum star degree two excludes homogeneous doubles. Otherwise every nonhomogeneous double has a six-cycle and a pendant edge. The star graph can contain at most one six-cycle: the nonempty symmetric difference of two would be an even-sized Eulerian subgraph with at most four edges, forcing a four-cycle. For a fixed orientation of its two alternating classes, the three cycle marks have precisely one marked edge between them (they are three of the four endpoints of an induced matching), so two doubles of that orientation cannot be assigned exclusively. If a homogeneous double occurs as well, some original star degree is at least four, requiring both edges outside the six-cycle to meet the same cycle vertex. Only its anchor orientation can have a pendant edge, leaving at most one further nonhomogeneous double. This proves the two-double claim.

**Base counts.** D expands through Q into a raw term D0 and a dirty term D1, both original anchored pure conjunctions implying D. Assign on deletions of 9 and 10, at most four batches, at most three edges per term over the two slices. Any pure full-graph clean term with at most three clauses covers at most one original residual: a double would require four orientation clauses. A two-clause protected term satisfying D's independent-set safety covers at most one across both deletions (6648-q3-inherited-pure-arm-positive-Q.md §5). Globally set D=false and forward M to 3 on remaining D-negative edges. This agrees on those edges and only decreases signals elsewhere. Follow D's mandatory additional reader along ANDs to the first OR or clean top. The OR can only be T and its dirty pin then implies D. A first clean top implies D and is centreless.

**Escape to T.** T becomes its positive raw-facing pin, possibly carrying the unique remaining selector Q. There is at most one ordinary correction, the pure B times a union of opposite guards anchored by the original positive raw support of Q. Centreless tops yield ≤2c anchored products. Thus ≤2c+5 batches. If U≥9 the one surplus gives ≤2c+6; otherwise c≥1 or j≥5 gives the result. At c=0 and j=4, U≥8 would imply at most 5+2=7 from the two-double bound. It remains c=0,j=3.

In that corner the entire D-negative residual total comes from one ordinary correction B⋅H. If B enters D multiplicatively outside Q, setting Q=true and retaining B makes D one protected anchored conjunction, so ≤3+2 residuals. Otherwise D0 has no B AND gate in its multiplicative expansion, and uses at most D and the remaining junk AND G; hence ≤3 primitive clauses and ≤2 residuals across the deletions. If B does not have G upstream, B is one AND of two pure clauses, so B⋅H has ≤3 and gives at most one; with D1≤3 the total is ≤6. If G is upstream of B but absent from raw D0's multiplicative expansion, then D0 has ≤2 clauses and ≤1 protected residual; with D1 and ordinary the total is ≤1+3+2. In the only remaining case G is upstream of B and belongs to raw D0. Before Q all routes from G to B are AND-only, giving B≤G. With no other dirty node and no multiplicative B pin at D, the two inputs of D must be G and a Q-containing OR frontier signal of form B∨R′ (this includes Q itself). Hence D=G⋅(B∨R′), implying B≤D. The entire B correction disappears on D-negative edges. All bounds fit six.

**Escape to a centreless top.** If T's supplier still collapses, use the preceding count with c≥1. Otherwise at least one original centreless top requiring D can be omitted. With distinct remaining positive suppliers B,V the two-selector decomposition of 6648-q3-sole-or-downstream-positive.md gives ≤3 ordinary batches (including opposite or nested donors), at most two per other centreless top plus two extra per genuinely four-mode top. Such tops require distinct direct dirty inputs outside D,B,V, so their number m≤min(c−1,j−3). This decomposition is on the D=false, M=3 projection and does not require Q to be downstream of M: B precedes Q and is pure; V can receive Q and be expanded with the same selector. If B=V only one bit and ≤2 separately anchored ordinary aggregates suffice, giving ≤4+2+2(c−1) batches. No count merges guards with different original anchors.

For the distinct case there are at most 4+3+2(c−1)+2m=2c+5+2m batches. If U≤8 the target holds. Otherwise add at most one surplus. The inequality follows when 2m≤j−3, or when the target is at least 16. The remaining integer cases (j,c) are (4,2),(4,3),(4,4),(5,3),(6,3). At least two edges are starred: the earliest dirty AND is essential on a graph edge (replacing it globally by false would otherwise preserve every edge, giving a smaller circuit by constant propagation), and two singleton raw operands would give a clean edge conjunction, a useless nonedge conjunction, or a duplicate raw literal. Thus some nonsingleton pure primitive lies earlier, necessarily Lambda since all exceptional gates directly read dirt. Its anchor stars two edges. Therefore U≤14, settling (4,4),(5,3),(6,3).

At (4,3), failure would require U≥14, s≤2: no double is possible, so at most 13 residuals. At (4,2), failure requires U≥12, s≤4; at most 11 batches exist. A double must therefore be homogeneous, exhausting S with the four spokes from 8 to 1,4,5,9. All nonsingleton anchored primitive supports lie within these four leaves, since all other star degrees are at most one. At least one protected term of D is dirty, as D is dirty and is their union. Its independent positive witness contains 10 and loses positivity when 10 is removed, by protection. A clause must contain 10, necessarily the singleton 10. Thus that term's restriction deleting 10 supplies no batch. At most 10 remaining batches plus one surplus cannot cover 12. This finishes all cases.

This is a source-level argument, conditional on the cited physical structure and matching lemmas.
