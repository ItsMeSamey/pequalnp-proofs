# Sole OR port, two dirty-reading ORs, positive upstream mixer

Assume the original G11 least size-21 TopForm circuit, j≥3 and q=3. Let M=x∨D be the sole raw reader of x=3, with dirty AND sibling D. Of the two distinct physical ORs directly reading a dirty AND, one is M and the other is Q=B∨R, where B is a dirty AND, R has nonempty raw OR support, and Q is a strict ancestor of D. The third charged OR E has no direct dirty AND input. It may inherit mixing from either Q or M. We prove U≤2c+j+3 for the original residual edges. All star incidences below are original, including when an anchor is deleted or a wire projected.

Use the size-21 private-donor exclusion and the sole-OR shielding lemma. D has a reader besides M, and is clean on both G−9 and G−10: every independent D-positive input contains 9 and 10. The extra reader cannot be an OR, since it would be a third physical dirty-reading OR (Q is upstream). Trace an AND-only path from D until its first clean AND. Such a gate must occur because each previously dirty AND can only be read by an AND on this path: it cannot supply Q upstream of D, or either fixed pin D and x of M. A dirty output would contradict realization. The resulting centreless top T requires D. In particular c≥1.

**The common exceptional support.** Analyze the cone below D before M, then analyze original tops on D-negative inputs after forwarding M to x. For the latter analysis it suffices to forward M alone, retaining D and all its other uses as actual gates. This decreases the circuit and agrees on D-negative inputs. In both analyses Q is the only remaining physical injection of a dirty AND into a pre-top OR. No clean AND can feed an AND, even through intervening ORs, by TopForm. The raw operand R is physically pure: before Q there is no earlier dirty-reading OR. Let S_E be E's original raw OR support.

In the raw mode replace Q by R. All ORs relevant to an AND are now pure disjunctions and retain their original raw supports (including the original support {x} of M). Every nonsingleton primitive other than E is originally Lambda-anchored, or is the raw projection of a removed Q or M. For Q use R instead, which is itself a singleton, a Lambda support, or E. For M use x. In particular S_E is a binary union of guarded terminals. To see this, take E's two physical pins, neither a dirty AND. No clean AND can occur as a pin of E because E is dirty, live and must have an AND descendant. Any OR pin of E with nonempty raw support is Lambda unless it is M or Q, since it is dirty on a singleton and precedes E. For M use x. For Q use R; here E comes later, so R precedes Q,M,E and is anchored or singleton. A raw-empty OR pin would require an earlier raw-empty dirty-reading OR on an OR path, impossible here. Original star anchors survive as certificates for the raw support.

In the dirty mode retain B as a conjunct and set Q symbolically true in its outgoing occurrences. On B-positive inputs Q was already true. Any OR on an OR-only path from Q is then true; an OR not reached in this fashion retains its raw-mode support, since the first later OR after any intervening dirty AND would have to be M, which has been forwarded or is outside the D cone. This leaves each conjunction with guarded clauses and at most the same S_E. In particular E becomes true or retains S_E; modes cannot produce different effective exceptional supports. B itself precedes Q and expands as an AND of guarded clauses with at most E (if E is that early it is pure). These facts do not require disjoint gates or private consumers. Expanding the two modes is exact: a monotone top evaluated at Q=B∨R equals its value at Q=R OR the conjunction of B and its value at Q=true. The equality also holds with shared B and Q occurrences.

**Accounting.** The same two-mode identity expands D into at most two conjunctions, each implying D. Each is clean on G−9 and G−10. Assign every D-positive residual exclusively to a conjunction and a deletion where it survives (9–10 is a nonedge). This gives at most four protected batches. For the separate c=1 estimate each conjunction accepts at most three distinct original residuals in total: apply the two-deletion, one-binary-exception lemma in singleton_6648_two_slice_arm_supplement.md. That lemma uses the same full support on the two slices.

For D-negative residuals the forwarded-M circuit preserves all evaluations. Omit T. Each remaining centreless top gives two clean conjunctions by the same expansion. A projected ordinary top's raw-only term gives original star edges. Each Q-dependent ordinary side is an OR of raw vertices and the same B disjunct, since direct AND inputs are raw-empty. Q cannot reach both sides by OR paths in the original circuit because its dirty witness would make the top dirty. Forwarding M removes AND injection and adds no OR paths. Thus the ordinary dirty corrections aggregate to one conjunction B times H, where H is the union of all opposite raw guards of ordinary tops receiving Q by OR paths in the projected circuit. This is clean as a union of clean subterms. If any such tops exist, any fixed vertex of the nonempty raw support of R is joined by original star edges to every vertex in H, so H is guarded. If none exist omit the batch. The sum is at most 4+1+2(c−1)=2c+3 prior to assigning multiple edges to a batch. Each batch accepts at most two by the one-binary-exception lemma. At c=1 the two protected terms together cost at most six and the ordinary term at most two, giving U≤8≤2c+j+3.

**Sparse-star count when c≥2.** A counterexample would have U≥11 and hence at most five original star edges among G11's sixteen. Assign each residual to just one of the above batches. A batch receiving two disjoint edges has an induced matching on its surviving domain by the one-exception lemma. At least three of the four endpoint orientations must miss guarded clauses, since each clause hits each marked edge and can miss just one orientation. Each of those three clauses yields an original anchor joined by star edges to its two opposite marks. The anchor lies outside all four marks, by looplessness and exclusion of the marked edges from the star set. If opposite corners share an anchor it sees all four endpoints and C4 forces all anchors for these corners to coincide. In full G11 the only such matched pair is 14 and 59 at anchor 8 (degree-four centers 9 and 10 have no disjoint pair in their neighborhoods). If no anchor covers four endpoints, three distinct anchors force six distinct stars. With at most five, two adjacent corners therefore share an anchor h seeing three marks, and the third corner has a distinct anchor k seeing the fourth and a shared mark. The resulting five-star graph is a tree with one degree-three vertex h, and k the unique degree-two vertex at distance two from h. Its other four vertices recover the four matching endpoints. Distinct perfect matchings on them would give a C4. The homogeneous and five-star possibilities cannot coexist (a five-star tree has maximum degree three). Therefore at most one exclusively assigned disjoint double exists globally, including across deleted domains.

For an incident double vu,vw in a surviving domain, every ordinary guarded clause contains v: if it did not, its anchor equaled v by C4 uniqueness and would star the marked spokes. To reject singleton v, the exceptional trace must omit v. It contains u,w and lies in the neighborhood of v on that domain, since pairing any of its vertices with v activates the product. A child of that trace meeting a marked mate cannot have a second surviving vertex, as its original anchor would then be v and would star that mate. Its two guarded children therefore give exactly the surviving mate pair. The applicable traces are the same S_E on the full graph, or S_E minus 9 or minus 10. A two-element full trace yields only one possible mate pair; a three-element full trace yields at most two, both from a deletion; all other sizes yield none. Each pair has at most one possible pivot by C4. Exclusive assignment thus permits at most two incident doubles. This argument counts distinct mate sets, not the number of times a projected clause occurs.

There are at most three doubles above the first edge per batch. Consequently U≤(2c+3)+3=2c+6≤2c+j+3. The original issued balance gives the desired selected-set inequality. This is a mathematical branch proof, dependent on the source lemmas for private use, donor shielding, one-exception clean products and two-slice arm counting. No compiled proof is asserted.
