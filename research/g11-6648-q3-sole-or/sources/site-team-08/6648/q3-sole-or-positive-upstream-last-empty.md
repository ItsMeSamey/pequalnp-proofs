# Sole OR at 3 with a positive upstream mixer and an external raw-empty mixer

Assume the issued least size-21 regime, j≥3, q=3, sole reader M=3∨D of raw input 3 with dirty AND D. The three exceptional OR gates all directly read dirty ANDs. Let Q be raw-positive in the ancestry of D and T raw-empty outside the ancestry of D. All stars, residuals and counts refer to the original graph and circuit. The proof uses the previously established donor protection and external-reader lemma, the ordinary correction lemma, and the physical last-empty terminal lemma.

**Seven-star rigidity.** For any exclusive assignment of original residual edges to clean anchored conjunctions on G11 or induced vertex restrictions, each batch receives at most two edges, by the anchored matching lemma. The two edges of a double form an induced matching ab,cd. The four cross-pair anchors supply original star edges even if an anchor was deleted. A homogeneous double must use the unique pair 14,59 in N(8). A nonhomogeneous double requires seven or eight distinct star edges, as in team 03's upstream-complete note.

If U≥9 then s≤7. A nonhomogeneous double therefore uses exactly seven distinct star edges, exhausting the entire original star set. Up to relabeling one anchor A sees a,c,d, and two other anchors B,C respectively see b,c and b,d. The nonisolated star graph is the six-cycle A,c,B,b,C,d with a pendant edge A,a. It has maximum degree three, precluding any homogeneous double. Its unique bipartition has sides of sizes three and four. The marks of every possible nonhomogeneous double must be the four-vertex side. These vertices induce exactly the two edges ab,cd because their original qualifying matching was induced in a vertex restriction containing all four. Consequently two exclusive batches cannot both receive a nonhomogeneous double. If none occurs, the unique possible homogeneous double still permits at most one double. In all cases when U≥9, at most one assigned batch can receive two edges.

**Protected edges.** Q=B∨R with B dirty AND and R pure raw. Neither T nor M can feed Q as it precedes D. No dirty AND except B can enter an OR in D's primitive frontier. Distributing Q as in the upstream-complete note expresses D as a union of at most two anchored products, each implying D. This includes reconvergent appearances of Q and direct B factors. On each of the deletions of 9 and 10 each product is clean by donor safety. Every original edge survives at least one deletion. Assign D-positive residuals to one of these at most four batches.

**Remaining edges.** On D-negative inputs substitute false for all uses of D and M reduces to raw 3. This preserves their evaluations, decreases all other evaluations, and keeps raw reach at M. Q still precedes the substituted D. T cannot feed Q, M, or D; an OR after its raw-empty output or after a dirty AND on its outgoing path would require a further charge. The physical last-empty terminal lemma gives a unique reachable clean top, necessarily centreless. This also proves c≥1. No reconstruction of a projected least circuit is needed.

Only Q can supply an ordinary correction, the single anchored product B times the union of the opposite pure guards. Each of the c−1 other centreless tops supplies at most two anchored products by Q expansion. T's terminal supplies at most four by choosing either original T arm and then expanding Q. This covers simultaneous positive arms and shared ancestors: each mode decreases the original clean root, and one choice attains its actual value. Direct D suppression elsewhere only kills dirty AND factors, or one T arm; uncharged dirty-AND-to-OR escapes are unavailable. Raw-positive projected clauses retain original anchor witnesses.

At least two of these allocated batch slots disappear. By the size-21 donor lemma D has a direct reader other than M. Follow a live path starting at that reader. Until a first clean top or a dirty-AND-to-OR exit it consists entirely of ANDs following D; Q is earlier, and M has only the pins D and raw 3, so an exit can only be T. If the path reaches a first clean top, it implies D and takes no D-negative edges. This saves at least two slots (four if it is T's terminal). If it reaches T first, the arm on the path implies D, including the possibility of a direct D pin. That arm vanishes upon suppression of D. All remaining T-terminal edges can go to the surviving arm, requiring at most two Q-mode slots and saving two. When both arms vanish the same upper bound holds. This argument does not require that the surviving arm dominate any ordinary guard or that D have a private cone.

The total number of batches is at most 4+1+2(c−1)+4−2=2c+5. If U≥9, seven-star rigidity yields U≤2c+6≤2c+j+3. Otherwise U≤8≤2c+j+3 since c≥1 and j≥3. The issued balance transfers this to the selected sets. This is a source-level proof.
