# Low-deficiency tableaux and adaptive preprocessing

For m clauses of affine Boolean literals over observed x and hidden y, let ρ be the largest number of linearly independent hidden coefficient vectors selectable from distinct clauses, at most one per clause. Write d=m−ρ. Adding a clause increases ρ by at most one, so d is monotone under clause addition, including when extra variables occur only in added clauses. The project's flat theorem gives an accepting affine flat of codimension at most d through every accepted x, even with cyclic dependencies. [Local theorem and hypotheses](README.md#removing-the-cycle-assumption-from-the-flat-theorem)

## Exact counts for tableau and circuit encodings

In the standard one-hot Cook–Levin tableau, each interior time-space cell has K≥3 private hidden symbol indicators, an at-least-one clause, and one pair-exclusion clause for each unordered pair. For N disjoint cells the gadgets alone have m=N(1+K(K−1)/2) and rank NK, hence deficiency N(K−1)(K−2)/2. In each cell, choose the K consecutive pair-exclusion clauses around a cycle on the K symbols. Select the first symbol of each oriented edge. These K unit hidden normals attain the rank upper bound. Adding transition and boundary checks cannot reduce deficiency. In fixed-alphabet rectangular tableaux with O(N) total cells and bounded local rules, m=O(N), so the full deficiency is Θ(N). Grounding the external input bits leaves these interior gadgets intact. This argument applies to the stated unsimplified one-hot encoding.

For a fixed Boolean circuit with all input bits observed, g binary AND/OR gates and t NOT gates, the usual equivalence encoding has 3g+2t+1 clauses including the output unit. Count occurrences before simplification. There are g+t distinct hidden gate outputs, and each gate supplies its own unit hidden normal from a separate clause. Thus d=2g+t+1 exactly.

For XOR/AND circuits, propagate affine expressions through XOR gates. Encode each AND z=u v using (z=0 OR v=1) and (v=0 OR z=u). The first clause supplies z's private unit normal. With h AND gates, a final affine output unit and no other hidden bits, d=h+1 exactly. With guessed witnesses hidden too, d≤h+1. Independently, existential acceptance has a deterministic algorithm running in 2^h poly(size) time: process the ANDs in order, branch on u, and substitute zero or v for z. Earlier substitutions make every branch condition and the output condition affine in the original inputs and witnesses. Test consistency by Gaussian elimination at each leaf.

## An exact adaptive tradeoff

Consider a program on observations x that forms arbitrary affine expressions, evaluates binary ANDs and branches on available affine expressions. At each leaf it substitutes affine expressions of its observations and computed wires for the observed inputs of an existential affine-clause verifier. The preprocessor has no access to that verifier's hidden coordinates. The leaf accepts exactly when this verifier has a witness.

**Every accepted observation lies on an accepting affine flat of codimension at most r+b+d**, where r and b count ANDs and parity tests along that particular path and d is the leaf verifier's hidden-normal deficiency.

Fix the path. Keep each computed AND as a fresh hidden z, with its two exact clauses above. Add b equations fixing the branch outcomes and attach the leaf verifier after its substitutions. Every model follows this path and witnesses acceptance, and the chosen observation has a model. If the leaf originally had m clauses and rank ρ, its ρ selected vectors remain independent on projection to its original hidden coordinates. The r new unit z normals, selected from distinct gate clauses, add r independent vectors jointly with those leaf normals. Thus the combined deficiency is at most (m+2r+b)−(ρ+r)=d+r+b. Apply the flat theorem to the combined formula.

Consequently for IP(a,b)=Σ aᵢbᵢ on 2n observed bits, n≥1, **every accepting path has r+b+d≥n+1**: the largest one-flat has dimension n−1. For every integer split r+b≤n the bound is attained at d=n+1−r−b. Query b coordinates of the first block and compute r other products in preprocessing. At the leaf encode the remaining n−r−b products with the two-clause gadget, then impose one affine terminal parity on their sum plus the contributions from the queried and computed coordinates. The known coefficients from the queried coordinates multiply observations by constants. With explicit accept/reject leaves, querying all n first-block bits then their specified parity also attains b=n+1, r=d=0.

## Decision-only reductions

The input predicate posing this obstruction is easy: a fixed two-state automaton reads interleaved symbols (aᵢ,bᵢ) and toggles its parity state precisely on (1,1). A polynomial-time decision-only reducer can run it and emit a constant satisfiable or unsatisfiable instance, both of deficiency at most one. Thus even a regular language forces linear deficiency for relation-preserving encodings, while its grounded decision instances admit a constant-deficiency encoding. The standard grounded tableau's linear deficiency is a property of that representation.

OpenAI Codex and Yichuan Wang give the randomized affine-clause solver used in this implication. Its parameter is maximum rank deficiency over clause subfamilies. On a grounded instance this equals the number of clauses minus the size of a largest independent transversal. [Solving affine-clause satisfiability by maximum rank deficiency, Theorem 1](https://airaxiv.com/papers/pdf/2611.0031/?t=1795383815&v=1)

If a deterministic polynomial-time many-one reduction sent SAT to constant-deficiency instances of the general affine-clause solver with its one-sided randomized polynomial-time guarantee, then composition would give NP=RP. A deterministic target solver would give P=NP. Conversely P=NP allows a deterministic compiler to emit a constant instance after deciding SAT. A guarantee for the grounded decision alone need not preserve a fiber of observations to which the flat argument applies. None of the counts above excludes an unrestricted decision-only compiler.

## Which observations can be released?

If only a₁,…,aₜ are hidden and all b remain observed, existential projection gives the disjunction of b₁,…,bₜ and IP on the last n−t pairs. For 0≤t<n the minimum attainable deficiency is exactly n−t+1. Setting the first t b's to zero restricts the predicate to IP on n−t pairs. Affine specialization of observed coordinates preserves the selected hidden normals before simplification, so the IP lower bound applies. Conversely encode each remaining product with two clauses and use one terminal disjunction containing the first t b's and the residual parity-one literal. For t=n≥1 this yields OR of all b's, of minimum deficiency one, since any accepted point of a deficiency-zero formula lies on the entire observed space.

In contrast, hiding just a₁ and b₁ makes every observation acceptable: the hidden product can realize either value to hit the required parity. The projected relation now admits the empty conjunction, of deficiency zero. This concerns projection of the observed relation itself.

More generally let H be the subspace of allowed hidden changes in the 2n input bits, so the observation specifies a coset v+H. Write B for the polar form of IP. If h,k∈H satisfy B(h,k)=1, then for every observation the four quadratic values at v, v+h, v+k, and v+h+k sum to one in GF(2). Thus each coset contains an accepting point. Two hidden directions with nonzero pairing suffice, regardless of the coordinates.

These two cases exhaust all linear observations. If B vanishes on H×H, let t=dim H≤n. Extend a basis of H to the first t members of the first half of a symplectic basis. Explicitly choose e∈H nonzero and f with B(e,f)=1. Replace the other basis vectors h of H by h+B(h,f)e so they lie in the orthogonal complement of span(e,f). That complement is nondegenerate. Repeat and finish with any symplectic basis of the remaining complement. In the resulting coordinates (s,u), the quadratic has form s·u+α·s+β·u, since subtracting s·u leaves an additive function vanishing at zero. Translation turns this into (s+β)·(u+α)+α·β. Its total sign sum is positive 2^n before and after coordinate changes, forcing α·β=0. Translation does not change the subspace of hidden directions. Therefore the observed cosets are affinely equivalent to hiding just the first t coordinates of one block in the ordinary inner product. Invertible affine changes of observed coordinates preserve the minimum deficiency in both directions. Thus every totally isotropic hidden subspace has exact projected minimum deficiency n−t+1, and every non-isotropic hidden subspace has exact minimum zero.

[Finite verification](check_tableau_erasures.py) enumerated every hidden linear subspace for n=1,2,3 (5, 67, and 2,825 subspaces). Non-isotropic kernels had zero rejected cosets. For isotropic kernels with m=n−t, the rejected-coset count was (2^{2m}+2^m)/2, as predicted by the ordinary inner-product normal form. The exact deficiency statement follows from the proof above.
