# An integral mask relaxation for a class of prefix guards

**Input class.** Coordinates e are partitioned into ordered blocks I_v. Each block has a starting mask S_v. Immediately before its jth pivot e the current mask A is S_v with the preceding pivots toggled. The row is x_e(1+parity(A))=0, and the next mask is A△{e}. Starting masks may intersect their own block. A row with the opposite offset can be written in this convention by toggling its own pivot in the mask, using Boolean idempotence. Given the blocks and starting masks, the format can be verified against the input. A balanced planted bipartite matching compiler is the case in which each coordinate occurs in one starting mask. The following class permits arbitrary numbers of occurrences.

Write h(e) for the block containing e and T(e)={v:e∈S_v}. Construct a directed multigraph with an arc h(e)→v for every v∈T(e). Assume there are positive block potentials satisfying

    λ_h(e) ≥ Σ_{v∈T(e)} λ_v     for every e.

This condition is recognizable from the directed graph: no coordinate with two or more tails may have an arc internal to a strongly connected component (SCC). Necessity follows from monotonicity of λ on arcs and strict decrease on every branching arc. For sufficiency process the SCC condensation backwards. Give each component a single positive value at least the maximum over the sums of tail values for its outgoing coordinates; also take the maximum with one. Internal coordinates with an internal arc have just that one tail and give equality. Empty tails are allowed. The input class uses a supplied prefix decomposition; the theorem does not require finding an optimal decomposition of arbitrary masks.

**Basic mask LP.** Use p_e as the real bit marginal and z(A) as the marginal that parity(A) is one. All are in [0,1]. Include z(A)≤Σ_{e∈A}p_e for each published mask and the row equation z(A△{e})=z(A)−p_e. These are the constraints in the published mask relaxation with the marker omitted. Additional singleton-mask identifications hold in every honest distribution. For a block with at least one pivot, telescoping from S_v to Q_v=S_v△I_v gives

    Σ_{e∈I_v}p_e = z(S_v)−z(Q_v) ≤ Σ_{e∈S_v}p_e.

For a block with no pivots use the trivial inequality 0≤Σ_S p. Multiply by λ_v and sum. The potential hypothesis gives the opposite inequality on the total, so equality holds at every vertex. Each strict potential column has p_e=0. Also z(Q_v)=0 for every nonempty block, and each member of S_v has marginal zero at an empty block.

For each transit h(e)→v, send ordinary real flow λ_v p_e. A positive p_e can occur only in an equality column, so its total departing flow is λ_h(e)p_e. Equality of the block inequalities says the arriving and departing totals at each vertex agree. In a nonnegative finite circulation every positive arc lies on a directed cycle, as follows by summing conservation over SCCs in topological order. Every branching column, every empty-tail column, and every singleton-tail coordinate crossing SCCs therefore has zero marginal. Denote the remaining singleton-tail coordinates by E*. In the directed graph D on E*, real p is an ordinary nonnegative circulation with throughput at most one at each vertex: the departing total is bounded by z(S_v)≤1 for every nonempty block.

**Exact real projection.** Split each vertex into an entrance and exit with a unit-capacity arc joining them, and send each original arc from its head's exit to its tail's entrance with capacity one. The possible p described in the preceding paragraph are precisely projections of feasible circulations on this split directed graph, with flows on the split arcs given by the throughputs. This polytope is integral. An elementary proof considers any circulation with fractional arcs. At each vertex conservation precludes exactly one fractional incident arc, so the fractional subgraph contains an undirected cycle (parallel arcs included). Perturb the cycle by ±ε with signs according to its traversed directions. Both perturbations preserve the bounds and circulation for small ε, proving this vector is not extreme. Loops are independent perturbable coordinates. The split graph also handles loops in D without exception.

An integral circulation of throughput ≤1 is a union of vertex-disjoint directed cycles, with loops allowed. Each such support is a genuine Boolean solution: at a used block exactly one member of S_v and exactly one member of I_v are selected. Its starting parity is one, and the unique selected pivot sees it before any selected pivot has toggled it. At unused blocks all pivots are zero. Removed columns are zero throughout. Every Boolean solution has an honest mask LP lift by evaluating the actual parities. It follows that the basic unmarked LP projects exactly onto the convex hull of solutions.

**Complete affine export and marked witness.** Export the unit zeros outside E* and the parity equations Q_v·x=0. For nonempty blocks the latter are certified by z(Q_v)=0; at empty blocks all terms are individually zero. After removing zero coordinates these are the binary incidence equations of D. Their common kernel is exactly the span of the directed simple cycles. One proof works dually: a linear form zero on directed simple cycles is zero on every directed closed walk by cycle decomposition. In each SCC, fix a root and define a vertex label as the sum of the arc labels on a directed root-to-vertex path. This is independent of path by appending a common return path. Each arc label is therefore the sum of its two endpoint labels, which expresses the form as a sum of incidence rows. Loops then have zero label as required. Consequently LP-to-GF(2) row addition is complete for every single parity marker, of any width. Either the marker is a linear combination of exports and its odd assertion refutes immediately, or one can return an actual simple cycle with odd marker. For construction, search the doubled SCC graph (v,b), b∈{0,1}, traversing arc e by adding its marker bit to b. A path from (r,0) to (r,1) gives an odd closed walk; split at repeated vertices, retaining an odd part until simple. Trying one root per SCC suffices, since at least one of a root-to-cycle-to-root walk with or without an inserted odd cycle is odd.

The result holds for arbitrary bounded or unbounded vertex degrees with polynomial input size. It concerns integrality on the original coordinates of the **unmarked** LP and completeness after a single affine export for the marked problem. Full integrality of the joint (p,z) polytope or of the LP obtained by imposing the marker directly is a stronger statement.

**Both engines are needed on an even directed cycle.** For even n≥6 take one pivot per block, with rows x_i(1+x_(i−1)) for cyclic indices 0,…,n−1 and marker Σ_i x_i=1. The standalone marked mask LP accepts p_i=1/2, singleton mask marginals 1/2, adjacent-pair marginals zero, and marginal one for the full marker mask. Every row, bound and support inequality holds. The basic mask LP nevertheless exports x_i+x_(i−1)=0 for every i. The sum of the n/2 alternate exported equations is Σ_i x_i=0, refuting the marker.

Ordinary marked PC of degree two alone also fails. Substitute x_(n−1)=1+Σ_{i<n−1}x_i and squarefree-reduce. Only rows 0 and n−1 change. The quadratic monomial x_0 x_2 is private to row 0, and x_(n−2) x_1 is private to row n−1, for n≥6. With those row coefficients forced to zero, each remaining row owns a distinct adjacent quadratic monomial. No nonzero combination of the reduced guards is affine. The reduced image of every original degree-two PC line belongs to their span: the Boolean axioms and marker map to zero, and the image of an affine multiplicand must already be zero. Hence 1 is underivable at degree two. This family illustrates a strict gain from combining the engines even when each vertex has only one incoming and one outgoing arc.
