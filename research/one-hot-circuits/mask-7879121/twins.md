# Duplicate-class normalization for L-meta-20555

The four row classes in order are 0,3,4,{1,2}. The column order is the same. The row-major target on this quotient is 43333.

For each duplicated row or column class independently, keep one of its two input wires as a representative and set its twin to false. The resulting restricted circuit still sees one representative for every possible row or column. Logical constants produced by fixing a twin can be propagated. If a fixed wire occurred as an operand of a useful gate, at least one gate disappears. An AND or OR becomes either its other input or a constant, and NOT becomes constant. A gate whose second NOT operand is unused may simply have that operand deleted in advance. All constants can be simplified symbolically; a nonconstant final output needs none of them.

If one twin was unused, choose to set the other to false. The restricted circuit then uses neither member of that class. If both were used, the circuit saves at least one gate. A shared upfront OR reconstructs the class from its two original raw inputs and can drive every surviving use of the representative. The target is invariant under exchanging the twins, so the resulting circuit agrees on every original pair. Applying the second coordinate restriction does not alter the preexisting OR for the first coordinate, except that simplification may render it dead.

Thus the original budget-nine question is equivalent to the disjunction of four quotient circuit questions. Supply the singleton classes 0,3,4 on each shore freely. Supply any subset of the two merged-class flags as additional primitives and ask for a circuit of size at most nine minus its cardinality. Each quotient witness expands via the corresponding upfront ORs. The four budgets are 9,8,8,7; each quotient circuit receives 16 distinct promised inputs.

For formulas the normalization can preserve tree structure by setting the more frequently occurring twin of each pair to false. Deleting constant arguments leaves at most the original number of unpinned leaf occurrences, and no additional unary gates. In a tree the number of binary gates equals the number of leaves minus one. Restoring each surviving representative occurrence with a separate binary OR therefore consumes at most the savings. Exact formula dynamic programming on 16-bit signatures with cost 1 for each merged-class leaf excludes all formulas of size at most nine. This calculation is implemented in form.py.
