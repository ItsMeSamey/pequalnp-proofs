# Classical projective incidence costs 3N+O(N^(3/4))

Let q be a prime power and N=q²+q+1. We construct a monotone binary circuit of size 3N+O(N^(3/4)) representing point-line incidence in PG(2,q) on the one-per-shore promise. The first-fork lower bound gives 3N−O(N^(3/4)).

Identify a line with its projective normal under the standard nondegenerate dot product. Distinct labels form an incident pair in either orientation precisely when their dot product is zero. O(q) points are absolute: in odd characteristic, on the chart (x,y,1) at most two y solve x²+y²+1=0 for each x, and the remaining charts have q+1 points; in even characteristic the absolute points form the line X+Y+Z=0. Form z_i=row_i OR col_i, costing N gates. Include row_i AND col_i separately for every absolute label. There are at most N(q+1)/2 distinct positive unordered pairs.

Take the least power of two t≥N^(3/8). The host has t² points (x,y) and t² nonvertical lines (m,b) over F_t. Its t³ edges indexed by (x,y,m) join (x,y) and (m,y−mx). It is t-regular and four-cycle-free. Embed N integer projective indices into 3 log₂t bits (t³≥N), then apply a fixed affine permutation j↦aj+b over F_(t³) with a≠0, viewing its output as the edge triple. This family acts transitively on ordered distinct pairs. The probability that a fixed pair goes to two adjacent edges is 2(t−1)/(t³−1). Some fixed permutation therefore maps only O(Nq/t²)=O(N^(3/4)) positive pairs to adjacent host edges; it can be found by enumeration of the polynomial-size family. Include z_i AND z_j for each such exceptional positive pair.

At each of the M=2t² endpoints build the OR of incident z_i, treating empty banks as false. All banks cost at most 2N since each label is used twice. A single label lights two endpoints, two adjacent labels three, and two disjoint labels four. All positives below four have already been handled.

Guard the remaining branch by at least four lit endpoints, which means exactly four on the promise. On that face the negation of an endpoint flag y_j equals the condition that at least four OTHER flags are lit. Compute all prefix and suffix thresholds k≤4 using T(i,k)=T(i−1,k) OR (y_i AND T(i−1,k−1)) and fixed boundaries; combine the five splits between the prefix and suffix around each j. All negative rails and the guard together cost O(M). Constants can be folded. A Boolean decoder can now be made monotone by dual-rail simulation at constant overhead. Wrong negative rails below four endpoints do not matter since the guard rejects.

On the four-endpoint face there are two point and two line endpoints. On each shore recover the minimum and maximum lit indices by a balanced binary tree in O(M) gates. Store at each node nonemptiness and the two indices relative to its interval (arbitrary if empty). Child nonemptiness controls the first/last choice; a node of height h costs O(h+1), and the sum of (h+1)/2^h is bounded. Test the two bipartite perfect matchings by the affine incidence equations. The genuine pair of disjoint edges supplies one and no second exists by absence of four-cycles. Recover both edge indices (x,y,m), invert the fixed permutation, decode the projective point indices and test their dot product. Indices are valid on the guarded promise.

Enumerate projective coordinates (x,y,1), (x,1,0), (1,0,0) by integer division and comparisons. For F_(p^e), represent elements in a fixed polynomial basis, multiply and reduce by a fixed irreducible polynomial using binary modular arithmetic on coefficients. These field operations, the fixed affine permutation and its inverse, and index arithmetic take Boolean circuit size polynomial in log N. The final OR collector is linear in the number of exceptional and absolute terms. The sum is N+2N+O(t²+Nq/t²+q+polylog N)=3N+O(N^(3/4)).
