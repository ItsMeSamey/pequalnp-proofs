# Point–hyperplane incidence has leading coefficient three in every fixed dimension

Fix d≥2 and let N=(q^(d+1)−1)/(q−1). There is one raw flag for every point and one for every hyperplane of PG(d,q), with exactly one active flag on each shore. For the minimum total gate count G of a binary monotone AND/OR single-output decoder of incidence,

    3N−1−N q^((d−1)/2)/(k+q^((d−1)/2)) ≤ G ≤ 3N+O_d(q^(d−1)),
    k=(q^d−1)/(q−1).

In particular G/N tends to three for every fixed d as q grows over prime powers. The lower error is O_d(q^((d+1)/2)).

## Lower bound

All row and column signatures are distinct and nonuniversal. All 2N flags are used and singleton inputs reject: an unused flag with a positive incident cell would force its opposite singleton positive below a negative cell. Use the first-branch group and the merge argument in projective-cold-empty.md. For every incident point p and hyperplane H choose a second point p′ in H, and a hyperplane H′ through p avoiding p′ (a linear functional with values 0 and 1 on their independent representatives suffices). Thus their incidence is the middle edge of an induced four-vertex path. The negative background (p′,H′) becomes positive after adding p and after adding H. At a common first merge of p and H the gate would have to be AND to accept their positive pair from two rejected singletons and OR to allow these two sensitivities on the negative background. The flags therefore have different first-branch groups. A singleton-cold flag cannot produce an edge with a cold flag in a later group: the later flag cannot influence the earlier first branching wire, which remains zero, and the output equals the later singleton output. Thus all cold points versus all cold hyperplanes form a zero submatrix. The terminal no-branch group is included in the cold flags. Each first branch hosts at most one singleton-hot flag per shore because two such flags in the group are interchangeable on every background, contradicting distinct signatures.

Let h be the number of branching wires. If h<N retain m=N−h cold points and m cold hyperplanes. The incidence matrix has all row and column sums k. Two different hyperplanes contain λ=(q^(d−1)−1)/(q−1) common points, and by duality two different points lie on λ common hyperplanes. Thus both Gram matrices are (k−λ)I+λJ, with k−λ=q^(d−1). On mean-zero vectors the operator norm is σ=q^((d−1)/2). Centering the two size-m indicator vectors gives incidence count at least k m²/N−σ m(1−m/N). This quantity cannot be positive, so m≤Nσ/(k+σ). The live gate count is at least 2N−1+h by summing excess fanout. The conclusion also holds when h≥N.

## Upper bound

Identify each hyperplane with the projective point given by its normal vector, and use the representative with last nonzero coordinate normalized to one. Chart j has varying entries at positions 0 through j−1, a fixed 1 at j and fixed zeros thereafter. For every label v form z_v as the OR of its point and hyperplane flags, costing N gates. On two distinct active labels the desired relation is the symmetric zero dot product. Diagonal incidence is handled separately by one raw conjunction for each absolute label. Chart zero has no absolute label, and in chart j≥1, after all but one free entry are fixed, the equation for that entry has degree two with leading coefficient one, so the total number of absolute labels is O_d(q^(d−1)) in every characteristic.

Create two banks of refined endpoint flags, each label contributing its z_v to exactly one OR in each bank. For charts j≥2, in the first bank fix all coordinates except coordinate 0, and in the second fix all except coordinate 1. Include the chart in every key. Inside any bucket distinct labels with omitted entries x,y form a positive pair exactly when xy=K, where K is fixed by the other entries. If K≠0 each vertex has at most one neighbor; if K=0 the graph is a star with possible isolated vertices. Color this graph properly in at most two colors, separately for each bucket, and refine its endpoint OR by that color. For charts j=0,1 one can simply give each label its own endpoint in each bank. There are O_d(q^(d−1)) refined endpoints altogether: each chart j≥2 has at most 2q^(j−1) per bank, and the first two charts contain q+1 labels. Although the buckets have arbitrary color names, the colors specify wiring only. The total number of binary bank ORs is at most 2N.

A distinct incident pair lights four different refined endpoints. Guard the decoder by the threshold of four on all endpoints. There can never be more than four lit endpoints on a promised input, and repeated projective labels give just two. On the guarded face every endpoint has an available negative rail: it is off iff at least four other endpoints are on. Prefix and suffix counters for thresholds one through four produce every such rail simultaneously in linear total size.

Partition the coordinate positions into two nonempty blocks, with coordinate 1 in the first and coordinate 0 in the second. Coarsen first-bank flags to (chart, first-block tuple) by ORs and second-bank flags to (chart, second-block tuple) likewise. Every retained coarse key is encoded by its chart and its varying block coordinates, so each shore has at most as many keys as refined endpoints. For example choose first block to contain only coordinate 1. Enumerate each chart's possible keys consecutively, enabling recovery of all tuple entries and fixed coordinates by rank arithmetic of size polylog(q) for fixed d. On each shore obtain the minimum and maximum active coarse key, allowing repeated values when a unique key is active. Order keys by chart first. Two labels in different charts align in the same chart order. When the charts agree the product contribution from a block is the dot product of its two block tuples and hence does not depend on their ordering. In either case summing both block dot products tests precisely the original incidence.

For size accounting, dual-rail the Boolean postprocessing on the four-endpoint face: OR gives its complement by AND of the negative rails, and bounded-fanin Boolean gates cost constant overhead. A balanced encoding tree produces the two extreme active indices on a shore in linear total size, using O(j) multiplexer gates at a node with 2^j consecutive leaf positions, since the internal indices have only j bits. Pad with zero leaves if needed. Finite-field arithmetic in a fixed polynomial representation, including ranking, has polynomial cost in log(q) for fixed d. The overall output is the OR of the guarded zero-dot test and the absolute diagonal conjunctions. Four-endpoint negative pairs decode to their actual dot product; fewer than four endpoints cannot represent a distinct positive pair. This gives the asserted upper bound. The circuit is nonuniform in q; all bucket colors may also be obtained by elementary componentwise two-coloring.
