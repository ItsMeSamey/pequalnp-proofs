# Empty cold submatrix and the exact Fano branching count

Use the first-fork groups and singleton-hot flags in projective-cold-forest.md. A pair of raw flags in one group has a first common merging gate K. Before K the flags have unique paths, and after K all paths from either to the output pass through K. If their joint input is positive and both singletons reject, K must be AND, since an OR merge at zero background could only attain the maximum of the two separate singleton values. If both single additions to some negative background individually produce positive outputs, K must be OR: sensitivity to each input of an AND merge forces its other input already true at baseline, so that both were already true. Earlier branching signals may feed both predecessor subtrees, but neither of the designated raw flags influences those side signals before K.

Consider an edge (r,c) in a C4-free bipartite host of minimum degree at least two. Choose p≠r adjacent to c and l≠c adjacent to r. Then (p,l) is a negative promised cell, while adding either r or c yields a superset of an accepted cell. The preceding two merge conditions are incompatible, so r and c belong to different groups. Generally the middle edge of any induced four-vertex path enjoys this property. Two cold endpoints in different groups cannot be positive since the later input cannot affect the earlier input's cold first fork. Consequently the whole cold-row/cold-column submatrix is zero. Here singleton-coldness is required, automatic for a target with no universal vertices.

In a projective plane, every group has at most one hot point and one hot line, since different same-shore signatures cannot be interchangeable. For h<N the cold sets thus contain k=N−h vertices on each shore. From zero incidences and the centered incidence estimate,

    0 ≥ (q+1)k²/N − √q k(1−k/N),
    h ≥ N − N√q/(q+1+√q).

Every flag is essential, so total binary size is at least 2N−1 plus the integer ceiling of the branching bound. When q=s² is an even square, a degree-s maximal arc has k=q(s−1)+s points and precisely N−k(q+1)/s=k disjoint lines. This attains the centered estimate exactly and establishes a barrier for this graph relaxation. This equality does not construct a circuit. The incidence-free problem and maximal arcs have an established literature. [Denniston's arc construction](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0021980069800955) [De Winter, Schillewaert and Verstraete](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v19i4p24)

For the Fano plane, at most six branching wires is impossible. Use the seven nonzero binary vectors as point and normal-line IDs and their binary dot product for incidence. For every possible pair of cold subsets require zero incidence throughout their product. There are p remaining hot point flags and l remaining hot line flags. Their assignment into at most h groups requires at least max(0,p+l−h) groups with one hot point and hot line. Match this many distinct hot flags; it is enough to consider the minimum number since any larger matching contains one of that size. A matched pair cannot be incident, because its flags are interchangeable and singletons reject. Two matched pairs (x,a),(y,b) satisfy M(x,b)=M(y,a) by the same interchange twice. The companion fano-hot-pairs.py checks every choice by subsets and permutations. At h=6 exactly 203 cold subset pairs survive emptiness and capacity, and none has a valid required hot matching. Thus h≥7. The ordinary row-wise DNF has precisely seven branching column raws and no other branches; the minimum branching-wire count is seven. This gives a twenty-gate lower bound; the finite certificate does not determine the minimum total gate count.
