# Sharp packing bound for arbitrarily many branching wires

Let b count wires, including raw flag wires, used more than once in a live monotone binary AND/OR circuit. On any row–column submatrix in which the physical evaluation rejects every single flag, at most 2b connected components of its positive graph fail to be complete bipartite. In particular this applies to every induced submatrix with no universal row or column: singleton rejection follows from monotonicity below a negative cell. Restrict all discarded flags to zero.

Induct on b. With no branching the read-once proof in one-fork-components.md applies. Otherwise select the earliest branching wire H in a topological ordering with raw inputs listed first. Its ancestors form a read-once tree and have no paths to the output bypassing H; if H is a branching raw this tree is just that raw. Partition the raw flags into its tree and the remaining flags. Even when H has arbitrarily many uses, the inside-only slice is a positive unary function of H or zero. Every outside row on the cross slice activates a unary function of H that vanishes at zero, since that outside row alone is rejected. Thus the two oriented cross bands each form a single biclique. Both details are proved in one-fork-components.md and need no restriction on the outer circuit.

Each cross biclique can meet at most one connected component. Among components untouched by the cross bands, the inside-only components are bicliques by read-once. Each other non-biclique component remains a non-biclique component wholly in the outside-only slice. To evaluate that slice set H=0, deleting the dedicated tree and replacing H's outgoing occurrences by separate zero leaves. The remaining circuit has at most b−1 branching wires. Zero leaves can be kept as separate constants; alternatively, bypassing a k-use identity gate through its other operand can create a new branch at that operand only if the bypassed gate itself branched. Removing unreachable gates cannot increase the count. The outside-only circuit still physically rejects single flags, including when its positive graph now has a universal vertex. The induction bound is 2+2(b−1).

An induced union of t vertex-disjoint copies of P4 with all off-block entries zero therefore forces at least ceil(t/2) branching wires whenever t≥2. For g binary gates reading d actual raw wires the total excess g−d+1 is at least b. Hence g≥d−1+ceil(t/2). At t=1 a row and column of the 2×2 path matrix are universal; here the physical singleton rejection hypothesis must be checked separately. In fact its matrix alone can be realized by r0 OR c0 without branching.

For each even t this is attained by OR-pooling disjoint copies of the two-path construction in one-fork-components.md. Each copy reads eight flags once except for its mixed helper H and rejects single flags. Distinct copies therefore never introduce cross positives. For odd t≥3 add the disjoint circuit ((r0 OR r1) AND c0) OR (r0 AND c1), which duplicates raw r0 and accepts exactly 00,01,10. A binary output OR tree joins the modules without further branching. Thus ceil(t/2) branching wires suffice also for every odd t≥3.

## Independent partition proof

Order the b branching wires topologically. Assign each used raw to the first branching wire encountered on the unique initial path from that raw, counting the raw itself if it branches; put raws with no such wire in a final group. Put any unused flags in the final group. A group can be empty. For group i belonging to H_i, every path from its raws to the output passes through H_i. Earlier branching wires evaluate to zero when only raws of group i or later groups are active: any upstream raw would reach an earlier or equal first fork. A raw in a later group cannot affect H_i. Otherwise the path to H_i already has an earlier first fork at or before H_i.

On pairs within group i the output therefore factors through H_i, with all other sources zero. Its nonconstant part is read-once: for inputs of group i all paths to H_i are unbranched, and earlier branching inputs to its computation are zero. The final no-fork group is read-once by definition. All these group diagonals are consequently disjoint unions of bicliques under the physical singleton condition. For a row r in group i and an opposite column c in any later group, the output sees r only through H_i(r); c does not affect that wire. With c fixed it is a unary monotone function vanishing at H_i=0 since c alone is cold. Thus all cross edges from group-i rows to the union of later columns form a *single* biclique; the reverse orientation likewise contributes one. There are at most 2b cross bicliques across the whole partition. Every component that is not a biclique contains a cross edge, and each such biclique lies in one component. This proves the same bound directly.

## Exact binary size on the path family

Every one of the 4t flags must be read on the target consisting of exactly t disjoint P4 components, for t≥2. If an incident row flag were unread, its positive edge would be accepted on the column flag alone; by monotonicity that column would be universal, contradicting its zero entry in another component. The column argument is identical. The live fanout count therefore yields the absolute size lower 4t−1+ceil(t/2), and the even and odd constructions above attain it. The same absolute lower holds on every matrix containing that induced target by restriction, even when used raws outside the submatrix simplify away.
