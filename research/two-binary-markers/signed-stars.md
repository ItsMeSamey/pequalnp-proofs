# Dense signed cut offsets supported at few graph vertices

Let G be a finite undirected multigraph, let T be an even demand in each component, and let σ be an arbitrary binary vector on the edges. Seek a T-join J and a disjoint affine cut D=σ+δ(S) with a prescribed t-bit score A(J)+B(D), where edge contributions add by XOR. Put d=|T|. Suppose G has a set Z of h vertices such that σ restricted to G-Z is a cut. There is a deterministic polynomial-time witness algorithm for fixed h,d,t. The support size of σ modulo graph cuts can be unbounded.

Enumerate all candidate Z of size at most h. Consistency of the XOR equations on G-Z can be tested by a graph traversal, including loops. Extend a successful potential to the whole graph and switch σ by its cut, so σ vanishes on G-Z. This reparametrizes the unknown shore and leaves the set of actual affine cuts unchanged.

Consider a successful shore and retain exactly the edges on which σ+δ(S) vanishes. Its T-join space contains a unique join P on a chosen spanning forest, since each forest component has even demand. Each leaf of a nonempty component of P lies in T, so max degree in P is at most d (and P is empty for d=0). Its residual ordinary even sets are spanned by fundamental cycles. Choose at most t of those cycles to reproduce A(J)+A(P), by basis extraction on their t-bit scores. Their symmetric difference with P gives a successful join whose degree at each vertex is at most d+2t. A selected loop counts twice, as usual.

Enumerate the selected edges incident to Z, up to h(d+2t) edges, and the colors of Z. For each selected uv require s_u+s_v=σ_uv. This checks edges internal to Z including loops and prescribes the colors of selected neighbors outside Z. At vertices of Z verify that the degree parity contributed by the selected edges is exactly the prescribed demand. Remove Z and all its incident edges. On remaining vertices the residual demand T' is the restriction of T toggled by the boundary of the selected edges. Thus |T'|≤d+h(d+2t). The residual cut is now ordinary.

To score the full cut, set w_v equal to the XOR of all original B-weights at incident edge ends, counting loops twice. Its contribution is B(σ)+Σ_v s_v w_v. Subtract the guessed star-edge J score and the fixed shore contribution of Z; retain the weights w_v on the remaining vertices, including incident contributions from deleted edges. The ordinary fixed-demand, pinned-color graph oracle of the regular manuscript accepts arbitrary vertex shore weights: its proof compresses the shore outside the selected support using directly the vector span of these weights. Use the guessed neighbor colors as pins. This gives the residual test with O(h(d+2t)) pins. Every accepted witness extends by the guessed stars, and every original witness has a branch by the degree bound. Enumerating stars costs n^{O(h(d+2t)+h+1)} in an edge-list encoding. All remaining oracle parameters depend only on h,d,t.

### Weak deletion case

For a supplied cographic minor obtained by deleting k elements of a binary representation, fixing the deleted bits induces a dense affine cut on its real primal and an ordinary even subgraph on its real dual. This follows by choosing one real particular primal lift and adding a homogeneous real cycle. The dual constraints on the k deleted bits add at most k linear markers on the real even subgraph, by the matrix calculation in block-demands.md. Interchange graph-side primal and dual roles in the above construction and use d=0, marker dimension at most t+k. Each branch whose representative is switchable to a star cover of size h is therefore polynomial at fixed k,h,t. Duality gives a graphic contraction result. For a supplied planar graphic deletion minor one can use a planar dual graph to obtain the same statement.

The class contains a nonregular example even when k=1 and h=2. Take the incidence representation of K4 and append the column corresponding to the set of all four vertices. With one incidence row omitted the seven columns are precisely all nonzero vectors in F₂³, giving the Fano matroid. In the branch where the appended edge belongs to the primal join, choose a perfect matching as a reference T-join, then pass to the planar dual K4. The two negative dual edges form a perfect matching. Every triangle is odd, so deleting a single vertex cannot balance the signature, whereas deleting two suffices. More generally a signed graph can contain arbitrarily many edge-disjoint odd triangles sharing one vertex. Any representative of its switching class is nonzero on at least one edge of each triangle, although removing one vertex balances them all. Thus the vertex parameter is distinct from real-coordinate sparsity.

If using an edge-score-only implementation of the residual oracle, choose a root in each residual connected component. A tree edge takes the total vertex weight in its descendant subtree. The resulting edge cut score equals the vertex score plus the component total times the root color, by telescoping over the tree. In a pinned component root at a pinned vertex and deduct its known contribution. In an unpinned component, toggling its absolute color changes the vertex score by its component total without changing join feasibility or its edge cut. Project the target and scores modulo the span of those free totals, and recover the needed component flips by elimination.
