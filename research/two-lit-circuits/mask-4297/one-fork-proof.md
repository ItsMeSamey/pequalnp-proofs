# The five-flag size-six statement has a four-flag obstruction

For the official word 4297, set physical flag 2 to zero. Its use in any correct circuit is essential: adding it to singleton 0 changes a positive to a negative. Its first live reader thus folds away when it is pinned to zero. (Simplify ignored operands, constants, unary aliases and unused gates before counting.) A hypothetical six-gate circuit yields at most five gates on flags a=0,b=1,d=3,e=4. The positives among the ten nonempty inputs of weight at most two are exactly a, b, ad.

All four flags are essential. Adding a to d changes the answer from zero to one, while adding it to b changes the answer from one to zero. Adding b to a, d to b, or e to a changes the answer from one to zero. In particular a has both signs of influence even on the promise. In a connected binary/unary circuit using all four flags, at least three binary gates are needed. If there are three, the underlying graph is a tree, even with unary NOT gates. In a read-once tree with AND, OR and NOT each input has only one sign of influence. With no NOT the circuit is monotone. It follows that precisely four binary gates and one NOT are needed in any candidate. Counting incoming edges now gives one excess use beyond the one required for each nonoutput wire. Precisely one wire W has two uses; all other nonoutput wires have one.

Since a has opposite signs of influence, its two paths to the output must separate at W. The unique NOT lies on exactly one path after that separation: if it belonged to both paths or neither, varying a would have only one sign. The subtree producing W is therefore monotone and read-once. Denote its input set by S, including a. No raw in S has any other route to the output bypassing W. These statements also hold if W is a raw.

For the output to change when adding a to each of b and d, monotonicity of W forces W(b)=W(d)=0 and W(ab)=W(ad)=1. If b and d both belong to S, their singleton inputs have identical W and identical outside inputs, yet different required outputs. Thus at least one is outside S and W(a)=1. The positive a and negative ab, ae show b and e cannot belong to S: adding either within the subtree would leave W=1. If d belonged to S then b and bd would have identical W (zero) and identical outside inputs, again with different outputs. Thus S contains a alone, so the only fork is a.

Unfold this fork into two separate a leaves. The resulting formula has b,d,e once each, and each of those three variables has a negative influence. Their unique paths therefore all traverse the sole NOT. Exactly one occurrence of a lies below this NOT. With only a single remaining leaf outside the NOT, the output must have the shape a AND NOT(G) or a OR NOT(G). The first rejects singleton b. The second accepts ab. Neither computes the promised function. This excludes six gates for the original problem. The seven-gate upper is independent of the lower.
