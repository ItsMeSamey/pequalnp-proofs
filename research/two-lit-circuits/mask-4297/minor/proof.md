# Seven gates are necessary and sufficient for mask 4297

The accepted tests read by bit 5i+j are 00,03,11,12,22. Pin raw 2 false. This essential raw changes the accepted singleton 0 to rejected pair 02, so at least its first live reader simplifies. The remaining four raws a,b,d,e (original 0,1,3,4) have exactly three positive tests: a, b, ad. A six-gate circuit would give five gates for this minor. Constants, unused wires and unary aliases may be simplified.

All four raws are essential, witnessed respectively by d versus ad, a versus ab, b versus bd and a versus ae. Raw a increases the output at d and decreases it at b. Thus neither a monotone circuit nor a read-once formula is possible. With four essential inputs and B binary gates the total excess fanout is B−3 by edge counting in the live circuit. With five gates the only possibility is four binary gates and one negation: three binary gates give a read-once formula and five binary gates leave no negation. Exactly one wire W has fanout two, every other nonoutput wire fanout one. The expression for W is a tree on a subset S of raws, and the tree above W has two W leaves and one occurrence of each raw outside S. Both occurrences of a must have opposite path polarities, so a lies in S and the unique negation lies outside W. In particular W is monotone with value zero at the all-zero input.

The d versus ad and b versus ab witnesses force W(d)=W(b)=0 and W(ad)=W(ab)=1 by monotonicity. If d and b both belonged to S, their singleton tests would be indistinguishable at the output. At least one lies outside S; the corresponding pair equality gives W(a)=1. Neither b nor e can belong to S: adding either to a leaves W=1 while changing the output. If d belonged to S, adding d to b would leave W equal to zero while changing the output. Hence W=a.

Each of b,d,e has a decreasing witness and occurs only once in the outer tree. All must be under the sole negation. Exactly one of the two a occurrences is also under it. The other a is the only leaf outside, so the root is either a AND the negated subtree or a OR that subtree. The former rejects b; the latter accepts ab. This contradiction proves the lower bound.

On the original five inputs the seven-gate expression is NOT((e OR (d AND NOT a)) OR (a AND (b OR c))). The independent computer check twin_minor.py exhausts all single-fork possibilities and finds 323 distinct signatures, none the four-raw target.
