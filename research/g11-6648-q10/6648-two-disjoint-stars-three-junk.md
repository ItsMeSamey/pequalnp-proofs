# Two disjoint stars, no centreless tops and three junk ANDs

For the issued G11 let the original least 21-gate TopForm circuit have c=0,j=3 and precisely two starred edges ab,cd, which are disjoint. Then the surplus bound holds for every q. We eliminate every candidate: failure U=14>j+q forces q≤10. All counts are original physical gates. Write t for the ordinary tops and l for Lambda. The size identity gives q+2t+l=19. Every ordinary top has raw sides {a},{b} or {c},{d}, since no other nonempty complete bipartite cross is contained in this matching. Both groups are nonempty. Each group of k tops needs k distinct physical inlet functions on each endpoint: two clean terminals with a shared operand can be fused, saving one gate after regrouping the clean OR aggregation. Functional duplicates can be identified first by leastness. The original graph has minimum degree two, no four-cycle, and vertex-cover number seven.

**Exterior readers.** Outside the four starred endpoints, an accepted graph edge must activate some terminal through nonraw disjuncts in both OR-only pin cones. A positive term entering such a cone from an AND must be one of the three junk ANDs by TopForm. Raw-only pin terms are false on that edge. The two contributing dirty ANDs must be distinct: if the same gate OR-reached both pins, its independent positive witness would pass through the supposedly clean top. Thus every exterior edge activates at least two distinct junk gates. If two junk functions each implied a raw variable then at least one of those two variables would be present on every exterior edge. These two vertices together with the four endpoints would cover G11, contrary to cover number seven. In particular, at most one junk AND has a direct raw pin.

There are seven relevant exterior raw variables. Each with one physical reader has its own private reader among the exterior reader set. A sole AND with another raw pin could not distinguish its at least two distinct singleton neighbors upon toggling the sole variable. For a sole OR v∨B, B is independent of v and rejects all singletons: otherwise an active singleton u masks v, forcing vu a nonedge and u adjacent to every neighbor of v, a four-cycle. Thus such a sole OR cannot have another raw pin. Vertices with multiple readers have at least two incidences each and every remaining binary reader has capacity two. This proves at least seven distinct direct exterior readers. None is an ordinary AND or a Lambda OR since either would star its exterior label. At most one is a dirty AND as above, so at least six are paid ORs.

**Endpoint ledger.** For each endpoint v trace all ordinary v-pins back along OR paths through raw-positive pins. All positive wires in this trace have raw support exactly {v}. Let m_v count its ORs with one raw-empty operand. These paid gates are disjoint across v and disjoint from the six exterior ORs (a raw exterior input would add its label to raw support). All two-positive ORs of the trace are Lambda: the opposite side of their witnessing ordinary top is a singleton. Conversely all Lambda gates lie in these four traces; write l_v for their counts. Every signal in a trace is v OR a subset of the m_v imported raw-empty functions. This follows by union along OR gates and allows imports depending on earlier wires. Distinct physical outputs realize distinct full-cube functions by leastness. Counting the raw input, m_v import gate outputs and l_v two-positive joins gives l_v≤2^{m_v}−1−m_v. A group of k tops at uv requires k≤2^{min(m_u,m_v)}. In particular m=sum m_v≤q−6.

For q≤9, either m would be negative or m≤3. The possible import partitions give 2t+l≤8. With all three on one endpoint, both groups have at most one root and l≤4. With a 2+1 split, t≤3 and l≤1. With 1+1+1, t≤3 and l=0. Fewer imports cannot increase these bounds. Hence q+2t+l≤17, contradicting 19.

For q=10, m≤4 and 2t+l=9. There are at least two groups, and l≥0 gives t≤4. If t=2 then l=5. With m≤4 and at least two nonzero m_v, the largest possible l is four (3+1); with all imports at one label, every trace function is v plus a subset of the THREE original dirty AND functions, even when an imported signal is itself an OR of multiple dirty wires. Thus at m=4 on one label, l≤8−1−4=3; at m≤3 the earlier bound gives l≤4. This rules out t=2 without the low-false AND floor. At t=3 the group sizes are 2 and 1, l=3. A Lambda split 2+1 needs at least five imports; 1+1+1 needs six. All three must be at one repeated endpoint, since on a singleton-group endpoint they would require three imports plus at least one at each repeated-group endpoint. Thus the repeated group has m counts (3,1), the single group (0,0). At t=4 and l=1 the 2+2 group pattern needs one import at every endpoint and a second at the Lambda label, impossible. The 3+1 pattern needs two imports at each repeated-group endpoint, leaving none at the others. Thus in both survivors m=4, all l are at repeated-group endpoints, and the singleton group cd physically equals the pure AND c∧d.

**Saturation.** Four endpoint imports and six exterior ORs exhaust q=10. The exterior reader set of seven therefore consists of six ORs and one dirty AND, call the latter W. The reader-count equality means every multiple-reader exterior vertex has exactly two readers, and each such gate reads two distinct multiple-reader exterior variables directly; otherwise the capacity bound is strict. Private sole-reader gates contain only their own exterior variable and no second raw input as above. No private readership of the AND is needed. For every pre-top wire, consider its original raw OR support restricted to the seven exterior labels. A raw input has at most one such label. Every AND output has empty raw support. Every Lambda or paid endpoint import has singleton endpoint support, hence empty exterior support. Of the six exterior ORs, a private sole reader has a singleton-negative sibling (zero raw support), hence singleton exterior support, and any reader of multiply-read vertices has two raw exterior pins. Thus every pre-top exterior support has at most two labels. This argument permits a shared two-raw AND under the incidence counting relaxation.

Take the six actual operand wires of the three junk ANDs, retaining multiplicities. Their exterior raw supports give six sets of size at most two. For each of the seven exterior labels v let its code be the set of positions containing v. Distinct labels must have incomparable codes. Indeed if code(v)⊆code(u) with u≠v then on ANY input containing u, toggling v does not change the raw OR disjunction on any junk AND input (other raw labels, including endpoints, are fixed). By gate order the values of all junk ANDs stay unchanged: each input wire to a junk AND is the OR of its raw labels and earlier junk AND outputs along its OR frontier; clean AND ancestry is impossible. The ordinary top pins have only endpoint raw labels and those same unchanged junk alternatives. Hence the output is unchanged. Comparing singleton u with uv makes uv a nonedge; for each of the two or more neighbors n of v, comparing un with uvn forces un an edge. This gives a four-cycle. Every exterior code is therefore nonempty and incomparable to each other code.

Six sets of size at most two cannot encode seven incomparable labels. Charge a singleton set to each label that occurs in such a set. Every other label occurs at least twice: if it occurred only in one two-set, the other member's code would dominate its code. The remaining two-sets have at most two incidences each, so at least as many of those sets as remaining labels are needed. At least seven positions in total would be required; there are only six. This finishes q=10 and the theorem. No claim is made here about the adjacent two-edge pattern, for which the independent adjacent-case proof is required.

The argument uses the original size-21 identity and the stated graph properties. It does not require the accepted six-AND lower floor. It uses no numerical mask enumeration, projected leastness, or solver.
