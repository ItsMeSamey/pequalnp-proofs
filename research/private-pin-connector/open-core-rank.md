# A rank obstruction to open cross-shore equality

Let a bipartite graph have arbitrary guarded private vertices and two sets of open data vertices on its shores. A guarded selected vertex requires even selected degree. Open data vertices impose no guard. The combined affine score target is H_L x + H_R y = t over F2. Assume the all-zero data assignment extends to some private witness. Suppose every nonzero assignment supported entirely on either one of the two data shores has no extension.

If the two data shores have sizes m and n, then m+n ≤ rank H_L + rank H_R. In particular four arbitrary markers cannot implement more than four cross-shore equalities with both endpoints open, even if all private vertices interact. Two opposite literal pins and two further mixed rows give at most three. Two literal pins alone give at most one, attained by the six-cycle in README.md.

Choose a zero-data witness of minimum private Hamming weight. Let it use a left and b right private vertices. Fix the right side and vary only the a originally selected private left vertices and all m left data bits. A selected private left vertex continues to see its original even selected neighborhood. The only guard equations to impose are the b selected right guards, linear in the variable left bits. The score contributes at most rank H_L further independent linear equations. Nullity of the resulting variation space is at least a+m−b−rank H_L. Its projection to the left data coordinates is injective: a nonzero variation with zero data simply drops at least one selected private vertex and contradicts minimality. Thus the left-only attainable data projection actually contains a linear space of dimension at least max(0,a+m−b−rank H_L). The exclusion hypothesis forces a+m≤b+rank H_L. The opposite shore gives b+n≤a+rank H_R. Addition proves the claim. The proof also applies with affine offsets in private guard neighbor sums.

More generally if the dimensions of the largest linear spaces of attainable pure-left and pure-right data are d_L,d_R, then m+n≤rank H_L+rank H_R+d_L+d_R by the same fixed witness. This is a lower bound on unavoidable pure-shore leakage in any proposed mixed interface.

The theorem concerns individual unguarded data bits. It does not rule out a simulation using ports whose own guards participate in computation, or wires with nonlinear encodings.

The two-pin-plus-two and unrestricted-four bounds are sharp: the six-cycle handles one port pair with the two pins, and two additional equations each impose equality of another isolated pair. Four general rows simply impose four isolated pairwise equalities. The rank proof extends to any nonempty relation that is single-valued in both shore directions (the graph of a partial injection). Minimize private weight among witnesses extending one chosen accepted tuple. Any pure-shore output change violates the relation; affine variations with fixed output still reduce private weight. No zero tuple is necessary in this variant with fully open ports.

For a semi-open bank at a zero-data witness, let f_L,f_R count guarded data vertices having odd selected neighbor parity on the fixed opposite shore (or not satisfying the affine neighbor predicate). The remaining data vertices on that shore can be varied in exactly the same argument. Thus exclusion of pure-shore nonzero assignments implies m+n≤rank H_L+rank H_R+f_L+f_R. Select a private-weight-minimizing zero-data witness to apply this necessary test. Every escape from the open bound must supply sufficient ineligible guarded data coordinates.

In particular the number of individual data endpoints allowed to remain open is at most rank H_L+rank H_R, no matter how many additional guarded endpoints and private vertices are present. Thus even a bank with just one open endpoint per independent wire has at most eight wires under four arbitrary mixed rows, or six under two opposite literal pins and two further rows. Every larger implementation must consume both endpoint guards for all but this bounded number of pairs.
