# A forced two-port current in rooted guarded interfaces

Let a bipartite guarded graph have two distinct fixed vertices of opposite shores. Its output ports lie on one shore, and every other data input is held at zero. Suppose it admits an all-zero-output witness and excludes every closed, singleton-output witness. For every two distinct outputs u,v there is a state with exactly these two outputs one, every selected private vertex of even degree, and both u,v of odd degree. The proof permits arbitrary interactions among private vertices. The data ports are treated as boundary vertices only when defining this current-producing state.

For nonadjacent pins their two-vertex set is a valid baseline. Every output must be adjacent to the opposite pin, or adding that isolated output would be legal. Select both pins and u,v; the opposite pin has degree two and the selected data ports have degree one.

For adjacent pins s,t, the all-zero-output witness induces an even graph containing st. Hence st belongs to a cycle. A shortest alternate s–t path in that witness, together with st, is an induced cycle K containing both pins. Each output x has exactly one neighbor on K. If there are none, K plus x gives a closed singleton. With at least two, take the extreme two neighbors in the linear order of K minus edge st. The arc between them containing st, plus x, is an induced rooted cycle, again a closed singleton. With the unique neighbors of u,v now established, if they coincide use K plus both ports. Otherwise take the arc through st between their neighbors and add the ports as endpoints. Since their neighbors lie on the same shore they are nonadjacent; the arc is induced and includes both pins. All selected private vertices have even degree and each selected port has degree one.

For one literal pin instead of two, the same output hypotheses force each opposite-shore port to be adjacent to it simply by testing the pin-plus-port set. Choosing the pin plus u,v yields the same conclusion.

Consequently a two-pin or one-pin arbitrary lower network cannot be composed as a black box into the asserted chordless-cycle conjunction of wide-and.md via its same-shore outputs. Assume no other cross edges between modules. Raise just the first and last output ports along the upper cycle besides its pinned anchor. Use the arc from the last output to the first through the pinned root edge, selecting no other upper vertex and no leaf. Each chosen port receives one unit of upper current, canceling its odd lower current. All actual guards and both upper pins now pass. With two distinct lower pins this uses exactly four literal rows. If the upper leaf checksum is retained, its value is automatically correct because the output is one and all leaves are zero; with a single lower pin this likewise uses four rows. This disproves generic truth-table composition under the stated lower zero-input hypotheses. A general existential SAT reduction and a lower network with extra nonliteral score constraints fall outside the theorem.
