## Single readers and the false cofactor

In a minimum binary AND/OR circuit every gate reaches the output. If a gate always computes an earlier available wire, all its consumers can be redirected to that wire. Under an input restriction, process gates in topological order, replace restricted operands with symbolic constants, and omit any gate whose restricted function equals its remaining operand or a constant. The nonconstant graph output survives as an ordinary wire. This produces an exact, backward two-input circuit with the appropriate relabeling.

Let x=3. In a minimum size-20 TopForm circuit the restriction x=false can delete only one gate, since its graph has the accepted lower bound 19. Every direct reader of x disappears on this restriction. At least one exists because the graph depends on x, and a raw x output would not compute the graph. A sole AND reader becomes false and cannot be the output, as the cofactor is nonconstant. Its consumer then disappears as well. Thus the unique reader is L=x∨w. Absorption and backward order ensure that w is independent of x.

For any degree-at-least-two vertex v with a sole OR reader v∨W, W rejects all singleton inputs. A singleton neighbor activating W would make the edge from v indistinguishable from that singleton. For a nonneighbor b distinct from v, if W accepts singleton b, then every neighbor of v must be adjacent to b, since toggling v on the pair consisting of b and that neighbor cannot change its reader. This gives two distinct common neighbors and a four-cycle. Singleton v cannot activate W because W is independent of v and the circuit uses no positive constants.

W must be dirty, meaning it accepts an independent vertex set. Otherwise W is clean and, as a singleton-negative nonraw wire, has a clean AND in its cone. For a clean OR trace to a clean child, since each child implies the graph function, until reaching an AND. Raw inputs are singleton-positive. The wire v∨W accepts singleton v, which the graph rejects, and it is live. Some AND must occur downstream, since an OR-only path would propagate that singleton to the output. The two ANDs would violate TopForm. Given an independent positive witness for W, remove v to obtain an independent positive witness T for W without v. T must avoid N(v), since toggling v would otherwise change the correct output without changing its reader. For every u in N(v), T must contain a neighbor of u; otherwise T together with u is independent and toggling v changes the correct output. In particular neither 6 nor 7 can have a sole OR reader: for either vertex its neighbor 3 is adjacent only to 6 and 7, both outside T. For v=3 the independent T therefore avoids 3,6,7.
