# Preliminary size-20 exclusion for a least TopForm circuit, for audit

Write x=3, y=6, z=7. Assume a 20-gate least TopForm realization. Use the established single-port reduction: x has sole reader L=x∨w, w is singleton-negative, w has an independent positive witness avoiding x,y,z, and neither y nor z can have a sole OR reader. These assertions use TopForm (a sole dirty sibling's independent positive set must dominate the neighbors while avoiding the closed neighborhood). In particular the conclusion here concerns circuits satisfying that reduction.

For reference the lower bounds on the x=true cofactor and its subsequent y=z=false cofactor are 17 and 15. If the first cofactor leaves exactly 17 gates then its essential free OR variables y,z each have exactly one OR reader: fixing either false leaves the other free over the 15-gate core, requiring at least 16. A sole AND reader would make its next reader redundant also. No essential raw input can be unread. Leastness makes every original gate live and forbids an input gate comparable by implication to its other operand when the operation then equals an existing wire.

L has at least one reader. Three readers already remove four gates when x=true. If two readers, both must be AND: a true OR child requires deletion of another downstream gate, unless its only reader is the other direct L reader, in which case that latter gate combines L and a wire implied by L in the direction L ≤ L∨b, an absorbable pair. Thus exactly L and its two AND children are removed, giving 17. Both y and z must occur on deleted gates, since all their other original readers survive with their original type and each original coordinate cannot have sole OR. Neither occurs in L, so the children are L∧y and L∧z. A surviving original direct reader of y would have to be the very same reader that consumes the bypassed child L∧y, as the 17-gate cofactor has just one y-reader. That would originally be an absorbable pair. The two children cannot read one another since they already have two operands. Thus y originally has the sole AND reader y∧L, impossible because L is false on singleton z, while yz is an edge. This proves L has one reader.

If its unique reader R is OR, the x=true reduction deletes L,R and the next reader. R has exactly one next reader and it must be AND, or an extra deletion occurs. (A forced-true output is impossible.) Write that AND as R∧P. P is independent of x, as R is its sole route from x to the output. It must be positive on singleton y and singleton z since the original output distinguishes xy from y and xz from z. P is nonraw. Among L,R,R∧P at most one of y,z occurs as a direct input (one free slot on R). Every original reader of the other variable therefore survives with type preserved in a 17-gate circuit, contradicting no sole OR. Thus L has unique reader A=L∧P. The same singleton argument gives P(y)=P(z)=1, P independent of x, and P nonraw.

Only raw x and L can be positive on singleton x: all paths from x to any other gate pass through A, which is false on singleton x since P is independent of x. Neither y nor z can have sole AND reader, as its other input would have to be true at x and at the other triangle vertex. Neither x nor L satisfies both. Together with the sole OR obstruction, each of y and z has at least two distinct original direct readers.

Project x=true, bypass A by the original nonraw P and remove L,A, keeping every other gate if desired, even dead ones. Then set y=z=false. Let U be the union of the distinct ORIGINAL direct readers of y,z. No such reader is L,A. Their raw operands remain present after the first projection. Every gate in U is eliminated by setting y=z=false, even if one is an ancestor of another (a binary gate with a constant input can be folded). The 15 lower bound gives |U|≤3. If h reads both y and z, then h is forced false. Every reader of h in the intermediate circuit outside U gives one additional gate removable beyond U, independently of other reconvergences. With |U|=3 no such reader is possible.

To cover |U|=2 explicitly, both gates would have to read both y,z, and leastness allows only one OR and one AND. At least one of them differs from P. Every original reader of that gate survives in the intermediate circuit (it cannot be L since w has its independent witness, nor A since it differs from P). None can lie in U since both U gates already read raw y,z. More directly: the AND and OR are the only channels for both y,z, so the realized function is invariant under swapping y,z; in the graph y is adjacent to 10 and z is not. Thus |U|=3.

A common reader h exists. If h differs from P, it must have an original reader (it cannot equal output by the graph). Its reader cannot be L (independent witness), or A. Any remaining original reader is also an intermediate reader and thus in U. But a gate combining h=y⋆z with y or z is absorbable by comparison, contrary to original leastness. Thus every shared reader equals P, hence P=y∨z by singleton positivity. There is one other y-reader Ry and one other z-reader Rz. By the same argument P's only original reader is A. P cannot be the original output. A also cannot be the original output, since edges avoiding y,z exist. Every original reader of A reads P in the intermediate circuit and therefore belongs to U. Its only possibilities are Ry and Rz.

If A has one reader, say Ry, use y∨(L∧(y∨z))=y∨(L∧z) in the OR case, and y∧(L∧(y∨z))=y∧L in the AND case. The first rewrite deletes P, the second deletes A,P; all other original uses are preserved. The z case is symmetric.

If both Ry,Rz read A, all outgoing raw y,z edges go to P (used solely by A), Ry, or Rz, with the latter two descendants of A. Neither y nor z can influence w, which precedes A. On every graph edge containing y or z, w is false by singleton negativity and independence from y,z. On all remaining graph edges P is false. Consequently A agrees with x∧P on every edge minterm. Replace all uses of L by x, deleting L. The circuit remains true on all graph minterms and globally decreases, which proves equality to the original monotone graph function. No assumption on external fanout of w, descendants of either R, or raw cofactors elsewhere is needed.

This is a mathematical argument, not a compiled certificate. It presumes the stated dirtiness/sole OR lemma and graph-specific lower bounds.

## Single-port prerequisites in detail

For any vertex v with at least two neighbors, a sole OR reader v∨b has b independent of v. If b is positive on a singleton t, toggling v cannot change the graph value whenever t is present. First vt must be a nonedge. Taking each neighbor n of v in turn shows nt must be an edge, producing a four-cycle through two neighbors. Thus b rejects singletons. The wire b is nonraw. If b were clean (every true assignment contains a graph edge), then either b is a clean AND or, by recursively expanding a clean OR into its two clean children, b has a clean AND ancestor. No raw input is clean. The OR reader v∨b has a downstream AND because the output rejects singleton v. The resulting clean-AND to strictly later AND path contradicts TopForm. Therefore b accepts an independent set. Remove v from that set since b is independent of v. The set excludes every neighbor of v by the same toggling argument; moreover every neighbor must touch the set, or adjoining that neighbor would create an independent positive assignment on which toggling v changes the graph value. For v=y or z its neighbor x has no other neighbor outside N[v], ruling out any sole OR reader. For v=x the witness avoids x,y,z as asserted. Finally the x=false lower bound 19 forces exactly one original reader in a 20-gate least circuit, since all such readers are removed. A sole AND reader is impossible since its false output eliminates one further reader and the graph contains edges omitting x. This gives the claimed sole OR L.
