# One charged OR, clean sole AND, nonempty visible operand

Let A=3∧S be clean and the sole reader of 3. Suppose S is impure and has nonempty raw support, necessarily contained in {6,7} by cleanliness. S is true at both singletons 6 and 7 by the sole-port argument. There is exactly one charged OR, P. An AND entering S's OR cone is dirty by TopForm. The OR that directly reads it is dirty and non-Lambda, hence is P. Both inputs of P cannot be raw-empty: a subsequent OR toward raw-positive S would be another charge. Its pins are a dirty AND D and a nonempty pure raw disjunction R. Replacing P by R projects all top operands to pure conjunctions of raw disjunctions, preserves original raw supports, and leaves every evaluation unchanged wherever D is false. The replacement only decreases signals, preserving cleanliness. Any nonsingleton pure factor has its original Lambda anchor; when a primitive boundary was P, R supplies the same raw support and is itself Lambda if nonsingleton. Thus D-false residual edges can be counted by the original centreless tops, at most two per top by the degree-four anchored matching lemma. Ordinary projected tops only accept original star edges. It suffices to bound D-positive original residuals by four, as j≥3.

Let H=G11−{3,6,7}. D is clean on H because adjoining 3 to an H-independent set preserves independence and would activate clean A if D were true. Before the sole charge, D is a conjunction of pure raw primitive factors, all nonsingleton factors anchored by original stars. Its accepted original residuals on H consequently form an induced matching of at most two edges by the degree-four lemma.

**Full support.** Both 36 and 37 are starred. The other edges outside H are 67, 6–10 and 7–9. If D accepted all three and two residual H-edges, every primitive would hit both disjoint H-edges, requiring at least two distinct H-vertices. To hit the three outside edges the same factor must also contain 6 and 7, 6 and 9, or 7 and 10. Their only common neighbors are respectively 3,7,6, with zero, one and one H-neighbors respectively. No such factor can be anchored. At most four D-positive residuals exist.

**Visible 6 only.** The case of visible 7 follows by exchanging the roles of 6,7 and 9,10 in these neighborhood counts. Since the singleton 7 activates S but has no raw path to S, it activates D. Every primitive therefore contains 7. If two disjoint residual H-edges were accepted, every factor would also contain at least two H-endpoints. Of the neighbors 3,6,9 of 7 only 9 has two H-neighbors. Thus all factors are anchored at 9. Every endpoint of either H-edge occurs in some factor, since its mate singleton is rejected but the edge is accepted. This would place four distinct vertices in N(9)∩H={0,5,8}. Consequently at most one residual H-edge is accepted. If any H-edge and 6–10 were both accepted, every factor would contain 7 and either 6 or 10. The common neighbor of 7 and 6 is 3, which cannot reach H. The common neighbor of 7 and 10 is 6, whose only H-neighbor is 10. Every factor would consequently contain 10, making D true at the H-singleton 10. This is impossible. Outside H, 36 is starred and only 37,67,6–10,7–9 remain. At most four D-positive residual edges exist in all. For the exchanged case, N(10)∩H={0,2,4}.

The two groups together give U≤2c+4≤2c+j+1, and the issued balance gives |X|≤2c+|J|+1. This does not require any uniqueness of readers of S. The argument is source-level and uncompiled.
