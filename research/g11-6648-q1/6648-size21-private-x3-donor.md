# A private donor at the sole OR port of vertex 3

Assume a least 21-gate TopForm circuit, x=3, y=6, z=7, x's sole reader L=x∨w, and w used only by L. The graph has edges xy,xz,yz and different external neighbors for y,z. The independent-dominator lemma gives a singleton-negative w with an independent positive assignment avoiding x,y,z and excludes original sole OR ports for y and z. Use the established true-card lower bound 17 and core floor 15.

At x=true delete w as dead in addition to every gate removed by ordinary forward propagation. Its own type is unrestricted. It has no y or z raw pin: an OR with such a pin is singleton-positive and an AND is false on the independent witness. It is distinct from L, from every descendant of L, and from every independent sibling used directly by a descendant of L (private readership). Keep its ancestors indexed even when dead; lower bounds apply after pruning, hence to this possibly unpruned gate inventory. When the inventory hits 17 for the true card or 15 for its y=z=false restriction, all remaining gates must be live. These facts provide the same three-gate and five-gate forward-deletion budgets as for the size-20 proof, at x=true and at (x,y,z)=(1,0,0) respectively. No bonus deletion at x=false is claimed.

Here are details of the transfer from 6648-size20-three-reader.md. Three direct readers of L remove four gates including L. Two direct readers must be two ANDs: an OR propagates true to an extra reader, and a reader combining L with its OR-child is absorbable. The resulting 17-gate true card has unique OR readers of y,z. Both raw variables must therefore occur in the deleted AND guards. A guard L∧y forwarding y into a surviving reader rules out any other surviving direct raw y reader: the unique reader would otherwise combine L∧y with y already in the original. The second guard L∧z cannot consume the first, and w has no raw y pin. The sole remaining original y port L∧y fails on yz since L rejects singleton z. Thus L has a unique reader. If it is OR, propagation forces one more sole reader of type AND (the output is not constant). Its sibling must accept both neighbor singletons to distinguish xy and xz, so is nonraw. At most one of y,z occurs on the three deleted gates other than w, again contradicting the two variables' lack of sole original ORs in a 17-gate true card.

Consequently L has sole reader A=L∧P, with x-independent nonraw P accepting both y,z singletons. Only x and L are true at singleton x. Hence a sole AND reader of y would lack a sibling true at both singleton x and singleton z, and similarly for z. Each variable has at least two direct readers. At x=true keep the 18 gates remaining after deleting L,A,w and forwarding P. The union U of original raw y,z readers is untouched, including its types and raw pins, and |U|≤3 by the core floor. If |U|=2, both gates would have raw pins y,z and the original function would be symmetric in them. Thus |U|=3. Any common reader h=y⋆z becomes false upon fixing y=z=false, so every reader of h in the 18-gate card lies in U. An original reader in U is impossible by absorption.

The extra recipient requiring inspection is w. If h=y∧z then h is a clean AND and cannot feed w upstream of the later AND A by TopForm. If h=y∨z, an OR reader w contradicts singleton rejection and an AND reader w contradicts its independent positive witness avoiding y,z. Thus h cannot feed w. It cannot feed L directly or be output. Therefore h=P=y∨z, used only by A. The other raw readers are Ry and Rz. Every original reader of A survives in the true card (w is earlier) and there reads P, so is Ry or Rz. If only Ry reads A, simplify y∨[L∧(y∨z)] to y∨(L∧z), or y∧[L∧(y∨z)] to y∧L, saving a gate; interchange the labels if needed. If both read A, all raw y,z paths go through P (private to A) or those two descendants of A. Thus neither y nor z can influence w. On every graph edge incident to y or z the donor is false by singleton negativity; on every other edge P is false. Replacing L by x preserves all positive edge minterms and only decreases the output elsewhere, saving a gate. This proves the claimed exclusion under the private-donor hypothesis. No conclusion about externally reused w follows.
