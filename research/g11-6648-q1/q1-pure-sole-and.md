# The clean sole AND with a physical neighbor disjunction

Assume the size-21 least TopForm case of 6648, with one charged OR, at least one centreless top, and sole reader A=x AND S for x=3, where S is physically the OR of y=6 and z=7. Use the 19-card common-reader classification in team 03's 6648-size21-sole-and.md. After deleting x, S has one reader B=S OR W. This gate is present before restriction: the removed OR reader of A cannot have supplied B's other input, since A is clean and dirty B has a downstream AND, contrary to TopForm. All counts below refer to the original circuit.

B is true at singleton y. Both y and z reach it by raw OR paths. If B were in Lambda, the anchoring vertex would neighbor both y and z, and therefore equal their unique common neighbor x. Only A can have x on a raw side because x's sole reader is A. B cannot reach the other side S of A because S precedes B. Thus B is the unique charged OR, regardless of W. A strict OR-only descendant would inherit both singleton positivity and both raw paths, and would be another charged OR. An OR directly reading any dirty AND would also be charged because that input has empty raw support.

Call a clean top affected if B is one of its ancestors. Every path from B to such a top consists only of AND gates after B. Indeed any first OR would either directly read B or would read a dirty AND, since TopForm forbids intervening clean ANDs. If an affected top has an AND input along such a path, it is centreless. If it directly reads B, a vertex on its opposite raw side would have to neighbor y and z by cleanliness (singleton rejection also forces the opposite side disjoint). This vertex could only be x, whose sole reader is A, again impossible. All affected tops are therefore centreless.

Expand all ANDs below an affected top down to B and pure raw disjunctions, treating primary inputs as singleton clauses. There can be no clean AND strictly below a top, and no OR other than B that reads a dirty AND. Every nonsingleton pure boundary clause is positive at a singleton, hence in Lambda since the unique charged OR is B. Its original ordinary-star anchor supplies all required original star edges. On replacing B throughout by W, W itself admits the same expansion, since it precedes B. Distinct paths that share a signal can reuse its value. Multiple occurrences of B in a single conjunction can be collapsed by idempotence.

The original affected top K is thus B AND L. With W substituted it becomes W AND L, a clean conjunction of anchored primitive clauses, since substitution only decreases its output. By the induced-matching and maximum-degree-four argument of team 03's singleton_6648_q1_sole_or_all_c.md, it accepts at most two original residual edges. The remaining original positives of K require S, since B=S OR W, and all such graph pairs contain y or z. The original A stars xy and xz. The only other graph edges meeting either vertex are yz, y–10, and z–9. This bounds the total extra residuals across every affected top by three.

An unaffected ordinary top has pure raw-disjunction inputs: it cannot have an AND on an input's OR-only boundary without either a direct AND pin (centreless), a clean AND ancestor (TopForm), or an OR directly reading a dirty AND (only B). Its positive pairs are original star edges. Each unaffected centreless top has the same anchored conjunction expansion as before and accepts at most two original residual pairs. Every edge activates some clean top by clean output aggregation. Consequently U ≤ 2c+3 ≤ j+2c+1 for the issued j≥3. This is an analytical branch proof conditional on the 19-card classification and the published anchored-clause lemma.
