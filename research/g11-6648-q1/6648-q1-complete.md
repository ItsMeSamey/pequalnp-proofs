# L-meta-6648 with one exceptional OR

For the issued 11-vertex graph, a least TopForm circuit of size at most 21 with j≥3 and q=1 satisfies U≤j+2c+1, for every number c of centreless tops. Here U counts the original Ustar edges. The selected cardinality balance then gives |X|≤|J|+2c+1. The claim does not require strengthening the issued conditions on intersections with gammaE.

Use the published G11 gate lower bound to obtain size 21. In the graph with x=3 removed, the AO lower bound is 19. Consequently x has at most two distinct direct readers. If there are two, neither can read the other by absorption. A direct AND would force a third deletion when x=false, because it cannot be the restricted nonconstant output and must have another reader. The two-OR case is excluded by 6648-x3-double-or-exclusion.md. Hence x has exactly one reader. If c=0 the already accepted L18392 also closes this window; the following proof covers the possible reader cases.

The graph has maximum degree four and no four-cycle. Throughout, a clean conjunction of raw disjunctions with original Lambda star anchors on all its nonsingleton primitive supports accepts at most two original residual edges. First the accepted residuals form an ambient induced matching, by the anchored-clause argument in singleton_6648_q1_sole_or_all_c.md. If it accepted three edges, each of their eight one-endpoint transversals would miss a clause containing the three other endpoints. Transversals differing in one choice would have clause anchors with two common neighbors, forcing the anchors equal. The common anchor of all eight clauses would have six distinct neighbors. This also applies to restricted graphs using original anchors and the original degree bound. All counts on projected circuits use the original starEdges and centreless tops, so neither minimality nor TopForm needs to be transferred to a projection.

In the clean case write A=x∧S. Its sibling S is independent of x, accepts the two singletons 6 and 7, and has raw OR support contained in {6,7}. When S is physically a raw disjunction, it is the wire 6∨7 by leastness. In the 19-gate x=false circuit this wire has a single remaining reader B=S∨W; see 6648-size21-sole-and.md for the common-reader derivation using H8≥15. This B is the exceptional OR, since every possible Lambda anchor would have to be x and B cannot reach A. Each affected top is centreless. Distribute its AND expansion over the S and W alternatives at B. The W terms and all unaffected centreless tops each have the two-edge anchored bound, and the S terms add at most the three remaining triangle-attachment edges (6,7),(6,10),(7,9) globally. The two x-edges are already starred.

If S is impure and raw-positive, a dirty AND enters its final OR cone through the unique exceptional OR P=D∨R, with R raw-positive and pure. On any pair where D is false, P evaluates to R; this counts at most two residuals per original centreless top and only original stars at ordinary tops. The nonsingleton raw clauses of D are anchored and its conjunction is clean on the subgraph H8 deleting x,6,7: an independent H8 witness could be extended by x to activate A. The degree-four lemma therefore allows at most two D-positive residuals inside H8. If both neighbors are raw-visible, only three outside edges remain potentially residual. These three and two H8 residuals cannot all activate D: every primitive would contain two H8 vertices and a vertex cover of the three outside edges among {6,7}, {6,9}, {7,10}. The unique anchors of these pairs have at most one neighbor in H8. Thus at most four D-positive residuals occur. See q1-clean-impure.md for the singleton-visible orientation check.

If the raw support of S omits a neighbor, the standalone omitted-support proof accounts for an AND at S itself and for an exceptional OR that has two dirty input pins. Expand S through its upper ANDs. Any primitive disjunction must accept both neighbor singletons. A pure anchored primitive would need the unique common neighbor x as an anchor and would then star the omitted x-edge, impossible. Leastness and the one-charge restriction therefore force the unique exceptional OR to occur in every primitive unless S is itself a pure disjunction, already treated. For a raw-positive exceptional OR, its dirty AND input is positive at precisely one of the two singletons; its paired Lambda anchor restrictions bound the additional residuals by four. For a raw-empty exceptional OR, S is that OR, with two dirty AND donors. Their anchored restrictions account for at most five residuals jointly, while A itself is centreless and need not be charged again, giving U≤5+2(c−1). The full dependency proof supplies the side conditions, including why a pure exceptional OR cannot be the missing raw support.

Since j≥3, all bounds above imply U≤2c+j+1. The reasoning is mathematical and does not have a combined Lean kernel receipt. It covers q=1 under the original G11 assumptions; q>1 is a separate obligation.
