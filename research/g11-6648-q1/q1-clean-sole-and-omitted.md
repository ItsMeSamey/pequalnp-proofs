# One charged OR and a clean sole AND with an omitted neighbor

Use the issued G11, write x=3, y=6, z=7, and let A=x∧S be the only reader of x. Assume A is clean, the circuit is least and TopForm, q=1, and at least one of y,z is absent from the raw OR-reachable support of S. Write c for the centreless count and j for the junk count. We prove U≤2c+j+1 when j≥3.

S is independent of x and true on both singleton y and singleton z, by sensitivity of xy and xz. S is clean on the restriction y=z=false: an independent positive input there could be stripped of x and then extended by x without creating a graph edge, contradicting cleanliness of A. The raw support of S lies in {y,z}, by singleton rejection of the graph. The omitted incident edge is not starred because the sole raw path from x to an AND input ends at A. The only common neighbor of y,z is x. Consequently no Lambda support can contain both y and z: its anchor would have to star both xy and xz. Likewise x cannot anchor any nonsingleton raw support containing y or z: its only possible ordinary top is A, with opposite raw support of size at most one.

**Why every primitive receives the unique charged OR.** Expand the output S through ANDs to primitive boundaries (OR outputs or raw inputs), preserving shared gates as needed. Each boundary must accept BOTH singletons because their conjunction S does. All AND ancestors of A are dirty, by TopForm. Any OR directly reading such an AND is dirty and charged, since the AND pin has empty raw support. Denote the unique charge by Q. If no impure OR occurs below or at S, each primitive is a pure raw disjunction containing y and z. It cannot be raw; its output is dirty on a singleton and cannot be Lambda by the anchor argument. Every such physical root must therefore be Q itself, including any OR above another OR that still contains both vertices. Hence S computes Q. If S and Q are distinct this violates leastness (substitute the earlier wire everywhere and remove the duplicate). If S=Q as a wire, its raw support contains both vertices, contrary to omission. This also excludes a pure unanchored Q located outside the relevant cone.

Thus Q occurs below or at S and is an impure OR directly reading a dirty AND. A pure primitive would now require a *different* charge by the preceding paragraph. Every primitive is therefore impure; following OR-only paths in that primitive to a last AND exhibits the unique Q. In particular Q implies EVERY primitive, and hence Q≤S. This implication holds even when S is an AND of distinct expressions Q∨R_i. It does not assume those expressions are identical or that S=Q.

**Donor counting fact.** Let D be a dirty AND directly feeding Q and positive on a singleton v∈{y,z}. Its AND expansion has only pure raw clauses, because no earlier charged OR can mix in an AND. All clauses contain v. A raw singleton clause forces D=v by monotonicity, forbidden by leastness. Each remaining distinct clause has at least two coordinates, a pure OR physically in Lambda (Q is later), and an ordinary-star anchor adjacent by star edges to all its coordinates. No clause can contain both y and z. Its anchor is either the triangle mate or the private neighbor h of v, since x cannot anchor such a clause. For a mate anchor the only possible nonsingleton clause has support {7,10} for v=7 or {6,9} for v=6: raw x has no path into D. At least one private-neighbor anchor is needed, since otherwise D would equal the single possible mate-anchored clause. Thus vh is starred.

After deleting y,z, D depends only on {10,0,5,8} for v=7 or {9,0,2,4} for v=6. It is clean there, since D≤Q≤S. Any accepted graph edge must have both endpoints among the listed coordinates: otherwise the remaining singleton (or empty input) would be accepted there, contradicting cleanliness. For v=6 the only induced edge is 0–9. For v=7 the only induced edges are 0–10 and 5–8; a mate-anchored clause rejects 5–8, and if no such clause occurs D ignores 10 and rejects 0–10. Thus D accepts at most one graph edge outside the triangle vertices y,z.

**Nonempty raw support of Q.** Here Q=R∨D for a pure raw disjunction R and one dirty AND D. If D rejects both singletons y,z, all primitives of S must receive both vertices via raw OR paths: on those singletons Q evaluates as R and no other paid OR mixes an AND. Thus none of these primitives can be Lambda; every one must be Q itself. This would force S=Q semantically and the raw support of Q contains both, the same leastness contradiction as above. D cannot accept both singletons, as every pure D clause would contain both and need an impossible Lambda anchor. Thus D accepts exactly one, v. Among the five edges touching y or z, D misses the edge from x to the other vertex (D ignores x), and the private edge of v is starred. At most three remaining residuals there, plus at most one away from y,z, are D-positive.

**Empty raw support of Q.** Its two inputs must be dirty ANDs: an earlier raw-empty OR input is impure and itself reads, or has in its OR cone, a distinct charged OR. Every OR directly reading raw-empty dirty Q would itself be another charge. So every OR-only Q-bearing S primitive is precisely Q; the prior AND expansion then forces S=Q as a wire by leastness. In particular A is centreless. Q=D_y∨D_z is positive on both singletons. Neither donor can accept both, by the pure anchored clause argument, so each accepts its indicated singleton. Both private edges are starred; at most three triangle edges and two off-triangle edges are donor-positive residuals. All residuals A can accept are among the triangle edges already allowed.

**Remaining residuals.** Project Q to its raw disjunction R, or false when Q is raw-empty. Values are unchanged on donor-negative pairs. Projected clean tops remain bounded by the graph everywhere because the substitution decreases signals. Every original ordinary top becomes the product of its two raw sides: any dirty AND entering an OR-only operand must pass through Q. An accepted pair there is an original star edge. Each remaining centreless top is a conjunction of pure raw clauses after AND expansion; discard unsatisfiable tops with a false clause. Every nonsingleton clause has an original Lambda anchor: an original nonsingleton OR root other than Q retained its original raw support, is positive on a singleton, and was not charged. If a primitive is Q itself, its replacement R is an earlier pure OR or singleton and has the same property. The anchored clause argument bounds accepted residuals at each such top by two: two sharing a vertex require a clause missing that vertex and containing both mates, forcing the center itself to be the anchor by C4-freeness and starring those edges. Two disjoint marked edges with a cross edge must have alternating clause traces, which force the other two crossing pairs to be edges by cleanliness and give a four-cycle. Thus marked edges form an induced matching. Were there three, each of eight independent endpoint orientations would miss a clause containing the three opposite endpoints. Clauses for neighboring orientations have two common opposite endpoints, so their anchors coincide by C4-freeness. Connectivity gives one anchor with six distinct neighbors, contradicting maximum degree four. This gives at most two residuals per projected centreless top; the output OR aggregation ensures every remaining residual has an active top.

For raw-positive Q the bound is U≤4+2c. For raw-empty Q it is U≤5+2(c−1), exempting A as above. Both imply U≤2c+j+1 for j≥3. The argument covers empty and singleton raw support of S; it does not address support {y,z}.
