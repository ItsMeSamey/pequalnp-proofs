# Left-bank parity screen for a saturated cut-199 tree

Use the six left and six right leaf rectangles of the monotone KW protocol on all sixteen positive edges and all maximal independent sets. The six leaf column vectors on each side are linearly independent, since each side's color matrix has rank six. For positive edge e write A_e for its six-bit left row mask. For negative I write c_I for its six-bit left leaf column mask. Every selected left port is true at e on its left raw inputs and false at I on its left raw inputs. At each cell the intersection of the two masks has size zero or one. If the right part of e is inside I it has size one; if the left part is inside I it is empty.

Assume a single right port Z is true at singleton 3. Rows 36 and 37 (edges joining 3 to 6 and 7) have right mask {Z}; a negative containing their left endpoint forces some right use. Therefore they have the same left mask g. Negatives extending {6,9} and {7,10} force Z false at singleton 9 and singleton 10. Write a,b,f for the left masks of (6,10), (7,9), and (6,7). The right mask of the latter is empty. The first-stage script excludes a XOR b XOR f equal to zero or g. In those cases the right masks of the first two rows have symmetric difference JF or {Z}, respectively, where JF is the mask of an all-right row. Every selected right leaf in the first two rows accepts the negative projection containing {9,10}; thus their union cannot cover that column as JF must. Neither mask can contain Z. The script also enforces a common admissible c_I across these five rows at each negative.

For the second stage, fix an extension of the left masks to every positive row. If U and V denote the six-by-24 left and right leaf-column-indicator matrices, the completed right side matrix is both B V and (A XOR f) U. Here XOR with f acts independently on every row: f U equals the all-ones vector because the internal-left edge has no right leaf. Both representations have rank six, so the row spaces of U and V coincide, and each column of B must be a nonzero linear functional t evaluated on A_e XOR f. Enumerate all 63 possible t. A functional active on edge 36 can only belong to Z, so it cannot be active on (6,10) or (7,9). A proposed right leaf cannot serve column I if any of its selected positive rows has its right input contained in I. It cannot serve that column if every possible c_I would already left-color one of its selected rows. Discard a t that cannot serve any negative. Finally require a c_I for each negative such that every remaining right-colored cell has some surviving candidate t able to cover it. This grants separate choices in different cells; rejection is sound without constructing a physical circuit.

Applying the first screen to the 277 prior order/cotree survivors leaves 8 portfolios. The second leaves one portfolio of cost four and no lower-cost portfolio. Its signed-mask key is -858993460,-252645136,-16777216,-5570646,-21846,-256. Its full surviving left row masks in the edge order in the second script are [32,32,8,16,f,16,8,1,1,2,2,0,0,0,0,0], f in {4,12,20,36}. This grants unrestricted right costs. Coupling with the independently obtained right lower bound seven excludes a budget of ten pure gates in the one-hit tree. The two-hit case, non-tree cases and upper witness are separate premises for a global minimum conclusion.
