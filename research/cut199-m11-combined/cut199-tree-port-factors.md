# Port factors at equality in the cut-199 KW bound

On all positive edges and maximal independent sets, the six right-leaf rectangles sum to the right-color matrix over GF(2). Its rank is six, so their column-indicator vectors are independent. Equal completed rows consequently use the same right-leaf subset. All internal-right edges have all-right rows, so some fixed subset of ports accepts every such edge. Every independent right assignment extends to a maximal global negative, and some port in this fixed subset rejects the extension. Thus the induced right edge function is a conjunction of right ports. Interchanging sides gives the left statement.

Assume the circuit has minimum pure cost at the given mixed count, so an idempotent pure gate can be deleted without changing any mixed gate. With just one pure left gate the six ports are five raw variables and its result. Its operands must be 6 and 7. Otherwise freeze any gate operand outside that pair to its absorbing value, fixing all other outsiders to zero. Vertices 0,1,2 have no edges to 6,7 or 3. The extra gate is constant or independent of the varying pair. The fixed tree LCA of 6 and 7 cannot realize AND for empty right input and OR for singleton 3.

For left centers 0,1,2 the raw center is the only left port true on any incident cross-edge row. It serves each such row since a negative containing the right endpoint forces a left message. The completed rows are identical. Therefore the same subset of right ports accepts all neighbor singletons of the center. If independent right input Y misses those neighbors, the center together with Y extends to a maximal negative, forcing a right message and rejection by some port in the subset. On independent right inputs, the neighborhood query therefore equals a conjunction of right ports.

If the extra gate is 6 AND 7, the conclusion holds for all five centers. Their queries, in order 0,1,2,6,7, are the ORs over {9,10}, {4,8}, {5,10}, {3,10}, {3,9}. Independent witnesses hitting all queries except 0,1,2,7 respectively are {3,4,5}, {3,10}, {3,4,9}, {8,10}. Each forces a distinct port eligible for that query only, where eligibility means accepting both of its neighbor singletons. Independent {3,8,10} hits all queries, demanding an internal-edge port eligible for none. Only the sixth port can serve query 6; it equals that query on independent inputs. Its singleton support {3,10} excludes eligibility for all others. Consequently all five neighborhood queries are distinct individual ports on independent inputs. Their values on right inputs already containing an edge remain open. The interior factor can still be a conjunction involving more than one port.
