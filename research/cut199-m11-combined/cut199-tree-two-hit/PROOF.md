# A two-hit cut-199 tree needs at least 22 gates

Consider the full edge function of the eleven-vertex graph. The cut has left vertices 0, 1, 2, 6, 7 and right vertices 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 10. Assume the mixed output cone consists of eleven binary AND/OR gates, with six distinct incoming pure ports on each side, and that **two right ports are true on the assignment selecting only vertex 3**. Under these assumptions the total circuit size is at least 22. The proof applies to ordinary binary monotone circuits with free fanout, including circuits that are not least or not in TopForm.

The twelve boundary wires and eleven mixed gates are connected by 22 input pins. Connectivity forces this graph to be a tree, and each boundary wire appears once. Fix right input 3 to one. Both activated ports become constant one by monotonicity. The other four ports are monotone functions of the five remaining right inputs. At least nine different left-response profiles occur on this face, so fewer than four Boolean signals cannot distinguish them. In particular these four functions are distinct, nonconstant signals.

The checker reads the source-pin masks for the sixteen graph edges from the bundled copy of the issued statement, after checking its SHA-256. Its five-variable truth tables number the raw inputs 4, 5, 8, 9, 10 from low bit to high bit. For each of the 32 right settings it computes the 32-bit response vector over **all** left settings on the face where vertex 3 is one.

1. **Directed comparisons.** If a left setting accepts right input A and rejects B, some right port must be true at A and false at B. A comparison with a smaller A and larger B implies the wider one by monotonicity. Keeping the 22 minimal comparisons is therefore exact for this test. The checker generates the 7,581 monotone functions on five bits from ordered low/high cofactors. A preliminary union-of-coverage test retains 344 possible members of a four-port portfolio. It then enumerates every unordered quartet of distinct members satisfying the comparisons. There are 12,248. No two different right inputs are required to have equal codes merely because their response profiles agree.
2. **A common read-once tree.** Delete all but the four varying right leaves from the mixed tree, contracting unary paths to obtain their common labelled skeleton. When the left input is fixed, any removed branch is constant. A path through such branches can preserve its retained subtree or replace it by a constant. Thus each residual right function is among the constant prunings of this single skeleton. The checker lists all 120 labelled binary AND/OR skeletons, with child ordering ignored. For each quartet it checks eight left inputs, varying 0, 1, 2 with 6 and 7 zero. For each input it allows *every* completion on unobserved four-bit codes. A single skeleton must survive all eight tests. Exactly 180 quartets survive. The test imposes no condition on how any left port is computed and uses no lower face.
3. **Physical right-producer cost.** All four functions in every surviving quartet are nonraw. An eight-gate binary circuit producing one quartet therefore has at most four distinct gates other than its four first goal-producing gates. Start with the five raw signals. Whenever a missing goal can be made from two available signals, add it immediately. This is safe because the goal must be produced eventually and its earlier availability cannot harm any other use. When no goal is immediately available, branch over all genuinely new pairwise AND/OR outputs as candidate helpers, consuming one helper slot. With just one helper remaining it must immediately unlock some goal. The search memoizes the complete signal bank, remaining helper count and tested portfolios. This counts shared gates once. Repeated values, repeated identical pins, constants and unary aliases cannot improve a realization after simplification. No surviving quartet is achievable with eight physical gates.

Consequently at least nine right pure gates survive when vertex 3 is fixed to one. In the original circuit at least one of the two ports true on singleton 3 must be computed, since only one right raw input is true there. That port's producing gate becomes constant on the upper face and can be deleted independently of the nine surviving gates. There are therefore at least ten right pure gates. Six distinct left ports on five raw inputs require at least one left pure gate. Together with the eleven mixed gates this gives 22.

Run `node verify.cjs` from this directory and compare its JSON to `replay.json`. The checker is standalone and uses only Node built-ins and the source file. Its proof uses neither a repeated donor nor anchored-majority ownership, lower-face lifts, donor placements, left-port shapes, or forced KW rectangles. The 180 quartets here come from the read-once tree test itself. Matching the count of a separate 180-portfolio computation would not identify its members; this replay checks costs directly on its own survivors.
