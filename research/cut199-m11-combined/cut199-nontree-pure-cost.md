# Eleven mixed gates on cut 199: non-tree cases

The left vertices are 0, 1, 2, 6, 7. The right vertices are 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 10. Costs count physical gates once, regardless of how many ports or consumers use them. These arguments concern circuits with exactly eleven mixed gates.

## Forks and the anchored restriction

Let p and q count distinct left and right ports at the mixed boundary. Including one output tap, the sum of (number of uses minus one) over the live boundary and mixed wires equals 12-p-q. Each positive summand is a fork. For five left ports, the 7-absent face literally encodes the other four inputs; singleton 7 occupies the spare unit. With vertices 0,1,2 all active, toggling 6,7 therefore toggles their two literal ports independently. The all-but-6 code on input {0,1,2,7} follows by monotonicity and by testing empty right. Toggling right vertex 3 with every other right raw zero gives majority on the three raw variables 6,7,3. Nine distinct profiles above singleton 3 leave at most q-4 active ports at that singleton. Empty has code zero in a positive AND/OR circuit.

With three changing ports, majority needs four binary gates. Every live four-gate realization has raw use counts 1,2,2 and single-use internal nonoutput gates. One can check all four-gate lists directly on eight inputs. Under constant propagation, unused branches can only lose fork excess. Contracting a unary alias moves a fork upstream or merges forks. If no excess is lost, both outgoing uses of each original fork survive. Two nested forks cannot become incomparable: there is no third fork on their connecting path that could preserve a branch removed by simplification.

For (5,5), both excess units survive. Thus there are two incomparable binary forks with disjoint input cones, and each restricts to a different literal majority input. Each donor contains exactly one anchor: an anchor in that cone cannot reach the output except through the donor, and every anchor stays essential. A donor consisting of pure ports alone can only be a single boundary port. For (5,6), one excess unit is insufficient for a three-port majority, so exactly two ports are active on right singleton 3. Before identifying these two varying ports, all four changing port occurrences must survive. Identifying them consumes the second excess; the repeated variable 3 uses its two occurrences exactly once each. The sole physical fork restricts to one left majority input. Its cone contains that left anchor and neither active right port.

## Upper-face enumeration and split codes

Pin graph vertex 3 to one. When the count of varying right ports is four, each port is a monotone function of raw vertices 4,5,8,9,10 (in that order). A positive/negative comparison of two right inputs is required when some common left assignment accepts the first and rejects the second. Keep the minimal first inputs and maximal second inputs for these directed comparisons. The **upper** comparison calculation pins 3 to one on every trial; it gives 22 obligations. Of all 7,581 five-variable monotone functions, 344 can occur in a tuple of four covering the obligations. There are 12,248 qualifying unordered tuples. No code equality is imposed on equal profiles.

For five total right ports and any fixed upper tuple, encode each upper input by its four values and a constant one. Each lower input initially has every five-bit code below its own upper code compatible with the upper inputs' comparison profiles. Apply arc consistency to pairs of lower inputs, requiring monotonicity on comparable raw assignments and order reflection whenever their full graph profiles are incomparable or ordered in the reverse direction. This necessary filter leaves 11,178 tuples. The older 7,355 count requires the four functions to be valid across both settings of vertex 3 and applies only to the isolated-3 case.

## Necessary decoder tests

A read-once tree induces disjoint monotone Karchmer–Wigderson leaf rectangles on any positive samples times negative samples. If only one leaf distinguishes a cell, its rectangle must contain that cell. Forced cells for each leaf force the entire product of their row and column sets. Overlap of boxes for distinct leaves precludes that tree.

For one repeated signal, contract its read-once input cone to one donor. All exterior ports are singly used. If a positive/negative pair has no exterior distinguishing coordinate, its donor values must be one and zero. Test the resulting donor obligations against monotonicity and its own read-once leaf boxes. If a pair then cannot be distinguished by the donor, its exterior witness must belong to one of the singly used exterior ports. For two disjoint donors, iterate the same rule whenever no exterior port and at most one donor can distinguish a pair. These are necessary tests, so discarding impossible placements does not assume a particular form for the external decoding function.

Freezing all left ports lets any mixed donor input cone prune to a common read-once right-port tree. Grant unrestricted zero/one pruning at every subtree. The two uses of a surviving donor must use the same pruned function. Compare this relaxation to all eight residual functions on left inputs 0,1,2 with 6=7=0. In the one-donor cases this leaves 711 different upper-face portfolios before lower-code filtering. Every one requires at least eight binary producer gates even with unrestricted sharing.

## Case results

* **(5,5).** The split-code check uses upper-face extrema and probes on which left codes are pinned. The lower-face positive is the entire left side at empty right; the two lower-face negatives are left masks 71 and 135 with empty right. The two-donor restrictions and mandatory boxes eliminate all 2,018 remaining projected signatures at any pure cost. See `cut199-two-donors-split-codes.cjs`.
* **(6,5).** If no pure gate uses raw 3, raw 3 is one port and the four remaining functions work across both settings. The 7,355-family full-cube checks in `cut199-one-pure-repeat-q5.cjs` and `cut199-one-mixed-repeat-q5.cjs` handle this case. Otherwise pinning 3 eliminates at least one paid producer. Eight required face gates leave at most one left gate in a putative budget of ten. In the corrected split-code check, arc consistency excludes every ordinary four-port read-once tuple. The donor and forced-box tests leave ten critical portfolios, none achievable with eight face gates. See `cut199-one-donor-split-codes.cjs`; it handles a pure repeated right port as an empty-left donor containing one varying right leaf.
* **(5,6).** At least one of the two ports active on singleton 3 is computed, so right cost is at least nine. Left budget at most one forces the five raw left ports. The anchored donor and mandatory boxes leave 180 upper portfolios, each with four nonraw outputs. None is realizable with eight gates. The shared-cost check grants every directly computable required output, branches on at most four additional physical helpers and caches a bank once per budget and portfolio. With at least nine face gates, at most one paid gate can disappear on pinning 3. Consequently the two activated ports must be raw 3 and a separate terminal OR of raw 3 with a signal H from the other inputs. Every other port is unchanged between the faces: another direct use of raw 3, or any gate consuming an output fixed to one, would lose a second paid gate. On the lower face order reflection constrains H. Grant every monotone admissible upper value on positives and lower value on negatives and test the full graph's edges and maximal independent sets. No donor placement survives, even if H uses an already-paid producer. See `cut199-one-donor-six-right.cjs`.

Thus every non-tree realization with eleven mixed gates requires at least eleven pure gates. The known 22/11 control is non-tree and attains this bound. The saturated-tree case has a separate lower bound of five pure gates; these results leave its exact cost and the unrestricted minimum at eleven mixed gates open. They also leave greater mixed counts open.
