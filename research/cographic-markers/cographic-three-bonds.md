# Cographicity after a triangle-to-star exchange on a three-edge bond

Let T={e1,e2,e3} be a bond in a connected undirected graph G of size three (i.e. the bond has size three). It is a triangle of M*(G). Write A and B for its connected shores and mark the three attachment vertices separately on each, with repetition when necessary. Assume T is coindependent in M*(G), as required for the ordinary Δ exchange; this holds for a cut matching. The following includes the single-cut obstruction even when the stipulated cut family is laminar.

**Prism obstruction.** If each shore has a rooted K3 minor at its three attachment vertices, contract and delete inside the two shores to obtain a triangular prism, preserving all three matching edges T. The dual operations on cographic matroids give a triangle-preserving minor. The Δ exchange commutes with operations away from T while T is retained as an eligible triangle (use the K4 generalized parallel connection, followed by deleting T). For the prism the planar dual is the triangular bipyramid K5 minus one edge. Exchanging its equatorial triangle performs graphic ΔY, giving K3,3. Hence the exchanged original matroid has M(K3,3) as a minor, precluding cographicity.

**Every simple three-connected nonstar bond of size three qualifies.** If a shore A has two vertices, their degree sum is at least six and they have at most one internal edge, requiring at least four outgoing edges. Thus a nonsingleton shore has at least three vertices. If its ports occupy at most two vertices, these separate the shore interior from the other shore, contrary to 3-connectivity. If some x disconnects A, each component of A−x needs at least two cut edges: otherwise deleting x and at most one external neighbor disconnects G. Two such components already need four, so A is 2-connected. In a 2-connected graph take a cycle through the first two attached vertices a,b. If c is off the cycle, take two internally disjoint paths from c to different cycle points p,q, meeting the cycle only at their endpoints (the fan lemma). If one p–q arc avoids a,b in its interior, use the other arc and the path through c for a cycle through all three. Otherwise a and b lie on opposite p–q arcs. These arcs and the path through c form a theta with a,b,c on separate branches. Contract the segment from p to a and from q to b, then shorten the three remaining joining paths to form a rooted K3. Thus both shores qualify. A star T, on the other hand, is converted by ordinary graphic YΔ on the dual side, preserving cographicity.

**Arbitrary connected graphs and one cut.** If a shore has repeated marked vertices, take the repeated mark x. If the three are distinct, represent each mark by a vertex node in the bipartite block–vertex incidence tree (each bridge forms a 2-vertex block). If the median of the three marks is a block node, the three arms reach that block at distinct vertices. Contract outside paths along those arms to bring the marks to these three vertices of the block. This block is 2-connected, giving a rooted K3 minor as above. Otherwise the median is a vertex x. In the repeated-mark case also, each component of the shore minus x meets at most one mark occurrence. Such a shore permits the following graph presentation. For each component carrying one mark p_i, isolate the subgraph of that component, its incident edges to x, and its cut edge p_i q_i. This subgraph joins the remainder only at x and q_i. Interchange those attachment vertices in this subgraph (a Whitney switch), preserving its cycle matroid, so the cut edge is now incident to x. The switches are independent even if q_i agree. Cut edges with a mark at x were already incident to x. A component with no mark is attached at x as a one-vertex sum and may be reattached elsewhere without changing the cycle matroid. These operations make the cut a literal degree-three star. Ordinary YΔ on this graph therefore realizes the dual of the requested exchange. Thus for one eligible cut, cographicity is equivalent to at least one shore lacking the rooted triangular minor. The criterion is graph-structural and does not require either side to be planar.

**Multiple cuts.** In a simple 3-connected graph, a family containing a nonstar three-bond already fails the requirement that every subset of cuts admit cographic exchange. The smallest witness is the prism itself, even for a singleton family. For pairwise edge-disjoint actual degree-three stars the centers are nonadjacent, and YΔ replacements at any subset of them preserve a graph presentation, in any order. Their replacements preserve the degrees of other center stars (which are not neighbors) and their incident edges. This also holds if all requested stars can be exposed together by one cycle-matroid-equivalent representation. Individual criteria in graphs with 2-separations alone do not establish simultaneous exposure.
