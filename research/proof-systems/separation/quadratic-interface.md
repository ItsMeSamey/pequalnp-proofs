# Counting with a recognizable quadratic interface

Normalize a supplied fan-in-two Boolean circuit on n inputs and g gates into affine gates and m binary AND gates over F2 using each gate's multilinear truth table (at most one product per gate). Regard AND outputs z_1,...,z_m in topological order temporarily as free bits. Propagation through affine gates gives both operand forms at every AND and the output as affine functions of x,z. Stack the z-coefficients of the 2m operands into B. Let D be a row basis, k=rank B. This parameter, and a factorization B=PD, are computable in polynomial time without enumerating original inputs. The row of the final output need not be in D.

Introduce y=Dz. Substitution of each operand's row of P yields an affine form in x,y. Denote the product for gate i by Q_i(x,y). Enforce y=DQ(x,y); enforce output=1 after the substitution z=Q(x,y). These k+1 equations have Boolean degree at most two. For any accepting input, its actual gate outputs give a solution. Conversely define z=Q(x,y) at any solution. Its equality y=Dz makes the original operand forms equal the substituted ones. By induction through the original gate order every z is the actual gate output, so y is unique for x. This argument allows the basis D to contain coordinates from later gates.

For the vector r of these k+1 residual polynomials, character orthogonality gives the count as 2^(-(k+1)) times the sum over the 2^(k+1) characters a of the quadratic sums of (-1)^(a.r(x,y)) over x,y. A quadratic sum is polynomial-time computable: choose a nonzero cross coefficient and write q=uv+uA+vB+C on its other variables. The identity summing over u,v gives 2*(-1)^(C+AB) for each assignment of the remaining variables. Continue pair elimination to an affine terminal, whose sum is zero for a nonconstant linear part and signed 2^d otherwise. Counts have polynomial bit length. Total time 2^k poly(n+g) and polynomial space by streaming characters. Self-reducing on x needs at most 2n solves, and pinning x preserves the quadratic equations and k. This is a direct original-input method; it does not invoke the October clock.

There is a separate recognizable interface when a designated set of t quadratic functions of x supplies all input dependencies of an arbitrary explicit Boolean circuit F of size g. Compute F on its full 2^t-entry truth table and transform the 0/1 values with the integer Walsh-Hadamard transform. For each character compute the quadratic sum on x of the signed combination of the t features. Multiplying corresponding coefficients and summing with normalization 2^-t counts accepting inputs. Time 2^t poly(n,g), and this implementation uses 2^t poly(n,g) bits. No assumption of independence of the features is needed. The feature degree can be certified by computing each intermediate ANF exactly up to degree four whenever its two inputs have degree at most two; reject if a higher term survives. This sufficient recognizer can miss cancellations in more complicated feature circuits. Pinning an input preserves degree and permits witness recovery.

For a parameter obstruction, take z_ij=x_i x_j for all 2≤i<j≤n, then t_ij=z_ij x_1, with the t_ij merged through XOR. The stack B includes a distinct unit row for every z_ij, so k≥binom(n-1,2) despite polynomial size. Factoring the common x_1 makes decision easy. More locally, take independent u_i=a_i b_i, t_i=u_i c_i, v_i=u_i+t_i, and XOR all v_i. The two paths from u_i reconverge at v_i. In the literal gate-cut variant (every cut equation and the output must be quadratic), each u_i is mandatory: without it, the first cut on t_i's unique path to the output, or the output itself, has the cubic a_i b_i c_i with coefficient one. The direct u_i term cannot cancel it. Cutting all u_i suffices. These are limits of specified representations, and provide no decision lower bound for unrestricted circuits.
