# Deterministic original-input transfer with dense readouts

Use the deterministic fixed-width arbitrary-density #CNF clock stated in the October source: on V variables and m clauses its bit time is (V+m)^O_d(1) times 2^{A_d V loglog V / sqrt(log V)}, for fixed width d and a fixed constant A_d. The bounded-density version omits loglog V. Here and below finite small lengths do not affect asymptotics. The unrestricted Circuit-SAT conversion introduces one variable per binary gate and per input, and at most four truth-table clauses per binary gate plus one unit output clause. Its bounded-density count thus costs poly(n+g) times 2^{O((n+g)/sqrt(log(n+g)))} on g gates and n original inputs. This does not provide the required original-n clock at g=n^k for k>1.

Fix d≥1. Consider a function of L input bits represented by g arbitrary binary preprocessing gates of unbounded fanout. A readout is a width-d CNF or DNF over the original and preprocessed bits, with canonical lists of m_i clauses/terms. The output is any Boolean function of b readouts, even specified by its full table. Remove constant readouts by fixing their table coordinates. Then b≤sum_i log2(1+m_i)=R. A single evaluating formula can be certified with at most 1+m_i choices: retain all clauses for true CNF, or specify one failed clause for false and pin each literal false. For DNF swap these two truth values and their constraints. Every actual evaluation admits a code, and any satisfying code forces exactly its recorded bit. Duplicate codes for a false CNF do not matter for decision.

Use exactly the lex-first witness language W in ../../eth-october-primary/quantitative-circuits.tex. Its length L=D n is a fixed linear padding of the unary hierarchy input. The local decoder has n+O(log n) input bits and O(n) binary gates and outputs three variable labels and signs. If W had the displayed representations eventually at all lengths with g≤epsilon L sqrt(log L)/loglog L and R≤epsilon L, the hierarchy machine could guess one and check whether any decoded clause is violated. It constructs three copies of preprocessing on decoder-produced labels, enumerates the three code tuples, and reads candidate output bits from the guessed table. Enforce violation signs with the decoder wires. Every branch of this deterministic test is a width-max(3,d) CNF with O(n+g) variables and polynomially many clauses, since m_i=O_d((L+g)^d) after canonicalization and b≤R. Reject the guessed witness exactly when at least one of these CNFs is satisfiable. Thus every candidate is rejected on hierarchy NO; on YES the lex-first W yields an accepting guess. There is no guess of UNSAT certificates.

The table guess and each sequential table lookup cost at most 2^{O(b)} times polynomial overhead, explicitly included. Code enumeration costs 2^{3R}; formula generation and candidate preprocessing/list guesses cost polynomial in n for fixed d, apart from the table. The resulting total nondeterministic bit time is at most 2^{O(R)+A'_d (n+g) loglog(n+g)/sqrt(log(n+g))}poly(n), using the source's polynomial-space solver and its multitape implementation. For epsilon=epsilon_d sufficiently small depending on D and the solver constants, this is below 2^{n/2}, contradicting the source's unary hierarchy witness. Consequently at infinitely many lengths W has no representation meeting both resource bounds. The same W works for all fixed d, with constants and hard subsequences allowed to differ. In particular b≤epsilon'_d L/log L suffices for the readout budget for polynomially dense lists. At those lengths the bound includes a single polynomially dense fixed-width top and also truth tables of superpolynomial length under the stated budget.

This consequence tracks a different resource from total gates. Even width-two readouts with zero preprocessing include 2^{choose(L,2)} distinct monotone graph CNFs (test an assignment with zeros exactly at the endpoints to recover each edge); counting binary circuits gives examples needing Omega(L²/log L) ordinary gates. It supplies no representation theorem for all polynomial-size circuits, no NP language with superpolynomial circuit lower bounds, and no separation of P from NP. The NEXP-versus-P/poly transfer in Williams Theorem 1.1 requires a superpolynomial original-input saving for every polynomial gate exponent. Safe-slope existence alone is total, and marked-rank savings require a separate parameter bound for every circuit in the target class.
