# Counting an operand-rank predicate with clauses

Let the scalar circuit have operand rank k and denote its indicator by f(x). For a disjunction of affine equations, its falsity set is an affine flat V_i (possibly empty). Construct all distinct nonempty intersections, including the full ambient flat; let their count be L. This threshold is recognizable by iteratively intersecting the current family with each next flat and storing canonical row-reduced systems. In parallel maintain signed inclusion-exclusion coefficients for the product of (1-indicator(V_i)). Start at coefficient one on the ambient space. Take a snapshot before each pass, and for every flat A subtract its old coefficient from that of A intersect V_i, ignoring empty intersections. Coefficients have O(m) bits for m clauses. Do not estimate L by counting only final nonzero coefficients. Obtain the exact answer by summing the restricted f counts with these integer coefficients. Affine parametrization on x preserves the quadratic residual equations on x,y, as well as uniqueness of y. Time (mL+L 2^(k+1)) poly(input). Pinning input bits only intersects old flats with one fixed flat; witness recovery is polynomial extra work.

Even without the full lattice one may compute Delta=#f minus the sum of #f on V_i. When Delta>0 there is an accepting assignment passing every clause. The gap is additive under splitting any original bit, so retaining a positive branch constructs one. If f is nonzero it has density at least 2^(-min(n,k+2)) by its degree and the Boolean polynomial minimum weight bound. The latter follows by writing f=x_n h+g at a variable occurring in a highest monomial: h has degree one less and every x' with h(x')=1 has one accepting extension. Hence sum_i 2^(-codim V_i)<2^(-(k+2)) guarantees that feasibility equals nonzeroness of f, even with arbitrary overlaps (remove empty flats). This is a sufficient promise.

Separately, constrain assignments by an ordered read-once deterministic width-w branching program on original inputs. Interleave auxiliary y coordinates as steps that leave its state unchanged. For each of the k+1 binary-character directions' combinations in the quadratic count expand the residual sum q; let M be its polar matrix. Process bits left to right. Alongside the program state retain u=M_(future,past) x_past and a signed partial count weighted by the quadratic restricted to past bits. An incoming bit b gets sign from its linear term and b times the entry of u at this bit, then project u to the remaining suffix and add b times its M-column there. The constant term is included once. At most 2^rho syndromes occur if rho bounds the polar rank at every prefix for the chosen character. Summing accepted terminal entries and applying the character normalization gives the exact count in w 2^(k+1+rho) poly(input) time, with pins handled by suppressing transitions. Ranks over all characters may be checked within that running time. A polynomially checkable uniform sufficient bound is the rank of stacked generator polar cut matrices or their horizontal concatenation; either may greatly overestimate. The program can be given explicitly; compiling CNF by conjunction of clause statuses may use width exponential in the number of live clauses. No universal width bound is assumed.
