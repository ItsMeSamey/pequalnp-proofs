# Fixed-width CNF and listed Boolean equations on the original inputs

Assume the October fixed-degree sparse-polynomial counting lemma and deterministic CNF sparsification and hash construction. Let N be the number of original bits. Take an arbitrary-density CNF of fixed width d and O(N) side equations of fixed Boolean degree e, with at most O(N log^C N) listed monomials in total for a fixed C. The exact number of common solutions can be computed in polynomial space and time

    poly(input size) 2^{O_(d,e,C)(N log log N / sqrt(log N))},

with constants also depending on the constants in the size promises.

First split each side polynomial into chunks of at most ceil(log^C N) terms and accumulate chunk parities by chained partial-sum bits, requiring the last sum to equal zero. There are O(N) new bits and equations. Every surviving original assignment has a unique extension. A fixed increase of the exponent in the sparse-query lemma covers the two extra carry terms per equation.

Run the original CNF disjoint sparsification and unmodified greedy hash selection on its declared original bits alone. For each nontrivial leaf, every satisfying assignment satisfies every selected parity row, because its entire clause-violation vector is zero. The final count equality proved by the source then makes the sets equal pointwise. To count the conjunction, append the side equations only to each final sparse system and use the sparse-query counting lemma on O(N) declared variables. Preserve the leaf's unit restrictions and free original bits. For a trivially true leaf, evaluate the side system with its restrictions; trivially false leaves contribute zero. Add the exact counts from disjoint leaves. Use sparse equations rather than the optional sparse-CNF network shortcut in this construction. For small N use exhaustive evaluation. Pinning originals preserves all size promises and permits witness recovery.

For example a parity of N log^5 N arbitrary pair monomials can be required alongside the dense CNF, without introducing one variable per product. The baseline gate-by-gate conversion can use N log^5 N extra variables.

There is a recognizable circuit version. Work with a normalized XOR-AND circuit; delete unused gates. Suppose it has O(N) AND gates, O(N) affine gates whose outputs feed at least two operand occurrences, and total g=O(N log^C N) gates. Name those AND outputs and reused affine outputs, and the root. Flatten every remaining single-use affine subtree into a sum of input bits and named bits. For each AND keep two extra named affine operands and one degree-two product equation, avoiding expansion of a product of two long sums. Each unnamed affine gate belongs to only one flattened tree. The total number of remaining summands counted with multiplicity is O(g+N), by binary fanin, including references to heavily reused named bits. Thus the newly introduced names and equations number O(N), and their total monomial count meets the budget above. These are chronological equations with unique completion. All quantities are computed from the explicit supplied DAG; neither minimum circuit size nor semantic optimization is presumed. Normalizing a general two-input gate must be charged in the resulting presentation. Circuit size n^k for a general fixed k>1 does not imply the needed parameters.
