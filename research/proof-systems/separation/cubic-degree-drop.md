# Cubic character preprocessing

Let f_1,...,f_t have certified Boolean degree at most three over the original n variables. For arbitrary explicit postprocessor F, evaluate and Walsh-transform its 0/1 truth table. For each character with nonzero coefficient, inspect T, the cubic principal part of the sum of its selected features. If T is zero use quadratic elimination. Otherwise solve the homogeneous linear system l wedge T=0 for a nonzero l. Its equation for a four-set I sums l_i T_(I minus i) over i in I.

The degree filtration of the Boolean ring has associated graded relations x_i²=0. Changes of basis therefore act on cubic principal terms by the third exterior power. In coordinates with first coordinate l, the system says each nonzero cubic contains l. Both restrictions l=0,1 are quadratic; either restriction quadratic implies the system. Sum their exact quadratic Gauss sums. Different characters may use different l. Empty solution space reports inapplicability for that character. A polynomial procedure on a supplied circuit certifies a sufficient degree-three class by evaluating each gate's ANF exactly through degree six from operands of degree at most three and rejecting higher surviving terms. It can miss later cancellations.

Total runtime is 2^t poly(n,g), including circuit table construction and all four-index equations. Pinning original bits preserves each character's degree drop (the old form may become constant, leaving one quadratic slice), and n count queries recover a witness. Arbitrary nonlinear original-input reductions need not preserve this promise. On x1 times the sum of x_i x_j for 2≤i<j≤n, l=x1 suffices. Thus the previously large operand-rank family takes polynomial time even after affine changes on n inputs.

Classical sources: Boyar, Peralta and Pochuev's XOR-AND multiplicative complexity minimizes AND count over all presentations (https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-3975(99)00182-6). Carlet presents the classical quadratic Walsh and Dickson theory (https://www.math.univ-paris13.fr/~carlet/book-fcts-Bool-vect-crypt-codes.pdf). Carlet, Feukoua and Sălăgean study degree-dropping hyperplanes and codimension (https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.20211). No historical priority is asserted.
