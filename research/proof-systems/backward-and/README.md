# Removing producers on the inactive side of an AND

Work with an acyclic circuit of m binary ANDs; every operand and its scalar output are affine functions of original bits and prior producer outputs. At a candidate gate g=u v, choose a nonconstant signal b occurring in the affine XOR u. Freeze the other terms of u as their actual original signals. Clone v's fanin, replacing b by 1+u+b as an expression of the frozen signals. Occurrences of every other frozen signal stop traversal and keep its original value, even if that value itself depends on b. Process the original gates preceding g before emitting the clone, so all these references already exist. Denote the resulting value by v'. On every original input with u=1 it equals v. When u=0, both u v and u v' vanish. Substitute the new gate globally, simplify constants and duplicate products, and prune unneeded ancestors. This uses internal observability don't-cares, a classical network-optimization principle (Mishchenko and Brayton, https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.4695). Here the test uses a single algebraically certified condition and no SAT oracle.

With an indexed normalized presentation, one proposal creates at most 2m+1 gates before pruning. Re-normalization uses O((m+1)(n+m+1)) affine coefficient bits after any strictly AND-decreasing accepted step. There are O(m(n+m)) choices of gate, side and pivot per pass and at most m accepted decreases. Naive repeated scanning is polynomial including XOR description costs. The executable cofactor.py implements a single proposal; the greedy scan is optional. Counts of original inputs are exact, so existing original-input AND-count solvers use the measured new m. All original wire values are reconstructed by ordinary evaluation on the recovered original x. No affine decoder for every eliminated wire is claimed.

First control: on L data bits plus t compute all pair products, XOR their outputs to Q, then output t((1+t)Q+sum x_i). Every old producer has a path to the output, and every original variable is essential. A terminal cofactor leaves just t times the data parity, exactly one AND. In contrast every compiler requiring every original wire to be affine in the raw bits and retained AND outputs must retain at least binom(L,2) of them, because the pair monomials are linearly independent modulo affine functions. This applies even if the prefix method's equality test is strengthened to a semantic oracle.

Dense m=n control: on raw x_1,...,x_L,a,t (n=L+2), let p_i=x_i(a+sum_(j<i)p_j), Q=sum p_i, and output t((1+t)Q+a+sum x_i). There are exactly n ANDs, and the normalized dependency graph on the p producers has every earlier-to-later edge. Every p_i has its marker monomial a x_i and no other p_j has it: all terms of p_j use x_j, and prior products contain no linear monomial a. Thus the all-wire affine requirement needs at least n-2 producers. The scalar rewrite leaves exactly one. Both constructions preserve raw satisfying inputs; an arbitrary payload H can replace the live parity, so this pass gives no universal bound for Circuit-SAT.

The replay checks all inputs on 230 independently drawn small gate proposals (90 internal pivots) and on ten values of L for each displayed family.
