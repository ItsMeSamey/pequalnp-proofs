# One shared derived line in BPHP(5,2)

On the sixty permutations of 00123, a leaf must identify the two positions of the repeated zero. Let L(T) denote the minimum number of leaves of an adaptive affine-parity decision tree on T, with L(empty)=0. The exhaustive result is

    min over affine flats A of L(A∩F) + L(F∖A) = 23.

Compile check.cpp with g++ -std=c++17 -O2 and run the executable with argument 22, then 23. The 22 run excludes every possible budget allocation. The 23 run reports a mask of 16777215 and a first-tree budget of nine. Since the assignment tuples are in lexicographic order, this mask means that pigeon zero is assigned hole zero. Its two optimal costs must be nine and fourteen by the budget-22 exclusion.

**Proof-length consequence.** In the ancestor DAG of an empty line suppose at most one derived line has at least two parent-reference uses, counting edge multiplicity. Axiom occurrences may be reused freely. If such a shared line exists, cut its b≥2 references. This leaves an upper sink tree whose leaves are axioms or b copies of the cut line, and a lower tree rooted at the cut line with only axiom leaves. Unary weakenings leave the usual binary-nodes-equal-leaves-minus-one identity intact. All other derived-line occurrences appear only once across these trees. The assignments falsifying the shared line form an affine flat or the empty set. Under each fixed repeated-hole promise, use the lower tree on the portion where the shared line is false and the upper tree on its complement. On the latter portion the upper tree never exits at the shared line, since a false resolvent routes to a false parent and weakening routes to its false premise. Every promised input violates a unique collision axiom. Axiom leaves used for different holes are disjoint, and constant queries can be suppressed. Thus there are at least 4·23=92 axiom leaf occurrences across the two trees. Their count plus b−2 is the total number of distinct binary inferences. At least forty distinct original axiom lines are also necessary, because each has its own exclusive falsifier. Total length is therefore at least 132. When there is no shared derived line, the full 100-leaf result gives at least 99 binary inferences and forty distinct axioms. No assertion about two shared derived lines or arbitrary DAGs follows.

The bound 92 from the four independent restrictions is jointly attained at the level of split domains: the same global flat, XOR of the two-bit words of pigeons 1 through 4 equal to zero, picks exactly those inputs where pigeon zero occupies the repeated hole. Translation of all five words by a fixed hole carries the two conditional trees between holes. This is not a construction of a refutation of length 132.

## One affine-flat test at any tree node

The exact minimum remains **23** if a tree may ask one arbitrary affine-membership question at a single node, with ordinary parity queries before and after it. For an ordinary-parity prefix reaching domain T, an exceptional question has YES domain S that is an ambient affine trace contained in T. Conversely every ambient affine trace contained in T is obtainable in this way. At an ordinary first query splitting T into U and V, the exceptional question may be used in U or in V. The other child must use only ordinary questions. These cases exhaust all finite trees.

Two independent C++ programs in this directory reject budget 22. Compile either with g++ -std=c++17 -O2 and run with argument 22. special.cpp generates subtraces by iterated hyperplane splitting under the stabilizer of the current domain; its cap-22 run memoizes six genuinely exceptional domain entries and 107,570 ordinary ones. independent.cpp first enumerates all ambient systems in reduced row-echelon form on eight bits. The low four pigeon words supply eight free coordinates, since the fifth word is their coordinatewise XOR on the fixed-hole promise. It enumerates 1,657,673 distinct nonempty affine traces and tests them by a separately implemented boolean budget recurrence. Budget 23 succeeds already with a root exceptional question.

For a DAG containing any number of shared derived lines, cut every reference to such a line and retain each proof component once. To classify a promised assignment, test falsity of the shared lines in increasing chronological derivation order. If any is false, enter the tree for the first false one; otherwise enter the final sink tree. The tree thus chosen cannot exit at an earlier shared line since all such lines are true. The combined decision tree uses at most the sum of actual axiom leaf occurrences across components. Falsity of a shared line is tested by membership in an affine flat; an empty or full falsifier is constant, and the falsity question for a rank-one nonconstant line is a single parity. Hence if at most one shared line has a nonempty falsifier of codimension at least two, the one-exception bound forces at least 92 axiom occurrences. More generally even a globally high-codimension shared line costs no exceptional question on any fixed hole slice where its falsifier reduces to an ordinary parity or constant. With s shared derived lines and b cut references, the forest has s+1 components, so binary inferences number at least 92+b−s−1. Since b≥2s, the total distinct line count is at least 131+s plus any unary weakenings. This count assumes the proof has been pruned to the sink's ancestors and sharing is counted by reference multiplicity. No claim about proofs with multiple high-codimension shared lines follows.
