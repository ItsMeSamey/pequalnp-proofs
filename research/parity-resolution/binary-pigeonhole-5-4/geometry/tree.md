# Unique-collision parity decision trees for binary PHP(5,4)

The exact minimum number of leaves to identify the unique violated input axiom on the 240 assignments with a single duplicated hole is 100.

Fix duplicated hole 0. The 60 assignments are the distinct permutations of (0,0,1,2,3). Their ten labels are the two positions occupied by 0. Encode the five entries using two bits apiece. Every affine parity question induces one of the complementary splits generated by the 1023 nonzero ten-bit masks (discard constants). This gives 255 distinct nontrivial splits modulo complement. For a subset S, a tree with at most b leaves exists precisely when it has only one label, or some nontrivial split U,V admits successful child budgets c,b−c. A child needs at least its number of distinct labels. The attached executable enumerates all these choices, recursing only on proper subsets. The results for b=24,25 are false,true. XOR-translation of every entry transports the result to each other duplicated hole.

A tree identifying the original axiom has disjoint sets of leaves across the four holes because an axiom specifies both a pigeon pair and a hole. Restricting the tree to each hole and suppressing constant branches proves at least 100 leaves. Conversely, the coordinatewise XOR of all five pigeons equals the repeated hole on this promise, because all four hole words XOR to zero. Ask those two parity questions and attach a 25-leaf tree on each branch.

On a false resolvent, the pivot routes to a false parent. Semantic weakening routes to its unique parent. Starting at the empty clause and unfolding all parent references therefore produces a parity decision tree on the promise. A tree-like proof needs at least 100 axiom occurrences and 99 binary inferences, hence at least 199 lines counting occurrences. For general DAG proofs this bounds the unfolding only. This result does not establish the requested unrestricted 128-line floor.
