# Two empty selectors and at most four dirty ANDs, visible S

Use the ordinary-A hypotheses and H8 reduction of 6648-q3-clean-two-empty-incomparable.md. Write P=H∨R for the unique positive entrance. The reduction requires no distinctness of donors: S has P on its OR cone, H is H8-clean, at most three near edges are unstarred, and a nonhomogeneous core double implies U≤7. In fact c≥1: if an empty selector lies outside H's ancestry, take a latest such selector. It cannot enter P's pure raw pin or H's cone. No later exceptional exit is possible, so its AND-only escape ends at a centreless top. Otherwise both selectors precede H; the 19-card escape from S yields a centreless top as in the cited visible-S proof. Thus U≤7 suffices for all j≥3, and any violation can use at most one surplus across all exclusively assigned core batches.

Assign H-positive core edges first. On H-negative pairs replace the physical H wire everywhere by false and P by R. This is exact there and decreases the circuit on all inputs, so every surviving clean top is clean; ordinary tops return original stars. In the projection only the two raw-empty ORs E,F can introduce sums at the frontier. Both have pins which are original dirty ANDs or earlier raw-empty ORs. Every other pretop clause on unbroken OR paths is a raw singleton or an originally anchored disjunction. Thus any AND-only stretch contributes a product of pure clauses and the empty wires that feed it. Decreasing also inside shared ANDs retains these properties. An identical analysis on the ancestors of H uses no P and at most j−1 dirty ANDs other than H itself.

With up to two available dirty AND indices B,C, ordered temporally, both have pure product values: any prior empty OR with at most one available AND input simplifies to zero or that earlier AND. Subsequent empty ORs compute zero,B,C or B∨C. Intersections at any clean root consequently expand into at most two pure products, since repeated use of the same sum does not add a choice. With up to three available dirty ANDs B,C,D, the corresponding bound is three. To see it, ignore zero pins and selectors that simplify to an existing pin. The first effective sum is of two distinct ANDs, rename them B,C. They have pure product values; a possible third previously existing index is pure too. A subsequent effective sum whose immediate surviving pin is the earlier sum either joins it to D (three terms) or is absorbed. If its inputs are ANDs, they must share at least one operand with the first sum. With D pure, conjunctions of the sums reduce by (B∨C)(B∨D)=B∨CD, and all remaining factors are products or those same sums. If D depends on B∨C it equals (B∨C)G with G a pure product (including possible B,C factors). Joining it to B gives B∨CG, which implies the first sum; joining it to C is symmetric. Joining it to the first sum absorbs D. In the absence of a second effective sum, all factors use at most the first binary choice. These arguments bound any product at a root, including further D factors: when the root requires D, it implies the first sum and any second sum involving D, so only D's two branches remain. Raw clause factors at the root are common multipliers. This bound also applies to the expansion of H as final product with the available strict ancestors.

At j=3, H and each projected centreless root have at most two batches. For c≥1, U≤3+1+2+2c=2c+j+3.

At j=4, let a count empty selectors ancestral to H. If a=0 then H itself is one product and both empty selectors project outside H. A latest selector is terminal and may affect only one centreless root by least factorization; all others give at most two batches. The terminal root gives at most three by the three-index bound. Thus U≤3+1+1+2c+1≤2c+7 (when c=0 use the raw bound 5). If a=1, H gives at most two products, and the remaining latest selector is again terminal, contributing at most one beyond the 2c count. Thus U≤3+1+2+2c+1=2c+7. If a=2, H gives at most three and all selectors precede S. The 19-card escape argument in the cited visible-S proof forces a centreless root implying S; it cannot realize a core residual with H false. Therefore c≥1 and the total core batches are at most 3+3(c−1)=3c. This gives U≤3c+4≤2c+7 for c≤3; when c≥4 the P≥14 exclusion applies.
