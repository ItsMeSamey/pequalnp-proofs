# Empty raw support with two exits from the same donor

Under the size-21 original card hypotheses, suppose A=x∧S is clean, raw(S) is empty, and both direct dirty-to-OR exits are raw-positive with the same physical direct dirty AND donor D. Let q=3. The remaining paid OR has no direct dirty pin and can be physically pure or inherit mixing. Assume the card reader classification gives at least one original star incident to y or z. Write H for the induced graph deleting x,y,z.

The threshold U≥12 implies at most four stars globally and at most three internal to H. Cut D as a flag at its OR exits. With D=false, raw-positive wires on OR paths evaluate to their projected raw supports. With D=true, every such path containing an exit is true. Thus the two modes of each centreless top other than A are conjunctions of projected clauses, with the second multiplied by the pre-exit product D. A direct multiplicative factor D can likewise be retained explicitly. Each mode is below its clean root on H. The root A vanishes on H.

For ordinary tops, base terms are starred, and the two exit-specific corrections each take the form D times a union of opposite raw guards. Guards for an exit are all anchored by any one raw leaf at that exit. A top cannot see D as an OR alternative on both sides, since then the dirty D alone implies the clean root. All resulting conjunctions share the same exceptional projected raw support, at the third paid OR. There are at most 2(c−1)+2=2c batches in total.

By the sparse-H one-exception lemma, three or fewer internal stars rule out a disjoint double and permit at most one additional touching edge in any disjoint allocation over all these batches. Therefore U≤2c+1+4, counting the four possible near residuals separately. As j≥3, this excludes a violation with U≥12. A violation at U≤11 needs 2c+j+3≤10, hence c=1 with 3≤j≤5, or (c,j)=(2,3).

This partial result concerns a repeated physical donor. Distinct donors can generate a joint mode and require a separate count. No finite enumeration was run for this argument.

## Four internal stars

At U≥11 at most four internal stars exist. Any disjoint double is induced by the one-exception disjoint-pair lemma. Of its four independent corner pairs, at least three require anchored witnesses, since one exceptional clause can reject just one corner. Their anchors are outside the marks, and different anchors use disjoint star incidences at marks. An anchor on two marks witnesses at most one corner; one on three witnesses at most two. A total of four star incidences suffices for three corners only if one anchor sees all four marks. Outside H an anchor sees at most one H vertex. Inside H the only possible anchor is 8 and the only perfect matching in its neighborhood is 14,59. Remove mark 14 from every batch for counting. Within each batch all remaining pairs now touch; the shared binary-exception lemma implies at most one additional edge across the allocation. Putting 14 back gives U≤2c+2+4≤2c+j+3. For U≤10 only c=1, j=3 or 4 can violate.

## Original singleton transfer

Evaluate D on singleton y and singleton z in the original circuit. If D accepts one, call it v. For every ordinary correction D·H_i, each guard leaf in H_i is adjacent to v, because activating v together with that leaf activates a clean top. A leaf equal to v is impossible by singleton cleanliness. In H, v has at most one neighbor (10 for y or 9 for z). With c=1 all internal residuals are therefore incident to that one neighbor, of H-degree three. Adding at most four near residuals gives U≤7.

Suppose D rejects both singletons. At these original inputs every OR-only frontier primitive then has the same evaluations as its raw support, since D alone enters ORs from dirty ANDs. Unfold S through its AND-only factors to primitive ORs or inputs. Every such factor must accept both singletons by bivalence of S, hence its raw support contains y,z. No lambda-anchored primitive can do so because only x is adjacent to both and x's sole reader A has empty raw S. No raw singleton can do so. Any bivalent OR wire is therefore one of the three paid ORs. At either mixer its positive raw-side pin must itself be bivalent because D is false at both original singletons. Following such pins backward terminates at the remaining paid OR Q. Induction over the at most two mixers shows that each bivalent primitive equals Q or Q∨D as a Boolean function on the full original cube. The conjunction of these factors equals one existing factor: choose one realizing Q when present and otherwise any factor. This factor is a strict ancestor of S because S is a dirty AND (there is no raw-empty OR). Bypassing S preserves the original function and contradicts leastness. This excludes the residual cases as well.

This theorem is for A with empty raw S and two distinct raw-positive ORs each reading the same physical dirty AND. Its graph and card hypotheses are those at the start, including an original near star.
