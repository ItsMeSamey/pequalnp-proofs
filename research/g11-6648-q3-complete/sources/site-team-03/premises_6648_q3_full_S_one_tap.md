# Full raw S, two raw-positive direct dirty exits, exactly one tap

Retain the hypotheses of premises_6648_q3_two_direct_taps.md, except that raw(S)={y,z} and exactly one of the two charged mixed ORs OR-reaches S. The other paid OR (q=3) has no dirty AND pin; its projected support is the only potentially unanchored exceptional support. All batches below use original star witnesses for other supports and allocate original residuals. The three edges in the near region outside the two x stars give a ≤3 near bound.

On H, S equals its tapped donor: the tapped OR's raw component and all other raw leaves in S lie in {y,z}, while the other direct exit does not OR-reach S. That donor is clean on H because S is. If an unanchored OR inherited a mixed signal, its raw restriction still fits the same single exceptional binary support in any flag branch: when a donor present on its OR closure is set true the wire becomes true, and otherwise its value is its original raw support. Thus no different exceptional support is created by different flag settings.

First charge tapped-donor positives on H. If the untapped donor D1 feeds the tap's dirty arm D2, write D2=D20∨D1K, D20≤K, by unfolding its AND cone, where K is the product of factors not discharged on setting D1 true. Earlier donor uses as direct factors are allowed; if D1 is multiplicatively required, D20 vanishes and only D1K need be counted. Both terms imply S and are clean on H. If the untapped donor does not enter the tapped one, the latter is a single pure product. On H edges negative at the tapped donor, decrease its exit to the raw arm and preserve output. Only the untapped mixer can supply an ordinary correction; aggregate its donor times the union of opposite raw guards, sharing an original star anchor from its positive raw arm. Every remaining centreless top supplies at most two pure guarded products, using its raw and positive untapped-donor modes. If the remaining donor lies downstream it becomes a pure product after the earlier dirty pin is erased. Expand original primitive mixed pins into their unchanged raw labels and dirty alternatives rather than postulating a new lambda. This gives at most 2c+3 batches (nested dirty tap) or 2c+2 (independent/upstream dirty tap).

S stays live after deleting x on the least 19-gate card and therefore has a use other than A. A direct OR reading S is paid: its side includes both neighbors and its only lambda centre could be x at earlier A. If this OR is the spare exceptional OR, then on H after assigning the tapped donor zero, S is zero and that OR yields just its other existing arm. In the nested case it is downstream of the tapped donor, hence also absent from both tapped-donor batches. In the independent/upstream case the single pure tapped-donor batch lies upstream as well. Thus the only exceptional support ceases to be an exception on every batch; all batches are guarded. If the OR is the untapped mixer it has S as its raw-positive arm. No ordinary correction can use that mixer (raw y,z force unavailable centre x; the tapped mixer is earlier and cannot be a later dirty exit). Its loss of an ordinary slot suffices. If no direct OR reader of S is used, follow a direct AND reader. If this runs multiplicatively into the untapped donor, that donor vanishes on the tapped-negative H slice, likewise dispensing with the ordinary slot. Otherwise a clean centreless top multiplicatively requires S and disappears on that slice, removing both possible batches. In the nested case neither untapped-OR exception can be downstream of S; here one either eliminates the spare exceptional support or removes the entire centreless root. In the nonnested case any saving of even one batch gives at most 2c+1.

With one effective exceptional support and ≤2c+1 batches, c=0,1 use the frozen local/single-exception H bounds (one clean batch ≤2; three jointly ≤5). For c≥2 any putative failure has U≥11, so at most three internal H stars remain after the two x stars. No clean one-exception H conjunction can accept two disjoint original residuals: they must form an induced matching by the anchored-conflict argument, and at least three distinct complementary corners must have anchored rejecting clauses besides the one exceptional witness. Each corner forces stars to its two original marks, and three corners together mark all four endpoints. Anchors are exterior to the four marks and must be H vertices since no near vertex sees two H vertices. Thus at least FOUR different internal stars would be required. All donor batches are now pairwise intersecting families; as in premises_6648_empty_tap_three_junk.md any two incident edges hb,hb' force the exceptional binary OR support on H to be exactly {b,b'}, hence fix h and the only double. Therefore at most 2c+2 H residuals and three near, sufficient.

When the spare OR collapses on H, every batch is fully guarded and at most one double may be allocated if U>7. A nonhomogeneous H double plus xy,xz forces U≤7; a homogeneous double {14,59} plus these two near stars gives U≤10. At 2c+2 batches this always suffices for c≥1,j≥3. At 2c+3 batches it suffices for j≥4 and also for c≥2,j=3 since any failure has U≥11 and no double. The case c=0,j=3 needs its own check rather than importing the U>7 shortcut.

For c=1,j=3 in the nested case, denote the five batches by D20,D1K,D1H0,T00,D1G. Here H0 is the aggregated ordinary guard, and T00,D1G are the two remaining top modes. A putative U≥9 needs six H residuals, all five batches nonempty and one double, necessarily homogeneous. If a multiplicative D1 occurrence kills D20 or T00, there are at most four batches and no failure. Otherwise D20 and T00 use at most two AND gates each (D2 or T and at most the one remaining dirty gate), hence ≤3 primitive clauses each, precluding a double. The remaining three batches all require D1. If any one owns the homogeneous double, each primitive D1 clause meets both 14 and 59; any such complementary marked pair has 8 as unique common anchor, so the full primitive support on H lies in N(8). Only graph edges besides 14 and 59 that can activate D1 on H are 4–10,2–5 and 0–9, requiring positive singleton 4,5,9 respectively. Filling the other two D1 batches therefore forces at least two distinct positive marked singletons. All D1 clauses together can reject at most one of the four independent endpoint transversals (none if the two singletons are mates). The extra factors H0,K,G give at most two further rejecting clauses: H0 is one; for K or G the relevant D2/T plus at most one additional dirty AND gives ≤3 primitive slots before forcing D1, at least one of which has a D1-positive alternative and is removed, since both modes gave distinct allocations. Hence three or fewer distinct transversals can be rejected, contradicting cleanliness of the double. This treats c=1.

The proof as written covers the stated c≥1 cases. The c=0 small-j boundary still requires explicit bookkeeping when a nonhomogeneous double only bounds U≤7 and the target is six.

## Closure of the zero-centreless boundary

If the tapped donor is genuinely downstream of the other mixer, both dirty-to-OR exits precede S. Its external live use on the exact 19-card cannot OR-only reach another ordinary top, since its raw side carries both y,z and the opposite raw leaf would be x, available only at upstream A. Once an external path descends through an AND, there is no later exceptional dirty-AND-to-OR exit, so its first clean top must be centreless. Thus the nested case itself excludes c=0.

In the nonnested case at most one tapped-donor and one ordinary H batch remain. An external-reader saving removes the ordinary batch, or else it removes the exceptional support as above. A single one-exception H batch has at most two edges by the frozen local relaxation. For two fully guarded batches suppose U≥7, the only case needing attention. At most seven internal H stars then remain, since xy and xz are stars. The explicit H corner footprint rows with ≤7 internal stars are the three previously recorded nonhomogeneous patterns, each excluding the other two as well as a separately allocated homogeneous pair. A homogeneous-only double also has unique marked pair. So the two batches together accept at most three allocated H residuals. Adding three near residuals gives six, as required for j=3. This closes the last boundary and supersedes the preceding provisional c=0 qualification.
