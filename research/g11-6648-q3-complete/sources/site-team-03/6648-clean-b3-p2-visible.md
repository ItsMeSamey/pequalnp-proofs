# Clean sole AND at 3, two positive direct entrances and one empty direct entrance

Assume the original least G11 size-21 TopForm, j≥3, q=b=3, sole clean reader x∧S for x=3 with raw(S) nonempty. Write the positive entrances P_i=D_i∨R_i and the empty entrance E=A∨B. Both empty pins are distinct dirty ANDs: no other raw-empty paid OR exists. No OR relevant to a subsequent AND may directly read E: its empty dirty inlet would make it paid; P_i require one dirty AND and one positive-raw pin, neither of which is E. Thus E cannot OR-reach S even through a positive receiver. Its influence on a positive receiver can only reappear through a dirty AND. Count taps by physical P_i outputs reaching S on OR-only paths. Project H=G−{3,6,7}.

Zero taps are impossible. With no dirty input in its OR-only cone S must be y∨z. The common-OR theorem forces the three distinct paid gates B,Ry,Rz all to have positive raw supports, contradicting E (premises_6648_q3_full_S_zero_taps.md).

With two taps D_i imply S, and each is clean on H. Cut the dirty pins of both P_i, simultaneously retaining all other uses of their donors. Every originally donor-positive H edge stays positive at its earliest positive donor; any donor-negative edge sees no change. Thus the two cut donors and the c cut centreless tops cover all H residuals; cut ordinary roots supply only original stars. After cuts E is the only dirty-AND-to-OR injection, occurring only multiplicatively in such roots. Its cut arms are pure anchored products. The positive receivers have retained their original raw supports, with raw-facing pins anchored or singleton (an earlier P is recursively forwarded). All remaining raw clauses retain original star certificates. Each root gives one or two anchored clean H products. By the near-reader result and the H corner result in premises_6648_q3_two_direct_taps.md and premises_6648_q3_visible_two_positive_addendum.md, ≥2 stars are near and, whenever U≥7, at most one exclusive double occurs among clean anchored H products. The count 2c+4 products therefore suffices for j≥5.

For j=3 or 4, if a tapped donor equals an E arm, pay its cut pure product first. On all remaining rows zero this wire globally, so E forwards its other arm. Each additional donor and centreless top then requires just one product. This remains valid whether that arm's cut function was false or nonzero. If both donors coincide, at most c+1 roots before expanding E give ≤2c+2 products. Either reduction suffices at all j≥3. For j=3 these exhaust the possibilities.

For j=4 with four distinct dirty gates A,B,D1,D2, failure requires all 2c+4 nominal modes to be necessary and a double. In particular each donor cut root requires E and has two effective modes. A multiplicative dependency on the other cut donor gives no additional edges after that donor is allocated; hence assume neither. By ancestry neither A nor B can depend on either donor in the cut circuit, as each donor requires E. A multiplicative dependency between A and B would imply absorption already in the original circuit: in the cut graph every AND-only path between dirty ANDs is also original (the forwardings at positive receivers pass their OR or raw positive pins and cannot directly forward any dirty AND). Thus each cut arm has at most two primitive clauses. Each donor with two effective modes is E∧K_i with K_i one primitive clause; a separate A or B factor would force E. Its two modes have at most three clauses. For a centreless root, a cut donor as factor gives nothing new after donor allocation. An A or B factor together with E permits only one effective mode. Hence any root supplying its full two modes also has form E∧K, with just one primitive clause. All these modes have at most three clauses, whereas a double in a clean anchored H product needs four distinct clauses rejecting the independent endpoint orientations. Failure is impossible. This proves U≤2c+j+3 for two taps at every c,j.

One tap is a separate case. Zero and two taps are proved here at source level.

## One tap, one visible label and exactly two near stars

The named-reader proof in premises_6648_q3_visible_two_positive_addendum.md gives two paid individual near readers. They must be P1,P2 (E is raw-empty), and have the form D_i∨w_i with w_i a near raw variable having no starred H neighbor. In the common-AND case neither near variable has a starred H neighbor; in the four-individual case both paid readers belong to the variable lacking a private starred spoke. Every Lambda OR extension of a positive reader then has no H raw leaf: its star centre must neighbor w_i by a starred spoke, hence can only be x or the opposite near variable in the common-AND case, each without a starred H spoke. In the four-individual private-spoke case x is the only possible centre incident to w_i. A path through the other positive gate as a raw-facing OR continuation is impossible since the other positive pin is the named primary input itself. Hence on H every mixed OR-only boundary supplies its corresponding donor, or a disjunction of donors if both meet. They cannot meet: if both reader outputs OR-reached one Lambda gate, the gate's ordinary Lambda witness would have a centre with no H spokes. If the centre is x then that gate OR-reaches S, contradicting exactly one tap. A centre at the opposite near variable needs the two named reader labels to coincide (otherwise a raw self-loop); this occurs only in the four-individual case in which x is the only possible centre. Thus the surviving mixed OR boundaries simply alias the single donor. No ordinary top contributes on H, because any opposite guard to these boundaries would need an original starred H neighbor of w_i; if both sides are mixed with only near raw leaves their dirty alternatives might multiply. For such a top both raw labels must be adjacent by an original star (no self-loop), forcing the common-AND case with different individual raw labels. The tapped positive wire accepts both near singletons, since it is the sole source at the hidden singleton in S and accepts its visible raw label as well. An ordinary top having this wire on one side and the opposite near label on the other would then accept that singleton, impossible. Thus no joint ordinary correction survives.

After restricting H, only E can introduce a genuine disjunction among dirty signals. Its cut arms are pure conjunctions by the aliasing just proved. S is the tapped donor on H and is clean there. The c centreless outputs and the tapped donor each give at most two pure anchored H modes by splitting multiplicative E. Consequently U≤2(c+1)+1+3=2c+6 whenever U≥7, enough for every j≥3; smaller U satisfies the target directly. The general one-tap case with more near stars or two visible labels remains outside this subsection.

## General one-tap upper bounds

Denote the tapped donor by T and the untapped by D, with P the latter positive receiver. On H, T implies S and is clean. For T-positive H residuals use two monotone modes in P (its raw partner, or true retaining D); only one mode is needed if P does not feed T. Each mode costs at most two products: the sole remaining disjunction E has two arms and cannot enter any OR directly, so it is a common multiplicative selector wherever present. If P is earlier than E, both arms of E are evaluated in the same P mode; D then precedes E when P feeds E. If E is earlier than P, D can require E but the true-mode cofactor can simultaneously require that same E: split the shared factor just once. Every cut raw clause retains its original support or is discharged. In the true mode of P a raw partner containing a previous paid outlet must use its appropriate cofactor; P's own donor strictly precedes P and is unchanged.

Forward the tapped receiver to its raw partner on T-negative pairs. The untapped P gives two modes per centreless top and one global ordinary correction comprising D times the union of its opposite raw guards. If the original raw partner of P carried the tapped receiver, its original raw support is unchanged by the projection and supplies the anchor. Dirty disjunction E can again occur only multiplicatively in each resulting mode and its expansion costs at most two products per mode. A repeated positive donor improves this: first assign the donor in at most two products, then globally zero that wire so both positive receivers forward their raw partners. The remaining c tops each take at most two products, and ordinary corrections vanish. Thus repeated donors close U≤2c+6 by the H surplus lemma at U≥7.

For distinct physical donors the general H count is at most 4c+6 (4c+4 if the untapped receiver does not reach T). Near residuals are ≤3. At U≥7 the surplus is at most one; at U≥11 it is zero since a homogeneous double requires four internal H stars in addition to the two near stars. These give necessary conditions on any still-open failure in the one-tap case, not a universal closure. In all cases c≥4 or 2c+j≥11 is immediate from U≤14.
