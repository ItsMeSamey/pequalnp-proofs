# Tight false restrictions at unique-AND critical inputs

The graph has degree-two critical vertices 0,1,2,3 with neighborhoods (9,10), (4,8), (5,10), (6,7) respectively. They are pairwise nonadjacent. The facts here apply to each critical input i whose unique direct reader is an AND; they do not classify inputs with OR readers. Assume the size-21 upper bound and the accepted size-19 lower bound for the i=false cofactor.

Write g_i=i AND w_i. The earlier wire w_i does not depend on i. On singleton assignments to either neighbor a,b it must be true, since the output reacts to i there and g_i is its only channel. Thus w_i dominates a OR b. Replacing g_i everywhere by i AND (a OR b) preserves the graph function: the circuit can only shrink and still accepts every graph edge pair. This is a semantic replacement and can cost a gate.

Setting i=false deletes g_i and a live reader. The 19 lower bound leaves exactly two deletions, so the input circuit has 21 gates, and the restricted circuit is least with 19. The only reader of g_i is M_i=g_i OR s_i. An AND reader would propagate false and require yet another reader to disappear (a false output is impossible); repeated pins are redundant. Both s_i and w_i are independent of i. The restriction bypasses M_i to s_i and otherwise renumbers unchanged gates. Since w_i is a nonraw wire it needs a surviving use or would be dead after restriction.

For every independent assignment Y hitting a or b, s_i(Y)=false: otherwise M_i cannot distinguish the two values of i where the graph function changes. Hence s_i AND (a OR b) is below the restricted graph function. In particular s_i rejects all singletons. A putative singleton r other than a,b would need to neighbor both a,b (test r plus either); their sole common neighbor is i by C4-freeness. The raw support of s_i is empty and M_i also has empty raw support. Any independent positive set of s_i avoids a,b and dominates both of them in G−i.

The three wires g_i,M_i,s_i have the same below-F status. If g_i is clean, TopForm prohibits any downstream AND and M_i must be clean because any dirty OR path reaching the output needs an AND to suppress an independent positive witness. If s_i is clean, trace its backward clean OR closure: it reaches a clean AND because raw inputs are not below F. A dirty M_i would then require a downstream AND, violating TopForm. The converse follows from the OR definition. Thus a dirty g_i gives a paid OR M_i whose other arm is dirty, independent of i, and has a distinct dirty AND within its empty-raw OR closure.

Surviving raw supports on the remaining variables are unchanged, since M_i and s_i both have empty raw support. Original clean gates remain below F on that slice. Only newly clean ANDs can erase additional old residual edges away from i; each contributes at most one raw star, containing at most four edges, and cannot erase both of two disjoint marked edges. The only raw top side that could use i is at g_i. If g_i is dirty its two incident edges are residual; its full gamma fiber is exactly those two incident edges and cannot be the disjoint witness. If g_i is clean there are no downstream ANDs, all surviving AND statuses are unchanged, TopForm persists and both marked residuals and their dirty witness survive whenever their endpoints avoid i.

A dirty AND becoming clean under this restriction must be downstream of M_i, must reject every singleton, and must have exactly one direct reader, an OR. Outside M_i's descendants dirtiness is independent of i: lower i on a dirty witness. At singleton i every noninput gate is already false, so no singleton-positive dirty gate loses all its witnesses. In any least cofactor a clean proper wire d cannot feed an AND d AND x directly: bypassing by d enlarges signals only where the correct output is already true. If d feeds only ORs, projecting d to false while retaining its own earlier cone and restoring d OR projected-output omits all direct readers at the cost of one OR; hence at most one distinct direct reader exists. For downstream gates in this exact restriction every direct reader survives. In particular an original junk wire with a direct AND reader or with two distinct readers cannot newly clean. The eligible single-OR-reader junk gates can be charged by input incidence to at most 2q gate slots; this is not a disjoint count across the four cofactors.

If k of these four unique-AND channels are dirty, their input gates are distinct, and any disjoint gamma witness is different from all of them. Thus j≥k+1 and at least 2k distinct residual edges are incident to them. Each M_i is paid and at most two input gates share one M. Moreover, follow only ANDs backwards from w_i to its OR/input frontier. Every frontier wire accepts both neighbor singletons, so it is an OR, never a raw input. It is paid: any Lambda witness with singleton opposite v would force v adjacent to both a,b and hence v=i, but the unique raw i top candidate g_i is dirty. These singleton-positive ORs differ from all the singleton-rejecting M_i. If M_i=M_j, the two input gates are its operands and w_i,w_j differ, since an external singleton in N(i) minus N(j) distinguishes the two input toggles. If their combined frontier were a single gate, both conjunctions would equal it. Thus for k=1,2,3,4 the respective lower bounds on q are 2,3,4,4.

Every graph edge contains at most one of the four critical vertices, leaving at least two avoiding any fixed disjoint gamma pair. This observation does not ensure that those critical inputs have the unique-AND form. A surviving gate can be newly clean in several cofactors if all its independent positive witnesses contain their critical inputs. The cofactor bounds above do not prove that u≤j+2c+q jointly.
