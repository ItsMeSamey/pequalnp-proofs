# Two raw-positive direct dirty exits both OR-reaching a clean positive-raw S

Assume x=3 has sole clean reader A=x AND S in a least size-21 TopForm realization of G11; y=6,z=7. Assume j≥3, q=3 and precisely two physical paid OR gates M1,M2 take a direct dirty-AND operand each, each with a raw-positive other arm. Suppose both root wires OR-reach S (they are both taps). In particular this argument includes full raw(S)={y,z}. No independence or privacy assumption is made about their donor ANDs.

Both donor functions Di are ≤S, so each is clean on H=G−{x,y,z}: an independent positive H assignment would otherwise stay independent on adjoining x and trigger A. Erase just the two dirty AND-to-OR pins by substituting false; retain the donor wire on any other use. Every originally donor-positive H edge is positive at its first positive donor after substitution, since no earlier donor exit was active. Allocate such residual edges there, and use projected top outputs for the remaining residuals. The projected two donor batches are clean on H by monotonicity. All nonzero projected centreless tops likewise give guarded clean H conjunctions, and any projected ordinary top accepting an edge already stars that edge in the original circuit. All these conjunctions share at most one unanchored binary exceptional clause: the two direct exits collapse to their residual raw arms and the only other possible exceptional OR is one physical original paid gate; its other subclauses are singleton/anchored after projection. Its full restricted H support is a union of two guarded terminal supports. At most c+2 H batches suffice. This argument retains original residual labels rather than using TopForm or minimality of a projected circuit.

There are at most three near residuals. If both neighbors are visible xy,xz already star. If only one neighbor is visible, a common AND supplies yz; in the four-individual reader case at least one is lambda (only three paid), with a second raw leaf different from its own by leastness, excluding the sole x anchor at A and forcing an additional near star. A common OR with a single visible neighbor costs four paid ORs: the two-spoke result forces B,Ry,Rz, and the common OR h cannot itself be lambda without both neighbors visible via A. This is impossible at q=3.

For c≥1 the frozen one-exception H calculations (premises_6648_projection_shared.py, tranche submitted to team 04) give at most 5,7,9 H residuals for c=1,2,3 respectively, by excluding size 6,8,10 with 3,4,5 batches. At c=4 the graph itself has only eleven H edges. For c≥5 the two near stars give U≤14 directly. With the three near residuals this proves U≤2c+j+3.

For c=0 the two one-exception H batches each have at most two residuals, giving U≤7 and sufficing at j≥4. At j=3, if the donors coincide use a single batch. If the later projected batch still multiplicatively requires the earlier donor, its positives are already allocated and add nothing. Otherwise both effective projected AND expansions have at most two of the three original dirty gates: the earlier one lacks the later; the later one lacks the earlier after dirty-to-OR pin deletion, except for the preceding multiplicative case. A connected binary AND DAG on two internal gates has at most three distinct remaining clauses. As shown in premises_6648_empty_tap_three_junk.md, with one common binary exception disjoint residuals require four clauses and a touching double uniquely fixes both mates as that exceptional support and its center by C4. Thus at most one exclusively allocated double; the two donors cover at most three H residuals, and the claimed bound holds at j=3 also.
