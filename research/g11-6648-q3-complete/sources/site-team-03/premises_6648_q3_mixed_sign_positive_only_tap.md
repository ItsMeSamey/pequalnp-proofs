# Visible S with P as unique tap, mixed physical signs

Use the original q=3,j≥3 clean sole x-AND assumptions. E=J1∨J2 is the empty physical exit, P=D∨V the positive physical exit, Q the only remaining paid OR and E does not OR-reach S. E is the earliest empty OR, with two distinct dirty inputs; no other empty OR can be formed without an extra physical entrance or duplicate pins. D is clean on H since D≤S and x can adjoin any independent H input. On H, S=D. There are ≥2 near stars and ≤3 near residuals. Whenever all H batches are anchored, U≥7 gives ≤one additional allocated double, and U≥11 gives none.

When Q does not directly read E there is no restoration of E into a positive raw OR region except by passage through an AND into P. If E does not feed D, all routes from E are uninterrupted AND paths to clean tops. The original common-factor fusion lemma (singleton_6648_clean_positive_s_last_empty.md) gives exactly one such centreless owner. Allocate D first, then the owner splits into two pure products; ordinary tops give stars and other centreless roots one each. The c+2 batches share at most one binary exception Q. c≥1, and F(1,3/4/5)≤5/7/9 with the graph bound for larger c give the target.

If E feeds D without restoration, D=E·K for pure-product K; allocate two clean products JiK and then two per remaining centreless root on the D-negative slice, all with one common exception. Thus n≤2c+2. At c≥2 any violation has ≥11 original residuals and ≤3 internal stars, permitting one extra beyond n. At c=0,1 and j≥4 use F(1,2)≤4 and F(1,4)≤6 (four-star strengthening from the visible two-positive index). At c=0,1,j=3 the only dirty ANDs are Ji,D: each Ji has only its own two pure factors, since a multiplicatively required other Ji would make E redundant. K has at most one factor: D directly requires E and its other input is a primitive or a Ji, the latter giving D=Ji contrary to leastness. Each JiK therefore has at most three factors. Each centreless top directly requires D,Ji or E. A D-owner vanishes; a Ji-owner inherits only one mode, sufficient at c=1 by F(1,3)≤5. An E-owner splits into two three-factor products (its residual other pin is one primitive, D vanishes, Ji would make B redundant). A ≤3-factor clean one-exception product cannot admit disjoint residuals and all touching doubles share the same pair by C4. Thus U≤2c+2+1+3. These arguments use no original disjointness beyond the explicitly distinct three gates in this j=3 case.

Otherwise Q=E∨R, where R has positive raw support. If E feeds D by any route, J1,J2 and Q's raw fallback are pure upstream except that Q may be downstream of D when E reaches D only multiplicatively. In every case on freezing E as a flag D=D0∨J1K∨J2K, with D0≤K; a mandatory E kills D0. Ji are pure when P follows E. On the D-negative slice shut P's dirty inlet; Q now provides the single mixed E alternative. Each ordinary correction is Ji times the same anchored opposite guard from Q. Each centreless root has three modes (pure base or one Ji); a direct E root needs two, direct Ji one and direct D zero. When Q is downstream of S or carries S, see the liveness rule below instead of assuming Q upstream. No residual exception survives: Q's raw fallback R is itself an existing anchored support once P is cut, or its original pure fallback when computing upstream terms. P has only its existing raw other pin in zero mode.

The exact x-false 19-card demands a live external consumer of S. With full visible labels, an OR-only lambda extension starting from S is impossible since its only candidate anchor is x via earlier A. With one visible label u, S=P=D∨u; P accepts the hidden singleton as well. Any ordinary top reached from S by ORs must therefore have opposite raw label x and be A. A lambda extension must reach A, cannot import E by ORs (unique P tap), and can add only the same raw label u, so equals S. Follow a live external path. If its first new OR beyond those aliases is Q, then R=S. Q cannot reach an ordinary top other than A since it is bivalent, and cannot reach A by ORs since E is untapped. On D-negative H rows Q=E, so there are at most two products per centreless owner, no ordinary corrections. If an initial AND path reaches one Ji before any exit, that Ji vanishes on the D-negative slice because it AND-requires S (which equals D on H). Otherwise a centreless owner AND-requires S, and all its projected modes vanish. An intervening clean top is already such an owner. These possibilities exhaust the paths because P is upstream of S and E,Q are the sole remaining potential interruptions.

When E does not feed D, the initial allocation costs just one pure product D. For the three possibilities just listed the total bounds are 1+2c, 1+1+2c, and 1+2+3(c−1)=3c respectively. In the middle case only one Ji remains, hence one ordinary correction and two modes per root. One assigned double and at most three near edges suffice for c≤2; at c=3 U≥13 precludes any double; at c≥4 use U≤14 directly. If D=Ji then that arm may be set false on D-negative rows.

When E feeds D via Q, both E and Q precede S, so the only live external possibility is a vanishing centreless owner. This gives n≤3+2+3(c−1)=3c+2. If E feeds D solely multiplicatively and Q is not upstream, the initial cost is only two JiK batches; the Q=E∨S possibility entails at most 2+2c terms with no ordinary correction and otherwise the same owner saving holds. E cannot be downstream of S in this case. These latter counts meet the target by the same small-j three-factor bound or the bounds below.

For the vanishing-owner count c≥4 is immediate. For c=1, j≥4 gives n+1+3≤9. For c=2,j≥4, n≤8 and a putative U≥12 rules out doubles. For c=3,j≥5 use U≤14; at j=4 assign distinct raw-empty direct predecessors to the surviving owners. They come from E,J1,J2,D and at most one other dirty gate, so at most one surviving owner is unrestricted; the other costs ≤2, giving n≤10 and U≤13 at the failure threshold. For c=3,j=3 the two surviving owners cost at most 2+1 so n≤8 and U≤12. Assignment is by original direct clean pins; two clean tops cannot share a pin by fusion.

For c=1,2,j=3 the three dirty ANDs are exactly J1,J2,D. When all three D products are needed, D has two primitive inputs after exposing the E alternative: any direct Ji on the other pin implies E and absorbs the mixed pin. D0 has at most two clauses, and JiK at most three since at least one pin is satisfied on E=true. A multiplicatively required E suppresses D0 and leaves only one other pin. Ordinary Ji times guard has at most three factors. The only extra surviving centreless top, if present, costs at most two terms. If it costs two it directly requires E, with a single other primitive after D's elimination; another Ji absorbs E and D vanishes. Thus in the maximum count of 5 or 7 all batches have ≤3 clauses and no anchored double. If a four-clause term from a surviving Ji-root occurs, that root has only one mode and the one remaining possible double is absorbed by its saved slot. Hence U≤8 or 10. In the Q=E∨S multiplicative case at j=3 the D products again have ≤3 factors; Q is E on D-negative H rows, all pure supports guarded, and every surviving owner directly requires E or a Ji. The same slot argument works (at most one two-mode E owner, other Ji owners one each). At c=0 this special case has just two D products with ≤3 factors and no H double. This closes P-only for every c,j, conditional on the original card, footprint and F bounds.
