# A disjoint bookkeeping index for q=3

This is a classification of original circuits rather than a proof of any unsolved row. It uses the established size-21 sole-reader classification for x=3. All supports, physical pin identities and causal tests refer to the original DAG before specialization.

Record each of the three paid ORs once in increasing physical gate index. For each record its raw support, ordered physical predecessor identities, and the subset of those *two pins* which are dirty AND outputs. Call a record direct if this last subset is nonempty. Let b be the number of direct records, p their number with nonempty raw support, and w the number of positive nondirect records. Thus p≤b≤3 and w≤3−b. Classify every nondirect record as physically pure or inherited, inspecting whether an AND occurs in its entire input cone. A positive direct record has exactly one dirty AND pin. An empty direct record can have two dirty pins or one dirty pin plus an earlier empty OR. Neither the number of physical suppliers nor the number of dirty gate-pin incidences equals b in general.

| x's sole reader | Necessary primary signatures | Distinguished owner data |
|---|---|---|
| Clean A=x AND S | 0≤p≤b≤3 | Original raw(S) is empty, one visible neighbor (also record which), or both. A physically pure S is a separate subtype inside the both-visible row. |
| Dirty g=x AND S | 1≤b≤3, 0≤p≤b−1 | g has unique reader M=g OR D, an empty direct record. Record D separately from S, including whether D is a dirty AND or empty OR. |
| M=x OR D with dirty AND donor D | 1≤p≤b≤3 | M is a distinguished positive direct record. No condition on whether other direct readers share a donor unless verified physically. |
| M=x OR D with empty OR donor D | 1≤b≤2, 0≤p≤b−1, w≥1 | M is a positive inherited record. The empty OR cone of D has an earliest member, which must read a dirty AND and be an empty direct record. When b=2, D itself must be one of the two directs since M occupies the sole nondirect position. |

The OR-donor split uses the sole-OR proof that D is singleton-negative, not a primary input, and has a dirty independent witness. Clean AND ancestry through this cone is excluded by the later AND on a live path from the dirty M. The bounds describe necessary candidates and do not assert existence of all indexed cells.

**All-paid-empty test.** Apply that theorem only when p=w=0. At b=3 this reduces to p=0. At b=2,p=0 the third paid gate may be a pure positive OR or an inherited positive join, neither covered by the all-empty theorem. At b=0 any physically impure OR below a top would have a first direct OR after a dirty AND; hence the all-pure theorem applies instead. The whole one-physical-direct theorem covers b=1 independently of p,w. To disjointize overlapping proven families, process b=0, then b=1, then the p=w=0 rows of b≥2, then the remaining structural rows.

**Clean owner.** At b=2,p=2 the reviewed source partitions first by raw(S) and then by the subset of the two directs reaching S through OR-only paths (including equality); also retain their causal order, position relative to each other's raw/donor pin, whether donors are identical, and the number of near stars. Raw-empty S has no positive OR-only tap. A mixed path to S through an intervening AND is not an OR-only tap. For p=0 or 1 use the same signature with positive and empty taps distinguished individually. With three direct records, p counts only positive ones; retain donor coincidences and whether an empty gate forwards an earlier empty OR. At all b a pure common S, exact equality S=an exceptional wire, or two OR paths from one receiver are physical predicates, not replacements for p.

**Dirty-AND owner.** Distinguish M from other records. For each of the others record reach into S and D *separately*; a supplier can reach both. Merely preceding M in the list does not make a gate an ancestor of S, nor does being downstream of S make it a downstream exit of M. If two direct gates exist, the spare is either pure or inherited. In the inherited case test whether M feeds it, and independently whether the other direct Q reaches its other pin avoiding M. A chain Q→S→g→M→W or Q→D→M→W supplies no separate Q entrance at W. Keep actual equality of D with an empty record and the bypass through an AND to D when the incoming Q is positive.

**Sole-OR owner.** Retain for each secondary its complete causal relation to D and to M individually. Zero upstream of D means no secondary is a proper ancestor of D; it permits later or incomparable secondaries, and depends on physically counting D as self rather than a proper ancestor. Do not infer all secondary signals are descendants of M, or that their raw arms avoid D. Distinguish escape through another direct pin, an ordinary top, or a centreless top via actual reader paths; several escapes may coexist.

**Unambiguous path signature for every case.** To normalize descriptions, for each named pair of wires (including each predecessor pin of named owners) keep flags for equality, a proper OR-only path, a proper path through an AND, and no path (both preceding path flags false). The two proper-path flags may both hold in a reconvergent circuit. For each needed gate-avoidance or first-exit test, compute these flags after prohibiting the interiors of the other named gates; preserve the endpoints even when they are named. For distinguishing an AND-only supplier implication, also record the AND-only path flag rather than treating every path through an AND as multiplicative. Include ordinary/centreless aggregation boundaries when testing exits and distinguish source and receiving pin. Gates have unique physical order, flags are Boolean, and all simultaneous relations are retained, so this constructs a unique key of every circuit. Named flags provide coverage, not authority to apply a theorem whose local reader, purity, or guard assumptions were not checked.

The three-direct mode projection is an interface for each of the b=3 entries. Its mere construction does not prove the count in every cell; separate the mode identity from its charging estimate. Likewise the clean two-positive theorem is scoped to the clean column, b=2,p=2. It does not fill the dirty-AND or sole-OR cells by relabeling.
