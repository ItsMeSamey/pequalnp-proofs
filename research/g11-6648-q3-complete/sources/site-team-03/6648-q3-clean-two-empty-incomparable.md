# Clean sole x3 AND with two empty receivers and one positive receiver

Assume G11, the issued least TopForm size-21 circuit, q=3 and sole clean g=x∧S at x=3. Suppose all exceptional ORs are distinct direct entrances: two raw-empty gates E1,E2 and one raw-positive P=H∨R. Suppose their five dirty suppliers are distinct, so j≥5. If the receivers are pairwise ancestry-incomparable, U≤2c+j+3 for every S. For centreless g the same bound holds without incomparability provided at most one Ei is an ancestor of H.

In the incomparable case each Ei has no further dirty-AND-to-OR exit on any path before the first clean top. In particular a pre-top OR directly reading the raw-empty Ei would be exceptional and comparable. All such paths are AND-only, terminal at centreless tops. The least clean-suffix factorization (6648-shared-donor-reader-bound.md) shows each Ei and every dirty multiplicative descendant has at most one next AND reader until termination. Thus each Ei affects exactly one centreless top. They may meet at the same top and their suppliers may share arbitrary pure conjunctive ancestors. There is at least one centreless top. Each supplier is a pure product with singleton/originally Lambda-anchored primitive clauses, as none of the other exceptional ORs precedes it. R is pure. A pure positive primitive gate not Lambda would require a fourth charged OR. The ordinary correction is at most one anchored product H times the union of opposite guards of ordinary tops reached by P, anchored by a raw vertex of R; raw-empty Ei signals cannot directly enter ordinary OR-positive operands.

If g is centreless, reserve its two x edges and apply at most two P branches to each other centreless root. Each empty receiver doubles only its terminal root's count. If both empty receivers meet at one root, the two-branch root gains at most six additional batches; if they meet different roots the additional count is at most four. Counting an unused root can only relax the estimate. Together with the ordinary correction there are at most 2(c−1)+6+1 batches. Since no original edge at x is starred, for U≥8 the no-x-star footprint permits one surplus globally, giving U≤2c+8≤2c+j+3. With c≥1 the smaller U range is immediate.

If g is ordinary, raw(S) is nonempty and lies in {y,z} where y=6,z=7. S cannot be purely raw OR: singleton sensitivity would force physical h=y∨z, and the certified common-OR card lemma (premises_6648_common_or_all_c.md) requires three distinct paid raw-positive readers of this region, whereas only P is positive. Thus S is a positive OR with a dirty injection. On its OR-only paths a raw-empty Ei cannot supply an AND contribution to S unless first passing through a positive paid gate; since P has a direct dirty pin the only dirty supplier that OR-reaches S is H at P. In particular H≤S, and H is clean on the eight-vertex deletion of x,y,z by adjoining x to any purported independent positive there. On this deletion charge H-positive residuals in one anchored batch. Replace P by R on the H-negative pairs. Ordinary tops yield only original stars. Centreless tops yield at most c+3 anchored batches: each Ei doubles only its own terminal's single remaining raw branch, giving at most three extras. All evaluations here use original star labels and clean conjunctions on the deletion.

At most three residuals touch y,z. If S shows both, xy,xz are starred. If S shows one, its spoke is starred and the common-AND reader of y,z would itself star yz. In the four-individual-OR alternative each variable has a Lambda reader (only one positive charge); at a raw reader of the omitted neighbor its witness cannot be x, since no ordinary x top shows that omitted neighbor. This supplies a second near star. The common-OR raw pair is excluded as above, or would itself require a fourth paid gate if only one neighbor were visible. For a nonhomogeneous eight-vertex double, its ≥7 original footprint stars lie inside the deletion because no exterior anchor has two core neighbors. Together with the near stars U≤7. Otherwise at most one homogeneous double is allocated over all batches. Thus U≤3+1+(c+3)+1=c+8≤2c+j+3.

For the broader centreless-g class, no receiver incomparability is required. The regime lemma disposes of c≥3 when j≥5; smaller c=1 has only the ordinary correction outside the two x edges. Its raw guard union lies in a single original vertex neighborhood with its incident anchor edges starred. At most six x-free graph edges can meet this guard (6648-lone-raw-positive-charge-projections.md), hence U≤8. At c=2 the single other centreless root expands into at most eight anchored products by the three-receiver joint-modes lemma. The sole ordinary correction H times its anchored raw guard expands to at most two products whenever at most one of E1,E2 precedes H. No unassigned global dummy obligations are introduced. A violation U>j+7 would force U≥13, leaving at most three original star edges. Any anchored double requires at least four distinct original stars, one to each of four exterior-matched endpoints, so no batch can receive a double. Then U≤2+8+2=12, a contradiction.

These source-only deductions cover the stated sign/ancestry classes. They do not assume the remaining nested cases satisfy the same batch counts. No search or compilation was used.

## Extension to every placement of the two empty receivers

The following additional arguments remove the incomparability condition throughout, retaining the five distinct physical donor ANDs.

For raw-visible S the impossibility of a physically pure S and the bivalent/card near-star count above persist. On an OR-only path to positive S, the sole point at which a dirty AND can introduce a nonraw alternative is P. A putative raw-empty Ei cannot enter a positive region at any other OR, since such a gate would be another nonempty exception. Thus S on H8 equals H on that slice, and H is clean there. If at most one Ei lies upstream of H, H has at most two anchored mode products. At least one other Ei does not precede H; take a latest such gate. It has no other exceptional descendant: it cannot enter P's pure R, cannot precede H or an Ei entering H, and cannot precede a later Ei outside that cone. Its AND-only paths end at a unique centreless top as above. On H-negative pairs P forwards R. The other Ei allows up to two batches at every centreless top; the single terminal Ei doubles only its own root, adding at most two more. Thus there are at most 2+2c+2 core batches. At most three near residuals and at most one extra homogeneous double give 2c+8; a nonhomogeneous double forces U≤7.

If both Ei precede H, all three exceptions lie upstream of S or at S. The tight 19-card requires S to retain some path to a clean top other than A: otherwise it dies along with A and the sole OR reader of A, losing at least three gates at x=false. An OR directly reading S on this escape before any other AND would be dirty, and could not be Lambda by its singleton y,z positivity and the unique possible opposite raw center x, available only at A. Hence there is no such OR. After an AND, a later OR before the first clean top would need another dirty-AND entrance, unavailable. The terminal top is centreless: if it directly consumes S with opposite positive raw pin then that pin would likewise need center x and the top be A; if there is an intervening AND the terminal pin is raw-empty. This root implies S, hence cannot cover any H-negative residual on H8. In particular c≥1. H itself expands into at most four clean anchored terms on H8 by the E1,E2 choices, and each remaining c−1 centreless top into at most four after P→R. Ordinary roots give stars there. With three near residuals and one possible homogeneous double this gives U≤4c+4. This suffices when c≤2 since j≥5. For c≥3 the established regime index closes the branch. This proves all raw-visible S placements.

For centreless A use the earlier c≤1 bound and c≥3 discharge, independent of ancestry. In a possible c=2 failure put s=16−U and p=j+2c+3. The indexed region gives j=5 or 6. At j=5 require s≤3 and the exact gate identity 2t+lambda=22−p=10; at j=6 require s≤2 and 2t+lambda=9. Here t counts ordinary tops. At least two stars touch y or z: with four different raw OR readers each of y,z has a Lambda reader because only one paid OR is raw-positive; if their sole incident star were yz, another raw leaf in that Lambda reader would yield a second star at its witness center. With common AND(y,z) it already stars yz, and at least one other raw OR reader is Lambda with the same second-star argument. A common OR(y,z) can neither be Lambda without an x star nor one of the three direct dirty-AND entrances.

With at most three starred edges, the star graph therefore has maximum degree two and has no triangle. A three-spoke center outside y,z would have to neighbor both y,z, forcing x as a center even though no star touches x. Center y or z has only two neighbors other than x. No graph triangle at y or z survives removal of x. Thus its shapes with three edges are a matching, a 2-edge path plus disjoint single edge, and a 3-edge path.

Fix one raw r in P's pure arm R. Every singly or multiply used raw-positive wire of support B computes either raw OR(B) or H∨OR(B). The latter alternative requires r∈B. This concerns actual wires, not merely raw projections: the only possible dirty contribution into a positive OR cone is through P, since anything directly reading an originally raw-empty wire needs a charge and only P restores raw support. P's arm R itself is pure. A Lambda OR with singleton raw support would combine comparable variants of the same singleton and be redundant. By leastness duplicate functions are forbidden. Therefore each nonsingleton B hosts at most one pure Lambda function and at most one mixed variant when r∈B. In a 3-edge path the only possible such supports are the two neighbor pairs of the internal vertices, which are disjoint; hence lambda≤3. The path-plus-edge has only one such support (lambda≤2), and the matching has none. With two or fewer stars lambda≤2.

A top with two crossed starred edges uses a fixed pair of leaves and a singleton center. There can be at most one physical top for the same underlying two-spoke star, since only the side containing r can have both pure/mixed versions. The other side is identical, and two clean products with a shared input fuse for a strict saving in the clean OR aggregation. The same argument forbids identical input functions at any two different clean tops, regardless of where they appear on the pins. Let m count two-spoke tops. The t ordinary tops require 2t−m distinct singleton input functions. At most |V(stars)|+1 exist (one pure per used vertex and a potential extra mixed version at r). On a 3-edge path there are four vertices and m≤2; on a path-plus-edge there are five with m≤1; on a matching of three there are six and m=0. Hence t≤3 in every case. With at most two stars, the matching has at most four vertices and m=0; a 2-edge path has three vertices and m≤1. Thus t≤2. It follows that 2t+lambda≤9 for s≤3, and ≤6 for s≤2. Both numerical candidates contradict the exact gate identity. This closes the final centreless placements and proves the full 0,0,+ five-distinct-donor sign class.
