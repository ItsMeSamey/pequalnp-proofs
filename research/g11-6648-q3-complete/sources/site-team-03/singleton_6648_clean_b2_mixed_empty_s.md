# Clean sole AND, raw-empty S, q=3 with one positive and one empty direct receiver

Assume the official least size-21 TopForm G11 circuit. Write A=x AND S for the clean sole reader of x=3, raw(S) empty; y=6,z=7. The two distinct physical ORs with dirty AND inputs are positive P=J OR R and empty E=K OR L. The remaining paid OR F has no direct dirty AND input. We prove U≤2c+j+3. Here A is centreless, c≥1. The clean A forces S to accept y and z individually and to be clean on the full H-cube, H=G minus x,y,z. The tight 19-card needs S live outside A after setting x=false. The original star set contains no x edge. There are five edges outside H.

An earliest raw-empty OR must read dirty ANDs. Any nondirect later raw-empty OR would have two empty OR inputs (clean AND forbidden), both necessarily E, so cannot survive leastness. Thus F is positive and E has two distinct dirty AND pins. A physical empty-to-positive OR transition directly using E must be F; in that event F=E OR V, with positive V. Conversely, if F is not such a direct reader, E cannot inject a dirty signal into an OR-only positive region except through the dirty supplier of P (if causally earlier). A paid gate with two positive pins may be genuinely pure or inherit J through ORs.

At least one near edge (incident to y or z) is starred: a common AND stars yz, or at least two of the four separate raw OR readers are uncharged. A common OR is excluded in this raw-empty-S case: it cannot be lambda without a star at x and the two other individual ORs plus its card reader are all paid by premises_6648_common_or_all_c.md, making a fourth paid gate besides the common OR itself. If there is only one near star, P and F must both be direct individual y/z OR readers. With a common AND the sole star is yz, and any lambda individual reader would need its other raw leaf, distinct by irredundancy, also starred by its anchor, impossible. With four separate OR readers a sole yz star similarly forbids any lambda individual reader; for a sole private spoke, both readers of the other triangle neighbor are necessarily paid. These observations use the original 19/15-card reader classification.

For batches of globally clean pure anchored products on H, a disjoint double forces four internal spokes if homogeneous (uniquely 14,59) and at least seven if nonhomogeneous. Original anchors must lie in H since no near vertex has two H neighbors. Thus with at least one near star a nonhomogeneous double gives U≤8. Otherwise use at most one total homogeneous surplus. With an extra common binary exceptional support F whose two child supports are individually anchored or singleton, the one-exception lemma allows at most two marks per product. At ≤3 internal stars no disjoint double can occur (it needs at least three anchored corner obligations, costing ≥4); all incident doubles fix the exact same two surviving F leaves and their unique common center. Thus there is at most one exclusive surplus across such batches.

## F does not directly read E

Each positive raw/OR wire computes its raw support optionally joined with the single dirty supplier J. Whenever J is present, the wire carries all raw leaves of R. E, if influential, enters via J rather than independently. Project E by choosing an actual arm globally and P by forwarding raw or forcing true under its projected supplier guard. Chronological evaluation allows P and E to nest in either order; an empty arm chosen after P may depend on its mode, and if earlier its choice is used everywhere. These four modes give at most four clean products per non-A centreless top. Ordinary residuals require only J times the union of opposite raw guards, anchored by a single original R leaf. This yields at most two products after choosing E's arm. No ordinary top can have P on both sides (positive singleton). The sole possible exceptional clause is the same physical F: in a raw P projection or on a P-free OR path it retains its original raw support; on a high P path F becomes true. All surviving children of F are anchored or singleton, including a child equal to P forwarded to its raw-facing operand. If F precedes P its raw support can occur in the projected J or R but remains that one exception. These products are clean because the guarded global projections only shrink their original clean terms.

At c=1, the two x edges are the only A edges, and all others must be ordinary corrections: their two terms have ≤2 residuals apiece on the full original graph by the one-exception lemma, yielding U≤6. With two or more near stars and c=2, failure implies U≥11 so at most three internal H stars. There are ≤4+2 batches, hence ≤7 H marks and ≤3 near marks, sufficient. At c≥3 the target p=2c+j+3≥12. A failure with p=12 or 13 leaves at most three or two stars, respectively. Use the physical one-optional-signal count in 6648-q3-clean-two-empty-incomparable.md (last paragraphs): with two near stars and no x star the star shape at ≤3 is a matching, path or path-plus-edge; ordinary positive signals have at most the unique optional J and carry R when mixed. Those are precisely its hypotheses for 2t+lambda≤9 at ≤3 stars, ≤6 at ≤2. These contradict the size identity 2t+lambda=22−p. For p≥14 a failure leaves ≤1 star, inconsistent with two near stars.

With a single near star, F is a direct individual raw near reader. On H that pin vanishes, forwarding F's other pin in all modes, so there is no effective exception. There is no ordinary correction on H either: P directly reads a near raw vertex. With sole yz its opposite raw guard has no H-star to that leaf. With a sole private spoke it reads the other near vertex which has no ordinary star at all. Thus there are at most 4(c−1) H batches and four near marks. For c=2 a violation gives ≤4 internal stars, hence at most one homogeneous surplus and U≤9. For c=3 strict failure leaves ≤2 internal stars, hence no doubles, and U≤12. For c≥4 a failure leaves ≤1 total star. Singleton OR variants carrying only the one optional J are comparable and lambda cannot build a nontrivial union there. On a lone star uv at most one endpoint can vary its singleton signal, since R is nonempty, so all ordinary tops share the other physical signal and factor to one. Thus t≤1, lambda=0 and the exact size identity is impossible (p≤15 in a failure).

## F directly restores E to positive reach, F=E OR V

All joint mode clauses here are originally anchored or singleton. For a positive cut J=false or E=false forwarding the respective raw operand gives its guarded raw support (possibly still carrying the other signal); at a true cut use the actual supplied arm or product as obligation. No third, unanchored raw OR is created. In particular a product with only E and F dirty can be expanded as at most three products: one E-false base and two E-true choices, using a single consistent choice everywhere. If a positive product is taken before E, only P can contaminate its primitive clauses.

First let S be a dirty AND, distinct from E. Since it has another direct reader besides A, there must be an AND-only route from S to a charged direct receiver. Otherwise every path from S to its first clean top is AND-only, and factoring S once around all its affected tops saves a gate from its ≥2 original direct AND readers (the last-empty factoring proof 6648-q2-clean-centreless-empty-mixer.md §3). F cannot be the first OR after an AND. Conversely S cannot precede both direct receivers, because then its primitive clauses are pure and none can accept both y,z: an anchored clause containing both would require anchor x, unavailable. Thus one direct receiver is upstream of S and the other downstream via an AND-only route. Endpoint cases with S itself a direct supplier are included.

If P precedes S and E follows it, every primitive of S must carry J through P: a pure primitive cannot accept both singleton neighbors. Thus J≤S and S can be counted on H by its two anchored modes. An arm of E implies S along the downstream AND route. On S-negative H inputs cut S and J to false, P to raw; E then disappears or reduces to its other, now pure anchored conjunctive arm. F becomes at most one positive selector with that arm as donor. Its remaining ordinary correction gives ≤1 mode (fixed anchor in the positive partner); the c−1 other centreless tops give ≤2 each. Total H batches ≤2+1+2(c−1). Adding ≤4 near and one homogeneous bonus gives U≤2c+6. A nonhomogeneous H double already gives U≤8.

If E precedes S and P follows it, every primitive of S is E or carries E through F; otherwise it cannot be bivalent. Therefore E≤S. S yields ≤3 protected H products as above. J requires S by the AND-only path to P. On S-negative H inputs put S,E,J false. All ordinary tops then give only original stars; each non-A centreless top gives one anchored product. Total H batches ≤3+c−1. Adding ≤4 near and one homogeneous bonus gives U≤c+7≤2c+6. Nonhomogeneous doubles as before.

It remains S=E (the only possible OR of empty reach). If P is not an ancestor of E, its two dirty arms are pure anchored products implying E and hence clean on H. Neither can accept both y,z because all primitive supports are anchored or singleton. Each accepts one of these singletons since E accepts both. Such a product accepts at most one residual H edge: two would be disjoint, and the four rejecting-corner clauses would each contain the fixed singleton as well as two H marks. Adjacent corners then force the same original anchor, needing five neighbors. Count these two arms and, on E-negative H, forward F to V. Only P remains: the usual single-selector expansion gives one ordinary and at most two products per other centreless top, including where its raw arm initially carried F. This gives at most 2c+1 H edges before the possible single surplus, enough with ≤4 near.

Finally if P precedes E=S, F is bivalent and cannot affect an ordinary top through ORs, whose opposite raw vertex would need to be x. There are ≥2 near stars: with only one F would directly read y or z as its raw partner, but E already dominates that singleton and F would be redundant. If c=1, the only H residuals are the ordinary correction for P, a single anchored clean product since its supplier J precedes E; ≤2 H marks suffice. For c≥2 E-positive H marks come from its two arms expanded in the two P modes, ≤4 products. On E-negative H, F forwards V, P remains; there is one ordinary and ≤2 products per other centreless top. Thus nominal H count ≤2c+3. Any failure has U≥11, which with ≥2 near stars rules out even the four-internal-star homogeneous double. Adding ≤3 near gives U≤2c+6. This finishes both classifications for every c,j without numerical enumeration.
