# Diagonal reduction and an obstruction to a single cut

Let d be the diagonal of a symmetric binary guard matrix A. Summing all guards gives d·x=0. The matrix A+ddᵀ is alternating and agrees with A on every vector satisfying this constraint. Hence the two-marker problem on A is equivalent to the alternating problem on the same vertices with the two original markers and the additional even marker d. If d belongs to the span of the two given marker rows, the specified target either contradicts d·x=0 or makes that extra constraint redundant. In the latter case the existing alternating solver applies.

More generally, compute the span of attainable two-bit labels on each connected component using the one-marker model basis. A component with label span of dimension at most one attains that whole span, since zero is attainable. A component with full span attains at least three of the four labels. Adding any label set of size at least two from a different component then realizes every label: a translate of the second set must intersect the first set because their sizes sum to more than four. Thus the only case that requires a full-span component's individual two-marker attainable set has all other components contributing zero.

The single cut used for alternating matrices does not always generalize to nonzero diagonals. A graph on vertices 0 through 4 has edges 01,12,14,24,34 and marked vertices 0,2,3. Require x₀+x₂+x₄=1 and x₂=1. In binary mask notation the rows are (3,21,22,24,14), and the diagonal mask is 13. The assignments satisfying the two markers and diagonal parity are exactly 12,14,21,23. Their sets of violated guards, written as masks in that order, are 12,10,17,6. All four are nonempty, so the instance is infeasible. Yet 12⊕10⊕6=0. No fixed subset of the guard equations can sum to one throughout this affine slice, since its inner products with those three masks would sum to zero. For a symmetric graph, summing vertex guards across a subset is precisely its induced cut parity plus its selected diagonal parity. This rules out even an arbitrary single such cut as a complete certificate under the diagonal and marker constraints. It leaves algorithms using several cuts or additional deductions open.
