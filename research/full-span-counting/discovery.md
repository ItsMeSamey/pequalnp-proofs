# Discovering high-degree intersection features without global rank

Let H be a bounded-width core, V the wide-clause violation indicators and Q their sum. Let U denote at least one violation, with expectations denoting unnormalized sums over H models.

**One missing conjunction.** Suppose f is a supplied rational combination of p nonconstant positive monomials. Compute L=E[Q(1−f)²]. If L=0 the fit is exact. Otherwise form C from all coordinates i for which E[Q(1−f)²(1−V_i)]=0, using differences of two moment expansions. This is the intersection of the profiles in the support of the nonnegative residual weight M=Q(1−f)². If the unknown target has U=f+cV_S for one nonempty S and c≠0, the residual-support profiles are exactly those containing S. Thus S⊆C, all such profiles contain C, and any realized profile containing C contains S and has residual c. In particular C is nonempty. Obtain c=E[M(1−f)]/E[M] and verify the updated fit by a zero Q-weighted loss. This returns an equivalent actual conjunction using polynomially many pinned core counts in n,m,p, with no dependence on its unknown degree or full candidate rank. Under a failed promise the verification prevents a false positive.

**Column generation.** If f is an exact least-squares optimum on the current dictionary and loss is positive, peel a maximal positive-residual profile T as in pattern-repair.md. E[Q(1−f)V_T] is nonzero since only T contributes nonzero residual among its supersets. Normal-equation orthogonality proves the new monomial is outside the current feature span. Adding it strictly decreases the refitted loss. One can remove coordinates as long as the signed gradient stays nonzero on a nonempty conjunction. The actual number of additions is an a posteriori parameter; no optimum-sparsity bound follows from maximality alone.

**Sparse predictors imply short covers.** If a k-term combination with nonempty monomials correctly predicts U outside s distinct positive profiles, choose one representative coordinate per term. Every correctly predicted positive profile must contain a designated representative, since otherwise all terms vanish. Thus at most s positive profiles evade the selected set of at most k original clauses. Conversely the OR of k representatives has at most 2^k−1 monomials of degree at most k.

To search, provision k and s. At a node with selected clauses A, restrict the base to H plus those clauses (their violation bits are zero). Use the known signed-selector conversion on these at most k arbitrary-width clauses, costing O(k) variables. Positive pinned-moment queries are still exact in the new base. Peel positive profiles and their masses until exhausted or until s+1 distinct profiles are found. In the first case subtract these masses from the base count to recover the global answer. In the latter case every valid completion of the k-cover must hit at least one of these profiles, so branch over coordinates of their union. At depth k an unsuccessful branch ends. Each node costs poly(n,m,1+s) fixed-width calls on n+O(k) variables. Branching factor is at most min(m,(s+1)w) when all encountered profiles have at most w active bits. Thus there is a native regime for k=O(n) and k log(2+m)+log(1+s)=O(n loglog n/sqrt(log n)), independently of monomial degree and full rank. This is a structural application, not a general runtime conclusion.

**Individual-moment needle.** For k≥2 put unit baseline mass on each nonempty support of size less than k, and at the origin also when k is odd. For unknown k-set S add (−1)^{k−|v|} to every v⊆S. The masses stay nonnegative. The upward intersection indexed by A changes iff A=S (alternating subset sum). Origin mass changes by (−1)^k. The baseline predictor is the OR inclusion–exclusion polynomial truncated before degree k; one additional (−1)^{k+1}V_S fits the perturbed histogram. Thus testing all binom(m,k) individual intersection probes can be necessary. At k=2 the supplied baseline has only m terms. This is consistent with polynomial dependence on the expanded baseline size and does not lower-bound direct SAT algorithms inspecting H.

**Original-literal shortcut.** The canonical shared clauses can be found using at most 2n conditional residual tests on original literals. Record which literals are common to all residual H assignments, then scan all wide clauses: a clause's falsifying cube is common precisely when all its defining pins are common.

**Any-degree signed expression reconstruction.** At a node with t<k selected monomials, extract an inconsistent list of at most t+1 actual positive-profile equations as in pattern-repair.md; if the fit is exact, return it. On a branch consisting of terms from some true k-term predictor, at least one missing term must be active on one of the conflict rows. Otherwise that predictor would contradict the conflict. Enumerate all nonempty submasks of the conflict rows as possible next terms; each chosen row of width w has at most 2^w−1. Branch for k levels, testing and returning a certified fit. There are at most [(k+1)2^w]^k nodes up to polynomial factors for maximal encountered w, independent of the full dictionary rank. Filtering columns already in the conflict span is sound because at least one missing true column must leave it. Equivalent monomials may be identified using positive intersection containment tests under Q. For counting alone the coordinate-cover consequence is stronger than recovering the entire signed expression.

**Degree-bounded gradient oracle by doubling the core.** Suppose f is the least-squares optimum in its current span. For each ordered tuple I of d clause indices, with repetitions allowed, let g_I=E[Q(1−f)V_union(I)]. The sum of all g_I² is nonnegative and is positive exactly when some nonempty monomial of degree at most d has nonzero gradient, for d≥1. Use independent core assignments x,y. In the doubled sum the common activated choices contribute |V(x) intersect V(y)|^d. Implement this by d independent ℓ-digit clause selectors requiring the chosen clause's falsifying pins in both assignments. Expand the remaining sparse signed factors Q(x)(1−f(x))Q(y)(1−f(y)) into ordinary jointly pinned counts. Fixing prefix selector entries preserves the sum-of-squares interpretation over remaining tuples and thus gives an exact self-reduction to a concrete improving monomial. For constant ℓ, this takes polynomially many bounded-width queries on 2n+O(dℓm^{1/ℓ}) variables per improvement step. For m polynomial and d≤n^{1−ε} a sufficiently large fixed ℓ keeps the variable count linear. No bound on improvement rounds in terms of optimum sparsity follows from this oracle alone.

The independent check_discovery.py passed 80 conjunction examples, 320 signed double-copy identities, and 119 isolated-moment perturbations. check_sparse.py matched exhaustive support selection on 144 budgets and check_column.py passed 61 weighted cases.
