# Pointwise export and exact composition on an arbitrary crossing core

Let H be a fixed-width CNF on n variables. Divide the additional wide clauses into groups; the groups can overlap in all their variables. On an H-model, group i has violation vector v and Q(v) equal to its number of false clauses. Let f be a rational sum of p nonempty positive monomials learned for a group by the exact sparse-pattern procedure in pattern-repair.md. It vanishes at zero and equals the group failure indicator U on all realized profiles except a known list E of e distinct nonzero patterns. This includes the harmless extra deletions of the greedy learner.

**Pointwise export.** Choose a positive-monomial polynomial h with h(v)=1/Q(v) for v in E. There is a direct e-term construction: use the full support of each v as a candidate monomial and solve the unit-triangular inclusion matrix of E in increasing support size. For e=0 use zero. Then U=f+Q(1−f)h at EVERY H-model. The identity holds at zero, at every undeleted nonzero point where f=1, and on E where Qh=1. In particular it supplies actual factor values pointwise on H rather than an estimated or averaged scalar. Each positive monomial tests a single compatible union of falsifying pins, hence is an original-variable cube, or is identically zero. Group satisfaction consequently admits a signed rational cube list with at most 1+p+m_i(p+1)e terms, where m_i is its number of clauses. Merge equal cubes and omit zero coefficients if desired. If a group was learned on separate leaves of an adaptive variable tree, include each leaf's path pins in its local list and concatenate the lists. This stays correct on H since paths partition its models. Bounded-arity predicate paths can instead be kept as short clauses gated by the same selector at constant added width.

**Arbitrarily overlapping factors.** Multiply these pointwise lists and sum against H by the shared radix compiler of result.md. Let L_i be the nonzero list lengths. Clear one positive denominator per factor to obtain signed integer weights w_it with bit length at most B_i. With d fixed choose d radix digits for the term and d for the ordinal of a set bit among the bits of |w_it|. If h_i is the largest number of set bits in a term, the two digit alphabets need O_d(L_i^{1/d}+h_i^{1/d}) single-value indicator bits. Enforce exactly one per digit with unique-extension trees and binary exclusions. For each valid term, digit choice gates the term's cube requirements by at most d additional literals. A valid pair (term, ordinal) forces its binary exponent j (the position of that set bit) and sign, using O(log(B_i+1)) bits and width at most 2d+1; invalid or padded pairs are forbidden. For power-of-two weights there is no extra choice of set bit. The sum J of selected exponents is implemented using column carry-save adders: each sum/carry on three bits decreases the total bit count by one. Once at most two bits remain per column, a ripple adder for the two words costs O(log(1+sum_i B_i)) more gates. This is O(sum_i(log(B_i+1)+1)) gates in total. XOR the signs. For each J≤sum B_i take positive-minus-negative counts, multiply by 2^J, sum and divide by the product of factor denominators. For polynomial encoded precisions this uses polynomially many ordinary fixed-width counts. Width is constant for fixed d and auxiliary count is O_d(sum_i(L_i^{1/d}+h_i^{1/d}+log(B_i+1)+1)). Thus a linear total budget gives the native fixed-width exponent with no disjointness, sparse-overlap, or common-tree condition. Off-core behavior of the exported factors can be arbitrary; H is imposed throughout.

The construction combines signed cube lists, finite-domain selection and weighted-to-unweighted counting methods, not a general polynomial SAT algorithm. Each stage requires its explicit output size and learning-oracle budget to meet the stated bounds. In particular clearing denominators can increase precision and should be accounted for separately. The executable check_export.py compared 180 learned factors over 60 overlapping systems against exhaustive truth tables and joint counts. All comparisons on H passed; 436 off-core discrepancies were observed.
