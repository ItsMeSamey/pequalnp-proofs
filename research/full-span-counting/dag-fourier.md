# Support-sensitive contraction on expanding scopes

Take a simple cubic graph with a supplied Hamiltonian cycle. Orient each edge by increasing cycle order, without wrapping. The first and last vertex have constant local table 1. Every other vertex has two possible ordered pairs consisting of one incoming edge and one outgoing edge. Give each edge a shared bit. A private selector at each internal vertex chooses one such pair and requires its two bits to be equal. Two selector-guarded implications per option suffice. This is a width-three CNF with at most eight occurrences per shared bit and four per selector.

Eliminating a selector gives the sum of two pair-equality indicators. Its normalized Walsh coefficients are 1 at the zero vector and 1/2 on each through-pair. In the sum of local Walsh expansions, shared edge frequencies must agree at the endpoints. Any nonzero compatible tuple therefore specifies a nonempty balanced subgraph of a DAG, impossible at its least selected vertex. The count is exactly 2^|E|, including on Hamiltonian expanders.

If one replaces each Fourier support by its linear span, each internal vertex instead admits the full even plane on its three ports. Matching the ports now gives the binary cycle space of the graph with first and last vertices removed. The remaining Hamiltonian path is connected, and the first and last vertices share an edge, so the dimension is |V|/2−2. Thus support pruning removes exponentially many terms permitted by the rank relaxation. Exhaustive audits at 4,6,8,10 vertices returned 64,512,4096,32768 assignments and hull dimensions 0,1,2,3.

Recognition without a Hamiltonian certificate is sufficient via the ordinary local marginal relaxation. For arbitrary bounded-arity factors on shared edge bits with zero in every local Fourier support, put a distribution on each support and impose equal one-marginals across each shared edge. If the maximum sum of edge one-marginals vanishes, no globally compatible nonzero tuple exists. Multiply the constant coefficients and 2^|E|. On the DAG examples, local balance yields a nonnegative fractional circulation, which vanishes by the same least-vertex argument. Positive optimum is inconclusive. More generally every local pattern whose maximum marginal under these constraints vanishes can be discarded before contraction.
