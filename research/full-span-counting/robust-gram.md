# Unknown sparse outliers for exact violation-polynomial learning

Let H be a fixed-width core. W is the set of variables used by any wide clause, V_i(z) its falsification indicator on a setting z of W, and Q the sum of the V_i. The multiplicity h(z) is the number of H models extending z. Let φ consist of the p nonconstant violation monomials of degree at most d, where p is the sum of binom(m,j) for 1≤j≤d. A vector λ predicts the union indicator by f=λ·φ and gives zero on the all-zero profile. For each z with Q(z)>0 and h(z)>0 the desired equation is λ·φ(V(z))=1. Suppose some vector fails on at most s distinct settings of W, with arbitrarily many short-core completions per setting. Both λ and the exceptional settings are unknown.

For a known excluded set E and partial assignment a on W, compute Q-weighted sums of feature products over H under a, subtracting h(z)Q(z) times the same products for every excluded z extending a. Each original sum is a linear combination of core counts imposing a and at most 2d+1 clause-falsifying pins. Cache each h(z) with a core count. This gives the exact nonnegative residual sum of Q(1−λ·φ)² outside E on every query prefix. Ordinary rational normal equations and a zero-loss test decide whether all remaining equations are consistent. These are the weighted Gram formulas from the compression team, now conditional and with finite deletions.

If feasible, recover some λ. The exact model count is #H minus the sum of f over H models plus sum_{z in E} h(z)(f(V(z))−1). Each excluded setting came from a positive-fault witness with h(z)>0. Unconditioned feature expectations use pinned core counts. No sign or integrality restrictions on λ are required.

If infeasible, extract an actual contradictory subsystem of at most p+1 equations. Starting with an empty feasible list, choose any rational solution λ to the list. The whole system outside E is inconsistent; its positive residual detects a violating setting outside E. Recover one by prefix self-reduction on W, selecting a child whose residual remains positive, updating the excluded corrections under each prefix. Add that row. If feasibility is preserved, coefficient rank strictly increases: an equation whose coefficients are in the prior row span either already holds on every solution or renders the enlarged list inconsistent. At most p+1 witnessed rows reach inconsistency. For exact counting remove ALL of the at most p+1 witnessed settings, record their multiplicities, and repeat. The contradictions so obtained are disjoint. A particular best explanation with s errors must hit every contradictory subsystem, since no coefficient vector can satisfy all the equations in one subsystem. Thus there are at most s rounds and the recorded list has size at most (p+1)s. The final coefficient vector is exact outside that list, which is sufficient for the displayed correction. No advance knowledge of s, minimum-outlier solver, or search tree is required. This is the elementary greedy approximation for hitting sets of bounded-size conflicts.

For fixed d≥1 and m≥1 the count uses poly(n,m,p,s) times the original fixed-width counting time. Each round takes at most O((p+1)(|W|+1)) aggregate prefix queries, with polynomially many pinned core calls and exact rational arithmetic per aggregate. All excluded-row subtraction is polynomial in the current list length. If log(1+s) is O(n loglog n/sqrt(log n)) on polynomial-size inputs, the native exponent is retained; if log(1+s)=o(n), the result remains subexponential. The guarantee is in the number of exceptional W-settings, not the number of exceptional violation patterns; counting completions for an arbitrary fully specified pattern would itself enforce many unviolated wide clauses. If minimum deletion itself is desired, branch instead on the members of a conflict, with at most (p+1)^s nodes up to polynomial factors. This exponential step is unnecessary for the counting application.

Conflict branching on infeasible subsystems is a standard approach in optimization with violated constraints. This application supplies otherwise inaccessible rows via conditional squared-residual model counts and returns an exact union size. [Matoušek](https://doi.org/10.1145/177424.178039) The maximum-spanning-tree algorithm in tree.md is a more direct specialization for a tree-shaped certificate, using at most triple intersections instead of intersections of order 2d+1.

## Sharp tradeoff on independent violations

Take t wide clauses (Y or x_i), with the common guard Y forced false by the short core, and let all x_i be free. Their violation profiles realize the full t-dimensional Boolean cube bijectively in the active W-settings. For any predictor f of degree at most d≤t with f(0)=0, g=1−f is nonzero of degree at most d. Pick a monomial of maximum degree k with nonzero coefficient. Under any fixing of the other variables, its coefficient remains, since there were no terms properly containing it. The resulting multilinear polynomial is nonzero somewhere on its Boolean cube. Thus g is nonzero on at least 2^{t−k}≥2^{t−d} points. One is the origin, leaving at least 2^{t−d}−1 exceptional settings where f fails its union equation. The bound is attained by f=1−prod_{i=1}^d(1−v_i). The resulting SAT instance itself is elementary; this is a sharp restriction on this representation, not on other algorithms.

The verifier check_robust.py tested 32 overlapping cube families with projection multiplicities zero through four. It independently computed the best deletion numbers, checked the greedy iteration and deletion bounds, and obtained the correct union count in every case.
