# Learning exceptional violation patterns through positive intersections

For bounded-width core H let h_v count its models realizing full wide-clause violation pattern v. Arbitrarily many wide-variable settings may give the same v. Use p specified positive monomial features on the clause bits, all indexed by nonempty sets. Any degree is allowed: an expectation of a positive monomial is one count of H under the implicated clauses' falsifying pins. Define s as the fewest distinct realized nonzero patterns on which some rational linear combination f of these features fails to equal 1.

**Theorem.** Exact counting takes poly(n,m,p,1+s) original fixed-width oracle calls, with polynomial arithmetic overhead. Neither s nor the predictor nor exceptional patterns or their masses are supplied. On polynomial inputs and feature lists, this retains the fixed-width native exponent if log(1+s)=O(n loglog n/sqrt(log n)), and is subexponential if log(1+s)=o(n).

**Recovering a witness and its mass.** Put Q(v)=sum_i v_i. Suppose E contains previously deleted patterns with known masses. For clause subset S compute B(S), the sum of h_v Q(v)(1−f(v))² over undeleted v containing S. Expand the weight times the positive monomial for S, evaluating each resulting monomial by one pinned core count, then subtract stored E contributions. There are polynomially many terms since f has at most p explicitly specified monomials.

When B(empty)>0, start S empty and scan all indices, adding i exactly if B(S union {i})>0. A failed extension cannot become possible as S grows under a nonnegative measure. At termination S is the only positive-residual pattern containing S. It is nonempty since Q annihilates the origin. Therefore h_S=B(S)/(Q(S)(1−f(S))²), with positive denominator. Strict supersets can carry positive original mass but have zero residual or are deleted. This recovers the full original multiplicity without enforcing that other wide clauses must be satisfied. Additive-query reconstruction for weighted hypergraphs is established, including work under other parameters. [Bshouty and Mazzawi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.0405)

**Greedy disjoint conflicts.** Begin each round with an empty feasible list of actual equations φ(v)·λ=1. Solve the list over the rationals to obtain f. If B(empty)=0 the fit is exact off E. Otherwise obtain a violated row and its exact h_v by the witness procedure, and append it. If the augmented list remains feasible, coefficient rank strictly increases: a row in the previous span is already implied or makes the system inconsistent. At most p+1 rows produce a conflict or terminate with a globally valid fit. Rows never repeat because the current solution satisfies all previous rows. Delete every row in a conflicting list and store its known mass. Lists from different deletion rounds are disjoint; every possible predictor fails on at least one row of each list. Consequently there are at most s deletion rounds and at most (p+1)s stored patterns.

At termination the answer is #H minus the sum of f over H models plus the sum of h_v(f(v)−1) over v in E. Unconditioned feature sums use the same positive intersection queries. The finite linear systems have zero-one coefficients and unit right sides, hence rational solutions of polynomial bit length. The oracle only sees H and original literal pins.

For p=0 all positive profiles can be peeled in at most m+1 aggregate questions per profile, avoiding enumeration of the many compatible clause subsets inside one profile. For general p the method also covers exponentially many profiles when all but a sparse unknown set obey the feature identity. The independent-fault cube forces degree-d predictors to miss at least 2^{m−d}−1 profiles, sharply, as proved in robust-gram.md, so this is no universal guarantee.

The independent check_peeling.py tested 60 systems and 158 conflict rounds with multiplicities above 2^227. Every recovered mass, corrected count and deletion bound matched exhaustive calculations. A separate border-ideal implementation verifies an alternate route.
