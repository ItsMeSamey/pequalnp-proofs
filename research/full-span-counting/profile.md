# Learning the multiplicity profile from exact partial counts

Let H be the bounded-width core and Q(x) the number of additional clauses falsified by x. For k≥0 set μ_k=sum_{x models H} Q(x)^k, using 0^0=1. The desired global count is the mass at zero in the nonnegative integer-valued measure w_j=number of H-models with Q(x)=j for j=0,...,m. The algorithm need not be told how many of these masses are nonzero.

To compute μ_k one may sum, over ordered k-tuples of clauses including repeats, the exact H-count restricted by all pins in those clauses' falsifying cubes. Inconsistent pins contribute zero. Equivalently introduce k independent radix-coded term indices as in result.md, each selecting one of the m falsifying cubes and enforcing its pins. There are Q(x)^k extensions to valid selectors for each x. This single ordinary count per moment has O(n+k d ceil(m^{1/d})) variables and width max(width(H),3,d+1). The former version uses at most m^k original-scale counts and keeps the original variable count. Both methods admit a zero-result shortcut if H is inconsistent.

If w is nonempty and has s distinct support positions, the Hankel matrix G_t=(μ_{i+j}) for 0≤i,j≤t equals V diag(w) V^T over the rationals, where V has columns (1,j,...,j^t)^T for the support values j. Positivity and the Vandermonde determinant give rank min(t+1,s). At the first singular matrix, t=s. A nonzero null vector gives coefficients of a polynomial P of degree s vanishing precisely at the s support points: the zero quadratic form is a sum of positive multiples of P(j)^2. Normalize P to be monic. If P(0) is nonzero then w_0=0. Otherwise its root at zero is simple and L(z)=P(z)/(zP'(0)) equals one at zero and zero at every other support point. Consequently w_0=sum_h L_h μ_h. No root-finding is required. Exact rational arithmetic costs polynomial time in n, m and the moments' bit lengths (μ_k≤2^n m^k). The same method works with any positive Boolean base count closed under the required restrictions. It does not apply directly to nonpositive measures.

For the native fixed-width estimate, the explicit k-tuple implementation costs poly(L) times m^{2s} times the original oracle bound. Thus if s log(2+m)=O(n loglog n/sqrt(log n)), the native exponent is retained. If the product is o(n), the resulting exact algorithm is subexponential. The radix implementation independently gives the native bound whenever s m^{1/d}=O(n) for a fixed d. One can also solve two rational linear programs after each moment, minimizing and maximizing v_0 subject to v_j≥0 and sum_j j^h v_j=μ_h. If the interval contains exactly one integer that value is already certified. For decision a lower bound above zero or an upper bound below one suffices. This is the discrete moment approach to bounds for a union of events, established in Prékopa’s work; the Hankel annihilator is standard finite-measure moment reconstruction. The transfer here uses bounded-width SAT counts to supply exactly the requested moments. [Prékopa](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.36.1.145)

## Explicit separation from pin-union enumeration

For q divisible by four, use variables x_i and z_j, q of each, and a nonempty common guard disjunction Y on Θ(q) other variables. Place all q² clauses (Y or x_i or z_j). The core requires numbers of zeros on each side to belong to {0,1,q/2}. Each side count has a uniquely extending balanced adder over its bits and a constant-number set-membership test on its binary output, implementable with O(q) additional variables and constant-width gates. For Y false, Q is the product of the two zero counts. For Y true, Q=0. Hence the only possible levels are 0,1,q/2,q²/4. Each wide clause is essential by choosing exactly one zero at its row and column and setting Y false. All wide clauses are compatible. Pinning the matching edges (i,i) for any q/4-subset S produces semantically distinct restrictions even under the core: for a different equal-sized S', extend both zero sets to size q/2 including S while omitting one member of S' outside S. There are exponentially many such restrictions, whereas the four support levels permit exact recovery after at most eight moments. Further arbitrary crossing short constraints can only remove support levels; they need not preserve essentiality. The complete bipartite pattern also has a separate syntactic factorization, so this compares two explicit techniques only.

## A moment-only obstruction

For t≥1 use t+1 private bits and clauses (Y or x_i) with a common disjunctive guard whose literals are all forced false by the short core. Impose even or odd parity of the number of zero private bits via a unique-extension binary XOR chain. The two resulting histograms have masses binom(t+1,j) at even or odd j, respectively, and vanish elsewhere. Their moments agree through degree t, since the alternating (t+1)st finite difference of every polynomial of degree at most t is zero. Nonetheless their global counts are 1 and 0. In the odd case deleting any single wide clause restores a witness. Thus low-order scalar moments alone cannot decide every instance, even as exact integers. The parity constraints are visible to an algorithm that inspects the formula.
