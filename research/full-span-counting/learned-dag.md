# An exact learned hierarchy with one wire per internal group

Fix a bounded-width core H on n original variables. The input supplies a directed acyclic grouping of the raw wide-clause violations, where an internal Boolean value is the OR of its child violations. The grouped root covers the desired clauses. Raw violations can be shared and internal groups may overlap in arbitrary sets of original variables. Gate j is learned on the true H-model traces using the profile method, optionally using hereditary local decision leaves. We account for the cumulative circuit that is actually learned. Nothing here asserts that an arbitrary CNF has a small suitable hierarchy or that one can find it universally.

Assume a rational cube polynomial P_j in earlier wire bits and original pins agrees with the true group bit on consistent H traces. Introduce just one Boolean output wire y_j and form the local signed factor
R_j = 1 − y_j − P_j + 2 y_j P_j.
When P_j is a bit this is the indicator of y_j=P_j. For any original H-model there is exactly one consistent full wire assignment, and it has every R_j=1. For any inconsistent wire assignment take its first wrong gate in topological order: all its predecessor wires agree, so P_j is a true Boolean value there and R_j=0. Consequently the product of all factors vanishes there, regardless of possibly non-Boolean signed values of later polynomials. Off-H values of the factors also do not matter. Cube products in one local expansion only combine original pins and signs of earlier/output wires. Raw clause-fault conjunctions impose the original literals directly; they need no individual Boolean wires.

Apply the radix and signed-digit compiler of composition.md to all local factors simultaneously and keep H. Equivalently, one can compile any already-learned prefix. Conditioning on any earlier internal outputs costs only unit clauses. Conditioning on any subset of raw clause failures adds only their original pins, even for unbounded degrees. Dividing out the product of cleared local denominators recovers an exact nonnegative count of true original assignments with the specified joint condition, despite signed intermediate sums. This is exactly the positive-intersection oracle required when learning the next internal gate. Use the previously learned prefix for all of its queries. Repeated gates or shared children need only one wire; distinct paths have no independent duplication of their values.

Specifically, use pattern-repair.md to fit a sparse f to the node's union indicator outside a finite set E of exceptional nonzero child profiles. Export an exact pointwise polynomial P=f+Q(1−f)h, taking h(v)=1/Q(v) on E as in composition.md. With e actual stored patterns, p learned terms, and m_j children, the expansion has at most p+m_j(p+1)e terms before merging. Its degree can be large without costing local output clause width, since the term gate is applied separately to each constituent pin. Each ancestor prefix supplies the exact moments for this adaptive construction; no explicit enumerations of combinations of local paths are required. Finite witness-based fits need polynomially many core counts in the feature ranks/widths and number of exceptional patterns per previously proved learning theorem. Conditional exact leaf fits can likewise be exported by including their path restrictions.

For a fixed radix length d, let L_j be the length of R_j after expansion, B_j the maximum bit length of its signed numerator after one common positive denominator is cleared, and h_j the maximum number of set bits in such a numerator. The compiled prefix has O_d(n+sum_j[1+L_j^{1/d}+h_j^{1/d}+log(B_j+1)]) variables and constant width, assuming H and local predicate-path tests have constant width. Its integer sign/exponent stratification adds polynomially many calls for polynomially encoded lists and precisions. If the additive budget is O(n) and the learning overhead per node is polynomial, the complete procedure retains the fixed-width native exponent with no depth dependence. More generally multiply the oracle time on the largest prefix by the actual number of learning and integer-stratification calls. Imposing y_root=0 gives the desired exact model count. Restriction to existing traces and use of the predicate R_j are essential, since a learned P_j need not have Boolean outputs on arbitrary independent settings of its child wires.

## Flattening can be exponentially more expensive

Take t independent bits with one raw fault per bit. The global OR has a positive-monomial expansion with exactly 2^t−1 terms by the uniqueness of Boolean multilinear polynomials. A binary hierarchy evaluates it with fewer than t internal Boolean wires and constant-sized local polynomials. More locally, g overlapping factors (1−u a_i b_i) have individual truth-matrix rank at most 2 across the row/column cut with u fixed; the u=1 slice of their product is a tensor power of the 2-by-2 disjointness matrix, with rank 2^g over every field. Replacing fewer than 2^g−r table entries cannot create a rank-r approximation, since each changed entry contributes at most one to the rank of the difference. These examples obstruct flattening only. A polynomial-sized dense input can combine a heavy hitting group of distinct full-width maxterms with a linear number of such small groups; the large group has a sparse signed OR formula (sum of disjoint failures). Its square-root-radix cost plus the small group costs remain O(n) for quadratic input density. Other counting strategies may also exploit this structure.

The fixed-width engine still supplies only a subexponential upper bound on arbitrary H, including instances in which no wide gates are needed. The representation does not establish polynomial-time SAT or a lower bound for SAT itself.
