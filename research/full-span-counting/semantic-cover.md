# Selecting a semantic group cover with sparse paid exceptions

Let h_v count the bounded-width core H's models realizing raw violation profile v. A retained polynomial library of verified candidate clause groups has positive additive materialization costs c_G. Group G fails on those profiles meeting its clause set. Give every positive pattern a singleton exception option of price λ>0.

Given selected groups A, the previously verified prefix compiler provides pinned counts of H with those groups passing. Every surviving pattern retains its original h_v because group outcomes depend only on v. Find a positive unpaid pattern through maximal positive intersection peeling with weight Q(v)h_v, Q=sum_i v_i, subtracting contributions of still-surviving paid exceptions. This returns a previously unknown exact h_v using polynomially many pinned calls in the selected base.

Maintain dual prices y_v, initially zero, totaling at most c_G on every group's patterns and satisfying y_v≤λ. Raise a newly witnessed price until an incident group or its singleton becomes tight, then select one tight option. A singleton choice records its mass. Later price increases are confined to rows outside all selected options; chosen options stay tight. On termination subtract from the count with A enforced only the masses of still-surviving paid patterns. Paid exceptions covered by later selections must not be subtracted twice.

Let f=max(1,Δ), where Δ is the maximum number of candidate groups simultaneously failing on a realized pattern. For any choice costing C* and missing s* profiles, weak duality yields sum y≤C*+λs*. Selected group cost is its incident dual prices with multiplicity at most f. Finally uncovered patterns have y_v=λ and contribute nothing to group costs. Thus C_selected+fλ|E_final|≤f sum y≤f(C*+λs*). All ever-paid exceptions, including later covered ones, satisfy λ|E_ever|≤sum y. Each iteration adds a group or singleton. With λ=C0/S0, C*≤C0 and s*≤S0, selected cost is at most 2f C0 and there are at most 2S0 ever-paid patterns. For fixed f and polynomial encoding overhead this preserves the native exponent if C0=O(n) and log(1+S0)=O(n loglog n/sqrt(log n)). The output is a compact exact core and residual profile corrections. The covering argument is classical; the SAT-specific part implements the witness and exact multiplicity oracle over exponentially many possible patterns. [Bar-Yehuda and Even](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0196677481900201) · [Könemann, Parekh and Segev](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~jochen/docs/partial-esa.pdf)

Example: raw faults A=a, B=b, C=ab, Z_j=a x_j for t payload bits, D_i=a[address=i] for t address values, E=(1−a)(1−b)e. Use groups G1={A,C,all Z}, G2={B,C}, plus a singleton for every D and optionally E. Each pattern triggers at most three candidates. A syntactic all-clause cover with all singletons costs t+3, while G1,G2 plus the single uncovered pattern {E} cost three at unit prices. There are exponentially many distinct patterns. Free bits can give every pattern exponential multiplicity; a common guard conjunction makes the corresponding clauses wide.

For counting alone, if polynomial-sized group failure lists are known and K (number of failing candidates) takes k distinct positive values, recover them from Hankel moments through degree 2k+1. Each moment multiplies at most 2k+1 group lists, for polynomially many pinned counts at fixed k. Coverage has indicator 1−prod_a(1−K/a) over these positive values. Peel uncovered profiles with Q_raw times the complement of this indicator; subtract covered and residual positive masses from #H. Fixed Δ also gives truncated inclusion–exclusion. This alternative does not return an explicitly selected small core.

check_group_cover.py tested 180 systems versus exhaustive optima and counts including 88 paid profiles later covered; check_prize.py tested the stronger cost inequality on another 500 systems.
