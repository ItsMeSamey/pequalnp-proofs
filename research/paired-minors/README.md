# Principal minors selected in pairs

Let the coordinates of two binary square matrices be partitioned into specified pairs. Testing equality of all principal minors indexed by unions of pairs is coNP-complete. This holds for matrices diag(P,Q) and diag(P,Q′), where each pair has one index in each block, P is the same permutation matrix, Q is a disjoint union of star adjacency matrices, and Q′ is obtained from Q by zeroing one row. The complement has a polynomial certificate consisting of a union of pairs with unequal determinants.

For hardness, reduce Exact Cover on a nonempty universe, discarding empty sets. Give each membership of an element in a set its own index and add a marker for each universe element. The permutation P cycles the memberships of each set and cycles all markers together. A principal minor P[S] equals one exactly when S is a union of whole cycles. In Q one star for each element connects its marker to the memberships of that element. Each selected principal star block is nonsingular exactly when empty or when it selects its center and exactly one leaf. Without the center its matrix is zero unless empty. With the center and no leaves it is a zero singleton, and two or more leaves have identical rows.

Zero a single marker row to obtain Q′. Paired principal minors are det(P[S])det(Q[S]) or det(P[S])det(Q′[S]). They differ precisely when all markers are selected and the selected set cycles cover each universe element exactly once. An absent chosen marker leaves Q[S] unchanged. A present marker gives Q′[S] a zero row, and the P block forces all markers whenever its determinant is nonzero. The construction has polynomial size. Exact Cover is NP-complete by [Karp 1972](https://www2.seas.gwu.edu/~simhaweb/champalg/tsp/papers/KarpOriginal.pdf). The checker compares the reduction with brute force on 109 systems.

## Application to direction-sensitive guards

For the singleton-guard marker criterion, take a=det A[S] and b the coefficient of t in det((A+t eᵢhᵀ)[S]), both binary. The determinant is affine in t because only one row changes. Choose ω in GF(4) outside GF(2). Then a+ωb is nonzero exactly when a or b is one. Its field norm to GF(2) equals this Boolean OR. Thus the norm differs from a exactly for the desired directed change from zero to one.

Replace each GF(4) scalar by its 2-by-2 multiplication matrix on a fixed GF(2) basis. The binary determinant of a whole-coordinate-pair principal submatrix is the norm of the corresponding GF(4) determinant. Indeed a singular matrix over GF(4) remains singular over GF(2) and an invertible one remains invertible. Comparing the two resulting binary doubled matrices on union-of-pair subsets therefore encodes the marker question. A generic algorithm for comparing paired minors would decide the coNP-complete problem above. The two specific matrices arising from field restriction have additional structure, so hardness for general paired matrices does not establish hardness of the marker problem. Ordinary principal-minor equivalence compares partial selections as well. Its polynomial-time algorithm therefore does not automatically answer the paired question. [Chatterjee, Ghosh, Gurjar and Raj](https://www.cse.iitb.ac.in/~rgurjar/papers/PMESTOC25.pdf)
