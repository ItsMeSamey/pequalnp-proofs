# Prime-power reconstruction for the interpolation layout

For p>Q work over Z/p^aZ. The denominator (R−1)!Q! is invertible; Hermite data at 1,...,Q recover the polynomial because the powered node ideals are comaximal. Select an F_p basis of the off-group replacement rows for each group. Vanishing minors show no rank increase after reduction. Enumerate tuples of these F_p measurements with one representative per nonempty local class, fixed before the Boolean-difference probes.

At node t replace each input by B(x_g)+Vδ(Y)+Σ_{i≥1}z^i L_i(Y). Here B is the zeroth-order row applied to the local variables and off-group representatives, and δ(Y) is its off-group correction, an affine linear function of Y. For actual tuple members x, δ(x) is divisible by p. Truncate at z^J and V^a. After setting Y=x, summing retained V coefficients gives the desired jet modulo p^a, since each omitted term contains ≥a p-divisible factors. Each retained coefficient z^j V^s has Boolean bookkeeping degree ≤j+s. Thus the paper's signed finite differences need only C_{q+a−1} pairs, for q=J−1. Probe values Y=1_U require no divisibility property. With this Y fixed all factors depend only on local x_g, and x_i=1 for i in E can be tested locally. Multiply before summing V coefficients. The remaining costs are p^{2τn} measurement tuples, 2^{2n/r} group scans, and polynomial overhead.

For direct clause products use all primes Q<p≤2Q, product B, and choose the least a with B^a>2^n. Since log B=Ω(Q) by the prime number theorem for large Q, a=O(1+n/Q). CRT therefore deterministically recovers the integer count. At the manuscript parameters the extra order is below its charged q=Θ(nR/Q) at linear input degree. This saves measurement bits while the group and finite-difference costs keep the same leading envelope.

## Sparse hash queries

Keep three independent markers z for Taylor order, V for p-adic off-group errors and W for the paper's 2-adic indicator lift. Truncate W at ℓ as in the source, retaining its degree bound d=k(m_test+2(ℓ−1)). Reconstruct the signed integer obtained by summing the retained W coefficients at the origin before reducing modulo 2^ℓ.

With h=y(y−1), e₀=1−y and e_j=(1−2y)(−1)^j binom(2j,j)/2 for j≥1. Indeed expand (1−(2y−1)(1+4h)^(−1/2))/2 to order ℓ−1 in h. At y=0,1 the corresponding formal square roots are 1−2y and 2y−1. The polynomial is 1 mod y^ℓ and 0 mod (y−1)^ℓ; its degree ≤2ℓ−1 gives uniqueness. If |y_i|≤S, every retained index product has magnitude at most (1+2S)^m [4(1+S)^2]^ℓ. At most 2^{m+ℓ} indices and 2^n assignments contribute. The signed scalar has O(n+(m+ℓ)log(S+2)) bits. Even the relaxed S=poly(n), m,ℓ=O(n) bounds cost O(n log n) bits. Use primes in (Q^4,2Q^4] with uniform a chosen to exceed twice this magnitude bound in combined modulus. Then a=O(1+n log n/Q^4) while log p=O(log Q). At the paper's Q this increment is smaller than its charged q. CRT recovers the signed lift, its 2-adic residue gives the exact count, and hence all greedy row comparisons are valid. This deterministic result applies to the rational interpolation route, not the separately sharper Appendix D result.
