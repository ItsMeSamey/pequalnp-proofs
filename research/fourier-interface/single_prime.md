# A single-prime deterministic exact interpolation layout

Let Q be the number of evaluation nodes, R the permitted replacement-degree bound, a=n+1 the final binary precision, and 1≤s<R an intermediate measurement precision. Coordinates within a swap orbit of h active positions are labeled by x,y in {0,1}^h. Define the integer matrix
C(x,y)=Σ_{j=0}^{h−R}(−1)^j e_j(x⊕y), and set C=0 if h<R.
Here e_j is the elementary symmetric polynomial and the integer bits x⊕y are the coordinate differences. Put M=I−C over the integers. For distance d≤h−R, binomial cancellation yields C(x,y)=1[d=0]. Thus each nonzero M entry has d>h−R and fewer than R mismatches between the source and target color schedules. This support statement is exact at all precisions.

In Z/2^s use x_i⊕y_i=x_i+y_i−2x_i y_i. Every quadratic choice costs a factor of 2. The surviving polynomial C has total degree ≤h−R+s−1 in x,y. Put b=floor((h−R+s−1)/2). Split its monomials according to whether the x-degree is ≤b; the remainder have y-degree ≤b. Collect by the low-degree monomial on the relevant side. This yields a factorization C≡AB mod 2^s with inner dimension ≤2Σ_{j≤b}binom(h,j). All entries of B are base-ring scalars. Polynomial size and construction time follow directly, since each active position has four choices (absent, x, y, xy), h≤Q, and full catalogue dimension D=r^{2Q}≥4^Q. This is a factorization over the ring; it requires no field-rank convention. Summing over the orbits gives fractional inner dimension ≤2 exp(−(R−s+1)^2/(2Q)).

Represent the coefficient ring K=(Z/2^a)[θ]/f, where f is any monic lift of an irreducible polynomial of degree b₀=ceil(log₂(Q+1)) over F₂. Brute enumeration of O(Q) candidates with a polynomial-time irreducibility test suffices. Choose Q distinct nonzero residue-field classes and their polynomial representatives t_i. Each t_i and t_i−t_j for i≠j is a unit, since each has nonzero reduction modulo 2. An inverse may be computed by lifting the residue inverse with Newton iteration. Interpolation from jets works by CRT in K[T], using powers of the pairwise comaximal ideals (T−t_i); this does not invert factorials, even when the jet order exceeds the characteristic of the residue field. Each element needs O(a log Q) bits.

Build replacement rows over K directly: add the constant integer correction C to M entries individually weighted by ∏_{i:mismatch}(1−T/t_i). At zero this restores the identity. At each node, all remaining M entries are local, and the only off-group contribution comes from the integer C. Measure the rows of B modulo 2^s, after assigning occurrences to copies and restricting rows to groups as in the paper. A group measurement needs s bits even though the interpolation ring is larger. Equal labels imply that all off-group effects agree modulo 2^s.

For each measurement tuple choose one admissible representative per group before the finite-difference probes. As in padic.md introduce a marker V for the off-group zeroth-order discrepancy, which now lies in 2^s K at each actual assignment. Keep fewer than L=ceil(a/s) discrepancy factors, since (2^s)^L=0 in K. Keep z^j for j<J in each Taylor jet. At probe assignments the discrepancies can be arbitrary. The coefficient of z^j V^v has Boolean bookkeeping degree at most j+v; following the paper's Boolean differences therefore costs C_{q+L−1}, q=J−1, followed by at most 2^{s c} tuple labels for c restricted measurement rows and 2^{2n/r} local group vectors. The order of multiplication and setting V=1 is as in padic.md. Polynomially many coefficients in z,V, and the separate hash-lift marker W suffice. Space and all auxiliary operations are polynomial in input length.

For direct clause indicators the result at the origin lives in the base Z/2^a, and its least residue equals the exact integer count. For sparse polynomial equations over F₂, use the paper's W-tagged integer parity-indicator lift, with precision ℓ=a and total Boolean degree k(m_test+2(ℓ−1)). All original 2-adic divisibility at Boolean inputs remains valid; the final W-coefficient sum is congruent modulo 2^a to the correct number of assignments. Consequently every deterministic greedy hash selection gets its exact promise-free count. No CRT over multiple rational primes or randomized assignment fingerprint is needed.

For λ=log n, ν=log λ, take the source's r=Θ(sqrt(λ)/ν²), Q=Θ(λ/ν), R=C sqrt(λ) for a sufficiently large constant C, and for example s=floor(R/2). Then R−s=Ω(R), so the rank tail meets the stated design requirement after a constant adjustment. The additional Boolean order is L=O(n/R), versus charged q=Θ(nR/Q) at linear input degree. Equivalently one may take s=ceil(Q/R) for large n, giving L=O(nR/Q) and s=o(R). The integer measurement cost O(log D) per component has become O(Q/R) at the second choice. The finite-difference and local-group envelopes still have exponent O(nν²/sqrt(λ)), with the matching charged-envelope limitation in decision.md. This is an intermediate alphabet and arithmetic refinement of the rational interpolation route. It neither proves polynomial-time SAT nor supersedes Appendix D's sharper asymptotic bound.
