# Partial jet aggregation for the actual replacement rows

At a source node t fix an integer 0≤a<J. Expand each polynomial replacement row as P(t+z)=B(z)+E(z), where B retains orders 0 through a and E is divisible by z^(a+1). In the product of source factors substitute <B(z),x>+<E(z),X> in every incidence. Substituting X=x restores the desired jet. Every occurrence of X has z-order at least a+1, so its degree in the coefficient of z^j is at most floor(j/(a+1)). Source Lemma boolean-differences therefore computes the order-J jet by at most C_q=sum_{i≤min(q,n)} 2^i binom(n,i) fixed substitutions and restrictions, with q=floor((J−1)/(a+1)). Set q=0 if every E vanishes. All arithmetic uses the truncated source coefficient algebra.

For each group h, concatenate the values of every coefficient row of B restricted to group h, for incidences belonging to factors assigned outside h. The resulting vector is the signature of an assignment to that group. Let L_h(a) be the number of distinct signatures among all its Boolean assignments. Enumerate assignments once to bucket them; the cost is polynomial overhead times sum_g 2^|S_g|. Restrictions in the Boolean-difference queries remove incompatible assignments. For each fixed X and global tuple of signatures, a factor depends only on assignments in its own group. Multiply owned factors, sum over that group's signature bucket, and multiply the resulting group sums. Summing over the signature tuples then gives that restricted query exactly, because each assignment lies in exactly one tuple.

For each group g there are at most product_{h≠g} L_h(a) outside tuples to process, and summing over all its buckets costs at most 2^|S_g| assignments for each such tuple. Thus the cost at the node is

    poly(input, J, coefficient bit costs) · C_q · sum_g 2^|S_g| product_{h≠g} L_h(a).

This specializes to the source oracle at a=0, with signature cardinalities bounded by the original group measurement ranges. When a≥deg(P) for every row, only one substitution is needed. Low-dimensional off-group coefficient spaces can bound signature growth if their Boolean images are small (e.g. k prime-field basis measurements give ≤p^k signatures). A rank bound alone over Q is insufficient: one integer row can encode an assignment injectively. A source implementation can select a independently at each node after enumerating signatures, or simply fall back to the source running time.

For a≥max deg(P), any outside-assigned incidence (f,v) with v in group h reveals x_v in h's signature: evaluate its full restricted jet at z=−t and use P(0)=delta_v. Thus L_h(a) is at least 2 raised to the number of distinct such boundary variables. Partial jets can avoid this obstruction but the source gives no uniform growth bound for their signatures. The tradeoff above is instance-sensitive; it proves no better worst-case bound by itself.
