# Joint Taylor contraction using connected sets of corrections

Fix a Taylor node and a realizable measurement tuple. Let independent data blocks be the group fibers, or any finer product representation of those fibers and their local baseline factors. At this fixed tuple write each factor as A_f + D_f(z), where A_f is the baseline and ord_z D_f≥w_f≥1. For each factor record the block containing its baseline and every block its correction reads. Join two factors when these recorded sets intersect. It is valid to use overestimates. Sharing variables is not the right test if conditioning induces correlations within a group.

Let B be the partition sum of the baseline and assume its block partition sums are units; then B is a unit. For U⊆F let Z_U be the partition sum with only the corrections in U enabled, divided by B. All Z_U have constant one. In the truncated power series ring define

    K_U = product_{V⊆U} Z_V^{(−1)^{|U|−|V|}}.

The negative powers are inverses of unit series with constant one, computable by multiplication and subtraction alone. By multiplicative subset inversion the sought series is B·product_U K_U. If U decomposes into two nonadjacent nonempty components, independence and baseline normalization give Z_V=Z_{V intersect component 1} Z_{V intersect component 2} for all V⊆U. Pairing factors in the product then gives K_U=1.

For the order cutoff, tag every correction by its own indeterminate y_f before forming K_U. Setting y_f=0 for any f∈U makes each Z_V coincide with the term indexed by V toggled at f and cancels the product. Thus every nonconstant monomial in K_U includes every marking variable y_f for f∈U. A correction occurrence with that mark has z-order at least w_f; this filtration is preserved by products and unit series inversion. Therefore K_U=1 modulo z^{q+1} whenever sum_{f∈U}w_f>q. These identities use no logarithms or integer factorial inversions and hold in all characteristics under the unit assumption.

If the overlap graph has maximum degree Δ and every w_f≥w, only connected sets of size ≤h=floor(q/w) remain. There are at most m(4 max(1,Δ))^h times a polynomial in h: root each set and encode a canonical spanning-tree depth-first traversal by an ordered tree shape and one of at most Δ choices per child. For each such set one may evaluate its 2^{|U|} subseries directly. A complexity bound must include these local evaluation costs. For rational finite group fibers zero baseline units can be repaired even for the source algebra Q[V]/V^ℓ: add scalar λ to each baseline, leave the positive-order corrections unchanged, skip empty fibers and choose generic integer λ. Each scalar constant-V baseline block partition polynomial has degree equal to its number of factors and leading coefficient equal to its positive fiber size. At most m scalar λ values fail across all blocks of one tuple. Among 2m+1 consecutive integers there are m+1 usable values; interpolate the unnormalized final series, degree ≤m in λ, and evaluate at zero. This argument is per tuple. Its leading-coefficient justification is characteristic-zero; the identities still work whenever units are separately available in other rings.

**Node on a bounded-degree expander.** Use negative edge clauses on a cubic expander, direct factor 1−u v, and rows (1−T)e_i at both endpoints. At t=1 the Taylor integrand after diagonal substitution is exactly product_{uv}(1−z² x_u x_v); its base partition sum is 2^n over Q and its normalized baseline is the independent uniform product on individual variables. The line graph has degree at most four. For each subset V of at most h edges its normalized subseries is computable by checking at most 2^{2h} assignments to their endpoints, with the rest integrating to one. The method above computes the full q-jet in n·2^{O(q)}poly(n,q) rational bit operations: normalization and unit-series inversion up to q have polynomial bit size as all denominators in this example are powers of two with O(q²) exponents and products, and logarithmic scalar heights remain polynomial in n,q. For an explicit implementation one can instead use formal log/exp over Q: divide by integers through q, use common denominator 2^{2q²}q!, and sum local coefficients across all connected sets before exponentiating. At q=Θ(log n), the time is polynomial versus exp(Θ(log² n)) for the fixed-subset count of the universal mask routine. This is a connected family with linear treewidth and no small separator in its variable graph. At this special node the constant base needs neither measurements nor assignments to a large group. The displayed rows are permitted general replacement rows of degree one. To embed in a whole admissible layout one may set r=2 with one group containing all variables, Q=Θ(n/log n), R=1 or 2; d=2m then gives q=Θ(log n). The displayed polynomial time is for the node t=1. Other nodes of this layout are not thereby tractable.

linked_cluster_check.py compares the equivalent rational log/exp calculation to exact diagonal enumeration on 40 graph/order pairs, including the empty graph, disjoint edges, a triangle with a tail and K4, through order 12. All agree. It also confirms 64 disconnected terms vanish and order ≥2|U| holds. The proof of the multiplicative formulation is independent of the logarithmic implementation.

This is an application of the linked-cluster principle to the manuscript's actual Taylor integrands. Efficient exact calculation of low coefficients from connected configurations on bounded-degree graphs is established in the partition-function literature, including [Patel and Regts, SIAM Journal on Computing (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1101003). No priority claim is made for the general connected-subgraph principle.
