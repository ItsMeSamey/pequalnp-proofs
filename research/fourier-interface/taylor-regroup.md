# One grouped interpolation evaluation per subset

Write G_x(Y,z) for the manuscript's truncated Taylor integrand and q=J−1. Its Boolean-difference formula runs over U⊆E⊆[n] with |E|≤q, with summands (−1)^{|E|−|U|} times the sum of G_x(1_U,z) over E⊆supp(x). Regroup by U. If U⊆supp(x) and h=|x|−|U|, the net multiplier is

    b(h,k)=sum_{j=0}^{min(h,k)} (−1)^j binom(h,j),  k=q−|U|.

For all other x the multiplier is zero. For k≥h it equals 1 if h=0 and zero otherwise. For 0≤k<h it equals (−1)^k binom(h−1,k). The integer identity works over any commutative coefficient ring by mapping the integer into the ring.

For fixed U call the original grouped measurement factorization once, pinning U to ones, over the original truncated Taylor ring extended by a marker w. In each local group multiply its factor product by w to the number of ones outside U in that group's assignment. Sum inside each measurement fiber, multiply the group polynomials, and add over all measurement tuples. The coefficient of w^h is the sum of G_x with exactly h extra ones. Apply b(h,k) to each coefficient and add. Group separability is preserved since each marker exponent uses only that group's local assignment. Representatives need not obey the pins. Polynomials in w have degree ≤n, so the additional storage and arithmetic per ring operation are polynomial. The algorithm can apply the weight operation inside each measurement tuple before accumulating, keeping the same polynomial-space streaming guarantee.

The exact grouped calls per point are L_q=sum_{j=0}^{min(q,n)}binom(n,j), replacing C_q=sum_{j=0}^{min(q,n)}2^j binom(n,j). For q=o(n), C_q/L_q=2^q(1+O(q/n)) since both sums are dominated by the final terms. For q≥n, this gives 2^n calls instead of 3^n. Every nonzero weight then forces x=1_U. At q=0 the multiplier is 1 on all inputs, including the empty assignment. This changes the general interpolation algorithm's combinatorial factor without changing its leading published asymptotic exponent. The result does not concern the separate headline tensor branch.

The same combinatorial identity applies to odd prime fields, characteristic two, prime powers and extensions: it divides by no field element. Compute binomial weights as integers or by Pascal recursion in the coefficient ring, which avoids inverses for j≥p. Coefficient size in the rational implementation stays polynomial: count-polynomial coefficients partition the same sums of products into bins by Hamming weight, bounded by the sum of absolute values used in the manuscript's norm analysis. Multiplication by b(h,k) contributes at most n bits, and summing at most n+1 bins another log₂(n+1). Marker multiplication and addition involve O(n²) extra coefficient operations per original operation by schoolbook convolution.

A smaller marker uses b(h,k)=[y^k](1−y)^{h−1}, with the inverse a truncated power series when h=0. Use local factors (1−y) to the number of extra ones, modulo y^{k+1}. Finally multiply by 1+y+…+y^k and take [y^k]. This uses no inverses of field integers in any characteristic. For q>n only the difference formula's outer subset cap may be replaced by n; retain the full original Taylor jet J. The smaller marker uses at most J·ℓ·(k+1) rational coefficients per working buffer in the source ring. Markers raise the binary logarithm of the sum of absolute coefficients of a term by at most n, adding no denominators. Sequential streaming gives polynomial space.
