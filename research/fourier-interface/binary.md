# Binary swap construction

Let Π_i swap the i-th ordered color pair. Over F₂ set N_i=1+Π_i, V=∏Π_i, g=Σ_{|S|<R}∏_{i∈S}N_i and M=Vg. The commuting N_i square to zero. Every term in M is a full swap followed by at most R−1 undone swaps. Any nonzero entry therefore connects source and target colors disagreeing at fewer than R positions.

On an orbit with h unequal pairs, the swap action is regular. Its algebra is F₂[u₁,…,u_h]/(u_i²), identifying the active N_i with u_i, while inactive N_i vanish. V−g is the sum of squarefree monomials of degree at least R. Correction I−M=V(V−g) has the same rank as multiplication by V−g. If h<R the correction vanishes. Otherwise split the inputs at grade b=floor((h−R)/2). The low inputs have dimension L=Σ_{j≤b} binom(h,j); every other input maps into grades at least b+1+R, a space of dimension ≤L by symmetry. Thus rank correction ≤2L≤2·2^h exp(−R²/(2h)). Conversely the Hamming ball of radius b contains no pair at distance greater than h−R, so the restriction of M to that principal block is zero and the correction has an identity minor of order L. This matching lower bound applies to any matrix on the orbit with the same support condition. Summing the upper bounds over all orbits gives fractional rank ≤2 exp(−R²/(2Q)).

Take binary row bases orbit by orbit for the correction, and use the paper's incidence schedule and group splitting. Coefficients supporting each orbit belong to at most two colors at a given position. M is constructed in polynomial time in dimension D and Q, e.g. by the elementary-symmetric recurrence in the N_i followed by row reduction. Choose distinct nonzero nodes t_i in a characteristic-two extension containing more than max(Q,2n) elements. Multiply each M[u,w] term by ∏_{i:source(w,i)≠target(u,i)}(1−T/t_i). This has degree <R, is one at zero, and leaves only locally assigned entries at each node. The constant correction needs only binary measurements, even though the field weights and interpolation nodes lie in the extension. Weighted assignment sums as in decision.md give a one-sided randomized decision algorithm for direct-clause inputs. The unweighted scalar gives only parity. C_q and the Boolean group scans remain superpolynomial under the current cost envelope.

The support and rank bounds were checked on 54 regular-orbit/degree combinations by binary_swap.py. The binary orbit rank estimate also follows directly from Dvir and Edelman’s Lemma 2.1 applied to the truncated squarefree polynomial Σ_{|S|≤h−R} ∏_{i∈S} zᵢ. On weights through h−R this polynomial is the indicator of the zero vector. [Dvir and Edelman, Lemma 2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.01646#page=3)
