# Scalar contraction on a ternary Fourier interface

Take a consistent explicit affine subsystem of constraints, eliminate it by binary Gaussian elimination and parameterize its solutions bijectively by d free bits z. Inconsistent systems have count zero. Suppose each remaining factor is a bounded-width truth table on affine forms in z (ordinary clauses meet this condition after substitution). Absorb constants into its table. Supply a binary tree on factors. For any subset S represented by a subtree, let U_S be the binary span of its factor argument rows. Write B_S = U_S ∩ U_complement. The maximum dimension of these boundary spaces is w. A tree with w logarithmic in the input length gives polynomial-time exact counting, including when there is a dense affine subsystem.

For S, keep normalized Fourier coefficients of its indicator product at s in B_S. Fourier support lies in U_S. The scalar count is 2^d times the root coefficient at zero. At a leaf enumerate its bounded-dimensional argument space, respecting dependencies, to compute its coefficients. At a fork write A, B, E for the respective row spans of the two children and exterior. For s in P=(A+B)∩E, the parent coefficient is the sum of f(a)g(b) with a+b=s, a in A and b in B. Such a,b necessarily lie in L=A∩(B+E) and R=B∩(A+E), so child messages suffice. Normalized coefficients of indicator products have denominator dividing 2^d and absolute value at most one. Temporary products and sums, including Walsh intermediates, use O(d+1) bits up to fixed constants.

**Batched fork.** Write p=dim P, k=dim(A∩B), j=dim J for J=A∩B∩E. The admissible pair space T={(a,b) in A×B : a+b in E} has dimension p+k by projecting onto a+b. J×J is a 2j-dimensional subspace. Enumerate one representative (a,b) of each of the 2^(p+k−2j) cosets using bases obtained by Gaussian elimination. For each coset, convolve (f(a+u): u∈J) with (g(b+u): u∈J) by the Walsh transform on J. Add the resulting 2^j entries to the message at a+b+J. This costs poly(input)·2^(p+k−j) per fork, including exact bit arithmetic. Quotient-basis enumeration is important; scanning all pairs of child index sets can exceed this time.

**Width bound.** Let q_A=dim(A∩E) and q_B=dim(B∩E). Projection of T onto its first coordinate has kernel dimension q_B, so dim L=p+k−q_B; similarly dim R=p+k−q_A. As (A∩E)+(B∩E) lies in P, p≥q_A+q_B−j. Thus 2(p+k−j)≤p+dim L+dim R≤3w. Total time is poly(input)·2^(3w/2). Space is poly(input)·2^w if the transforms and one coset at a time are streamed. This is a restricted result and a derivation, with no claim of literature priority.

The original OpenAI paper explains in its algebraic-methods discussion that it enumerates assignments within groups after fixing shared integer measurements, and also sums truncated polynomials for Taylor data. These are weighted subproblems. A recursive use of the unweighted k-CNF counting theorem does not by itself evaluate these subproblems. The binary Fourier algorithm above applies directly to affine-factor instances. To extend it to arbitrary formulas one would need a uniform construction of a suitable tree and factorization, or another way to evaluate high-dimensional interfaces. Relabeling integer-valued measurements as parity measurements would lose information.

Reproducibility: python fork.py runs 150 random whole-tree examples and 300 random three-subspace forks against direct sums. The small reference implementations materialize subspaces for clarity. The complexity proof uses Gaussian elimination and quotient-basis enumeration.
