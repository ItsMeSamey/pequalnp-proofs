# Decision over a small field

## Replacing integer measurements in the direct clause routine

Use the bounded-occurrence direct clause polynomial of the OpenAI preprint, Sections 2–3. Write d for the input-factor product-degree bound, R for replacement degree, Q for the number of separating points and c ≤ 2τn for the number of shared measurements. At the origin the sum is the count. Instead use the generating polynomial
P(y) = Σ_{satisfying x} ∏_{i:x_i=1} y_i.
Its distinct monomials make it a nonzero polynomial over every field if the formula is satisfiable. Select a prime Q<p≤2Q for Q≥2 and a finite extension K of F_p with |K|>2n. The least such extension has polynomial cardinality under the paper's parameters, so even exhaustive irreducible-polynomial search and polynomial-time divisibility tests suffice. Independent uniform y_i in K give a one-sided randomized SAT test with false-negative probability ≤n/|K|. Enumeration of field elements is unnecessary for sampling. Absorb the assignment weights into the sums over actual local bits, outside the polynomial substitution. This changes neither T-degree nor Taylor order.

At each interpolation point and group g, stack the restrictions to g of the replacement rows from factors assigned elsewhere into matrix M_g. Over the rationals its row rank is ≤c_g by the kernel condition in Definition 3's layout (see source label def:layout). The common denominator divides (R−1)!Q!. Reduction into F_p therefore exists and cannot increase rank, by vanishing minors. Choose a row basis of the reduced M_g after reduction. The effects on all other groups are determined by these ≤c_g measurements in F_p, even if the original measurement basis degenerates modulo p. The Taylor jet method fixes Boolean substitutions for the derivative terms as in the source; at the base point only these reduced measurement rows are needed. All Taylor identities are algebraic, and the Hasse interpolation map is invertible because the ideals (T−t)^J for distinct nodes t are pairwise comaximal, including when J≥p. No ordinary derivative factorial is inverted.

The vector-scan bound becomes
2Qr C_q min(p,2D+1)^{2τn} 2^{2n/r}.
When integer summaries are smaller, keep the rational measurement classes; their equality implies rational equality of the relevant evaluated replacements, hence equality after modular reduction. The remaining polynomial factors have small-field instead of rational coefficient arithmetic. This applies to direct-clause products. The general proof also uses 2-adic lifts, least nonnegative residues and exact counts to select tests. Reducing those intermediate counts modulo p would not implement its comparisons or its 2-adic congruence.

**Optimization audit.** For the design and sufficient conditions actually used in the direct bounded-occurrence construction, write λ=log n and ν=log λ. The polynomial-dimension balance condition D²=r^{4Q}≤O(n) forces Q=O(λ/log r). The tail certificate 2 exp(−R²/(8Q))≤τ/(3r) forces R²≥8Q log(6r/τ)=Ω(Q log r). With d=Θ(n), q=floor(dR/Q) is at least a constant times n log r /sqrt(λ) for large n. For r≤sqrt(λ)/ν² the group enumeration term already has log ≥Ω(nν²/sqrt(λ)). In the other case log r=Ω(ν), so q≥q₀=floor(c nν/sqrt(λ)) for a small absolute c. Then log C_q ≥log binom(n,q₀)≥q₀ log(n/q₀)=Ω(nν²/sqrt(λ)). The published parameter choice matches that order. Even eliminating the measurement charge entirely leaves the same optimized order for this *specific proof envelope*. This does not bound actual solver runtime and does not address the separate sharper Appendix D construction.

## A width-preserving white-box test for the affine contraction

In the separate algorithm of [the Fourier interface](README.md), choose a basis b₁,...,bᵤ of the span of all argument rows from the rows themselves. Assign variable y_j to the existing factor that supplies b_j and multiply its table value by y_j to the power b_j·z on assignment z, absorbing affine constants in its truth table separately. The spans, boundaries and tree remain unchanged. Every attainable assignment to the basis occurs 2^{d−u} times, and distinct satisfying basis assignments give distinct monomials. Over any odd field, 2^{d−u} is invertible. Random evaluation in a field of size greater than 2u gives error probability less than 1/2 with the original fork exponent.

For a deterministic decision test work over F₃. Distinct weights belong to distinct leaves or at most k weights to a leaf, and sibling weight sets are disjoint. For each node store a linear basis of message vectors actually attained at Boolean evaluations of its weights. Also keep, if desired, a realizing point for each vector using pointers to children. At a leaf enumerate ≤2^k points. By bilinearity and disjointness, the images of all pairs of spanning child vectors span the full evaluation space, and every such candidate pair is realizable. Select a basis of original candidates by elimination. A root nonzero vector exists exactly when its multi-affine polynomial is nonzero, since Boolean evaluations determine every multi-affine polynomial even over F₃. Each space has dimension ≤2^w. The crude deterministic bound is poly(input) 2^{O(w)} with no integer counting. This is formula-dependent white-box PIT rather than a general black-box hitting set, and does not reduce the high-width worst case.

## Narrow sets and fixed parity

For a promised model bound s≥1, deterministic sparse isolation can use the first n(s−1)+1 primes q. Encode an n-bit assignment as an integer in [0,2^n). For any fixed solution the product of its nonzero differences from the other solutions has fewer than n(s−1)+1 distinct prime divisors. For at least one q the chosen assignment occupies a unique residue bucket. Evaluate the assignment-generating polynomial in F₂[X]/(X^q−1) with y_i=X^{2^i mod q}, indexing bits from zero. A bucket coefficient 1 witnesses satisfiability. These probes have polynomial size and number when s is polynomial. They need a weighted parity evaluator, whose cost is separate. Under the same promise the exact unweighted count modulo one integer greater than s already decides SAT.
