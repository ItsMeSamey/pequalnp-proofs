# Mixed weighted supports and a source-layout family

At node t let q=J−1. Assign each variable i a cost c_i in the positive integers or infinity. In an incidence row write the coefficient multiplying coordinate i as its full polynomial if i belongs to the factor's assigned group or c_i is infinite; otherwise split it into a part through order c_i−1 in z and a residual divisible by z^{c_i}. This is a coefficientwise split, so it also applies when different incidences have different full degrees. Keep retained parts on x and put residual parts on X. Off-group partial signatures collect all coefficients of the retained parts as in taylor-bulk.md. Every occurrence of X_i costs at least c_i in z. Boolean reduction replaces a repeated variable power by one copy and cannot increase its weighted support. Only E in the down-set K={E: sum_{i in E} c_i ≤q} survive.

For fixed U in K impose x_U=1 and substitute X=1_U. Sum over formerly enumerated supersets E of U by multiplying each group summand by

    product_{i in supp(x_g) minus U, c_i finite}(1−y^{c_i}).

Take the sum of the marker coefficients through order q−sum_{i in U}c_i after group multiplication. Expanding the marker selects the remaining variables of E with exactly the required signs and budget. The per-node cost is a polynomial factor times |K| times the partial-signature group cost in taylor-bulk.md. Integer marker norms are ≤2^n along each global term, and A=Q[V]/V^ell of the source is valid. In characteristic two the minus signs become plus, but the algorithm requires no change of characteristic and retains the source's exact final readout.

A strict comparison of Taylor evaluation bounds is possible on nonconstant-width graphs. Take r on the order of n^(1/3) balanced variable groups, each supporting an arbitrary linear-size bounded-occurrence bounded-width CNF, with a linear total number of incidences. Join consecutive groups by a bridge clause on two designated endpoints, one from either group. The primal graph can be connected and can contain any chosen bounded-degree graph on a group (one binary clause per edge); it can consequently have unbounded treewidth. The number of distinct bridge endpoints is O(r). At every evaluation node measure all bridge endpoints separately in their own group; the matrices have unit row norms and O(r) total rows.

Put L=ceil(log2 n), Q=2L, R=L. Designate m=floor(sqrt(n)) exceptional variables, all others ordinary. For each internal factor with natural group h, assign it to h at nodes 2,...,Q and to h+1 cyclically at node 1. Use incidence row (1−T)^c delta_v with c=1 for an exceptional input v and c=L otherwise. For bridge factors use the constant coordinate rows and assign them to one endpoint's group. These are valid source Definition-layout rows: initial values are delta_v; the only off-group internal rows are zero at node 1, and all off-group bridge inputs are measured. Coefficients meet its height condition after clearing the specified denominator, and r, tau and D can be taken as above with tau=O(r/n), D=1. This is a directly specified layout, independent of the source's generic auxiliary-label construction.

For degree O(n) products the source order bound gives q=O(n), and for the supplied degree bound d=Theta(n) its generic choice has q=Theta(n). At node 1, truncate internal variables with c_i=1 or L according to their designation. No retained off-group coefficient from any internal row then survives, since (1−(1+z))^c=(−z)^c. Bridge rows reveal only O(r) measured endpoints. Hence

    |K| ≤ 2^m sum_{j≤q/L} binom(n,j)
        = 2^O(sqrt(n) + n log log n / log n).

The group enumeration and signatures cost at most 2^O(n/r+r) times polynomial overhead. At the other nodes all internal inputs are local, and all bridge inputs are measured: the full jet can be evaluated with one substitution. The resulting bound for this layout is 2^o(n) compared with the source's uniform Boolean-difference enumeration count of 2^Omega(n) for its generic q=Theta(n). If every variable occurs internally, full off-group jets at node 1 reveal all variables, so unconditionally storing every full jet does not explain this improvement. The separation compares evaluation bounds on a supplied layout. The formula family also has small intergroup separators, so it is not a separation from general exact counting algorithms or a worst-case improvement to the paper's constructed layout.
