# A characteristic-zero permanent reduction to a seven-terminal grove

For each N≥1 there is an explicit undirected multigraph with O(N²) vertices and edges and seven distinct marked vertices whose exact (3,3,1) grove polynomial computes the ordinary N×N permanent by a polynomial arithmetic reduction over every field of characteristic zero. The same construction works for the weighted polynomial of three prescribed pair separations in a spanning three-forest.

Let Z(G) assign the monomial ∏_{e∈F}w_e to each spanning forest inducing the prescribed three blocks and having exactly three trees. All ordinary edge weights in the original Z(G) are different formal variables. The proof makes signed, constant-degree polynomial substitutions for them and extracts one trivariate coefficient. It does not use an oracle for exponential multidegrees.

## The local four-port signature

Use the four-port graph of weighted-permutation.md (SHA256 14bb419a8fc89d2112c9e8f662ffacbc37c00c0d4ce33a4b18da15ca350c614a). Its star-center conductances are (x,1−x,1,−1), and between every two ports ij it has conductance −g_i g_j. In any surrounding grove its exact signature on local forests all of whose trees touch a port is I+x(1−x)D. Here I consists of the all-distinct partition of the four ports, and D has +1 on the three pair-pair partitions, −1 on the four triple-single partitions, zero elsewhere. This follows by the star-center cofactor and enumeration over which block contains the center. In particular D vanishes when any unmarked port is incident to no other active state: integrating out that port on the remaining three separates, sums the two opposite two-block terms, or requires an all-connected term, in every case giving zero. An inactive I acts as the identity under gluing, with weight one.

## Three layers

Make vertices a_0,...,a_N, c_0,...,c_N and, for each middle label j, four distinct interface vertices p_j,q_j,r_j,s_j. Install the following independent gadgets with private centers.

* For each ordered (i,j) with 1≤i,j≤N install a left gadget on (a_{i−1},a_i,p_j,q_j) with x=L Xij.
* For each j install one middle gadget on (p_j,q_j,r_j,s_j) with x=M.
* For each (j,k) install a right gadget on (r_j,s_j,c_{k−1},c_k) with x=R.

There are no bare edges along the two chains. Attach a pendant copy a'_0 to a_0 and a pendant copy c'_0 to c_0 with unit edges, and add an isolated marked vertex z. The seven terminals have target blocks {a_0,a_N,a'_0}, {c_0,c_N,c'_0}, {z}. Both pendant edges are forced. In the remaining proof one can discard them and z and ask for a spanning two-forest with blocks a_0 a_N | c_0 c_N.

There are 6N+2 interface vertices. An active D performs precisely two joins, while I performs zero. In an accepted gluing no join is redundant, because a redundant join makes a cycle. Thus the accepted terms always have exactly 3N active gadgets. Extract the coefficient [L^N M^N R^N]. Each active scalar x(1−x) contributes at least one tag degree, so every active scalar must contribute its linear term. Exactly N are active in each layer, making all middle gadgets active. No quadratic correction contributes.

An unused middle index on the left leaves p_j and q_j incident only to its own active middle D, hence contributes zero by the single-port cancellation. Thus the left set uses every middle index exactly once. The right set uses each middle index exactly once by the same argument. On the outer a-chain both marked endpoints require positive active incidence. An unmarked internal vertex must have incidence ≥2: no active occurrence leaves it alone and exactly one is killed by port integration. The total left incidence on the a-chain is 2N, so both endpoints have incidence one and each interior vertex has incidence two. Therefore consecutive row counts k_i satisfy k_1=1 and k_{i−1}+k_i=2, forcing one selected cell in every row. The identical argument gives one selected cell in every last index k. The selected left and right sets are independent permutations π:i↦j and σ:j↦k. Their tag-free scalar is ∏_i X_{i,π(i)}.

## The amplitude and its symmetrization

Fix π,σ. Every unmarked interface now lies in exactly two active cells. Represent it by an edge between those two cells. Represent each of the four marked exterior endpoints by a dangling halfedge at its cell. The value of this pure-D network at the requested two-tree split is the number of ordered decompositions of its internal edges into two spanning trees. For clarity, fix a global coloring of all interfaces by their target tree, with the prescribed two halfedges of each color. If a cell has four equal colors, its contribution is zero after summing local choices. Indeed the entire tree on that monochrome vertex set sees no effect from replacing the star-plus-clique by its electrically equivalent inactive graph, by the reduced Laplacian Schur complement; the D term of the difference vanishes. This holds with the exterior forest choices fixed, even when they disconnect the remaining graph. Equivalently the local D is in the kernel of every unmarked spanning-tree gluing functional. At each remaining cell both colors occur, so its two local blocks must be exactly its color classes. After contracting each such local block, the global two-tree condition says the two edge classes of the cell graph are spanning trees. Conversely any ordered complementary spanning-tree pair uses both colors at each cell (there are at least three cells), determining one allowed local state. The product of local signs is +1 because the sum of the first-color degrees including its two dangling halfedges is even.

Define H_τ on N vertices as the union of a labelled path in natural order and a path in τ-value order, retaining distinct parallel edges. Let a(τ) be its number of ordered complementary spanning-tree pairs, with both empty trees on the singleton when N=1. In the full cell graph each selected left cell has two parallel edges to its corresponding middle cell, which has two parallel edges to its corresponding right cell. Any complementary pair must assign one edge of each parallel pair to each tree. Contracting one in both colors identifies these three vertices; in the full graph this gives disjoint contractions for each of the N triples of cells. The remaining edges form precisely H_{σ∘π}. Conversely each decomposition of this contracted graph lifts in exactly two independent ways for each of the 2N parallel pairs, giving factor 2^{2N}. Thus
[L^N M^N R^N] Z(G)=Σ_{π,σ} 2^{2N}a(σ∘π)∏_i X_{i,π(i)} = c_N Per_N(X),
where c_N=2^{2N}Σ_{τ∈S_N} a(τ).
For every τ its two construction paths give a complementary pair, hence c_N>0 and is invertible in any characteristic-zero field. Its bit length is O(N log N) since a(τ)≤binom(2N−2,N−1). The fixed permutations on the right layer implement the required symmetrization inside one grove. Division by this one N-dependent field scalar is legitimate for nonuniform arithmetic complexity. A uniform arithmetic evaluation reduction can evaluate the identical coefficient at the identity matrix once and divide by that value.

## Size, scope and checks

The graph has 2N²+N private gadget centers and O(N) other vertices, and ten edges per gadget before padding. Each edge substitution costs O(1) arithmetic operations. Coefficient extraction can propagate truncated arrays on degrees 0,...,N of each tag, multiplying arrays in O((N+1)^6) operations per gate even with unrestricted intermediate degrees. Together with scalar normalization this proves the polynomial arithmetic reduction.

The source degree-component lower bound for Per_N in characteristic zero is exp(Ω((log N)²/(log log N)²)). Therefore this explicit family of formal grove polynomials has superpolynomial exact arithmetic circuit size in every characteristic-zero field, and the same lower bound expression up to constants when written in terms of its graph size. Multiplying a hypothetical circuit by polynomial overhead does not absorb the bound. [November 24 primary theorem](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openai/math/main/preprints/Circuit-lower-bounds-for-Hamiltonian-cycle-and-the-permanent-November-24-2026/build/intro.tex).

On the same graph the three pairs (a_0,c_0),(a_N,c'_0),(a'_0,c_N) enforce exactly the same accepted forests. The isolated z supplies the third tree. The first pair forces a_0 and c_0 into the other two distinct components; detaching either pendant would create an extra component. With both pendant edges present the other two tests then put a_N with a_0 and c_N with c_0. Alternatively omit the two pendant copies and directly use (a_0,c_0),(a_N,c_0),(a_0,c_N) with repeated terminals. The prescribed (3,3,1) grove itself forces the two pendants without a tag. An arbitrary-host theorem remains valid if connected host graphs are required by adding a zero-weight edge to the singleton in the reduction.

This result supplies no characteristic-two lower bound: already the 2^{2N} factor is zero. Over a fixed odd characteristic it works at orders with nonzero c_N; no eventual such claim is needed or made. It gives a bound on exact formal arithmetic computation, without implications for Boolean grove feasibility, the dense binary graphic-host decision problem, or P versus NP.
