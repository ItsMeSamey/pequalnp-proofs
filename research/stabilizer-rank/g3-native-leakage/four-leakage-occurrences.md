# Transporting a canceled row excludes three wrong occurrences

Keep the native three-check coordinates and definitions of three-leakage-occurrences.md. Suppose a wrong outcome v has a vanishing sum of p stabilizer terms v_j, all with a shared good outcome s in their affine supports. Each one's contribution at s is U_j v_j for a data Pauli times a fourth root U_j, by the shift-syndrome tableau. Choose U_p and subtract U_p times the zero sum. Then
    Σ_j U_j v_j = Σ_{j<p} (U_j−U_p)v_j.
Each term on the right is zero or a single stabilizer up to scalar, since two phased Pauli translates of a stabilizer decode to computational labels with fourth-root relative phase. This identity allows arbitrary distinct stabilizer frames at v and arbitrary complex coefficients.

If exactly three wrong occurrences exist in all, all are at a single v since one occurrence cannot cancel. Write p for the number of copies of the unique three-good plane through v and q=3−p for the number of other columns hitting v. Other columns meeting v can cover at most one good point. Hence the good incidence total is at most 3p+q+2(7−p−q)=14+p−q. Requiring sixteen gives p=3, q=0. There are at most seventeen good incidences, so at least two of the three plane-good points are tight four-term rows. At any such point the transported zero-sum identity replaces the three plane contributions by at most two. This contradicts χ(G2)=4. Together with the reviewed exclusion of zero, one and two wrong occurrences, at least four are necessary. This counts term/outcome incidences, not the number of distinct wrong syndromes.
