# At least three wrong-sector occurrences per block

In a hypothetical exact seven-stabilizer decomposition of G3, measure Z on two bits of any one CCZ and X on its remaining bit. In the binary outcome triple (a,b,c), the correct points form D={(a,b,ab)}. At each correct point the target on the six remaining qubits is a fixed nonzero scalar times G2; all other points have zero target. Count one wrong occurrence per parent with nonzero projection at one wrong point. At least three wrong occurrences are necessary.

With zero wrong occurrences every parent's affine outcome support meets at most two points: the four points of D have nonzero XOR and contain no affine plane. Seven parents then give at most fourteen good occurrences versus sixteen required by χ(G2)=4. A wrong point cannot have just one occurrence because the target there is zero. If there are exactly two wrong occurrences, both occur at the same point v. In D∪{v} the unique affine plane consists of v and the three points of D other than its vertical mate v+(0,0,1): XOR of all points of D is (0,0,1). Every other allowable affine support has size at most two. If p columns use the plane, there are at most 3p+2(7−p)=14+p good occurrences. Since there are only two wrong occurrences, p≤2; hence p=2, all four good rows are tight, and the other five columns each meet two good points.

At v the two plane columns cancel exactly, so their data rays there coincide. For each of the three good points s on the plane, coherent stabilizer transport from v to s acts on each data slice by a Pauli times a fourth root of unity, using the shift-syndrome element of its stabilizer tableau (the element contributes only a Pauli phase on computational syndrome basis states). The transported pair therefore has the form λP|h>, λi^k Q|h> for data Paulis P,Q and the same stabilizer h. Clifford-decode h to a computational ket; conjugating each Pauli changes only Pauli phase, so the two vectors sum to zero or to a scalar times a stabilizer: a relative fourth-root superposition of two distinct computational basis vectors is a stabilizer. This merges two of the four terms to at most one, contradicting χ(G2)=4. All coefficients may be arbitrary complex numbers; the relative phase is forced by the physical cancellation. The bound counts term/outcome occurrences, not distinct parents or distinct bad points. It does not settle existence of a seven-list.
