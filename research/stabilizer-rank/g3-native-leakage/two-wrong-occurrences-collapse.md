# Two wrong occurrences collapse a tight good slice

Use the supplied native syndrome incidence profile. Two parent stabilizers occupy a common plane whose single bad point has zero target. They are the only terms there. At a good point on the plane the target has stabilizer rank four and the complete list there has four nonzero terms. These conditions alone are inconsistent.

The two bad slices cancel as actual vectors. In particular their common data ray has one unsigned maximal stabilizer group L. Each column's other slices differ by a data Pauli up to scalar, hence they also have unsigned group L. Apply the SAME data Clifford in all sectors to take L to the computational Z group. At every occurring point each transformed column is supported on a single computational data label. The transformed coherent parent is itself a stabilizer times its original scalar, so all its nonzero entries are a common scalar times fourth roots of unity. At the bad point the two labels coincide and the two entries are negatives. Consequently the ratio of the two nonzero entries at any common point is again a fourth root.

At the tight good point, if the labels agree their sum is zero or proportional to a basis ray. If the labels differ their sum is a scalar multiple of a stabilizer: use an affine basis permutation to take the two labels to zero and a unit vector, leaving a one-qubit state with amplitude ratio in {1,-1,i,-i}. Thus the good target has a decomposition with at most three terms. The data Clifford preserves its rank, giving the contradiction. This reasoning needs a common coherent data chart and actual cancellation at the bad point.
