# A single stabilizer-bra distribution scores all local Schmidt ranks

Write h=χ(G_{k−1}). Choose uniformly one of the 135 unsigned maximal commuting Pauli subspaces L on the three qubits of a chosen block. Measure normalized g in its stabilizer basis, sampling the resulting bra with its Born probability. This finite distribution ignores exactly-zero target overlaps. For every possible outcome the same contraction applied linearly to the proposed G_k expansion gives a nonzero multiple of G_{k−1}; every surviving term is a stabilizer on the remaining system. Coefficients therefore remain consistent in every projection.

For one parent's signed local stabilizer subgroup S, dimension d, the reduced state is supported on its signed code. For J=S∩L in unsigned labels (with signs inherited from S), a bra survives exactly when its signs agree on J. Thus its Born survival probability averaged over bases is
 p(S)=A_d+B_d Σ_{P∈S, P≠I}⟨g|P|g⟩,  A_d=E_L 2^(−dim J),  B_d=(1−A_d)/(2^d−1) for d>0, and p=1 for d=0.
Indeed for each L the compatible-outcome sum is the expectation of the projector onto J. The coefficient of each nonzero S label after averaging is equal by symplectic transitivity of the stabilizer of unsigned S; the sum of all such coefficients is 1−A_d.

For u=0,1,2,3 the probabilities a fixed u-dimensional isotropic space lies in L are 1,1/9,1/45,1/135 (numbers of maximal isotropics containing it are 135,15,3,1 by symplectic quotient). Expand 2^(−j) in the numbers of subspaces of each dimension in a j-space, with coefficients 1,−1/2,3/4,−21/8 respectively. Summing over subspaces of S yields A_1=17/18, A_2=17/20, A_3=17/24. Each nonidentity Pauli expectation on g has magnitude at most 1/2: for an X shift a≠0 the cubic derivative has quadratic polar rank two, hence Walsh coefficients zero or magnitude 1/2 after normalization; a pure nonzero Z has zero expectation. Equivalently, g is the uniform plus state with a single phase flip: for a≠0 the phase product differs from +1 at exactly two translated points, giving that same bound directly for every Z twist. All nontrivial unsigned pure X shifts attain +1/2. Consequently the sharp worst-case survival constants for this specific distribution by increasing d are 1,35/36,37/40,41/48. For a parent d=3−log₂(Schmidt rank) across this block. Summing p(S) over the actual same parent list gives h≤Σp(S); apply without enumerating hypothetical decompositions. No universal survival bound below one follows from this distribution.

For joint measurement of any at most c original blocks, consider a stabilizer parent without any nonidentity stabilizer supported on such a subset. Every such reduced state is maximally mixed. Every nonzero joint bra on the measured blocks survives, including product bras selected along an adaptive branch. Such parents exist for any fixed c and sufficiently large k: a uniform random 3k-qubit stabilizer includes a given nonzero unsigned Pauli with probability 1/(8^k+1). A union bound over at most Σ_{j=1}^c binom(k,j)63^j low-block-weight Paulis is below one eventually. The same argument works whenever that sum is below 8^k+1. This prevents any per-parent contraction-only survival gap independent of entanglement for bounded block sets. It does not show that an entire exact G_k decomposition can consist of such parents or preclude joint cancellation or merging after projection.
