# A scalable projection bound with an exact fractional optimum

Write h=χ(G_{k−1}). Suppose every parent splits the chosen block with one of the sixteen minimizers e_s or v_s=g−2g_s e_s. Then 23r≥44h for the parent count r, regardless of repeated coordinates or coefficients.

Choose a linear functional ℓ with ℓ(g)=1, writing d_s=g_s ℓ(e_s) with Σd_s=1. Each surviving parent stays a scaled stabilizer because it splits the chosen block. An e_s term survives iff d_s≠0 and a v_s term iff d_s≠1/2. With probability 7/11 choose two uniform distinct coordinates with d=1/2 and the rest zero. With probability 4/11 choose a uniformly exceptional coordinate with d=−5/2 and seven halves. Both survival probabilities are 23/44. Each outcome needs h survivors to represent G_{k−1}.

This constant is sharp for worst per-ray survival over all distributions of such functionals. Assign total mass 5/11 uniformly to e_s and 6/11 uniformly to v_s. Each functional has weighted survival (5u+6(8−b))/88, with u nonzeros and b halves. If all nonzeros are halves then u=b=2; otherwise b≤u−1 and the numerator is ≥54−u≥46. Thus the worst local ray survives with probability ≥23/44. This LP optimum does not imply existence of a corresponding rank decomposition.

For arbitrary parents the same nonstabilizer bra can convert a maximally entangled stabilizer into a nonstabilizer. Even a nonzero stabilizer bra never kills a maximally entangled parent. Thus the per-column survival guarantee cannot extend this way. For arbitrary lists, direct projection still gives χ(G_k)≥h and tensor product gives χ(G_k)≤2h. No unconditional supermultiplicativity follows here.
