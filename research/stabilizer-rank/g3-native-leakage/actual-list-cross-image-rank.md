# Cross-image constraints on the physical parent list

On one original three-qubit cut, vectorize each normalized parent as a linear map A_j from the conjugate eight-dimensional block to the remote register. After absorbing any common normalizations, an actual decomposition satisfies Σ c_j A_j = |G2><conj(g)|. Choose a maximal-Schmidt parent i, so A_i†A_i=I/8, and c_i≠0. The exact matrix identity is
 Σ_{j≠i} c_j A_i†A_j = (A_i†|G2>)<conj(g)| − (c_i/8) I.
Its right hand side has rank at least seven. In particular Σ_{j≠i}rank(A_i†A_j)≥7, and the intersection K of all kernels of A_i†A_j (j≠i) has dimension at most one. The span of their column images also has dimension at least seven. A nonzero vector in K cannot be orthogonal to conj(g). These conditions hold even when other parents have smaller Schmidt rank, repeated rays, or arbitrary complex weights. The same c is used for every i and all three cuts. For specified normalized ray gauges the displayed linear equations in c can therefore be imposed simultaneously. These necessary overlap constraints concern nonzero cross maps; nonorthogonality alone does not guarantee a postmeasurement ray merger.

A compact genuine seven-ray control can evade every local pair merge. Identify each block's computational labels with GF(8), take t outside {0,1}, and use seven distinct a with states
 φ_a=8^(−1/2) Σ_z |z,z+a,z+t a>.
These are normalized stabilizer rays because the support is affine binary and the phase constant. Every cut is maximally entangled. Its remote code is spanned by pairs with fixed coordinate difference, respectively (1+t)a, t a, or a depending on the omitted block. All three multipliers are nonzero. For each cut the seven images are pairwise orthogonal, so no nonzero local bra kills a parent and no local bra merges any pair. All linear combinations have one-block marginals proportional to identity since all pairwise remote cross maps vanish. No nonzero choice of coefficients produces G3. This is an example on physical stabilizer parents, not a counterexample to an obligatory overlap in a true target decomposition.
