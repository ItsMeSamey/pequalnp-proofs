# Three native bit choices on one physical stabilizer

Every stabilizer ray on the whole system incurs at least three wrong occurrences across XZZ, ZXZ, ZZX on the same block. In each chart the desired X bit is the product of the two Z bits. If each chart has some wrong outcome the bound follows. Otherwise the support in a zero-wrong chart is an affine subset of four noncoplanar graph points, hence a singleton or a pair. For a singleton both other charts change two bases, giving four outcomes and exactly two wrong in each. For a pair the points differ on some Z control k since the X bit is determined by the Z bits. Switch to the remaining axis j, retaining Z_k. The points cannot interfere. Each remote data vector now produces all four values of the changed bits, filling all eight outcomes, four wrong.

Any seven parents therefore contribute at least 21 wrong occurrences per original block across its three charts, so some chart has at least seven. Equality requires every parent to contribute (1,1,1).

The count bound is sharp even requiring four occurrences at each good point and forbidding singleton wrong points. Translate |+++> by C_v=CCZ X^v CCZ for v=000,011,101 with multiplicities 3,2,2. Each C_v is Clifford since the derivative of the cubic is quadratic. In chart i the translated phase differs from the target cubic by a translated cubic, whose derivative is (a XOR v_j)(b XOR v_k); hence its X sign disagrees only at 11 XOR v_{−i}. The three projected shifts are distinct at each i, so wrong multiplicities are 3,2,2,0 and correct ones are 4,5,5,7 in every chart. Distinct remote stabilizer factors can make the seven global rays distinct. These are actual local incidences without a coherent target realization.
